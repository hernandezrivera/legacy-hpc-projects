Project Summary

Needs:
GBV continues to be an issue of great concern in Federal members States Galmudug, South West State, Jubaland, Hirashebelle Puntland and Somaliland with the event of the COVID19. Recent spikes in the increase of Intimate Partner Violence, rape, sexual exploitation, sexual harassment and abuse have multiplied GBV risks for women and girls with worsening impact on women and girls living with disabilities. In IDP camps and host communities – inadequate physical infrastructure, distance to water points, markets, health facilities and schools, poor lightening, lack of doors on toilets and lack of disaggregation of sanitary facilities are some of major factors that increase GBV exposure. In addition, distance to distribution centers and lack of specific measures to ensure women’s inclusion and participation in food distributions, continue to worsen levels of exposure of women and girls to GBV risks. In the July 2020 “GBV/FGM assessment during the COVID19”, 38 percent responders indicated that there has been an increase in GBV incidents; 35 percent indicated GBV incidents are the same. The number of GBV survivors call in to hotlines for help also increased by 283% in FMS and 767% in Somaliland despite the inactivation of some of the hotlines in the states.
In addition, GBVIMS 2020 data reports a trend indicating an increase of GBV noting that over half (61%) of reported GBV incidents were Physical Assault, 13% Rape, 12% Sexual Assault, 6% Psychological/Emotional Abuse, 5% experienced Denial of Resources, and the remaining 3% percent Forced/Early marriage. Furthermore, Anecdotal media and programming report has indicated a spike in rape of women and girls including a specific targeting of recent on boys. Majority of Women and girls fleeing violence in Somalia do not have options for safety and protection. In 2020, there has been significant efforts to broaden women and girls’ safe spaces, however, this has been severely impacted by funding limitations and also due to COVID.
GBV service provision remains low as compared to the need and geographical landscape. Recently, worsening relations between communities and service providers exacerbated by the heightened political atmosphere impacted provision of GBV services or coordination. GBV service provision in Somalia has historically been characterized by limited specialized services such as rape treatment for rape survivors, psycho-social support and higher levels of mental health care for traumatized women and girls are major hindrances to expanding provision of timely, confidential and quality GBV services. Also, lack of actors to provide specialized services undermines efforts invested to assure access to GBV services by survivors. At present, the GBV AoR in Somalia has 58 partners that reports on the 5Ws.
UNFPA interventions will target the displaced and non-displaced communities including all age groups and persons with disabilities, and with the following specific objectives: a) Facilitate access to comprehensive multi sectoral GBV services (medical, legal, psychosocial) for 6000 women and girls. B) Enhance the capacity/and mobilize 200 PSS counsellors; 150 health workers to provide timely, quality and confidential PSS and rape treatment. C) Promote mobilization of women and girls to access services through the establishment of 15 safe spaces for women and girls (WGSS) to reach 3000 women and girls. D) Support the operationalization of integrate referral pathways for dissemination and access to services reach 2000 women, girls, boys and men. E) Provide 2,000 dignity kits and 36 post rape kits dignity protection for women and girls and treatment of rape. F) Support 200 women and girls’ survivors of GBV with cash voucher assistance and grants for livelihood. G) Support the mainstreaming initiatives on IASC Guidelines for cluster programming on GBV for 300 cluster-coordinators at national and sub national levels.

Project Activities

The major objective of this project is to enhance access for services for GBV survivors through improving capacity of service providers, strengthening of referral pathways and provision of material, cash assistance and livelihood opportunities for women and girls. The project will target all age groups, displaced and non-displaced including persons with disabilities, and through the following activities: a. Support 10 GBV one stop centers through which survivors access multi sectoral services and other GBV response services including Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and medical assistance, legal aid, Psychosocial Support (PSS) and counselling, material assistance and safety planning for female and male GBV survivor.b. Procure and distribute 72 post rape treatment kits for treating GBV survivors and 2,000 dignity kits.c. Support capacity building/and mobilize 200 PSS counsellors to deliver cultural appropriate, age and gender responsive PSS service.d. Organize refresher trainings/and mobilize for 150 health workers on provision of treatment of rape, collecting and preserving forensic evidence for prosecution of perpetrators e. Support training and mobilization of 100 police and community police on management of GBV survivors and collection/preservation of evidencef. Support referrals and legal aid support for 150 GBV survivors g. Support the development and update of referral pathways and dissemination to communities to reach 2000 personsh. Establish 15 women and girls’ friendly safe spaces

Mitigation Measures

Risk 3) UNFPA has a PSEA policy in place for reporting and handling Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA)-related complaints and all its national and international staff have completed the mandatory SEA training. UNFPA has PSEA focal points at country office and sub-offices which regularly conducts training and awareness sessions with implementing partners. As part of project agreement, UNFPA implementing partners sign a commitment to ensure that no SEA related offences will be committed by any of their staff, and in the event that it occurs, appropriate actions will be taken. UNFPA uses the “Leave no one behind” strategy to maximize the inclusion of men and boys and persons with disability in its programming. The project will use tracking mechanisms for the reports to ensure the target beneficiaries reported are the same as those planned. Risk 5) The project will endeavor to ensure maximum consultation with marginalized communities in all phases of the program. The project will ensure inclusion of marginalized individuals in community-based structures to ensure their priorities are tailored in to the program. Risk . Risk 8): UNFPA will use community-based committees composing of all segments of the community including marginalized/minority groups. UNFPA partners will closely work with community committees to select beneficiaries for the project. Also UNFPA and its implementing partners will ensure consistent sex-disaggregated programming to ensure men, women, girls, and boys are targeted adequately.
