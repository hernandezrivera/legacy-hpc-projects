Project Summary

The proposed WASH project will be implemented in Leer & Pibor, as well as other emergency and IPC 4 and 5 locations. According to the WASH Cluster's county prioritization for 2022, Leer and Pibor are Priority 1 counties, while Renk is Priority 2. The project is designed based on Core Humanitarian and Sphere Minimum Standards to ensure that affected beneficiaries receive timely, coordinated, adequate, resilient, and impartial basic water, sanitation, and hygiene services. Medair engages the affected beneficiaries in all stages of the project cycle and ensures that the 'Do no harm' principle is followed by not exposing affected beneficiaries to any risk as a result of project implementation. Furthermore, the proposed WASH project will adhere to the WASH Cluster technical guidelines and WHO drinking water quality standards. Medair will continue to integrate WASH and nutrition internally, as well as collaborate with other nutrition and protection partners, to maximize the impact of the proposed WASH project. WASH activities will also be integrated into health activities in the context of disease prevention and response. It aims at breaking the transmission cycle of water related diseases including diarrhoeal, cholera, vector borne diseases and COVID-19. This is intended to be achieved through multiple integrated approaches. Overall, the project is targeted to reach 148,835 people through improved access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services by the end of 2022.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan has continued to deteriorate for the last few years due to multiple shocks including economic downturn, the ongoing impacts of consecutive years of severe flooding, recurring inter-communal violence and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. The WASH Cluster estimated that the living standard and well-being of 6.4 million people will be impacted in 2022 as a result of either inadequate WASH services or a lack of access to WASH services. This represents an increase over the 5.6 million people who required basic water, sanitation, and hygiene services in 2021. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions were found to be most severe in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Unity, Warrap, Lakes, Eastern Equatoria and Northern Bahr el Ghazal States (WSC, 2021). All Medair’s static project locations in Pibor and Leer counties are located in two of the above-mentioned states; Jonglei and Leer respectively. The humanitarian funding trend has been observed to be declining in recent years, which has had a negative impact on WASH partners' capacity to respond to the growing chronic humanitarian need. As a result, the WASH Cluster is recommending partners to target people who are in extreme or catastrophic WASH vulnerability. This also prompted the Cluster to classify counties into different priority levels based on the WASH Severity Classification 2021 and FSNMS+ data collected for the HPC 2022.According to the WASH Cluster classification, Leer and Pibor Counties, where Medair has static projects, are classified as Priority 1. In November 2021, Leer County experienced insecurity, which continued until February 2022. As a result, thousands of people were displaced to Leer town and the surrounding villages. Pibor County has also experienced insecurity and temporary suspension of humanitarian services at the last quarter of 2021. These situations, impose extra pressure on the already stressed WASH services in these locations. Unfortunately, due to the COVID-19 pandemic, Medair could not conduct KPC/KAP and SMART surveys in the project locations to accurately determine outcome indicators and GAM prevalence of the project locations. However, in 2021, Medair ERT WASH team conducted 12 rapid needs assessments in Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, and Western Equatoria States. The assessment report findings corroborate the HNO 2022 report that states only 39% of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide and 49% of households reported relying on surface water or unprotected sources as their main water source. The Medair ERT WASH assessment again aligned with the HNO 2022 report about sanitation coverage which revealed 80% of households did not have access a latrine across the country. Lack of access to basic water, sanitation, and hygiene services, combined with insufficient healthcare, has put communities, particularly the most vulnerable groups such as the elderly, women, and children, at greater risk of water-related diseases.

2 - Project activities

The proposed WASH project will carry out various activities. The first and most important activities are rapid needs assessments, focus group discussions, KII, baseline surveys as needed, and desk reviews to understand the context and environmental and climate factors that may influence the project during implementation. Supporting nutrition programmes and improving access to basic WASH services are integral parts of WASH activities in the WASH Cluster First Priority Counties Pibor and Leer and other WSC and IPC 4/5 locations across the country. The activities include construction and rehabilitation of resilient water points such as boreholes, water yards and surface water treatment systems (SWATs). Medair will scale up raising handpump platforms in flood affected areas. In areas where there is no safe water source, Medair will consider water trucking and distribution of household water treatment systems such as chlorine products and water filters with water collection and storage containers to targeted households. The proposed project aims to improve access to safe and gender-segregated sanitation with handwashing facilities in health and nutrition sites. However, due to limited available resources, Medair will only consider emergency latrine construction in active displacement and disease outbreaks and if their absence has a significant public health risk. The proposed WASH activities also include improving infectious waste management practices in health and nutrition facilities and promote personal and household hygiene through the Care Group network and traditional hygiene approaches. In all project locations, Medair ensures the ‘Do No Harm’ principle is implemented by training the WASH staff on PSEA and creating community wide awareness about PSEA. Furthermore, Medair will collaborate with protection partners to address GBV concerns related to WASH activities.

3 - Project indicators

The proposed WASH project sets various output and outcome indicators which enable Medair to accurately track the progress made against targets, as well as indicate areas of attention. These indicators will regularly be monitored by the Monitoring, Evaluation, and Learning (MEAL) team using standard and custom monitoring tools. The WASH technical team also plays a pivotal role in making sure all the critical project indicators are accurately tracked, compiled, and reported to the respective donors, clusters and other stakeholders. The MEAL team ensures all the collected data is accurate, without double counting and evaluates the project against indicators. If gaps are identified and the project is not on track, corrective measures will be taken, and the project will be re-aligned. The main indicators of the project include # of people who have access to safe water for drinking and domestic use; average liters/person/day collected from all sources for drinking, cooking, hygiene; % of water points developed, repaired, or rehabilitated with 0 fecal coliforms per 100 ml of water sample; % of water points developed, or rehabilitated with a free residual chlorine (FRC) ≥ 0.2 mg/L; % of households receiving point-of-use products whose water supplies have free residual chlorine (FRC) ≥ 0.2 mg/L; % of targeted households whose drinking water supplies have 0 fecal coliform per 100 ml sample; # of people having access to dignified, safe, clean and functional excreta disposal facilities; % of excreta disposal facilities built or rehabilitated in health and nutrition facilities that are clean and functional; # of targeted health and nutrition facilities implementing basic infection prevention and control (IPC) WASH services; # of people receiving direct hygiene promotion (excluding mass media campaigns and without double counting); % of people targeted by the hygiene promotion program who know at least 3 of the 5 critical times to wash hands; % of households targeted by the hygiene promotion program who store their drinking water safely in clean containers. Medair also collects outcome and intermediate outcome indicators such as beneficiary satisfaction, coverage, and beneficiary perceptions using the KPC survey. These include % of households reporting satisfaction with the contents and quality of the WASH NFIs received through direct distribution, vouchers, or cash.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Medair ensures all members of the affected population’s voices are equally heard and their concern, comments, and perceptions will be taken into consideration during programme design and implementation. The feedback collection mechanisms and focus group discussions particularly target the most vulnerable members of the community such as marginalized community members, people with disability, individuals who are not literate, women and girls.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Group’ or ‘Cascade Group’ networks in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities. In addition, before project implementation, Medair will carry out assessments, focus group discussions with different gender and age groups, and key informant interviews that will inform the type of assistance the affected populations need, and the modalities they would want to receive. Barrier analysis will be carried out to engage community participation in identifying and overcoming obstacles that limits them from implementing safe hygiene behaviour and practices. During implementation, Medair will undertake regular meetings with the affected communities and community leaders throughout interventions and feedback mechanisms will be put in place. Medair will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. A beneficiary telephone hotline, community focal point, information box, post distribution monitoring and exit surveys will be conducted to collect feedback on the affected population concerns, perceptions, and interests. Medair will respond to all complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place and conducts and community wide awareness raising session will be conducted.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Medair will be transparent with the affected population by providing them with accessible and timely information about PSEA and set up confidential feedback collection mechanisms as mentioned above. Additionally, Medair will provide information about PSEA to beneficiaries through focus group discussions, community leaders meetings and during WASH NFIs distribution. Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Care Groups’ or ‘Cascade Groups’ in all static locations, in which information is cascaded through communities. To give more emphasis on PSEA, community wide awareness raising session will be conducted.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries’ feedback will be collected with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct by programme staff. Medair analyses all feedback thoroughly to adjust the projects’ design according to the beneficiaries’ interest, context and resources. In situations where Medair is unable to make any change due to seen and unforeseen reasons, the affected beneficiaries will be contacted to recommend alternative options.

In order to mitigate this risk, Medair assesses the available protection assistance in all project locations. In many operation areas, none or very limited protection assistance is available. Medair is involved in national and sub national coordination, including the protection cluster, to advocate for addressing gaps in coverage. Medair further designs project interventions to be able to support survivors of protection incidents. All Medair staff are trained in psychological first aid and Medair supported clinics are equipped to provide medical support through clinical management of rape in a confidential matter. Where possible Medair will support the referral of the beneficiary for further protection assistance. Furthermore, Medair is cognizant of unforeseen risks that might arise in the course of implementation. During beneficiaries’ registration, Medair engages with local community leaders, chiefs and community members to reduce the risk of violence and the registration will be conducted in house- to-house approach to accurately target the most vulnerable groups. Medair consults the affected beneficiaries on most appropriate, safe and central locations that minimizes travel time. Distribution will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before dark. All COVID-19 prevention measures will be enforced to avoid the risk of transmission during distribution. Medair mitigates the risk of PSEA through training of all staff and a clear reporting mechanism for both beneficiaries and staff. Medair will also clearly communicate that any assistance is delivered free of any charge or compensation. During intervention delivery Medair works in teams rather than individuals providing services, and in activities that involve home visits have staff travel in pairs ideally with both genders. General protection mainstreaming is done throughout Medair’s project to prevent beneficiaries from harm. Medair has learned through experience that marginalized groups have less engagement in general community activities. In order to address this Medair specifically targets these groups during community consultation. This is done by facilitating access for marginalized groups to participate in meetings or focus groups discussions or, if appropriate, having consultation with these specific groups. Medair further advocates with community leaders and other stakeholders for the inclusion of marginalized groups in all interactions with providers of humanitarian aid and in coordination mechanisms.

Improved access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene services contributes to the collective outcome 1 for communities facing IPC phase 4 and 5. The proposed WASH project aims to provide resilient water supply schemes in areas repeatedly classified as IPC 4 and 5. The resilient safe water sources include flood and extended dry season resilient handpumps, upgrading existing handpumps to solar systems, and creating ownership of the constructed water points and awareness amongst the community. This activity will be complemented by hygiene promotion using the Care Group network and community level house-house hygiene promotion focusing on safe water and food storage, excreta disposal, and handwashing. Furthermore, Medair aims to work with Food Security and Livelihood partners to maximise the outcome of the project.
