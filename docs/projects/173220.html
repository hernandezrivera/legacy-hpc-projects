Project Summary

"Children represent 53.03 percent of all Rohingya refugees (Population Planning Figure_Protection sector) and the majority of whom need child protection services. The study J-MSNA, 2020 has shown the vulnerability of refugee camp, mostly relevant to Child Protection, like- Increased of Child marriage, and children missing, robbery, kidnapping, sexual harassment, threats by local people/armed groups, mahjee demanding money/using violence. Caritas Bangladesh has been implementing the project titled “Protection for Rohingya Children: A project to foster the well-being of Rohingya children and their families in Bangladesh” for around three years through operating ‘Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) and “Multi-Purpose Child and Adolescent Centre (MCAC). The objective of the project is at-risk children of Rohingya community hosted in Bangladesh are living in protecting environment and Rohingya community is strengthened to provide child protection services within the project period.
Activities including games, sports, expressive/creative activities, life skills educational activities and other activities that promote early childhood development/psychosocial well-being and coping skills. Through Community meetings and door to door visits, the most vulnerable children including children with disability and families will be identified for participation in the programs. The adolescents are able to protect themselves from exploitation, abuse, neglect, violence & discrimination and became resourceful in potential skills. The PSS team, caregivers and PSS volunteers will attend community meetings and make door to door visits, identify the most vulnerable children and families who need special care and support. Case Management Services refer to the service that supports individual children and their caregivers. Parents and communities address child protection issues by using their increased knowledge and skills. The lactating and expecting mothers will be mobilized to participate in the designed activities.
The project will integrate protection measures against COVID-19 in all its interventions.

1 - Identified Needs

Boys and girls represent 53% of all refugees and the majority of whom need child protection (CP) services. The emergence of new protection risks necessitates a coordinated and harmonized approach by all CP partners to ensure sustainability and effectiveness of child protection systems and services. Child labour (16%) and child missing (16%) remains a key concern (J-MSNA, 2020). Boys and girls, including adolescents, face a wide range of protection risks including violence, neglect, abuse, sexual violence, separation from caregivers, child marriage, child labour, trafficking and psychological distress. Children are also exposed to significant threats within their own families and communities and are therefore in need of critical lifesaving services and support. The gendered dimensions of child protection risks, with its related consequences, persist in the lives of adolescent girls and boys in the camps. Adolescent girls are particularly susceptible to child marriage whilst boys face heightened risk of child labor, exploitation and human trafficking. Therefore, there is a need for targeted gender-sensitive CP interventions for adolescent girls and boy that take into account the specific issues they face due to intersecting identities and vulnerabilities. There is very limited space for play and recreation in the camps. Furthermore, adolescent girls’ movements are restricted due to many factors and this results in them being confined to their sheds, further limiting their access to lifesaving care, support, services, and resources. It also denies them the opportunity to develop support networks that aid in their healing and recovery. Therefore, child-friendly spaces remain an important entry point for children and adolescents to access psychosocial support, child development, life skills and pre-vocational sessions to stimulate their learning, growth and emotional, physical and mental well-being in a protective environment. A number of children were separated from their primary caregivers and there is an urgent need for robust, family-based alternative care arrangements for unaccompanied and separated children, family tracing and reunification as well as support to foster care families. This reinforces the need for mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) for children and their caregivers and for support to alternative care arrangements and family tracing and reunifications.The insufficient open spaces and shade for essential recreation and community-based cohesion activities, to address the psychosocial needs of children, hampered their natural coping mechanisms and can worsen protection risks.Community-based protection initiatives can capacitating and empowering refugees in protection knowledge as well as CP and skills, including psychosocial support, so that they can support children in need. Engagement of all community members in protection, including men, boys and religious leaders, is needed to ensure a supportive and protective environment for all wherever the community is in the future, considering the many uncertainties surrounding them. In COVID 19 pandemic the protection risk has been increased but pre-existing gendered risks are exacerbated. GBV is increasing, including IPV, polygamy, transphobic violence, and violence against female sex workers, while access to support is more restricted. There is an increase in forced/child marriage, a halt in education and livelihood services, reduced access to women and girls’ spaces, and a lower presence of camp authorities. (IN THE SHADOWS OF THE PANDEMIC: THE GENDERED IMPACT OF COVID-19 ON ROHINGYA AND HOST COMMUNITIES, October 2020)

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Operation of 11 MCACs to provide safe, secure, stimulating, participatory and supportive environments for children and adolescents. Activity 2: Providing of aid and supportive devices to the children with special needs;Activity 3: Specific sessions on life skills activities, positive masculinities for boys and healthy relationships as well as positive coping for both adolescent boys and girls will be conducted at the community centre as well as the MCACs to ensure wider coverage and sustainability. Activity 4: The MCAC team consists of PSS workers, case workers and facilitators. They are trained on global guidelines and minimum standards related to CP, GBV, CCSS, Male Survivors, MHPSS, PFA and case management (except facilitators). Activity 5: Providing of vocational training to the adolescents. Activity 6: HH visit, Session observation to identify the children in need of focused care and increase awareness of parents and children to use masks, hand gloves and teaching them proper way of washing hands to protect themselves from COVID-19.Activity 7: Providing of structured psychosocial support to children in need through case management using CPIMS+. Activity 8: Capacity building of community-based child protection committee members to address protection issues themselves. Special attention will be given that they themselves practice hgiene against COVID-19 and ensure the community do the same. Capacitate community mobilization animators and run awareness sessions by them. Activity 9: Facilitation of awareness sessions with the parents, religious leaders and elders. Appropriate IEC materials, videos, pictures will be used to ensure they understand the threat COVID-19 poses and thus preactice proper hygiene with their family and community. Activity 10: Conduct sessions on child care with lactating and expecting motherActivity 11: DRR intervention: In addition to prioritising a participatory method to DRR awareness raising, CB will conduct quarterly DRR trainings for its staff as well as community members to increase their understanding of preparedness activities, contingency planning exercises and measures to mitigate family separation and other risks to children in case of an emergency/natural disaster. These sessions will be conducted in collaboration with CB’s Shelter and WASH teams to ensure CFSs remain a safe and secure place for all.Activity 12: Feedback and complaint mechanism: CB has a feedback mechanism with different entry points for feedback collection- electronic devices, Hotline, and complaint boxes. These feedbacks are processed, and regularly analysed by the Safeguarding and M&E team and shared with higher authority to action & program team to re-design the programming.Activity 13: CB has a national policy for PSEA and Child Safeguarding. Complaints can be collected through the several entry points and are directly processed by the PSEA Focal Point. In 2021, CB will continue conduct regular trainings for its senior management, sector managers and field staff on PSEA reporting and response. This is besides the Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding trainings that will also be organised for all staff. Activity 14; Inter-agency and inter-sectoral coordination: CB participates in various inter-agency and inter-sectoral coordination for a and mechanisms. In Camp 4 Extension, CB is both the Protection Focal Point and Child Protection Focal Point. At field-level, CB attends Child Protection coordination meetings and engage with the CPFP. It will follow Referral pathways established by the Sub Sector and liaise with relevant service providers of other sectors. At CXB, CB will continue participation in the Child Protection Sub-Sector. CB will continue reporting in the 5W and contributing to the SitReps.Activity 15: COVID 19 integration: In all activity the principle of social distance will be ensured. COVID prevention activities will be integrated in all activity planning and implementation.
