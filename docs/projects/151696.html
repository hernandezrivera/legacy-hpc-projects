Project Summary

This project aims at addressing the needs of affected population in Donetska and Luhanska Oblasts of Eastern Ukraine in terms of water, sanitation and hygiene. Following interventions articulated further below in the document strictly apply to the goals defined in WASH monitoring framework 2019-2020, i.e.:
- Ensuring immediate and sustainable access to sufficient safe water, and minimal levels of sanitation provision, for conflict affected people;
- Provision of critical WASH-related supplies and information for the prevention of water- and sanitation-related diseases.

Needs

Reports, including Humanitarian Needs Overview, indicate that approximately 3.2 M people need assistance in terms of WASH in the conflict affected areas. Conditioned by the conflict critical water infrastructure located close to the LoC is put at risk of being affected by the armed activities. Poor condition of WASH infrastructure results in applying negative coping strategies among people. At the same time the level of unemployment rises and purchasing power drops and as a result, people face an increased food and water insecurity, lack of access to healthcare and other impediments forcing them to leave places of their origins.Waste disposal and sewage systemsAccording to the data of WASH cluster, towns and villages near the frontline report problems with garbage as reduced income for village councils means they can no longer afford to pay for trucks to remove it. Very similar situation applies to sewage where in more rural, frontline areas, people also need to empty household level septic tanks which is more difficult as pump trucks refuse to visit frontline areas, or charge a premium for the risk incurred. WASH implementing agencies reported difficulties for people to empty septic tanks near the LoC, with people instead using 200 liter drums and other means to empty septic tanks manually. Old sewage networks and wastewater treatment facilities pose a significant risk of water contamination. According to the data shared by WASH cluster, up 1500 schools, kindergartens and vocational colleges in two conflict affected oblasts need the improvement in their sanitary conditions. In relation to the ongoing conflict, the socio-economic situation has also significantly deteriorated in the region. Communities and institutions located in the region are excessively underfunded from the government’s side.Water pipe systemsWater infrastructure is constantly exposed to the warring activities in the vicinity. In particular, construction of military fortifications and movement of heavy hardware in the vicinity often endangers underground utility systems and renders it inoperative. Internal needs assessment findings attested that the noted cases are common for the 20km zone towards LoC where military presence is unprecedentedly high.Total dilapidation of pipeline infrastructure also incurs negative repercussions. Being established in 1950s-1970s water pipelines and its facilities undergo persistent dilapidation given the critical underfunding and lack of capacity from government of Ukraine. 83% of respondents (46) attested that there is no possibility to allocate state funds to execute rehabilitation activities that clearly depicts the above mentioned notion. The surface of pipelines, mostly made out of cast-iron, gets clogged due to water sediments, significantly reduces flow passage and at some sections produces excessive pressures. That leads, in turn, to massive leakages. As a result, settlements and its surroundings may suffer from swamping of farm fields or, on the contrary, face drying-out. That makes people to resort to the negative coping strategies and use precarious water sources and inappropriate facilities which do not comply with due quality standards, hence, exposing people’s lives to adverse repercussions (e.g. waterborne diseases). High density of military presence also endangers affected population to even more threat, including instances of gender-based, sexual violence and looting. It is girls and women who are predominantly exposed to such types of violence. According to the latest research (Institute for Demography and Social Studies, NAS of Ukraine) 19.4% of women aged 19-49 suffered from physical violence in Ukraine, while another 7.9% - from sexual violence; moreover, almost 70% of victims do not apply for any kind of assistance owing to general unease about this issue, as well as the stigma and trauma associated with it. The noted tendency is a common pattern for the conflict affected area.
