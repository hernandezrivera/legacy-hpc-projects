Project Summary

This education proposal will be implemented in Mayom, Magwi and Abiemnhom counties of Unity and Eastern Equatorial States respectively. The overall project objectives will primarily focus on increasing basic access to safe education services for conflict affected children in the three counties and to enhance the capacity of education actors to provide better learning outcomes to children and youths in their communities. The project will target an estimated number of 63,670 IDPs, returnees and the host community beneficiaries (adults, children and youths under age of 3-18 years, boys & girls, men & women). The key project activities will include establishing and rehabilitating safe temporary learning spaces, providing key life-saving and life-sustaining activities/ psycho-social support services, EiE learning and teaching materials as well as ECD kits, conducting school hygiene and sanitation education as well as hand-washing stances, providing school meals in Magwi county alone (CHIDDO is currently providing school meals in Mayom county while Samaritan Purse is providing school meals in Abiemnhom), teachers’ incentives in some selected schools, rehabilitation and establishment of segregated pit latrines and strengthening the capacity of county education officials, teachers and SMC/PTAs members in the targeted counties. The project estimated cost will amount to USD 369,000.

1 - Needs

The HRP 2020 estimated that there is 3 million people in need for education services with a total number of 900,000 children (53 % girls and 47 % boys) to be targeted in the country. Furthermore, the HNO 2019 stated that every third school in South Sudan has been damaged, destroyed, occupied or closed since 2013 and more than 70 per cent of children who should be attending classes are not receiving adequate and quality education opportunities such sufficient classrooms, minimum standard and segregated pit latrines, hygiene promotion activities and lack of incentives for teachers. Some 2,784,276 girls and boys between 3 and 18 years of age in conflict and crisis affected areas are estimated to not have access to per-school, learning and teaching materials, primary and secondary education. In addition, some 42,902 teaching personnel and members of school management committees are in need of capacity building and humanitarian assistance. It is also worthy to mention that apart from the man-made conflict and humanitarian dynamics, the current natural floods disaster especially in Greater Upper Nile and Bahar-El gazel regions had also escalated the already weak and precarious education situations in the country. The Sep-Oct 2019 abnormal heavy rains and subsequent seasonal flooding have been devastating large areas of greater Upper Nile region with an estimated 908,000 people affected (OCHA 2019 Press release). This includes IDPs, refugees and host communities. In Mayom county 62 schools with a population of 22,226 pupils (9,363 girls and12,863 boys in 8 Payams have been completely submerged with uncounted classrooms being affected by the floods making parents to face serious challenges to re-enroll their children again in in those collapsed schools. Education facilities are filled with water or used to shelter people who have fled the flooding. Reduced access to basic education services caused by floods have increased children’s vulnerability to dropping out of schools. In Nyoat Primary school of Mayom county, the deep water streams blocked children from accessing their classrooms and preferred to stay at homes. Diseases are spreading with contaminated water causing a death of 9 infant children in Bieh, Ruadh and Kueryiek Payams. According to September 2019 IRNA reports conducted by Care, RCDI, SP, SPEDP and county officials in Abiemnhom county, there were 4 primary schools with 12 classrooms and number of 965 children (375 girls and 590 boys) that have been completely affected and submerged by water. Most of the segregated pit latrines in those schools have already collapsed. Access to hygiene and sanitation is limited, especially for boys and girls in the schools. Magwi county is one of the counties in Eastern Equatoria that that received a huge number of 3,555 IDPs and 20,689 individual returnees between Jan-Mar 2019 from internal displaced locations in the Greater Equatoria and Uganda as a consequences of the revitalized peace agreement in Sep 2018 according to IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round 5 conducted in March 2019. These returnees settled in Magwi county and its surrounding Payams and Bomas such as Kilio, Amika & Lobure. Out of many needy schools in the county, CHIDDO staff managed to visit seven congested primary schools with about 3,237 children in Bomas of Amika, Akilio and Lobure that have been facing high and serious shortage of TLS, learning and teaching resources, safe and segregated pit latrines due to the returnees and IDPs children that settled in those areas. The issue of capacity building for both teachers and SMC/PTAs was also reported by the county education and head teachers met by CHIDDO staff last Sep 2019. The school authorities also raised concerns about recurring dropping out of children in schools due to lack of school feeding Programme at schools, which reduced children concentration level during classrooms lessons making some of them to leave schools.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, this education intervention will legally recognize the rights of all beneficiaries as enshrined in South Sudan National Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management (MHADM) Framework for Return, Reintegration and Relocation of Displaced Persons Framework (February 2017). The Framework asserts to provide special consideration to conflict-affected persons -including children, women, widows, persons with disabilities, etc. in the provision of public service, including access to health and education services and granting the host communities the same protection and humanitarian services and to secure access to civilian populations in need of emergency humanitarian assistance and protection, to create a safe and secure environment by establishing the rule of law to achieve durable solutions, including return, reintegration and relocation. CHIDDO will ensure that all the conflict affected IDPs, returnees, host community children and other vulnerable beneficiaries access the needed child protection services in proportion to their needs without any barriers or discrimination.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, this education project is linked to the national education Act 2012, state and county structures and strategies in regard to education long term goals and short term objectives of the National Ministry of General Education and Instruction as stated in its strategic plan (2017-2022). CHIDDO has signed an MoU with the national Ministry of Education to carry out its main humanitarian and developmental activities in line with the Ministry of Education strategies, goals and objectives along-side the state governments and county departments structures since 2016. This project is strongly linked with the Ministry of Education Strategic Plan (2017-2022) that aims to achieve four important national priorities: to increase equitable access to general education; to improve the quality of general education; to enhance the management capacity of senior staff of the Ministry, State Ministries, the County Education Department and affiliated institutions. CHIDDO' strong presence in the two counties (Magwi and Mayom) established good relations in Abiemnhom and engagement with the local county and state education structures.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes,The education in emergency project locations (Mayom, Magwi and Abiemnhom counties) are environmentally safe and stable. The emergency education intervention will be implemented in a secure, friendly and neutral environmental areas (unless there will be unforeseen future risks) that will not pose harm to children and other beneficiaries since most of the activities have no too much hardware activities apart from TLS and pit latrines construction and rehabilitation, which be done by experienced engineers that could be a source of danger to children or the surrounding environment.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, CHIDDO also will focus on capacity building training and mentorship for all education stakeholders (women and men, girls and boys) at county and schools levels with the aim of increasing their capacity and help them to develop a clear project process monitoring and detailed project sustainability plan/exit strategy schedule. The project will consult the views of the stakeholders on education program in all the targeted counties, Payams and Bomas levels to ensure smooth exit strategy that will lead to sustainability and ownership. CHIDDO will first hold community based participatory planning and consultative meetings (from the project outset to evaluation level) with the key beneficiaries and education stakeholders including (SMC/PTAs members, State Ministry of Education Officials, County Education Department Officials, Payam Education Supervisors, teachers and Head teachers, community leaders, etc.…) who will be involved actively to ensure collective participation and decision making are unanimous to define the project exit strategy (goal, objectives, outcomes and activities) in preparation for exit strategy and brainstorm their minds to think and suggest ways that will make education in emergency project sustainable and community ownership after the funding stops.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The most vulnerable people with disabilities and other groups will be provided with the necessary information like any other person at the start of the project to make sure that they are aware to claim their rights and report their complaints to the right channels given to them and will be free to express their satisfaction or dissatisfaction with the project deliverables. To access their feedback, CHIDDO will ask the illiterate beneficiaries to look for their trusted and close relatives (translators, literate and sign language experts) who will in turn sign a written commitment in front of PMC members or SCM/PTAs members and other local community leaders to make sure that they do not mismanage or misuse the allocated services given to their illiterate persons. Vulnerable young girls, women and boys will all be prioritized in every activity or services given using vulnerability selection criteria for balanced inclusion purposes.
