Project Summary

IMC will continue its current programming implemented within its community center (RAC – Recreational Activity Center) in Jaramana and 2 mobile teams (currently deployed in Babella, Yalda and one collective shelter in Damascus). As IMC proposes to open a Community center in Babella, RAC will continue providing PSS structured and non-structured activities including non-formal education and skill development (literacy, English and Arabic courses), social and recreational activities (handcraft, music, sport, theatre, art), targeting children, adolescents and their parents divided in groups according to their age and interests. A rehabilitation and inclusion program will also continue to be implemented targeting children with disabilities and their parents. Other positive parenting skills programs such as Early Child Development (ECD) programs, Caregivers Skills Training (CST) will continue to be offered at the RAC and at IMC PHC clinic in Jaramana, where also case management services are offered. Information and awareness activities as well as training on different child protection topics will continue to be conducted to support the beneficiaries and their communities to establish positive coping mechanisms, reduce protection related risks and enhancing their resilience. Within the mobile teams, IMC will continue provide case management services including referrals and PSS structured and non-structured activities, similar to those mentioned above for RAC (although no parenting skills programs will be implemented) as well as awareness and information activities. At the proposed community center, IMC plans to provide similar package of services as those currently offered at RAC, in addition to CP case management services. The center will constitute a safe space where boys and girls (in addition to mothers who will be engaged in certain activities with their children) can meet for social and recreational activities and obtain integrated protection services ranging from education and livelihood support to PSS and Child Protection interventions, in accordance with their needs. The services provided in the center will aim at empowering children and their families to cope with the consequences of the crisis and strengthen their resilience while at the same time strengthening the capacities of the community to prevent and respond to child protection concerns. When dealing with CP cases, the case managers will focus on the needs of the child and his/her family, ensuring that concerns are addressed systematically and in consideration of the best interests of the child, building upon the child and his/her family’s natural resilience and ensuring the child’s meaningful participation and family empowerment throughout the process, with the overall goal of ensuring safety for the child.

4 - Needs

The HNO 2021 has confirmed the complex and inter-linked protection needs prevailing across Syria, with an estimated 13.1 million people in need of protection assistance. Out of those, 45% or 6 million are children; 2.45 million are estimated to be out of school and 27% of the surveyed households reported that their children show signs of psychological distress. Violence, multiple displacements, family separation, coupled with the deterioration of the socio-economic situation and the impact of COVID 19 on livelihoods and access to basic services and assistance have further contributed to the depletion of coping mechanisms of individuals and communities and led to increased adoption of harmful coping mechanisms. Child labor and schools dropouts are reported to have increased. While the lack and/or loss of civil documentation impacts all population groups, it adds specific vulnerabilities for women (especially widowed and divorced) and children, preventing access basic services and rights. HNO 2021 reported that 50% of the assessed communities are now relying on illegal activities, and 32% on humanitarian assistance to meet their most basic needs. Begging is prevalent in 24% of assessed communities. Early marriage was reported in 62% of the assessed communities and child labor prevents school attendance in 67% of the cases. The above findings are in line with observations from IMC team, with major concerns related to different forms of child abuse, lack of documentation, early marriage and psycho-social stress. It has been observed that many parents seek case management services for children with behavioral problems, symptoms of depression and anxiety, and those who have attempted to harm themselves.

5 - Implementation of activities

1) CP case management at IMC PHC clinics, mobile teams (ongoing) and Community Center (planned). Services will include PSS, internal referrals for services provided by IMC and external referrals to other services (legal, health, livelihood, safe shelter). IMC case managers will ensure provision of services in the respect of the principles of safety, confidentiality and self-determination. 2) PSS activities at the RAC, through mobile teams (ongoing) and Community Center (planned): These will include non-formal education and skill development, social and recreational activities and Youth Empowerment Program (YEP). 3) At RAC (ongoing) and Community Center (planned), rehabilitation and inclusion program for children with intellectual disabilities and developmental delays and their caregivers. Children will then join social and recreational activities alongside their peers with no disabilities (inclusion program). 4) At RAC and IMC PHC (ongoing) and Community Center (planned): Parenting skills programs: a) Early Childhood Development; b) positive parenting skills program for caregivers c) Caregiver skills training. 5) Peer to-peer support sessions 6) At RAC, IMC PHC clinics and mobile teams (ongoing) and Community Center (planned): information and awareness raising activities for beneficiaries and community members: these will be designed based on the most prevalent child protection risks reported within each community and will utilize context adapted IEC materials. 7) Training to IMC staff as well as community members in different topics including PFA, , facilitating CP activities and Child Protection, as per needs assessed in each community of intervention.8) Vocational training for youth and caregivers.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: low quality of services and referrals expose boys and girls to further harm. Likelihood: medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: 1) conduct case management by trained staff only 2) Supportive technical supervision conducted regularly by qualified staff. 3) Service mapping including only providers implementing ensuring survivor centered approach, updated on regular basis. Risk 2: Low participation and stigmatization of beneficiaries, resistance from the target community. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: high. Mitigation measures: 1) Community and local leaders’ engagement in all phases of project cycle, including joint discussions on activities that are considered priority for the target beneficiaries. 2) Adaptation of messaging to the context and culture, and based on feedback and inputs from targeted communities and beneficiaries; 3) only female staff working in the safe space 4) Community based feedback and complaint mechanism in place. Risk3: Risk to the well-being of child protection service providers due to exposure to violence (e.g. secondary or vicarious trauma). Livelihood: high, impact: high. Mitigation risks: 1) IMC developed a staff care policy. 2) Provision of staff peer-support groups and team building activities when needed.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

RISKS: 1) Sexual abuse and exploitation. 2) Sexual activity with beneficiaries, including with children below 18 years of age. 3) Sexual harassment. 4) Sexual violence. 5) Physical/Emotional Abuse. 6) Child marriage (IMC staff engaging in marriage with a child below 18 years of age)Mitigation and prevention measures: The above-mentioned acts are prohibited and a violation of IMC Code of Conduct which all IMC employees must sign, and as such, subject to disciplinary actions including termination. Mandatory trainings and refreshers on PSEA and safeguarding issues for all IMC employees and volunteers. Awareness and information sessions to beneficiaries regarding PSEA and reporting mechanism. Availability of effective, safe and confidential reporting mechanisms. Internal mechanisms and procedures to investigate suspected violations.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK 1 – Delayed approval to establish the new center or for mobile teams to operate. MITIGATION 1 Regular follow-up on approval process, submission of approval letters ahead of time; advocacy through relevant stakeholders. RISK 2: Resistance and non-acceptance of IMC activities from the community. MITIGATION 2: Community engagement throughout all phases of implementation; involvement of beneficiaries on IEC materials and eventual adaptation. RISK 3: Sanctions affecting/delaying bank transfers to Syria. MITIGATION 3: Maintain bank accounts with at least two banks; continue to advocate through INGO forum on waiving sanctions for humanitarian funds transfer; maintain information sharing with other INGOs to identify alternative solutions. RISK 4: COVID 19 causing activities to be suspended. MITIGATION 4: establishment of remote management plans for case management services to ensure uninterrupted support; adherence by staff and beneficiaries to preventive measures; maintaining communication channels with community members to inform on disruption of services.Assumptions: 1) Continued and uninterrupted access to the areas of intervention for implementation, monitoring and supervision. 2) Acceptance of IMC activities and services from the targeted population. 3) Adequate financial resources. 4) Security situation remain stable. Mitigation strategy; establish a remote supervision plan in case of temporary adoption of remote management systems. Informing and sensitize communities on IMC activities and services. Continuous monitoring of market prices and budget realignments submission to donors. Continuous monitoring of the security situation, liaising with UNDSS and other humanitarian partners for exchange of information.
