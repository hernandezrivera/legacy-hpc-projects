Project Summary

The project will be implemented in Middle Shabelle and Bakool regions where WARDA currently implementing integrated food security, WASH interventions. The organization will scale up its protection intervention and Improve equitable access to GBV prevention and response services for survivors of GBV from IDPs and vulnerable host households affected by crisis and emergency conditions and promote community understanding and capacities in GBV prevention and response. The organization will conduct community mobilization and sensitize the community on the available CMRS services and referral pathways. At the end of the project, A total of 16000 affected women, girls, boys and men at target districts will have access to integrated multisectoral GBV interventions.

Needs

According to FSNAU, 4.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance across Somalia. This including, 1.5 million in crisis and emergency conditions (IPC 3 and IPC 4,). According to UNHCR report 2018 , there are estimated 1.6 million drought displaced persons in Somalia of which, over 70 percent are in the south-central regions of the country and account for the highest poverty levels (71 percent) in the entire country. The report highlighted major protection concerns such as sexual assaults ,gender-based violence (SGBV) and violation of children’s rights. The reports also covered serious crisis consequences against children and minors. Key risks such as vulnerability, exploitation, neglect, trafficking, psycho-social distress, a lack of access to education, and hazardous child labor. The scope and severity of the situation on the target group is aggravated by the state fragility and prioritizing other interests over internally displaced people’s need and rights. . Most of the IDPs in Middle Shabelle and Bakool regions live in settlements with precarious humanitarian conditions and have limited access to protective facilities and basic services. Thus, facing common protection risk exposures such as gender-based violence and evictions. The above problems indicate the need to maintain and a scale up lifesaving protection services for forcibly displaced person such as stop centers, access to GBV prevention and response services, community participation and awareness raising initiatives to promote protective environment, overall health and well being. at the end of the project , WARDA will provide lifesaving GBV services to estimated 16000 affected people with equal access to women, girls, boys and men in Middle Shabelle and Bakool regions.

Activities

1. Organize community education and awareness sessions to estimated 2600 community members on available services, services utilization and referral pathway. 2. Conduct clinical management of rape training to 10 health staff.3. Scale up two GBV stop centers to provide Clinical Management of Rape services to 2,100 GBV survivors with equal access to male and female survivors. PEP kits will be received from UNFPA. 4. Organize six sensitization sessions and campaigns for 200 community members ((male and female) on GBV prevention, legal assistance and referral pathway.5. Procurement and distribution of dignity kits to 400 vulnerable women and girls.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Majority of the IDPs at target districts live in settlements with precarious humanitarian conditions and have limited access to protective facilities and basic services. Thus, facing common protection risk exposures such as gender-based violence and evictions. Our stop centers will provide response and preventative activities and engage local stakeholders.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

According to Somalia Protection Cluster HRP 2018, 96 per cent of GBV survivors who accessed services in 2016 were from female; 76 percent form IDPs alone. The reports also covered serious crisis consequences against children and minors. Key risks such as vulnerability, exploitation, neglect, trafficking, psycho-social distress, a lack of access to education, and hazardous child labor.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Our project aims to follow UNHCR protection guidance closely and collaborate with agencies at the outset of implementation and put forward an effective plan while ensuring centrality of protection in the humanitarian response through providing timely and efficient referrals to GBV survivors and those at risk, expanding the existing GBV and child protection service provision, employ awareness raising campaigns and increase community participation in action and preventive.
