Project Summary

Libya’s urban centers are in serious need of clearance of explosive remnants of war (ERW), so that critical infrastructure including schools, hospitals, electricity, and water, as well as residential areas, can begin reconstruction and enable safe return for displaced people. Benghazi, Sirte and Tripoli are key cities in the ongoing conflict and have all seen battles between warring parties, which has led to high urban contamination and displacement. Both children and adults require explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) to teach them the dangers of Unexploded Ordnance (UXO) and how to adopt safe behavior in the face of contamination.
DanChurchAid (DCA) has worked in Libya since 2011 and have presence in both Benghazi, Sirte and Tripoli. From here national and international staff deliver a wide range of projects both through direct implementation and through our civil society organization partners.
For this project DCA will train five teams of deminer/searchers and mentor them in clearance of UXO in priority areas of the three cities, so the citizens can access essential services safely. DCA will train three EORE teams to deliver risk education to children and youth in schools, colleges, and universities, and to adult workers, and female headed households. Furthermore, DCA will utilize its expertise to support and capacity build national stakeholders.
Integrated Mine Action is a crosscutting enabler, securing humanitarian protection needs and facilitating early recovery for development. The project aims to strengthen the protection of individuals and communities from the threat of ERW, through capacity building, risk education and clearance and by this allowing for the socio-economic rebuilding of Libya. The project will be delivered by DCA’s national and international staff in Libya and have a duration of 12 months

1 - Needs

Frequent accidents from UXO and ERW left behind after years of fighting burden Benghazi, Sirte and Tripoli. These items, causing injury and fatality, pose a barrier to accessing education, medical services, and essential utilities such as water and electricity. DCA’s proposed project targetsthese needs by 1) clearing explosive ordnance which enables access to vital services and rebuilding of essential utilities. This will not only address citizens protection needs, but also be a steppingstone for socio-economic development. 2) by conducting EORE which increase individuals and community’s safe behavior and reduce injuries and fatalities. DCA will target a wide group of people who are affected by UXO; children, youth, utility workers, female headed households and returnees. Our local and mixed gender EORE teams will be able to access these groups directly, while media campaigns will target society more broadly. On top of clearance and risk education DCA will further capacity building of national stakeholders, to ensure that needs will be addressed sustainably and with larger buy-in from local actors.

2 - Activities

Under DCA’s project national and international staff in Libya will conduct two main activities; Clearance and Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD), including Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and EORE. Five EOD teams of deminers/searchers will be selected and trained to international mine action standards and mentored by international technical staff to clear priority sites in the cities. The teams will be quality assured and accredited by the Libyan Mine Action Centre (LibMAC). Local authorities and communities will be included, and their needs assessed to priority tasks. To that end, teams will conduct EOD spot tasks in response to items found by returnees and battle area clearance in more substantial sites such as hospitals and schools, so that they may reopen. All reporting will be conducted to LibMAC and into IMSMA. To increase safety and reduce injuries and fatalities three EORE team will be deployed. Team members will receive refresher training and reaccreditation were needed. Training will be delivered by DCA’s international risk education coordinator. Teams will design and print fresh RE materialsto distribute during sessions, including posters, leaflets and comic books. A risk education message campaign will be designed and delivered via SMS, Radio and Facebook across Benghazi, Sirte and Tripoli. As this project is already partially funded, some of the staff capacity mentioned above will already be in place.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

DCA teams will work with local authorities to ensure that suspect hazardous areas are clearly marked and will deliver EORE messages, in particular targeting returnees and IDP sites, to educate them to the risks of ERW/UXO. This will mitigate the risk of exposure to explosive hazards, as well as make the population aware of referral services for mine action. Staff uphold close relations with involved communities to ensure high levels of acceptance. All national staff are protected by Libyan labour laws, including healthcare and life insurance. All staff have received basic life support trainings in the event of accidents during and outside working hours. Movement of national staff only takes place after acceptance of local stakeholders. Staff are all vetted through international and national security systems.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

DCA aims to respect the humanitarian standards and principles such as Do No Harm, children rights, and the prevention of sexual exploitation and harassment. All staff must sign and familiarize themselves with DCA’s Code of Conduct. Central to the accountability efforts is the Complaints Handling System (CHS) to ensure accountability throughout all of DCA’s programs and activities. The CHS serves our partners and beneficiaries and improve the quality of our work. An effective CHS helps us respect the dignity of those who raise a complaint and improve our accountability to partners, rights-holders and other stakeholders. The CHS is set to alert us to issues of abuse of power, misuse of funds, human rights abuses and possible adverse effects on human rights in our work. Moreover, it can assist us to rectify and remedy mistakes, manage risks, improve security and generally improve the quality of our work. Complaints can be submitted by a range of actors; beneficiaries and other people affected by the project, partner organizations, local institutions, agencies, and donors as well as DCA’s own staff. This mechanism ensures a clear and accountable process for handling of complaints.
