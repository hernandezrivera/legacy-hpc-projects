Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect the livelihoods, improve the resilience,
productive capacity and enhance the food security of vulnerable farmers and herders who
are living in Area C in north of West Bank and in Jordan valley.
The objective is drawn from the FSS country Strategic Objective: SO1 The resilience of
vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive
capacity restored/enhanced.
Food security in Palestine continues to be affected by protracted nature of the crisis,
repeated shocks and continued restriction of freedom of movement, mainly for the most
vulnerable families under the national social safety net (SSN), in term and type of
frequency assistance.
The proposed project goes in line with the first impact/ result “Agricultural assets and
productive capacities of farms and damaged productive assets (wells, irrigation systems,
cisterns, greenhouses) restored, and the clusters plans of the ministry of agriculture in
Area C in north of West Bank and in Jordan valley especially in the Jenin, Nablus, Tubas
and Jericho governorates. Where more than 11,100 living in 49 communities will be
directly supported through the project’ activities.
The main activities will focus on rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and
inputs, for farmers and herders including promotion of technical capacities and small
scale production units, and using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased
resilience.
To strengthen its accountability toward beneficiaries and partners, CARE applies and has
an easily accessible, community-based Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM),
which also attends to more sensitive, SEA-related complaints. CARE has a global policy on
Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Child Protection explicitly
outlining unacceptable behavior, and investigation processes, and follow up, as well as
the Safeguarding Code of Conduct. All project’s team will receive an orientation on the
PSEA related issues.
The proposed activities will focus to improve the access to the essential resources (water,
lands,… etc) through well planned rehabilitation and provide inputs to increase the
production capacities of the targeted farmers and herders in the selected areas. As part of
CARE strategy; and given the nature of the protracted crisis.
CARE work closely with and through AIDA toward coordinated advocacy, for accountability for IHL violations in Area C. The project targeted communities are located in most vulnerable areas in Area C in north of West Bank and in Jordan valley who most of them were under threat of displacement, ISF. or /and settler’s violence and/or land confiscation and demolitions of assets, high increase in agriculture inputs prices due to Ukraine crisis and who are affected by the continuing restrictions specifically after September 2022 due to closure of Nablus and Jenin.

1 - Needs

Palestine’s context became more complicated as result of the deterioration in the political situations accompanied especially with the dramatic escalation in the West Bank mainly in Nablus and Jenin. The challenges originating from the protracted conflict, economic stagnation, high unemployment, gender inequalities and high rates of poverty and food insecurity continued to deteriorate. In the WB, the prolonged period of slow economic growth, restrictions on movement, trade, investment and access to land and water resources, together with higher food prices, continue to erode the fragile livelihoods of poor Palestinians. Data from MSNA survey conducted in 2022, points to a percentage of 33.6 per cent of the population (or about 1.8 million people, approximately 50% females, 44% children- 22% male and 22% female children, 52% 18-64 years- 26% female and 26% males and 3-4% elderly) being moderately or severely food insecure. The national average hides large differences, with a prevalence of 24 per cent of the households in the West Bank, and 75 per cent in the Gaza Strip. The design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of the targeted communities, based on a number of key sources, including the data and analysis exercise led by the FSS needs analysis for most vulnerable locations that are in need of livelihood/food security support in developing the HNO. CARE and partners were among the group who worked with the HNC to develop and update the matrix of the locations all over West Bank, MSNA results/findings. In the West Bank, the demolition and confiscation of agricultural structures and restrictions on the maintenance and productive assets, movement restrictions and settlement expansion, all contribute to livelihood instability and deterioration of the socioeconomic environment. As of September 2022, a total of 220 structures were demolished, which represent more than 34% of the total structures demolished (OCHA). Palestinian Bedouin and herding communities are disproportionately affected due to their location in sensitive areas and their reliance of agriculture-based livelihoods, as their sole or main source of income. farmers and herders face Israeli settlers' violent practices. These attacks resulted in Palestinian injuries and casualties, or damages to Palestinian property, including vandalizing trees, crops, and other agricultural properties and assets. The ongoing Ukrainian crisis is placing significant stress on all dimensions of the food system, threatening food security and livelihood of thousand people in the West Bank. CARE/PARC have conducted needs assessment that included several meetings and discussions with targeted community members including PWD, male and female herders/farmers from different age groups were engaged like adolescents, youth and elderly where their inputs guided the design of the intervention. MoA Head quarter and relevant offices in the respected governorates were consulted and guided the targeting activities/locations. The assessments conducted in late September took into considerations all new needs/implications. According to all assessments including the FSS/HNO and CARE’s quick assessment for the targeted communities, the small breeders don’t have access to natural grazing areas or services, suffer from high production cost and low market value, lack of vaccines, high inputs prices and lack of veterinary and extension services, high prices of fodder and inputs and poor farming practices, thus those small scale breeder became more aid dependent and the most affected groups noting that at least 80% of small ruminants breeders are small scale dominated by male farmers, most of them are Bedouins living mainly in Area C. According to the assessments; Small scale farmers especially women and PWD have access limitations to agricultural assets/inputs and productive capacities of farms and damaged/non maintained productive assets (land, wells, irrigation systems, cisterns, greenhouses, etc) as well access obstacles to access the markets. Based on the assessments/findings, CARE/PARC identified 49 vulnerable communities in Jenin (11), Tubas (20), Jericho (7) and Nablus (11) to be targeted. The proposed activities will focus to improve the access to the essential resources (water, lands, etc) and provide inputs to increase the production capacities of the targeted farmers and herders in the selected areas through tailored activities that will focus on rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for farmers and herders including promotion of technical capacities and using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased resilience. As part of CARE strategy, and given the nature of the protracted crisis, CARE work closely with and through AIDA toward coordinated advocacy, for accountability for IHL violations in Area C. The project targeted communities are located in most vulnerable areas in Area C in West Bank and in Jordan valley who most of them were under threat of displacement, annexation, confiscation, demolition of assets and/ or soldiers’/settler’s violence. Women and children in agriculture are particularly disadvantaged, despite being a significant unregistered portion of the workers in this sector, resulted in limited economic opportunities, restricted mobility, and limited technical preparation. As a result, food insecurity has also disproportionately impacted women/children and marginalized groups. ALL proposed activities were designed and developed in close coordination and cooperation with MOA in each district.

3 - Indicators

Indicators and targets: 1) 250 barns for small scale female and male herders will be rehabilitated (30 females and 220 male including 7 PWD) 2) 350 dunums of greenhouses rehabilitated for 350 small scale female and male farmers (50 females and 300 male including 11 PWD) 3) 15 km of water carrier lines / pipes installed and provided and will connect with at least six sources of water which will provide at least 310 famers (40 Female and 270 male including 10 PWD) with water to plant at least 1500 dunums of irrigated lands 4) 25 km of agricultural roads rehabilitated to improve access to 1000 farmers (920 male and 80 female including 30 PWD) who will have access to least 6250 dunums 5) 2500 cubic meters of 5 water metallic pools installed / or rehabilitated and supplied water for at least 125 small female and male farmers (20 Female and 105 Male including 4 PWD) and 18 soil pools (average 250 m3 each )will be rehabilitated serving 72 farmers (64 male and 8 female including 2 PWD). CARE oPt’s FAM, which will be implemented during the life of the project, ensures that such reports are identified and community members’ complaints addressed and that steps are taken toward redressing any power imbalances.Additionally, CARE has a “Tell Us” whistle-blower policy and has mechanisms in place to report abuse or harassment of any kind, including anonymously to a third party via the internet or phone call.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

In Kind delivery is the most suitable modality to address the identified needs in the targeted communities as this is the fastest and safest way to ensure access to inputs and resources (water, Land), collective purchasing and distribution of the supplies/inputs proved to be more efficient and effective modality especially in the targeted areas that face serious access issues. This was also reflected in the MSNA data as well as CARE’s assessment that confirmed no potential harm of SEA. Also, CARE will distribute the FAM contact cards through which beneficiaries, partners and project’s gender-balanced team members can bring forth any programmatic or SEA related complaints/feedback in a confidential manner, and CARE will also monitor distribution modality.All identified barriers including physical access for most of the targeted communities due to settlersand ISF violence or the financial/economic burden of lack of affordable inputs are addressed throughthe direct provision of in kind support.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

CARE is committed to assuring accountability to stakeholders in all aspects and phases of the project. At the inception stage, community members helped inform the design of project activities, the composition of the teams, type of the support, urgent issues/needs, etc. CARE took into consideration the different needs of the different groups residing in the targeted communities (sex and age). Monitoring has been carried out through the on sight monitoring of distributions and activities, and it always includes segregation of data by sex and age (SADD). Partners follow the same monitoring guidelines and reporting.CARE’s Feedback and Accountability Mechanism (FAM) builds on our previous experience with humanitarian accountability, and it is in-formed by standards and guidance to ensure quality and accountability to our partners and stakeholders, and to support existing organizational and technical standards, with which to bring about sustainable change to communities in need. It was designed and piloted in other projects within CARE allowing targeted population to reach out to CARE for any feedback/complaint about the project activities, criteria, inputs, behavior by any of the project’s staff (CARE and Partners) including SEA-related issues. CARE will continue to raise community awarenessregarding FAM, regularly reporting on feedback/complaints as part of CARE’s continuous learning agenda. CARE has started using the FAM cards in targeted communities and started to receive feedback/complaints via identified emails and phone numbers. Feedback/complaints are documented in a log sheet by the Focal Point / Accountability Officer responsible for monitoring the progress of each input until the feedback loop is closed. The data will be reported and used for management decisions and analyzing trends. CARE also is planning to conduct a community-based survey that will further assess accessibility of the mechanism, as well as preferred channels of communication.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

CARE’s ‘Do No Harm’ principles are embedded in all approaches and interventions by CARE, from the ideation process of a project, throughout its duration and after the project is finished. CARE has identified risks, their potential impact and mitigation strategies, and all activities proposed are designed based on numerous discussions with community members, partners and other stakeholders, as well as on needs analyses (i.e. rapid / gender needs assessment) that are sensitive to the complexities of the local context to ensure that people are not exposed to additional risks through our action. CARE is committed to protection and ensuring safety and security of participants, dignity and accessibility of services/project activities; CARE’s activities are inclusive, and designed with the community members’ feedback, and the selection criteria for activities are transparent; the data collection processes are sensitive to the context, power dynamics in communities, and also the management of sensitiveinformation is conducted with caution and care. CARE will also FAM that would include any SEA related issues.
