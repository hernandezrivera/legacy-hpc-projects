Project Summary

This project targeting 26000 beneﬁciaries which is very focused on addressing COVID-19, the disease already spread all over the world. The impact of this disease could be more devastating in dense and crowded settlements like recent huge Rohingya makeshifts the largest refugee camp in the world in Cox’s bazar in Bangladesh. As hand washing with soap and water and social distancing are the key suggested preventive measures to fight against COVID virus by the WHO, this project is designed mainly to implement the activities related to hygiene promotion to keep health safe from the virus. To achieve the goal of this project, the activities will provide both hand wash NFI (HK, Gloves, Mask, Soap etc) supplies and facilities like latrine, hand washing devices and water supply. The project will be implemented in camp 10 and 13 where already WVB is implementing WASH activities.

1 - Identified Needs

"54% of refugees in the camps are now accessing chlorinated water through piped distribution networks as partners have achieved 102 water networks while 68 are still under construction over the 189 planned as part of the Water Network Plan. The remainder continue to access water from tube-wells fitted with hand pumps. Water quality surveillance shows that 75% of water analysis done at household level were without fecal contamination and 58% with adequate chlorine residual. 88% of households reported having enough water to meet all domestic needs but local disparities exist especially in Teknaf area. Despite 99 percent of households reporting using pit latrines in the camps and acceptable quantitative standards reached (18 persons per latrine and 38 persons 5 per bathing cubicle), many qualitative challenges remain with 22% of sanitation infrastructures requiring O&M and 10% being built in flooding areas. Sanitation coverage remains challenging due to lack of space in the camps with the highest density (e.g: 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 3, 9, 10). 68% of households reported having a private space inside or attached to their shelter for bathing but those bathing places sometimes associated with laundry (56%) and dishwashing places (52% ) are informal and often unhygienic. One of the major concerns regarding sanitation is the quality of the fecal sludge treatment as facilities require upgrades and improvements. With 500 cubic meters of Solid Waste produce daily in the targeted area, SWM remains one of the main challenges as only 51 percent of the refugee households are disposing waste in designated area and 27 percent reporting the presence of garbage near their houses.Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement. Appropriated hygiene practices remains low despite the fact that progresses have been observed partly due to fear from Covid-19 and focus on hand-washing promotion. As a result, 98% of the refugees increased handwashing practices since the COVID-19 outbreak and 95% reporting having access to soap. Those improvements need to be confirmed on longer-term through both practice on knowledge as only 60% of the refugees can mention three critical time to wash their hands. In respect of Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), MHM kits were provided to women and girls on twice in a year,"
