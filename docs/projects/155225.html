Needs

As of November 2018, 919,000 refugees need lifesaving humanitarian assistance. Also, the crisis has significantly affected hundreds of Bangladeshi’s acting as host to refugees and at peripheries of burgeoning camps and settlements, many of whom were already facing a range of economic and social poverty as well as protection risks such as trafficking, gender-based violence, and exploitation and abuse. Cox’s Bazar is first and foremost experiencing a protection crisis in which cycles of violence, coercion and deprivation as both a root cause and an impact of protracted displacement is clear. Based on numerous protection assessments, needs analysis and vulnerability profiling including IOM’s own direct observations (protection by presence) and information from case management support, suggests an acute protection context in which the most vulnerable refugees and host community populations remain highly exposed to critical threats and risks. Amongst the most prevalent that shall be tackled through the General Protection IOM JRP submission. Data is still being gathered as IOM is launching a database for general protection case management to supplement existing GBV and Child Protection information collection. • Endemic Trafficking in Persons: IOM has identified through its MIMOSA platform over 100 Victims of Trafficking (VoT) since August 2017, many of them are women and girls with over 42% children. These are cases recorded with over 100 or so cases a month coming through direct case management activities as well as referred by the Counter Trafficking Committee (CTC) at Cox’s Bazar level that IOM supports. Risks include false labour opportunities, prostitution, engagement in drug smuggling activities, and disappearances/abductions. Cox’s Bazar District has long been an area in which trafficking in persons has been significant especially forced labour into domestic servitude, sex industry, garment industry and unpaid/low paid menial labour. The initial assessment of the Asia Dialogue Forum for Dialogue (AFDM) on trafficking of which IOM supported also collaborated many of the above findings in November 2018.• Lack of access to appropriate dispute resolution mechanisms to find redress for pervasive protection threats: IOM is working with Arts for Action to better understand localised dipute resolution practices. During a scoping study conducted by AfA in May 2018 it was found that across 8 camps that levels of inter-familial and communal: access to formal legal system and chaotic dispute resolution pathways; legal, mediation and conflict resolution mediation structures • Meaningful access to information and appropriate services: In a recent assessment by IOM Communication with Communities (CWC) partners over 70% of refugees report lack of access to appropriate information about services available in their camps and ways to access support with often confusing information provided through humanitarian actors, CiCs and Mahji’s. Access to food was reported as a critical barrier requiring protection support. Refugees consistently report to IOM protection teams high level of vulnerability around FCN registration, access to GFDs, loss of food tokens and theft of food, as well as control of distribution by Mahji’s and other power brokers in the camp. High mobility constraints as well as the difficult terrain in the camps has also meant refugees have been unable to access food on a regular basis. • Trends in increased intimidation, harassment and direct forms of physical violence: IOM has recorded consistent incidents of refugees in peripheral areas of the mega-site experiencing harassment and intimation by Mahji’s, between Mahji’s and refugees and host communities triggered by control to access of resources and services. This has included at least 50 separate confirmed and unverified reports (June- November 2018) of physical and verbal attacks by host community on refugee populations around payment of rent, access to markets and land.

Activities or outputs

IOM implements a multi- intervention model of protection that includes Gender Based Violence (GBV), Child Protection, Counter Trafficking and General Protection activities, including dedicated Protection Mainstreaming capacities and has been scaling up presence, referral, case management and ensuring community driven approaches. Under the 2019 Joint Response Plan (2019) IOM seeks to strengthen protection presence, general case management, community protection planning with communities utilizing risk reduction and protection approaches, supporting access to services and ensuring inclusion and participation for the most vulnerable. Objective 2: Promote a community- based approach to the response and provide protection services to persons at heightened risk (including girls, boys, men and women of all ages and diversity determinants);2.1 Protection monitoring at household and community level for documentation, verification and trends analysis of protection risks; 2.2 Community risk mapping and risk reduction to support emergency preparedness/response and overall mitigation strategies;2.3 Direct case management: house-to house identification, referral and follow up of extremely vulnerable individuals (EVI);2.4 Establishment and capacity building of community level protection groups and community centers;2.5 Awareness raising sessions, trainings and meetings on protection risks;2.6 Interactive research to develop baseline on knowledge, attitudes and practice to identify and strengthen dispute resolution mechanisms driven by participatory community driven approaches. Human Trafficking Assessment on the risk and trend of trafficking in Rohingya and host community in Cox's Bazar;2.8 Develop and promote updated referral pathway for Victims of Trafficking (VoT)2.9 Direct assistance for Victims of Trafficking (VoT) and other extremely vulnerable individuals (EVI) 2.10 Maintenance of Shelter for Victims of Trafficking (VoT) with integrated support;2.11 Training and capacity strengthening of IOM, implementing partners on community based protection and human trafficking risks including legal aid service providers;2.12 Support the Counter Trafficking Committee meeting in District, Sub-district and Union level. 2.13 Support National, Local Governments and Law Enforcement Agencies to eradicate human trafficking.Objective 3: Support the protective system strengthening, by promoting peaceful coexistence and enhancing social cohesion within and between refugee and host communities;3.1 Joint community awareness raising and establishment of protection advocates across Rohingya and host communities to work jointly on mitigation of pervasive inter communal and pervasive protection risks; 3.2 Support Recreation, cultural and sporting activities to promote community understanding and dialogue. Objective 6: Promote an integrated multi-sectorial approach to protection.6.1 Mainstreaming of protection through training of staff of all IOM Units and its implementing partners including comprehensive monitoring and feedback for program adaptation;6.2 Establish integrated services of GP, CT, CP and GBV. IOM Protection Unit is an active member of Protection Working Group and participates regularly in the Protection Working Group meetings. It was part of EVI Referral Pathway Task Force until it was dissolved. It was also a part of Protection Advocacy Task Force and contributed to development of advocacy papers on various protection issues. Currently it is a part of Core Protection Task Team which was established specifically to monitor the repatriation situation and address in time the risks related to repatriation.
