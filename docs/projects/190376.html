Project Summary

In Ethiopia the main driving forces of causalities and human suffering are mainly related with conflict & drought. The ongoing conflict in the norther part of Ethiopia left millions of people displaced & heightened the humanitarian need in conflict prone areas of Amhara region. Likewise, the three consecutive failed rainy seasons have brought on severe drought in southeast & southern lowland regions of the country. The drought caused the drying up of water wells. killing livestock and crops and pushing hundreds of thousands of children and their families to the brink. “Children and their families are struggling to survive due to loss of livelihoods and livestock and it is projected that more than 6.8 million people will be in need of urgent humanitarian assistance by mid-March 2022. We are also witnessing major displacement in affected areas. Thus, the integrated multi-sectoral intervention seeks to provide life-saving and immediate assistance to IDPs & host communities in Somali, SNNPR and Amhara regions. The intervention mainly focuses on emergency WaSH, essential ESNFI, education and protection. To support the ongoing humanitarian supports & address the basic needs of affected population identified most prioritized sectors and activities in the area of improving access to clean water & sanitation services; protection activities focusing on child protection and GBV; and educational assistance through establishment of TLS, rehabilitation of partially damaged schools, provision of teaching and learning materials, ALP (accelerated learning program). At most attempt will be made to avoid duplication of resources through coordination with other actors in the same location & with relevant clusters at national and sub-national level. The project endeavors to ensure most underserved communities are addressed with live saving basic services.

1. - Projects activities

WaSH Activity: Emergency water trucking, roof water harvesting, distribution of water purification chemicals, rehabilitation & repair of water schemes, undertaking hygiene and sanitation promotion; building the capacity of WASHCo on rehabilitated water schemes will be strengthened to ensure sustainable use of rehabilitated water sources; distribution of WaSH NFIs to most vulnerable households;ESNFI Activity; Procurement and delivery of emergency shelter items, Provide training for beneficiaries in constructing emergency shelters with the items in the kit, Procure & distribute NFI kits for most vulnerable IDP households, and Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM). PDM will be conducted two weeks after the emergency shelter and non-food items kit distribution. Protection Activities; Provide comprehensive case management services through social workforce to survivors of various forms of violence & abuse, Community awareness creation & sensitization on child protection ( including COVID 19 mitigation), community based MHPSS support for children with MHPSS needs including children with disabilities, Alternative care and reunification for unaccompanied & separated children (UASC), Socio emotional learning to children and adolescent boys and girls, Provision of Integrated services for Survivors of GBV through direct and referral mechanism with other service providers( which the service include material support, transportation cost coverage, Legal support, and other related services); Establishing safe, accessible, and child-friendly reporting mechanisms for survivors of GBV & PSEA, Raise awareness among community members on sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and CBCMs including how to report SEA-related complaints. Risk mitigation, prevention and response interventions to address gender-based violence (GBV) which includes distribution of dignity kits, Conduct awareness raising campaigns to increase knowledge and understanding of GBV prevention and response programming and to promote positive behavior change that can prevent GBV, Ensure GBV survivors have access to high-quality, life-saving multisectoral services such as health care, including post-rape treatment, MHPSS and legal aid (referral pathways) & establish women and girls safe spaces that offer survivors and other vulnerable women and girls the opportunity to access information and participate in community-based activities that reduce their isolation. Education activity; Establishment of Temporary learning spaces(TLS), rehabilitation of partially damaged schools, provision of scholastic materials to emergency affected out of school pre-primary and primary students both in displaced & host community, teaching material support to schools damaged by crisis, accelerated school readiness and learning program to out of school pre-primary and primary school children. WaSH & protection activities will be integrated into schools to improve access to safe learning environment for crisis affected children.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The identified interventions are based on the need on the ground. Community consultation sessions were conducted & target groups & local government entities were consulted (FGM & KII). The community will be active partaker of the implementation of the integrated program. The government structure across the layer will play key role in selection of beneficiaries & in the process of service delivery. Beneficiaries, the community & government stakeholders will periodically monitor & evaluate the proper implementation and impact of the project. Community-based organizations and groups will be engaged throughout the project cycle. OWS-DF staff will work closely with change agents, teachers, parents, Community Health Volunteers, Women Development Groups (WDG), Women’s Development Army (WDA), & Youth associations among others to strengthen their capacities to carry out effective and efficient intervention. As a community-based organization –international actors investment in OWS-DF will contribute to enhancing the capacity of the organizations which will remain active in the community even beyond the project life time.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

To ensure community participation and collect feedback, regular community meetings and focus group discussions will be held. OWS DF will also use these forums to share information on OWS DF, the project activities, selection criteria, procedures for communities to raise complaints or give feedback as well as address other community concerns. This information will also be shared with communities through other platforms such as community representatives, posters, household visits. To ensure representation of vulnerable groups, FGDs conducted with communities will also purposely target women, persons living with disabilities, the elderly, the youth and other marginalized groups.In addition, OWS DF will use a complaint handling mechanism which is part of CFM where any members of the community can anonymously drop comments/complaints they may have regarding the services provided and behaviors of OWS DF staff for example. The aforementioned committee will be formed, strengthened to address such concerns. This committee will be responsible for regularly reviewing all complaints and based on their decision, take appropriate action to address any genuine complaint or grievance presented.Based on the feedback identified OWS DF has various response mechanisms to the beneficiary based on their decision levels. Field level: the need of the community or individual will be responded by field staff, volunteer and CFM Example. Home visit timing, discussion session, urgent consultation needed and similar issues. Project level: Project level staff can respond and solve community or individual needs. Office level: Regularly adjusting project monitoring activities based on community feedback and learning is key to delivering high-quality services accepted by the community. example, following monitoring finding decisions can be made by OWS DF management. Donor: If the need of community requires the involvement of the donor, in consultation with the donor modification can be made.OWS DF will protect and keep the confidentiality of reports, feed backs and any sensitive issues regarding the project both from the beneficiary and the employee. In response to any irregularity and sensitive issues regarding the project or protection issue, OWS DF will conduct careful identification, analysis and drafting results that can help to discuss with the concerned parties to solve the problem. After discussion and reach on common agreement, planning for the way out will be done together. Because the solution cannot be drawn in one side (OWS DF), but from different actors (stakeholders). To reduce existing gender disparity, harmful traditional practice, gender-based violence and protect the environment from degradation, awareness raising session with the communities will be prioritized. Women, persons with disabilities, older person, child-headed households will be enabled to participate in planning, implementation and evaluation. These marginalized sectors of the community will be encouraged to actively get involved in FGD and village project related communication.
