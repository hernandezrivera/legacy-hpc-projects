Project Summary

From the Somalia HNO 2022, the FSNAU and FEWS NET Post-Gu Assessment in September 2021 projected that nearly 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least December 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. That includes 1.1 million internally displaced persons who will face moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021. Rural populations in particular are experiencing multiple declines in food and income sources. The 2021 Gu harvest in southern Somalia is 60 per cent lower than the long-term average for 1995-2020. It is the third lowest Gu harvest since 2010. The lower production in 2021 Gu mainly reflects the impact of the poor rainfall, ongoing civil insecurity, river floods, shortage of farm inputs and pests. In urban settings, consequences of COVID-19 are likely to further exacerbate food consumption gaps, poverty levels, and protection concerns; particularly among vulnerable groups like Internally Displaced Persons, women headed households, girls, elderly and minority communities and persons with disabilities.
The project aims to increase food availability and food access for drought affected communities in Mogadishu and Lower Shabelle IDPs. This will be done through commodity food voucher distribution to the affected households. Voucher distributions are the most appropriate modality in all the targeted areas, because there are functional markets. Low purchasing power of vulnerable local inhabitants dampens market incentives and limits the incentive of traders and wholesalers to expand markets. Through the provision of food vouchers, this project will assist in stimulating these markets. Using vouchers is also preferential as there would be cost saving rather than cash transfers which is costly due to higher transfer costs charged by money vendors. Food vouchers will be provided on a monthly basis for 3 months of the project period to 2000 targeted households (12,400 Individuals) in Afgooye and Merka district IDPs of Lower Shabelle regions.
This assistance will address acute food needs by bolstering the purchasing power of targeted households through the projected lean season. The proposed voucher will provide access to a food basket consisting of national rice/Wheat our/Pasta, beans/lentils/Tuna fish, and vegetable oil) supplying 80% of daily caloric needs of a family of 5-7 members. Conditionality is subject to completion of a variety of infrastructure and environmental rehabilitation projects aimed to improve local food security, environmental degradation and market access conditions. The monthly voucher value will be based on the CWG MEB for Somalia (where the MEB includes enough to invest in livelihoods to avoid creating dependency on aid which antici[ate stronger linkages between humanitarian and development financing). Possible cash for work activities will be bush clearing, erosion control, digging latrine pits, and work to improve access to markets, basic services and other work to preserve community assets and recover from effects of drought. Women and men will be equally involved in determining the gender appropriate cash for work activities. Care will be taken to select activities that allow a high percentage of women to participate. Work norms will be established to ensure a reasonable quantity of work but allow women time to carry out their family responsibilities. SOYDA will also provide agricultural input support targeted to 750 HH. Agricultural input support shall be included seeds (15 kg of maize/sorghum, 8 kg of beans and 4 kg of sesame) for 750 farmers HH and 750 sets of farming tools, each set shall contain (1 hoes, 1 Stainless Pick & Mattock, 1 Machete, 1 Shovel and 1 Rake).

1. - Projects activities

1. Provide food voucher value of USD 60 per month for 3 months and consisting of cereal 50 kg, oil 5 kg, sugar 15 kg and pulses 10 kg to 2000 households (12,400 beneficiaries) of vulnerable people in Afgooye, Marka district of lower shabelle region. 2. SOYDA will prioritize unconditional cash transfers especially for the vulnerable 750 HH (378 men headed households and 372 women headed households) for 5 months IDPs population through a voucher system for five monthly cycles. SOYDA will prioritize cash in its target areas since markets are viable; are physically and socially accessible to all segments of the population. In line with recent Somalia Cash Working Group guidance, transfers will be 100 % of full Food MEB valued at 60 USD. Throughout the project implementation period, SOYDA will coordinate with the cash working group to determine the value of cash transfers. SOYDA will use Money Vender through a competitive process as its service provider for redeeming the cash voucher for the beneficiaries since its easily accessible, credible and secure for the beneficiaries in target districts.3. Creating of temporary job opportunities to 200 households (94 men headed households and 106 women headed households) just before the rainy seasons through cash for work activities and providing 5 USD daily rate to the laborers per 0.5 M3 for canals, 0.3 M3 for water catchments and 5 M2 for feeder roads working for 24 days a month. 4. SOYDA will conduct serial community mobilizations; sensitization and participation at each target villages that will bring together with the local leaders, women, youth and disabled representatives among target beneficiaries to inform about the activities, agree with the beneficiary/farmers selection criteria and allocations of each targeted villages considering poor farmers and drought affected HHs with access to land, women headed households. However, once the selection criteria and allocations agreed, SOYDA field staff in collaboration with respected village elders/women and in coordination with the respective district authority will select the target number of beneficiaries as per agreed selection criteria and allocations.5. SOYDA will also provide agricultural input support targeted to 750 HH. Agricultural input support shall be included seeds (15 kg of maize/sorghum, 8 kg of beans and 4 kg of sesame) for 750 farmers HH and 750 sets of farming tools, each set shall contain (1 hoes, 1 Stainless Pick & Mattock, 1 Machete, 1 Shovel and 1 Rake). 6. Organize and conduct capacity building training on resource management, early warning system, disaster risk reduction, good agricultural practices and conflict resolution for the 200 community, farmers, religious/community leaders, women leaders (60% women) and (25 persons per week) selected from the targeted communities in Lower Shabelle 7. Provision of vocational trainings to 70 (30 young men and 40 young women) individuals especially the unemployed youth in urban area and provide them with materials to start up a business of their own. 8. Provision of vocational trainings to 30 unemployed young men in fishing communities and provide them with boats, fishing nets and other equipment to enable them start up a business for themselves9. SOYDA will hire consultant that work with SOYDA field staff to conduct the post distribution assessment (PDA) of agricultural input distribution to determine the degree of effectiveness, efficiency and impacts of the agricultural input distribution to the drought affected farmers HHs.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CTP is more easier, safer and beneficiaries can manage to use based on their food consumption diversity. With CTP, SOYDA will be able to minimize the risks affecting vulnerability benficiaries who might resort to negative coping mechanisms if they rely only on in-kind support. CTP provides the beneficiaries with reasonable flexibility to buy tailored items which satisfy their specific needs , instead of in-kind approach which operates on the assumption that one size fits all. With different priorities based on their lifestyles, back ground, the beneficiaries are empowered by CTP to make choices which suites them best.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project has incorporated findings of the market surveys by WFP, Reach and SOYDA. Which analyzed needs. Somalia HNO 2022 also show the vulnerability, and with gaps which if filled by CTP which can guarantee rejuvenation of the local market considering the beneficiaries will be supported with capital to buy locally. Additionally, by supporting other aspects such as agriculture and rehabilitation of infrastructure will also stimulate growth within local market.
