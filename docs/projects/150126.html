Project Summary

The project of " Provision of Specialized Mental Health and Free Telephone Counseling (HL) Services to Women, Men, Boys and Girls Affected by Violence in the Great March of Return at Gaza Strip" aims at contributing to alleviate the effects of the Israeli occupation practices through delivery of quality specialized mental health services to the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys who are affected in the context of the Great March of Return (GMR) and living in the most affected and marginalized socio-economically vulnerable and under-served communities. The project has three main interventions as follows:
A1) Provision of specialized mental health services for 400 children (boys and girls) and 600 adults/parents (women and men) who have displayed symptoms of severe psychosocial distress. By using the existing referral system, those cases will be detected and referred by the case managers and psychosocial support staff working in family centers and Child Protection and MHPSS networks. Additionally, other cases will be detected through structured activities implemented through other venues such as GCMHP’s community centers and their field teams. The clinical services will be provided by specialized multi-disciplinary team including psychiatrists, psychologists, psychiatric nurses, social workers, and rehabilitation specialists at GCMHP three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah and Khanyounis. Therapy plans include psychotherapy, play therapy, medication, family counseling and home visits. GCMHP has procedures that offer practical guidance and assistance in taking protection issues seriously in the workplace, and to make staff aware of their roles and responsibilities while interacting directly with women and children.
A 2) Provision of free telephone counseling (Hot Line) service for 400 persons including children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who couldn’t reach mental health centres due to economic or social barriers. Through this service, the telephone counselors will provide psycho education, individual and family counseling, and also detect and refer children and adults with severe symptoms to GCMHP community centres to receive specialized mental health services including medications if necessary. This service helps overcome stigma attached to mental health problems. This service provides another differentiated aspect, where some cases will be detected and referred to legal and protection services provided by other service providers in Gaza Strip. GCMHP through its awareness raising activities including (public meetings, radio spots and programs, street billboards, publications, etc,) enhances access of the target group to this service.
A3) Provide training courses to 50 child protection workers (50% women) who are working in the field of child protection and MHPSS sector. The training topics will address the most common disorders among children and adults, and detection and referral skills.
A4) Produce one fact sheet, including gender and age-disaggregated data, to document trends/patterns of psychological disorders among those assisted to be shared with all stakeholders to help improve the prevention and protection response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions.

Needs

The CP/MHPSS WG’s data estimated that there are around 290,988 children living in the most marginalized and vulnerable communities, are still in need to structured psycho social support and child protection services in 2019. In fact, GCMHP found a significant increase in the number of cases who received its therapeutic services in 2018, as GCMHP’s specialized therapeutic services reached in the first half of 2018 to 1,950 including 1,076 males (55.2%) and 874 females (44.8%). GCMHP three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah and Khanyounis received 1,950 new and follow up cases including 483 children (239 were between 1 10 years and 244 were between 10-18 years, and the total number of girls was 198) and 1,467 adults (687 women and 780 men) the numbers of men were 297 between 19 30 years, and 434 were between 31 60 years and also the numbers of women were 239 between 19-30 years, and 389 were between 31-60 years. In addition, the number of beneficiaries from training activities tripled to reach 175 professionals; 50 men, 125 women working with CBOs and NGOs in all Gaza governorates. The majority of trainers received training courses on psychological first aid for children and adults, stress management, most common disorders among children and adults. Those trained professionals will be an important source for referral in the coming period. In 2018, CP/MHPSS WG's members referred monthly around 30-40 of vulnerable children and adults facing protection risks such as GBV, psychological distress and mental disorders. These risks and needs are the main priorities that will be addressed by this project. GCMHP is committed to involve both men and women in data collection and needs assessment phase considering their environments, economic situations, gender and age groups needs. In response to the mass demonstrations taking place in the Gaza Strip every Friday, GCMHP is currently implementing an emergency intervention since 15th May 2018 targeting the affected family members of injured and killed persons including men, women, boys and girls. Up to August, 3,174 people benefited from Psychological First Aid, of which 549 boys, 309 girls, 818 women and 1,498 men. 369 cases (11.6%) were referred to receive therapeutic services at GCMHP mobile stations or community centers. In addition, 283 people, of whom 18.73% were persons with disabilities, benefited from psychological therapeutic and specialized interventions at mobile stations, of which 94 boys, 31 girls, 52 women and 106 men. GCMHP teams observed high levels of trauma, depression and anxiety symptoms among injured people and their families including children and adults. Moreover, women reported high levels of violence practiced by the injured men towards wives and children.Moreover, free telephone counseling services have a part to play, alongside other services, in enhancing identification and engagement of people at risk, directing them to appropriate services, helping them through suicidal crises, and enhancing the continuity of care provided to individuals at risk. It is worth mentioning that GCMHP’s free telephone counseling is the only operating one in Gaza strip. Within the first half of the year 2018, the GCMHP records showed that 774 callers benefited from this service, and the vast majority of them were women and girls (62.4%) including (4.3% females under age 18 years, 57.2% females aged 18 59 years, and 0.9% females aged 60+), while men and boys were (37.6 %) including (1.1% males under age 18 years, 36% males aged 18 59 years, and 0.5% males aged 60+). Also, the cases were from Gaza governorates (16.9% Northern Gaza, 56.9% Gaza, 10.4% Middle zone, 8% Khanyounis, 7.8% Rafah). There were differences between men and women in terms of problems, men and boys suffered from psychological problems more than women and girls; while women and girls suffer from social, emotional and conduct problems more than men and boys.

Activities

The exposure to violence continue to have significant mental health and psychosocial (MHPSS) ramifications.This project will support the delivery of quality specialized mental health services to the most venerable women and men, girls and boys, affected by the mass demonstrations taking place at the border areas in the Gaza Strip, and living in affected and marginalized socio-economically under-served communities, and access restricted areas in Gaza Strip through the following: A1) Provision of specialized mental health services for children (boys and girls) and adults/parents (women and men) who have displayed symptoms of severe psychosocial distress. By using the existing referral system, those cases will be detected and referred by the case managers and psychosocial support staff working in family centers and Child Protection and MHPSS networks. Additionally, other cases will be detected through structured activities implemented through other venues such as GCMHP’s community centers and their teams can be provided with required support. The clinical services will be provided by specialized multi disciplinary team at GCMHP three community centers located in Gaza city, Deir El Balah and Khanyounis. Therapy plans include psychotherapy, play therapy, medication, family counseling and home visits. GCMHP has procedures that offer practical guidance and assistance in taking protection issues seriously in the workplace, and to make staff aware of their roles and responsibilities while interacting directly with women and children. A 2) Provision of free telephone counseling (Hot Line) service for children (girls and boys) and adults/parents (women and men) who couldn’t reach mental health centres due to economic or social barriers. Through this service, the telephone counselors will provide psycho education, individual and family counseling, and also detect and refer children and adults with severe symptoms to GCMHP community centres to receive specialized mental health services including medications if necessary. This service helps overcome stigma attached to mental health problems. Some cases also will be referred to other service providers when needed.A3) Provide training courses which will target 50 child protection workers (50% women) who are working in the field of child protection and MHPSS sector. The training topics will address the most common disorders among children and adults, and detection and referral skills. A4) Produce one fact sheet, including gender and age-disaggregated data, to document trends/patterns of psychological disorders among those assisted to be shared with all stakeholders to help improve the prevention and protection response and for joint coordinated advocacy messages and actions.GCMHP Users: the users of GCMHP community centers are adults/parents and children who are direct or indirect victims of human rights violations, e.g. any individual has direct/indirect personal experience in the context of Great March of Return. The access of beneficiaries to the community centers is ensured through addressing any economic, physical or social limitations ( eg. providing transportation for cases, conducting home visits and availability of free telephone counseling (hotline).Complaints Mechanism: GCMHP is always in place to respond to and address complaints through the following :1) Complaints boxes at community centers and Head Quarter of GCMHP. All these boxes are easily accessed by beneficiaries. 2)A specific part at its account page on official website and Facebook to receive complaints and inquires, these tools are available for all audience). 3)An employee is allocated and responsible for following up these complaints if founded and sent them to the High management. 4) All issues and publications of GCMHP such as brochures, billboards, radio spots, magazines and newsletters, etc.. include Full Contact Information of GCMHP (addresses of centers, telephone numbers, mail, and Hotline number).
