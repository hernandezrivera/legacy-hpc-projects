Project Summary

Return of the TDPs in tribal districts is 98% completed. As of November 2018, over 1,303,530 people have returned to their place of origin. A small number around 100,000 people are still in displacement which will likely be returned by end of the year 2019. Most of the returning population are settling in the newly merged districts of Khyber, Orakzai, Kurram, North Waziristan and South Waziristan in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province. Access to health services by the returning TDPs and the host population is poor. Findings from vulnerability assessment revealed that only 30% of the population in the target districts have access to essential life-saving health interventions . The average distance traveled by the population in these districts to the nearest health facility is 5.1 Km. According to WHO HeRAMS assessment, more than 31% of health infrastructures have been damaged as a result of civil unrest. Many of the displaced health staff, especially the female health worker, have not returned to duty. Nearly 50% medical officers are not available. Only 10% of the responders in these districts have ever been visited by LHW. Availability of essential lifesaving health care services is very low such as Routine immunization services at the primary healthcare level on average is 47%. For management of sick child, the application of integrated management of neonatal and childhood illnesses (IMNCI) protocol is very low at the primary healthcare level and stands at 23% only. Availability of nutrition screening services is also low at 25%. Availability of basic EmONC is 23% at PHC level. Availability of ANC services was 29% hence only 11.4% of the pregnant women visit the health facility 4 times during pregnancy. That aside the disease surveillance activities were very weak in most of the health facilities visited, and outbreaks of Measles, Cutaneous Leishmaniasis, Dengue Fever, Diphtheria and Pertussis were reported. The Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rate in tribal districts is 19.5% and the Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) rate is 4.4%. This GAM is above the 15% emergency threshold. In the last two year the districts registered disease outbreaks of Measles, Cutaneous Leishmaniasis, Dengue Fever, Diphtheria and Pertussis. These outbreaks were notified late.
Access to safe water and sanitation is also poor with 45% of the population in return areas relying on unsafe drinking water. 36% of returnees have no toilets and practice open defecation. The presence of water-borne diseases is high with 33% household-reported diarrhoeal cases in tribal districts. 16% of the responders reported that they have ever suffered from mental illness.
We propose to rehabilitate damaged health facilities, revitalize non-functional health facilities, strengthen service delivery particularly for the vulnerable groups such as women, children <5 years, adolescent boys and girls, elderly and the disabled by existing primary health care facilities, and establish community health services. In addition, we will strengthen disease surveillance and response to mitigate and control communicable diseases outbreaks which carries high morbidity and mortality at both community and facility level.
We expect that through this project, we will ensure improved access by the 1,303,530 returning population and the 200,000 IDPs & host populations to essential life-saving health interventions.

Needs

Department of Health Tribal Districts with the support of WHO conducted HeRAMS in all tribal districts in June 2018. The findings of the assessment show that about 31% of the health infrastructure, mostly PHC health facilities had been damaged as a direct result of the crisis. Health human resource was also affected and were displaced and reportedly has not returned to work particularly female health staff. Only 50% PHC facilities have medical officers available. Staff absenteeism is a major issue as some staff are on long leaves or extraordinary leaves and absent since long. The situation of Female Medical Officers, LHWs and LHV is of particular concern as the data shows deficient female staff presence due to various reasons, which has created a gap in service delivery particularly for women of reproductive age. Similarly, Medical Technicians are available in 71% health facilities but has high absenteeism rate and thus leads to gap in service delivery. Secondary health services are largely deficient and availability of General Physicians, Gyneacologists and Pediatricians is as low as 36% in whole of tribal districts. There is no single Psychiatrist available in the whole of the tribal districts. Provision of health services is low. Only 47% health facilities were providing EPI services, only 15% were treating children according to IMNCI protocols, only 23% PHC facilities were offering ANC services, while only 23% facilities were providing BEmONC services and 37% were providing PNC services. Therefore only 11.4% of pregnant women visit the health facility 4 times during pregnancy which is the required standard. Only 22% health facilities have Safe Delivery Kit available of which major contribution was from partner support. Only 23% health facility have delivery table available, while only 9% health facilities have availability of Oxytocin. ORS is available in 58% health facilities and antibiotics in 72% health facilities while only 52% health facilities have analgesics/antipyretics available at the time of assessment.Findings from ex-FATA Vulnerability Assessment exercise conducted in August/September 2017 revealed that 64% of the population in tribal districts faced difficulties in accessing health care facilities. The mean distance travelled by the population to the nearest health facility in tribal districts is 5.1 km. 24% of CBAs reported that they didn’t seek any medical help or services during their pregnancy, a significant proportion, 40%, reported delivery at home. Around 20% of the households children were not vaccinated during routine EPI. Around half of the respondents i.e. 51% in tribal districts reported that during the last three months, they were unable to seek medical help even if it was needed. Key reasons provided include the unavailability of relevant staff, relevant services and financial constraints. Only 11% responders in tribal districts have ever been visited by Lady Health Workers during the last three months preceding assessment. 16-39% of respondents reported a major episode of anxiety, depression and other mental health disorders. A significant caseload opted to visit health facilities outside of FATA due to non availability of required services in thier place. Access to safe water and sanitation is poor with 45% of the population relying on unsafe drinking water. 36% Returnee have no toilets and practice open defecation. The presence of water-borne diseases is prevalent with 33% household reported diarrhoea in tribal districts. Disease surveillance system is dismal in tribal districts due to lack of technical capacities, weak communication and reporting mechanisms, hence prompt identification and reporting of outbreak of communicable diseases with subsequent response is a major challenge as the diseases are notified late resulting in excess morbidity and mortality. Outbreaks of Dengue, Measles, Diphtheria, Pertusis and Cutaneous Leishmaniasis have been documented in the tribal districts in 2018.

Activities

There is need to urgently support the government line department to improve access to the population by strengthening community health care services, rehabilitating damaged health facilities, capacity building of the available health workers, provision of tools, provision of equipment to the functional health facilities and support to disease surveillance and response. WHO will undertake the following priority health actions and activities: 1. Support to coordination of the health response through stregthening national coordination capacities through cluster mechanism and health working group in KP as well as the national level by strengthening the National Health Emergency Preparedeness and Response Network2. Ensure continued access to health care by the returning population and or displaced population living far away from the health facilities through appropriate modalities3. Operationalization and Revitalization of non functional health facilities for Integrated Primary Health Care through minor repairs and rehabilitation of strategic health infrastructure, provide pre-fabricated structures (where needed) 5. Strengthening the much needed Reproductive, Maternal, Neonatal, Child and Adolescent Health services (RMNCAH) services in the return areas through appropriate modalities6. Fill gaps in provision of equipment and medicines for smooth running of the health services7. Mitigate the risks of communicable disease outbreaks through supporting and strengthening Integrated disease surveillance system8. Strengthen government capacities in surveillance, emergency preparedness, contingency planning to respond to public health risks & threats and disasters both natural and manmade.9. Capacities of health care staff on modern essential health services delivery package such as implementation of IMNCI protocols to identify and treat sick child, Kangaroo Mother care, Helping Baby Breathe (HBB) and Early Essential New Born Care (EENC) will be enhanced through standard WHO training modules10. Strengthen Referral mechanisms and capacitate referral health facilities for receiving high risk cases11. Mechanisms to include the most vulnerable groups such as disabled and also the elderly in the health response with careful inclusion in the interventions
