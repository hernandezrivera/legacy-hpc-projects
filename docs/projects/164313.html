Project Summary

The proposed project aims to provide life-saving and life-sustaining WASH assistance in North West (NWS) and North East (NES) of Syria. The strategy of intervention is designed on evidence-based needs taking into consideration this Partner’s strong community acceptance, humanitarian access and operational capacities in targeted areas. This Partner has three approaches to WASH in Syria:
1. provide a sustainable and cost-efficient response for IDPs in IDP sites who have been displaced in the mid- to long- term with limited perspective of return in the near future;
2. provide ongoing emergency services, such as water trucking, for those newly displaced or living in informal settlements to ensure access to basic and vital services; and
3. repair and rehabilitate damaged or non-functional WASH infrastructure to restore or improve water access at household level for returnees, host communities (and IDPs within these host communities) and conflict-affected people who remained in their area of origin.
This partner’s WASH package includes water supply (such as water trucking and water infrastructure rehabilitation), sanitation assistance (such as desludging latrines, and operation and maintenance of sanitation facilities) and hygiene promotion (such as distribution of hygiene kits and awareness campaigns). This Partner aims to include local communities and local authorities as much as possible to foster a sense of ownership and self-autonomy, encouraging the continuity of activities which would also reduce dependency on humanitarian funding to provide such services.
Whenever possible, this Partner aims to ensure the transition between emergency response towards a more sustainable approach for access to safe water and WASH services, in a protracted displacement context. However, there remains populations in camps and settlements in dire need of continuous provision of WASH services, including lifesaving water supply until long-term cost-recovery systems can allow for a truly sustainable solution.

4 - Needs

Needs analyses are based on monitoring and reporting mechanisms (data collection, KIIs, ongoing coordination with humanitarian actors) & secondary data reviews. Primary assessments include RNAs, voucher/cash studies, FGDs, market assessments & satisfaction surveys.NWS: According to REACH’s (Factsheet, Dec 2019) there are severe negative coping strategies used for those without water access and 73% of respondents said the high price of private water trucking the main barrier to water access. Where the sole WASH actor, this Partner’s water trucking remains the main source of free, safe and accessible drinking water. Sanitation and environmental health standards are still low: SI’s PDM surveys report that half the female population in targeted camps use communal latrines, highlighting protection and access issues, especially for women. According to SI’s field assessments, IDPs are constructing their own latrines, creating health risks due to lack of maintenance, highlighting the need to create community hygiene groups. As violence in Idlib escalates, significant influxes of IDPs continues, exacerbating emergency needs for WASH services. In November 2019, the key recommendations from SI’s RNAs were to continue water supply activities, ensure adequate sanitation standards and strengthen HP activities.NES: The outbreak of violence in Oct 2019 caused new widespread displacement and damage. Access to safe WASH services remain critical due to destruction of water infrastructure and slowing down restoration of services, putting people at risk of infectious and water-borne disease. This Partner’s water quality testing results in Raqqa show a high contamination of all networks due to substandard chlorination levels, and this Partner own assessments reported damage to 85 water stations, disrupting safe water access to 400,000 people. Waste management and sanitation services are lacking, with an increase of health risks due to contamination factors associated with poor health.

5 - Implementation of activities

Thanks to the long term operational presence in targeted areas, this partner will be able to combine direct and remote management, in accordance with the continue evolution of the security context. As demonstrated by the last events of October 2019, this partner has a flexible capacity to rapidly revise and adapt its implementation setup according to the context challenges. Using direct implementation to oversee the work of contractors and suppliers through close monitoring, this Partner will maintain contingency capacity to respond to new shocks or emergencies through framework agreements with suppliers enabling stockpiling and rapid refurbishment of the stocks, and swift water trucking and latrine installation. According to needs and risk analyses and contextual development across NES and NWS, the strategy of implementation aims to provide life-saving or sustainable access to safe water and WASH facilities for those populations facing a protracted displacement in target locations. This action (1) ensures sustainable access to safe drinking water to displaced people in the camp through an integrated approach of water trucking (for the period of time required for the finalization of the water network in the camps) and water network rehabilitation and maintenance; (2) provides lifesaving access to safe water through water trucking when more sustainable alternative solutions are not available and (3) provide WASH services including solid waste management, sewage networks, sanitation facilities (and required desludging services) and community health and hygiene outreach activities. This Partner focused on coordination and information management, and developed a new MEAL strategy for 2020 focusing on ensuring monitoring data, learning and beneficiary feedback is integrated into assessment, program design and learning throughout the program cycle to ensure acceptance and accountability.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

For emergency water supply, there are risks relating to movement of trucks, not meeting humanitarian standards and equal access to water. For emergency sanitation, there are risks in toilet accessibility, movement of desludging trucks and cultural sensitivity of facilities. For SWM, risks include movement of waste trucks, potential exposure to UXOs and general health risks relating to garbage. For rehabilitations, risks include unequal distribution of services which may benefit some groups, risk of theft, improper storage of supplies, potential exposure to UXOs and general health risks associated with construction and chemicals. Finally, regarding Hygiene Kit distribution, risks include tensions between beneficiaries due to unequal distribution, GBV risks for women and girls at distribution points, gender insensitive kits, theft, sale or exchange of kits and distribution of expired/unhealthy items. For all WASH activities, there are also protection risks associated with child labour and GBV. Mitigation measures will be taken throughout all WASH programming, including but not limited to: consultation with community (women and girls, people with disabilities and older people) regarding all activities to ensure the specific needs, risks and considerations are applied to activity planning and implementation; establishing and communicating clear and safe routes for all trucks; conducting safety training (operational safety, PPE, hazard awareness etc.) with all individuals involvement in programming (drivers, monitors, kit distributors etc.); strong awareness of all national and international standards for WASH activities (SPHERE, sector/cluster/WG standards etc.); continuous use of monitoring and feedback mechanisms; establish strong links with local authorities and other humanitarian actors to promote equity and reduce gaps/overlaps; ensure proper registration for all involved in programs; and, consider the specific risks and needs of women and girls for all programs.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The main risks include but are not limited to: impact of the UN cross-border resolution on humanitarian access, cross-line humanitarian access is further restricted; authorities in areas of intervention do not allow cross-border or internal access; registration delays or refusal by GoS for INGOs in receiving authorisation to work; local authorities’ control and monitoring of humanitarian assistance jeopardizes the ability to respect core humanitarian principles; border closures in neighboring countries inhibit the flow of good or personnel; escalation of conflict; shift of frontlines; potential conscription measures coming into effect that would impact the work and movement of national staff in areas under SDF control. Assumptions for this project include but are not limited to: humanitarian workers and programs are not directly targeted; dormant cells from radical groups preventing access to the intervention area are not activated; the security situation does not deteriorate; cross-border humanitarian access and intervention continue to be permitted and feasible; borders remain open with neighbouring countries; market prices, inflation rates and exchange rates remain stable and accessible. Mitigation measures include but are not limited to: providing support to advocacy efforts by the existing coordination forum at national and international level if cross-border implementation is hindered; continue the existing partnership with national NGOs as well as identifying new potential partners, in tandem with launching the registration process with GoS in Damascus and identifying alternative solutions to completing activities in case of delays in the registration process; maintain strong relationships with local authorities to ensure acceptance, information sharing and strong communication channels between parties; monitor the context closely, continual updating of internal contingency planning; monitor security context closely and ensure continual security plan update
