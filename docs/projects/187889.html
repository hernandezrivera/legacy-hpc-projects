Project Summary

This Project will provide structured support to 600 small businesses and enterprises in target locations, enabling them to re-start, increase trade and become more profitable. For many who do not have access to land or livestock assets, particularly those displaced from, or residing in, urban and peri-urban areas, petty trade and other small private businesses is their main source of income. For those who have had to relocate and have left behind assets, or where there is increased demand for their services but they do not have the ability to restart due to a loss of capital, the action will provide conditional cash transfers (CTP).
This will be based on the delivery and adherence to tailored business plans.
Cash transfer amounts will vary based on the type or planned scale of business, but will range from USD 800 to 1000
If cash is not an appropriate modality, e.g. the materials required by business grant recipients are not available, then the project will use a voucher based approach or provision of in-kind support.
Cash allows for a more normalized market environment (greater choice and competition between shops) and protects beneficiary dignity.
Detailed SOPs are in place to manage the implementation of cash transfers inside Syria.
IAC will also conduct verification to check how the cash grants have been used in relation to the business activities.
Participants will be supported to conduct a simple business feasibility exercise to determine their needs, provide assurance that there is demand for their services, and demonstrate the viability of the activity in the current economic climate.
These exercises will be assessed and the most viable will be selected for funding.
From experience in target locations in Hama and Aleppo governorates, common trades will likely include home-based production and processing, restocking of small grocers, restocking of petty traders, resupplying equipment to tailors, barbers/hairdressers and animal husbandry.
IAC teams will review and approve 300 applications for small business grants. If conditional cash transfers are used as the modality for the business grants, this can be split into two transfers, the second transfer being released upon successful implementation of the agreed upon steps in the business plan.
The applicants will also provide a basic package of business training and coaching/mentoring for the successful applicants.
Female participants to date have reported a preference for group-based activities, which will be explored and facilitated as appropriate.
Selection of enterprises will be linked to existing value chain assessments wherever possible in order to connect participants to the market and design activities that meet longer-term market demands.
Small business support will focus on the 'poor' wealth groups who have limited access to resources to continue their usual activities though already have some previous experience of developing small enterprise activities.
These households will be reliant on petty trade, and/or may be embarking on new ventures based on their existing experience but lack capital to start, or they may have lost assets or resources through displacement and are therefore unable to restart their enterprise without an injection of capital or resources.
Targeting criteria is anticipated to include:
1. Limited or no regular income
2. Limited or no productive assets
3. Sufficient skills and experience to complete proposed activities.
Vulnerabilities will determine prioritization of target BNFs, including those with a high dependency ratio, female-headed households and families caring for one or more disabled or chronically ill members. However, the primary criterion for this intervention will be previous experience and capacity of the potential beneficiary to implement the planned activities.
Women will be a particular focus of this intervention and activities will be culturally appropriate to enable their participation for example home-based activities.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, 13.8M in 2022 and 15M in 2023 in need some form of assistance in the ER sector. The protracted crisis in Syria, the knock-on effects of COVID19, and major losses in food production sector due to climate change have intensified needs, have intensified Humanitarian needs. Aleppo is the most populous governorate in Syria, it was the major commercial and industrial hub of the country before conflict, until it has been embroiled in heavy conflict since mid- 2012. Much of the infrastructure has been destroyed by ground fighting and aerial bombardment, and large population movements have occurred both within and outside the governorate causing a greater deprivation of access to main services. Most of population in the eastern half of Aleppo has fled to the Northern and western countryside of Aleppo and the Turkish border. Atareb is close to Aleppo, it is relatively calm. but it has critical needs for early recovery and livelihood to revive the market. In the other hand Hama is located on the main road between Damascus and Aleppo, and is one of the most important agricultural and industrial center in Syria, and received a large number of IDPs adding significant pressure on the limited available resources and increasing need for humanitarian aid. According to the assessment implemented in targeted location about the current small jobs, respondents indicated that grocery was the most frequent choice. Then barberry was the second and then the small repairs.

5 - Implementation of activities

Project will be implemented in 2 phases 1. phase in Aleppo governorate and 2. phase in Hama governorate, support will be provided at each phase to 300 BNFs.The overall approach for the small business grants will be developed, including selection criteria and process, scoring system for selecting proposals and the value of the grants to be provided. IAC Team will explain the objectives of this component to target communities. They will conduct an orientation for potential beneficiaries on how to fill out the business plan template as well as basics of business management and planning. Once applications are submitted, IAC will review them and score and approve based on the developed criteria. Each proposal from the selected beneficiaries will include a proposed budget and plan, if items are required in-kind(e.g. they are not available to purchase locally directly by beneficiaries) then IAC Logistics Officer will be responsible for procuring these items. If applications are based on cash grants, IAC finance team will be responsible for contracting the money transfer agent to make payments and overseeing the reporting and reconciliation of all beneficiary payments. IAC Officers are responsible for ongoing monitoring and technical support to the beneficiaries. IAC MEAL Officers and Assistants will be responsible for collecting baseline and end line data and monitoring the cash payments by BNFs, as well as Post-Distribution Monitoring following provision of small business grants

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

To prevent any PSEA issue from occurring during the implementation period of the project activities, especially the activities in which the project staff are in direct contact with the beneficiaries. All field activities have been reviewed and assessed for risk factors for PSEA with the use of the PSEA Network Field and Organizational Mitigation and Prevention Checklists and the following prevention and mitigation measures will be taken: 1- IAC will make sure that All staff, and volunteers understand and sign the Code of Conduct.2- Field staff with direct contact with beneficiaries will receive PSEA Training.3- IAC will review internal reporting mechanisms and ensure all staff know about reporting mechanisms.4- Activating complaints mechanisms to protect PSEA in particular, placing them in easily accessible places, designing awareness-raising banners, placing boxes for complaints and suggestions, and activating the hotline number (WhatsApp).5 - provide awareness-raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA and methods for reporting them and gaining the trust of society and spreading humanitarian principles and stick to them.
