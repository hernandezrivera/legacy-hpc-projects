Project Summary

Plan International will provide support and assistance for South Sudanese refugees living in camps in White Nile, and urban refugees in Khartoum, targeting Eritrean, Syrian and Ehiopian populations. Among urban refugees in Khartoum, the target group are ranked in high need category based on protection risks and education needs. In 2020 response, Plan in Khartoum will increase understanding of child rights and improve protection environment and response mechanisms, as well as increase access for out of school children and improve quality of education available to target populations. White Nile remains one of the most vulnerable states in Sudan with a fragile humanitarian situation. To mitigate vulnerabilities and address needs among South Sudanese refugees in White Nile, Plan will deliver interventions in child protection, WASH, livelihood and health. Girls are also particularly exposed to early and forced marriages, sexual violence, and the curtailment of their education; Interagency assessments and community discussions revealed that, there are significant gaps in targeted humanitarian assistance provided to adolescent girls. Prioritizing adolescent and young girls across all activities, while Plan has a leading role in some sectors like child protection, for other sectors like health the proposed activities will play a complementary role to existing structures by filling the gaps to support female adolescents and youths in refugee and surrounding host communities practice greater autonomy and improved self-care with access to age and gender responsive hygiene and reproductive humanitarian assistance in White Nile.

1 - Needs

Children are further at risk for exploitation, GBV and other forms of abuse in target communities. The lack of child protection mechanisms and low capacity of local authorities to prevent and response are another need this planned response will address. Plan will also prioritize needs of UASC, especially the new arrivals from South Sudan and Eritrea, and address gap in care including but not limited to need of appropriate alternative care arrangements, assistance, basic services and possible family reunification. For the children identified as out of school, integration in formal systems will be the goal, though Plan recognizes that targeted programmes for adolescents and youth are needed, including life skills training, vocational training and recreational activities for those unable to access formal learning opportunities. Specific attention to the needs of girls and older children will be incorporated by Plan interventions to ensure these vulnerable groups are prioritized. The estimation number of adolescent are 38378 girls and 38214 boys. There are 2 secondary hospital operated by MSF Spain and 7 health clinics operated by UNHCR, SMOH and SCRS as well as WHO (Joury, Alradis1, Alradis2, Umsangor, Daba Bosin, Alagya and Algamia Camps) but insufficient access to PHC services, including RH activities such as are family planning, counselling for SGVB survivors, STIs testing, safe delivering are available inside the camps. Based on the UNHCR assessment for the refugees in White Nile camps livelihoods strategy (attached) , the main income sources of the refugees are agricultural laborers (93%) and casual labour (7%) of poor households and crop sharing (86%) and petty trade (14%) for the middle income households and crop sharing (52%), skilled labour (40%) and commerce (8%) for the better-off households. About 13.5% was found unemployed. Through implementation of this response plan, Plan International will meet identified needs of refugee children and mitigate protection risks, including child labour, early marriage and out-of-school. The assessment indicated that; Out of the 448 surveyed households 56.2% females and 43.5% males; Women as food producers were lacking access to production inputs, knowledge and skills and farm land enabling them to carry out viable / sustainable food production strategies. The assessment was also indicated that both refugees and host community have no or very limited opportunities to be recruited in formal employment either within the private sector or public sector. The assessment results reported that most of households' income 40.6% were came from informal labour market compared with only 1.1% from the formal / or semi-formal labour market due to lack of skills and education qualification among the targeted beneficiaries. The lack of stable employment opportunities means that people resort to seek different ways of securing their survival which subject to additional risks of adopting negative coping strategy such as smuggling of food and goods, selling of food aid items, processing and selling of alcoholic drinks, human trafficking, women and girls working as house maids, child work, tea selling, etc.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Base interventions on gendered analysis of the causes and impacts of crises, the different need of young women and men, and design programs to meet those different needs, including collecting and having a regular analysis on SADDD, in order to target assistance towards those most at risk. • Look for opportunities to support young women and men in ways that contribute to the transformation of gendered power relations: lay the groundwork for gender equality and greater resilience to conflict and crises, by actively supporting women’s leadership and by challenging attitudes and beliefs that undermine women’s rights, including engaging with men and boys. • Promote the full participation and leadership of young women and men in all aspects of programming – from planning to evaluation. • Have gender-sensitive M&E system by developing gender sensitive indicators (quantitative and qualitative), as well as conducting regular tracking on the impact of the emergency response towards young women and men.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Above can only select one but will have multiple channels including face-to-face with community leaders, billboards, complaints box, and help desk during distributions. For Community Feedback & response Mechanism (CFRM) Plan International Sudan will ensure that target communities are fully oriented and consulted on establishing the CFRM; how they want to raise feedback and complaints, to interact with the organization, and to be acquainted with the purpose of this system. CFRM will ensure multiple layers or channels are available for affected populations to report, share their feedback, suggestions for improvement and report for any type of violations or abuse of resources. This will include Focus Group Discussions, high visibility of Plan staff e.g. community meetings, sharing of appropriate contact details with community members (hotlines and national hotline for PSEA), billboards and suggestion boxes. During activities banners/signposts with PIS details will be visible, especially during distributions. This will help the organization to avoid risks of serious harm of the organizations’ image and profile as well sustaining good relationship with the people it is trying to serve and impacting their lives. Plan International’s Global Policy on Safeguarding Children and Young People puts procedures in place to prevent and deal with the actions/behavior of our staff, associates, visitors or us as an organization that result in violence against a child or young person and/or places them at risk of the same. Plan staff are obligated to ensure children and young people we work with are aware of our responsibilities to prevent and respond to any harm against them arising from actions and behaviors of our staff, associates and visitors, and the routes for reporting such incidents. In addition to Plan’s global safeguarding policies, Plan International Sudan has committed to the Joint Framework for Action on PSEA under leadership OCHA and in partnership with other humanitarian actors.
