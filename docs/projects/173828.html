Project Summary

The project aim at enhancing restoration of livelihood amongst IDPs and affected population through agricultural, livestock and fishery support (small ruminant, local poultry, table birds, piggery, fishery and agriculture) in Mezam, Bui, Boyo, and Ngoketunjia divisions in the NW/SW region of Cameroon. The project approach include; capacity building and support with start-up materials. The project primary target includes; 1500 women led household leaders and youths in the 5 targeted divisions in the northwest region. This project will increase community impact and built on the successes of our just completed early recovery project with UNDP, while ensuring respect for covid-19 preventive measures.
The project is going to ensure gender mainstreaming in it design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. We are going to carry out a gender analysis and collect age and sex disaggregate data. This will help us determine the food security-related needs, capacities and aspirations of women, girls, men and boys in the affected population. This will help us identify groups with the greatest food security support needs, disaggregated by sex and age.
In order to ensure accountability to affected population, the project is going to create community complaint and feedback mechanisms committees in each targeted community. This will help us learn from beneficiaries, communities and other stakeholders and be responsive to their views, opinions and complaints, so improving the quality and impact of our work
Upon completion, the project will increase productivity of small scale vulnerable farmers as a result of capacity building and support with farm inputs. The project is also expected to enhanced social cohesion between IDPs and their host communities, and restores livelihoods of 600 vulnerable women and youths affected by the humanitarian crisis through capacity building and support with start-up materials.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The humanitarian situation in the NW/SW regions of Cameroon continues deteriorating with more than 1.5 million people presently in need of humanitarian assistance. Insecurity is forcing more people to flee their homes. More than 980,000 people need shelter, and some 918,000 people in need of food and non-food assistance, thousands of youths and women are jobless with livelihood destroyed. An estimated 3,000 children are suffering from life-threatening severe acute malnutrition requiring urgent treatment. Subsistent farming which has been the means of survival in the past has been greatly affected as the displacement and violence increases hindering farming and even food harvesting. According to OCHA on 1st of January 2019 about 3000 children are suffering from severe acute malnutrition. Malnutrition is a critical risk in the NW region and good nutrition and access to adequate food remains a challenge to child survival especially in the context of the current sociopolitical crises. Reports of assessment by World Food Program (WFP) indicate that 900,000 people in the Northwest region are food insecure. In this population 13% of IDPs and 10 % of non IDPs are severely food insecure. 36.6% of IDPs and 29.9% of NIDPs are moderately food insecure while 38.2% of IDPs and 29.5% NIDPs are marginally food insecure. The continuous aggravating situation and sustained prevalence of critical Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) levels (2.8%) in the Northwest region suggests that general food assistance is needed to treat children with moderate acute malnutrition and prevent malnutrition. This could be as a result of the fact that subsistence farming which was basically the means of survival has been greatly affected due to farmers not having access to farms, unable to harvest their food and even continue farming or transport food to market. A report by the Famine Early Warning System Network as reported by the US agency for International development asserts that, insecurity, displacement, a decline in crop production and subsequently strained livelihoods will likely cause vulnerable households in the Northwest regions to experience crisis levels of food insecurity through May 2020. There is therefore a need for general livelihood assistance in the Northwest region.With the outbreak of the coronavirus, IDPs and families who are already vulnerable in the region due to on-going political crises are particularly at risk. COVID-19 has quickly changed the context in which children and families live in Mezam. Urban consumption and expenditure is likely to experience a sharp fall in light of COVID-related lockdowns and reduced restrictions; IDPs and vulnerable families may not be able to provide for their basic needs. This region is therefore in need of humanitarian assistance to help restore the livelihood
