Project Summary

This program is a continuous intervention by SDH and its partners to contribute (securing the operational costs of the centers and raising coordination through the referral system) in reducing morbidity and mortality, improving comprehensive health services for conflict-affected Syrians, and providing equitable access to health care in order to support the health care system in targeted areas, as well as providing life-saving and sustainable humanitarian assistance.
The intervention will primarily focus on providing and improving primary health care and other specialized services.
The program will provide basic health care services to several targeted districts for beneficiaries through subsidized health facilities in northern Syria.
This program consists of these projects:
1- Supporting Primary Health Care Centers (PHC):
Afrin PHC B0203081.
- Primary Health Care Center in Bab al-Hawa Camps G0703148.
- Primary Care Bearing G0705035.
- Sahara Center for Health Care in Atarib.
(The plan is to fully support 5 primary health care centers so far in line with the basic health package, these centers are distributed in Aleppo and Idlib, targeting: 96000 BNFs / for men: 12,400, for females: 33,600, boys: 22,800, girls: 27,200).
2- Supporting FH field hospitals:
- Apamea FH G0703170 in Harem (with the possibility of moving the site to cover the needs in the gap of the other site).
(The plan is to fully support the hospital at a fair operating cost to be able to provide the basic healthcare package, targeting: 60,000 BNFs / men: 11,100, female: 23,700, boys: 7,300, girls: 17,900 ).
3- Supporting physiotherapy centers:
- Batbo Physical Rehabilitation Center B020206.
- Martyr Mustafa Abed Center for Physical Rehabilitation B02207.
- Youth Center B0200112.
(The plan aims to provide full PC support at a fair operating cost to be able to provide the basic healthcare package, which targets: 12,000 BNFs/men: 9500, females: 1,700, boys: 1,000, girls: 800).
4- Support for CCTCs SDH.
Al-Sarraj Center in Atarib, CCTC_3.
- Al-Sarraj Center in Hazano, CCTC_5.
- Al-Sarraj Center in Darat Azza, CCTC_7.
- Siraj Kelly Center CCTC_20.
(The plan is to continue to fully support the CCTCs at a fair operational cost to be able to provide the required IPC level and treatment procedures for COVID-19 cases, target: 6000 BNF suspected + confirmed + probable cases / men: 2800, female: 1900, boys: 800, girls: 500).
Within all its projects, the program will provide health services to the population of the targeted areas, including the host community and the displaced.
Some projects of this program have already been funded, and we are reaching out to more partners and donors for more collaboration opportunities.

4 - Needs

According to 2022 Syria (HNO), 13.4 M people inside Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance (including 2.4 million in acute need). While the disrupted health system impacts the entire population, the health sector utilizes its severity scale to focus its support on sub-districts with the level of Areas of highest severity that are generally found in the North of Syria. Among the 12.4 M people in need of health services, 5.6 million children under age 17, 6.4 million women of reproductive age, 0.5 M elderly, and 3.1 M with disability. (Compared to 2021, the health sector PiN has increased 3%) Th COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated weaknesses in the health system, till 20 of JAN 93,043 COVID-19 cases and over 2356 deaths reported in the NWS. Relatedly, areas of active hostilities – such as north-west and north-east Syria, IDP camps, and informal settlements, and locations without reliable healthcare systems and shelter remain highly vulnerable to epidemic-prone disease. Recent assessments indicate the need for an improved supply of medicines and services within PHCs, – particularly for non-communicable diseases, where NCDs and chronic conditions particularly affect vulnerable groups such as IDP women and girls, elderly people and PwD (an estimated 28% of people over 12 years of age live with disabilities in target locations according to HNAP 2021) and require continuous care by PHC providers. CDs remain a priority. Need for increased COVID-19 testing and vaccination.

5 - Implementation of activities

In order to carry out the proposed activity, SDH plans to support the existing employees of the proposed facility with SDH Syria Payroll. The immediate staff that will be supported are the health facility staff as all the mentioned health facilities are already operational. The field staff will be the SDH field team. The support staff will be the health department team. With regard to the proposed program to be implemented, the project activities will cover: 1. Improving access to primary and secondary health care and trauma through full support covering operating costs, rehabilitation, medicines and consumables: Activities: 1.1: Providing primary health care consultations to IDPs and the host community. 1.2: Providing trauma services, including minor and major surgeries, to IDPs and the health center. 1.3: Providing medicines, supplies and equipment necessary for hospital maintenance. (Free), 1.4: Tracking malnutrition cases (children >5, lactating and pregnant women) and referring them to the specialized centers. • Providing treatment and accommodation services for COVID-19 cases in the targeted areas. 2.1: Providing mental health and psychosocial support services to IDP camps (covering counseling, medication and other services). in the targeted areas. Monitoring and evaluation will be provided by the M&E department, through routine data collection, spot checks,KII & PDM, focusing on beneficiary satisfaction,areas for improvement,potential challenges, and access to services

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: possibility: high effect: high mitigation measures: - Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities. - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), - Providing preparedness training for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents. 2- Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding and shared toilets as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery: possibility: high, effect: high mitigation measures: Establishing separated toilets for men and women in the health facility - Ensuring that the waiting areas are sufficient for all patients – Allocating someone from the guards to regulate the flow of patients – Establishing an easy-to-reach complaints mechanism( complaints box, WhatsApp number, exit interviews) - Training all employees on the policy of preventing harassment in the workplace and including this policy in their contracts. 3- neglection of Healthcare for Vulnerable Groups and Equitable Access to Health Services: possibility: moderate, effect: high mitigation measures: - Analyze and disaggregate all data by sex, age, and disability. Analyze the needs of vulnerable and minority groups (such as adults and children with disabilities), throughout the program cycle (assessment, analysis, design, implementation, and monitoring)


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SDH is committed to protecting beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse, in compliance with PSEA standard policy and procedures. In the SDH new staff recruited will have to sign the CoC released by the PSEA network recently and will be fully oriented about PSEA and the available complaint mechanism. Awareness raising of the community about SEA will be a part of activities with full illustration of available compliant mechanism. PSEA focal points are responsible for mainstreaming PSEA into all departments and projects, through developing the measures and practices including feedback and compliant mechanism along with concerned departments. Posters and Banners raising awareness about PSAE will be used in all facilities.New modalities of PSEA raising awareness - due to COVID-19 crisis are followed, such as sending text messages to the beneficiaries, online training, using Banners in the distribution locations. The project ensures the community and beneficiaries' engagement in project designing, and planning since the beginning through the need’s assessments and so on during the next phases, and through targeting young girls and women with socially accepted activities including life skills. There will be multiple channels to report feedback and complaints privately and confidentially, including hotline number.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Assumptions: SDH will coordinate closely with the health clusters, as well as local authorities (including health directorate) and local NGOs operating inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, sending feedback to the cluster when interventions are needed to be addressed. Risk: an emergency status occur (increase the #COVID-19 cases "the second wave"). Mitigation: SDH will use its coordination channels for developing a coordinated response plan with the partners. Risk: The close of borders which prevent the shipments of supplies and medications to access to facilities. mitigation: Develop a strong sustainable strategy for accessing and deliver goods through local actors. •Assumptions: health facilities remains accessible for the SDH team and for the beneficiaries. Risk: The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities. mitigation: SDH through the S&S team has developed a strong risk management strategy for emergency situations. •Assumptions: currency exchange rates remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned activities. Risk: high volatility of exchange currency. mitigation: fixing the operational cost inside Syria in USD. •Assumptions: the medical supplies & medicines & PEE are provided to the supported HFs. Risk: UNSCR wasn't renewed, So, interruption of WHO supply line as well as the supply chain from Turkey to Syria. mitigation: SDH has a strong supply chain based on Turkish & Syrian companies that move the commodities.
