Project Summary

Médicos del Mundo Spain (MdM-S) will contribute through this project to the achievement of Strategic Objectives No. 2 and No.3 of the Protection Cluster Strategy.
In particular, this project will strengthen resilience, improve access to services and pursue for long-lasting solutions, thus mitigating the effects of occupation and conflict-related violence in the West Bank and of blockade in Gaza. The project focuses in increasing the access to quality enhanced public Mental Health services and MHPSS outreach services at the community level. The project will specifically target population, and in particular children, women and adolescents, affected by the conflict in the West Bank and Gaza. This will be done incorporating the IASC MHPSS and GbV guidelines, the Minimum Standards for Ch.P. in HA (CPWG), applying as core principles: human rights and equity, participation, do no harm, integrated support to systems and building on available resources and capacities; and the enhancement of a coordinated and effective response to critical incidents and MHPSS support to survivors of GbV and Child Abuse.
MdM intends to pursue the following activities between 2020 and 2021: •Provision of Child Protection response, with special emphasis on safe spaces for responsive interventions in emergencies, including case management and PSS for children and families, as well as Early Stimulation Programs.
•Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial services (MHPSS) for adults and youth, with special emphasis on gender equality and detection and referral of GbV.
•Capacity building of public Health Services and Mental Health Services to address children and adolescents needs following appropriate guidelines and standards.
•Advocacy initiatives among the international community that aim to address protection concerns; prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations of IHRL and IHL by all duty bearers.
• Contribute to coordination and participation in the Protection Cluster and progressively increasing contributions to GbV subcluster.

1 - Needs

MdM-S has focused its needs analysis on the most vulnerable people living under occupation in Area C (Jericho and Ramallah Governorates) and E1 Area, and under blockade in the Gaza Strip. In the last years, several assessments have been conducted in the framework of current actions, identifying humanitarian needs and response gaps, in coordination with MoH, MoSD, MoEHE representatives, first responders (school counselors, community mental health professionals, primary healthcare professionals, CBOs and mobile clinics health staff), as well as focal group discussions with target population. In addition, this project is aligned with 2020 HNO needs identification and takes into consideration the increasingly coercitive environment in Area C and the rise in settler violence in the first half of 2019, as well as the increasingly destabilized economy in Gaza. Gaza is one of the locations with highest vulnerability in the world (ranking #16 in INFORM 2019) based on the socioeconomic situation, exposure to violence, unemployment (86% in women 15-24 y.o.). According to UNRWA 49% of adult refugees have weak psychological status, out of which 63% require depression screening, and 30% of children face serious difficulties. In the last year, contributing factors to GbV have worsened, and correlation between violence against women and child abuse is well documented. Women and girls experience higher stigma in accessing MHPSS services (causes include jeopardizing marriage options), but men and boys make less use of the services.Based on these assessments, the main humanitarian needs identified are: *Since cutting funding for UNRWA’s emergency programs (including mobile health clinics and MH assistance for Bedouin communities), over 220,000 West Bank Palestinians lost access to permanent clinics. They face critical challenges in accessing primary health and MHPSS services, according to OCHA, access to healthcare could be hampered by expensive or no transport. Women face higher constraint in access to transportation (cars and financial means) *Need to increase in Gaza and Area C/E1 access to MHPSS services (especially outreach), primary health care and GbV survivors tailored services due of a shortage in service provision and degradation of contributing factors. *Need to increase the quality of MHPSS services and its integration into general health services and connection with the community services. *In the oPt the successive conflicts and the blockade/occupation created high levels of psychosocial distress and eroded infrastructures, negatively impacting children and their families, and specifically the caregivers (women of the family in most cases), therefore making it essential to include Early Stimulation Programmes (ESP) and MHPSS capacity building in the education environment to foster the wellbeing of children exposed to conflict related violence. Moreover, the Mental Health (MH) services need further adaptation (appropriate infrastructure, confidentiality, privacy, appropriate information management, and GbV sensitivity). *Conflict related violence, risk of forcible transfer, settler violence and the impact of blockade and restrictions of movement makes it necessary to strengthen first responders self protection and safety, according to the IASC MHPSS Guidelines, including the provision of tools to mainstream gender perspective. * Need to tackle duty bearers according to International Humanitarian Law, through advocacy with International community, to address protection concerns, prevent violations or enhance accountability for violations of IHL and IHRL. *Need to tackle Responsibility bearers and first responders to sensitize about the different aspects of the Right to health, and in particular, non discrimination, stigma (MH/GbV), and confidentiality.

3 - Indicators

The overall objective of the project is to improve protection mechanisms, both responsive and remedial, for most vulnerable groups in the West Bank and Gaza through increased access and quality of mental health and psychosocial support services with comprehensive and gender sensitive approach. The action is targeting the most vulnerable communities in Area C and E1 affected by or under the threat of forced displacement, and Deir al Balah in the Gaza strip.All targeted Bedouin communities fall within the 46 Bedouin Communities at Risk of Forcible Transfer in the Central West Bank. ••Specific indicators: •2 reports and factsheets on IHL and IHRL violations, conflict-related violence and relevant trends (Right to health) • 3,775 children affected by conflict related violence benefiting from structured psychosocial services and child protection interventions • 100 children affected by conflict related violence benefiting from specialised individual case management • 625 vulnerable men and women affected by conflict related violence benefiting from structured psychosocial services • 1 duty bearer’s field visit carried out to the area of intervention to witness IHL violations, during the project implementation (12- month). • 2 articles published on the media and social networks, during the project implementation (12- month). • 378 children aged 0 – 6 years in the West Bank (200 boys and 178 girls) have accessed to an Early Stimulation Program, during the project implementation (12- month). • 60 child protection workers (women and men) trained on guidelines on mental health and psychosocial support • 130 families in the West Bank have participated in psychosocial education sessions (90% women and 10% men) to strengthen Parent-Child Relationship during the project implementation (12- month). • 8 focus group and need assessments (4 in the West Bank and 4 in Middle Area and Gaza Governorates) with at least 6 people each (approx. 40% women and 60% men) carried out during the project implementation (12- month). • 1 report about public and private organizations in the area of intervention in the Gaza Strip that work with children and youth in the field of mental health; will be digitally distributed among the different child and youth mental health stakeholders in Gaza. • 3 group working sessions developed to identify, needs and challenges; and lessons learned of MHSPSS interventions with children and youth in emergencies carried out during the project implementation (12- month) with a participation of approximately 50% men and 50% women • 60 service providers (primary care, specialized care, hospitals, NGOs and educational centres, at least 50% women) both in Gaza and the West Bank have been trained to provide an adequate response to the situations caused by conflict related violence during the project implementation (12- month). • 100% of the interventions documented and reported to the relevant UN Agencies and Cluster system, during the project implementation (12- month). • 10 GBV survivors (women, girls, men and boys) receiving MHPSS services • 60 front line GBV staff (women and men) who received training in detection, treatment and referral • 15 humanitarian staff (non-GBV specialists) who have received training in initial detection and referral • 15 Awareness raising for humanitarian actors (non-GBV specialist) (Remedial) • At least 60% increase in knowledge through trainings (pre-post). • At least 70% satisfaction with services delivered.The action is in line with the Sustainable Development Goals, specifically contributing with Goals 3 and 5. M&E: The Evaluation of the overall Intervention will be done through quantitative data collection (Technical surveys) and qualitative data (through Focus groups and individual interviews).

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

MdM adopts an approach centred in the community and people affected by the crisis, and the response is always adapted to the specific needs, capacities and priorities of the people.MdM has local staff in the field and is part of several networks with local and international organizations, as well as coordination mechanisms and diplomatic fora to ensure a good understanding of the context of the operations.Moreover, information on the interaction of MdM actions and the contexts is gathered in several ways to be able to anticipate and correct negative impacts and maximize the positive ones. In particular, relations with systems and institutions, attitudes, actions, values and interests are analyzed and integrated in the strategic and operational levels.M&E tools are in place to follow up on the effectiveness and efficiency, and to inform accountability, both to the local communities, authorities, donors and the international community through various tailored means, including informative briefings, factsheets, website/social media, and annual reports.The project is based on the needs identified in a participatory way, and integrating the humanity principle (action focusing in the most vulnerable groups, differentiating capacities and vulnerabilities of the different groups, including age and gender, and engaging with total respect for cultural particularities). MdM incorporates the Neutrality principle while at the same time raises its voice in face of human rights violations, operating independently from any political, economic, or military power. MdM incorporates advocacy components addressing root causes of humanitarian needs.All MdM Staff are subject to a Code of Conduct, a Protocol for Sexual Harassment and Gender and Gender Identity Harassment, an Anti-Fraud protocol, and to Humanitarian Chart. Moreover, continuous training and self reflection sessions are in place for all MdM staff in relation to Core humanitarian standards, Gender perspective and diversity, Gender Based Violence, and Negotiation.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In direct interventions in MHPSS impartial assistance is ensured via enhancing transparency and proactively providing information to all the population, ensuring availability of safe, confidential and private services, and by approaching people with reduced mobility at the household level.In capacity building, all professionals in the area of intervention participate. If needed, transportation means are facilitated. In all trainings, specific time is dedicated to debate and reflect on equality and impartial access of populations, specially considering financial, cultural, gender and gender identity constraints. Efforts to combat stigma in MH are done at community, health and education levels.
