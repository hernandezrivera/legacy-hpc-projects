Project Summary

This project is the WASH component of the LHF-funded food security and nutrition project entitled “Food security for 665 migrant households and nutrition prevention for 1000 migrant children in Beirut and Mount Lebanon”, under the 2021 first standard allocation, with a WASH budget of 37% of the total budget, which comes to $287,874.
This project will target 665 vulnerable migrant households (2660 people) in Beirut and Mount Lebanon with 6 months-worth of hygiene materials; and for all households with babies, 6 months-worth of baby care materials alongside balanced and nutritious food (both dry rations and fresh vegetables – see food security sector) and nutrition education (see nutrition sector). All recipients will also receive multi-sector messaging translated into languages appropriate for migrant workers in Lebanon (Amharic, Bengali, Singhalese, French, English).
The project will work with members of three Migrant-Led Initiatives (Mesewet, Together Forever and AUCL) and focus on building their capacity in warehouse management, distributions, M&E, accountability to affected populations and referrals; ensuring benefits that last beyond the duration of the project.
As there are no migrants aged 60+ years this project will not distribute elderly kits.
IEC materials for translation and dissemination will include hygiene, sexual and reproductive health, vaccination, PSEA and nutrition awareness materials.
This project is embedded within Concern’s strategic approach to working with migrant workers in Greater Beirut, which combines addressing immediate and basic needs with long term efforts to address the underlying and structural causes of migrant vulnerability, and works with and through migrant-led initiatives in a migrant-community based, bottom-up approach

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

In conjunction with food security sector activities; Identification and verification of vulnerability of beneficiaries including determining numbers of babies at different ages for procurement of diapers to the right size; define menstrual hygiene management materials preferences for adapting hygiene kits; warehouse operation by MLI members for receiving hygiene/baby materials and packing to household kits based on preferences and household sizes; door-to-door distributions by MLI members on weekends over a 1 month period (distributing 6-months-worth of materials in one go); translation and dissemination of IEC materials to all households; post-distribution monitoring; identification of additional protection concerns and referrals to appropriate service providers; dietary diversity assessments (pre- and post-distributions); complaints response mechanism

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Concern globally adheres to its internal standards for protection, with policies in place. The majority of interaction between project staff and beneficiaries will be through members of 3 migrant-led initiatives. This ensures that barriers related to trust, language and culture are minimised, and close working relationships forged. Distribution teams will always distribute to the same households, forging close personal relationships to facilitate the identification and referral of protection concerns.Migrant-led initiative members will receive safe identification and referral protection training, including PSEA, from Concern's Senior Protection Officer.Concern's Migrant Protection Team maintains a service map and close relationships with service providers. Concern's referrals procedures follow all inter-agency guidelines. Concern's Outreach Officers will assist the migrant-led initiative members in making referrals, receiving feedback and addressing complaints.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Concern globally adheres to internal policies on safeguarding. Concern Lebanon has an established Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) to ensure accountability towards all beneficiaries. It collects any complaints and feedback from beneficiaries and ensure genuine beneficiaries’ complaints are addressed.Dedicated communication lines are managed by Concern staff to receive concerns and provide responses on timely basis. Projectparticipants are also encouraged to give verbal feedback, especially through Concern’s outreach officers who, as members of migrant communities themselves, are an additional mechanism of cross-community communication
