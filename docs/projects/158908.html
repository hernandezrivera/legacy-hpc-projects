Project Summary

Project’s Multiyear Objective: Ensure access to basic education of vulnerable 6 to 12 years old girls and boys in the Gaza Strip.
2018-2020 HRP Strategic Objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
Education Cluster Objective 1: Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children.
Education Cluster activities: Provision of emergency education supplies to vulnerable and unserved students and teachers.
2018-2020 HRP Strategic Objective 3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
Education Cluster Objective 3: Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs, and school-based psychosocial services.
Education Cluster activity: Provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff and students affected by the education-related violations; provision of remedial education to vulnerable school-aged children.

1 - Needs

The recurrent outbreaks of violent conflicts and the ongoing blockade continue to pose a serious threat to life, liberty and security of affected all 480,013basic, secondary and kindergarten students in the Gaza Strip as follows the Education Cluster and OCHA 2018assessment. As per HNO, armed conflict, military incursions and violent manifestations like Great March of Return and the resulting damage and destruction of facilities and property(incl.IDP shelters) disrupted educational services and impacted psychosocial wellbeing of children and teachers, adding to deteriorating living conditions, unpaid teacher salaries, lack of school budgets and uncertainty about UNRWA education provision. The closure of the Gaza Strip, combined with the trauma of wars 2008,2009,2012and2014add to continuous power outages and electricity rationing, poor shelter living conditions, economic deprivation, psycho socia impacts from the escalations, overcrowding, limited hours of instruction due to schools double or triple shifts, and overloaded and unpaid teachers – which all affect educational completion rates and increase dropout. Overcrowding due to severe restrictions on the construction and upgrading of educational infrastructure was already a serious challenge for Gaza’s schools prior to2014conflict. The already overstretched capacity of the education sector greatly suffering from a shortage of schools (running on double and triple shifts) and KGs, both with limited resources and overwhelmed staff, was further exacerbated by2014conflict that affected 462,770students at that time (basic, secondary, KGs) both girls and boys. The effects continue till today when of particular concern are students in the North Directorate, East Gaza Directorate and East Khan Younes Directorate, as well as 5,239 students attending the schools in ARAs. 67localities have no schools and46localities have no kindergartens,76.7%lack access to education support services,93%lack access to children friendly spaces(IRPAL Vulnerability2018). Education Cluster identified 149,878girls and 153,729boys with 12,619education staff adults are in need of school-based psychosocial support, 241,554girls and 238,459boys in need of emergency education supplies and 12,121girls and 12,268boys need remedial education to prevent dropout and ensure access to education. Assessments show that lack of PSS, among other factors, undermines students’ learning ability(SYFS2018). Children are exposed to range of protection concerns including exposure to violence at home, school, and community, impacted parental capacity, eroding community cohesion, poverty(CP HNO2019, CRS2016) resulting in increased negative coping mechanisms, including excessive disciplinary measures by parents, caregivers and teachers, child marriage (mainly affecting girls) and child labor (mainly affecting boys)(CP HNO2019) which additionally contribute to dropout. Child labor and domestic violence against women are common(IRPAL Vulnerability2018). Women and girls do not benefit equally from humanitarian response, protection and access to services, while carrying main responsibilities for caring for children within families while being distressed themselves, and therefore continue to be a key priority groups(Protection GAM2019). Recommended intervention providing material support, school-based psychosocial support and remedial education, all adequately tailored to age and gender of students, came from students, teachers and caregivers during SYFS work with communities, schools and KGs. Women, men girls and boys from the communities in different parts of the Gaza Strip, and especially in East Gaza, East Khan Younes and North Gaza, asked SYFS to act as intermediary towards the donors to fund the urgent need for such activities for children in these communities. Thus the proposed intervention for 1,500girls and 1,500boys of 5 to 12years old, 3,000women caregivers and 200teachers (150women and 50men), including those affected in/by GM

2 - Activities

Selection of the target group takes place based on cross checking SYFS schools information with assessments of other organizations and in coordination with Education Cluster and CPWG to avoid duplication, as well as through community engagement of women and men caregivers, teachers and relevant education staff. According to all assessments the priority for intervention are the schools, both UNRWA and MoEHE, located in border areas as mostly affected by the crisis, particularly East Gaza, East Khan Younis and North Gaza, including ARAs (however, on case by case basis, other schools underserved in terms of similar services are also identified and targeted), as well as children affected in the Great March of Return. All activities include involvement of professional Case Managers who identify cases for further follow-up case management. In case need of further specialized assistance (e.g. psychological, GBV, child labor, child marriage) is identified, they will be referred for specialized assistance to existing programs providing such help, based on existing cooperation with other organizations specialized in this domain, including through CPWG. Activity1: Conduct 450school-based psychosocial support sessions through arts to6to12years old girls and boys in schools in the targeted areas (HEART methodology with use of diverse arts techniques such as drawing, painting, drama, music etc. to name just a few, all designed to age and gender of the children; identified cases followed up by Case Managers). Output1:3000children6to12years old, 50%girls and50%boys, provided with school-based psychosocial support sessions through arts. All identified cases followed up by Case Managers. Activity2: Provide psychosocial support to women caregivers through150theatre shows with nonviolence content followed by group discussion with specialist addressing their concerns (methodology used by SYFS in previous crisis in Gaza that has been highly appreciated by women for facilitating opening of difficult topics while also offering group support and recommended for further use in the future; identified cases followed up by Case Managers). Output2:3000women caregivers provided with psychosocial support through theater shows with nonviolence content followed by group discussion with specialist addressing their concerns. All identified cases followed up by Case Managers. Activity3: Provision of psychosocial support combined with specialized training on child rights, nonviolent learning environment and identification of children who are subject to violence to teachers and educational staff in schools. Output3:200teachers and educational staff in schools provided with psychosocial support and training on child rights and nonviolence (depending on the gender distribution of this group, at least75%women). Activity4: Provide remedial education in English, Arabic, and Math to school children with distress symptoms and/or learning difficulties for the duration of two school semesters. Output4:1000children with distress symptoms and/or learning difficulties,6to12years old, 50%girls and50%boys, provided with remedial education or learning through playing skills enhancement activities Activity5: Provide school uniforms, winter clothes, school bags and stationary and learning materials for2semesters to children with distress symptoms and/or learning difficulties. Output5:1000children with distress symptoms and/or learning difficulties,6to12years old, 50%girls&50%boys, provided with school uniforms, winter clothes school bags, stationary and learning materials

SYFS uses an integrated, systematic approach to monitoring and evaluation: identifying, capturing, evaluating, managing and sharing all program knowledge through periodic tracking of outputs and outcomes and dissemination of regular reports. In the starting phase of the project the baseline data will be collected against which progress will be tracked, as well as specify data sources and collection methods and schedules, explain limitations and quality protocols, and describe evaluation methods. Indicators will be quantitative measures that can be tracked and standardized. The project staff will also employ qualitative methods as appropriate in order to capture the impact of the project. Sex and gender disaggregated data collection includes review and analysis of project documents, structured and semi structured surveys, focus groups, interviews with beneficiaries and relevant stakeholders (women, men, girls, boys), on site visits and observation. Ongoing feedback from beneficiaries is regularly reviewed during weekly staff meetings to address any arising issues, including protection, and to assure the accountability to the affected and targeted population. Following the baseline, activity/output data are collected monthly, entered into monitoring database, internally assessed against relevant benchmarks and compiled into reports used to inform project management and donor. At the end of the project external evaluation is conducted to explore the extent to which results are achieved and the underlying reasons. It assesses the achievements of the project, as well as implementation process and management. It is also used to identify best practices and lessons learned for future relevant programming. Findings from such evaluation are shared with donors and relevant stakeholders.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention avoids exposing people to further harm as a result of the action through a safe selection of the intervention locations, ensuring that the beneficiaries will not be exposed to additional harm. In the case of escalation at the security level (internal clashes or military strikes), the activities will be postponed until the situation becomes stable. The nature of activities implemented are educational, recreational, psychosocial and referral support for most prone children to dropping out, teachers and caregivers who are very vulnerable. Target groups are consulted at the level of intervention design, implementation and end where a midterm review will take place within the project intervention and final term review will be consolidated and shared with all project populations whereupon need corrective measures will be taken. Targeting parents specifically women will be reached in suitable spaces in their areas, where easy access to both genders and people with disabilities to activity locations. The information will be managed and handled in a sensitive matter with restrictive measures on sharing information with stakeholders. Psychosocial and referral services will be according to the good practices and referrals across child protection partners and family centers. The intervention is based on the human rights approach, thus takes into account the rights of the beneficiaries and promote it. Access to education will be addressed with caregivers and teachers with protection mainstreaming with the activities delivered to children and parents. The targeted population will be given a choice to choose between the items, such as the winter uniforms where a small scale shopping events (free of charge) will be conducted so that the children jointly with their parents select the school stationery and the uniforms will be conducted jointly with intervention staff and volunteers

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The intervention addresses groups who are constantly facing financial and psychological issues, retainment to education facilities is the prominent area of support within the intervention where the primary target group is children. Conducive elements of support are to address women being educators in the school facilities or caregivers in their role in the family, the center location of the dominant support is school and surrounding community. Children who will integrate with the additional support of the project will be targeted in the remedial and recreational support alongside with psychosocial support and referral. The psycho-social support and referral services within the intervention are designed to tackle the most in need while letting no child behind within the category of support. Stationery and uniform support will be conducted at facilities outside the schools where families and children will participate and identify the needs they want within the intervention where specific measures will be taken through the pre-assessment to include all persons and children with disabilities with equipped staff who can deal with different types of limitations/disabilities. Assessment of the target group will be fully managed by the SYFS system-online to verify and crosscheck the information of beneficiaries and manage their distribution times. Implementation of HEART manual (Healing and Education through Arts) as a means of communicating educational aspects of the project alongside catch-up and active learning modules to enhance their opportunities to education and means to obtain them.
