Project Summary

The Syrian conflict has caused massive displacement within Syria as well as to neighboring countries and beyond, with a large number of IDPs experiencing multiple displacement. As the crisis continues, the movement of large parts of Syria’s population has not only affected the situation of those displaced but also of those hosting them.
As a result of the decrease of conflict intensity in some areas, the return movements was noticeable, even despite the destruction of homes, infrastructure and the lack of minimum living conditions.
The project is considered a continuation of projects that have been or are being implemented in the area by the Trust due to the high severity of needs in Raqqa, Rural Damascus and Dier Al-Zor. This is because they are rated as the most governorates which have been prone damages and deterioration. The project aims to rehabilitate damaged houses in order to assist the maximum number of beneficiaries to return to their homes, enhance their living conditions, and provide a secure accommodation to the local community and returnees.
The project will be able to target a total of 3,750 beneficiaries in all 3 locations.

Needs

The rate of displacement in Syria is a huge threat to achieving its desired recovery. However, the fact that huge efforts are consistently being paid to reduce it, is well observed. The Syria Trust for Development has collaborated up with numerous UN agencies and INGOs in order to provide and supply shelters to benefit returnees, IDPs, and host communities, which were harmed by the conflict. During the period between 2016 to the mid of 2018, the Trust was able to help 10622 PIN across Syria to return to their original homes.This has gained Syria Trust the experience and knowledge needed to accomplish the rehabilitation of partially damaged houses in several governorates (Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Rural Aleppo, Deir-ez-zor, and Raqqa). It has become well known of its surroundings (concerned authorities and requirements, various humanitarian actors, variation of procedures regarding obtaining approvals,….etc). Nevertheless, the project is considered a continuation of numerous projects that have been or are being implemented in the previously mentioned governorates.Regarding Deir-ez-zor, and after several years of conflict, in 2017 The Syria Trust for Development was the first responder there to provide support through Legal aid. Nevertheless, the ongoing rehabilitation project of 100 damaged apartments in collaboration with UNHCR. Both interventions helped the team to gain the required experience and knowledge about the area. Rehabilitation of partially damaged houses in Raqqa, Rural Damascus, and Deir-ez-zor is crucial due to the huge number of people in need (PiN) in all of them; It is stated in the HNO 2019 that the city of Deir-ez-zorincludes 191.121 PIN, 184.476 in Raqqa, and 533.959 in Rural Damascus. As a result, Syria Trust has conducted site visits to the project locations and carried out various assessments with the objective to assess and evaluate possible shelter interventions.According to the assessments made, In Deir-Ez-zor, 70% of its space is severely damaged which is the main reason why the city has been targeted by various WASH and shelter projects to enhance its infrastructure and encourage the comeback of returnees. Whilst regarding Raqqa- Maadan, it is one of the safest cities in Rural Raqqa which lead to large numbers of returnees taking Maadan as their asylum place. It is worth mentioning that in both Deir-ez-zor and Raqqa, people face huge difficulties in repairing their homes, this is due to mainly and significantly inability to afford the materials and/or professional assistance required to carry out the rehabilitation, and also due to lack of skilled labor.As for Rural Damascus, numerous projects has been and will be implemented, which wouldn't be enough to cover the increasing needs entirely as the soaring damage scale is well observed to us and many other humanitarian actors. These needs are illustrated in the large number of people who are in acute need to return to their original homes so the burden of over capacity will be alleviated from Damascus City. This project will contribute to the support and protection of beneficiaries, through damaged house rehabilitation enhancing living condition for the returnees, IDPs, and host communities of Rural Damascus, Raqqa, and Deir-ez-zor.

Implementation of activities

This project will be divided into several stages as follows:1. Obtaining all necessary approvals from the local administration2. The Trust team will be responsible for Measuring and adjusting sizes3. collecting ownership legal documents for each house4. obtaining the required approvals on the collected names (owners of houses/ apartments)5. tender announcement, and then bids analysis6. signing contracts with the chosen/winning/ contractors7. implementation by contractors with a continuous supervision of the Trust team in each locationAfter finalizing the work inside houses, site hand-over will be carried out with a written proof to concerned authorities as the governorate and the municipality will be fully responsible of housing process of returnees and host communities in the rehabilitated houses.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Community tension due to houses selection process leading to a feeling of discrimination.Mitigation 1: Clarify the criteria and procedures for choosing houses accordingly to ensure beneficiary selection criteria is uniform/agreed among sector members and with the government. Raise awareness about HLP through engaging with community leaders taking into account not to evacuate any families from homes, while maintain cooperation with shelter sector members and all operating actors to find solutions to HLP issues with respect to people right of having a home. Establish complaint mechanism at community level, conduct training to operating staffs. Risk 2: Security stability in the area.Mitigation 2: There are no major disruptions in the current stability of the area, besides a security approval before and during the project will be obtained. Risk 3: Lack of required documentation (property or / and civil documentation) to prove property/occupancy rights.Mitigation 3: The Trust will:- Work together with MoLAE to ensure that alternative documents to ownership are respected by all governorates.- Share the list of alternative documents to prove ownership with all partners. Work together with the local authorities, partners and communities to establish a mechanism to deal with such issues at local level. Work on referring the beneficiaries to benefit from Trust legal assistance. Risk 4: The risk of communities being physically harmed due to structural integrity of the building and/or the presence of ERWs in or in close proximity to work sites. Mitigation 4: The Trust will:- conduct structural assessment of buildings by qualified teams.- Start the rehabilitation works after ensuring that the building is structurally safe.- Make sure that the project area is free from ERW before implementing any rehabilitation works, through coordination with local authorities, communities and agencies involved in ERW clearance.- Orient contractors/workers on risks associated with the presence of ERW and ask them to report immediately if suspicious object is found and suspend the work until suspected object is removed safely.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Rainfall is likely during the implementation process and thus impede the progress of project implementation.Mitigation 1: Rehabilitation will begin during the period from May until the end of the year. Risk 2: Delay in obtaining the the needed approvals form concerned authorities.Mitigation 1: maintain constant coordination and communication with relevant authorities to obtain the needed approvals on time. Risk 3: Shortage of materials in the locations or nonavailability of materials consistently.mitigation 3: securing a warehouse in the area to ensure materials availability for 10 days in advance especially in Dier Al-Zor & Al-Raqqa. Risk 4: Duplication in the the beneficiaries names whose homes will be rehabilitated through other projects in the same areaMitigation 3: Coordination with humanitarian actors working in the same area and municipalities to make sure no duplication in services occurs.
