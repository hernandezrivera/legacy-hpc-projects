Project Summary

This project aims to provide integrated health care and rehabilitation services to 1000 patients, elderly clients
and persons with physical disabilities it will also offer education and awareness to 20,000 persons in community about COVID_19.
Services will be provided within 12 months with full coordination of health providers in the governmental and non-governmental sectors.
This project’s services are divided into two parts according to the patient’s needs and place of service:
First is outreach program for 600 patients, elderly and persons with disabilities, whose names will be nominated by Ministry of Health in addition to 1200 family members who will receive education and awareness , (three home visits per week per patient).
This project will result in a total of 4,200.0 home visits throughout the project duration.
The services included are medical and evaluation visits, study of patient’s needs to adapt their environment, medical examination and follow-up, physical and occupational therapy sessions, psychological and social support, and nursing care like diabetes foot and awareness.
If a patient needs to stay in the hospital, they will be transferred to El-Wafa Hospital to complete the treatment plan and rehabilitation (expected number of admissions 100 client for a length of stay for average 3 days per patient)
Second, 400 patients, elderly and persons with disabilities along with 800 of their families will receive medical and guidance services at the hospital mainly in the out-patient clinic.
The project will give 8 appointments per case per month. This will result to a total number of 4000 hospital visits throughout the project duration.
Health and Awareness services will be provided along with physical and occupational therapy, nursing care during their stay in the hospital.
In addition, they will be given hygiene kits and healthy nutritious meals that are high in protein, calcium, vitamins to Improve internal immunity to avoid Covid 19 the risks.
The project is designed to provide us with:
1 - Medicines and medical supplies needed to heal wounds and assist in the rehabilitation process.
2 - Medical aids, hygiene kits and assistive devises
3 - Fuel to operate the generator and Ambulance.
4 - Emergency medical equipment.
5- Healthy foods high in protein and calcium vitamins.
6- Specialized and experienced medical and technical cadres (4 doctors,4 physiotherapists, 6 nurses, 4 occupational therapists, 3 psychologists and social workers, and 1 laboratory tests)

1 - Needs

The 2021 forecast for the Gaza Strip warns that challenges including poverty and health insecurity will only intensify. Gaza Strip has been under health blockade since June 2006. It's health services are severely crippled in the longer term under restrictions of the access to movement resources. COVID-19 epidemic put further strains on an already fragile healthcare system in the occupied Palestinian territory (OPt). During COVID-19 epidemic, the essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities will be interrupted.Poverty and older age increase the risk of infection by the virus and the probability of death increases as well.According to the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), 5% of the population in OPTs are elders and 4% of them live in the Gaza Strip, 46% of them are with disabilities and 47 % are below the poverty line.Moreover, Gaza Strip has been besieged by the Israeli occupational authorities and also in a political division for the last 14 years. This led to great deficiency in providing medical and healthcare services to many in need, according to WHO Gaza office.According to the health cluster prediction of 2021, the elderly and people with disabilities (PwD) are at high risk of morbidity and mortality.According to the 2017 statistics from the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS)1 released in July 2020, the number of persons with disabilities was 255,228. This constitutes 5.8% of the total population in Palestine; 127,266 in the West Bank 5.1% and 127,962 in the Gaza Strip 6.8%. The disability rate among males was 6.3% and 5.4% among females, and mobility difficulties are the most common type of difficulties, which reaching 32.2% with a prevalence of 2.9% among the population. In this context, persons with disabilities in Palestine, particularly in Gaza Strip, face number of challenges in acquiring basic human rights, obtaining basic services such as health, safe access to services and information, and poor access for persons with disabilities in the public and the governmental services centers.According to the Ministry of Health data until 14 October, the total number of positive cases (Covid19) reached 4285, of which 1737 are active cases. According to the Ministry of Social Development data, the number of quarantined persons in Gaza Strip reached 1649 of whom 34% were women, 24% were children, and the number of quarantined persons with disabilities was 15, including 6 women and 9 men. In Gaza Strip, there are 15 quarantine centers, most of which do not provide the necessary facilities for persons with disabilities.After the President declared the state of emergency in Palestine, most of the services provided closely (face to face) for persons with disabilities stopped.The conditions and needs of persons with disabilities were not taken into consideration in decisions and implementation procedures. Persons with disabilities were left behind in a flagrant violation of the Sustainable Development Goals 2030 and the International Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities.The absence of awareness issued by official and non-official bodies related to harmonization of information (numbersand advice that were directed to the general public) to persons with disabilities, which in turn contributed to the absence of persons with disabilities from the scene and isolation of the surrounding community. limited our capability to extend our hands to more PWD. this proposal will enable us to reach clients in their houses as we will hire more staff and procure more medical supplies and assistive devices through which we will expand our entire services. All these conditions lead to an increase in the chance of the spread of the Coronavirus, which increases the risk to the lives of the elders.WCS, with its 40 years of experience as the only free elderly nursing home in Gaza, and its medical rehabilitation hospital writes this proposal

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

At all stages of the Health Project Cycle management : Diagnosis, Implementation and M&E. Protection mainstreaming is a cross cutting theme for Elwafa programs and projects. Elwafa understand and fully adhere to the core principle of “Do No Harm” as a humanitarian imperative, a cornerstone in all programmes followed by the pyramid of IASC minimum standards in regards to MHPSS. All both organizations remain vigilant throughout project implementation to identify and prevent unintended negative consequences of their actions. In this regard, Elwafa and here partners are adopting a number of steps to ensure that its interventions maximize positive impacts for community stability, mainly by strengthening their capacities and resilience, while minimizing harm. Moreover, in order to further enhance protection mainstreaming the project will ensure that health staff will be capacitated on the following 4 topics: - MHPSS IASC minimum standards: Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings (WHO guidelines) - GBV: Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-based Violence in Emergencies (following UNFPA standards) - Child Protection Minimum standards in Humanitarian action (following Unicef and Sphere guidelines) -Minimum Standards for Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (following Sphere standards and MdM guidelines) - Disability mainstreaming and health: CBR(Community based rehabilitation guidelines) health component of WHO.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Elwafa bases its intervention in ethical and humanitarian principles. This project employed a principalist approach that is widely accepted in the humanitarian community through such vehicles as the SPHERE Humanitarian Charter. Ethical principles in health care include respect for persons to advance human dignity and for individuals’ autonomous choices; beneficence, the promotion of others’ well being, commonly known as “do no harm,” and justice, encompassing both the fair distribution of resources and fair processes for decision-making. Humanitarian principles is the a second source of values and norms that animate all Elwafa actions. HP include humanity, requiring that suffering must be addressed wherever and for whomever it is found; neutrality, the duty of humanitarian actors not to take sides in a conflict; impartiality, which stipulates that humanitarian actors must not discriminate or give preference to any nationality, race, religious belief, class, political opinion, or similar status; and independence, which demands that humanitarian actors retain their autonomy and remain independent of political or military objectives of other actors.
