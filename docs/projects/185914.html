Project Summary

Since April 2020, AAH delivers health, nutrition and MHPSS activities through mobile clinics in Meme Division. This current project aims to meet the urgent health needs in the increasingly unstable southwest region of Cameroon, through the provision of emergency primary health care (PHC) services integrating MHPSS to populations affected by the crisis and particularly children under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLW)
Through this project, AAH aims to consolidate the achievements of previous projects by taking into account experiences to adapt the approach. A mobile clinic will be deployed in Meme's division providing primary health care to beneficiaries, with a focus on children under 5, PLW, but is also delivering services to adults for most common pathologies. AAH integrates into its intervention support for functional private structures and the implementation of a community approach through identification and training of CHWs and thus hope to perpetuate the activities of the project.
At the MC site, all children aged 0 to 5 years, PLW and adults (malaria, STD and IRA) will be offered free medical services. All beneficiaries will benefit from medical and preventive consultations by qualified medical staff. The staff will provide treatment for common childhood illnesses using national guidelines / and standardized IMCI protocols. The immunization status, especially of children requiring measles vaccination, will be assessed.
Additionally, as preventive consultations, children will be growth monitored, screened for malnutrition using MUAC (6-59 months), weight, size and oedemia, dewormed (12-59 months) and provided with vitamin A supplementation (6-59 months). Children (0-23 months of age) will also benefit from IYCF counselling provided to their mothers and caregivers.
The curative consultations component for children over 5 years old and adults will be added at the MC level. These consultations will be linked to the management of the most common diseases (malaria, respiratory infection, skin diseases), including malaria treatment according to the national protocol for adults.
The targeted communities and this age group will be informed to the fact that all chronic diseases will not be managed but if potential sick patients visit AAH MC, they will be referred to the appropriate structures. The medicines used will also be essential medicines according to national pharmaceutical guidelines for the level of care as a mobile clinic at the community level.
In regards to people leaving with disabilities (PLWD), AAH will offer the same package of services. General support will be provided with sensorial impairments and other disabilities, as well as orientation to specialized services, but no specific treatments will be provided. The mobile clinic will also be an opportunity to provide sensitization about various topics (common hygiene and nutritious messages, SGBV awareness, COVID preventive measures...).
At last, a systematic referral will be implemented for mental health identified patients at the mobile clinic level.
The package of care will also include medical and MHPSS care for GBV victims (including rape clinical case management), especially women and children, and referral to appropriate facilities for those whose post-rape condition requires further in-depth care, including, for example, social reintegration.
This project will target all people affected by the crisis: men, women, PLWs, children aged 0-5 and above, adolescents, IDPs, host populations, people living with a disability, the elderly, health staff from targeted private health centers and community actors.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the SW region, violence between Government forces and separatist armed movements has erupted predominantly along the main road axes and, in most cases, forced the local population to flee for their safety either deep into the surrounding forest or to larger urban centres under Government control, such as Buea and Kumba. Just like in most conflict-induced crises, children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) are among the hardest-hit. Despite difficulties in accessing nutritional data status within the region the growing challenges in accessing food, combined with poor hygienic and sanitation conditions (especially in deep-field locations), are expected to lead to a deterioration of the nutritional status among U5 children. Moreover, given the daunting access constraints faced by NGOs, the possibility that actual SAM rates in H2R areas are considerably higher than those recorded so far cannot be ruled out. In addition, the ongoing conflict is also likely to have a detrimental effect not only on children’s psychological conditions, but also on their caregivers’ mental wellbeing. Violence and ensuring displacement impact social fabric and weaken resilience at individual, family and community levels. The loss of property and employment, along with the financial hardship, generates psychological distress, which in its turn has detrimental effects on parent’s childcare practices and, as a consequence, on children development. Comprehensive care addressing both the physical and psychological dimensions of child development is therefore essential to address effectively malnutrition. Violence and instability in the region further affect the access and quality of basic social services, including due to the destruction of infrastructure and the lack of physical access. This makes it difficult or impossible to address issues of violence and trauma caused by conflict. Mapping and referrals mechanisms with specific actors have been strengthen throughout 2021. It helped us in covering specific needs that occurred during our interventions. This is especially relevant regarding child protection and GBV case management.MHCP teams will provide psychoeducation sessions for the most vulnerable children over 6 years and adults where they would be able to share their stress levels, their experiences with traumatic events in order to facilitate the understanding of normal reaction to stressful situations and to help people to feel more comfortable and to have more reassurance about their feelings.MHCP teams will offer group psychological support for people in psychological distress depending on the level of psychological distress. The approach will take into account the local culture and context. Gender exclusive group can be offered, as some participants might not feel comfortable in sharing personal experiences in front of the opposite sex. The proposed psychosocial support aims to strengthen individual and collective resilience to crisis context. Given the specific target groups of AAH’s activities, the existing mobile clinic teams comprise mostly of female staff to ensure potential beneficiaries feel comfortable accessing medical services provided in the field, first sexual and reproductive health services. Moreover, despite the challenging settings the mobile clinic teams operate in, equipment to enhance privacy are being procured. A gender-sensitive approach will also inform as much as possible the recruitment of community health volunteers and community mobilizers in the early stage of the project. Communities will be requested to ensure an adequate quota of female candidates be nominated for vetting by AAH. Recruited volunteers and mobilizers will then be inducted to the AAH Code of Conduct and PSEA Policy, placing great emphasis on the non-discriminatory and non-exploitative nature of aid. During our activities, specific attention will be paid to young people accompanying children, who are often more vulnerable;
