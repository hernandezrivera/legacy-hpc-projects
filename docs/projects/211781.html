Project Summary

The project will ensure availability of GBV services to create a protective and supportive environment for vulnerable groups, including children, youth, adolescent girls, vulnerable women, elderly individuals, and persons with disabilities.
Adolescent girls are particularly exposed to various forms of violence, including early marriage, harassment, sexual abuse, and movement restrictions. Married or pregnant girls and young mothers lack access to education, job opportunities, skills development, and social support networks, increasing their risk of GBV and limiting essential service access. Women's limited access to the labour market, lack of independence, and exposure to intimate partner violence have severe consequences for their physical and mental health, leading to unwanted pregnancies, permanent disabilities, and increased psychological distress. NCA needs assessment showed that adolescents’ girls continue to be exposed to a variety of risks and forms of violence and their prevalence is reported including early marriage 13%, harassment 12%, and movement restrictions 12%. While women are mainly significantly impacted by sexual violence/abuse at 21%, harassment at 17%, and sexual exploitation at 13%. Both women and girls reported that the perpetrators imposing this risk are mainly intimate partners, and male family members
This will involve providing GBV prevention services through the Family Support Centres, women and girls safe spaces (WGSS) and mobile teams in the 7 governates; Swaida, Daraa, Damascus, Rural Damascus, Homs, Idlib and Aleppo. Aiming at fostering community engagement, challenging harmful practices and attitudes towards GBV, offering survivor-centred case management and support for pregnant and lactating adolescents, and integrating economic empowerment activities into livelihood programs, and Cash in GBV assistance to respond to GBV risks and promote survivors healing and recovery. This emphasizes survivor empowerment and rights, safe referrals to essential services, and coordination to ensure survivors access to opportunities and support. Challenging existing harmful practices and negative attitude towards GBV through outreach and awareness-raising services. These initiatives aim to increase community knowledge and reduce stigma associated with GBV, enhancing the safety, security, and accessibility for GBV survivors.
NCA will implement a training to NCA staff and its implementing partners on (PSHEA) and safeguarding policy in order to ensure ethical, safe and accountable service provision. The capacity building on the Policy will take place to inform all project staff on the code of conduct, reporting, and responding to cases of SEA, including the training on Survivors assistance strategy (with particular focus on GBV case management teams, and relevant referral pathways); complaint report mechanisms will be set up with multiple entry points; PSHEA focal points for each project location will be appointed; awareness raising session and distribution of PSHEA will take place among affected communities (sensitive to age, gender, and cultural context). PSEA messaging and entry points for incident reporting will also be included in all other outreach and IEC materials as well.
PRA:Risk Lack of support from family to allow women and girls access services. Mitigation: To ensure acceptance, communities will be engaged in awareness raising as allies in outreach, promotion of services. Parents of adolescent girls will be targeted (PSS). Risk: PwD meet barriers in accessing GBV services. Mitigation: PwD will be included in outreach and IEC materials, while physical access to WGSSs will be adapted; women and girls with disabilities will participate assessments; staff will be trained to communicate with, identify risks and provide GBV protection services for PwD.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Following to the first part of the project summary Risk: Breach of confidentiality with GBV survivors. Mitigation: lists for distributions will contain only the necessary data for verification. GBV cases data will be collected and stored with privacy and security, following ethical standards for GBV data management. No data will be shared with local authorities or third-party monitoring teams.NCA will inform the beneficiaries and other stakeholders about how to access to safe, confidential, and multiple CRM channels which are designed in consultation with the communities to ensure that the reporting channels are culturally sensitive. These channels will include complaint boxes at easily accessible locations and dedicated emails address to register complaints and face to face sessions between staff and beneficiaries. Each consortium member will maintain an internal database to register and monitor the complaints status in accordance with data safety and confidentiality guidelines. Through the CRM, NCA and partners will be able to identify, and segregate issues related to urgency, location, and type and when issues have been identified related to processes or activity outputs/outcomes, the Consortium Management Unit (CMU) will follow up with each partner to understand the causal factors and confirm the outcome of the concern/issueAs part of Action’s Monitoring and Evaluation system, M&E plan will be developed consolidating information form the logical framework and will include data collection tools, methodologies, data management procedures, frequency and roles and responsibilities identified for the project indicators. MEAL will support with the streamlining of the data collection tools and templates; conduct field monitoring of project activities; support measuring indicators; and undertake data quality reviews including data verifications and data validations. Different types of monitoring activities will be undertaken throughout the lifetime of the project. Specific objective indicators have been selected to assess changes as a result of the project. Additionally, an internal review of the project will be carried out documenting the lessons learned during the intervention. Progress against project results will be tracked monthly. Result monitoring data will include quantitative and qualitative information through direct observation, focus group discussions, document verification and satisfaction surveys. Through (PDM) and (PIM), Case studies and human-interest stories will also be routinely collected in order to document the change process as it happens, these monitoring activities will be checking for any unintended negative impacts and risks of the intervention. It will ensure that potential risks are identified and addressed before becoming a serious threat to the programme and its intended beneficiaries.
