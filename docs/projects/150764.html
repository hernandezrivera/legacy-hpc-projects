Project Summary

The project aims to mitigate, prevent and respond to Gender Based Violence (GBV), supporting GBV survivors and people in risk of GBV living in the eastern area of Khan Younis (including ARA). Increase in tension, violence and casualties related with Great March Return (GMR), serious deterioration of socioeconomic situation and the financial crisis, in addition to the lack of GBV services, aggravate the existing fragile living conditions of the target population and increase on GBV cases.
This project will be implemented on a complementary and coordinated partnership between APS and UHWC both with strong expertise in GVB and solid mutual collaboration. Humanity Inclusion will be a strategic partner to ensure the disability mainstreaming compo-nent throughout the project.
The proposal organises around 3 main components:
1) Providing access to coordinated, safe and confidential GBV multisectoral services in the new “One Stop Center” of Khan Younis, and through a mobile clinic and home visits in the East of Khan Younis (including ARA). Provision of case management and GBV multisectoral response includes: Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services as a needed entry-point for GBV survivors, psycho-social support (PSS) services, emergency health care, SGBV case management, referral, specialized legal assistance and economic enablement (the economic empowerment component will be worked only with a selected group of GBV survivors). Furthermore, the response is complemented with the distribution of dignity kits.
2) GBV awareness-raising workshops and campaigns will be conducted for GBV survivors and people at risk of GBV, CBOs and vari-ous social services platforms (kindergartens, schools, etc.) in East Khan Younis (including ARA).
3) Trainings will be carried out for GBV non-specialized humanitarian actors on GBV detection, first support and referral. The workshop is designed to cover all non-specialized actors working in the Khan Younis area, contributing to the enhancement of community ca-pacities in responding to GBV. This training aims at contributing to the integration of GBV in a more holistic concept of Protection and to ensure those cases detected by CBOs, LNGOs and other non-specialist actors reach the GBV specialized pathways.
The project will provide GBV multisectorial services and awareness-raising sessions predominantly to GBV survivors and people at risk of GBV. This selection criteria will include the following target groups: women and girls impacted by GMR (including women whose husbands have been killed or injured, mothers with injured children and girls who lost a father or who are expected to have a father or caregivers with disability), refugee women and girls, female head households, widows, women and girls with disability(ies) and wom-en cancer survivors. Through the intervention, these women and girls will be able to strengthen their ability to cope in a more positive way, not only with GBV, but also with protracted threats and shocks related to the heavily and permanent exposure to policies and practices related to the blockade and the occupation.
The project is guided by community-based protection approach, survivor-centred approach, right-based approach and do-not-harm approach. It has been designed and formulated mainstreaming gender and diversity (focused on disability). At the beginning of the implementation, Humanity Inclusion will lead workshops on disability and GBV to the project staff in the One Stop Center and the staff of the Health and Community Center in Khan Younis followed by monitoring at mid-term and evaluation at end of the project.
The project is aligned with the priorities set by HNO 2018, specifically in relation to gender and protection concerns. Outputs are designed to meet the three HRP 2018-2020 strategic objectives. It has also been designed in line with IASC standards and the Minimum Standards for Prevention and Response to Gender-Based Violence in Emergencies from UNFPA.

Needs

GBV is a key protection concern in Gaza. It is recognized that GBV in humanitarian settings affects men and women, boys and girls, with and without disabilities differently. The impact of the humanitarian crisis in Gaza - especially marked in recent months by the Great March of Return (GMR), the vulnerable socioeconomic situation, and UNRWA’s current funding crisis - has exacerbated GBV in all its forms (especially domestic violence, psychological/emotional and physical abuse, sexual assault, denial of resources, opportunities and services and forced marriage). All through Gaza this context faces lack of multisectoral protection and availability of response services. In March and October 2018, ApS and UHWC conducted GBV rapids need assessments (RNA) in Gaza´s middle area and in Bani Suhaila (Khan Younis). The RNA carried out FGD with women, men, girls and boys, with and without disabilities, interviews with CBOs and with relevant stakeholders in Khan Younis and document analysis using a qualitative research method. FDG: All people involved identified deteriorating economic conditions, high unemployment rate due to the blockade, GMR and policies of the Occupying Power among GBV main causes. Everyone involved in FGD stressed lack of information on their rights, GBV and the nearest GBV specialized service.Adults highlighted the fragile economic situation and consequent increase in psychological stress as the main threats to family stability as the central cause in both negative changes in family dynamics and increase of GBV. A few men and women identified forced marriage (including child marriage) as a type of GBV, but most of them perceive it as a protection mechanism, especially in cases of food insecurity. On the contrary, FGD carried out with boys and girls highlighted forced marriage as a main cause of GBV. Women emphasized domestic and psychosocial violence, along with physical assaults were the most common types of GBV, related mostly with their intimate partner; pointing out economic dependence as one of the main reasons why women cannot leave the circle of violence. In addition, some women express their concern regarding widowed women (by GMR) forcefully married to their husband´s brothers. Girls emphasized sexual assault in and outside families, behaviour-controlling and rights, opportunities, resources and services denial. Regarding perception of GBV causes, girls mentioned patriarchal culture and lack of electricity, while both adults and boys pointed out occupation, economic situation and high unemployment rate as the main cause. Furthermore, men identified only sexual assault and rape outside the family as a type of GBV. Common views of all participants were the lack of information on their rights, GBV or the nearest GBV specialized services available. Interviews: Although there are no statistics on GBV in Khan Younis and ARA, key actors interviewed believed VAW cases are increasing as a result of emotional and psychological abuse, sexual assault, physical abuse, forced marriage and denial of resources and opportunities. Key national actors in the area indicated that the effects of patriarchal culture are being emphasized by the occupation, economic situation and the GMR culture, which are also main causes of GBV in Gaza. In relation to GBV and disability, the specialized actors point out that women and girls with disabilities in Gaza experience double the discrimination, which places them at a higher risk of GBV, sexual abuse, being neglected, exploitation and harm. Lack of services (including referral) in eastern area Khan Younis and nearby ARA were raised in reference to prevention or response to GBV.While GBV non-specialized actors interviewed (CBO, LNGOs,…) often come across GBV cases (including on women and girls with disabilities), they stressed being unable to provide support as they lack expertise in dealing with such cases and do not know how and which specialized service the person needs to be referred
