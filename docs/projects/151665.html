Project Summary

To ensure access to integrated reproductive health/maternal health services through improving the provision of quality and timely lifesaving SRH services for the crises effected communities in Somalia.
Specific objectives:
a) To ensure availability of quality emergency obstetric and newborn care services for the affected population.
b) To manage and prevent consequences of morbidity and mortality among pregnant and lactating mothers related to low access to maternal nutrition services.

Needs

The country remains one of the largest and most complex emergencies in the world, as it still experiences protracted conflict in addition to natural disasters such as drought, floods and cyclones. A fragmented health service infrastructure is a phenomenon in this resource poor setting country. Somalia is ranked the worst place to be a mother. 1 out of every 22 women in Somalia dies from pregnancy related causes. The Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR) is 732/100 000 live births. The average fertility rate is 6.4 children per woman, which is the fifth highest total fertility rate in the world. The Somali Neonatal Mortality Ratio is estimated at 40 /1,000 live births. Access to maternal health services is still low with up to 33% of births attended by skilled health personnel. Women in Somalia have a high rate of unmet need for family planning at 26%. In 2017, there were 284,000 unintended pregnancies. Mothers die due to lack of access to emergency obstetric care for the timely treatment of the main complications of childbirth; the leading causes of maternal deaths are: anemia, malaria and infections (29%); hemorrhage (25%); hypertension (16%); abortion (10%); and sepsis (10%). UNFPA supports the provision of life-saving services for routine EmONC facility births, emergency cases, referrals and emergency transfers from other health service providers in the region, including within the essential package of health service system (EPHS). As a result, 37,963 safe deliveries were supported. UNFPA is supporting the training of obstetric fistula surgeons as well as service provision. In 2018, 50 cases of obstetric fistula were repaired in Somaliland adding to a total of 609 surgical obstetrical fistula repair since 2014 in Somalia. Excess mortality is driven by malnutrition among pregnant mothers which leads to obstetric complications. UNFPA is engaged on a joint initiative on maternal nutrition which aims to prevent consequences of poor nutritional status of pregnant women.
