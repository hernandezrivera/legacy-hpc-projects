Project Summary

Ongoing armed conflict, intercommunal violence and climate change (drought and flood) continue to be the key driver of humanitarian needs in Ethiopia and increased displacements across the country. Specifically, humanitarian situation in Northern Ethiopia remains challenging due to the conflict in regions of Tigray, Amhara and Afar. In Tigray, 5.2 million people are reported to need urgent humanitarian assistance according to the OCHA Tigray region Humanitarian Update from early September 2021. IOM’s draft Site Assessment (SA) Round 28 identified a total of 4,509,081 internally displaced people (IDPs) displaced across different sites in the country. From this, 2,452,077 are found in Tigray, Amhara and Afar regions.
Affected populations face longer term protection concerns arising from shocks such as conflict, climate change, and disasters. Systemic, long-standing protection concerns such as gender-based violence (GBV) – including harmful practices, lack of access to specialized services, as well as other basic needs are exacerbated due to conflict or climatic shocks.
Often in emergencies, women and children with and without disabilities, constitute majority of affected population with heightened risk to different rights abuse including GBV. With limited access to services such as health and MHPSS, food, WASH, shelter, psychosocial support, education and livelihoods coupled with weak social support system, IDPs are living under undignified and distressed conditions. These factors entail increased vulnerability to engage in negative coping mechanisms such as forced marriages, child labour abuse and transactional sex. Additionally, lack of awareness of the right to assistance and limited coverage of response services, complicated by deep-rooted social norms related to gender make accessing services ever more challenging.
Despite the high number of IDPs, there has been inconsistent humanitarian assistance to the affected population due to limited resource and ongoing security concerns in the regions. Poorly coordinated relocations and returns which fall short of meeting standards also negatively affected the service delivery rendering affected population to secondary displacement and ending up living in collective centres or within host community.
With the goal of contributing to enhancing protection environment of affected populations through prevention, risk mitigation and response efforts. Following the Protection Cluster priorities, IOM will target primarily IDPs and returnees affected by conflict and drought through general protection and GBV prevention and response integrating Mental Health and Psychosocial support (MHPSS) interventions in Amhara, Tigray, Somali, SNNP, Diredawa, Afar and Oromia regions.
The response and activities will be in line with the three overarching protection objectives in the HRP.
1. Mitigate and respond to violence, abuse or neglect and restore capacities: through protection monitoring, individual protection assistance (IPA), including multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) and/or in-kind assistance and community based Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS).
2. GBV response and prevention services: including case management, Psychosocial assistance, set-up of women and girls friendly spaces (WGFS) providing safe entry points for women and girls to access GBV services, and skill-building and awareness and knowledge on GBV and Protection issues.
3. Strengthening capacities of government counterparts, partners, and IOM staff on Protection and GBV service provision

1. - Projects activities

To identify, monitor and mitigate protection risks towards improved programming and evidence-based protection response, IOM will undertake protection monitoring covering sites and in areas of new displacement and returns in conflict and crisis-affected regions of Ethiopia. The intervention will take place through mobile protection teams – integrating monitoring and individual protection and GBV response - in dispersed locations and on-site where sites are easily accessible from IOM’s Sub-Offices. IOM Protection staff will also closely work and coordinate with sectors to undertake sector-specific risk assessments on Protection and GBV – ensuring a principled response that actively understands key risks and devices context-specific mitigation measures in response. By strengthening disability inclusion, IOM will contribute the risks of children becoming caretakers for adults with disabilities is minimised. Disability will also be a key component of these monthly protection monitoring exercises. Further, to improve protection of crisis-affected populations through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions, IOM will scale up its protection response expanding into strengthened community-based protection mechanisms in areas of displacement and returns and ensure that persons at-risk receive the required support at an individual-level through IPA and community-based protection mechanisms including strengthening community based MHPSS activities through individual counselling, group discussions, Psychological First Aid (PFA), socio-cultural and recreational activities.Under objective 2: to set-up of Women and Girl Friendly Spaces and provision of GBV response services, given the significant gaps in GBV response and continued reports of displacement with limited response, IOM proposes to set-up WGFS in areas with static IDP populations, where a mobile modality is more suitable, the response will be integrated with protection monitoring and response mobile teams IOM will aim to provide safe and confidential GBV case management, including ,timely referrals to specialized services including Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and follow-up with inclusion of material and/or cash assistance to survivors and those at-risk of GBV, as a part of the case management process within the affected populations. Integrated with WGFS services and community PSS centers, IOM will provide individual counseling for people with psychological distress and preexisting mental health conditions including referral assistance. Further, to improve awareness amongst communities on Protection and GBV, including service availability, using coffee ceremony settings, IOM will engage communities, particularly, women and girls, in camps and camp-like settings, on building awareness and knowledge about GBV – its forms, consequences and importance of seeking care in a timely manner IOM also coordinates with other protection partners and Organizations of Persons with Disabilities (OPDs) to conduct joint awareness raising and information sessions on child protection, mine risk awareness and rights of person with disabilities in sites and will continue to do so under this activity. People with disabilities with various impairments will be able to receive support to ensure full and meaningful participation that allows for reasonable accommodations, a critical human right which is protected by Article 2 of Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD). IOM also proposes to pilot SASA! Together which is a GBV prevention approach aiming to address root cause of gender inequality and work with affected communities at grassroots level. Given the approach is phase based and takes longer period to implement, IOM will select areas where there is static IDP population. Under Objective 3: IOM will provide capacity building support to its staff, stakeholders and partners on on protection mainstreaming, GBV risk mitigation, disability inclusion.

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Rapid response modalities, such as cash distributions or local procurement have been prioritized in view of the short implementation timeline and feasibility on the ground. Depending on the context and situation IOM will be flexible in using both cash and in-kind modalities to address protection needs. In Tigray region specifically as there is limited cash flow, IOM will prioritize in-kind assistance, in case cash is not feasible, while in other regions where there is limited access to local markets, IOM will prioritize cash support to assist people access services and address protection needs. To ensure protection is mainstreamed across the project, IOM will give due emphasis on conducting protection risk assessments by engaging the affected populations, including people with disabilities to identify possible risks and design mitigation measures to address and reduce the risks.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

IOM works towards delivering assistance and services in an accountable manner that respects and fosters the rights of beneficiaries by taking account of the diversity of communities. Guided by IOM’s global Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) Framework, IOM Ethiopia will continue to mainstream accountability throughout its responses. This helps to ensures that the organization deliver quality and responsive programming in line with the evolving needs of beneficiaries, affected populations and communities and enforce IOM’s zero tolerance against sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) and other misconduct. In line with its commitments to AAP, the mission maintains a participatory approach by involving direct beneficiaries, host communities and other stakeholders such as women and children affair office, organization for persons with disabilities and relevant community level associations throughout the full project cycle, enabling them to influence and shape the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of activities and decision-making processes. It also continuously consults extensively with the local community including women, men, boys and girls, youth, religious and community leaders, minority groups, people with disabilities and local administration to better understand their needs. The mission also recognizes that information sharing, listening to and involving affected community’s decision-making contributes to more effective programmes and improves the quality of IOM service delivery. Accordingly, it will create ways for the affected populations and communities to access relevant information about IOM activities and processes that affect them in a reliable, contextually relevant and timely manner and in line with literacy levels and language diversity. Relevant information, including IOM’s mandate, project beneficiary selection criteria, rights and entitlements, and other topics will be shared with targeted communities, beneficiaries, community leaders, suppliers, contractors and other stakeholders through various channels. The channels include different printed materials (i.e. posters, banners, leaflets), face-to-face interviews and community meetings and different awareness sessions. Following distributions, post distribution assessments will be conducted to assess beneficiary satisfaction and any possible risks encountered because of the support. This will be used as an input to mitigate risks in future distributions.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The mission established diverse and contextually appropriate mechanisms and systems that enable communities to provide feedback on its programmes and submit complaints. The complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFMs) include kebele committees, different community and government representatives, site management committees, community meetings, help desk, focus group discussions and exit interviews. The mission also launched a toll-free hotline to diversify CFM options for the affected population. These CFMs regularly collect feedback from beneficiaries and community members, enabling IOM to respond to the gaps and needs of the affected population and modify its responses in a timely manner. Moreover, the CFMs have a written system (SOPs) and a referral structure with designated tasks and responsibilities within IOM programmes/teams and externally to other partners in place for processing complaints and feedback with corresponding time frames for action. Additionally, IOM has a designated AAP focal person who works together with programme and field staff to facilitate this process. Finally, as part of monitoring and evaluation, overall AAP data will be analyzed periodically to generate lessons learned which will be used to adjust ongoing project implementation and inform the future design of projects.
