Project Summary

South Sudan continue to reel from the impacts of years of conflict, violence and limited in development investment and face a convergence of high needs related to food insecurity, protection, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). According to Humanitarian Need Overview 2023, Over 9 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance which is nearly 80% of the total population and are affected by multiple risks of protracted violent conflicts, climate change causing flooding which resulted in adverse food insecurity
TADO wishes to support the vulnerable and affected populations by targeting of 2,000 beneficiaries (Girls=500, Women=800,Boys=,400 men-300 in Kapoeta East, Uror, Akobo and Nasir Counties) under IPC 3 and 4 according to IPC report of Nov 2024.
TADO will carryout service provision of survivor-centered psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery, through training of women, men, girls and boys with individual, group, specialized PSS like recreational activities-Games , livelihood services-Like Bee farming, Making door mats, Bed sheets and Kitchen Gardens, parenting skills-like child care, children upbringing and sensitivity on child stress, Training of staffs, community/frontline workers trained on Psychosocial support service provision like Counseling, giving referrals, GBV detection and Reporting and help desks services.
TADO will provide appropriate GBV case management services including coordinated care and support to women, men, boys and girls through training community health workers, counselors, establish referral systems, reporting of suspected GBV/Protection cases and feedback mechanisms. Establish and strengthen GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support to women, men, boys and girls through re-enforcing and restructuring the already existing systems.
TADO will work with the community stakeholders, women leaders, faith -based systems and Partners in the ministry of gender, child and social welfare, UNMISS-Rule of Law systems, UNHCR for protection support and NGO Partners-(National and State Protection, GBV,CP,GP-Clusters) for available response services.
TADO will set-up Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services like Information sharing, Counseling, Peace-building at household level, Intimate Violence early detection, Trauma Healing, charcoal saving stove making skills ,Kitchen garden set-up skills, Saving skills, Yoga, this information and activities will promote healing, well-being and empowerment.
TADO will promote legal services that protect the rights of GBV survivors and access to justice through advocacy training of community leaders, Youths groups, women groups on Gender human rights ,Early detection of human Rights, Proper reporting protocols of human rights violation acts and legal aid available services and follow-up on justice. Engage community and carryout awareness activities on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures and on how survivors can access support and services in line with the “do no harm” principle through training of community mobilisers on GBV risk mitigation and prevention measures-like Awareness on Causes of GBV by Intimate partners, Abuse of Power, prevention measures include early warnings of GBV at household level, in the community and PSEA.
TADO will provide capacity building trainings on various topics of GBV to frontline service providers like Basic advocacy tips and strategies, Gender/GBV referral pathways and how to establish women and girls friendly spaces, protection measures and other related challenges it has on women and girls.
A community centered-approach will be the focus, TADO will work closely with the community stakeholders in mobilization, coordination and accountability to ensure that they receive appropriate services.
TADO will do report to the cluster through the 5W reporting mechanisms and to the financial tracking system.

1. Needs

According to (HNRP -OCHA report 2023), 9 million people are affected by multiple risks of protracted violent conflicts, climate change causing flooding which resulted in adverse food insecurity worsened by protracted intercommunal conflict that and returnees from Sudan has increased the vulnerability of the communities Kapoeta East, Akobo, Nasir and Uror Counties are in IPC level 3+ according to IPC analysis in November 2024 IPC report. This high food insecurity has caused high protection needs as women girls and boys are highly stressed due to lack of food and therefore exposes them to gender based violence and other serious protection concerns as they move in search of food and other essential commodities. They are also exposed to sexual exploitation and abuseA percentage of the affected are in need of general protection needs while 916,840 of this population are in urgent need of GBV specialized services, in such a situation, persons with disability are more vulnerable as the study reveals that they are 3 times more at risk and exposed to discrimination, exclusion and majorly suffer the impacts of gender based violence (Oxfam, care international, HI 2012). According UN-OCHA led IRNA report in Nasir County in August 2024, sporadic fighting in the county has also caused displacements, food insecurity and exposed women and children to gender based violence. conflict-affected women and girls in Nasir county experience elevated levels of psychological distress causing mental health conditions which is widespread and largely untreated, a percentage of women and girls, boys reported signs of distress including depressive symptoms. Coupled by these, displaced women and girls continue to bear the brunt of GBV, a third of people displaced live in communities where women and girls avoid certain areas due to fear for their safety. Sadly, basic GBV risk mitigation measures around sanitation facilities remain extremely rare.(Nasir IRNA 2024)Uror County has also experienced immerse flooding that has also exposed women and children to serious issues including shelters that have been swept away by flooding exposing them to risk of sexual abuse and other forms of violence. According to the Uror County OCHA led multi agency IRNA assessment conducted, serious protection issues were identified after flooding and intercommunal fighting caused displacements in the communities. There were reported cases of gender based violence, rape and communities were lacking shelter exposing women and girls to rape and other protection concerns including violence to women, girls and boys. The communities lacked awareness on GBV and there were no clear referral pathways to victims of GBV and other protection concerns. (Uror IRNA report Uror 2024). These GBV gaps needs emergency humanitarian response to ensure that women, girls, men and boys are able to access GBV services, build resilience and recover from multiple shocks of displacements, conflict, flooding and severe food insecurity.
