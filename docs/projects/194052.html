Project Summary

The IRC will implement WASH activities at supported Health Facilities (HFs) to support refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host community across Gedaref, Khartoum, Blue Nile, and South Kordofan states. The IRC will increase the availability, quality, accessibility, and reliability of potable water sources by strengthening the maintenance and operations of drinking water sources. The IRC will also promote the uptake of positive hygiene behaviors and practices of households, especially regarding safe water chain activities (from collection to consumption of potable water). The IRC will determine and administer project interventions in Blue Nile’s Wad al Mahi locality and South Kordofan’s Dilling and Habila localities which have the highest level of unmet needs. The IRC will also administer WASH activities in other areas that can be safely accessed by project staff. To further complement the impact of water supply infrastructure activities, the IRC will primarily utilize local methods and materials to promote post-project sustainability. Water User Committees (WUCs) will be formed by the IRC to support projects and will include members selected based on coordination with community leaders. Each WUC will consist of approximately 5 or 6 members, with the selection criteria including: individuals from the targeted community; those over 18 years of age; if possible, persons with an ability to read and write in Arabic with preference given to a literate person(s) for record keeping and written communications roles; an active participant of community work and those with technical skills or a trusted human resource such as masons, teachers, community health volunteers (CHVs), and religious/customary leaders. Women are specifically targeted to be trained and selected to take on leadership and management roles within the WUCs, and the IRC will advocate to ensure women make up at least 50% of the WUC members. Additionally, WASH volunteers will operate within the project’s three target locations and the IRC anticipates having at least two CHVs (segregated by gender) for every 1,000 individuals within the target communities. Women in particular will be empowered and trained to take on the role of CHVs to serve as agents for change within their respective communities. As well as the IRC response to the emergency situation to support the affected people from the ongoing conflict in Khartoum and the IDPs in Jazera state by providing WASH services for the health facilities and communities.

1 - Needs

The WASH situation in Sudan is deteriorating due to a worsening economic crisis, political instability, non-functional or aging WASH infrastructure, a five-fold increase in operation and maintenance cost of water infrastructures, insufficient revenue collections, poor budget allocation by the Government, and limited external investments. Most affected WASH population groups are IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable populations battling food insecurity and extreme poverty who cannot afford WASH services and practices. In addition, these population groups are mainly under the threat of WASH-related epidemics, severe acute malnutrition (SAM), the COVID-19 pandemic, and water resource-related conflicts. As per the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 10.9 million people (excluding refugees) who fall under the above categories need WASH services. Therefore, the WASH Sector's overall Objective is to increase access to humanitarian WASH services for 4.8 million most-in-need populations with a focus on building their resilience to WASH-related recurrent shocks. Emphasis will be placed on community empowerment and engagement of women, girls, and young people as enhanced enablers. In terms of water needs, 5.6 million people need basic water services, 4.5 million IDPs, returnees, and other vulnerable populations will be targeted . Of these, 2.2 million rely on the water surface and other unimproved water sources. The rest of the targeted population (more than 2.3 million) need water quality improvements through disinfections at the source and household level, operation and maintenance, and water trucking services. In addition, sanitation remains a crucial challenge in Sudan with 1.66 million people out of the 10.6 million requiring adequate sanitation. 1.2 million people (72%) are still practicing open defecation in the targeted areas and cannot afford latrines and are more vulnerable to disease outbreaks.Furthermore. 4.8 million people need hygiene promotion interventions using risk communication and community engagement (RCCE) strategies focusing on handwashing with soap. Out of them, half a million girls and women of reproductive age need to be reached with menstrual hygiene management (MHM), and half a million people also need to be reached with vector control and solid waste management.In Jezera state the assessment conduct by NRC showed that the main source of water reported was tap water (89% of therespondents). 85% indicated that the quantity of water collected was adequate for their daily needs whilst 94% reported that the quality of water was acceptable. Lack of water storage containers was however reported by 62% of the population. Sanitation and Hygiene: 94% of the respondents reported that they have access to latrines. A few issues were noted on handwashing behaviours which would need to be addressed to avoid public health risks as 66% of the respondents indicated that they do not wash their hands with soap or ash.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The IRC will employ an approach that builds the capacity of local communities, as well as improve community health and protect vulnerable individuals from WASH-related diseases. This will be done through increased access to potable water and sanitation while promoting positive health, sanitation, and hygiene practices. The IRC achieves this through the following key outputs:1. Output 1: Strengthen water supplies both within supported health facilities and within local communities.Activities:• Improve water supply by expanding access to water points, including repair of hand pumps with locally procured supplies in water-stressed areas. • Strengthen the community’s capacities to operate and manage water supplies through the inclusion and training of selected community management structures. Women will specifically be targeted to be trained and selected to take on leadership and management roles within the community structures. 2. Output 2: Facilitate hygiene promotion and environmental improvements through clean-up campaigns, improved waste management, and conducting hygiene promotion.Activities: • Conduct clean-up campaigns in and around households to remove domestic waste, potential vector breeding sites, and feces. • Through a network of CHVs, conduct routine awareness meetings to promote waste management and hygiene behavior change messaging. Topics will include treating stored drinking water at the household level, household handwashing practices, identification and mitigation of WASH-related diarrhea, COVID-19 messaging, and hygienic health practices throughout the water supply chain. • Distribute basic hygiene kits and consumables including appropriate materials for menstrual hygiene to women and adolescent girls. All hygiene kits are provided following discussions with the local communities to ensure all needs are addressed. Kit contents typically include water storage container, handwashing soap, cloth masks, powder soap, disposable sanitary pads, laundry basin, plastic ebrik, and empty sack for packaging. 3. Output 3: Offer sanitation facilities which meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups, such as women, girls, elderly, and people with disabilities.Activities:• Design, construct and monitor sanitation facilities, including latrines and bath-wares, in close collaboration with local communities. This will include discussions with women, girls, the elderly, and people with disabilities to ensure facilities meet the needs of these vulnerable groups. • Construct and/or rehabilitate water systems, including replacing damaged toilet and bath-wares such as basins, drainage, showers, sinks, taps and toilets.• Install handwashing stations and water taps within or near the latrine stalls; establish a water treatment and quality monitoring mechanism; and as necessary, construct ramps to ensure the accessibility of latrines for persons with disabilities and the elderly.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will not impact negatively on the environment. Although latrine construction activities are likely to have minimal on-site environmental impacts, most of the negative environmental impacts are likely to be seen off-site, where construction materials such as sand, fired bricks and timber will be sourced. Therefore, all tender/bidding documents for latrine construction activities will include clauses to ensure that contractors/suppliers obtain construction materials from authorized/certified sources. In addition, where appropriate, the IRC will promote the use of soil stabilized blocks (SSB) in lieu of fire bricks and contractors/suppliers will be required to adhere to the stipulated environmental mitigation measures below which will form part of the contract conditions

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The WASH activities will be inclusive of feedback and participation from target beneficiaries, particularly women, including through focus group discussions (FGDs), and inclusion within the WUCs and as hand pump mechanics. The IRC will increase the registration and identification of persons with disabilities and the elderly to ensure that unique needs are addressed, while also strengthening existing community support structures for these groups, such as surrogate water collectors. Hygiene promotion activities will also include information on services available for survivors of abuse and sexual violence. Beneficiaries will also be made aware of the IRC’s Code of Conduct and opportunities for reporting to prevent and reduce the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The IRC confirms that the proposed project will not be drilling any new boreholes but rather will be repairing existing water sources to reduce local tensions around new boreholes sites. The IRC will engage with the targeted communities and local authorities at the village and locality levels to explain its principles of impartiality and provide opportunities for complaint and redress through focus group discussions, community leader meetings, and suggestion boxes within supported health facilities. During project start-up, the IRC will conduct a needs assessment to further identify households that have people living with disabilities and elderly people to ensure the successful delivery of interventions, particularly regarding water collection and hygiene awareness. Although local communities and households have performed inclusive behaviors for vulnerable groups (such as collecting water for them), the IRC will identify and strengthen these existing cultural and behavioral practices through awareness sessions, as appropriate. Through house-to-house visits, CHVs will also identify people with disabilities or special needs within their respective communities, conduct follow ups, and mutually agree on how to ensure that their unique needs are continuously being met and properly addressed.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The IRC will conduct regular feedback sessions with women and girls within the community to ensure activities and services are adequately responding to their needs. Women, girls, and parents will be involved in the decision-making regarding the location of the latrines, contents of the MHM Kits and water points. Client satisfaction surveys will also be conducted with survivors of WASH on a voluntary basis. The IRC team will engage with women, girls, children, and caregivers from crisis-affected communities in planning and implementing strategies to prevent, mitigate and address the protection issues with WASH intervention. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its Meal and Evaluation (M&E) and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action. The IRC will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the IRC Code of Conduct, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse policies, Child Safeguarding policies, process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). The IRC will provide multiple channels of feedback to beneficiaries including FGDs, key informant interviews (KIIs), post-distribution monitoring (PDM) surveys, KAP survey, feedback boxes, and a hotline. Moreover, the IRC has zero tolerance for bullying, harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC has the following policies in place: Child Safeguarding Policy; Beneficiary Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy; Harassment Free Workplace Policy. All allegations and investigation information received is maintained in a confidential centralized case management system. Cases are evaluated and prioritized when received considering, among other things, whether the alleged conduct is ongoing or historical. The IRC will ensure that all new staff hired for IRC Sudan are trained on and understand the seriousness with which the IRC takes such issues. The IRC also has a neutral department called the global Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, responsible for all major complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
