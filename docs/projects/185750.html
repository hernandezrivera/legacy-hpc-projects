Project Summary

BRAC is proposing centres in the following areas Camps: 1E: 1SSW, 1MPCC 1W: SSWG 1MPCC, Host Communities: 2SSWG, 2MPCC. BRAC’s Male Role Model distinctive characteristics include opportunities to learn new knowledge, modify attitudes, and practice alternative behaviors for 120 men and boys in Camps 1 E and 1W, and same number in in host communities. BRAC will implement a community mobilization approach to prevent violence against women by transforming power imbalances between women and men at all levels of the community. Trainings will include community activists, community leaders, and institutional allies on exploring community norms about violence against women and IPV. In two camps, 1E and 1W, 3000 girls, women, men, boys, LGBTIQ+, PWD will be target beneficiaries. In two host communities, community based mechanism to engage in awareness session to reduce GBV will target 1000 women, girls, men, boys, LGBTIQ+, PWD. BRAC will provide vocational skills training and livelihood opportunities for adolescent girls who are affected on early marriage, women, prioritize women-headed households and survivors of GBV. BRAC will support women leadership, empowerment and mentorship of the women in order to enhance their meaning full participation in decision making and provide support to the whole community. Women's empowerment will be provided on financial literacy, communication and negotiating and decision-making skills. Participants will participate in discussions about their own attitudes and beliefs regarding financial decision-making, the significant contribution of women to the household, and alternatives to using violence as a means of asserting power and control. Case management and PSS services will be provided to SGBV survivors. BRAC will continue to mainstream SGBV interventions in emergency preparedness and response plans, through specialized capacity building for refugees, and contributory stocks of dignity kits. BRAC will continue to mainstream SGBV interventions for DRR, through capacity building for refugees, contributory stocks of 500 dignity kits, enhanced case management and follow up for SGBV survivors living in heightened risk zones. Preventive measures will be in place and measures n accordingly to ensure COVID-19 does not spread. BRAC will submit an action plan to achieve the overall objectives of the project based on the contextual change and time limit. Coordination include the local and regional government, RRRC, CiC, law enforcement agencies, UN agencies, local NGOs and with the Majhi structure to ensure information is effectively exchanged, needs and gaps are identified and there is no duplication in services. BRAC will regularly consult and provide information to the different ISCG sector coordinators and relevant Sector and Sub-Sector Working Groups including representation in Protection Working Group, Male Engagement Task Team, Case Management Task Team. In recognition of its ability to play a leading role in the overall sector response, BRAC has taken a number of leadership positions within the coordinating bodies and strategic groups in Cox’s Bazar. Feedback mechanisms such as complaint boxes in service points, hotlines numbers for PSEA focal, sensitization on right-based assistance in all existing information provisions, train community volunteers on PSEA matters to ensure BRAC’s commitment to AAP and GBV principles and do not harm. BRAC will engage relevant stakeholders, and organize evidence-based advocacy and capacity building sessions in PSEA and AGD for service providers in other sectors and relevant local government on protection mainstreaming.

1 - Identified Needs

According to the latest GBV Information Management System (GBV IMS) reports (September ,2021), GBV types reported include: rape, physical assault, sexual violence, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. Factors such as congested space, lack of privacy, inadequate lighting, and limited access to essential services for women and girls, are among the factors that increase the overall risks for their protection and are part of the conditions sustaining unequal power dynamics, which contribute to the high rates of IPV. The COVID-19 outbreak and ensuing lockdown since 2020 has disproportionately affected women and girls by limiting their mobility, decision-making and access to lifesaving services. Confinement, a rise in tensions, restrictions on services, and the limited access for humanitarian workers as a result of the government-issued lockdown has also increased levels of GBV, child abuse and neglect and sexual exploitation and abuse. According to the GBVIMS data (GBVIMS Sept 2021), intimate partner violence (IPV) constitutes 84% of total reported SGBV cases. IPV has been identified as the overwhelmingly dominant form of sexual and gender-based violence reported in Cox’s Bazar. The inter-agency gender analysis (UNHCR, CARE and Action Aid, August 2020 and BRAC’s Gender Analysis 20221) confirm that men in both refugee and host communities remain the ultimate decision-makers. According to the JMSNA 2021 , in Palongkhali union, 33% of females aged 20-25 years were reported to have married before age 18. Total 47% of households reported the presence of child marriage and 42% of HHs reported the risks of sexual abuse and violence for girls. In Rajapalong Union, 25% of females aged 20-25 years were reported to have married before age 18. Total 48% of households reported the presence of child marriage and 39% of HHs reported the risks of sexual abuse and violence for girls. Women and girls, particularly those living with disabilities, and Hijras, remain vulnerable to physical, psychological and sexual harassment and abuse. Women and girls in polygamous marriage situations are particularly at risk of experiencing IPV. In order to effectively prevent GBV, address gender stereotypes and support survivors' access to quality services in the Rohingya and host communities, there is a need to promote community-based approaches. According to GBVSS Facility Mapping 2021, camp 1E, has a gap of 3 facilities for females and 2 facilities for male. In 1W there are gaps in 3 female and 3 male facilities. BRAC intends to fill the gap through its service facility. Advancing age, and other certain impediments were mentioned more frequently by the communities. In certain cases, regardless of other characteristics such as disability, increasing age appeared to contribute to significantly higher percentage of people experiencing obstacles. For example when it came to barriers to self-care, with a higher proportion of older women with disabilities reportedly unable to shower or bathe without assistance, compared to older men with disabilities, younger people with disabilities, and older people without disabilities.
