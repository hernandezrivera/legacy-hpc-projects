Project Summary

Proposed lifesaving and life sustaining interventions including provision of Unconditional / Multipurpose, conditional cash, improved agricultural production (cereals and vegetables), asset building through cash for work initiatives, and provision of fodder / vouchers to pastoralist and agro-pastoralist particularly in desert locust affected communities. During the conditional cash transfers, NRC will focus on messaging on dietary diversity, hygiene and COVID 19 control (Will ensure that SOPs on social distances and use of protective kits are followed during communities engagement processes). These initiatives will not only help beneficiaries to deal with negative coping strategies on immediate basis rather will also help communities to protect their productive assets and recover from shocks through improved asset building, enhanced capacities and strengthened linkages. Livelihoods and Food Security will continue to engage & build partnerships with other actors from public / private sector institutions for market sensitive diversified livelihoods.
For Cash transfer programs, NRC will be using electronic services of telecom companies & has already signed framework agreements in each of the operational area. This has been learnt that electronic transfers are swift, safe and ensure upholding dignity of the beneficiaries.
NRC will conduct standalone / will participate in interagency rapid needs assessments in disaster hit areas to define needs and devise and implement lifesaving humanitarian and life sustaining (resilience building) initiatives. These programs will not only help communities to manage immediate needs rather, will help affected communities in dealing with adopted negative coping strategies and will enable them to protect their assets base and become resilient to shocks. Over the period, NRC has learnt through experience-integrated programs funded by various donors help to deal with multiple needs of the communities. Therefore, identified and registered HHS Where appropriate will benefit from a companion program for agriculture production, diversified livelihoods and awareness raising around good nutrition sensitive intervention. These companion programs will ensure that poorest HHs have improved capacities and are able to access food and livelihoods options available in the market. Small farmers will be provided with agricultural inputs for improved agricultural production and access to nutritious crops. NRC will make sure that seasonal calendar is adopted during devising interventions so that maximum community members could participate and benefit. NRC will also be devising and implementing cash for work programs wherever appropriate. CFW initiatives will be identified in close collaboration of communities and local authorities; usually these initiatives include establishment or rehabilitation of irrigation channel, water harvesting structures, protection walls and or repair / establishment of farm to market roads. CFW initiative will not only provide labor opportunities to the casual laborers rather will also help beneficiaries to sustain shock, protect and conserve natural resources asset base. NRC will identify neediest villages in close collaboration with cluster and other key stakeholders in the area. Furthermore, looking into the impending desert locust infestation challenges, NRC plans to provide fodder / fodder vouchers to pastoralist and agro – pastoralist communities on the move so that they are able to manage and protect their livestock during the acute phase of the DS attack.
Protection mainstreaming will be done in all Livelihoods and Food Security interventions. NRC will make sure to uphold protection-sensitive cash-based intervention in line to the organizational risk mapping, cluster recommended guidelines, communities led targeting and using toll free hotline number.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Unconditional and multi - purpose cash grants by their nature are the most flexible response as they can meet a variety of needs. However, it is important to ensure that the value is set to a realistic level, a clear exit strategy is defined and payment frequency and clear targeting strategy is adopted. While in conditional cash grants recipients only receive grants when they meet a set condition. These types of grants are used when Beneficiaries may need to send children to school and or Beneficiaries may need to attend a specific training. Therefore, conditional cash grants are used therefore to ensure beneficiaries meet certain needs, whilst still allowing the flexibility of a cash grant. Whereas, cash for work projects may not be suitable for all people. For example children, elderly and those who are unable to undertake physical activity. Because of this cash for work projects should always consider to have an element of cash grants or vouchers for households who are physically unable to participate. In addition the selection and intensity of works that are undertaken can be adapted so it is more suitable to more groups. Cash injection supports local markets and economic regeneration, empowers recipients, and allows them to prioritize their needs. Up to 80 percent of people in crises prefer cash to in-kind aid. Cash cheaper to deliver and helps in mitigating the imminent protection challenges. Cash and vouchers offer increased flexibility and choice for beneficiaries when it comes to their own needs prioritization, thus contributing to the protection and recovery of livelihoods as well as support markets and market recovery following a shock. For NRC, cash and vouchers can provide efficient and effective response modalities in certain circumstances.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Program is proposed in line to the market needs assessment. We understand that market monitoring is as important as project monitoring when it comes to the use of cash and vouchers and also forin-kind projects. Market monitoring is essential to ensure that the project is having no adverse impacts on market systems as well as a means to adjust and change the project . In case of acute emergencies, needs assessments are conducted rapidly in order to mobilize resources, especially where lifesaving interventions and urgent livelihood protection (such as preventing the sale of key assets or damaging coping strategies) are needed. This is common following a disaster, which could be economic (e.g. food and fuel prices rises), environmental (drought, hurricane, tsunami, earthquake etc.) or manmade (conflict related), etc.. Initial assessments should be followed by more detailed assessments, when time permits. However, in case of slow onset disasters, there is usually more time to carry out a comprehensive assessment in a chronic emergency or slow onset crisis than in an acute emergency, as there is less pressure to mount an intervention immediately. Therefore, analysis must be able to identify the relevant political and institutional factors that may need to be addressed in order to improve access to food. Early warning and surveillance approaches are also appropriate here and can help to monitor the situation and implement disaster risk reduction and preparedness activities rather than responding to a crisis. Though NRC will be conducting rapid / detailed needs assessment according to the situations, however proposed interventions are in line to the cluster (FSC) and cash and market working group recommendations. These interventions will help markets to recover and improve.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

In a rural context, communities or groups either are forced into temporary displacement because of climatic shocks or conflict, or might join urban centers in search of better access to humanitarian aid (pull factor), access to services and economic opportunities. During slow onset and long- term crisis such as the recurrent drought and conflict, the same communities may partially displace to neighboring towns. NRC will devises and implement all LFS support programs in close coordination with cluster, local / regional authorities and other agencies working in the area therefore, impact of armed conflict and violence will be mitigated through proper coordination, advocacy at national and international level, media and referral mechanisms. NRC applies communities led targeting approach and this is ensured that marginalized groups who are more prone are included. M&E and program teams sensitize communities on use and working of the CRFM system. Communities are encouraged to use this system and get their concerns / complaints addressed immediately. This not only helps individuals satisfaction rather also assures that protection is ensured and inclusion and exclusion are managed. Exclusion and marginalization: The Federal Government of Somalia has committed to women’s empowerment and eradicating violence against women, recognizing that without institutionalizing the equal rights of women, the country will never be able to stabilize and develop in a sustainable way. Somali women and girls continue to bear an unequal burden of the hardships occasioned by poverty, conflict and clan-based culture, which promotes strict male hierarchy and authority. This is further exacerbated by cultural limitations on the role and status of women in Somali society. Women with disabilities are often marginalized due to their disability and their gender. They may be more vulnerable to poverty and social exclusion, and often have limited social, political and economic opportunities and lack of access to basic services. Women with disabilities may also be at greater risk of sexual and physical violence and abuse. Girls with disabilities often experience discrimination, for example in education and family life. This project will ensure engagement of people, especially women with disabilities, and the elderly at the Country Office management level and will ensure that programs interventions do not generate negative side effects. The beneficiary selection will be conducted in a participatory approach with the community leaders and the local authorities that will identify the most vulnerable people. Women and Child headed households will be given special priority. Exclusion and marginalization: Targeted assistance will be provided to the most vulnerable groups including women, girls and boy. Beneficiaries will be selected through cluster recommended communities led targeting approach, which will identify the most vulnerable based on certain criteria as female or child-headed households, elderly, disabled, large families and the chronically ill. NRC will train VRCs for beneficiary selection and will work for a participatory approach. VRCs and local authorities will help to identify the most vulnerable people. Women, girls, boys and Child headed households will be given special priority. Protection mainstreaming: Protection mainstreaming will be done in all Livelihoods and Food Security interventions. NRC will make sure to uphold protection-sensitive cash-based intervention in line to the organizational risk mapping. Risk mapping is often reviewed / conducted at the start of the project launch. Secondly, NRC uses a toll free hotline number to get feedback from beneficiaries. Calls are managed by M&E team, and communities are sensitized on use and working of the CRFM system. In addition to dealing with the protection challenges this also helps in mitigating inclusion and exclusion.
