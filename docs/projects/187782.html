Project Summary

The proposed project aims at improving food security of conflict-affected communities in Northeast and Northwest Syria. This will be achieved through a combination of different complementary interventions at the individual household and community-level. At the household level, emergency responses assistance will be delivered through distribution of in-kind food baskets, Ready-to-Eat Rations and Cash-for-food assistance, reaching 76,334 individuals overall, including recently displaced persons. In addition, ACTED will enhance food production at HH level, through kitchen and home garden kits for 858 HHs, enhancing individuals' food security. These activities will be completed by Nutritional Awareness Sessions which will strengthen HHs’ ability to make informed choices regarding nutritional values. ACTED will also target 33,132 farmers and livestock holders with longer-term support, delivering agricultural inputs to redeem seeds, fertilizers, polytunnels and tools, and training on climate-smart agriculture and best practices. ACTED will also provide solar energy equipment to two water pumping stations to ensure the pumping of water into irrigation canals, necessary for agriculture. All project activities will be designed in a way that enables resilience to be built at a local level and that builds the capacity of men, women, boys, and girls to access basic items and meet basic needs themselves. Specifically, at the household and community levels, ACTED will support men and women in building their own capacities to meet their basic needs so that they are more resilient and better able to access commodities and services in the future. Lastly, ACTED will work in coordination with local technical department with the aim of building their capacity to provide essential services for communities. This project is estimated to benefit 193,312 individuals overall.

4 - Needs

In Northeast Syria, the traditional breadbasket of the country is experiencing a severe water crisis affecting the ability of the population residing in the area to meet their basic needs. This crisis is also impacting the food value chains of the country. The crisis is mainly generated by drought caused by the low precipitation in the country and the progressive reduction of water levels in the Euphrates River. As a result of water shortages, the livelihoods and income of farmers have also been negatively impacted, particularly in Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor governorates. The most affected crops as highlighted by the farmers are wheat and barley. While wheat production was expected to hit 1.2 million tons in 2021, it has not reached even 300,000 tons, despite the cultivation of additional 300,000 hectares, increased irrigation and seeding rate this year In Northwest Syria, affected populations cope with the purchasing of lower quality food, increasingly borrowing money to afford food, cutting down on non-food expenses, and skipping meals. Furthermore, food prices skyrocketed in the midst of the SYP collapse, are eroding the purchasing power of the most vulnerable households. Movement restrictions cost remain major barriers to physical access to food markets across NWS. Lack of access to agricultural tools and to quality seeds further compounded the situation, resulting in reduced agricultural production that affected food’s availability on the market.

5 - Implementation of activities

ACTED will deliver an emergency food response, specifically geared towards the most vulnerable households and recently displaced persons, through Ready-To-Eat Rations, as well as Cash-for-Food and kitchen gardens kits. ACTED will assist some 81,482 persons under these emergency activities. Additionally, ACTED will roll out monthly food assistance over eight months or more, mainly through in-kind food baskets or voucher modalities, depending on market accessibility in each targeted area. 73,334 individuals are expected to benefit from monthly food assistance across Northern Syria. ACTED will also enhance long-term food security and resilience to future shocks at the household level through the distribution of 858 home garden and kitchen garden kits, allowing targeted households to produce food at a small scale for their own consumption, thereby reducing their dependency on humanitarian assistance and unstable market accessibility and affordability. ACTED will also support farmers and livestock holders with vocational training, as well as grants for seeds, fodder, and tools. At the community level, ACTED will rehabilitate two irrigation and water assets, including canals and wells (directly benefiting 12,000 persons). Lastly, ACTED will work in coordination with local technical department with the aim of building their capacity to provide essential services for communities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementation Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitization, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse Likelihood: Low – Impact: High Mitigation measure: all ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to PSEA and GBV. As part of ongoing efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as that of the contractors, and to relay any issue, including SEA or GBV.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. ACTED staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behaviour of the contractor employees towards camp residents. Information on the CRM will be communicated to camp manager and community leaders and as much as possible directly to camp residents, especially those who may be directly impacted by the road rehabilitation. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. Mitigation measures are monitored through regular on-site monitoring at the rehabilitation sites. The presence of women staff, in the programme and monitoring teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff, programme and monitors, are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.

• Conflict escalation: movements are suspended in specific areas; relocation of staff to other offices to continue activities • Community acceptance: monitored daily by liaison assistants, regular meetings with local community leaders and Key Informant network in order to prevent any tensions; regular dialogue ensured with all relevant authorities, with support from various coordination mechanisms at area level. • Road closures: ACTED diversified its network of suppliers through tender processes and reinforced local suppliers’ capacities to ensure that items are procured regularly and prepositioned• Procurement related risks: 2-week local advertisement in multiple locations and ACTED’s transparency@acted.org email, analysis of all offers led by regional office.
