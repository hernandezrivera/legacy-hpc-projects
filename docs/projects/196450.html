Project Summary

The main goal of this Action is to ensure targeted crisis-affected households, including those in underserved communities have their essential non-food item and winterization needs met before the winter of 2023-24. The Action will ensure immediate access to non-food items and winterization items to the population in need to maintain health, privacy, and dignity.
WWGVC plans to distribute core NFIs and minimum winterization package (excluding fuel) to 2,000 households with vulnerable and underserved adults and children (12,000 individuals) in 2023 in GoS areas of rural Aleppo (districts of A'zaz, Menbij, and As-safira), Deir Ez Zor (districts of Deir Ez Zor and Abo Kamal), and in accessible areas of Ar-Raqqa (Maadan and Sabka subdistricts). Preliminary data shows the majority being host community (94%), followed by returnees (3%), IDPs (2%) and refugees (1%). The kits will be distributed for HHs in rural areas with high severity and with non-suitable shelter conditions, and upon identification of needs through other WeWorld GVC programmes, especially education. The distribution of items will comply with the NFI cluster specifications. Beneficiaries will be selected also according to vulnerability criteria set in close consultation with the target population. Different stakeholders, targeted population, community leaders and SARC will be consulted in setting the criteria, to ensure transparency, acceptance and accountability. Selected HHs will include female-headed HHs, HH including elderly people, and/or people with disabilities and/or infants, families with out of school children, and families with low or no income.
The project will be initiated with 1)Needs assessment, in coordination with SARC, taking into consideration the existing databases of WWGVC's education program. The assessment will also study the different needs of the gender and age groups of the targeted population. 2) Market assessment to study the availability of core NFI and winterization kits in the country. 3) Procurement and storage of kits in SARC warehouses in each governorate. 4) identification of community distribution points taking into consideration C-18 precautionary measures following the guidelines of the S/NFI cluster. This will be followed by the in-kind distribution of core NFI kits and winterization kits to 2,000 HHs. 4) Conduction of post-distribution monitoring survey.
GVC will coordinate with SARC, MoE, NFI and WASH Sectors as well as other present humanitarian actor (including UN Agencies, i.e. UNICEF and UNHCR) to ensure information sharing, and avoid any duplication and overlapping during implementation.

3 - Needs

In Syria, compounded crisis has significantly intensified the severity of need for NFI support. Worsening economic conditions, depreciation of the Syrian pound, reduction of purchasing power, coupled with the lack of access to fuel and other basic services and commodities have determined an overall increase NFI PiN of 5.8 million (from 4.90 million in 2022). According to the HNO 2023, nearly 85% of the overall population reported a decline in their ability to meet basic needs, and inaccessibility of basic NFI items. The number of people in need of NFI support is expected to further increase in 2023. Harsh winter conditions have a high toll on the most vulnerable communities, thus making winterization responses imperative and needed as life-saving measures. Key NFI items such as, winter clothing, thermal blankets, and heaters are essential, accessible yet unaffordable to most individuals. The lack of access to livelihood opportunities and HHs ability to meet basic needs is foresees as a major driver for increased severity and intensity of shelter and NFI needs across population groups in targeted governorates. Findings from analysis conducted in 2022 already validates this assumption/expectation, given that the average monthly shelter and NFI expenditure has risen to 26% and further expected to rise in 2023. The population at risk includes the female head of HHs, youth, and people with disabilities and a high number of out-of-school children with their parents and caregivers.

4 - Implementation of activities

The activities planned to be implemented are the following: A1) Assessment of family needs in the prioritized areas of Aleppo, Deir Ez Zor, and Ar-Raqqa. A2) Market assessment to study the availability of the supplies in the local market including the intended quality. A3) Procurement of kits including quality inspection at bidding and reception phase in addition to the storage of kits in SARC warehouses in each governorate. A4) Identification of community distribution points taking into following the guidelines of the S/NFI cluster, and distribution of core NFIs (1 Kitchen kit, 6 mattresses, 6 blankets, 1 jerry can, 1 solar lamp, 1 plastic sheet), and core winter items (1 Winter Clothing Kit, 2 Winter Jackets, 6 winter boots) to 2,000 HHs. The kits constitution will be in line with the NFI cluster's guidelines and could be customized depending on the results of the needs assessment.The NFI clusters' safe distribution guidelines will be followed. A5) Monitoring, evaluation, and reporting: standard monitoring tools will be used to collect qualitative and quantitative data from the field. The data collection tools will include distribution reports, key informant interviews (KIIs) and observation checklists. PDM will also be conducted after the distribution following the unified template by the NFI sector. The distribution will be monitored by WWGVC program and MEAL Staff in each location utilizing support from SARC volunteers to conduct the distribution in each location.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) R:Inequality in distribution of NFIs; MM:promote involvement and engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups; Selection criteria are transparent and communicated to communities and stakeholders in advance. 2) R:Gender insensitive content of kits; MM: follow sector unified kit constitution standardizing gender-friendly kits. 3) R:Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at risk of GBV; MM:adequate distribution management plan is set, training of staff on PSEA and GBV principles and SoPs. 4) R:exposure to physical harms and/or explosive hazards during distributions; MM:security assessments conducted prior to any distributions in targeted areas; consultations with authorities and communities to identify distribution points deemed safe; work closely with Mine Action actors and carry out distributions only in areas declared cleared; 5) R:increase of tensions among households receiving and not receiving assistance; MM: ensure that distributions are conducted based on needs and prior consultations with all relevant stakeholders; ensure communities are informed and briefed before, during and at the end of the intervention in a participatory manner by applying a robust complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM); 6) abuse of power by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion); MM: ensure the team and partners are aware of and abide by WWGVC Code of Conduct.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Core principles and preventative measures are set out within a PSEAH Policy aimed at creating a safe working environment with the zero-tolerance principle. WWGVC does not tolerate any form of SEAH and will not knowingly engage anyone to any capacity who might pose a direct risk to children and vulnerable adults. WWGVC also has a child safeguarding policy aimed at ensuring that children’s rights are recognized and respected and that children are protected from any form of violence, including placing children’s safety well-being above any other considerations; reporting any concerns cases of suspected child abuse; planning and organizing project activities whilst minimizing risks for children. PREVENTIVE MEASURES include: All new staff is introduced to the PSEAH and Child Safeguarding Policy, are required to sign the policy, and attend periodical training. RISK ASSESSMENT: WWGVC endeavors to reduce these risks to a bare minimum. Regular risk assessments are carried out to identify, evaluate and mitigate risks throughout the organization's operations. SAFE PROGRAMMING: WWGVC takes proactive measures at every stage of project implementation to avoid causing harm inadvertently whilst mainstreaming best practices. Among WW-GVC staff members, PSEAH focal points are appointed and trained to ensure the implementation of the policy and operational guidelines. Allegations of PSEAH cases are received, addressed, and responded to with zero tolerance according to an internal system.
