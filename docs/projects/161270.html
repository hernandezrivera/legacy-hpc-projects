Project Summary

Action Africa Help International is registered in South Sudan as a National Non-Governmental Organization (NNGO) whose experience spans over 32 years in delivery of lifesaving interventions in Health, Nutrition, Education, Livelihoods and Food security as well as managing large scale Humanitarian Logistics project funded by UNHCR. It implemented EVD Preparedness and Response project in 2019 and also HRP 2019/20 in Yei River State.
AAH-I proposes a one-year Emergency Health and Nutrition Integrated Response in three counties of Yei, Morobo and Kajokeji in Yei River State providing mobile teams, PHCC and PHCU packages among the IDPS, returnees and host population. This will target 11,383 people in the categories of host population, returnees ad IDPs.

1 - Needs

Narrative to explain the need of the project It is estimated that over 3,296,823 people are in need of health and nutrition humanitarian assistance in South Sudan in 2020. The three counties in Yei River state targeted has health/nutrition needs of 257,036 people of which the target is 11,383 (Health Cluster 2019 updates). Yei River State was greatly affected by the 2016 conflict that led to mass influx, displacement and out-migration of thousands of the host populations into Uganda and Democratic Republic of Congo. Following the return of peace and signing of peace agreement in 2018, many returnees have been registered and estimates vary according to UN and government figures. Despite the returns and settlement into the locations, insecurity remained prevalent in most parts and to date Kajo Keji County access from Yei is quite limited while Morobo mostly was accessible through Kaya border point and Koboko in Uganda, this made service delivery a challenge to the vulnerable populations especially children under five and women of child bearing age. Following the declaration of Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) Oubreak in Ituri province of DRC in August 2018, Yei River state has been in a state of alert and has very high risk of transmission due to numerous porous border points as well as broken down infrastructures at health facilities, coupled with displaced persons and limited staffing at health facilities. To address this mobile clinics along with health facility clinical packages are recommended.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, within the context and limitation of the project scope, eg participatory approaches in planning health care services, getting feedback from them and monitoring their satisfaction. 6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, this will be reviewed as migratory trends are monitored and service delivery given in context.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the implementation is based on the government system of health service delivery at state, county and health facility levels. The County Health Department Directors will directly support and supervise the projects to ensure the contribution to the overall health sector objectives at county levels.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project aligns to the national objectives, IDPS returnees and host population as targets within guidance from the humanitarian assistance partners. This is guided by reports on displacements,returnees and migration of population as well as settlements and locations to prioritize interventions.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. The project will ensure that services provided ensures safety and protection of the people involved including beneficiaries.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project works directly with Ministry of Health Structures, staff recruited locally from the counties and have likelihood of staying longer and give service to their people, they are also acceptable to the host population from which they come. The staff recruited are deployed in government health facilities and paid incentives based on government scale of harmonized salary incentives. This helps to ensure they get absorbed into the government payroll and become permanent staff supporting service delivery. This model has been used in HRP project and Ebola Preparedness Response Project implemented in Yei River State by AAH-I.
