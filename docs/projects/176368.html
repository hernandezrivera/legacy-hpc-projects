Project Summary

The project will upscale provision of comprehensive child protection services including case management, family tracing and reunification, reintegration support for CAAFAGs, psychosocial support for children and their families and community-based child protection to target communities where Save the Children has presence and that need to be strengthened, or require expansion into new geographical locations that lack child protection services and yet have been flagged as locations with acute emergencies.
Children, youth and families in the selected locations have experienced trauma, gender-based violence, forced displacement resulting from conflict or floods, risks of recruitment and abduction of boys, child forced early marriage, among others.
Through this one-year project, Save the Children will identify children with critical protection needs to receive appropriate and targeted support and provide access to girls and boys to child protection services, coordinated assistance, referrals and community-based interventions to ensure that girls and boys affected by complex and multi-faceted humanitarian crisis (including armed conflict, famine, flooding and the impact of COVID-19) in South Sudan, are protected from all forms of violence, abuse and neglect. In all new locations the community based child protection networks(CBCPNs) will be formed and where they are existent, they will be strengthened; by training on their roles and responsibilities
The project will work in collaboration with the community based child protection committees that are existing and those that will be set up in the new areas where they are non-existent, to ensure that they participate in identification and referral of unaccompanied and separated children to initiate tracing. The local partners and administration will also be oriented on the FTR process to make it participatory. The case management system will take the lead in FTR process during case plan implementation and also ensure that appropriate alternative care options are availed to the children in process. The alternative care system will also be a major focus of the CBCPN in collaboration with the local administration and participation of the larger community; foster care givers will be identified and oriented, closely monitored and supported by the community mobilisers and case workers.
During the community awareness campaigns or dialogue events with the CPCPN, will ensure that the messages that are shared and discussed are in line with the prevalent child protection issues and incidents in the community. They will be factored into the routines of the community such as market days or any gathering that brings community together while observing the COVID-19 protocol. This will focus on both prevention of the incidents and response that the different stakeholders should undertake.

1 - Needs

Communities in Akobo were displaced by floods and inter-tribal clashes last year and are still unable to return; they lost property, livelihoods, livestock, and food in their farms. It is also among the counties classified in emergency (IPC phase 4) acute food insecurity. Each of the payams of Bilkey, Nyadit, Gakdong, and Dengjok in Akobo East have a settlement camp with no safe play areas for the children. There are 2 CFS in the vicinty which do not have the capacity to serve all the children , highlighting the urgent need to set up three more CFS to provide this essential service to children, including a safe place to engage in stress busters and an entry point for MHPSS interventions. With the consequent inability to provide basic needs after families lost everything they owned, leads to the first level of psychosocial distress in children, parents and caregivers. In a June 2020 RNA by SCI – Risk Communication and Community Engagement” (RCCE), the majority of the sampled respondents raised concerns of not having basic needs due to loss of income.. According to the 2019-2021 child protection sub cluster strategy, girls in are at potential risk of sexual violence, child marriage and early pregnancy, while young boys are at risk of being engaged in active conflict adduction and exploitation through child labour when their families cannot provide. Displacement and family separations further exacerbates these risks.The second scale up sitrep, indicated that 119 documented unaccompanied and separated children are in urgent need of case management services while family tracing and reunification (FTR) services are needed in Pibor, Akobo, and Aweil South counties. HRD documented at least 434 abductions (96% women and children) in Jonglei, and a 140% increase during the first half of 2020 compared to the same period in 2019.In Bor, there are 6 IDP camps where people settled when floods commenced last year, which are still difficult to access leading to an increase in risks as child protection issues cannot be followed up or monitored on time. Bor South has also had incidents of inter-tribal clashes between the murle, Lou, Bor and Gawer people hence the risk of child abduction is high. It has also been classified in emergency as phase 4 of acute food insecurity. This indicates the need to strengthen case management, community based interventions and PSS.However, the on-going child protection resources and interventions so far for the response in Bor are not adequate to cover the gaps in these locations effectively. During these movements, many children could have been separated from their parents/caregivers as a consequence of displacement hence the need to set up community structures to support reunification and raise awareness on issues such as violence against children, GBV, sexual exploitation, CEFM, child abduction and recruitment into inter-tribal/clan conflict and child labor to obtain basic needs for children and their families, all of which increase as a negative coping mechanisms during conflict, displacement, and food shortage. There is need for the case management system to be strengthened for effective family tracing and reintegration for UASC, setting up of CFS’s to ease the crowding in the ones being operated so far and provide adequate services.In greater Pibor Administrative Area 24,000 people were classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, in Pibor County (11,000) in Pibor Administration Area; which shows that it is a high needs area where child protection risks and concerns are likely to increase and hence the need for child protection measures to prevent this and respond as and when the incidents occur.The upscale sitrep also showed that existing activities had been affected by flooding in Pibor including damage to WGFS and CFS, resulting in further services deficit. At least six additional mobile CFSs are required in Pibor Town, Verteth, Gumuruk and Likuangole in GPAA.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Information will be shared using different media; written posters will illustrations, feedback boxes at service points and child protection help desks. It will also be integrated in the community awareness campaign, meetings with beneficiaries, one on one dialogues, or counseling etcAll staff and community volunteers and front line workers will be trained on child safeguarding along with code of conduct and PSEA for prevention and given the opportunity to report, whistle blow accordingly. Any staff, volunteer would have to adhere to the policies which are already existent in the organization.And in case of an incident, a standard process would be followed to handle it in standard and responsive approach.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

They will be part of any community consultation or engagement and the and would have information on all avenues for feedback and make use of them as and when they desire. Separate for a in the community such as women groups or any marginalized groups would be deliberately accessed and encouraged to take part.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community meetings will be held in identified locations from the onset of the project, where the project details would be shared at all points such as targets, selection of beneficiaries. Feedback will be provided and queries responded to in an organized and mechanism. Confidentiality would also be considered for anonymous reporting of complaints as well as a plan for deliberate, timely, and substantial feedback.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback from the beneficiaries will be assessed just like lessons learnt, documented and used to inform future actions, interactions with community or project plans.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Though the comprehensive case management system and functional referral pathways, the assessment comprises of medical issues, legal needs MHPSS and all other risks what are usually the essence why the identification was done and the case plan would be implemented.Referrals will also be made to other sectors for services that cannot be provided through child protection services.The child protection help desks at all service points will ensure that information on all services is provided and disseminated for their access to timely and quality interventions.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The targeting of the communities was informed by the IPC classification among other issues such as chronic vulnerability, displacement and conflict. It was in the design of the objectives and activities. The focus for child protection interventions in this project considered the escalation child protection concerns such as psychosocial distress among children and their families, risks of abuse, exploitation and neglect among other risks, due to the integrated food security as it a push factor of negative coping mechanisms against child wellbeing. Integration with the FSL and Nutrition sectors would be one of the strategies to tackle this; child protection help desks at OTPs, health centers, food distribution points and FSL service points like trainings for farmers, inter-sector referral of vulnerable families identified through case management or community dialogue to be included on beneficiary lists for interventions to curb food insecurity. This would not only offer a holistic approach but also reduce the numbers and impact of populations facing acute food insecurity and malnutrition.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

From the initiation of the project to its full development, interventions will be inclusive of all categories of people. The community will be involved at planning for activities, and other key decisions along the course of the project. Community based structures will comprise of women, men, disabled, youth, children, elderly, and any other groups as identified by the community. All target communities will be given equal attention and consideration.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Child protection assessments and monitoring will have an indicator of the children’s health status whether it is for case management or general community child protection monitoring. CBCPNs and child protection front-line staff will be trained in identifying signs and symptoms of malnutrition, and have it included in their case plans for appropriate interventions while the nutrition front-line staff will also be trained on child protection and case management processes, so that they can detect and identify cases of malnutrition that could be a result of neglect or abuse and requires further handling. At all nutrition screening points, CP desks will be set and information disseminated. When more frontline structures are looking out for these children, both malnutrition along with child protection can be prevented before severity and responded to accordingly.
