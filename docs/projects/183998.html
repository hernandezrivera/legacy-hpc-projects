Project Summary

With the looming economic crisis, uncertain political environment coupled by recent waves of conflict and displacement in North and West Darfur, the context in Sudan remains very fragile. IDP's in the 3 States, North Darfur, West Darfur and South Kordofan continue to face significant challenges to access documentation and enforce their rights to housing, land and property. At the same time recent developments such as the Juba Peace Agreement and the National Protection of civilians plans provide opportunities that could potentially crystallise to support displacement affected communities to attain durable solutions. Conflict over land and natural resources in Darfur and South Kordofan regions remain prevalent and have resulted in massive destruction of property and displacement. Rampant secondary occupation and ongoing disputes over land and natural resources have been identified as a major cause of conflict and hindrance to attaining durable solution in the return context. Inability to access legal identity and civil documents additionally gives rise to various protection concerns including; limited access to essential services, livelihood and employment opportunities.
This project will aim to support displacement affected communities to claim their rights through;
1) Promoting the exercise and enjoyment of housing, land and property rights - The project will create awareness on housing, land and property rights in displacement and contribute towards conflict prevention and non-escalation through peaceful resolution of disputes over land and natural resources. The HLP response will focus on supporting IDP’s and displacement affected communities to attain adequate housing, security of tenure, eviction monitoring and strengthening dispute resolution processes and structures.
2) Facilitating increased access to legal identity and civil documentation. The project will aim to contribute towards the ability to exercise and enjoy rights as a result of increased access and use of legal identity and civil documentation (LCD). Access to legal identity and civil documents is expected to result in increased access and enjoyment of basic services such as education, health, financial inclusion as well as increased access and enjoyment of protection, freedom of movement, security and due process. for displacement affected communities as well as the
To achieve these objectives the project will 1) ensure that displacement affected communities are better informed about their HLP and legal identity rights through information sessions, information education and communication (IEC) materials, mobile counselling, individualized legal assistance and collaborative dispute resolution 2) enhance understanding amongst authorities, community leaders, and humanitarian actors on HLP rights and the importance of legal identity and civil documentation through integrated capacity building and 3) promote HLP mainstreaming through adoption of due diligence approach before humanitarian interventions which have an implications on land use and ownership.
The project will target vulnerable displacement affected populations including; IDP’s, returnees, host community and conflict affected communities in Darfur and South Kordofan. Access to HLP rights and legal identity documents are gendered issues with regularly recurring instances of gender inequality regarding ownership of housing, land and property and access to legal documentation. This project will target 60% of female beneficiaries to address this gendered gap.

1 - Needs

Sudan’s history has been defined by violent conflict over land and natural resources which has resulted in massive destruction of property and displacement. Unresolved land conflicts have remained a major cause of violence and featured predominantly in the recent peace negotiations culminating in the 2020 Juba Peace Agreement. One of the key barriers preventing achievement of durable solutions is the lack of restoration of housing, land, and property and the right to compensation. Access to land, disputes over rights to use natural resources and widespread secondary occupation of land left by IDPs and refugees continue to drive conflict in Darfur and South Kordofan. Weak land governance and administration processes continue to have a significant impact, leaving families without security of tenure, limiting access to adequate housing, agricultural land and grazing opportunities that can help to strengthen household resilience. Unresolved conflict over land and natural resources remain a major cause of violence in both Darfur and South Kordofan. While most land-related conflicts have been “reported” as occurring between pastoralists and farmers, there are other causes of conflict related to customary and statutory land governance, environmental issues, poverty, ethnical identity and exclusion. Managing land conflicts and disputes is closely linked to comprehensive political settlement and re-establishment of the rule of law throughout Sudan. In Darfur, the juddiya and native adminstration are a customary dispute resolution mechanisms that are largely involved in resolution of disputes over land. In South Kordofan, customarily, disputes are resolved through the Native Administration court. There is need to reinforce and increase capacity of these existing dispute resolution structures so as to improve their effectiveness in managing both existing conflicts, future conflict. IDPs and host community members have limited access to legal identity and civil registration documents or registration services that will prove their legal identity. Access to legal identity and civil documentation is crucial for displacement affected communities as it enables access to humanitarian assistance, access to basic services and freedom of movement within the country. Lack of access to these documents may consequently mean that IDP’s would be unable to prove ownership of land or seek restorative remedies for lost properties. Students who lack birth certificates additionally face difficulties enrolling in schools and sitting for national exams. Some of the main obstacles preventing access to legal identity and civil documents include high costs associated with application procedures, administrative challenges, and the lack of functioning civil registries within close proximity. This project will work closely with displacement affected communities and duty bearers to enhance protection of HLP rights and access to legal identity and civil documents.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Provision of information on access to essential services, housing land and property rights, legal identity and civil documentation - ICLA staff will raise awareness about HLP rights, the importance of legal identity, civil documents, and the procedural requirements associated with securing either of these rights. Information sessions are generally brief and generic touching on the above thematic areas. Contextualized information, education, and communication (IEC) materials related to access to essential services, HLP and legal identity including civil documentation will be developed and distributed during the information sessions. Provision of counselling related to HLP rights, legal identity, civil documentation and access to essential services – Legal counselling is carried out by ICLA staff to explain the more complex requirements needed to; access essential services, exercise ones HLP rights or access legal identity and civil documents. Through the counselling, NRC will empower beneficiaries to pursue these claims on their own however where the beneficiary is particularly vulnerable more comprehensive legal assistance will be provided. As part of its counselling services, ICLA staff will also facilitate referrals to other service providers to help beneficiaries’ access essential services and other protection interventions. Provision of legal assistance - Legal assistance is provided where the beneficiary is unable to pursue a case by themselves. It covers a range of activities depending on the circumstances of each case including; accompaniment to relevant authorities, support with complicated administrative procedures and even financial legal assistance. The project will additionally provide legal assistance through collaborative dispute resolution over conflicts related to land. This will include providing parties with advise the parties on the most appropriate approaches, procedures to resolve the specific dispute and where needed will support them to engage in the collaborative problem solving process. Integrated capacity building of local authorities, community leaders and humanitarian partners through trainings, workshops and provision of technical and material support on HLP and legal identity including civil documentation. Strengthening collaborative dispute resolution through mapping of dispute resolution mechanisms, training and material support to dispute resolution structures dealing with land issues and supporting access to these structures. The training for dispute resolution structures will aim to improve their understanding of HLP rights, improve their dispute resolution skills, increase access for displacement affected communities and promote women’s HLP rights.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection mainstreaming in NRC is integrated through our Safe Programming approach, which is the responsibility of all technical sectors and program teams. This includes: ● Do No Harm: NRC will review the different modalities and processes for intervention as the situation evolves to ensure they are accepted and safe for the community. ● Community Engagement: community consultations were undertaken to identify key threats, worries, and opportunities on the part of the community. These consultations will continue through the project cycle and will inform design of our intervention. It will also be one of the key parameters to weigh the benefits of our intervention vs the different risks it entails. Before project implementation the project will undertake a gender sensitive assessment that will analyse potential protection risks associated with the programme activities to inform project targeting and implementation strategies. The assessment will consider different needs and capacities of men, women, boys, and girls, and of specific groups that may be vulnerable, marginalised, or discriminated against. The project design prioritises participation of vulnerable groups such as women and persons with disability ensuring that they are able to access services and assistance and removing any barriers that may prevent full and effective participation. ● Inclusion: While the project will be blanket coverage for all POCs, there are segments of the population that will require additional support and/or specific assistance. This includes people with disabilities, the elderly, and other groups that communities themselves will identify as in need of help. To this end, NRC will have a dedicated team to engage in these specific cases when they arise to ensure all are able to access and enjoy the assistance to be provided. The project design ensures that that activities have a ‘protection lens’, intentionally addressing safety, dignity, inclusive participation, diversity, rights, and vulnerabilities. ● Accountability: the Sudan country program is in the midst of establishing its Complaints and Feedback Mechanism. The mechanism will have different channels to ensure all stakeholders of NRC programming are able to share complaints and feedback. Regular monitoring activities with the affected populations around their perceptions of safety, dignity, and agency practice will be carried out. ● PSEA: the Sudan country program has a zero-tolerance policy and approach to reports/incidents of PSEA. Data protection: NRC has SOPs in place for the safe handling of beneficiary and partner data
