Project Summary

The project responds to Cluster Objective 3 “Children and school staff in vulnerable schools are supported in preparing and responding to emergencies.” The Education Cluster estimates that 503,927 people (47% females) need Education in Emergencies humanitarian assistance across the West Bank and Gaza. The estimated figures reflect an increase in the need for humanitarian assistance in 2021. This is based on an analysis of trends over the recent five months which revealed a marked deterioration in the overall humanitarian situation in oPt due to the Covid-19 pandemic. More families have been pushed into poverty, undermining their ability to afford basic education supplies and increasing the risk of school dropout.
The project will support over 30,000 poorest students in West Bank and Gaza aged 6-17 (50% girls), who may be at risk of dropout due to poverty, to continue in their education by providing basic, urgent supplies including school bags, and winterization items such as school uniforms, warm clothes, and shoes. The provision of the winterization items to children will be through the e-voucher programme with WFP to allow the targeted beneficiaries the option to select their own supplies. In the past, distribution of school uniforms was done through schools and the feedback from beneficiaries was that the children felt stigmatized by their peers as poor children. Providing options to the families to procure the uniforms through e-vouchers would minimize stigmatizing children in that their peers will not know those benefitting from the free uniforms. The target vulnerable households will be selected from the database of relevant authorities, cross referenced with the vulnerable community assessment list developed by UNICEF and relevant authorities and verified by the OCHA list of hardship cases. Priority will be given to female-headed households, households with children with disabilities and households with children affected by the Great March of Return. Due to a lack of an operational budget, 421 schools in Gaza are starting the new school year without basic teaching and learning supplies and struggling to afford basic stationery, paper and chalk. These schools will be supported with basic teaching and learning supplies.
The COVID-19 emergency has further exacerbated the already existing vulnerabilities created by an unprecedented fiscal crisis for the Palestinian Authority (PA) which worsened after the PA’s decision of not receiving tax collected by the Israel as a response to the threat of annexation. The education sector has particularly been hit hard affecting over 1.4 million children in both the West Bank and Gaza. Schools are unable to provide the necessary school hygiene materials to operationalize the guidelines for Safe Schools Operations. In this context, 600 schools (179 in the West Bank and 421 in Gaza) will be supported with school hygiene kits to help them operationalize safe schools protocols in their schools.

1 - Needs

The deterioration in the humanitarian situation has pushed more families in the West Bank and Gaza into poverty. The COVID-19 emergency has further exacerbated the already existing vulnerabilities created by an unprecedented fiscal crisis for the Palestinian Authority (PA) which worsened after the PA’s decision of not receiving tax collected by the Israel as a response to the threat of annexation. The PA’s monthly revenues have fallen to their lowest level in at least two decades due to a collapse in domestic economic activity and external trade. The impact on Palestinian economic productivity is significant. The June 2020 World Bank economic monitoring report estimated a contraction in the economy by at least 7.6 per cent for 2020. Depending on the length of the lockdown, this contraction may be the among the largest recorded. The education sector has particularly been hit hard affecting over 1.4 million children. There is little doubt that the COVID-19 emergency threatens progress on the Sustainable Development Goals and on the 2030 Agenda in the Occupied Palestinian Territory and is deepening pre-existing inequalities, discrimination and inequities. In Palestine, the capacity of the education system to deliver adequate education to more than 1.4 million children is compromised by deteriorating living conditions: teachers receiving partial salaries; 421 public schools (serving 50 per cent of children in Gaza) lacking operational budgets; limited accessibility, in particular for children with disability (CWDs); and psychological distress that children and teachers face. The findings of a Household Expenditure and Consumption Survey, released by Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) in May 2017 show a significant increase in poverty rates in the Gaza Strip from 38.8 per cent in 2011 to 53 per cent in 2017, which is equivalent to around 1.01 million people, including over 400,000 children. The COVID 19 pandemic has worsened the already poverty situation and pushed more families into poverty. The World Bank estimates that 30% of households in West Bank are now under the poverty line, as compared to 14% before the COVID-19 outbreak. In the Gaza Strip, the World Bank estimates that as many as 64% of households now live under the poverty line. More parents may not be able to afford basic school supplies for their children, placing them at risk of dropping out of school. Due to the lack of an operational budget, schools in Gaza are not able to procure basic teaching supplies and the new school year has been ushered in with many schools, in the most vulnerable areas, struggling to afford basic stationery, paper or chalk. The Education Needs Assessments report (2018) shows that education has been negatively affected in terms of quality and quantity of services provided by schools due to the financial crises in and the very limited ability of Ministry of Education (MoE), UNRWA and families to financially support the education services. Under the project, students from schools located in marginalized or underserved areas will be provided with essential teaching and learning materials to enable them. 200 targeted most vulnerable schools in the West Bank and Gaza will be provided with school hygiene kits to support them to continue operationalizing the guidelines for Safe Schools Operations amid the COVID-19 pandemic. School children from the poorest communities of area C, H2 Hebron, East Jerusalem (EJ) and Gaza Strip, have been particularly hard hit by the COVID-19 pandemic. These children do not have access to internet or do not have the required devices to access the remote learning opportunities that are currently being used by the education system to provide education during the COVID-19 pandemic. If not supported, the children from these vulnerable households are at risk of dropping out of school and the inequities will be further exacerbated. To address this, 900 boys and girls in the most vulnerable communities will be provided with tablets loaded with offline self-paced materials. The content will include self-learning sheets based on the required curriculum developed by relevant authorities and uploaded to the tablets at the district level by technical unit staff.

2 - Activities

The following activities will be conducted after the vulnerable children and schools have been selected. The criteria for the selection of schools will include the number of vulnerable children in the vulnerable localities indicated in the poverty score matrix issued by the PCBS. The names of beneficiaries will be verified through the OCHA list of hardship cases. 1. Provision of teaching and learning materials for 421 schools in Gaza Approx. 280,000 school children (50% girls) will benefit indirectly 2. Provision of school bags with basic stationery kits to 30,000 children ages 6-17 (50 % girls) from the most vulnerable households in Gaza. The target beneficiaries (50% girls) will be selected based on vulnerability criteria, such as female-headed households. Priority will be given to school children in these families. Disaggregated data about male and female school children will be verified by relevant stakeholders through the School Management Information System (SMIS). 3. Provision of school uniforms, winter clothes and shoes to approximately 24,560 children (50% girls) in Gaza using the e-voucher programme (with WFP). Families affected by environmental threats/ natural disasters e.g. seasonal foods will be prioritized. Through the provision of the school bags, stationery and winterization items, the priority will be given to female-headed households, households with children with disabilities and households with children affected by the Great March of Return. 4. Provision of school hygiene kits for 600 most vulnerable schools in the West Bank and Gaza to support them continue operationalize Safe Schools Operations in light of the COVID-19 emergency. 5. Provide tablets loaded with offline self-paced materials to 900 children to support vulnerable boys and girls access remote learning. The uploaded content on the tablets will include self-learning sheets based on the required curriculum developed by relevant authorities and uploaded to the tablets at the district level by technical unit staff.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Central to upholding Do No Harm principles, UNICEF and partners in this project will ensure the application of confidentiality and the selected mechanism for the distribution aligned with the best interests of children and households. UNICEF will make sure that all the beneficiaries can easily access the locations where the supplies will be distributed. Moreover, UNICEF will make sure that the beneficiaries are not subject to be stigmatized when distributing these supplies.
