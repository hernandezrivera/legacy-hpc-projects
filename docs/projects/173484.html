Project Summary

MONCLUB INTERNATIONAL aims to minimise the undermining effects of breastfeeding practices and to improve knowledge of optimal infant and young child feeding practices among affected IDP camps and host communities in 7 communities across Yobe and Borno states. The targeted beneficiaries are caregivers and their children 0-59 months. This requires specific expertise and technical support, that MONCLUB will provide with her experienced staff and consultants. MONCLUB is well positioned and already on ground in Monguno and Kukawa LGAs, as well as targeted communities in Yobe state and will leverage on that to provide response to the affected communities.

1 - Needs

In 2021, People In Need (PIN) for Borno and Yobe states, according to HNO 2021 is estimated to 613,909 and 511,182 respectively. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) levels in children aged 6-59 months is on the increase, with Yobe and Borno state rates above the emergency threshold at 13.6 per cent and 10.7 per cent respectively as at September 2020. If untreated, acute malnutrition could have debilitating consequences on the growth and development of infants and children, further exacerbating the already existing burden of malnutrition in the northeast. Malnutrition is a major contributor to disease burden, with more than half of the death in children under five years. One in every five of children with SAM and one in every 15 with MAM are at risk of death if their malnutrition status remains untreated. Female-headed households, the disabled, as well as orphaned, separated or unaccompanied children, face additional challenges in safely accessing humanitarian assistance and basic services and are at higher risk for sexual exploitation and abuse, and gender-based violence. Women in rural setting are less likely to exclusively breastfeed their infants less 6 months due to increased workload/labour and lack of access to skilled IYCF counselling at health facilities. According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute Malnutrition analysis 2020 (IPC AMN), the nutrition situation in Borno and Yobe State is classified as Phase 3 (Serious) and Adamawa at Phase 2 (Alert) level, with GAM levels of 13.6% in Yobe and 10.7% in Borno. The nutritional situation is also expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying issues including increased displacement and the impact of COVID on the socioeconomic status. According to HNO 2021 sectoral overview, approximately only 54% of infants less than 6 months are exclusively breastfed in the BAY States.

2 - Activities

Monclub will work at the community level and in health facility to strengthen IYCF programming.1.1. Community Mobilisation and stakeholder’s consultation1.2. Identification and selection of Community Nutrition Mobilisers.1.3. Training of Community Nutrition Mobilisers.2.1. Promote optimal Infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices through community-based support groups.2.2. Form mother- to-mother groups and father-to-father groups. 2.3. Disseminate key messages on hygiene, good practices and COVID-19 using sector guidelines to PLWs and caregivers.2.4. Raise awareness on exclusive breastfeeding and complementary/ supplementary feeding targeting PLW and caregivers. 2.5. Conduct community outreach sessions and house to house meetings to encourage caregivers of SAM and MAM children to visit OTPs2.6. Raise awareness on GBV prevention/protection while assessing nutrition services.3.1. Strengthen/establish/retrain hygiene promoters to reach new mothers and caregivers with hygiene promotion including exclusive breastfeeding messaging, and conduct routine discussions and counselling with caregivers of SAM and MAM children, and conduct COVID-19 prevention session using house to house approach.3.2. Train caregivers, mothers and fathers on IYCF keys messages using cooking and eating demonstration with locally sourced food monthly.Community Nutrition Mobilisers conduct daily mass MUAC Screening of U5 children.3.3. Train mother-to-mother groups and father-to-father groups on screening of malnourished children using MUAC and referrals.The participating women will be empowered with messages to help them adopt optimal behaviours on infant and young child feeding (IYCF), nutrition, health and hygiene. The women will be engaged to propose when they would like to hold their meetings and where they would prefer to have the meetings. In the scale-up, fathers will also be engaged and empowered with key messages so that they can understand their role in supporting their wives achieve optimal nutrition, health and hygiene practices. In addition, involving the men will help in overcoming barriers that could prevent women from getting permission to participate in programme activities such as the care group meetings. Also, both boys and girls in the supported LGAs will be targeted and screened and all children identified with SAM will be referred for treatment regardless of gender.
