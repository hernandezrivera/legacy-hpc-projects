Project Summary

The overall intervention aims to support populations affected by disasters in target areas to meet their basic food needs during and after crises. Food-insecure people affected by long-term conflict and/or displacement are able to meet their basic food needs and increase their self-reliance throughout the year.

Needs

According to Sudan’s 2019 HNO and The Government led Food Security Technical Secretariat (FSTS) it is estimated about 5.7M people are experiencing crisis or emergency levels of food insecurity in Sudan. This has been exacerbated by the current macro-economic situation including increasing year-on-year inflation rates-73% in December 2018, compared to 63.9% in June 2018. Food and livelihood insecurity is the result of multi-dimensional challenges affecting availability, accessibility and environmental sustainability of Sudan’s food system. The economic crisis is therefore negatively impacting the food and nutrition security of a large share of the population including IDPs, refugees, and resident population. This is manifested by households already experiencing food insecurity prior to the onset of the crisis sinking to even deeper levels of food insecurity and previously marginally food secure people becoming food insecure. Under normal circumstances, IDP and refugees are already the most vulnerable, given their limited livelihood opportunities and these are most affected by the economic crisis. During 2018, people living in camps have suffered an alarming deterioration of their wellbeing due to their dwindling access to livelihoods opportunities and already high levels of pre-crisis food insecurity. On the refugees, Sudan continues to host the second-largest number of South Sudanese refugees in the region, with nearly one million refugees living across all states in Sudan. Ten of thousands of refugees have arrived in Sudan in 2018 with worrying levels of food insecurity whilst hundreds of thousands live without a predictable line of funding of food assistance due to funding shortages. Amid these challenges, Sudan continues to receive regular flow of refugee arrivals each month. The number of new arrivals peaked at nearly 200,000 people in 2017, and the rate of arrivals has slowed in 2018 compared to previous years. Almost 90,000 refugees are expected to arrive in Sudan from various countries in 2019. Further, the recent influx of refugees from bordering countries has increased the need for humanitarian assistance across Sudan with new arrivals from South Sudan. Malnutrition rates are above the emergency threshold in several areas. Approximately 76% of the current South Sudanese refugee population are living outside of camps alongside host. Many out-of-camp settlements are in remote and underdeveloped areas, where resources, infrastructure and access to basic services are extremely limited. In some out-of-camp areas of East Darfur, South and West Kordofan, insecurity and geographic isolation can pose challenges to regular access by response partners. With a commitment to zero hunger by 2030, there is a need to strengthen and continue the refugee response within the food security sector on access and coverage of refugees in and out of camp settlements through a diverse set of activities for them to effectively meet their food security needs.

Activities

Activities: Provide unconditional general food assistance to people affected by shocks; Provide integrated conditional and unconditional food assistance packages to vulnerable households.
