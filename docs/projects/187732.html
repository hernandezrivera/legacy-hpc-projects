Project Summary

With this project, UOSSM seeks to contribute to achieving the following objectives:
Increase access to life-saving and life-sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need
Strengthen health sector capacity to prepare for, detect and deliver a timely response to disease outbreaks
Strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience, and respond to IDP movements and changes in context
This project will contribute to strengthening the health care and wellbeing for conflict-affected communities in NS by improving access and ensuring the sustainability and quality of health services targeting Syrians including the hosts and IDPs with more focus on the most vulnerable people including women, children, and the elderly as well as the PWDs by supporting 21 HFs (4 hospitals,4 specialized centers, and 13 PHCs) in Idleb, and Aleppo. As such, this project will mitigate the economic burdens that patients face in securing medicines, especially for patients with chronic diseases, children, and pregnant women. The intervention will also contribute in response to the COVID-19 by providing the HFs with PPE and early detecting of the suspected cases and referring them to PCR Labs.
The mentioned HFs are equipped and staffed but have a lack of medicine mainly, the HFs located in areas with high priority according to health cluster and the TWGs, where distributed in densely populated and accessible locations, most of the people are IDPs with a very low level of medical care and bad economic situation.
All of the HFs were included within the central referral network where the capacity of the HFs gives them an important role in the referral system, and the HFs have close coordination with IPC central desk as well as HFs commit to sharing the health data through HIS, 4Ws monthly.
Supporting and upgrading the capacity of these HFs is very important in light of their strategic locations in addition to the size and variety of health services provided through these HFs that include:
PHC services: management of childhood illness, management of NCDs and CDs, providing RH services, providing dental services, applying IPC measures, vaccination services, lab tests, and bandage services)
Hospital services: general surgical, obstetrics, RH services, internal medicine, laparoscopic services, urinary surgeries, orthopedic, and ophthalmic services their related surgeries.
Specialized centers services diagnostic, treatment, and blood transfusion services for thalassemia patients, specialized radiography services, dialysis services, and providing patients with blood products and blood transfusions for them as well as providing the surrounding hospitals with blood units through the blood bank.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

1- Risk: Sexual Harassment: among employees, volunteers and in work environment context, Level: Low, Mitigation / Prevention: all UOSSM staff have been trained on PSEA and above all has signed a TOR and are aware of the internal policy of PSEA which has been monitored by both the HR & MEAL teams. 2-Risk: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: between service delivery personnel, towards beneficiaries, Level: Low, Mitigation / Prevention: All UOSSM staff (employees and volunteers) signed on a code of conduct that includes of refers to the commitment to PSEA, that is issued by the UN Secretary-General Bulletin for the PSEA procedure. In addition, UOSSM has a PSEA focal point in addition to a dedicated hotline to receive complaints from beneficiaries in case of PSEA incidents. 3-Risk: Gender-Based Violence: targeting the vulnerable groups with communities (i.e., women, elderly, children, special needs …), level: Medium, Mitigation / Prevention: The project design is built on robust need assessment and is also targeting the most vulnerable groups in the community without discrimination or exclusion. Above, the project is a multi-sectoral one where all sectors are aware and acknowledged of detection and referral of GBV either internally to the protection department or externally to other service providers.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:-UOSSM will coordinate closely with the health clusters, as well as local authorities and humanitarian actors inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, informing the cluster if and when adjustments are needed to the intervention.-Location remains accessible for the team and the beneficiaries.-Inflation and currency exchange rates remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned deliverable quality and quantity.-Local qualified staff required for project activities are available and adequate.Risk:-The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities.-The close of borders prevents the shipments of supplies and medications to access facilities.-New peak of COVID 19 -The spread of Covid 19 infection among the project staff significantly-Lack of specialists and qualified staffmitigation:-Develop a strong risk management strategy, (preparing an emergency plan /plan B/ according to the expected scenarios in conflicted areas.-Capacity building plan for the staff.-Enhance the coordination with the health partner and active participation in the TWG to fill up the gaps in medical supplies.-Activating community COVID19 treatment center in light of a new peak of COVID 19 (UOSSM can implement if the fund is provided).-Implement strict recommendations on quarantine, isolation, and provision of IPC means and PPEs-Conducting an enhancement training course (the proper use of personal protective equipment).
