Project Summary

TThe overall objective of this Project is to support vulnerable people in Iraq affected by the crisis to receive inclusive humanitarian assistance and have greater access to protection services.
This project has two specific objectives that respond to the extensive explosive ordnance contamination that remains across the five governorates of Nineveh, Salah al Dinh, Kirkuk, Anbar and Diyala as a result of the occupation by ISIL and the military action to defeat them since 2014. Firstly this project will produce a collaborative non—technical survey (NTS) to identify suspected hazardous areas. Secondly it will provide Risk Education to at risk vulnerable communities whose homes and local areas have been contaminated, teaching them key safety messages as well as how to report hazards and how to stay safe. These will be achieved through:
1. Increasing efficiency in mine action responses through a better understanding of location, level and type of ERW contamination through the provision of Non-Technical Survey.
2. Raising awareness of the risk of explosive hazards and as a result reduce civilian casualties and promote safe behavioural change among the most vulnerable communities (returnees, IDPs, stayers), through the delivery of tailor-made Risk Education sessions, while creating an increased feeling of safety and protection among beneficiaries.
In order to achieve the specific project objective NPA, in partnership with MAG and DDG will recruit and train local residents from areas of Ninewa, Anbar, Kirkuk, Diyala and Salah al-Din to conduct non-technical survey, producing full survey reports. In addition, teams will provide risk education for population at risk in the governorates of Ninewa, Anbar, Kirkuk, Diyala and Salah al-Din.
Within this Project NPA directly, and through its partner organisations, will be achieving the following results:
• Recruited, trained and deployed 3 multipurpose heavy NTS and RE teams across Anbar Governorate, 3 multipurpose heavy NTS and RE teams across Diyala Governorate, 3 multipurpose heavy NTS and RE teams across Salah al Din Governorate, 1 multipurpose heavy NTS and RE team across Kirkuk Governorate to conduct RE and NTS activities, 5 multipurpose NTS and RE teams across Nineveh Governorate.
• Surveyed scope and nature of contamination identified in explosive affected communities surveyed.
• RE sessions provided to identified target groups.
• Children provided with Risk Education, disaggregated by age, gender and diversity.
• Adults provided with Risk Education, disaggregated by age, gender and diversity.
• Community Focal Points provided with Risk Education training and materials.

Needs

Iraq is one of the countries in the world most severely affected by landmines; cluster munitions remnants and other explosive remnants of war. According to the Mine Action Review’s publications “Clearing the Mines 2018” and “Clearing the Cluster Munition Remnants 2018”, Iraq is among the four most contaminated countries in the world. The already serious contamination has been made significantly worse by the recent fighting in Iraq and enormous quantities of improvised mines and explosive devices in areas recaptured from ISIL. The extensive military conflict has had profound and far-reaching consequences on human security in Iraq. The use of complex improvised explosive devices (IEDs) in Iraq is unprecedented, and the Government of Iraq suffered numerous losses from the use of such devices as part of asymmetrical campaigns in recent years and the remains of explosive hazards (EH) in the aftermath of the conflict has compromised the safety and security of civilians and public spaces, such as schools and hospitals. Despite the dangers, people are returning to claim their lives back. Based on the Multi Cluster Needs Assessment, conducted in July 2018 amongst a representative sample of 12,261 conflict-affected households nationwide, 22% of IDP households citing explosive hazards as a top reason for not intending to return (in camp), 18% of IDP households citing explosive hazards as a top reason for not intending to return (out of camp), 6% of households with members reported to be disabled due to explosive hazards (returnees). Vulnerable people in liberated areas continue to be severely impacted by the presence of IEDs, landmines and other ERW, while their safety is continuously put at risk, especially in the case of children. While disaggregated casualty figures are not available, according to casualty figures recorded by the United Nations Assistance Mission for Iraq (UNAMI) in 2017, at least 3,298 civilians were killed and another 4,781 were injured in acts of terrorism, violence and armed conflict in Iraq. The safe removal of EH, including IEDs, is critical to facilitate the safe return of millions of displaced people, as well as to allow the GoI and the international community to undertake critical post-conflict interventions, particularly in areas liberated from ISIL. Equally critical is capacity development support directed towards relevant national structures to ensure continued sustainable national response. The scale, scope and complexity of the new explosive contamination problem in liberated areas is substantial and beyond the Government’s national response capacity. The nature and extent of contamination requires a comprehensive specialised explosive hazard management response to allow the full complement of recovery, stabilization and development assistance efforts in Iraq to proceed. This entails an effort in which civilian organizations complement the work of the ISF and the police and the UN and partners support and help build capacity. The DMA, in its National Strategic and Executive Plan for Mine Action 2017-2021, outlined its commitment to work towards an Iraq “free from explosive hazard, where everyone lives in a safe environment.” The Government understands that addressing explosive hazard threats saves lives and enables the provision of humanitarian, stabilization and development support to its population. The strategy highlights that DMA aims to “mitigate the risks posed by improvised explosive devices, mines and explosive remnants of war (ERW) contamination and reduce the associated social economic and humanitarian impact resulting from EH contamination” with the aim to be able to survey, mark and render safe 50 per cent of known contamination by 2021. Risk education programmes targeting at-risk communities and liberated areas in Anbar, Ninewa Salah al-Din, Dyala and Kirkuk have been identified as the highest priority for risk education.

Activities

1. Non-technical survey (NTS); assessment of the scope and impact of contamination threat in accessible liberated areas: data collection and mapping. Production of individual and community-based survey reports2. Risk education (RE); Provide information to highly at-risk groups of girls, boys, women and men how to report hazards and how to stay safe; Providing community-based RE Community based risk education will be conducted based on the community needs assessment, that will include identification of risks in an explosive hazard contaminated community, identification of impacted group of population. Based on the assessment a community-based risk education will be implemented including providing of RE sessions for affected groups, through meetings with local population at community’s centres, IDPs camps, presentations by method door to door. The above activities will be implemented across Ninewa, Anbar, Diyala, Kirkuk and Salah Al din Governorates.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Community liaison and impact assessment is conducting as integral part of NTS activities, with the purpose of understanding of affected communities, defining needs and target groups for risk education, justification for clearance/technical survey in terms of benefiting the defined target group and beneficiaries. It enables affected communities and local authorities to gain a better understanding of mine action and to participate in defining their requirements for risk education, survey, clearance. Active participation of the affected women, girls, boys and men is necessary to accurately identify their needs and priorities. RE sessions, delivered by NPA and subcontracting organisations, will be tailored to best reach different age and gender groups and all subsequent data will be disaggregated. In addition, all NTS/ RE staff will be trained in Community Liaison techniques. Appropriate and context relevant materials such as posters, leaflets and note-books with IED/ERW awareness messages designed for different age groups will be distributed amongst targeted population. Community based risk education will be conducted based on the community needs assessment, that will include identification of risks in an explosive hazard contaminated community, identification of impacted group of population. Based on the assessment a community-based risk education will be implemented including providing of RE sessions for affected groups, through meetings with local population at community’s centres, IDPs camps, presentations by method door to door. An important part of community based RE will be to identify community members such as community leaders, teachers and health care and provide for them RE package, to enable the sustainable delivery of risk education and incident/casualty reporting. NPA and partners will undertake regular monitoring sessions, measuring the retention of knowledge among the beneficiaries at least two months after the RE sessions have been delivered. Using separate questionnaires for teachers, adults and children the monitoring measures what and why the former participants remember parts or all of the sessions, how if relevant the subject has applied the knowledge, and the subject’s own sense of need for further IED/ERW RE in the community/camps.During monitoring visits NPA and partner organisation activities will be tested by internal quality assurance and quality control processes, and program impact is checked to ensure the realization of goals and that impact monitoring post-intervention is being undertaken.
