Project Summary

This project aims to address urgent needs of flood-affected populations in BAY States, Nigeria. Led by Care Best Initiative (CBI). The project focuses on health, gender-based violence (GBV) prevention, child protection, and psychosocial support for displaced populations and vulnerable host communities. CBI will spearhead health and WASH activities, providing primary healthcare services through existing facilities and mobile medical outreach teams. Their interventions include risk communication and community engagement, essential health services, reproductive health support, disease surveillance, mobile medical teams, and distribution of hygiene kits and supplies. These efforts aim to improve access to healthcare for 38,744 individuals, prevent waterborne diseases, and strengthen referral systems for complex health cases. CBI will integrate GBV prevention and response activities. They will establish Women and Girls Friendly Spaces, provide case management support for GBV survivors, raise awareness through community engagement, and mainstream protection activities for women and girls. All interventions will integrate disability inclusion to ensure that people with disabilities have access to awareness messages and services. The project, set to run for six months, will collaborate closely with state and local government authorities to ensure sustainability and smooth transition at the end of the intervention period. Through these comprehensive efforts, the project seeks to address the most pressing needs of flood-affected communities in BAY State, promoting health, safety, and well-being for vulnerable populations. This project will last for 12 months with the support of the state and LGA governments in targeting the total number of 38,744 individuals.

1 - Needs

In BAY State, over 60,856 people across 17 LGAs are impacted by severe flooding, displacing 18,788 individuals and causing 1,392 casualties annually. The floods damaged nearly 5,865 shelters and 65 public facilities, including schools and healthcare centers. Infrastructure damage, has isolated communities, hindering aid delivery. Compounding this crisis, the state government has declared a cholera outbreak in several LGAs, signaling healthcare access challenges. The dual crises of flooding and cholera create a complex emergency situation, threatening public health and livelihoods, particularly as damaged water and sanitation infrastructure facilitates the spread of waterborne diseases. Vulnerable populations, including women, children and internally displaced persons (IDPs), face crowded living conditions and inadequate access to clean water and sanitation, increasing the risk of further disease transmission. A coordinated humanitarian response is urgently needed, focusing on strengthening healthcare, disease surveillance, WASH interventions, and protection measures. Long-term investments in resilient infrastructure and disaster preparedness are essential to mitigate future crises in Yobe and the broader BAY states (Borno, Adamawa, Yobe).

2 - Activities

The complex emergency in BAY State, driven by widespread flooding and a cholera outbreak, has worsened food security and limited access to nutritious foods for highly vulnerable communities. Rising inflation and soaring food prices further compound these challenges. Without urgent humanitarian intervention, over 23,000 children under five are at risk of death due to acute malnutrition, while 690,000 malnourished children face worsening health conditions. This project aim to support lifesaving health services for 38,744 vulnerable individuals, focusing on children under five and pregnant/lactating women. The project aims to improve inpatient and outpatient healthcare, strengthen disease outbreak surveillance, and provide SAM/MAM treatment, counseling, and essential medical supplies. Key activities will address the treatment of cholera and other waterborne diseases, crucial as the cholera outbreak has already resulted in 132 cases with a 6.8% fatality rate. Care Best Initiative (CBI) will provide medicines, hygiene kits, LLITNs, cholera RDT tests, and reproductive health supplies. Risk communication, disease prevention education, and surveillance will be conducted through mobile health teams and static facilities, offering routine immunizations, mental health support, GBV response, child protection and referral services. These interventions will address the immediate crisis and build long-term resilience in affected communities.
