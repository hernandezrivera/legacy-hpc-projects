Project Summary

More than 7.2 million people have been displaced inside and outside Sudan since fighting broke out between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces on April 15, 2023 and More than 500,000 people have been displaced because of clashes between the SAF and RSF in Wad Medani and surrounding areas in Aj Jazirah State based on OCHA Sudan Humanitarian Update (28 December 2023).
Over 66 percent of the IDPs reside within host communities in rented accommodations, schools, gathering sites, improvised shelters, and camps. Sudan faces the worst education crisis globally, with 6.5 million children losing access to schools due to insecurity, 19 million children in Sudan out of school as conflict rages on and at least 10,400 schools have been shuttered in conflict-affected areas, while 5.5 million in less affected areas await reopening (UNICEF report October 2023). BN state ranks low in terms of availability, accessibility, and quality of education, according to the latest need assessment and Ministry of Education records, with a low attendance rate of 55% for school-age children and 38% literacy rate for the population aged 15 and over, and 44.8% of school-age children outside the system. The conflict exposes vulnerable children to various life-threatening risks, such as gender-based violence, child marriage, female genital mutilation, human trafficking, recruitment by armed groups and child labor. The proposed project main objectives is to Increasing access to education in safe and protective learning environment for 14,286 crisis-affected vulnerable children their aged 6-18, and also to Improve equitable quality education within a protective environment for 14,286 crisis-affected vulnerable children (aged 6-18) including those with disabilities. The project will work to Strengthening the capacity of the education system, partners, and communities to deliver a timely and coordinated education response. 14,286 student (6,842 in Blue Nile and 7,444 in Sinnar) (7,857 girls and 6,429 boys), will be benefited from the project, those children come from various backgrounds, including 8,736 IDPs, 5,550 host communities, and 1,938 disabled from BN and Sinnar states. The project will be implemented in Sennar, Alsouki, Abu Hujar, Ad Dinder, and Sinnar East localities and Kurmuk and Geissan localities in BN. The overall objective will be accomplished by implementing various activities. These include providing recreational materials and dignity kits, as well as MHM education. Additionally, temporary learning spaces (TLS) and alternative learning methods will be established. Damaged classrooms will be repaired, and handwashing stations will be installed. Safe drinking water will be provided, along with psychosocial support. An alternative education program will be implemented, and teachers and Parent Teacher Association (PTA) members will receive training on MHPSS, safe schools, (GBV) and child protection. Furthermore, teachers will be trained in professional development, and emergency incentives will be provided for them. PTA members, both male and female, will also be trained on school management, GBV, child protection, and school safety.These interventions align with the Humanitarian Plan for 2024. IRW will coordinate its project activities with other partners and relevant government departments in the project sites, such as the Ministry of Education (MoE) and UN agencies like UNICEF, WFP, UNOCHA, WHO, and UNHCR. The implementation strategy will involve direct implementation by IRW, while also engaging implementation partners in community mobilization and engagement.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project has been designed to respond to the different needs of groups at the activities, outputs, and outcomes levels. The project implementation team have vast experience on gender sensitive implementation and will practically implement project activities in a manner calculated to advance gender equality. The implementation teams will include both men and women. A significant proportion (55%) of the proposed beneficiaries are females. Girls and boys will be mobilized to benefit from education different activities, Education Facilities will be constructed in safe locations that minimize risks and threats to beneficiaries and in particular women and girls for easy access. School latrines will be gender and culturally sensitive and will be constructed separate for men and women, boys and girls thus promoting safety and privacy thus reducing dropout rates more so for the girl child. Psychosocial support including Social Emotional Learning will be gender sensitive, with topics applicable for girls and for boys being delivered separately. Female hygiene will be sensitively addressed as per the norm of the community. Some activities will directly target women and Female for instance Provision of dignity kits and MHM Education. Facilitators will ensure that issues addressed are gender balanced and engage both girls and boys, IRW organization has been working with schools in Blue Nile State for more than 20 years, frequently targeting IDPs, Returnees, Refugees and Host Community and has noted that girls tend to suffer disproportionally when there is a shortage of educational facilities. By providing safe learning spaces this project will encourage girls to enroll and attend school (and families to send their girls to school). Livelihood interventions will also ensure women participation and representation in community structures and capacity building opportunities.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

There is clear connection between our security and program that are appropriate, timely, participative and transparent in the identification of beneficiaries and entitlements. Our program always gives priority to saving lives, doing no harm and alleviating suffering and IRS allocate assistance on the impartiality basis of need, remain neutral within conflict and independent of governments foreign or domestic agenda. The relationship between programming and humanitarian identity is of utmost importance and plays a vital role in gaining acceptance and consent from the beneficiary’s community and other social power-breakers for our presence and programme delivery. IRS has well established beneficiary selection committees that are representative in terms of gender that ensures PWD and the marginalized rights and entitlements are equally given to them and that they are discriminated against at all times. IRS also prescribes and adheres to the 9 CHS standards that over the years has ensured beneficiaries are not discriminated against and provides beneficiaries with a platform for Complaints and Feedback and in all the cases IRS takes decisive actions and decisions that ensure non-discriminatory actions by her staff at all times. The project and activity design have considered the DNH principles thus will ensure that they are not a source of conflict, disagreements and discrimination but rather source of peaceful co-existence and harmony while fostering sustainable utilization and management of community resources. In addition, the project has been designed to minimize harm and support local capacities to build peace. Therefore, IRS will Cooperate and work with relevant partners to help promote social, environmental and economic well-being in a cohesive and inclusive manner and provide structures for handling feedback and complaints, Communities will be sensitized so that they know how to complain if project commitments are not met and finally, Appropriate awareness-raising tools and approaches are used with communities, which are relevant to age, language, social status and capacity. IRS and all partners will apply a conflict sensitive lens throughout project and implementation. As part of the baseline assessment, IRS will undertake a conflict sensitivity analysis that will assess issues of conflict and existing community structures that can contribute to peace-building and conflict resolution. The results of the analyses will serve as a guide for ensuring that IRS and project partners understand the complex dynamics within each beneficiary community. IRS will also ensure staff; partners, and communities involved in the projects are aware of and take into consideration actions that have the potential to cause conflicts within and between community and social. Implemented activities have minimal effect on the environment in the project target areas. IRW-Sudan is committed to implementing environmentally friendly activities
