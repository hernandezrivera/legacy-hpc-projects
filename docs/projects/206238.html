Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to meet the lifesaving food requirements of the most vulnerable, food and nutrition insecure people in South Sudan, including IDPs living in Rubkona POC and Malakal ex-POC, recent returnees from the Sudan crisis and protracted and new caseload of refugees (from Sudan crisis). A second objective is to ensure that boys and girls in the most marginalised and acute food insecure areas have access to quality education and receive safe, nutritious school meals to improve school attendance and eventually their education outcomes. Finally, WFP will provide an integrated package of health and nutrition support to malnourished children under 2, pregnant women and girls and new mothers living in these same areas where access to health and nutrition services and education is severely limited.
WFP will provide general food distributions (GFD) to the most food insecure people in South Sudan. At the field level, food assistance will be overseen by the WFP field offices and implemented by cooperating partners. WFP provides food assistance and common services through a comprehensive and collaborative humanitarian intervention by deploying mobile teams, supported by cooperating partners to reach populations facing the most urgent needs in the most inaccessible areas of the South Sudan, subject to the availability of resources.
Complementary to food distributions, WFP will seek to offer value-added solutions (light resilience- building activities, peace/conflict resolution training also called GFD+), in response to needs and priorities identified by the community. Through GFD+, WFP together with FAO and their cooperating partners and other stakeholders will provide unconditional opportunities, identified through a community-based, people-centric participatory approach to targeted beneficiaries. GFD+ adds value to emergency food assistance with a focus on solutions that address the root causes of food and nutrition insecurity and better equip beneficiaries to fight hunger.
The GFD+ activity type, duration, frequency, and participation will be catered to specific local sites and may range from agricultural training, or sensitisation on peaceful resolution of conflicts, nutrition education, dyke rehabilitation and communal asset management, income-generating activities which lead to women's economic empowerment. WFP will also implement resilience building activities including Asset Creation and Livelihoods (ACL) to support communities to improve their food security, restore their livelihoods, strengthen their resilience to shocks and build self-reliance, while contributing to peace through strengthened social cohesion and community structures and collaboration with local authorities and peace actors.

1. Needs

Since independence South Sudan has faced prolonged armed conflict interspersed with subnational violence, economic shocks and consecutive years of extreme flooding and localized drought. Poor transport and communication infrastructure perpetuates marginalization and inequitable access to social and economic opportunities. Humanitarian needs remain alarmingly high, far exceeding available resources. Furthermore, humanitarian access is constrained by protracted insecurity, threats and violence against humanitarian personnel and assets, operational disruptions, and extremely poor infrastructure. According to an UN Common Country Analysis (CCA) for South Sudan, approximately 74 percent of the population is under 30. Limited livelihood and employment opportunities drive high youth unemployment rates, estimated at 29 percent in 2019. This leads to disengagement from school, workplace exploitation and the recruitment of young men into armed groups, undermining social cohesion and prospects for peace. Wasting prevalence remains concerning at >10 percent, while stunting is high at 31 percent. This is driven by deteriorating food insecurity, poor diet, disease and limited access to health and nutrition services. Suboptimal maternal, infant and young feeding practices and harmful sociocultural practices are also contributing factors. According to the recently released IPC results, the severe food security crisis in South Sudan is expected to continue with 7.1 million people (47 percent of the population) projected to face high levels of acute food insecurity during the height of the 2024 lean season (IPC), compared to 7.8 million people during the same period in 2023. 38 out of 79 counties (48 percent) are expected to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity during the lean season (April to July). In 2024, due to reduced funding, WFP plans to respond to these needs through the provision of relief assistance to close to 3 million people living in counties classified as IPC Phase 4 during the April to July lean season. This includes 137,000 mt of food and USD 53 million in cash-based transfers. In counties with people facing IPC Phase 5 Catastrophic levels of food insecurity, WFP will provide 70 percent rations to prevent famine, while all other targeted locations will receive 50 percent rations. This is to ensure the maximum spread of WFP reduced resources in the most affected and vulnerable communities.

2. Project activities

WFP response will provide this lifesaving food and nutrition assistance by prioritizing communities facing the highest levels of food insecurity and malnutrition using three modalities: in-kind food, cash-based transfers (CBT) and a hybrid food basket depending on the local context and market functionality. This approach is aligned to the humanitarian-development-peace nexus where WFP promotes a “do no harm” approach. WFP promotes livelihoods following community identified priorities while meeting immediate needs of the vulnerable communities. Household targeting is based on vulnerability criteria (e.g., child or female-headed households, households with a high dependency ratio, heads of households who are chronically ill, etc.). These vulnerability criteria are agreed upon by the participating communities and they help identify the most vulnerable households for food assistance. Geographic targeting at county and payam level is guided by Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Survey and Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) results, gender and protection analysis, and other assessments. Due to resource constraints, assistance is only provided to households facing the most severe levels of food insecurity. Alongside its crisis response efforts, WFP, together with FAO, will seek to jointly build the resilience of the most vulnerable people and facilitate self-reliance by promoting value-added solutions (light resilience/early recovery promoting, income-generating activities and peace training called GFD+) that gradually reduce dependence and need for protracted humanitarian interventions by transferring knowledge, skills, and tools. WFP plans to target 129,000 school children in these same areas of high acute food insecurity with safe and nutritious school meals to support learning outcomes, keep girls in schools and reduce child and forced marriage and early pregnancy. In 2024, WFP plans to reach 1,229,407 individuals with 15,637 mt of food under its malnutrition prevention activities. Under this activity, WFP will provide an integrated package of health and nutrition support to malnourished children under 2, pregnant women and girls and new mothers. These activities will include blanket supplementary feeding, gender-responsive social and behaviour change communication (SBCC), nutrition counselling, awareness-raising activities and other essential services. People living with HIV or TB will receive essential health support and specialized nutritious foods to improve their nutrition status (if malnourished) and adherence to treatment.
