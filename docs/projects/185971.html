Project Summary

This project started in January 2021. As part of this intervention, AAH provides technical and organizational support to the health centers of the two health districts regularly affected by shocks in order to better equip them to respond to health / nutrition emergencies and to address psychological distress. Health workers and community volunteers participated in trainings on health / nutrition / mental health and care practices so they can better address the different needs and facilitate referral to activities provided by AAH and other actors in the region. AAH sets up mobile clinics to provide emergency assistance in nutrition / health / mental health for affected and isolated vulnerable populations in the Mayo Sava & Mayo Tsanaga divisions). The package of services and care is delivered free of charge. It targets children under five, pregnant and lactating women, but also disabled people and the elderly among the newly displaced. With regard to the MHCP (mental health and care practices) component, the activities targets children over six and adults with a specific focus on pregnant and lactating women to ensure they follow appropriate care practices and to limit the impact of traumatic stress on pregnancies and infants.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The insecurity in the Far North region, combined with strcuctural weaknesses specific to Sahel, such as slow-paced and limited economic transformation and climate changes, are contributing to deteriorate the humanitarian situation, with expected IPC phases 3 to 5 affecting 32% of the population in the areas of intervention of AAH. 901 042 individuals are expected to face food shortage (IPC, for Mayo-Sava, Mayo-Tsanaga and Logone-et-Chari divisions, Far North, Cameroon, October 2021). The rate of poor food consumption score is 39.54% and no grain banks were created in 2021 (Bulletin de surveillance et d’alerte précoce de la sécurité alimentaire et nutritionnelle pour la région de l’Extrême-Nord, juin 2021.). A SMART survey conducted by AAH in Logone-et-Chari health districts bordering Nigeria shows GAM rate of 13.9% and SAM rate of 1.8% and a mortality rate of children U5 of 1.03/10000/day, with girls being more affected (SMART survey in Logone-et-Chari division, in 4 health districts, Goulfey, Makary and Kousseri, in December 2020. Regarding maternal and child health, indicators of the use of health services also show poor access to care for these targeted groups. According to data from our program (March-October 2021) in Mora health district, ANC 1 rate was 10% even if that of 1st contact was 56%. The rate of assisted delivery in health center was barely 17% with, however, a high rate of home delivery of 41.2%. The rate of attendance of the curative consultation by U 5 was establish at 40% (0.4 contact / child / year). In Koza health district, an attendance rate among children under 5 of 50% was assessed with more than 75% of health workers trained on the IMCI approach. The assisted delivery rate was very low (11.24%) in these health areas.An MHPSS assessment (April 2021) done by AAH in two health districts (Koza and Mora) underlined that 83% of the persons show psychological distress and weak resiliency. Throughout this first year, AAH teams carried out psycho-education adapted to children and adults. Depending on the level of distress of the participants, a mental trauma management protocol is offered at the mobile clinic sites. In the case of responses to shocks in brief interventions, the teams propose emotional stabilization exercises to help individuals better manage stress.Output 1: The specific capacities of affected local populations are strengthened in terms of treatment of simple cases of childhood illnesses, prevention and health and nutritional surveillance.Activity 1: Implementation of the iCCM + approach in areas without access to health careActivity 2: Implementation of the MUAC / oedema strategy by mothers in areas without access to health careActivity 3 Identification of isolated communities and set up of health surveillance system in conjunction with the RRM teamsActivity 4 Training of CHWs on mental health and care practices and community leaders on psychological first aid / referral of GBV cases Output 2: The health facilities of the Mayo Sava & Mayo Tsanaga divisions, the most confronted with the influx of populations affected by shocks, have their response capacities in Nutrition / health / mental health improved, for the most vulnerable groups (PLWs, children 0-5 years)Activity 1: Support to affected health facilities for preparing responses to different degrees of potential shocksActivity 2- Provision of free care for U 5, antenatal and postnatal care for PLWs Output 3: Emergency assistance in nutrition / health / mental health is provided via mobile clinics for affected and isolated vulnerable populations in the Mayo Sava & Mayo Tsanaga divisions (Cameroon) Activity 1: Establishment of 2 mobile clinics offering free PHC including mental health and care practicesActivity 2: Free treatment of children under 5 for common illnesses in mobile clinicsActivity 3: Free antenatal and postnatal care in mobile clinicsActivity 4: Establishment of an emergency referral system
