Project Summary

The humanitarian context in Somalia has remained fragile for a number of seasons; the impact of the prolonged 2016-17 drought is still being felt. Subsequent hurdles, including poor Deyr rains in 2018 (October-December), an unusually hot dry Jilaal season in 2019 (December-March), and abnormal, erratic rainfall during the same year's Gu rainy season (April-June). Even as this needs overview is being written, flash flooding is currently affecting 540,000 people across Middle and Lower Juba, Bay, Lower and Middle Shabelle, and Hiraan - of these, 370,000 have already been displaced. Climatic shocks, combined with other persistent drivers of needs - such as armed conflict and/or displacement - have left around five million Somalis in need of humanitarian assistance.
Shelter and non-food item (NFI) needs are also very high, especially in IDP sites where many IDP households live in makeshift shelters. These makeshift shelters do not provide adequate privacy and protection against weather elements. Lack of tenure security, random evictions and fire incidents further exacerbate the shelter needs of IDPs. About one-third of the Somali population lack essential NFIs.
Access to improved water supply in Somalia is estimated at 32%, while populations with improved sanitation are estimated at a staggering 24%, one of the lowest in the world. Recurring droughts, now a common natural feature of Somalia, combined with internal displacement and a deteriorated network of water points to compound poor access, forcing supply needs often to be met through emergency operations, such as water trucking. Lack of access to clean and safe water has exacerbated incidences of water-borne diseases especially cholera which is endemic in Somalia. This has contributed to a high under-five child mortality rate of 133 per 1,000 live births. Climate change continues to negatively impact Somalia’s ability to achieve food and water security. In rural areas, an inadequate network of pastoral water structures that supply both domestic and livestock water remains the major cause of conflict between pastoralists and settled communities.
The organization proposes sustained solution to improve access to WASH services of the affected people in line with Somali WASH cluster response plan:
1. Provision of sustained access to safe drinking water through construction/rehabilitation of boreholes and water pans. the water pans will done using cash for work. Three strategic boreholes will be equipped with hybrid solar system, this will reduce the fuel cost which has pushed the cost of water upwards. This will benefit 15,000 drought affected men, women, girls and boys in Afmadow and badhade district.
5 shallow wells will also be rehabilitated and fitted with hand pumps. To ensure the sustainability and the management of water facilities, WASDA will ensure to enhance the capacity of WASH committees to keep the maintenance and operation of the water source after the project is faced out. WASDA will ensure the maintenance and the appropriate use of water facilities in close coordination with IDPs mainly camp committees (men and women).
WASDA will also construct 100 flood proof gender sensitive latrines with hand washing facilities and with inside and outside lockable door to ensure the privacy of women and girls. The toilet will also be PLWD friendly. comprehensive Hygiene and promotion awareness campaign will also be carried out to promote adoption of good hygiene and sanitation practices to the affected men, women and children.
WASDA will closely work with people living by including in the project committees and beneficiaries which will be captured in the data segregation during targeting and registrations. Project staff will be trained on how to identify and mitigate barriers and risks faced by the PWD. To create positive gender inclusion WASDA will facilitate participation of women, girls, boys and men through out the implementation of the project.

Project Activities

Activity 1: Rehabilitation of 2 high yielding strategic water bore-holes that will benefit a total of 10000 individuals Water:The project will support the rehabilitation of 2 high yielding water boreholes in Afmadow district. Borehole rehabilitation will include; construction of water storage tanks, generator house, livestock troughs, community tap stands (water kiosks), replacement/extension pipelines, provision of generating sets and solar system, control panels and fast moving spares.Activity 2: Rehabilitation of 6 high yielding shallow wells that will benefit 1800 individuals.The rehabilitation of shallow wells will include: cleaning debris, deepening, construction of aprons, fitting of solar pumps and doing water treatment. Activity 3: construction/Rehabilitation of 2 water pans.The rehabilitation of the water pans will entail desilting of existing water pans using cash for work. The pans will have a volume of 10,000 meters cubic, also opening up inlets and spillways as well as reinforcement of embankments will be done through cash for. This will benefit the vulnerable people in Kudaa and Kamboni whose access to clean drinking water was a heavy task. Activity 4: Water quality analysis and treatment by chlorination and distribution of water purs/aqua-tapsWASDA will purchase from the local market and distribute water treatment chemicals, chlorine power and aqua tabs to communities using contaminated water from unprotected water sources including shallow wells and water pans. WASDA and the Ministry of Water technical officers will conduct routine water tests throughout the project period. WASDA will continue to use water-testing kits procured under the previous project. Water testing will be carried out under the supervision of the WASH manager. During the first 3 months of the project, WASDA will purchase the local market and distribute water collection/ storage containers for 1,400 IDPs and Returnee households. Each household will receive two jerry cans.Activity 5: Formation and capacity building of 10 Water-management committeesWASDA will establish 10 WMCs (one per rehabilitated water source) where there are no water management committees. Each WMC will include 5 members, with at least two women members and will receive operational and management training for the water sources. The training will cover ownership and rights to water, pricing (tariffs), affordability and equity, conflict management, monitoring plans, roles and responsibilities as well as accountability. Activity 5: Construction of 150 household’s latrines, 100 latrines will benefit 600 IDP's households while 50 latrines will benefit 50 host community households.A 300 gender-sensitive Ventilated Pit Latrine (VIP) households’ latrines for IDPs, host community and Returnees with hand washing facilities will be constructed. Three households will be sharing one latrine for the IDP's. The latrines will use simple locally available construction materials (timber, iron sheets and concrete slaps). Cash for work will be used to dig the pits, the beneficiaries will be vouchers to access materials from selected vendors through the procurement process. Skilled labour will procured from the local market. Each latrine will have hand washing unit and will be well lit to ensure safety with solar light.Activity 6: Hygiene Promotion campaign on cholera and AWD prevention (will benefit 20,000 beneficiaries) 12 awareness raising campaigns will be conducted in target IDPs, Returnees and host communities on cholera and AWD prevention. The campaigns will be conducted through community hygiene volunteers. WASDA will produce and distribute IEC materials with safe hygiene promotion messages. Radio messages will be also used to expand reach. Campaigns will focus on: safe excreta disposal; critical methods of hand washing and treatment of drinking water. WASDA will conduct GBV training for the promoters and community volunteers to create GBV awareness in the comm.
