Project Summary

The proposed Project will be implemented in Uror, Twic East and Duk counties of Jonglei state, and in Aweil East and Centre of Northern Bahr el Ghazal state. Poor food security in all locations, coupled with poor WASH practices, is leading to high rates of global acute malnutrition, over WHO’s critical level of 15%. The strong relationship between poor WASH infrastructure and practices, food insecurity and malnutrition is perpetuating high SAM and MAM rates. Use of unsafe drinking water and poor hygiene and sanitation practices are contributing to the high incidence of diarrhea and water-borne diseases in vulnerable communities, sustaining the malnutrition-diarrhea cycle and reducing the nutrient uptake in infants and young children.
The project will target over 200,000 individuals, ensuring access to safe water through rehabilitation/ construction of water points, water quality testing, provision of WASH NFIs, ensuring access to safe sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion.

1 - Needs

Uror, Twic East and Aweil East are predicted to remain at Crisis levels (IPC 3) in January - April 2020, while Duk county is predicted to continue being in Emergencies level (IPC 4) in the same period. However it is likely the counties would be one phase worse with prolonged heavy rainfalls expecting to last till end of November 2019 destroying harvests and livelihoods.The high rates of malnutrition in Jonglei are, in part, being driven by a lack of clean water and sanitation facilities, and poor hygiene practices, which are contributing to the high incidence of diarrhea and waterborne diseases. This affects the nutrient uptake in infants and young children, thereby sustaining the malnutrition-diarrhea cycle. Poor Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices, low food production and consequently low food reserves are also preventing children from receiving balanced diets. Tearfund’s May 2019 SMART survey (233 boys and 250 girls under 5, 442 households) found that only 36% of households were utilizing latrines and 57% reported to have been defecating in undesignated open areas. While over 76% of households reported they wash their hands before cooking foods, only 50% reported to have washed their hands before feeding children; oral-faecal diseases are prevalent as 11% of households reported that a child had suffered from diarrhoea in the previous two weeks. Safety concerns are preventing women and girls from accessing their preferred water points in 65% of settlements in Duk (REACH, May 2019), less than 40% of settlements have access to safe drinking water and over 80% settlements reported less than 50% of households using a latrine (June 2019). WASH related vulnerabilities in both counties have recently been exasperated due to heavy rainfall. Several boreholes have flooded whilst a significant number of school latrines have collapsed (July 2019), meanwhile both Duk and Twic East have seen increases in diarrheal diseases. Poor food security in all locations, coupled with poor WASH practices, is leading to high rates of global acute malnutrition, over WHO’s critical level of 15%. The strong relationship between poor WASH infrastructure and practices, food insecurity and malnutrition is perpetuating high SAM and MAM rates. Use of unsafe drinking water and poor hygiene and sanitation practices are contributing to the high incidence of diarrhea and water-borne diseases in vulnerable communities, sustaining the malnutrition-diarrhea cycle and reducing the nutrient uptake in infants and young children. In Twic East and Duk, poor hygiene and sanitation practices remain prevalent, with increasing numbers of returnees arriving in Twic East and new settlements being established without sufficient access to water. In line with WASH Cluster CO1: “Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package” and WASH Cluster CO3: “Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees.”, Tearfund will (1) Improve water supply infrastructure to increase access to safe drinking water and (2) Facilitate hygiene promotion throughout the targeted communities to encourage behavior change relating to hygiene and sanitation practices. The proposed intervention will enable Tearfund to continue providing critical services addressing access to safe drinking water and increased sanitation facilities, and improved hygiene practices to vulnerable communities in Aweil East & Centre, Uror, Twic East as well expand activities to targeted communities in Duk. Tearfund has a static presence throughout Jonglei and Northern Bahr El Ghazal, with bases in Aweil East and Centre, Uror and Twic East. The proposed project is part of Tearfund’s wider programme, which integrates WASH, Nutrition and Food Security.

2 - Activities

The proposed project will carry out the following activities:(1) Rehabilitation of boreholes: These rehabilitation will be carried out in Karam (2), Motot (1), Pathai (3), Pulchuol (1), Pieri (1), Wuror (2), and Aweil East (5) prioritizing villages with high rates of GAM and SAM. Tearfund will test the water quality to ensure that it remains safe from bacterial contaminants after activity completion. In order to prevent contamination of the borehole after rehabilitation, the WUCs (one at each borehole) will be trained and equipped to fence off the boreholes, in order to prevent animals from accessing them. Pump Mechanics at each rehabilitated borehole will receive technical training including on the construction of platforms and basic repairs in order to ensure they are well capacitated to maintain the boreholes in working order. By deepening their knowledge of borehole repair and rehabilitation, Tearfund is adding to the sustainability of boreholes in situations where overuse frequently causes breakdowns. (2) Water quality testing carried out on boreholes in targeted areas: Tearfund will conduct water quality tests on each of the rehabilitated boreholes to ensure that SPHERE standards for water quality are met. (3) 10 hand pump mechanics trained and equipped: Tearfund has trained pump mechanics linked to WUCs for the last 5 years. This is becoming a sustainable structure for the maintenance of water points, with the pump mechanics embedded within the community (and therefore easily accessible) and WUCs collecting contributions from the community which are put towards the operation and maintenance of the water point. The added value of creating exchanges between new and experienced pump mechanics has become evident from previous/ current interventions. Through this exchange, Tearfund has been able to broaden the knowledge of experienced pump mechanics by introducing them to new topics during the training of the new mechanics. Tearfund will train 10 new pump mechanics - targeting 4 females and 6 males where possible in order to enlarge the pool of pump mechanics in the targeted area and to close the gender gap in WASH. (4) Water User Committees supported: All communities where borehole rehabilitation is undertaken will receive support for setting up WUCs and corresponding training. While additional selection criteria will be defined in consultation with the community, at a minimum, members will be permanent residents of the village, respectful of others and trustworthy. The selected community members will be trained by Tearfund and enlightened on their roles and who they report to and how. Training of WUCs will include the chief and Payam administrator. Trainings will be carried out based on a 14-step curriculum developed by Tearfund’s WASH Advisor, led across a 2-3 day training, based on Tearfund’s experience in Aweil. (5) Construction of households and institutions latrines with hand-washing units addresses issues of open-defecation and the spread of oral-faecal disease. Gender and protection needs will be taken into consideration in the design of the latrines, ensuring safe access for women, girls, men and boys alike. (6) Tearfund will sensitive targeted communities on 5 different areas of hygiene i.e. personal hygiene, food hygiene, water hygiene, domestic or environmental hygiene and safe excreta disposal. This will be conducted through Village Hygiene Promotion Resource Persons (VHRPs). A Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) approach will be applied to address the general hygiene and sanitation issues that affect the targeted area and which increase the risk of fecal oral diseases and, consequently, malnutrition. (7) WASH NFIs will be distributed to households affected by floods to ensure access to basic WASH supplies,including aquatabs, mosquito nets, water buckets, soap, etc, reducing the risk of waterborne diseases and malaria.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, beneficiary accountability is upheld throughout the project cycle. Beneficiaries are consulted on their preferred feedback mechanisms and those feedback mechanisms once set up will be communicated with beneficiaries through community sensitization meetings at the beginning of the project, during sensitization before each distribution or activity and regular community engagement and accountability meeting where it serves as an avenue for feedback and beneficiaries are reminded of other feedback mechanisms they can access.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, through needs assessments, beneficiary feedback mechanisms, regular household visits and community accountability and engagement meetings, we ensure displacement-affected people’s voices are heard. We ensure the committees that we set up will include the most vulnerable displacement-affected people.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, we work closely with existing community based structures like payam leaders, IYCF and local authorities like county health departments and the Water Department. We are also integrating WASH projects into pre-existing community structures such as community sanitation committees. As part of the borehole rehabilitation, Tearfund establishes and trains Water User Committees in the target communities to ensure sustainability of the intervention, and we include at least one government official and the chief in the Water User Committee that we help establish to ensure local ownership.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with the National objective Rural Water Supply and Sanitation policy chapter 4 stating “the overall goal of Rural Water Supply and Sanitation Policy is to improve access to safe water supply and sanitation facilities and to promote hygiene education for all people living in rural areas of South Sudan”.Tearfund work will support and adhere to the national Guiding Principles for Rural Water Supply and Sanitation, Specific Objectives for Rural Water Supply and Sanitation and Key Issues and Priorities (Service Levels and Quality, sanitation and hygiene education, technology choice and sustainable operations and maintenance, community participation, Gender issues,etc). The project also supports the return of IDPs to the place of origin/ preferred resettlement area. The vulnerability of returnees and IDPs are recognised and therefore they are specifically targeted as part of our intervention when returnees settlements are formed, Tearfund ensures they are included and ensure critical WASH services are expanded to new communities.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, vulnerable groups are consulted at the onset of the project separately through FGDs and household visits to ensure their specific protection concerns are addressed in the project design, facilitating a protection environment that is sufficient safe and stable.Particularly in WASH, Tearfund follows SPHERE standards when selecting boreholes to be rehabilitated to ensure that communities, in addition to accessing adequate amounts of potable drinking water, are also within close walking distance of a water point. As women are often tasked with fetching water, this reduces the associated protection risks. In line with this, the Water Users Committees (WUC) will be trained to ensure that the sites are monitored and protected from any loitering of men, and to report any incidents to the village chiefs Furthermore, Tearfund actively promotes the participation of women in WUCs to further ensure that women’s viewpoints are taken into account. All committee members are trained to prioritize vulnerable groups including persons with disabilities, during water collection times. Both at mass awareness campaign as well as house to house visits are included in the hygiene promotion component, with the latter aimed at ensuring vulnerable people can effectively be reached through the intervention. All assessments and surveys aim to have a representative sample of all groups within the community (IDPs, Host communities, women, elderly etc). This ensures that programming is responsive to the needs of all members of the community. For latrine construction, community members will be consulted on the locations and design, with women and adolescent girls, People Living with Disability being consulted separately to ensure gender and disability sensitivity in the design and that vulnerable groups’ concerns are taken into account. Locks and clear demarcation for male and female latrines will be installed in all latrines for safety and protection.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Three approaches are embedded into Tearfund’s programming in the target communities; the implementation of sustainable project activities, rehabilitation and recovery focused interventions, and integration with existing local government and community structures. The project is using a community based partnership approach to ensure the sustainability of project activities and prioritizing high levels of beneficiary participation to foster the conditions for a transition or exit by Tearfund in the future. By training community volunteers to conduct hygiene promotion as well as increasing their knowledge of improved hygiene and sanitation practices, gender and protection and GBV prevention, Tearfund is able to ensure that local people are equipped to continue promoting good hygiene, sanitation, and mitigate GBV risks in their community after the project has ended. Tearfund’s approach to improving water supply infrastructure is designed to encourage community level ownership and leadership in the intervention and encourage rehabilitation and recovery in local communities. Pump mechanics and WMCs will be trained to oversee the management and maintenance of each borehole, ensuring that the facilities continue to function and the knowledge and skills to repair and rehabilitate boreholes remains in the community, even after a transition or exit by Tearfund. The intervention will be integrated with and strengthen local government and community structures so that emergency response functions can be handed over to the local government when sufficient capacity has been developed. Staff from the Water Department, Ministry of Health and County Health Department are engaged in the maintenance of water infrastructure in the feeding centres, while community leaders are included in Water User Committees and trained accordingly so that the WMC is tied to existing community structures and continues to function in the event of Tearfund’s transition or exit.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To ensure women, girls, boys and men are protected from WASH related protection risks including sexual and gender based violence, Tearfund will train WASH staff in gender sensitive WASH programming including gender based violence awareness and psychological first aid. This will assist staff to be able to offer appropriate support when they encounter survivors of gender based violence. Further to this, WASH staff will be trained to facilitate safety audits in order to promote ongoing integration of gender and protection issues within WASH. Staff will receive training on safeguarding and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse as part of our accountability to ensure our programs and operations do no harm. Issues arising from the safety audits will inform the community dialogues on gender, protection and disability inclusion in target communities. Tearfund will create linkages between the WASH project review committee and community based protection committees to ensure protection committees will implement timely survivor centered interventions. Tearfund will forge linkages with GBV actors in program areas to ensure referrals to survivor centered GBV and child safeguarding services.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Tearfund will promote transparency in the complaints and feedback mechanism by involving community members especially women, children, elderly and other excluded groups in designing it, ensuring the mechanism is accessible and clearly communicated to communities. Also a ‘referral pathway’ that explains who to contact when a child protection or safeguarding case is identified whilst outlining the required response will be embedded. The agreed feedback mechanism will be explained by staff and/or community volunteers repeatedly in community meetings, before distributions and community hygiene promotion sessions, and also displayed via banners and display boards in the community.Multiple tools and communication channels will be used to facilitate community feedback including hotlines, suggestion boxes and complaint and feedback desks will be used during all community meetings, project sites including distributions, to enhance greater accountability of the project to affected populations. The various channels will offer individuals different options to provide feedback, either verbal or written. Tearfund staff are also trained on how to handle feedback from community members who may want to remain anonymous, and pass on any concerns to relevant staff members. Furthermore, the community volunteers are aware of how to refer community members if concerns related to other sectors being implemented should arise. Feedback is recorded is incorporated into the current and future project designs. Post-distribution monitoring will also be a key method to ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, such as individuals living with disabilities, elderly or households that are farther away will be targeted by programme staff in PDM household visits. Through the PDM survey and interaction with staff, these vulnerable households will have the opportunity to voice out their feedback and concerns.
