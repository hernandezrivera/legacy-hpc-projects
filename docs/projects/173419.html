Project Summary

To achieve the impact, this project works across two objectives. Firstly general and sexual and reproductive (SRH) health services will be provided through health facilities which will rove around the target areas to provide services to people. The inclusion of general health services is important in ensuring that everyone can easily access the health clinics. However, there will be a particular focus on SRH which is a particular gap in the camps and host communities due to stigma. The project will provide sexual and reproductive health services like ANC, PNC, family planning, STIs, menstrual health management etc. and undertake prevention and response to gender-based violence activities. The project activities are informed by CARE’s on-going gender analysis and operational expertise and are adapted to meet the differing needs of men, women, boys and adolescent girls. It has been observed that there are lowest use of contraceptives, highest maternal mortality rate, significant level of lack of knowledge about sexual reproductive health due to lack of awareness, misconception, sensitization and demand side barriers. 61% of households reported to have received a visit from a community health worker in the 14 days prior to data collection (J-MSNA). Thus, CARE will also work to increase health-seeking attitudes and behavior by visiting house-to-house to inform people of available services, and provide counselling on family planning options. The inclusion of general health services is important in ensuring that everyone can easily access the health clinics. However, there will be a particular focus on SRH which is a particular gap in the camps and host communities due to stigma. CARE proposes an integrated approach to sexual and reproductive health and gender-based violence that will reach 43,700 people living in Camps 12, 13 and 14 at Ukhiya, Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh.

1 - Identified Needs

In August 2017, violence against Rohingya in Myanmar provoked a massive population movement of Rohingya refugees to Bangladesh. The UN independent fact-finding mission concluded that the “scale, brutality and systematic nature” in Rakhine State of “rape, gang-rape, sexual slavery, forced nudity, sexual humiliation, mutilation and sexual assault […] frequently followed by the killing of victims” indicate “a deliberate strategy to intimidate, terrorize or punish a civilian population […].”(A/HRC/39/64, 2018, Ibid. p.15, paragraph 79). There are now 861,545 (UNHCR, September 2020) refugees in Cox`s Bazar out of which 52% are women and girls, mainly residing in what is now the largest refugee camp in the world. Health service provision including sexual and reproductive healthcare (SRH) and the need to treat or refer mental health cases is another critical gap in this response. According to CARE’s July 2019 assessment, only 25% of females and 12% of males of reproductive age reported using contraception with only 39% of female respondents stating that they can make decisions about their own healthcare (CARE, July 2019). There is critical gaps in the health sector, these include family planning provision, particularly long-acting methods; availability and timely access to 24/7 comprehensive clinical management of rape/ intimate partner violence services; and first line support for gender-based violence survivors. SRHR needs of Rohingya women and adolescent girls require urgent attention. Though facility bases delivery is increased but still traditional birth attendants remain the preference for many women as they feel comfortable being delivered by a person familiar to them. According to J-MSNA (July – August 2020) 51% of households among Rohingya community and 31% of households among host community reporting that all pregnant women were enrolled in an antenatal care (ANC) program, therefore still there is gap for enrolling pregnant women in the antennal care program. Conferring J-MSNP (July-August 2020) 9% of individuals among Rohingya refugee community and 14% of host community were reported as having had an illness serious enough to require medical treatment or to have required a regular medical check-up. Due to cultural factors limiting women’s mobility and placing stigma on SRH and GBV, there is a need to access women and girls at their doorsteps. The GBV sub-sector is calling for SRH to be routinely integrated into GBV programming. Recognizing the role of family members in terms of accessing women and girls, thus it is necessary to extensive engagement of men and boys and other family members through household’s visits. J-MSNP (July-August 2020) report says that 61% of households reported having received a visit from a community health workers and a significant number of people (64%) are receiving health services from the facilities of non-government organizations in the camps, thus there is substantial needs to increase household visits by community health workers towards improving health seeking behavior of Rohingya community people as well as increase level of awareness about Covid-19. Household’s visits and health education sessions by health workers will also serve to reduce the stigma and barriers to women accessing GBV and SRH information, as well as surfacing and challenging harmful gender norms, including those which perpetuate gender-based violence. To harmonize the sexual reproductive health services including general health and enlightening health seeking behavior CARE is targeting 43,700 men, women, girls and boys to reach through providing services and information in camps 12, 13 and 14 at Ukhiya, Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh.
