4 - Needs

After 10 years of protracted conflict, increasing economic hardship, and the COVID-19 pandemic, the situation across Syria remains dire. According to the 2021 Needs and Response Summary, 13.1 million Syrian people are in need of protection, and 27% of households report that children show signs of psychological distress. According to the Syria HRP (December 2020), an estimated 2.5 million children face elevated protection risks. A recent study conducted with Dorcas in Aleppo showed that there is a need to address child protection issues in Aleppo. The lack of safe play and recreational spaces in the community appeared to be an issue, where over 11% of respondents do not think their children are able to safely play and have recreation activities in their communities. Whilst the FGD participants from Aleppo mentioned a number of spaces dedicate to play and recreation, they mentioned their need for rehabilitation and protection. In general, there is common concern about the safety of children (29%), most frequent concerns are abduction, kidnapping, harassment, and explosives. Similarly, the FGD with children reveal that they sometimes feel sad and/or scared at school, mostly due to risks of kidnapping and harassment. The survey revealed that 25% of households report one or more of their children working. Regarding the safety of women and girls, 43% of the respondents report one or more concern regarding girls and women’s safety.

5 - Implementation of activities

In order to meet some of the needs of those most vulnerable, Dorcas will engage in numerous protection activities targeting GBV, CP, and also engaging in general protection case management. Activities will include general protection and GBV awareness raising, psychosocial support, case management, provision of assistive devices and hygiene kits for people with disabilities (PwDs), training of caregivers and staff on Protection Mainstreaming, Social Inclusion and Community Support for People with Mobility Impairments, and cash for protection for vulnerable victims of GBV. Dorcas will use an integrated program approach combining provision of protection awareness raising and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) to respond to immediate needs of assistance and identify sustainable solutions. This will be achieved through the use of the social work methodology including individual vulnerability/needs assessments (through interviews and home visitFor child protection, Dorcas suggests to implement preventative activities where games, sports, recreational activities are used to communicate preventative messages to the targeted children at-risk of child labour, children associated with violence or in conflict with the law as well as children who dropped out of school or are unaccompanied. Preventative messages in the sessions will focus on nonviolent communication, child rights, healthy life habits, dangers of drug abuse, road safety, personal hygiene, security measures, self-confidence, and self-protection. Creative techniques including drawing, theatre, role play, or dancing activities have been successfully applied in child protection programming as they help children to internalize and process important messages.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Safe access to services for women and girls: partners work through community centres and one partner will work through physiotherapy centres. In addition to this, mobile teams are deployed to cover the areas surrounding the intervention area. The referral pathway that partners have established also allow to reach more people. AAP: Accountability to the beneficiary population is ensured through consultation, where possible. Through (semi-structured) FGD’s, household surveys, assessments and key informant interviews, community input is collected. This allows to include concerns, suggestions and challenges identified by the target beneficiaries into the program. Through these FGDs it is also possible to get input and adjust beneficiary selection criteria when needed. In case of trainings, FGDs are conducted to make them relevant and acceptable to communities. Gender and inclusion: partners will apply agender lens across all phases of the project to understand the differing needs of men,women, boys, and girls and ensure gender integration is mainstreamed throughoutactivities. Partners will also look at where this lens intersects with other forms ofdiscrimination such as ageism or against people with disabilities to identify barriers thatprevent individual and community participation to humanitarian programming, and alsoidentify capacities for self-reliance. Female staff will be prioritizedduring recruitment to ensure that household visits to women are culturally appropriate andeasily accepted and include targeted awareness to women.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

The main risks that can be seen are tethered to the protracted conflict coupled with economic hardship and COVID lockdowns. In Syria, this includes an increase in domestic violence, especially during lockdown and when women and children do not have access to essential services & education. Another risk can be from negative coping mechanisms, especially in related to the economic crisis. Prevention and Mitigation Measures: Dorcas requires staff signature and adherence to a strict Code of Conduct. Working in the sector of protection, Dorcas also requires staff training on sensitive topics such as integrity and PSEA. Dorcas has has strict policies on child protection and is signed by all staff and volunteers upon hiring. Dorcas has an internal whistleblowing structure and utilizes a robust complaint feedback mechanism with beneficiaries for two-way communication in order to track any potential staff misconduct or wrongdoing

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access: Where possible, partners will be moving to a regional hub-based approach in order to improve access to hard-to-reach areas, and recruit local staff who can access sites more easily, have local knowledge and experience, and rapport with local actors. This will also apply for the position of Field Coordinator, as the current expat Field Coordinator will be leaving from January 2020, the vacancy will be open for both expats as well as Syrian Nationals. In the case of equal qualifications, preference will go to Syrian Nationals.Community (leader) involvement: Based on the learning that it can sometimes be challenging to identify the best candidates for community committees, partners will try as much as possible to involve local authorities from the start of the process of developing criteria for community committee membership to ensure members are selected as objectively as possible.Currency flux: Internal mitigation is to have a small amount of SYP at any time and keep funds in hard currency. In addition, there is a contingency budget in the SJR which could be released in the case of hyperinflation. Finally, partners and the donor need to be flexible to adjust targets and budgets when necessarySecurity: Staff must follow a security management and evacuation plan that is updated periodically. Follow UNDSS security updates and safety advisories and share these with staffMEAL- Dorcas integrates MEAL plans into all projects to track quality and ensure beneficiary feedback and mid-term evaluations are utilized to enhance project quality during the implementation period.
