Needs

The Gaza Strip is in a continuously worsening crisis with visible impact on the living conditions of its 2.1 million people. Main reasons are: ongoing Israeli blockade, internal political division between PA and de-facto government, massive cuts in UNRWA funding by the U.S government, regularly repeating rounds of hostilities, recurrent military incursions and Great March of Return (GMR) mass demonstrations. HLP and eviction concerns constitute a major challenge for the population in Gaza. Women still suffer from limited recognition of inheritance and HLP rights, since their security of tenure is only achieved through their relationship with men. Especially widows and divorcees are vulnbeerablevulnerable, since they need to go through complex inheritance proceedings and they risk facing societal consequences with their families if they resort to formal legal mechanisms to claim their HLP rights.Populations and protesters in ARA have borne the brunt of Israeli incursions into the zone during military operations. Several violations have been committed in the area over the years, and victims have until now neither been afforded recourse nor have perpetrators been held accountable due to restrictions on access to justice placed by restrictive Israeli legislation. Due to the blockade, the freedom of movement from Gaza of Palestinians and others remains a challenge. The need to travel to pursue professional or educational opportunities, visit family, achieve family reunification or in needfor of medical assistance is hampered by an increasingly restrictive Israeli permit system, which in addition makes it virtually impossible to travel out of Gaza for those residents in Gaza without identity documents. In October 2018, PC conducted 2 rapid needs assessments on evictions due to inability to pay rent as a result of worsening economy. Main findings on legal aid:- Generalized lack of legal awareness among tenants on their HLP rights and responsibilities and eviction process, and on the rights and responsibilities of their landlords. Landlords’ understanding of the eviction process and minimum standards is still low. 42% of landlords rent their houses based on a verbal agreement. Tenants constantly feel insecure in their rental units due to, for example, fear and constant threats from landlords, lack of privacy, and lack of stability resulting from frequent moving from one rental unit to another.- Most vulnerable tenant-groups: 1) 2014-IDPs, non-refugees and hardship-cases, at critical risk of eviction due to not receiving TSCA while waiting to access reconstruction funds; 2) Hardship-cases registered with MoSD and UNRWA evicted and displaced in public areas/under eviction/at critical risk of eviction; 3) Refugees from the Region, of Palestinian and Syrian origin, at critical risk of eviction.In October 2018, PC also conducted a needs assessment on GMR. Main findings on legal aid:- There is no legal information being provided to GMR-injured and families of GMR-deceased on their rights, available legal procedures to seek justice and/or compensation, or legal actions to receive adequate medical treatment. Neither is legal aid being provided to them to facilitate access to these rights. Only 10% of interviewees reported that they know their rights in case of an attack resulting in injury, death or destruction of property and know where to go to seek more information and legal support.- Women who have become widows as a result of the GMR are now more vulnerable to eviction (as their HLP rights and security of tenure depended on their husbands);, to overcrowding and loss of privacy;, to increased responsibilities as they will be taking care of injured or disabled members of the family;, and to shifting roles to become the family breadwinner.

Activities

Whilst the Israeli system remains inadequate for protecting HLP, freedom of movement and legal status rights under international law, legal interventions to engage Israeli and local authorities can mitigate the effect of current violations and prevent future violations.In accordance with the Protection Cluster SRP (objectives 1 & 2), ICLA will provide:• Legal information, counselling, assistance and representation to persons and communities facing HLP issues and/or eviction, access restrictions and IHL violations• Legal assistance through court representation and/or CDR to women claiming their HLP and inheritance rights in Gaza• Legal information, counselling, assistance and representation to persons and communities facing access restrictions, with focus on medical patients and undocumented and stateless Palestinians and others in Gaza• Legal information, counselling, assistance and representation to persons and communities facing IHL violations• Coordination between International and Palestinian legal aid providers through the LTF, and active participation in fora such as HCT, Protection & Shelter Clusters• Advocacy on legal issues related to HLP (incl. for women), freedom of movement, lack of legal identity and accountability for IHL and IHRL violationsAn integrated legal response will be provided through PC and partner organizations. The role of our partner organizations will be:- PCDCR: court representation, mediation & arbitration, cooperative dispute resolution mechanisms (with focus on women’s rights), awareness sessions for mukhtars and community leaders.- Al Mezan: monitoring and documentation of Israeli violations, especially in the ARA and legal aid to seek justice/redress on behalf of victims.- PCHR: monitoring, documentation and legal aid on access restrictions in front of Palestinian administrative bodies and Israeli authorities to facilitate freedom of movement outside Gaza. - Gisha and HaMoked: legal counselling and assistance on issues related to freedom of movement in front of Israeli authorities.PC will provide partners with technical support on IHL violations, HLP, and access restrictions through in-house lawyers. It will also build their capacity in terms of M&E and financial compliance. Important to note that PC upholds ICLA Global Guidelines to ensure all clients are received and provided legal services with privacy and confidentiality held to highest standards. This also applies to all partners. When/ if receiving a client requiring a legal service outside ICLA scope of intervention, the case will be referred to the specialized agency member of the LTF.When/ if receiving a client that require protection services beyond/in addition to the legal services, the case will be referred to the specialized agency member of the protection cluster. Whilst legal aid services will be provided without discrimination, there will be a particular focus on the ARA communities. When intervening at a community level, ICLA will hold consultations with community and take their consent beforehand for any action regarding their legal status in respect of do no harm principle.PC Shelter/Wash, ICLA and UDOC staff will work together to respond to the multiple needs of beneficiaries when feasible. Furthermore, ICLA with UDOC will ensure community engagement on different levels of implementation and echoing community feedback. In addition, PC’s advocacy unit will seek to maintain the humanitarian situation of Gaza on the international agenda in an effort to shape priorities and address the impact of the blockade through an evidence-based approach with the consent of the involved individuals while upholding the do no harm principle. All beneficiaries’ files and data will be preserved in the highest standards of security and confidentiality. No sharing or referral of files for any legal intervention will be carried without the consent of clients.

Indicators

Monitoring and evaluation of the project will be conducted through a combination of statistical analysis, visits to the community, beneficiary surveys and end of project internal evaluations of results to ensure community feedback is considered throughout the project cycle and for redesigning/ reshaping the intervention according to their needs. Partners will provide monthly updates on services provided and results. The results will be used to show progress against indicators. Beneficiary surveys will be conducted to assess the relevance of services provided by partners and PC, and to gauge the beneficiaries’ abilities to act to defend their rights against violations. Policy change outcomes will be monitored by documenting instances in which power holders have acted positively on information provided under the project. All data will be gender disaggregated.Progress on the ICLA legal response in the Gaza Strip will be measured through the following outcome indicators:- 70% of surveyed beneficiaries who report ability to act on their rights (HLP rights, freedom of movement, IHL violations)- 50% of female beneficiaries assisted who report resolution of an HLP issue by using cooperative dispute resolution and/or court representation- 60% of beneficiaries assisted who report obtaining ownership documents, a rental contract or a resolution of an HLP issue- 80% of beneficiaries participating in information sessions who report the information presented will assist them in their daily lives- 20 instances of change in policy or practice by authorities or stakeholders in which PC information has played a contributing role Beneficiary targets (3,500) under the project will be as follows:• 1,200 persons receiving legal counselling in Gaza Strip (900 males and 300 females) - 700 legal counselling for HLP, 300 legal counselling for victims of IHL/IHRL violations, and 200 legal counselling for freedom of movement. • 800 persons in the Gaza Strip receiving legal assistance (600 males and 200 females) - 500 legal assistance for HLP, 200 legal assistance for victims of IHL/IHRL violations, and 100 legal assistance for freedom of movement• 1,500 persons receiving information services in the Gaza Strip (750 males and 750 females)
