Project Summary

In January 2019, Polish Humanitarian Action (PAH) received requests from community leaders and PAH’s community-based DRR teams that nine villages, located in Jowhar West, were experiencing complete water sources dry up and were forced to travel between 8-15 km to collect water each day. The villages, namely, Buulo Maxamud, Khalafow, Guumeys, Gumarey, Guumeysta, Caliwarabey, Duray, Maryan Keynan, Dhay-Gawan are mainly pastoralists and agrarian communities practicing on a small scale. They are highly dependent on rain to fill the water canals connecting them to the Shebelle river. These canals, are their source for not only drinking and domestic use but also for irrigation and livelihood purposes. Based on the initial assessment conducted in February 2019, PAH identified that without humanitarian intervention, the villages were at risk of slipping into IPC 3 & 4 status. In response, as of February, PAH has been provided water via trucking due to their WASH vulnerability and food insecurity and lack of available WASH infrastructure in the area; coupled with high population density and the absence of a permanent water source as evidenced by the attached needs assessment report.
PAH is submitting this proposal to increase the resilience and recovery capacity of these at-risk communities in the long term to prevent further deterioration and higher severity of needs. Moreover,, the project will complement the ongoing nutrition project with the objective of contributing the reduction of morbidity and mortality among moderately malnourished under 5 children and PLW in Jowhar district.
The exact activities prioritized based on the allocation strategy (SO4 “Increase resilience capacity of at-risk communities and promote complementary interventions of development partners in social services” and WASH cluster objective to “provide sustainable access to safe water and sustainable sanitation services” to support the integrated approach include:
•The provision of sustainable access to safe water through the construction of strategic water infrastructure. Namely, the construction of a borehole, identified with a hydro-geological survey and environmental impact assessment. In line with the strategy, the borehole will have equipped with Gen-set and other infrastructures such as Water kiosks, and animal troughs.
•Borehole construction will be followed by the extension of existing water infrastructures through pipelines and the construction of 4 water kiosks in a strategic location in the villages targeted in this proposal. This will increase access to water in a sustainable and functional way as well as mitigate the Gender-Based Violence and protection risks associated with women and girls walking a long distance to fetch water. The villages targeted are Buulo Maxamud, Khalafow, and Guumeys.
•Construction of 12 handpump shallow wells in 6 villages: Gumarey, Guumeysta, Caliwarabey, Duray, Maryan Keynan, Dhay-Gawan. The depth of the shallow wells will be increased to 15m to ensure that water is available during the dry season.
•PAH will establish gender-balanced water committees to manage water points and ensure equity of access, functionality, and sustainability. The committees will additionally uphold disease mitigation measures such as borehole shock chlorination, sanitation surveys, and site cleaning to ensure the safety and continuity of services of the water facilities beyond the project period.
•Access to adequate sanitation will also be ensured through the construction of communal latrines, which will be designed in line with the protection guidelines (covered with a roof, solar light, lockable from inside and outside). Hygiene promotion and hygiene kits distribution activities will be conducted for a period of 6 months. Hygiene promoters will be hired and trained on the door to door hygiene promotion sessions and reporting tools to ensure monitoring of the progress of the campaign.

Project Activities

1. Water point construction or rehabilitationIn order to provide reliable and sustained access to sufficient safe water to vulnerable communities affected by water scarcity, PAH will construct a new deep borehole, at least 300m, in West Jowhar serving nine villages in Middle Shebelle. The villages targeted are Buulo Maxamud, Guumeys, and Gumarey. They have been targeted based on the communities’ severe WASH vulnerability and food insecurity; lack of available WASH infrastructure in the area, coupled with high population density; and the absence of a permanent water source (currently water is trucked from distant boreholes, highlighting the need for a more sustainable intervention) as evidenced by the needs assessment report. Possible site selection was carried out with Jowhar district authority and the community leaders on the 11th of March 2019. Khalafow Villages was identified as the central location for the borehole drilling. 2.Water point construction or rehabilitationPAH intends to construct 12 shallow wells with complete hand pumps in 6 villages. Activities will take into consideration both environmental and functional sustainability. Due to the fact that project locations are in a remote drought and flood-affected areas, standard shallow well construction costs will be higher in order to ensure the sustainability of the project, and due to increased operational costs. Sustainable design features will include: 3.Chlorination (standalone separate to O&M)To ensure the functional sustainability of the water sources constructed and rehabilitated, PAH will ensure that all water sources are free from microbiological (pathogens) and chemical contamination and that contamination testing takes place both at the water source and at household level to avoid contamination in delivery system e.g. piping systems, water tanks, distribution tap-stands, and water kiosks. In order to ensure this in all areas of implementation, PAH will carry out monthly water source monitoring. 4. Capacity building (water committees and WASH training)PAH will provide capacity building and training to community members in order to ensure the sustainability of community water management systems. For water management at community level PAH will select and train Water Source Managers (50% female) from the respective villages who will be responsible for the day to day management and maintenance of the six water kiosks. 5. Latrine construction or rehabilitationPAH will construct 80 communal latrines under this project. The targeted HH/ latrine sharing ratio will be 5 families per latrine - 30 people per facility (targeted 2400 beneficiaries). Latrine allocation will be preceded by a mapping of existing safe facilities (i.e. erected through community contribution) which will be included in the HH sharing ratio count. Women girls and people with disabilities will be involved in site identification to choose locations safe and accessible to them. PAH will ensure latrines distance to the household is not > 50M. Latrines will be constructed in line with the protection guidelines (covered with a roof, lockable from inside and outside), to ensure safety and comfort for female users. Some of the latrines will also be adjusted to the needs of the elderly and PWSD6.Community Hygiene promotionAs part of the complementary of the water provision, sanitation, and hygiene kits distribution activities, PAH will implement Hygiene promotion activity under this project in Jowhar. Community Hygiene promoters will be selected from all the targeted villages under the project. A total 9 hygiene promoters will be trained and engaged for a hygiene promotion campaign in 6 months of the project duration supervised by PAH WASH officer.7.Hygiene kit distribution (complete kits of hygiene items)Distributions will be done by trained Community Hygiene Promoters (CHPs) to ensure beneficiaries know how to use items received.

Mitigation Measures

2a/2b. Exclusion and marginalizationIn Middle Shebelle, minority groups and newly displaced groups are highly vulnerable to protection risks, exploitation and exclusion from humanitarian assistance, this social/economic vulnerability further contributes to marginalization denying them from benefiting community-based safety nets or social protection. This combined with lack of any legal protection framework or law enforcement makes the community prone to wide range of abuses. Forced-taxation and related abuse of human rights is a frequently encountered based on PAH findings. The process is rarely transparent and beneficiaries learn about the expected contribution usually only after they receive assistance. to increase accountability and empower minority groups PAH will has undertaken context analysis and consulted this groups during assessment to identify specific vulnerability and risks and to address this PAH will promote community engagement and participation at the design, implementation and monitoring of project activities while giving specific attention to marginalized groups. A community based committee representing minority groups, community and local administration will be created tasks to identify how participation of marginalized and people with disability will the promoted, members from this group will be selected to join community hygiene promoters, water points and latrines caretakers and will participate in beneficiaries’ selection process to ensure minority groups interest is protected.3b/3c/3d-External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selectionThis project will ensure the external stakeholders should not interfere the beneficiaries’ selection process. The community will be actively involved in the selection of beneficiaries through community-based targeting during the targeting of hygiene kits distribution and their participation upheld in the entire project cycle. Beneficiaries selection criteria will be developed and inputs from the community representatives will be incorporated for a fair and transparent mode of selection. The project will ensure effective participation of women in implementation and monitoring, and equitable gender representation in decision making processes4a/4b/4c/4d. Protection mainstreaming:Protection will be at the core of this intervention ensuring that the proposed response contributes to saving lives, ensuring safety and security, alleviating suffering providing dignity for women, girls, boys, and men, particularly marginalized groups such as older people, people living with disabilities, and minority groups. The project will also embrace protection principles from the SPHERE Standards: PAH will aim to avoid exposing women and girls, and the rest of the beneficiaries from further harm that may result from project implementation; ensure people’s access to impartial assistance especially for women, girls and the minority groups in the community; assist beneficiaries to claim their rights through the establishment of a solid and effective feedback response mechanism that is accessible to all. PAH will gather gender segregated information for planning and reporting and will ensure that service delivery considers and addresses the distinct needs of women, girls, boys and men in a systematic manner. To mitigate the risk of violence against women, children and vulnerable groups when accessing WASH services, the infrastructure location (distance from settlements) and design in all project sites, will ensure safe and equal access. The queuing time will be reduced to less than 30min per water point which will also decrease potential protection risks. Latrines, with hand washing facilities will be constructed, based on community consultation on their location and design, ensuring needs of people with disabilities and elderly are addressed as well as gender-specific protection risks mitigated.

Protection of Environment/SustainabilityThe most sustainable element of this project stems from the basic fact that the construction of a borehole resolves the need to provide sustainable water trucking to the same vulnerable populations year after year during the dry season. However, PAH will further ensure environmental sustainability by undertaking a hydrogeological survey and environmental impact assessment prior to constructing the borehole. Moreover, with respect to sanitation, all communal latrines will be constructed/rehabilitated using standard designs to avoid contamination of groundwater and surface water (1.3m and 30m) (WASH technical standard). Details of latrine design will also be consulted with the communities, especially women to increase a sense of ownership and encourage latrine use. At each latrine, a handwashing station will be installed (total of 145 hand washing stations). The quality of the construction materials used for the latrines is resistant to wind and water intrusion since Jowhar is highly affected by flood during the rainy season.
