Project Summary

Medair will be implementing an MHPSS project in Leer to improve the psychosocial well-being of women in Leer and to support those suffering from mental health conditions. The project includes a network of mother-to-mother Cascade Groups and integrated MH services in 3 health facilities in Leer. The project is supported by staff trained in appropriate modalities of mental health and psychosocial support. Child-friendly spaces complement Medair’s integrated programming in Leer

1 - Needs

The 2021 HNO estimates that 4.77 million women, girls, men and boys face protection risks and violations in the year to come. Insecurity, violence and natural disaster causes continuous protection concerns, particularly to vulnerable groups such as displaced populations, women, children, older persons and persons living with disabilities. GBV is a main concern to which primarily women are subjected: 97% of GBV survivors are female (HNO, 2021). The continued crisis is resulting in high levels of psychological distress taking its toll on mental health. The 2020 FSNMS assessment found that 30 per cent of children had behavioural change, showing signs of distress due to repeated exposure to conflict and shock. Additionally, the 2021 HNO highlights that South Sudan had some of the highest suicide rates in the world and that 59% of South Sudanese report signs of distress including depressive symptoms. Mental health services remain scarce in South Sudan, providing none or very limited support to those suffering from mental health conditions and their families as those affected find more difficulties in providing for their families or caring for their children. Leer County has been identified as having severe protection needs. Years of recurring conflict and brutal violence in Leer County has included killing, rape, abduction and loss of homes and livelihoods for thousands of people, as well as large-scale displacement. This has resulted in extensive trauma among the population and high MHPSS needs in the area. The security situation in Leer has been relatively stable in 2020 although the situation remains vulnerable. This allows early recovery, but more importantly allows strengthening of resilience of the local population. Without appropriate services the ability of the population to cope with further shocks will be severely compromised. Medair’s protection programming in Leer will be integrated alongside its ongoing nutrition, health, and WASH programme.

2 - Project activities

This project includes Medair MHPSS services in Leer County, which is integrated with nutrition programming and community-based health care services. Medair provides integrated mental health services according to mhGAP-HIG in three facilities in Leer County. These services are implemented by MH staff trained on the mhGAP-HIG protocols and receiving technical oversight by the MH supervisor and MHPSS Project Manager. This activity provides access to first-line management for mental, neurological and substance use conditions to a population highly affected by years of conflict. The services are integrated with local health care and not provided by any other partner accessibly from Leer. In addition, Medair implements a peer-to-peer group module on psychosocial support topics for use in cascade group networks. This project empowers women and builds their resilience through training and supporting them in positive coping mechanisms, parenting skills, and how to stay safe and to reduce stigma with regard to mental health and SGBV. Furthermore, the cascade groups create an affective system to raise community awareness of available mental health and SGBV services and enables referral pathways from the community to clinical support services. The close relationship as a result of this successful project further allows Medair to continue to address sensitive subjects like SGBV with influential members of the community aiming to create lasting change and protection for vulnerable groups. Lastly, Medair is implementing child friendly spaces in the supported health and nutrition clinics. These spaces provide a safe environment for women and children in order to address their protection and psychosocial needs. Medair works through a team of trained MHPSS staff who receive regular supervision and refresher training.

3 - Project indicators

Medair will measure the quality of it programme and success of the intervention through the following objectives: PO1. • # of women, men, girls and boys provided with individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services, parenting skills)• (GP)Number of women, men, girls and boys provided with individual, group, specialised PSS and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services, ) PO2. • (GBV)Number of women, men, girls and boys reached through awareness raising activities• # of staff/frontline workers trained on GBV minimum Standards (Case management, PSS etc.) Medair will collect project data on a weekly and monthly basis from all project locations, which is monitored by the MHPSS Project Manager. This data includes new and follow-up MH consultations and participation in the cascade groups. The MHPSS Project Manager compiles monthly sitreps in which progress against indicators is measured. Medair also submits monthly reports to all relevant partners, including the 5Ws report to the Protection Cluster. Medair has a separate monitoring, evaluation and learning sector which is responsible for measuring projects’ success using standard and custom monitoring tools. The M&E team ensures all the collected data are accurate and without double counting and evaluate the project against the indicators. If gaps are identified and the project is not on track, corrective measures will be taken and the project will be re-aligned. Technical oversight of data collection and progress measurement is provided by Medair’s Health Advisor.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Medair will be transparent with the affected population by providing them with accessible and timely information about PSEA and set up confidential feedback collection mechanism as mentioned above. Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Cascade Groups’ in Leer County, in which information is cascaded through communities. Additionally, Medair will provides information about PSEA to beneficiaries through focus group discussions and in meetings with the community and community leaders. All Medair staff receive training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Medair ensures all members of the affected population’s voices are equally heard and their concern, comments, and perceptions will be taken into consideration during programme design and implementation. The feedback collection mechanisms and focus group discussions particularly target most vulnerable members of the community such as marginalised community members, people with disability, individuals who are not literate, women and girls.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Medair implements mother-to-mother peer-support ‘Cascade Groups’ in Leer, in which information is cascaded through communities. In addition, Medair carries out focus group discussions with different gender and age groups, and key informant interviews that will inform the type of assistance the affected populations need, and the modalities through which they believe assistance is best delivered. Barrier analysis will be carried out to understand and identify barriers experienced by the community in relation to protection related issues. During project implementation, Medair will undertake regular meetings with the affected communities and community leaders and feedback mechanisms will be put in place. Medair will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Medair will respond to all complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place and conducts and community wide awareness raising session will be conducted. Medair tracks beneficiary feedback, the response to the feedback, and satisfaction of the community in an AAP databased monitored by the M&E team

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries’ feedback will be collected with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconducts by programme staff. Medair analyses all feedback thoroughly to adjust the projects design according to the beneficiaries’ interest, context and resources. In situations where Medair is unable to make change due to seen and unforeseen reasons, the affected beneficiaries will be informed and further dialogue will take place to find alternative approaches.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The main risks Medair identified are the access of marginalised groups in community consultation and decision-making processes and the risk that beneficiaries are not accessing complaint and feedback mechanisms. This is based on the cultural understanding Medair has in which marginalized groups are not actively engaged in their communities, and in which women have less say in family and community matters. Through Medair’s cascade groups good relationships have been build with the local community, allowing staff to know the different groups and identify who is particularly vulnerable. In this regard Medair pays particular attention to those with mental illness. Medair actively invest in engaging these groups through facilitation access to activities, having where appropriate focus group discussion with specific vulnerable groups, and conducting interviews about the experience of beneficiaries with mental illness. Medair also has built in feedback mechanism, and since this in integrated in the mother to mother groups it allows especially women to access complaint and feedback mechanisms.
