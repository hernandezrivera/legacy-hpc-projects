Project Summary

In order to fight the spread of the Coronavirus, the Government of Nigeria has enforced several measures, such as the closure of public places, including schools and markets and called on the population to reduce movements. These measures with restrictions of activities in different sectors affects everyone but directly affect women and girls disproportionately. Just like any other global emergencies, COVID19 could magnify all existing inequalities, including gender inequalities. Majority of women mainly operate in the informal sector and have very limited economic power. They are also at the frontline of the health emergency, as they are first responders within their communities and within health facilities - therefore, likely to be disproportionately affected by the effects of COVID19. The situation for women and girls in the Northeast already suffering from the impact of the ongoing conflict is potentially exacerbated as they struggle with food insecurity, suffer being exposed to COVID 19 infection as they provide care and experience a decline in their income which will potentially expose them to trafficking, sexual exploitation and negative coping mechanism such as survival sex and in overall increase their vulnerability to COVID 19 infection. Since the beginning of the COVID19 pandemic in Nigeria, and in Northeast in particular, interventions have put strong emphasis on the medical aspects without considering the importance of addressing other socio-cultural factors that affect the lives of women and girls. Within the lockdown, trained women in hygiene products including antiseptic soap and hydrogel using online technology such as Zoom. UN Women approach seeks to harness opportunities to strengthens women’s resilience to overcome the negative consequences of the COVID19 pandemic building on existing initiatives UN Women Project objectives are aligned with existing national, state and humanitarian frameworks with focus on strengthening women resilience during the COVID19 by empowering women’s MSMEs, women leaders to design and deliver gender sensitive emergency aid.
Component 1: Develop and implement an aggressive communication campaign, awareness programs tailored for the needs of women and girls. This will entail designing gender-sensitive educational materials that considers the specific needs of women and girls facing the COVID19 emergency as well as the existing barriers for women and girls to access quality information and other essential services. This will include materials focusing on prevention measures, and communication materials adapted to illiterate women and translated in local languages.
Component 2: Empower women’s CSOs and networks, as well as women led MSMEs to design, produce, deliver hygiene products to women and girls that are most affected by the COVID19 outbreak
At least 10,000 resilience kits including sop, hand sanitizers, face masks will be produced and delivered, based on the consultations with local women’s networks and the state Ministry of Women Affairs and Ministry of Health about the relevant items to be included. The kit will contribute to protect women, taking into consideration their difficulty to influence the spending decisions to purchase soap, disinfectant, and other hygiene products.
Component 3: Social Accounting for gender mainstreaming in the COVID-19 Reponse
This component will support gender-focused monitoring of ongoing interventions by enhancing the capacity of women networks to monitor, document and share information on support to vulnerable communities are reaching and impacting women and girls. Collection of gender and age disaggregated data at the community level through women’s networks UN Women and other humanitarian actors will enable a better understanding of how women and girls are affected by the COVID19. Findings will be addressed, in collaboration with the sectors, to the COVID19 task force to orient policies and interventions from a gender perspective.

1 - Needs

Although the COVID19 is not discriminating according to sex, traditional gender roles and pre-existing gender disparities put women at a higher risk of contracting the disease. Women are the primary caregivers for sick members, also accompanying them to health facilities. Moreover, women have a higher need to access health services, especially for reproductive health (antenatal and postnatal care). Another risk factor is the higher mobility of women in the community and surroundings to perform domestic work charges, such as water collection, collective clothes-washing, and purchase of food in the markets. Women’s reduced access to information on COVID19 prevention due to the lower rate of literacy further exacerbates their vulnerability. High incidence of SGBV and sexual abuse is persistently increasing during emergencies and lockdowns periods and perpetrated by intimate partners Given the fact that a higher number of women are engaged in the informal sector, characterized by daily income with no social security or savings, they are hit by economic losses that can lead to food insecurity at household level.
