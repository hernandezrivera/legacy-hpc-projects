Project Summary

The project seeks to provide gender-based protection services aimed at reducing negative practices towards women and girls in conflict situations through a safe space center for women in the northern countryside of Aleppo, Azaz area. Camps and local community The duration of the project will be one year only
The project will provide the following activities:
1- Implementation of mental health and psychosocial support activities
2- Implementing awareness-raising activities targeting IDPs and the host community of men, women, girls and boys about protection risks related to gender-based violence
3- The project will provide referrals only for cases that it receives from local communities, and the referral process will take place within the protection pathways, taking into account confidentiality and privacy.
4 - Building the capacity of protection workers in the field of gender-based violence
5 - Training activities related to life skills and vocational training that seek economic empowerment will be provided
Work will be carried out in accordance with the IASC guidelines for integrating GBV interventions into humanitarian action.
The project team will consist of 1 project manager, 1 female psychologist, 5 male - 5 female outreach workers, 4 psychosocial supports, 4 contract trainers,
Contracting a means of transportation to transport the mobile awareness team
Al-Sham Humanitarian Foundation will form community committees from the same communities and train them on gender-based violence topics such as; basic concepts of gender-based violence,
. Strategies for the empowerment of women and girls will be strengthened, with a particular focus on the most vulnerable groups (ex-adolescents, divorced and widowed girls, women and girls with disabilities, and older women) to prevent gender-based and gender-based violence.
You will work in accordance with inter-agency guidelines and tools to benefit from capacity building. The project will target host communities, IDPs and returnees, community centers and camps. All activities are aligned with the 2023 Humanitarian Response Plan as well as the Protection Cluster Strategic Objectives. The project will coordinate with affected populations and stakeholders and is designed to promote gender balance. The project aims to involve people with disabilities by classifying and identifying types of disabilities, providing them with community advice during project design and implementation, addressing the risks they face, and activating their participation in the project through employment and activities. Al-Sham Humanitarian Foundation is an active member of the Gender-Based Violence Sub-Cluster, and this strengthens the coordination mechanism and contributes to effective response interventions. It will also coordinate with the focal points within the project area to ensure further interventions.
The monitoring plan will ensure that:

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance- a 21% increase compared to 2021 - with needs increasingly exacerbated by the economic downturn. Of the 13.4 million people in need, nearly ten million (75 percent) are women and children; Including approximately 2.3 million females and males with disabilities aged 12 years and over. The above needs are underlined in the Aswat 2022 report which notes that according to a group of women and girls who participated in community focus groups in 2021, a range of different forms of violence affected them similarly to previous years. Sexual, emotional, psychological, and physical violence was mentioned as occurring commonly within the community, as well as early marriage, economic violence, and denial of opportunities and resources. The same report, Aswat 2021,stated that divorced and widowed women and girls continue to face various forms of gender-based violence, which have been more evident in the emerging coronavirus crisis. Also, social conditions greatly affected women and girls and helped expose them to risks of discrimination and societal violence A group of women also mentioned various forms of discrimination directed towards them, such as work or access to services, in addition to issues related to divorce and inheritance. The project will help respond to current needs through awareness-raising sessions, capacity building and implementation of referrals to specialized service providers

Risk2: Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging and lack of support from family members to allow women and girls to travel to WGSS and access services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Low level of obligations related to sexual exploitation and abuse, women disproportionately exposure to misconduct by humanitarian workers at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, and low level of safe reporting of complaints of SEA. Mitigation: 1 - Impose mandatory training on protection from PSEAon all employees, including management, field staff, day laborers, volunteers, contractors and vendors, in each area where there are active processes to ensure that employees are aware of their obligations related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and to equip employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Response to both. 2 - Create mechanisms for reporting protection from SEA at the community level, including establishing contact points, according to the location and settings for reporting and posting. 3 - Engage with communities and key stakeholders in strengthening community complaints mechanisms, including determining the provision of victim assistance services in all targeted locations. Conduct regular awareness-raising activities on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, policies, standards, and codes of conduct related to protection from sexual exploitation. 4 - Provide specific awareness sessions to target groups such as women, girls, boys, and men in reception centers, camps, sites for displaced people, host communities, and society All project personnel sign the Code of Conduct for PSEA
