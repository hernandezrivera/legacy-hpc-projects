Project Summary

The proposed project entails to contribute to reducing the incidents of water raltes (borne, based and washed) diseases and diseases caused by poor and inadequate sanitation and hygiene practices. This can be achieved by increasing access to safe drinking water, safe excreta disposal and improved hygiene practices among displaced and other vulnerable communities in targeted states.
HA-UK with its local partners plans to target 95,700 (48,807 M - 46,893F ) individual through providing sustainable access to safe water supply, improve sanitation coverage and enhance hygiene practices to IDPs and people effected by economic crisis, COVID -19 Pandemic, climate change (flood. drought), malnutrition, and food insecurity in Blue Nile and South Kordofan states. HA-UK will ensure appropriate and cost effective technology products and clean energy such as Solar energy are installed for safe drinking water. Safe handwashing facilities will be provided in schools, hospitals and villages. Demo latrines will be constructed for the most vulnerable families to encourage all the families in villages to have latrines and avoid open defecation. There will be monitoring of water treatment at the source to ensure safe wate is provided to the communities. Most vulnerable families will be provided hygiene kits. Massive hygiene promotion campaign will be launched in schools, villages and health services.

1 - Needs

Blue Nile and South Kordofan states remain one of the affected states in Sudan. Moreover, the deterioration of economy, Corona Virus Pandemic, food insecurity and climate change have resulted in increased the number people in need in the target states. The HNO 2022 indicated that in Blue Nile state At Tadamon - BN ranked as the lowest locality have access to improve water sources and sanitation coverage and Kurmak ranked the second lower locality. Total of 29,685 people in need (IDPS / vulnerable communities) were estimated in kurmuk locality while ElTadmon locality not indicated the number in HNO -2022. For South Kordofan State HNO -2022 estimated 36,309, and 36,169 people in Abu gbieha and Abu krshola respectively were targeted for WASH services. This target includes IDPS, vulnerable communities and returnees.WASH SDG- 6 planning and budget report estimated that in Blue Nile state access to BASIC water supply for the state’s schools is estimated at 63.0 percent while access to BASIC sanitation is estimated at 73.4 percent. Out of the state’s 331 schools, only 209 have BASIC water and 243 have BASIC sanitation facilities. On the other hand, the report stated that in South kordofan state access to BASIC water supply for the state’s schools is estimated at 57.5 percent while access to BASIC sanitation is estimated at 61.7 percent. Out of the state’s 601 schools, only 346 have BASIC water and 371 have BASIC sanitation facilities. The latest S3M - 2018 survey estimated that in Blue Nile the proportion of house hold with access to improved latrines is 44.29 percent, 37.37 percent non-share improved latrines and 20 percent practice open defecation. On the other hand, South Kordofan had the lowest percent in access to sanitation facilities. Whereas proportion of households with access to improved toilet facilities is 16.48 percent, 10.07 percent non-share improved latrines and 49.49 percent open defecation. Low access to basic hand washing facility have recognized into tow states. The percent of access to basic hand washing facilities estimated by 3.36 in Blue Nile state and 0.12 in South Kordofan state. Furthermore, S3M identified low knowledge of 5 critical hand-washing times among the communities in both states.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

HA-UK seeks to provide public information to the beneficiaries about the program provided through local government, community outreach and facility based awareness and hygiene promotion activities. HA-UK consults with local authorities, community leaders and staff regarding decisions to commence, adapt or complete program. Emergency Response Team assessments include key informant interviews and group discussions within communities and HA-UK uses household surveys to assess program coverage. Every staff member working with HA-UK in Sudan receives an orientation on the Code of Conduct and has to sign it, together with a “Summary of Minimum Standards for the Protection of Women and Children Against Sexual Abuse and Exploitation” which form part of the National and International Staff Guidelines. HA-UK also has Fraud and Misconduct Notification Guidelines in place.
