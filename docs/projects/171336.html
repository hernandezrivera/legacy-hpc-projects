Project Summary

Through implementation, ACTED intends to save lives and improve the ability of Somali households to meet their basic needs through multi-purpose cash transfers for severely disaster-affected populations and to show progress towards the long-term goal of a Somali safety net. Through this project, the Somalia Cash Consortium, of which ACTED is a member, intends to address the needs of the most vulnerable disaster-affected households by providing multi-purpose unrestricted cash transfer. ACTED’s involvement in this project aims at supporting vulnerable households in Baidoa IDP sites of bay region, El-Afweyn and Erigavo districts of Sanaag region, through unconditional cash transfers through six months of mobile money transfers. The Consortium will target the most vulnerable households with life-saving multi-purpose unconditional cash transfers. The Action will specifically target populations in IPC 3, 4 and 5, especially newly displaced and worst-affected Pastoralist and agro-pastoralist communities. In addition, this Action seeks to provide life-saving assistance to food insecure communities in Bay, Gedo and Lower Juba regions while implementing recovery and resilience activities to
restore livelihoods and respond to the needs of IDPs and rural communities in Baidoa, Kismayo, El-Afwayne, Badhan, Diinsoor and Buurhakaba districts who are impacted by floods and drought, lack sustainable livelihoods, at risk of displacement and with limited access to adequate WASH and shelter services.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Universal Cash Transfers are flexible and allow households to tailor the use of the assistance to meet the particular needs of household members, making them an appropriate modality to address diverse needs of vulnerable groups. ACTED and implementing partners will prioritize cash assistance to provide the beneficiaries with the much needed purchasing power, enabling them to respond to their basic needs while offering flexibility and diversity of procurement at household level. Cash preserves the dignity of the households and reserves their right to purchase items according to their needs.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

In the designing of the project, ACTED carried out a market analysis in all targeted districts. Prior to project kick-off, all markets were warranted as functional and the local inflation and market dynamics analyzed in order to provide beneficiaries with adequate amounts according to the prevailing conditions. In the unlikely event of a nation-wide inflation, ACTED would revise the amount accordingly after due consultation with the donor. In addition, the Consortium reviews market monitoring conducted regularly through the Consortium Working Group (CWG) and REACH’s Markets Dashboard and FSNAU markets bulletins. This helps to ascertain if markets in target locations are sufficiently elastic and competitive in order to respond to increased demand with sufficient quantity and quality of goods without resulting in inflation. Food prices and supply, among other market indicators, will be monitored monthly to determine if price and supply changes could impact beneficiaries’ ability to use Universal Cash Transfers to meet their basic needs. Guided by the CWG, the monthly transfer rates could be adjusted accordingly to ensure households can access sufficient commodities to meet their basic needs despite higher prices, thus ensuring objectives are met. The transfer values will vary based on the geographic location to account for differing market prices for basic needs items in the Minimum Expenditure Basket.

Mitigation Measures

Risk 1- ACTED has been implementing activities within the area since 2007 and remains fully aware of the security dynamics and risks involved. For this project, eligible households will be registered by staff using a harmonized Registration Form, collecting basic demographic and contact information through Mobile Data Collection (MDC) on smartphones. This will eliminate the risk of beneficiaries travelling to be registered for humanitarian assistance. With regard to Cash for Work activities (CfW), ACTED and implementing partner, in collaboration with the communities, will select assets within close proximity to the villages in order to reduce the exposure of beneficiaries to security risks. Risk 2- ACTED has an existing policy on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse which is readily available to all staff members. This document is part of the code of conduct handbook signed by all ACTED staff and volunteers when working with ACTED. Strict adherence to this policy is expected within the organization and in case of violation, internal reporting mechanisms exists which employees are encouraged to utilize in the event such incidences occur. With regard to employee to beneficiary interaction, the PSEA also provides clauses on this and repercussions in case ACTED employees infringe on this. Beneficiary hotline numbers exist and shared with all beneficiaries and posted in all sites which ACTED is active. In implementing this project, ACTED will conduct all cash transfers through mobile money in order to erase the long distance beneficiaries used to walk in order to receive payment. Additionally, on CfW activities, the selected areas for rehabilitation are within close proximity to the villages and thus ensuring the vulnerable members of the society are not exposed to dangers of sexual violence and abuse. Lastly, ACTED, during implementation, will conduct gender and protection sensitization sessions, with practical examples on how involvement of both men and women in said community politics is rewarding. To reduce risk of bias, ACTED and partners will work to ensure women and minority representation in Village Relief Committees (VRC). Trainings on vulnerability criteria with all VRCs, and supervise VRC development of beneficiary will be conducted. Risk 3- During project implementation, ACTED and IMPACT Initiatives, ACTED’s sister organization, will conduct intense sensitization regarding channels for feedback and complaints through community meetings/events, flyers, posters, oral announcements during registration and verification and discussion with VRCs. ACTED will leverage the CRM already in place in target areas and sensitize beneficiaries on available channels of communication to provide feedback and file complaints when necessary, ensuring impartiality. The CRM will enable beneficiaries to voice their concerns about the project and will be monitored regularly by ACTED staff. The system allows beneficiaries to privately register their feedback, giving a voice to those most vulnerable, specifically women, the elderly and minorities. Male and female field staff will be utilized in conducting sensitization to ensure that beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries in target communities remain fully aware of and are able to raise any issues of concern for timely action.
