Project Summary

This project is planned to target up to 3,333 (1,1607 are males and 1,726 are females children, elderly and adults) of vulnerable IDPs, Returnees and Host community in the areas of Canal/Pigi, Fangak, of Jonglei, Panyikang Upper Nile andTwic of Warrap to improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations from 1st January to 31st December 2020. Healthcare Foundation Organization (HFO) will use a modality of Primary health services, (PHCC, PHCU, and Mobile response packages to achieve the following objectives:
1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities.
2. Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations.
3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders
HFO response activities will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, protection and FSL services. Healthcare Foundation is taking into account the protection area of responsibility especially SGBV and MHPSS- dealing with provision of services pertaining to clinical management of rape and mental illnesses. Our response will focuses on both IDPs, and host communities.
With the high mortality rate of children under 5 (90.7/1000), maternal mortality rate-MMR (789/100,000) people with disabilities and mental disorders are included as vulnerable groups owing to their high risk during emergencies. The response plan will be prioritized based on epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, cholera hot spot locations, measles outbreaks and the of Ebola Viral disease and malaria seasonality.
Response modalities will be static, fixed and rapid response mechanism in an integrated approach. IDSR, EWARS, attacks on health care and disability tracking tools will be used to determine the feasibility of the response. Mobile response mechanisms would comprised up to 50% of the response.
Our operational plans will always involve the community members, women girls youths and PLWDs (people living with disability) County Health department (CHD) in planning and organization of health services. Meetings conducted with the communities and the CHD are some of the indicators used to monitor the accountability for affected populations. Meetings are conducted to facilitate that all stakeholders like partners, beneficiaries, age groups and local communities are involved in the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the projects in all stages of the program cycle.
Looking into the better levels of stability in the country refugee returnees are coming back resulting into an influx of 193,000 refugee returnees. This has led and will continue to increased humanitarian challenges in 2020 compared to the previous years. People in Need of humanitarian services are 3,296,823 Health Cluster target beneficiaries are 2,049,990 and HFO will mobilize and contribute funds to HRP for 2020 project for population of 3,333 within the country’s areas we will intervene.

1 - Needs

According to an Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) update September 2019, released jointly by the Government of the Republic of South Sudan, the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), the United Nations Children's Fund (UNICEF) and the World Food Program (WFP), it is estimated 6.35 million people (54% of the population) in South Sudan are still severely food insecure. The report estimates that 10,000 people are currently in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and facing an extreme lack of food, while about 1.7 million are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and another 4.6 million people are experiencing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) levels of acute food insecurity compared to the same period in 2018. While the Greater Upper Nile region continues to be the most food insecure, followed by the Greater Bahr el Ghazal region, those in Catastrophe are in Yirol East of the former Lakes state Canal/Pigi of former Jonglei state and will need urgent humanitarian support to save their lives. Although the food security situation is severe, the recent improvement can largely be attributed to the Revitalized Peace Agreement (R-ARCSS), signed in September 2018. The decrease in armed conflict has encouraged the voluntary return of farmers, increasing access to livelihoods and improving markets. A more stable political environment has also allowed for improved delivery of humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable populations. most of areas in Jonglei and Upper Nile are prone to flooding, that can lead to possible occurrence to outbreaks such malaria, Cholera, measles and EVD. skyrocketing despite continued humanitarian efforts (food insecurity has doubled compared to yearly trends). These target areas are either flooded areas, with lack of health facility functionality, malaria upsurge and prone to cholera Ebola and measles outbreaks. In 2019 Disease outbreak has been a bigger challenge to health actors across the counties affected more than 2018, and it is anticipated that more disease outbreaks are to happen in 2020 due fragile health system structure in South Sudan. The Ebola viral disease in Congo is still a threat to South Sudan with case fatality keep on increasing weekly. South Sudan has about 7 hot spots the states bordering Congo and Uganda, that makes South Sudan number one vulnerable to Ebola outbreak any time. Though the huge money was invested into preparedness since 2018 through 2019, there is still need for more funding in preparedness in the area of IPC-WASH in health facilities, case management and communication for development (C4D). Having said this, Equartoria, Juba and Wau are the prone areas, Ebola can happen anywhere in the Country due long incubation period. That makes it possible for partners to prepare and be vigilant for any eventualities. These preparation can be done through training of health workers about EVD measles cholera, case definitions and signs and symptoms to detect and report any suspected case early. All these preparedness have to be incorporated in any stage of primary health services implementation such as PHCU, PHCC and Mobile outreach for community awareness and liaise with partners that have C4D project as tool of communication with community levels. the project will make sure qualified health workers are mobilized to deliver essential healthcare services and make scale up response happen to vulnerable population, both in local community with the involvement of community stakeholders chiefs, women group, The initial plan was shared with the stakeholders and HFO will plan the initial phase of implementation with local authority once this project is funded in 2020. The project reduce the burden of disease outbreak malaria increase and risk for possible outbreak of EVD, the will be done through robust techniques of integration modality.

2 - Activities

This project will focus in the following Activities: 1. OPD services that will provide treatment for common diseases such malaria with ACT, Acute water diarrhea, (AWD) with ORS and zinc sulfate tablets (children U5), acute respiratory infections (ARI) Amoxicillin, active case finding for TB/HIV defaulters, Visceral Leishmaniasis (Kala-Azar) and refer such cases to nearest TB-HIV or Kala-Azar treatment center early.2. Antenatal care and maternity services, providing the mothers with ferrous + folic acid, Albendazole for deforming, screening of pregnant mothers of Syphilis, HIV and other STIs, rapid test for malaria (RDTs) support of normal deliveries in health facilities and community using UNFPA commodities and referral of complicated pregnancies and deliveries to the nearest hospitals for emergency surgical intervention.3. Immunization services to respond to outbreak such as measles vaccination and Vitamin A supplement (targeting 6 months to 15 years and through engaging UNICEF to provide Artech, refrigerator for hard to reach areas and refer the rest of children under 5 years and women of child bearing ages to receive routine immunization in static and outreach EPI sessions.4. Detect, investigate, report and respond to outbreaks example of cholera, measles, EVD, in the areas of target through strengthening EWARS/IDSR system in training and coordination with stakeholders (Health Cluster-WHO, MoH and CHD).5. identification, giving psychological support and referral of Sexual and Gender Based Violence (SGBV) through protection partners in the areas of our operation, HFO facilities in Fangak (Juaibor and Keew PHCC,+Canal/Pigi) under World Bank have qualified Midwives that have advanced training on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR).6. Screening of MUAC in health facilities and outreach sessions for children under 5, pregnant women and lactating mothers-PLWs (PHCC, PHCU and Mobile outreaches)7. Identification of PLWDs (People Living with Disabilities), mentally ill persons among the community and refer them to specialized centers with specialists after giving them basic health care at our health facilities.Healthcare Foundation is willing and able to carry out the task of humanitarian responding to emerging crisis, emergency that are anticipated in 2020. We have the capacity to work in many areas if this project funded it will contribute to the strategic plan of health cluster, WHO and ministry of health in the year 2020.These activities will tailor to achievement of health cluster strategic objectives of;1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities in the country. 2. Improve access and scale-up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations in Upper Nile, Jonglei, Warrap and Northern Bahr El Ghazal. 3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders.Reporting tools will be ERWARS weekly report, IDSR, monthly HMIS and 5Ws to Health Cluster, WHO, CHD and MoH. All these forms will be availed to the field staff in all locations and make sure all indicators are captures and any outbreak investigation will be recorded and reported during implementation.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Healthcare Foundation Organization has coordinated and will continue to coordinate with developmental partners and established humanitarian development strategies such as BHI, community outreach to avoid duplication, we will identify the gaps and move towards smooth transition whenever and wherever feasible. Procedures on transition from humanitarian to development will be agreed between the CHD, community leaders, developmental and humanitarian partners. Activities specialized for developmental and emergency partners will be identified and agreed to avoid gaps during humanitarian and developmental phase. Periodic coordination meetings will be agreed between the partners and other stakeholders. Locations with pre-existing static health facilities will be made functional to deliver health services to the accessible population within a radius of 5-10 km radius. The fixed sites are primary health care units with limited capacity and primary health carecenter which also serves as a referral center. For remotely located and nomadic populations, mobile response comprises 50% of the overall response and serves to be the best choice because of the seasonal movement of population, geographical barriers and poor road network. The safe environment will be maintain by proper waste management at all costs.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

In South Sudan, Healthcare Foundation Organization (HFO) strives to engage beneficiaries in any decision making stage that affects their life and livelihood the AAP is one of our deliverable that all our staff take this seriously to ensure existing community leaders and members participate in implementation process and take ownership of the project rather than watching what HFO is doing afar. During project implementation, a mechanism called participatory review and reflection (PRR) will take place, providing beneficiaries the space to voice their opinions about the project and express their level of satisfaction in the services provided. The PRR will also serve as a mechanism for beneficiaries to participate in joint monitoring activities and express their feeling in owning the project. Beneficiary participation is required to improve the quality of the project implementation, these mechanisms worked very well during first allocation and we will make sure the same way AAP procedures is used at the Humanitarian Response Plans of 2020 so that community feels involved in their services. The feedback mechanisms can be sent to most vulnerable through leading groups such as women, youth (girls and boys). Disabilities individuals will be involved in decision making and implementation modalities to get the information (Feed backs) from project through local language that the community use. Everyone will be informed be the People Living with Disability (PLWDs), girls women and men through local committee that meet regularly with the HFO project staff
