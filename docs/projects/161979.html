Project Summary

This project aims to increase resilience, self-reliance and socio-economic empowerment of Rohingya and host community women and adolescent girls through enhanced skills training, start-up support and marketing opportunities.
It generates better opportunities for women and adolescent girls to learn, develop skills and access services, through context specific learning programmes of life and livelihood skills combined with income generation activities, tailored not only to their needs as learners, but also towards their future as earners. Based on the needs assessment/baseline, adolescent girls and women will learn livelihood skills (a mix of vocational, technical and business skills during 6 months courses ) and will be supported to participate in self-reliance/livelihood activities and cash for work programs that will help them contribute income to their family. These activities will be organized initially in UN Women’s five existing Multi-Purpose Women’s Centers in the camps, which provide basic social and GBV counseling/ referral services for women, and in three additional MPWCs in the host communities. Each center will organize four courses for two groups during the project life. Selection of participants for these activities will be done on improved criteria, so that the most marginalised women/girls, including women with disabilities and female headed households, will be selected for these activities.
Addressing immediate needs of refugees, UN Women has opened and runs five multi-purpose women’s centres (MPWCs) in camps 3, 4, 4 extension, 5, and 18, and will establish three new MPWCs in the host community, for comprehensive services for women and girls as well as for women to receive livelihoods and portable skills training. Based on the needs assessment/baseline, adolescent girls and (young) women will learn livelihood skills (a mix of vocational, technical and business skills during 6 months courses ) and will be supported to participate in income-generating/livelihood activities and cash for work programs that will help them contribute income to their family. Rohingya and host community women and adolescent girls who complete the livelihood training will be provided with in-kind support to start or expand self-reliance/livelihood activities. Women’s Centres will have production units that women can join for collective production of goods (e.g. reusable sanitary pads to supply to humanitarian agencies, or crafts to be sold in commercial outlets) that will be used in the camps. At the same time, central cooking facilities will be established for women to use to run snack making and catering services following the current pilot in Camp 4. Joint socio-economic activities will also be conducted between Rohingya and host community women and adolescent girls in order to promote social cohesion. One of the Women’s Centres will have a “Women’s Market Place” established for both Rohingya and Host community women to buy and sell their goods and products. Market driven self-reliance/livelihood interventions will be explored to promote socio-economic self-reliance and reducing over-reliance of refugees on in-kind humanitarian assistance.

Needs

Gendered isolation and restricted mobility of women and girls according to traditional norms of Rohingya are limiting their access to life-saving assistance, services and information as well as their participation in decision making. The Rohingya community is highly conservative in their gender norms and practices. Practices such as purdah are restricting the mobility of women and their access to lifesaving assistance, services and information. Adolescent girls, young women, and women heads of households are some of the most at-risk in this context. Rohingya women and girls could play a key role in increasing the resilience of families and communities. Family structures have changed, and all persons in the household now face new duties and must engage in new activities and this is giving space for new gender roles to emerge. There are Rohingya women leaders, including survivors of conflict related sexual and gender-based violence, that are self-organizing and forming their own groups in the camps to advocate for their rights and call for justice. Humanitarian responses often miss opportunities to transform socio-cultural gender norms and relations through the leadership and empowerment of women and girls, as well as by promoting positive forms of masculinities — notwithstanding the fact that these are key to a right-based and effective response and to communities’ longer-term resilience and social cohesion. The UN Joint Response Plan of 2019 states that the “lack of income-generating opportunities and transferable skills development has catalyzed the exploitation of adolescent girls and women in the form of forced marriage, survival sex, trafficking for commercial sexual exploitation, drug smuggling and forced labour.” Human trafficking for sex work and labour is reported to be on the increase. Young women and girls are especially vulnerable in highly volatile refugee condition and so requite especial attention and measures to protect their safety and security and facilitate flourishing of their potentials.Women and girls, and other vulnerable and marginalized groups among the Bangladesh host population critically face barriers to accessing already scarce and overstretched social services, inflation, as well as labour competition and increase in protection and safety risks. A narrow asset base, gendered burden of domestic work, limited access to economic resources combined with the lack of alternate livelihoods mean that recovery from shocks for women takes longer than for men. Furthermore, disrupted social relations and gender norms especially put women and girls at increased risk of gender-based violence, and they are bearing the brunt of the negative consequences and coping mechanisms from breakdown of protection mechanisms and lack of livelihoods options. There is a negative perception in the host community that the Rohingya influx is driving an increase in sex work, child marriage and illegal activities such as human and drug trafficking.
