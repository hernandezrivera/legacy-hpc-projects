Project Summary

Oxfam began responding to the Rohingya response program in September 2017 to provide essential WASH, emergency food security, and protection assistance. Oxfam has established field offices in Cox Bazaar in Teknaf, with a field office to open in Uhkia in the beginning of 2019. Protection is central to Oxfam’s response strategy. The protection program is established in Uhkia and Teknaf areas, and protection teams are working in host community areas as well as in refugee camps.
The project aims to respond to protection risks identified in refugee camps and host communities by addressing critical living conditions, strengthening the protection environment, and preparing for durable solutions.
Gender is the heart of Oxfam’s response, and the protection program has a focus on women and girls, recognising their specific protection needs and challenges.
Underlying the entire Oxfam response is the commitment to safe programming; taking proactive measures to ensure the response does not cause inadvertent harm, is conflict sensitive, and reduces the risk of GBV.
The project continues the 2018 program with an expansion in the current areas of working to cover more beneficiaries. The protection program aims to reach 27,000 households, 4000 of which are from the host community.
The project will include supporting community protection structures, capacity building with key stakeholders, and facilitating access to services. There will also be a focus on protection monitoring to provide the protection sector with analysis of protection gaps and trends, as well as to guide protection programming. The project will work in cooperation with the sector through the Protection Working Group.
Oxfam’s protection baseline survey demonstrates that community protection needs include NFI items. To provide a comprehensive protection package, as well as soft activities the project includes an NFI component including solar street lights to address safety issues, particularly for women and girls, in camps and in host community.

Needs

Across all settings, participants indicated feeling unsafe or insecure in their respective communities either in general, at hot-spot locations, when conducting certain activities or during certain times of the day and night. Within the camps there are reports of increasing violence and crime. There are growing concerns about the increase of radicalisation particularly targeting young men. Lack of consistent, accountable security actors throughout the communities means a decrease in overall deterrence of violence and a perceived increase in opportunistic violence, criminality, abuse, abductions and trafficking. Oxfam protection response did not begin until January 2018. In order to avoid duplication and provide consistent coverage, Oxfam focussed on camps where there was little protection activity. The protection program has chosen to expand its coverage for a number of reasons; in camp 22 currently the program covers three blocks, in covering an extra 1200 households the whole camp will receive programming. This has been specifically requested to Oxfam by CiC and site management services. The changing of boundaries in camps and blocks in camp 4 and camp 12 means that the program coverage is no longer blanket. The program aims to expand the program to ensure that areas where the program is already running are covered completely. The Rohingya Response Gender Analysis identifies the need to be targetting community leaders and mahjis to support protection activities for women, as well as to be creating more inclusive power structures to include women. The gender assessment, protection monitoring, and the Teknaf assessment all identify the need for women and girls to be active in the community for decision making and community leadership. The gender analysis reports that 38 per cent of women have some level of restriction to freedom of movement in camp. It also indicates that one barrier to accessing services is a lack of female staff and unsegregated services. Within the refugee and host populations there are multiple changing dimensions linked to cultural and religious norms and power dynamics, which causes tensions and vulnerabilities. Boys and girls are particularly vulnerable to protection risks. There is a lack of knowledge of human rights and protection related services amoung Rohingya women. GBV is common amoung both communities and affects women and girls disproportionally. There is stigma and a lack of information that prevents women from accessing GBV services. Female’s freedom of movement is also restricted by the observation of purdah. Adolescent girls’ freedom of movement is highly restricted which limits their ability to access GBV or other humanitarian services. There are increasing tensions between host and refugee communities. The lack of formal access to livelihoods has been identified as a cause of protection concerns and negative coping mechanisms NFI needs have been identified through community consultation and protection assessments and the expansion of multi purpose cloth kits and solar lighting has been recommended by the gender analysis and protection monitoring reports. The lack of Abaya which affects freedom of movement and lack of lighting leads to restrictions of movement as well as the increased risk of GBV. DDR is another need that has been identified in the gender analysis, the Teknaf assesssment, protection monitoring report and Oxfam community consultation project conducted in May 2018. The lack of clear communication channels beyond informing mahjis has been identified as a major gap, and all reports indicate that women do not have access to DRR information. There is a need for DRR projects that are responsive to community needs with a particular focus on women, elderly, and people with disabilities.

Activities or outputs

Provide NFI (multi purpose cloth, solar lights, monsoon preparedness kits) to support protection needs focussing on safety, dignity, and access to services for women. NFI provided to refugees and vulnerable Bangladesh people in host communities, using a voucher system where possible. Blanket distribution for camp and refugee living in host, targeted according to vulnerabilities in host community. Provide solar street lights using a community based approach. Installation to include community needs and reduce risk of harm, particularly concerning the placement of lights. FGDs with men, women, children, and elderly and consultation with local authorities. Support existing community based protection structures, including youth groups, and create new structures. Focus on involvement of women. Structures comprised of 50 per cent women and 50 per cent men. Collaborate with gender programs to incorporate issues identified in women's groups and build relationships between group members. In host communities structures are 50 per cent refugee and 50 per cent host to facilitate social cohesion, joint decision making, and joint community actions. Community based protection activities in host and refugee communities with a focus on women and girls and vulnerable groups. House to house engagement, conducted by Community Based Volunteers (CBVs) using protection messaging and raising awareness to ensure access to services particularly by women and adolescent girls. CBVs are 50 per cent men and 50 per cent women. Training with CBVs and field staff on confidentiality, consent, and do no harm. Community based research on social cohesion in host community. Capacity building with key stakeholders including local leaders on protection mainstreaming. In host communities training with host and refugee local leaders held together. Advocacy and negotiation with authorities and humanitarian actors to promote protection mainstreaming and equal access to services. Create and support referral pathways in collaboration with other organisations. Provide humanitarian actors access to training, tools, and information to ensure interventions adhere to safe-programming. Support protection mainstreaming through all programs. Safe programming training for Oxfam staff and partner organisations. Protection monitoring using FGDs and KIIs with men, women, adolescent girls, adolescent girls, elderly, and people with disabilities, and house to house surveys. Focus on protection trends and protection mainstreaming concerns and gaps. Analysis and evaluations leading to protection monitoring reports. Findings shared with other protection actors through the protection working group. Organisation wide PSEA policies and complaint mechanisms. PSEA staff focal points. Training to all staff regarding PSEA policy and reporting mechanisms. PSEA training to vendors and contractors. Awareness raising with CBVs and protection committees of PSEA policies and reporting mechanisms. Active follow up with community groups to monitor PSEA issues. DRR Work with community based structures to develop risk and resilience mapping as well as EWER responses. CBVs focal points of disseminating information to the community. Collaboration with other programs to deliver EWER messages. DRR messaging in existing community activities. Complaint and feedback measures include complaints desk staffed by MEAL at every distribution, post distribution monitoring conducted by MEAL, Oxfam feedback hub to be created in each camp, feedback through CBVs. Specific community based social cohesion activities as recommended by the social cohesion research. Integration with other sectors in of the project activities. Coordination and cooperation with programs within Oxfam through joint community initiatives. Feedback and integration of different program community groups to ensure coordination of services and identify needs and gaps. Community based protection activities integrated with other programs.
