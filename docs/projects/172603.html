Project Summary

Chipinge District, located in the eastern parts of Zimbabwe is host to the Tongogara refugee camp. The camp was established in the 1980s to cater for refugees fleeing civil conflict in Mozambique. It now accommodates 14 542 people mainly from the Great Lakes region, DRC and Mozambique. Initially planned to host 3000 maximum residents, the ballooned population has since exerted enormous pressure on the social services amenities including but not limited to water, sanitation, education, shelter giving rise to the risk of epidemics, SGBV, and greater susceptibility to recurrent localized emergencies [floods, fires etc] as well as the novel COVID-19. Macro socio-economic conditions [flagging economy, recurrent droughts, poor health delivery and multiple disasters] in the country iteratively worsens the conditions in the camp. The project seeks to contribute to safe and dignified living through integrated health and DRR programming that protects the fragile population in the Tongogara Refugee camp. ZRCS, using the tried and tested Community Based Health and First Aid approach (CBHFA), building upon the already existing presence of programming in Restoration of Family Links in the camp, purposes to provide; [1] follow on basic needs cash support for 254 unaccompanied minors in foster households and enabling health/wash services (drilling 6 boreholes solarized piped water supplies and related hygiene promotion, SGBV prevention/support services, Sexual and Reproductive Health Rights promotion. [2] DRR interventions focusing on inculcating resilience and safety culture, school safety (including EiE), due to exposure to floods risk of adjacent Save River- early warning, First Aid/Disaster preparedness and response training and equipping/prepositioning)

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

The ZRCS aim is to effectively and systematically engage communities as they are experts on the challenges that affect them and their solutions in anticipation to improve programme quality and building safer and more resilient communities. ZRCS consulted with different stakeholders, Camp Administrator and community at large with inclusion on persons of all gender identities, ages, disabilities and backgrounds about their specific needs, concerns and priorities which were then used to inform the design of the project, services and activities. The communities will continuously be involved and engaged for participation during implementation so as to ensure accessibility and active outreach to collect feedback that can assist in adjusting services and activities. The ZRCS have different feedback mechanisms that are in place, some of the reactive mechanisms include the use of suggestion boxes, help desks, community feedback meetings, face to face with volunteers and staff that live in the communities. The National Society is also in the process of establishing a toll free line third party managed feedback mechanism which will be up and running by end November 2020. This will provide a safe and anonymous space for sharing feedback by the targeted population so as to strengthen engagement and participation. Other proactive feedback mechanisms such as monitoring and evaluation tools will be used by the ZRCS to gather perceptions and preferences of the affected population.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project is going to positively impact environment through activities which directly promote good environmental stewardship. Leveraging on the already existing partnership in Chipinge with WWF, Environmental Management Authority and the Zimbabwe Forestry Commission will promote environmental awareness, encourage planting of trees, reforestation and discourage deforestation. Among the community action plans ZRCS will encourage planting trees along the Save River banks as natural mitigation measures against flooding. Some of the activities they will engage in include rehabilitation of the degraded land surfaces through growing of vetiver grass. Promotion of energy saving Rocket Lorena cookstoves within the camp and the host community will reduce deforestation rate and due to its energy efficiency it will reduce the carbon footprint. Use of renewable solar energy in water pumping has a direct contribution to macro-environmental protection. Keyhole gardens at household level are a smart adaptation innovation that recycles domestic wastewater - another fragile natural resource. These household level innovations are ideal in arid areas through the use of domestic waste water to provide a steady supply of a diversity of vegetable types for domestic consumption. Most of the inputs for this garden are locally available.
