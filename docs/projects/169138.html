Project Summary

The multi-level response aims at alleviating the immediate negative effects of the Beirut blast and the impact of the socio-economic and health crisis on the accessibility to and quality of learning opportunities for all children. The response will encompass repairs and rehabilitation works for 4 public schools in the most affected areas, involving vulnerable individuals in Cash for Work schemes and prioritizing second shift schools to reach refugees students; cash assistance to most vulnerable and affected families to cover education-related expenses such as transportation, non-IT school material and data bundle; distribution of IT equipment (preferably laptops) to children and teachers in the selected areas; technical capacity building to teachers on distance learning teaching methods; capacity building on Psycho-Social Support to teachers and educational personnel through ToTs, based on the “Tutor of Resilience” model.

1 - Needs

The devastating blast had a serious impact on 21 neighborhoods of Beirut and Mount Lebanon governorate, leaving 180 dead, more than 6,000 injured and destroying and damaging businesses, houses, hospitals, water and sanitation facilities and public and private schools.The series of explosions that hit Beirut Port on the 4th of August has significantly exacerbated the pre-existing humanitarian needs for Lebanese and non-Lebanese population. The blast came amid a socio-economic crisis that the country has been going through since October 2019 and an increasing number of Covid-19 transmissions which are critically affecting the health system ability to cope with the virus.In this context, it is of vital importance to restore the education system so that continuity of quality education services in ensured for all children. To this purpose, there is an immediate need to repair and rehabilitate school infrastructures damaged by the blast, after assessing the severity of the damage and the costs of the repairs. In fact, a total of 159 schools in the blast radius have reported damages, of which 92 are public schools. In the event that a blended or remote approach is adopted by MEHE for the resumption of the school year, on the basis of the trend of the Covid-19 epidemics, support will be needed for teachers and students to prepare for distance learning modality: devices such as laptops and access to internet are a priority to allow attendance to and delivery of remote classes, while technical capacity building for teachers is a complementary need to enhance digital skills and support them in the evaluation of school performance and in the dialogue with families for monitoring children's participation and progress (Cesvi, Education during Covid-19: Changing Needs and Emerging Challenges, May 2019).Furthermore, the blast has exacerbated the negative consequences of the socio-economic crisis and the Covid-19 outbreak on the access to livelihoods and income for the affected and vulnerable families in Beirut and Mount Lebanon: therefore, in order to ensure equal access to and participation of children in learning opportunities, cash support is needed for those families who are not able to pay for transportation, school material and access to internet. Lastly, the multi-level crisis that has hit both the refugee and host communities in the Beirut and Mount Lebanon area (socio-economic crisis, Covid-19 outbreak and the port blast) are jeopardizing the psycho-social wellbeing of children, caregivers, teachers and other educational personnel. Although PSS training and pathways have already been developed and piloted prior to the blast to support teachers in delivering quality education to children affected by trauma, further capacity building to teachers who haven’t received training and other educational personnel is needed to bridge the existing gaps.

2 - Activities

1. Removal and disposal of debris and rehabilitation works. The activity, based on the results of the assessment on school infrastructure damage conducted by MEHE and in coordination with UNESCO and the Education WG, will target 4 public schools in Achrafieh, Jdeideh el-Matn and Bourj Hammoud. The suggested ones are CERD4 (moderate damages), CERD92, CERD62 and CERD66 (light damages). The pre-selected schools may vary according to MEHE indications. The removal of debris will be performed through CfW. The activity will include the distribution of furniture and essential non-IT equipment and build on Cesvi experience in the rehabilitation of public schools in BML. The budget for rehabilitation and repair has been calculated according to MEHE rapid needs assessment findings.2. Comprehensive assessment of needs for continuity of learning activities. In order to avoid overlaps with MEHE schools damages assessment, the study will focus on additional needs of children and educational personnel in terms of: equipment, access to data bundle and technical capacity for distance learning, leveraging on results pre-identified by Cesvi (“Education during Covid-19”) and LeaRa; access to income/cash in order to pay for education-related expenses; PSS capacity building. The areas considered for this needs assessments are Achrafieh, Jdeideh el-Matn, Bourj Hammoud (where the 4 public schools under activity 1 are located, including 2 second shift ones) and Karantine. The activity will be launched at the end of August 2020 and will be covered by Cesvi private funds. Cesvi will ensure complementarity with other assessments, coordinating with the Education WG. The assessment outline has been already shared with UNESCO for facilitating coordination with MEHE Beirut Blast Committee and the Education sector. 3. Provision of cash for education to most vulnerable HHs in Achrafieh, Jdeideh el-Matn, Bourj Hammoud (same target schools) and Karantine to cover the costs of education-related expenses (transportation, purchase of non-IT school material, data bundle). The activity will target Lebanese and non Lebanese affected by the blast, with priority to: families who lost house/job, children at risk of being out of school, female-headed HHs, HHs with a disabled member, high dependency ratio, HHs not targeted by other MPCA programmes (coordination with Cash Task Force ongoing). ECA amount (400.000 LBP) has been set based on the Protection cluster indications and in line with the NPTP threshold to avoid inequality of treatment and social tension and will be delivered in cash, one off.4. Purchase and distribution of IT equipment for distance learning. The activity will consists of the purchase and distribution of new devices for children and teachers of the 4 targeted schools for remote learning support. 5. Technical capacity building on distance learning techniques. The activity will be realized through intensive training sessions to 40 teachers in the 4 target schools on how to adapt teaching methods to the distance learning modality, how to maintain effective dialogue with families and how to evaluate their performance. 6. Capacity building on PSS to teachers of the 4 targeted schools. The activity, implemented in collaboration with HOOPS Club and the Resilience Unit of the University of Milan (RiRes), is based on the “Tutor of Resilience” model, successfully implemented by RiRes in emergency contexts, included Lebanon. The activity consists of two phases: 1) ToT to 3 psychologists from HOOPS staff on the model. The intensive training lasts 8 days and will be delivered to staff who has pre-existing knowledge and skills (namely those who already received training on psychosocial care under HOOPS “Sport and Resilience” component of Cesvi Education project, completed in August 2020) 2) HOOPS staff replicates the training to 90 teachers as follows: 3 pshycologists*6 sessions (8 days each)*5 teachers each.
