Project Summary

The aim of this intervention is: conflict-affected communities in MMC and Jere LGAs are sustainably protected from, and treated for, the main causes of morbidity and mortality; supported to meet immediate and long-term Shelter and Settlement, WASH and Protection needs, while being protected and provided with the legal assistance they need. To achieve this, the proposed project will be implemented by a consortium led by the International Rescue Committee (IRC), in partnership with the Danish Refugee Council (DRC) and Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC). The consortium will leverage the expertise of each partner to deliver a multi-sectoral, area-based response, providing integrated services across the following sectors: Health, WASH, Nutrition, Food Assistance, Multi-purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA), Nutrition, Economic Recovery and Market Systems (ERMS), Protection and Shelter and Settlements. The consortium aims to meet the most immediate and life-saving needs of IDPs, host communities and returnees in MMC and Jere, which will translate into reduced mortality and improved living conditions. The consortium will also address the ongoing, protracted needs, and supporting early recovery through capacity-building, the strengthening of existing health systems and community-based structures, and increasing the ownership of life-saving, life-sustaining, and life-building services by local authorities, service providers, and local communities to ensure sustainability. In order to build the resilience of individuals and communities, the unique humanitarian and development challenges in MMC and Jere LGAs (presence of service delivery actors against an ongoing flux of IDPs in and out of camps and host communities throughout the city) must be addressed simultaneously by increasing access to quality, sustainable basic services, building sustainable livelihoods, and strengthening local systems and structures.

1 - Needs

Based on the Nigeria Humanitarian Needs Overview 2021, and considering the context described above, the IRC aims to implement an area-based, multisector emergency response to respond to the immediate and protracted needs of conflict-affected populations in MMC and Jere LGAs in order to improve their well-being and strengthen their resilience.In Nigeria, the IRC has been operating and responding to the humanitarian needs of the most vulnerable crisis-affected and displaced populations in the North East (NE) since 2014. Over five million people in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states require protection from violence, abuse, exploitation, and violations of their fundamental human rights of which over half are women and girls and children and 1.9 million IDPs. According to HRP 2021, an estimated 1.7M (1.6 million children and 100,000 caregivers) need child protection services; this includes an estimated 21,000 children and caregivers living with disabilities, at least 1.4M children in need have extremely little or no access to essential child protection services in addition to concerns about high rates of malnutrition. Furthermore, over 1.5M people—IDPs, members of the host communities, and returnees—need GBV prevention and response services across north-east Nigeria (HNO 2021). The elderly and chronically sick, persons with physical and mental disabilities, women and child-headed households, unaccompanied or separated children, adolescent boys and girls, children associated with armed conflict, highly vulnerable children, pregnant and/or lactating women and people returning from captivity have been the most affected (HRP 2021). With the firm desire by the Borno state government to close all IDP camps in Maiduguri, the state have started facilitating the relocations of IDPs to their local government areas (LGAs) of origin. Most returnees have either returned to unfavorable conditions forcing them to return back into their communities of origin or within MMC/Jere without proper profiling, overstretching the existing humanitarian resources. This has resulted in tension and conflicts between IDPs and host community members. Also there is limited capacity and awareness for service providers, including duty bearers and community members in order to reduce protection risks and identify and support the most vulnerable. Both IDPs and host community members are at heightened risks of violence, exploitation, serious neglect or discrimination; and at risk of negative coping mechanisms. Parents are forced to adopt negative coping mechanisms which have a detrimental impact on children who are exploited The push by parents to adopt negative coping behaviours continue as children exposed to exploitative child labour and are encouraged into child and forced marriages in violation of the rights of the children.

2 - Activities

Conflict, violence, and OAG activities has significantly affected the civilian population in Borno state. Vulnerable groups such as female and children including child-headed households, Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), children at risk of recruitment, children exploited in worst forma of child labour adolescent girls and boys, pregnant and lactating women, the elderly and chronically sick, persons with disabilities, are among the most vulnerable groups at risks of GBV, child protection and other protection concerns in Borno state. Children also face serious protection concerns including physical abuse, child neglect and child labor all of which have long term impact on childrens development and wellbeing well into their adult years. Protection risks are heightened for children, including young adolescent girls who are most at risk of early and forced marriage and because of the pandemic, they are at a high risk of losing out on education opportunities amongst denial of realization of other rights. The proposed intervention will enable children and their caregivers access timely services in a dignify ways through a multisector approach that will produce positive outcomes for children. The IRC’s child protection prevention and response programming will consider five thematic areas: 1) Structured group psychosocial support and life skills for children and adolescents through Social Emotional Learning (SEL) and Supporting Adolescents and their Families in Emergencies (SAFE), 2) community-based child protection and prevention activities, 3) Case management response for children that experience abuse, exploitation, family separation, neglect or violence, 4) Caregiver's support through the Families Makes the Difference parenting program as well as Strengthening Protection and Reintegration for children (SPARC) and 5) integrated child protection services in health and nutrition facilities. The targeted beneficiaries will include unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), highly vulnerable children and adolescents that will be identified within the communities by IRC staff and volunteers, children with acute malnutrition and health issues that experience combined protection concerns, adolescent boys and younger adolescent girls, community members (opinion leaders, community based child protection committee members, religious leaders, etc.) and caregivers (including foster caregivers).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The IRC will use listening sessions to ensure barriers preventing women and girls from accessing services and how to minimize them are discussed with women, men, boys, and girls. The IRC will identify safe and unsafe places in communities and engage communities at the local level to identify and execute risk reduction activities. In all personal information sharing, consultation and counselling, the IRC will ensure that confidentiality and privacy are respected in all forms at all time. All IRC counselling rooms have soundproof material and are set aside from WGSS meeting spaces with an alternative exit point. Both survivors and non-GBV survivors will be encouraged to visit WGSSs and CWCs to reduce stigmatization. Child protection services will be provided to children, adolescents and their caregivers in ways that reduce the risks they face and meet their needs in a dignified manner, prioritizing their safety and well-being. The Child Protection team will work closely with community structures to identify and address barriers children with protection risk, including children with disabilities, face to ensure services are provided based on the needs identified. All community incentive workers will be trained on the child safeguarding policy and the IRC’s Code of Conduct as part of efforts prevent and mitigate abuse, exploitation and gross misconduct. IRC will closely monitor their activities through engagement of community members, encouraging them to utilize accountability mechanisms put in place my IRC such as toll-free hotlines and suggestion boxes. Through these feedback mechanisms, as well as focus group discussions and safety mapping, the views and safety concerns of children, adolescents and their families will be taken into account, and they will be engaged in decision making that affects them.
