Project Summary

The conflict in Syria has entered its ninth year, taking a devastating toll on the lives of the Syrian people. Civilians living in northwest Syria have been enduring a drastically worsening humanitarian situation as hostilities continue to intensify since November 2019. From 1 December 2019 to 29 January 2020, 517,000 people – around 80% of whom are women and children – were displaced in northwest Syria (CCCM Cluster Snapshot IDPs in NWS, 29/01/2019). Many have been displaced multiple times, with each new displacement compounding experienced risks and vulnerabilities. With further mass displacement taking place since 27 January, this number is likely to continue increasing significantly.
The newly displaced populations are predominantly moving north within Idleb governorate to urban centers and IDP camps along the Turkish-Syrian border. Thousands of newly displaced people are also moving to Afrin, A’zaz, and Al-Bab areas in northern Aleppo governorate (OCHA, NWS Situation Report #2, January 2019). As the fluidity of the security situation persists across Idleb area, civilians are faced with difficult decisions about staying where they are, moving to areas that they believe are safer or back to their home communities. This dynamic puts a strain on humanitarian programming.
The main needs of the most recently displaced individuals, as reported by the Camp Management and Camp Coordination Cluster (CCCM), are non-food items, shelter, food and cash assistance. Currently, almost half of those newly displaced are living with host families and rented houses while 32 percent are living in camps or individual tents. In particular, the winter weather is adding another layer of urgency to the situation with flooding frequently affecting camps and informal settlements. The last Rapid Needs Assessment conducted by REACH in camps and sites in NWS also reports food items being the third most common priority need (particularly, bread, rice and cooking oil). Despite a significantly improved cereal harvest in 2018/2019 as compared to the previous year, wheat production was still approximately 40 percent below the pre-crisis average (FAO/WFP, 2019). The agricultural sector, which normally serves as a major engine of the economy and a source of cheaper, more abundant food, continues to be threatened by factors such as land contamination, lack of availability of and/or financial means to access essential inputs, and damaged infrastructure (FAO/WFP, 2019).

4 - Needs

Conflict in Syria has transformed the relationships between Syrians. Displacement has uprooted entire communities, undermining displaced communities' social fabric and placing significant strain on host communities (HRP 2019). Across the country, many communities have banded together in response to pressures and insecurity, contributing to increased in-group/out-group dynamics. The areas targeted by our project (Tal Alkrama, Azaz, Afrin, Ras Al Aen, and Maaret Masrin) are witnessing the most complex humanitarian emergency: on top of the poor security environment, the areas are heavily overcrowded with IDPs from different governorates, including Iraqi IDPs. In order to support local communities and local institutions to improve social inclusion and local development, WATAN will target both IDPs and host communities equally, ensuring the equal participation of males and females. In order to maximize its impact and avoid duplication, the intervention addresses the gaps identified by the FSL cluster, which keeps track of partners' activities and food-related needs on a monthly basis.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

WATAN will apply Minimum standard commitments to gender and diversity in emergency programming including mainly, and not limited to, Commitment to Dignity, Commitment to Access, Commitment to Participation, Commitment to Safety and Gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and response and Child protection. As specific actions are taken to reduce the risk of GBV, food distribution will be conducted by a distribution team consisting of male and female members. Distributions will be carried out during daylight hours and in locations reported to be safe by women and girls. GBV consultation services will be in place to ensure safe, confidential and appropriate systems of care for survivors who report experienced GBV violence. Food assistance is given on the basis of vulnerability only.
