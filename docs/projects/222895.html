Project Summary

Over the last seven years, the Rohingya crisis has shifted from an emergency to a protracted crisis. However, a significant gap remains in providing equitable health services. People with disabilities are still deprived of essential rehabilitation services, along with primary healthcare. According to the WHO, rehabilitation is an integral part of the primary healthcare system. Through this project, CBM Global and its implementing partner, CDD, will provide rehabilitation services through both home-based and center-based approaches. Additionally, CBM Global and CDD will offer rehabilitation services at various mainstream health actors’ posts, ensuring that people with disabilities receive integrated health services according to their needs. For those unable to visit centers, home-based rehabilitation services will be provided at their doorsteps, while their other mainstream health needs will be addressed through a referral system.
Considering the needs and gaps in the provision of Rehabilitation services, CBMG and CDD have designed this project and set the activities. In accordance with the CBMG – CDD Inclusive Rohingya Response Program Strategy 2022-26, CDD will provide community-focused Multi-Disciplinary Rehabilitation Services to the people with disabilities and people without disabilities who are at risk of developing disability.
The overall objective of this project is:
“People with disabilities have improved their health and well-being through quality rehabilitation services and accessibility to humanitarian services in the Rohingya and the host community”.
Rehabilitation: Provide Home-based and center-based rehabilitation services in the following project areas; Camp 11, 13, 18, 19, and Rotnapalon, Holdiapalong & Palongkhali union in the host communities. Activities under the rehabilitation component will include:
• Provision of therapeutic services (PT & OT) at the doorsteps of people with disabilities.
• Provision of Rehabilitation services from the Static center at camp-8W & 18 from where Children with severe disabilities and people with disabilities will receive multi-disciplinary rehab services.
• Conduct Hearing and Visual screening through center-based and home-based approaches. Assistive devices will be provided to people as part of their rehabilitation process.
• Referrals will be an integral part of this project for services that the project will not be able to provide.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

According to the 2023 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA), 5% of Rohingya and 2% of host community individuals with disabilities experienced challenges in accessing health services due to their impairments. This is primarily because the specific treatment or service needed (Rehab) is not available, and the health facility is too far away The Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment (ADINA) assessment in Rohingya camps by REACH found 12% with disabilities in the Rohingya camps. The rapid assistive technology assessment (rATA) study showed that 1% met assistive device needs, 51% had unmet needs, and 48% lacked such products. The situation of people with disabilities in the host communities’ mirrors that of the Rohingya camps. However, the presence of organizations in the host community is notably scarce compared to the Rohingya camps. Rehabilitation services are severely lacking in the host communities, with only CBMG & and CDD actively addressing community-based Rehabilitation & Disability inclusion in the host communities. It is reported that women have less access to health and other services compared to men. Considering the above issues, CBMG and CDD will design the service package and approach. Through the home-based approach, the project will aim to reach the maximum number of women with disabilities.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection will be a cross-cutting theme throughout the project cycle. The project will adopt the four protection principles of prioritizing safety and dignity and avoiding causing harm, ensuring meaningful access, accountability, and participation and empowerment. The project will be designed and adapted to ensure that it is inclusive of and accessible to everyone, including persons with disabilities, older people, and gender-diverse populations. The project will also identify a Protection Focal Point to report protection issues in the project and ensure staff participate in protection training and adhere to protection guidelines.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project mainly focused on the Inclusion of the persons with disabilities & older people in the health care system. At least 70% of the project participants will be persons with disabilities. CBMG & CDD will conduct a screening process to identify the project participant by using the Washington Group Short Set of Questions Enhanced (WGSS-E). A comprehensive GEDSI action plan will be developed in the inception phase. Across all outcomes, WGSS-E will be used to identify persons with disabilities. During the project design phase, we consulted with members of the Self-Help Group and Disability Support Committee to gather their opinions and insights about the current needs. Furthermore, the proposed interventions were selected based on recommendations and opinions from project participants who have disabilities. We collected information through an inclusive feedback and response mechanism. Persons with disabilities, especially SHG/DSC members, will be actively engaged throughout the project cycle. The project will prioritize the disability movement through various interventions, involving SHG/DSC members on different platforms to advocate for the rights of persons with disabilities. The project will also improve the capacity of persons with disabilities in the camp and host community to improve the active participation of persons with disabilities in the humanitarian response through training in advocacy, monitoring, and evaluation and conducting accessibility audits. The project will use the twin-track approach to disability inclusion, mainstreaming, and disability-specific activities.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

CBMG-CDD will ensure accountability to affected populations by using already functional inclusive complaints, feedback, and response mechanisms that proactively capture systematic feedback from beneficiaries with disabilities and set strategies to remove barriers accordingly. The iFRM will follow the following five steps. Step 1: Embed inclusive feedback & and reporting (iFRM) mechanism in all new projects.Step 2: Analyze the community profile and local context.Step 3: Consult with communities.Step 4: Decide on the most appropriate feedback and reporting channels.Step 5: Develop a plan of action for handling feedback and training staff. CBMG-CDD will ensure that women, men, girls, and boys receiving humanitarian assistance are the primary stakeholders of the project and that they have a basic right to participate in the decisions that affect their lives, receive the information they need to make informed decisions, and to complain if they feel the help they receive is not adequate or has unwelcomed consequences"

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the project incorporates Climate Action by addressing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Disaster Risk Management (DRM), and anticipatory actions. It strengthens Disability Support Committees (DSCs) and Self-Help Groups (SHGs) to promote resilience among persons with disabilities. Key actions include: Inclusive DRR and DRM training for DSC and SHG members, with accessible emergency preparedness plans.Anticipatory actions such as early warning systems accessible to persons with disabilities.Climate adaptation through sustainable practices, reducing vulnerabilities, and building community resilience.These actions ensure that climate response efforts are inclusive and protective of persons with disabilities.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project includes Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures. It incorporates:• Emergency Response Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) tailored to ensure inclusive response actions, with specific protocols for assisting people with disabilities.• Contingency Funds are allocated for rapid mobilization of resources to meet urgent needs.These measures enhance the project’s ability to respond quickly and effectively to emergencies, ensuring the protection and inclusion of vulnerable community members.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project will actively engage youth by establishing school brigades made up of school-going students. CBMG and CDD will work together to develop these student groups, enhancing their knowledge and skills on disability inclusion and general health care issues. Through targeted training and capacity-building sessions, the students will become advocates for inclusive practices. Once trained, these youth brigades will raise awareness among their peers and community members, promoting understanding and support for persons with disabilities. Their activities will include organizing awareness campaigns on health awareness, peer discussions, and community outreach efforts.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project promotes self-reliance and resilience for refugees by empowering Disability Support Committees (DSCs) and creating skill-building opportunities for people with disabilities through the referral linkage. Through training inclusive practices, life skills, and protection issues, DSC members are equipped to support their communities.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project activities in the host community align with development priorities in Cox’s Bazar district. By strengthening Self-Help Groups (SHGs) for persons with disabilities, the project supports the district’s focus on inclusive development and social cohesion as well as health related initiates. It contributes to priorities outlined in the District Development and Growth Priorities of Cox's Bazar (Dec 2022) by promoting disability inclusion in the health services, and resilience-building initiatives. The project will work with District administration to ensure Rehabilitation services to people in need. Additionally, the project cooperates with local stakeholders to ensure that its interventions complement district-level planning instruments, promotion sustainable development and improving the quality of life for vulnerable populations in the host community.
