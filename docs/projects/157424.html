Project Summary

SSR, IDPs and vulnerable host community households (HHs) in North Darfur are in urgent need of cross-sectoral emergency relief to alleviate suffering and prevent potential conflicts between the three groups. Owing to the remoteness of the area and inadequate funding, the humanitarian needs are not entirely met, despite several international and national humanitarian agencies already responding to the crisis. Severe gaps exist in the areas of WASH, food security and shelter/NFIs.
This project therefore aims at contributing to meeting the immediate humanitarian needs of the SSR and vulnerable host community members by addressing WASH, food and nutrition security, shelter and NFI challenges faced in Al Lait and El Fasher localities.

Needs

The South Sudanese Refugee (SSR) crisis remains the fastest growing and largest refugee situation on the African continent since the onset of conflict in South Sudan in 2013. The Sudan 2018 Humanitarian Overview estimates that over 455,000 SSR have arrived in Sudan as of October 2017 with an additional 200,000 SSR expected by the end of 2018. Based on most recent assessment conducted by UNHCR and the Commissioner for Refugees (COR) in June 2018, a total of 13,070 SSR is hosted in Al Lait and El Fasher Localities in North Darfur. Given the currently prevailing economic situation and inflation, the continued fuel shortage and accumulated food gaps due to poor harvests in North Darfur, many vulnerable host community members are equally in dire need of humanitarian assistance. The high number of refugees presents a strain local resources and on the already weakened capacities of host communities. Therefore, SSRs and vulnerable host community households (HHs) in North Darfur are in urgent need of cross-sectoral emergency relief to alleviate suffering and prevent potential conflicts between the three groups. Owing to the remoteness of the area and inadequate funding, the humanitarian needs are not entirely met, despite several international and national humanitarian agencies already responding to the crisis. Severe gaps exist in the areas of WASH, food security and shelter/NFIs.

Activities

Result 1: Improved access to water, latrines and hygiene productsIn coordination with UNHCR, the State Ministry of Water and Environmental Sanitation (MWES), the WASH sector, HAC and the target group, WHH will drill 3 new boreholes in Al Lait locality (Andrab, Al Lait Town and Dalil Bakiker) based on existing gaps and recommendations by UNHCR. WHH will constitute one Water Management Committee (WMC) for each borehole that will be capacitated to manage and maintain the boreholes upon WHH exit. The WMC will be trained on Operation and Maintenance (O&M) as well as Management and Organization (M&O). The design of the HH latrines and the scope of involvement of the target HH will be discussed and agreed upon with the mentioned stakeholders prior to the start of the activity. Finally, WHH will conduct hygiene promotion trainings and distribute basic hygiene products to the target group.Result 2: Improved food and nutrition securityThis result will be realized through the distribution of crop seeds, hand tools and lactating small ruminant animals (goats and sheep) to 4,800 SSRs and IDPs (ca. 2,400 HH). The SSR are familiar with the ‘contract farming’ approach through which they get permission to cultivate land belonging to host community members and in return they share the harvest. Distribution of crop seeds and livestock will be conducted through a voucher system. Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) will be trained in key nutrition topics such as breast feeding, Infant and Young Children Practices (IYCF) and cooking demonstration. Impact on environment/natural resources

WHH will distribute energy saving ovens, which will contribute to environmental conservation by reducing the number of trees cut down by the SSR population. In addition, the ovens will minimize the risk of women and girls of being subjected to abuse while fetching firewood outside of settlements. The same HHs will be targeted for the distribution of shelter materials and NFIs.
