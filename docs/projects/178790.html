Project Summary

The proposed project is aiming to contribute in achieving the SO3 and SO1 by achieve two goals:
1) Supporting the widows camps in addition to the most vulnerable populations (IDPs in Camps) in Idleb and Aleppo.
2) Enhancing wheat value chain in two governorates (Idleb and Aleppo), and consequently enhancing food security in the target areas through inter-linked components.
The project was built on achievements of the previous interventions of HIHFAD (irrigated farmland, a farmer association, agricultural inputs for the vulnerable households and free bread distributions) in the target areas.
10300 HHs in total will directly benefit from this project.
The project consists of five main components:
1) Supporting 800 vegetable farmers with supplementary irrigation costs (Using of diesel vouchers modality), access to irrigation channels located in the Al-Rouge plain, an area of 2 Donums of vegetables agricultural land will be supported for each farmer in addition to do a light rehabilitation to 20 km of the water canals which serving a 800 hectares of strategical agricultural lands in Alrouge plain area, the project engages cash for work scheme for the irrigation works.
2) Supporting the wheat value chain through purchasing wheat seeds from the local harvesting session, where the project is aiming to purchase 1000 MT from 1000 farmers (700 from Idleb and 300 from Aleppo).
3) Supporting 4000 farmers (2800 in Idleb and 1200 in Aleppo) with agriculture inputs (wheat seeds purchased from the local farmers and supplementary irrigation) to support the wheat cultivation in the Al-Rouge plain in Idleb and Albab Subistrict in Aleppo.
4) Providing training on good agriculture practice, storing, marketing and strategic planning for 1270 farmers and staff of the Farmers Associations in addition to members from the local entities at the area of Idleb and Aleppo as well.
5) Processing 455 MT of purchased wheat into 347 MT of wheat flour and distribute it as a fresh bread bundles over 4500 HHs, this component will target the widows camps at the targeted subdistricts in addition to the IDPs in camps for 6 months of distribution 26 days per month via contracting local bakeries.
GAP sessions will train Bas regarding the appropriate and recommended dosage and amount of pesticide and fertilizer to make sure they have good results and benefits of them and decrease the negative impact on the environment.
In order to avoid an escalation of any tensions and to enhance social cohesion, the project will clearly inform the selection criteria to the target communities, and let the communities understand that the vulnerable households are prioritized through the project.
The FB Beneficiaries and worker in all of CFW activities will be selected from the IDPs and HC in coordination with the field local authorities via public registration and according to a specific criteria:
-Households with member/s with disabilities
-Women headed household
-The families with no income source
-Age more than 18.
-Have the capability to carry out such works.
-Host families who are hosting IDPs
also HIHFAD will build on the already existing beneficiary committee in Al Rouge plain and the agriculture offices in Albab Local council, where HIHFAD will make sure that it includes people from the host community and the IDPs in addition to women in order to reach all groups in the targeted, where the selection criteria for the farmers will be:
1. Full access to land
2. Land size (in Dunum) between 5 to 10 Dunums for wheat and 2 Dunums for vegetables
3. Settled in the relevant location for a minimum of 6 months in case of IDP.
4. Previous experience in relevant crop farming.
5. Access to the irrigation system for the vegetables.
6. Agriculture is the main source of income for the household.
where the priority will be given to the vulnerable households from the applicants.
the Project is considered under planned.

4 - Needs

The humanitarian consequences for people suffering the crisis in Syria are wide-ranging and profound. Overall, 14.2 million people need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68.6 per cent of the total population, based on a countrywide assessment, this includes at least 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 million people are considered severely food insecure, significant increase of 4.5 million people facing acute food insecurity in 2021 compared to 2020, where protracted crisis in Syria has intensified needs over the past year through a complex and deepening socio-economic crisis, degradation of livelihoods, knock-on effects of COVID-19, the ongoing conflict in the North of the country and major losses in the food production sector (due to conflict and climate change). These have combined to severely compromise the national food security situation, prices of bread have also tripled for a bundle of bread and only 58 per cent of the population’s minimum bread needs were met in 2020 (HNO 2021).The similar trends in food insecurity were confirmed in HIHFAD specific needs assessment, where stated that the crisis brought devastating impact on the infrastructure including irrigation systems, accessing to the core agricultural inputs, and the economic effects on farmers income. Many people were obliged to discontinue agricultural production, including crop and livestock production, because they did not have the means to afford through the crisis, despite the deterioration in farming activities across the country, agriculture still represents an important source of income and food in all assessed areas.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Persons with disabilities are at higher risks of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers. Women and girls with disabilities are two times at higher risk of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers, to compare with their peers without disabilities all of this makes the persons with disabilities in higher risks of SEA . Females with disabilities remain most invisible groups in the Syrian society, they may not leave their houses, and not participating social events in the communities hugely isolated. Thus, the project will ensure outreaching house-bounded females by ensuring appropriate gender balance within the team, so ensure our access to this group. HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per area. HIHFAD has a pre-established feedback system and safeguarding reporting channel that supports the ability of the targeted populations to submit their complaints, comments, and suggestions in a beneficiary-friendly and easy-to-access way. The system provides diverse options for the convenience of the beneficiaries to deliver their feedback including WhatsApp hotlines, feedback boxes presented at all HIHFAD supported facilities in addition to the direct feedback

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Regarding the COVID-19 Pandemic Risk assessment for the proposed interventions, the probability of this Risky is moderate:Mitigation Measures:-HIHFAD already assigned a special medical task force to follow up all of its activities and provide SOPs and advices regarding COVID-19 issue and sharing its updates.-all of the farmers will receive 700 g of soap based on the recommendations from FSL and WASH clusters.-all of the bread beneficiaries will be received 700 g of soap on monthly basis for 6 months.-all of the distribution points will follow the agreed distribution SOP of the FSL cluster and the internal HIHFAD distribution SOP under COVID-19 pandemic.-all of the training session will take in consideration of the COVID-19 mitigation measures where no more than 10 trainees in each session, or it will be online.-all of HIHFAD field staff is already aware regarding the COVID-19 mitigation measures and will avoid any direct touch with the beneficiaries and will be always wearing the protective masks and gloves.-HIHFAD will print out a big awareness signs and banners and will keep it clearly shown in all of its activities (Distribution points, training, office field visits).Regarding the personnel Risk Assessment for the field Team and the Management and the Resources for the Risk:In general, AL rouge plain is Stable from the security point comparing to the other parts of Idleb Governorate ,this area has not seen any major security incidents within the last years.In general, ALbab is a Stable from the security point comparing to Idleb Governorate areas, except for the UXO/IEDs incidents and the internal tensions between the OGA groups.
