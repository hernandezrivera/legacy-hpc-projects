Project Summary

The HOPE Field Hospital for Women is one of few inpatient facilities operating in an area currently inhabited by over 300,000 refugees. This is one of the facilities providing 24/7, uninterrupted services and designated as an official referral center, receiving continuous transfers. The services this 50-bed maternity hospital provide include 24/7 access to cesarean delivery, NVD, paediatric care, newborn care, neonatal intensive care, emergency antenatal and postpartum care, general emergency and critical care, an Outpatient Department and is fully equipped with an isolation ward in the event of an outbreak. The HOPE Field Hospital for Women is staffed by nearly 200 staff members which include 13 general doctors, 9 specialist doctors including gynaecology, anesthesiology, and pediatrics, 20 nurses, and 13 midwives from Bangladesh. It is also supported by international medical professionals recruited both as employees and volunteers. We are currently focusing on raising funds to continue the vital operations of this 24/7 facility so that we may continue to operate as long as there is a need for our service.

Needs

Women and children make up the largest contingent of the Rohingya refugees who have crossed into Bangladesh. The majority traveled on foot for several days before reaching. Most arrived in Cox’s Bazar very sick from mental and physical stress, starvation and severe injury; with many of the women pregnant or having lost children as a result of illness or violence from the conflict. This under-served, vulnerable population is struggling to survive while living in poor conditions where staggering numbers of people are suffering and dying from preventable diseases. Women face safety and social barriers when required to access health clinics that are not gender-segregated. Of the Rohingya refugees, 52% are women and girls (ISCG, 2019). Rates of pregnancy are high and pregnant women in the Rohingya camps require basic or comprehensive emergency obstetric care. The HOPE Field Hospital for Women is a facility that prioritizes safe spaces for women and also provides basic to tertiary level obstetric care. While 98 percent of health facilities report that women have access to some level of care, according to the ISCG 2020 mid-term Needs Analysis, less than half of women will give birth at a health facility. A major barrier to delivery in a health facility is transportation to access services. This barrier is directly addressed through the location of the facility and also the extensive transportation support network we have created utilizing 4 ambulance and a fleet of 20 TomTom vehicles. Knowledge deficit and lack of socio-cultural acceptance of sexual and reproductive health and family planning are an even larger barrier. In order to encourage and improve health seeking behavior and utilization of essential services, significant outreach, education and community engagement is being performed by Community Health Workers (CHWs). Healthcare costs are also reportedly creating undue burden within households; making access to care that is free-of-cost essential in improving outcomes. Due to the 24/7 operational standard of the HOPE Field Hospital for Women, there is continuous access to a fully staffed and functional operating theater, in-patient care for 20 general female patients, 10 pediatric, 5 newborn and sick newborn patients, 3 labor and delivery patients, 2 post-operative patients, 10 isolation patients and an OPD serving patients of all ages, genders and acuity levels.

Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Increase patient access to care within Rohingya camps by providing inpatient and outpatient services to more than 300 consultations/day or 9,000/month in the refugee and host-community. Activity 2: Increase facility based delivery from 20 to 40 percent of the pregnant women in our catchment area by expanding outreach and conducting more than 250 deliveries per month in the hospital. Activity 3: Provide multiple means for patients to arrive at the HOPE Field Hospital for Women through our 4 ambulance and, as a result of close proximity of service locations, on foot. Activity 4: Improve the quality of care by providing 24/7 un-interupted tertiary services and apply the "Golden Chain Model" of women-centered care, among the Rohingya and host-community populations. Activity 5: Expand the reach of services by educating as many of the 90,194 Rohingya women of Camp 3, Camp 4, Camp 4 extension, and Camp 5 within our catchment area as possible, about the women's health services available, free of charge, through our hospital and SRH centers. Activity 6: Reduce incidence of preventable disease within our catchment area by expanding the reach of our vaccination program through education and increasing patient uptake to a minimum of 10% of all patients being administering government approved immunizations. Activity 7: Provide services to patients of all ages, genders, and acuity who are seeking care through our outpatient department and provide admission for all who are in need of inpatient service. Activity 8: Continue to train and respond to all emergency and disaster related incidents through our Mobile Medical Teams (MMT).
