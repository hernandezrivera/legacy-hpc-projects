Project Summary

Recently the effects of climate change have become more visible in Syria, especially in the last two rainy seasons 2020/2021 and 2021-2022, where there was a significant delay in the onset of rainfall for at least two months across all Syrian governorates, along with an earlier cessation of rainfall. In addition to the poor temporal distribution of rainfall, which is compained with temperature anomalies from their long-term averages during the season, especially during critical periods of crop growth, resulted in drought-like conditions and significant water scarcity in Syria, further worsening food insecurity prevalence.
The water scarcity, damaged irrigation infrastructure and the low rainfall level has forced most farmers, to rely on wells to cover part of their irrigation needs. This will have negative impact on groundwater decline and depletion, leading to high pumping costs. The low production of wheat and other crops during the 2021 & 2022 harvest seasons has resulted in a significant gap in supply and demand, mainly for wheat and other crops such as legumes. Expectations indicate a most probability the agro-climatic conditions of the last two seasons will be highly frequent, especially the distribution and amounts of rain during the upcoming agricultural seasons, especially in northern and southern Syria.
The limited natural resources in Syria, and the exacerbation of the impact of climatic changes, especially drought, led to a decline in the production and yield of crops and livestock, an increase in the vulnerability and food insecurity of farming households. The effects of climate change call for urgent actions, measures and approaches that will help farmers sustainably increase their agricultural productivity and incomes, adapt and build resilience to climate change and greenhouse gas emissions, where possible.
In this context, the proposed project targeting 128,000 households (equivalent to 768,000 people) is based on two main outputs:
Output 1: Awareness raising of farmers’ on the risks of climate change and adoption of climate-smart agriculture (CSA) approaches enhanced. Output 2: TOT for agricultural extension technicians to conduct farmer field schools (FFS) and train them on climate-smart agriculture approaches.
This project will help farmers rationalize the use of natural resources in a sustainable manner, as well as adopting climate-smart agriculture (CSA) approaches that will contribute to improving local food production at the household and national levels as well, and alleviating losses due to extreme climatic condition.

3 - Needs

Agriculture remains a key economic pillar in Syria and a major source of livelihoods for most crisis-affected HH. Crop production being sensitive to weather shocks and climate volatility is being affected by climate change (CC). Many strategic crops in Syria contribute significantly to the countries food basket, including wheat, where Syria is recently a wheat deficit country, and does not produce enough to meet its needs. CC remains a contributing factor to the worsening food insecurity. Overall estimated FSAS PIN for 2023 at 15 million. Furthermore, the limited natural resources in Syria, the effects of CC, have led to a decline in crop and livestock production, increased vulnerability of farming HH and food insecurity. There is urgent need to scale-up and enhance adaptive actions, and taking measures and practices that contribute to adapting to the impact of CC. This project will raise skills, capacity building and increase the resilience of farming HH to cop challenges of CC, by; increasing awareness of the CC risks, the importance of preparing to confront them and reduce their severity, training technical trainers and then training farmers in theory and practice on climate-smart agricultural practices, This intervention will enable technicians as well as targeted farmers to identify climate-smart agricultural patterns and practices and adopt them as an approach that helps mitigate the effects of CC and adapting it, that will enhance the local agricultural food production

4 - Implementation of activities

Output 1: Raising farmers’ awareness on climate change risks and adopting climate-smart agriculture (CSA) approaches. Activities: 1.Determine the prevailing agro-climatic patterns and trends in targeted areas and their impacted by CC. 2. Identify the most vulnerable farmers groups, 3. Determine the identification climate-smart agricultural CSA practices that can be adopted in each region (conservation & mulching cultivation, rain water harvesting, recycling farm residues, use cover crops, soil and water management, adapted varieties..),4. Contracting IP able to facilitate and deliver the required training content, 5. Organize pilot farmer field schools (FFS) in the main districts/governorate according to the agreed approach, 6. Organize FFS in the rest districts through the technical trainers who has trained in the project, 7. Follow-up the implementation of planned activities. Output 2: TOT for agricultural extension technicians to conduct FFS and train them on CSA applications, Activities: 1. Select the extension technicians and assess their efficiency in implementing FFS for CSA practices (20 - 25/governorate), 2. Design a training program to raise the efficiency of agricultural extension trainers on CSA practices, 3. Contracting IP able to facilitate and deliver the required training content, 4.Conduct the training according to the agreed curriculum for agricultural extension technicians, a central, course/governorate, 5.Follow up the implementation the planned activities

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Beneficiaries being targeted due being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiary’s selection. Mitigation: Inclusion different categories affected population (IDPs and host communities; returnees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based on their specific needs and clear selection criteria. 2) Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation: Establishing local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection; and ensuring effective communication for guidance. 3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. Mitigation: Establish community-based beneficiary selection and monitoring. The project taken into account protection elements, in line with the do-no-harm principles. Measures will taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted. Women and youth will be prioritized, trainings will take account the different requirements of women and men, boys and girls. Returnees will also be targeted helping the re-settlement. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well, to limit possible friction. Implementing field activities will be in line with the principles and guidelines on protection developed by Protection Cluster. An accountability to affected population system including beneficiaries’ participation and appropriate information and feedback/complaint mechanism will also be put in place.
