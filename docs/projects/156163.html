Project Summary

In line with the priorities for the Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector (ERL) in HRP 2019 and the ERL Objective 1, the project aims at improving livelihood opportunities of vulnerable unskilled workers in the Aleppo Governorate, such as women heads of households (HH) and young adults between 18 and 35 years of age breadwinner of the family.
The action will be implemented in the areas of Sheikh Najjar and Kallaseh areas (located in Jebel Saman, with a score of 4,19 in the Livelihood and Economic Severity scale). The Aleppo Governorate has been chosen as area of the intervention, as it was one of the main industrial district of the country before the conflict started and economic activities were suspended. From 2017 onwards, companies began to re-open and new enterprises started their business. Thus, the action intends to both enhance the opportunities of more vulnerable unemployed individuals to re-enter the job market as more qualified workers and to provide the business in the areas with the more skilled labour force needed to re-start their economic activities.
Specifically, the action intends to:
1) Provide vocational and skills training to the identified vulnerable unskilled workers. First, businesses in the area in need of qualified workforce will be identified/mapped (in cooperation with the Labour Market Observatory and the Industrial Association of Aleppo) and potential beneficiaries will be identified through a community-based campaign (in cooperation with local NGOs active in the targeted areas). Participants in the training programme will be identified among breadwinners of HH presenting vulnerability factors (e.g. women and young adults of 18 to 35 years old), in line with the prioritazion criteria defined by the ERL Sector. Secondly, businesses and beneficiaries will be matched, and individual training plans will be defined. The project will cover the general training costs (teaching materials, small equipment, etc.).
2) Provide cash assistance to 500 vulnerable HH. The internship programme will last for 3 months and beneficiaries will be supported by a monthly cash assistance covering also insurance and transportation costs.
3) Evaluation of the trainees’ internship performance by GVC and the companies to assess their hiring at the end of the internship programme.
The project foresees the activation of 500 internship in 2 cycles of the duration of 6 months each.
The intervention has been designed based on a previous pilot implemented by an International NGO in cooperation with MOSAL and the Labour Market Observatory in the Damascus industrial area.

Needs

Securing a livelihood is one of the most critical defies faced by people in Syria, with over half of the 5 million pre-crisis jobs no longer existing. The estimation unemployment rate is 53 per cent, reaching as high as 75 per cent among youth 3 (15-24 years) and are significantly higher among women. The proportion of the population living in extreme poverty or on less than US$1.90 a day, has climbed to 69 per cent, over twice the pre-crisis rate. (HNO, 2018)Repeated shocks, including multiple displacements and reduction in livelihood opportunities have contributed to an uncertain future for the majority of Syria’s population. As a result, people have resorted to multiple negative food and livelihoods coping strategies that have often exacerbated the protection risks they are exposed to. Harmful coping mechanisms include early school living, child labour, spending savings, borrowing money, reducing non-food items and depleting productive assets.Adolescent boys are exposed to exploitation, abuse while girls to forced early marriage. Divorced women and widows are at risk to stigmatization and discrimination, forced marriages. The pressure and stress to provide for their families can lead to increased risks of violence against them within the family and at their workplace.Together with Idleb, Aleppo governorate was considered the primary industrial zone in Syria with textile, engineering, chemical and food industries. In 2009, industrial establishments in Aleppo formed 30% of all establishments in Syria, and the sector provided 30% of the country’s total industrial production. Industry used to be the most important income source for workers in the city of Aleppo (Der Spiegel 2013/03/06, SCBS 2009, SCBS 2009).The conflict, coupled with increasingly frequent power outages, has brought virtually all factories to a standstill. Starting from 2017, the productive sector has begun to take off again. Since mid-2017, 400 companies re-opened; while, in 2018, some 150 new enterprises started their business in Jabel Saman District (GVC Need Assessment, May 2018 and January 2019). The proposal has been designed taking into consideration the specific needs of the stakeholders involved. Moreover, the action is also based on the findings of a pilot program implemented by an International NGO in cooperation with MoSAL and the Labour Market Observatory in Damascus Industrial area that successfully involved 500 individuals in internship programs. At the end of the program the companies involved hired the 90% of them, in a stable form. The main identified challenges and needs are:- Lack of qualified personnel: because of the crisis most of the workers are no longer available and the majority of the labor force is made up of unskilled women or young boys and girls 18-35 years old; - Companies in order to resume or start production are facing huge investments and do not have the necessary resources to take on the costs of training workers as well;-Women Heads of Households do not have the required technical skills to access into the labor market and, consequently, they are forced to accept unskilled jobs with low salaries that do not permit them to sustain the family's economic burden, leading them to adopt harmful coping strategies;-Young boys and girls (18-35 years old) are often the only economically active member within the family, but having had to leave or not complete the school they do not have the necessary skills to perform a qualified and well-paid job. Given their low level of education and training, young men often faced two alternatives: either join the military or enter the informal job market or the ‘war economy’ (World Bank, 2017).

Implementation of activities

ERL Objective 1: Strengthen access to livelihood by creating income generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery- relates to SOActivities: 1.1 Vocational and skills training provided to 500 vulnerable unskilled workers ( indicator: 500 people provided with vocational and skills training). This activity foresees the follow preparatory actions: -Identification/mapping of companies in need of workforce: definition of the required profiles and the number of workers. This activity will be implemented in cooperation with the Labour Market Observatory and the Industrial Association of Aleppo; -Identification of potential beneficiaries. A tailored community-based campaign will be implemented in order to inform potential beneficiaries about the job opportunities offered by the companies located in Sheikh Najjar and Kallaseh areas (located in Jebel Saman 4,19 Livelihood and Economic Sevirity scale). The campaign will be run in cooperation with the local NGOs active in the targeted area. The internship program will address vulnerable people, giving preference to women heads of Households and boys / girls between 18 and 35 years of age breadwinner of the family. 1.2 Cash assistance to 500 vulnerable HHs (indicator: 500 vulnerable households (Female headed households, elderlies, etc..) provided cash assistance -Selection of beneficiaries through the matching of the profiles identified by the companies and the applications received. This will be done in cooperation with the local NGOs involved in the selection’s campaign. -Definition of the individual on job training plans that will be carried out within companies by the enterprise staff (the training period will last 3 months). The project will cover the general training costs (teaching materials, small equipment, etc.);-Internship activation: the internship period for each beneficiary will lasts 3 months. The project will support each trainee with a monthly cash assistance that will include insurance and transportation costs. 1.3 Regular employment accessed (indicator: 425 people obtaining or accessing regular employment, as a result of sector support)Once the internship period is over, the companies together with GVC will evaluate the trainees’ internship performance and will decide whether to employ them in compliance with national laws and regulation. The project foresees the activation of 500 internship in 2 cycles. It is estimated that 85% of the beneficiaries involved in the project at the end of the training period will be permanently hired by the companies.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISKS: 1. Security situation deteriorates to the point to prevent the implementation of some of the activities. 2. Shrinking of the humanitarian and operational space in some intervention area due to military operation. 3. Major change in demographic in the area of intervention due to population movements requires modification in the strategy.4. Major change in attitude of authorities and local NGOs toward the planned interventions. 5. Diversion of aid from staff. RISK MITIGATION STRATEGY:GVC ensures a continuous coordination with local partners (SARC) and international specialized agencies (UNDSS) in order to monitor the situation. GVC adopted a security plan and SOP in case of any major accident.1. Policy environment is monitored through the attendance to Cluster, Working Groups and Coordination Mechanisms.2. GVC monitors the demography in the area and liaise with other agency in the area.3. GVC ensures good relationship with authorities in the area, informing them of the planned activities. It ensures a transparent and accountable way of working.4. Plans of intervention (rehabilitation) are shared and discussed in order to increase acceptance.5. GVC ensures directly the monitoring and evaluation of the activities. CONTINGENCY MEASURES1. Security alert system in place prevents risk and different routes or timing are adopted to assure the service. In case of prolonged period of instability, activities will be stand by and information released to the donor.2. If it is impossible to access the area, it will be studied the change of the area (any change in the implementation strategy will be discussed with the donor).3. Any unpredictable decision of MoLA on priorities to assure livelihood support and development in the target areas will be discussed and solutions found according to the final objective of the project and to the improvement of the conditions of the beneficiaries. Any corrective actions will be shared with the donor and be accountable to the budget availability.4. Performance of staff will be monitored by the Syrian and Regional coordination staff. Any breach will result in expulsion from the staff and the replacement with other personnel.
