Project Summary

ACTED will aim to implement FSL interventions in 2020 with the following objectives:
1. To provide immediate short-term food assistance to 431,477 vulnerable individuals in Greater Equatoria, Western Bahr el Ghazal, Jonglei, Warrap, Upper Nile and Lakes states, to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies. Immediate food assistance will be done through GFD + distributions either in-kind or through cash, complementary with BSFP where appropriate.
2. To enhance emergency food production, livelihoods and build resilience against future shocks of 47,037 beneficiaries in targeted communities by provision of unconditional cash transfers, conditional cash transfers, and seeds and tools input. Conditional cash transfers will not only improve purchasing power of beneficiaries, but will also contribute to creation, construction and rehabilitation of key livelihood assets such as water catchments and dykes to manage floods. Restricted cash will focus on purchase of livelihood inputs and assets such as seeds and tools to enable beneficiaries invest on their lands thereby increasing amount of land planted, and addressing the challenge of seeds scarcity. ACTED will also provide the most vulnerable HHs with fish, vegetables, and crop kits and respective appropriate tools.
3. To reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs through long term investment on productive assets through resilience programming. ACTED will adopt the APFS model to encourage transfer of knowledge and skills in modern farming techniques, encourage community engagement through participatory risk assessments, toward participatory planning and joint ownership of resources. Such an investment will be pegged on utilization of local capacities and knowledge, which will then be reinforced through interactive sessions toward realizing improved livelihoods, reduced gender inequality, inclusive economic and social growth, and overall transformation of communities. ACTED will target 20,486 beneficiaries under this objective.

1 - Needs

- Years of violent conflict have left millions of South Sudanese displaced as the country is facing one of the most devastating humanitarian crises in recent history. Based on the latest IPC analysis from September 2019, 6.35 million people in South Sudan are classified in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) or worse acute food insecurity from August 2019 to April 2020. - WBeG: The cattle migration crisis that started in February 2019 again caused massive displacements and disruptions in livelihoods activities. Border conflict between Wau and Tonj might cause new waves of displacements. Resulting in impeding trade flows and market functioning, as well as disrupting livelihoods and triggering displacements. Over 55,000 IDPs are reported to live in five collective centers across the county, with 13,072 individuals currently residing in UNMISS POCAA (BMR, as of September 2019). - Jonglei: across South Sudan there has been a 25% decrease in annual cereal production from 2014 to 2017, resulting in a 500,000 metric ton deficit for 2018 (FAO, 2018). Unchanged from the previous reporting period, in January-March 2019, REACH has shown that only 32% of assessed settlements reported adequate access to food in Akobo county (REACH, 2019) - Lakes: A REACH situation overview from April to June 2019 found that insecurity and food shortages due to poor harvests continued to drive large-scale humanitarian needs across the eastern Lakes region (REACH, 2019). Access to food remained low in this period, with 70% of assessed settlements reporting inadequate access to food in June. The last IPC findings of the FSL Cluster show that Awerial, Yirol East and West counties are all classified by the IPC as in Crisis (Phase 3) and Emergency (Phase 4) acute food insecurity, with 8000 HHs facing famine in Yirol East (FSL Cluster, October 2019). - Warrap: Episodic cattle raids and insecurity in Tonj North and East counties, primarily near Toich along the border with Unity State caused unseasonal population movement and minor displacement west primarily to Warrap town, Akop and Theit in December 2018. Recent floods in August and September 2019 have worsen the populations living condition as it inundated shelters, water sources, crops and crucial productive assets in many areas. They have also damaged essential service infrastructure like schools and health facilities, impacting women and children in particular (UN OCHA, 2019). - Upper Nile: this county shows a unique humanitarian context: it is not only the source of significant South Sudanese cross-border displacement, but is also home to 143,496 refugees (Source: UNHCR), most of whom came as a result of the ongoing fighting that erupted in the Blue Nile State of Sudan in 2011. As a result of this population influx, Maban host community has witnessed significant changes in their land and environment since 2011, and competition for scarce resources have led to several episodes of tensions between the different communities. Most specifically, tensions have crystallized over land grazing, water, fuel sources, and rampant deforestation as a result of charcoal burning and timber harvesting. Recurrent and recent floods in Maban have showed high imminent food needs in both the camps and host communities (Inter-Agency situation report, October 2019). - Western Equatoria: With a poorly functioning national and local economy and markets, and limited business and livelihood opportunities with a reliance on mostly subsistence farming, unemployment levels are high, particularly for youth. ACTEDs recent needs-assessment had identified the key areas of support needed to improve current livelihoods as improved roads, access to capital, and increased skills in both agronomic practices and business management (August, 2019). The main challenges affecting livelihoods were identified as high food prices (61%), human health (53%), as well as farm inputs, roads, water, and training.

2 - Activities

1. Immediate food assistance 1.1 Unconditional GFD distributions (in-kind and cash) in the states of Wau, Kuajok, Bor, Mingkaman, Rumbek, and Maban, complementary by BSFP. 1.1.2 GFD+ activities to complement GFD and BSFSP: GFD + activities will be provided in accordance with a) type of beneficiary (IDP, REF, or RES), and taking into account whether beneficiaries have already received GFD+ activities before, and whether they have access to land. Activities: a) engagement in land preparation activities; b) Financial literacy and nutrition listening sessions; c) Hygiene and sanitation trainings; d) Exploring Agronomic businesses; market linkages 1.2 Unconditional cash transfers in hard-to-reach locations through the mobile team:This will complement conditional cash transfers, and respond to immediate, unforeseen & catastrophic crisis in areas of intervention. The selection criteria will prioritize HHs with PLW and malnourished children <5, child-headed HHs, displaced populations, HHs headed by older persons and other marginalized groups. SGBV survivors will be reached through a Cash for Protection program. 1.3 Construction/rehabilitation of communal assets through a CfA scheme:Livelihood assets will be rehabilitated/constructed to increase access to shared resources, thereby easing tensions related to resources. Assets will also improve both agricultural and livestock production, by serving the communities during droughts, floods and other calamities. Activities include: Construction of haffirs; dykes; & community access roads; construction and rehabilitation of shallow wells; fencing of livelihood assets. 1.4 Provision of emergency food and livelihood support through fishing & quick maturing vegetable seeds in counties with IPC Phase 3+:Most vulnerable HHs will be provided with fish, vegetables, and crop kits and respective appropriate tools 2. Long term resilience activities 2.1 Formation and training of APFS especially for pastoral communities, provision of inputs:In order to foster knowledge and skills in modern farming, ACTED will work with APFS groups. Beneficiaries will learn about modern farming techniques in demonstrations gardens. They will replicate what they learn in their own individual gardens and share knowledge with their neighbors. This model will promote large-scale production of agricultural produce & improve animal husbandry including management of crops & livestock diseases. Groups will receive improved seeds, tools, & ox-plough services to enhance their production.2.2 Agri-business/entrepreneurship training:This will aim to increase the business skills and knowledge of farmers, to encourage and support them in marketing and selling their produce effectively, as well as to incentive increased surplus production by highlighting the benefits and profitability of agricultural production. 2.3 Investment in seed multiplication farms: APFS members will be encouraged to produce good quality seeds for subsequent planting seasons & also for sale in local markets. ACTED will work with farmers, linking them to local suppliers & potential customers through trade fairs. 2.4 Vegetable production during the dry season:APFS modules will be nutrition-sensitive to emphasize importance of diet diversification. This will be in the effort to address nutrition of the target population. Vegetable production will be a step forward to encourage diet diversification. 2.5 Formation and training of Village Savings & loans associations:These are informal institutions that continue to play a key role in enabling access to credit, savings in form of assets in the face of hyperinflation, and they encourage livelihood diversification to both APFS/FFS members and other interested community members. 2.6 Post-harvest trainings: These will be factored into the APFS model, and emphasized throughout the year. Post management structures such as granaries and drying slabs will be constructed to promote proper handling, drying & storage of produce.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

ACTED will focus on the integration of protection in its approach during all FSL interventions. ACTED will conduct a protection analysis of the activities and beneficiaries (including collecting gender-sensitive data) at the onset of the project to identify/ map potential risks to the beneficiaries and communities and the underlying factors and drivers of these protection risks, in collaboration with the AME Department. Protection outcomes will be integrated into the activities based off this assessment, such as reducing negative coping strategies and risks to beneficiaries by improving their access to food, livelihoods and income. ACTED will ensure that all project activities and locations of sites (e.g. demonstration plots, haffirs, apiaries) are in safe and accessible locations, and that they do not pose protection risks. ACTED is aware that prevailing gender norms present challenges in engaging and ensuring the participation of women in certain project activities. For instance, women and girls generally have responsibilities of farming, collecting water and firewood, preparing food, cleaning, and childcare. Additionally, due to the restrictive and deep-rooted cultural norms and mind-sets regarding women, ACTED must respect to a degree some of these norms to ensure acceptance and ownership of the project by the community, and therefore not realistic to target an equal number of women for various activities such as CfW labor, blacksmiths and CAHWs. Nevertheless, the project overall will aim to target an equal, if not higher, amount of women beneficiaries than men, however this target is not feasible for all activities. Taking into consideration the above gender norms and household roles, the timing of activities will aim to be compatible with women’s daily activities to avoid conflict and to ensure maximum participation of women. ACTED will continue to sensitize the community and encourage women, girls, youth participation in all project activities.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with a dedicated Accountability Officer based in Juba that will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) in WBeG, and oversee reporting for the project to ensure beneficiaries can objectively report complaints and feedback. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels, as well as the training of project staff on collecting and responding to complaints, and sensitization of the community in accountability practice will be utilized. All complaints will be compiled and reported on each month and the Accountability Officer will ensure that follow up is conducted by the relevant people. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and the community, and improve the quality of our programmes. For mobile responses ACTED will initiate bi-monthly feedback sessions with the community, in which they will be able to provide feedback. In here ACTED will ensure that most vulnerable individuals will have access to these sessions. Overall, ACTED in camp settings, ACTED will ensure to collaborate with Protection partners, to ensure that the most vulnerable community members will have access to feedback.
