HRP Project Code
Project Summary : The proposed action aims to provide life-saving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable displaced populations living in camps and camp-like settings by providing WASH and winterization services. Despite humanitarian presence, most IDPs continue to face critical WaSH needs, including a lack of water tanks and sustainable solid waste solutions (garbage bins, sewage networks). This is particularly problematic in light of the COVID pandemic.
Findings from the CCCM Cluster confirm that, despite the humanitarian presence, WaSH needs are still not fully covered in Azaz. In fact, according to Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme (HNAP) report of Dec 2020, 40% of IDPs in NWS report that water is their first priority. In terms of infrastructure, at least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated, while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional and 29% of garbage is inappropriately disposed within communities (HNO 2021), with 64% of water stations not functional, according to the Syrian Water Resources Platform. SI assessments in the area of operation indicate that respondents rely heavily on NGO supported water supply sources, primarily water trucking.
SI seeks to address identified needs with a comprehensive WASH approach, including:
• Water network rehabilitation: 4 water supply systems will be rehabilitated; 1 in Al Bab, 1 in Ghandorah and 2 in Aghtrin. (Target 23.718 individuals)
• Water trucking as a last resort for three months while SI rehabilitates the water network in Ghandorah (Target: 24.932 individuals) in areas without a viable more sustainable alternative
• Distribution of 106 water storage tanks in particular for households with PwDs (Target: 3556 individuals)
• Solid waste management: Distribution of garbage containers nearby the PWD (in the camps where we are already working). Waste removal will be funded in some locations by SCHF, while other locations are already covered with other funds. (Target: 38.705 individuals)
• Hygiene promotion in all 17 targeted locations in three sub-districts of Azaz, coupled with hygiene kit distributions in those locations not covered by other funding. (Target: 33.091, including 1.098 individuals that benefit from HK distributions)
• Installation of solar lights in shelter and WASH facilities in camps and informal settlements. (Target: 11.157 individuals)
Beneficiary selection criteria for individual activities vary, but are always based on needs and vulnerabilities. Gender equality will be prioritized and beneficiary engagement throughout the project cycle will ensure transparency and awareness of project activities. Targeting will be based on needs analysis, inter-agency coordination, and SI’s ability to maintain humanitarian safe and principled access. SI will use the Washington Group questionnaire in the identification of barriers and risks of persons with disabilities in accessing water, sanitation, and hygiene services. Protection mainstreaming occurs by installing solar lights in targeted camps, increasing safety and security of vulnerable beneficiaries in public areas during the dark winter months. The sustainability of the intervention strategy is further integrated by inclusion of rehabilitation of water- and sewage networks.

4 - Needs

SI has conducted assessment in camps, informal settlements and villages hosting new arrivals in different sub-districts in NWS (Jarablus, AlBab, Al Ghandorah and Aghtrin; ensuring data and analysis reporting to the WASH cluster as well as to key authorities for actively contributing to the mobilization of new emergency response capacities. Many sites identified are dependent on external support in order to ensure adequate quantities (98%) on water supply, solid waste and hygiene items (see ANX 1). SI's next assessment report is expected in February 2022. In addition, SI conducts environmental health surveys on a bi-monthly basis (see ANX 18). These surveys are based on observation of sanitation, access to water and solid waste management in all camps; helping SI monitor the situation in all locations to adapt the intervention when needed.

5 - Implementation of activities

WASH:SI activities focus on ensuring that WASH life-saving emergency supplies and services are provided within targeted IDPs. The 2021 HNO report highlights this gap and adds that access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation, solid waste management and/or hygiene supplies will remain a real challenge for 1.75 million people living in IDP sites that are highly dependent on continued humanitarian assistance. Through this program SI will support all newly and longterm displaced people in and out of camp, with: - Daily supply of safe, sufficient and treated water in the form of water points for an average of 35/40 Liter per person per day through water trucking and pumping. - SWM- HP and Hygiene kit distribution

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks include: • Inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across locations • Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees due to varied assistance packages • Abuse of power by staff distributing goods or delivering services to vulnerable populations. • Road accidents, physical harm • Restricted access (due to insecurity of COVID-19 lockdowns) • COVID-19 public health concerns Mitigation measures for the identified risks are integrated in SI’s implementation strategy, starting with transparent selection criteria agreed upon in coordination with the relevant clusters. The risk of abuse of power is mitigated by thorough vetting of locally recruited staff, who all abide by the SI Code of Conduct. SI also has a Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM) in place that allows for real-time feedback from beneficiaries and other stakeholders. The FCRM is an accountability tool that simultaneously facilitates the collection of lessons learnt that will be used to improve future interventions.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks:Gender-based violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), tends to increase during times of crisis. Risks of this occurring further increase in situations of aid dependency and power imbalances. SI is cognizant of the inherent risks related to service delivery, notably for cash and in-kind distributions. To ensure that assistance delivered is safe, relevant and timely, SI has accountability systems and procedures in place.Mitigation and prevention measures:SI is determined to prevent the sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers and reaffirms its goal of achieving full implementation of the 6 core principles adopted by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee Task Force on Prevention and Response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. SI's PSEA Policy complements the Code of Conduct and Protection Policy, both of which state that it is strictly forbidden for any employee i) to use its position to obtain any favor, including sexual favors, and ii) to have sexual intercourse with minors. SI’s contracts and partnership agreements include a standard clause requiring sub-contractors to adopt policies that prohibit SEA and to take adequate measures to prevent and respond when required. A Whistleblowing Policy and a Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanisms Framework are key pillars of SOLIDARITES INTERNATIONAL Policies package. SI has PSEA focal points to identify and address any PSEA concerns and implements a referral system when required.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• Access to vulnerable population and vendors is not further reduced by additional border-crossings restrictions • Operations are not further limited by regulatory restrictions inside Syria • Safety and security of SI and partner staff is not compromised • SI is able to appropriately manage the risks of aid diversion to designated terrorist groups or through corruption and bribery • Exchange rates and material/service costs remain reasonably stable throughout implementation period of the project • Sufficient materials and skills required for the output are available in the targeted geographic areas• Construction companies with sufficient capacity are available inside Syria• Labour with sufficient technical skills is available inside Syria• Security situation allows regular sharing of information between stakeholders• Communication channels remain open and enable relevant monitoring methods• Payment mechanisms remain open for the reimbursement of suppliers• Inter-agency coordination mechanisms minimize the risk of duplication and variations in levels of assistance. Risks: Absence or non-materialization of the assumptions mentioned aboveMitigation: • Use money transfer system (hawala) for operations in North Syria including for the payment of its staff and contractors • Cooperation with Turkish authorities and capacity to work Cross-Border from Turkey. • SI maintains a representation in Gaziantep in Turkey with its main coordination office in Amman
