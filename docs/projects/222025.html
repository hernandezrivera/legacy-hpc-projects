Project Summary

This project seeks to address critical education in emergency needs in displacement-affected communities, focusing on displaced and returning children, youth and teachers including some host community to help them achieve self-reliance and endure the continuation of their education.
NRC team, in close collaboration with relevant authorities, identified the gaps on the education sector due to the conflict, and based on the understanding of the context, NRC will address the urgent needs of education such as learning gaps and psychosocial support integrated into the education system. The response will also target teachers, who are essential supportive resources for the quality education in the communities, since teachers themselves face difficult conditions, including overcrowded classrooms, limited resources, and insufficient training in critical areas such as supporting children with disabilities, providing psychosocial support (PSS), and implementing conflict sensitive education and learning strategies.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Considering educational needs has been increased in Cabo Delgado province due to the conflict, the 12-month project will support vulnerable boys, girls and youth to be enrolled into formal and non-formal education. Programmes such as AEP, catch up and remedial programmes will be conducted, as well as their reintegration into formal education, including children with disabilities. This project will be conducted in Cabo Delgado districts considered as priority 1, 2 and 3 in a way to strengthen quality of improve access to education and trainings. To address barriers to accessing and promoting continuation of education, the project will address the general shortage of supplies through the Community Based Approach/School Improvement Plan to let the community identify and develop their school plans based on the realistic needs, supported by NRC through cash provision. To ensure out of school children have access to education, non-formal education programmes such as Accelerated Education Programs, Catch-up classes and Remedial classes will be offered to enable the children to recover lost lessons and keep up with others. To strengthen inclusive education, referral pathways will be in place for referrals on community sensitization messaging on the importance of inclusive education at community level. Additionally, teachers will be provided with professional development on various aspects, including training on psychosocial approaches, 'teachers in crisis contexts' packages, ´Better Learning Programme´ and conflict sensitive education to ensure all children receive quality education in a Safe and Inclusive environment. The project will also conduct enrolment campaigns, including door to door, community dialogues and drives targeting out of school children, girls and children with disabilities will be referred to other specialised partners. The project will also strengthen and improve the learning environment by ensuring access to clean drinking water and access to inclusive and gender sensitive sanitation and hygiene facilities through construction of local model of water harvest System, gender sensitive latrines and promotion of good hygiene practices through campaigns led by the ABC-Education Community. Our response will be focus on improvement of the quality of available services due to prolonged disruptions and attacks on education. The programmes are to ensure the continuation of education process for OOSC in Cabo Delgado, whose right to education was severely affected by the displacement. The target will not be only returning children and youth including teachers but also will include host communities and new displacement affected children and youth and teachers. For the conflict response, key interventions include addressing key learning gaps and ensuring access to formal and non-formal education basic learning materials, for displaced children and implementing programs that are tailored to meet different age groups' needs: Accelerated Education (Accelerated Learning, Catch-up and Remedial) focus on children aged 6-12 for reintegration into government led formal education, while Alternative Education (Life skills, basic literacy and numeracy) target adolescents aged 13-17, who have been out of school for long periods or have never been enrolled in formal education face greater risks of not returning to/ entering the school system. Learning supplies, hygiene kits, and menstrual hygiene materials will be distributed to support school enrolment and attendance, particularly for adolescent girls, and prevent school dropout. School feeding programmes encourage attendance and provide essential nutrients to support cognitive development and learning in emergencies.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The NRC project will target the districts of Cabo Delgado such as Meluco, Nangade, Mueda, Muidumbe and Quissanga, where education services have been severely disrupted and thousands of children lack access to safe learning opportunities. In Meluco, due to the number out of school children reported, NRC plans to implement Accelerated Education Programme to absorb out of school children from out of school ending up re-integrating them in the formal education. At the same extent, NRC intends to integrate the PSS activities at school level to support children recover and overcome trauma and distress. To make sure that the learning process is safe for children, teacher training on TICC, MHPSS will be part of the project, so that teachers are ready to deal with conflict-affected children taking into account their different experiences and behaviours.In Nangade as well, NRC will assist schools with cash modality for schools themselves to conduct teachers training on TiCCs, MHPSS and inclusive education. NRC itself will conduct the PSS activities including exercises that can help children to overcome trauma and stress, ending up on improving their learning outcomes and wellbeing. These all programmes will be integrated on the Accelerated education programme including catch up. In Mueda, NRC will prioritise the Accelerated education programme to target out of school children who have lost their learning for 1 or more year or even 6 months. NRC will include in this assistance the MHPSS activities focused into the better learning programme. In Muidumbe, the priority will be supporting children to deal or manage stressful events, and dear with trauma and overcome. Finally, in Quissanga, NRC will assist schools with cash modality for schools themselves to conduct teachers training on TiCCs, MHPSS and inclusive education. NRC itself will conduct the PSS activities including exercises that can help children to overcome trauma and stress, ending up on improving their learning outcomes and wellbeing. These all programmes will be integrated on the Accelerated education programme including catch up.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

NRC will ensure PSEA measures are integral to the education response, with mandatory training for all staff and partners on PSEA principles and reporting procedures, ensuring compliance with inter-agency reporting and referral pathways. Community-based complaints mechanisms will be established within educational facilities, providing children and parents with a safe, confidential way to report issues. Monitoring systems will ensure accountability and responsiveness to PSEA incidents, with a focus on protecting children and supporting survivors.The project meets the needs of the displacement affected population by targeting IDPs, Returnees, and host community most vulnerable populations to ensure the DO NO HARM principles are met.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The NRC Education will prioritises community engagement and accountability to ensure that diverse population groups, including women, girls are informed of their rights to educational assistance. Efforts are made to provide access to safe, trusted, and existing reporting mechanisms adapted to the needs of all groups. These mechanisms allow beneficiaries to express concerns and provide feedback on services. The education Team will work to ensure that inclusive feedback mechanisms are in place within school communities, and to enable parents, students, and caregivers to actively participate in decision-making processes. Gender-sensitive approaches will ensure that the voices of girls and women are amplified, addressing barriers they may face in accessing education and reporting concerns. By fostering a participatory approach that respects their needs. NRC will ensure that education interventions are safe, and inclusive. The project will guarantee accountability to affected populations is further reinforced through community engagement, particularly with women and girls, in the design and delivery of interventions. Monitoring systems are in place to protect beneficiaries from exploitation and abuse, while also tailoring education programmes to meet the intersectional needs of school-aged children, including gender- and age-specific vulnerabilities. These efforts aim to prevent prolonged disruptions to learning pathways and foster continuity within the education system in Mozambique.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Although NRC is not a specialised organization in Disability, a mechanism will be put in place to make sure that identified cases of children with disabilities who demand a specialised support are referred to other organization. The identification processes will be through the assessments, PDM and FGDs which will be conducted at the beginning and during the assistance to make sure our intervention is supporting and creating an equal access to education for OOSC. Also, community engagement and accountability will be conducted to ensure that diverse population groups, including children with disabilities, and vulnerable individuals, are informed of their rights to educational assistance. Efforts are made to provide access to safe, trusted, and existing reporting mechanisms adapted to the needs of all groups. These mechanisms allow beneficiaries to express concerns and provide feedback on services.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

One of the NRC Mozambique Country strategy objective in 2024 states that the “Displacement affected people can access effective and relevant assistance, because people are allowed to define their own vulnerabilities and needs, and can drive the design of the humanitarian response.” and based on this, the modus operandi of the CCs is encouraged to integrate mechanisms that “NRC programmes DO NO HARM to the environment.” NRC Mozambique as a country office, is on an adaptive process to maximise de Environmental & Climate Change. At that extent, some fundamental measures have been included, such as: prevention, mitigation and restoration.• Migration from the use of generators to solar panels;• Environmental work that promotes no west of paper through recycling;• Migration to digitalisation of processes;• Cleaning Campaigns for school environment cleaning on daily basis and waste management;• Trees planting at school;• Project participant sensitization on felling trees. At the field level, the project's environmental impact is to ensure that the school is a safe place for learning, including the issue of sanitation. School cleaning activities, including ABC education through the cash distribution, will create the possibility of building cisterns that act as rainwater collectors and effectively contribute to mitigating erosion and landslides.
