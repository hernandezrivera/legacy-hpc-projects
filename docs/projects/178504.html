Project Summary

The project aims at providing community based health services to over 258,000 most vulnerable returnees, IDPs and affected communities in high return areas, supporting 70 health workers and rehabilitate 43 health facilities. The project will effectively contribute to building the resilience of the community in the early recovery phase through rehabilitation of infrastructure, building the capacity of health professionals, NGO partners, and communities while ensuring community participation. The project is designed to contribute to the sector response strategy in addressing health needs of new born, children, adolescents, women in reproductive age and beyond, persons with disability, older person, and persons with chronic medical conditions, while mainstreaming GBV prevention and MHPSS in services and in the community through capacity building.
UNHCR community centres will be used to ensure linkages with other relevant sectors and address other factors determinants of health. The project will be implemented in all governorates in Syria, prioritizing areas hosting large number of IDP and refugee returnees.

4 - Needs

Health facilities, the waste management system, sewage and water networks are either damaged or in a state of disrepair after over a decade of neglect and are in need of maintenance and rehabilitation. Only 58% of hospitals and 53% of primary health care centers (PHCs) are fully functional. A chronic shortage of health workers is estimated at 70%, which impact the access and quality of health services. There is also a severe shortage of medicines available in the market. The unhealthy shelter conditions and poor access to safe water are negative factors impacting health, especially for children, older persons and people in camps or collective shelters. The morbidity rate for epidemic-prone and non-communicable diseases had increased by 40%, while mortality rate of children increased by 7.5 per 1,000 births. Nutrition of children, and pregnant and lactating women is affected.Distance, overcrowding and long waiting times are the top barriers reported by patients to access health services. Public health facilities, which provide free of charge health care are facing challenges relating to a shortage in health professionals and an interrupted supply chain of medical supplies. The acute shortage of health workers, estimated at 70% and driven by displacement, death, injury and the departure of health workers from The Syrian Arab Republic, is a chronic issue that has an impact on access to and quality of health services. This issue is difficult to overcome within a short timeframe, even with sufficient financial investments. Transportation to the nearest health facility ranges between 25 minutes to two hours and it is often unaffordable.

5 - Implementation of activities

Local NGOs partners will provide community based health services. Collaboration with UN agencies (WHO, UNFPA and UNICEF) and “ MoH healthy villages program “ will support awareness materials, survey tools, and capacity building. Establishing strong relationships by partners NGOs and UNHCR field with community leaders, other NGOs actors and private actors in the geographic areas of interventions, will be vital in advocacy and knowledge sharing. The rehabilitation of health facilities to revive the free of charge access of PoCs to health services will be implemented by INGOs. Partnership agreements will guide and support the INGO work in the rehabilitation of health facilities. Health facilities meeting the general criteria for rehabilitation adopted by the health sector will be selected in coordination and cooperation with the health sector and health authorities. Bills of quantities will be revised and approved by health authorities. Cooperation and joint work when feasible with health actors will be maintained. Monitoring will be conducted weekly by partners, monthly by UNHCR teams in the field, as well as third party monitors and UNHCR country office when need be. Partners reporting focal points will share monthly reports in UNHCR online reporting system which feed a dashboard that include reporting requirement of the sector and of the public health section in HQ. UNHCR balanced score card will be used initially to set the base line in the assessment of PHC services. Specially designed monitoring checklists for PHC and CBH services will be used and will feed the online HIS on the progress and quality of services. UNHCR will report to the sector while mainstreaming AAP and Gender thought out the response

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Sexual exploitation and abuse by service providers Mitigating measures: Partners to adopt code of conduct that include PSEA, and training provided to all partners by UNHCR Awareness sessions and materials on free services and information regarding PSEA posted in health points and PHCs. Female nurse/physician assistant is always present with the male physician when patients are received and examined. Receptionists and some of the guards in the PHCs are female. Risk 2: Lack of community engagement and feedback. Mitigating measures: Community participation is clearly integrated in the project design and implementation. Establishing community feedback mechanisms.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network to ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do no harm” principle. PSEA is a strategic priority for UNHCR. In addition to mandatory trainings to its own staff, since 2021, PSEA capacity assessment has become mandatory for all UNHCR partners. Partners who failed to meet the standard of the PSEA checklist will not be able to continue its partnership with UNHCR in the following year.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Limited ability to conduct monitoring of delivery of assistance results in potential loss of resources. Mitigating measures: Advocacy with authorities for access to beneficiary data. Strengthen risk based monitoring tools and establishing remote/third party monitoring arrangements where UNHCR does not have direct access. Support partners to establish harmonized monitoring systems and provide training to partner staff on monitoring. Strengthen communication with communities and feedback mechanisms. Risk 2: Planned activities obstructed by the volatile economic situation, such as price inflation, currency depreciation and shortage of supply Mitigating measures: Early release of resources to partners to allow for more time for implementation. Flexibilities in planning and implementation in response to market changes, such as allowing for increased price and reduced target. Risk 3: Limited national capacity of MoH in staffing and running health facilities might hinder the plans for the long term sustainable solution in ensuring health services for PoC in rural areas. Mitigating measures: Coordination and investigation of the capacity before investing in health infrastructure rehabilitation. Supporting cheaper options, such as mobile health points. Community empowerment.
