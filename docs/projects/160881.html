Project Summary

There are several decades of armed conflicts between different groups in South Sudan which have resulted into many contaminations by explosive ordnances (IMSMA, Aug. 2019). This is a dilemma for most community members who would prefer to use the contaminated land for agricultural purposes or for Government of South Sudan to put up infrastructure such as schools, hospitals, among others. Periodic statistics show that there are protections risks including those which need immediate risk education on dangers of explosive ordnances, safe behaviour and how to report any harzardous objects. In Jonglei State where Duk and Twic East Counties fall, the data provided by UNMAS on number of hazard areas by type, county, payam in June, 2019 indicates that there are confrontation area, minefield and UXO spots which form part of the reported cases. The reported cases in Terekeka County (Central Equatoria State) is even more higher making the location fall under Tier 1 in the severity list. For years, Terekeka area has been affected by conflicts related to cattle raiding, competition over grazing land and water sources, and cycles of revenge attacks; despite numerous dialogue and peace conferences (UNMISS, April, 2019). During such cases, the people endanger their lives as they flee active conflicts or in search of grazing land, firewood or water as they cross places which have not been cleared of mines. The main objective of this project will be to sensitize and educate the men, women, girls and boys on the dangers of explosive ordnance, how to report them, stay away from marked locations and how to call for help in such cases.
There could be many incidences which are not reported due to various reasons ranging from lack of knowledge on who to report to; not aware of what explosive ordnances really are, being ignorant, among others. Therefore, the reported number could not be the exact case on the ground. The purpose of this project will be to raise awareness through explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) which will have an impact on the capacity of civilians to safely access basic services such as health, NFIs, education, water and even land for settlement provided by other agencies after they stay on safe path as they access such services thereby increasing their coping mechanisms in Terekeka, Duk and Twic East Counties. CINA project will therefore contribute to the reclamation of a safe and inhabitable surrounding which will also contribute to improved outcomes across different sectors in the humanitarian response by providing EORE awareness to the elderly, IDPs, returnees, host community who have a lot of potential in socio-economic activities which can be of benefit to the societal economic building.
This project will target 50,000 (14,000 men, 14,000 women, 11,000 boys, 11,000 girls) beneficiaries who will include persons with disabilities, the elderly, IDPs, Host Community and Returnees. Explosive hazards inhibit socioeconomic development activities and Jonglei and Central Equatoria States are among those which have critical mine actions needs where EORE activities will help provide both direct and indirect protection to the community members because EORE messages are key to ensure that people’s behaviour towards unexploded ordnances within their surrounding change for better. The project is seeking for an amount of USD 500,000 to implement this project in the year 2020 for considerable impact in the project locations.

1 - Needs

The total contamination area is estimated by UNMAS (as of September 2019) to approximately 3,361 football pitches with 345 remaining tasks comprised of 185 minefields, 128 cluster munition strikes, and 32 confrontation areas. UNMAS believes that all these tasks can be cleared within five years – given safe access and appropriate funding. Duk recorded by June 2019 a minefield contamination of 3 Hazardous Areas in an area of 927,626 Square meters and total casualties of 50 people (16 killed and 34 injured). Only 831 people were reached with EORE activities by that period. Similarly, 2 HAs were reported in Twic East at the same period with 11 casualties (2 killed and 9 injured). It has been realized that explosive hazards prohibit civilians from a safe and secure environment in which they can access life-saving services, collect water, firewood or other daily subsistence needs such as cultivation of land and/or fishing. This is more practical in deep field where explosive ordnance inhibit the ability of women, men, girls, and boys to meet their basic needs and access opportunities to survive. The risks of explosive ordnances are expected to heighten in flood affected counties of Duk and Twic East as water has submerged and removed local marking signs from the previously marked Hazardous Areas. Given the level of contamination in Terekeka, Duk and Twic East Counties compounded by flooding which has submerged previous marked HAs, there is need for enhancing the EORE to ensure that the at-risk population needs are met and are able to live in safe and secure environment. From the Feedback and complaint mechanism for CINA’s previous EORE projects, the IDPs have been found to be having limited knowledge of the effects of mines. With expected peace opportunity, IDPs and returnees are expected to return presumably unaware of threats and dangers of explosive hazards in these areas. As part of the ongoing EORE project in Jonglei (Duk and Pibor), community members were involved during the planning stage through the focused group discussions (FDGs) which were captured during the project planning phase. CINA’s 5 RE Teams who have been trained and accredited to implement the RE in different locations that is; Duk, Bor South, Twic East, Awerial, and Pibor Counties were recruited locally which also helps in terms of available responders in case further needs assessments are to be done in future with other inter-sectoral agencies. There will be a team who will also be recruited locally in Terekeka County to conduct similar EORE activities. All CINA RE teams will be able to help IDPs and the returnees who are less likely to have received EORE activities due to relocation or movement to look for safe area to settle in. From CINA previous projects, it was also learnt that there are other category of people who are at higher risk of unsafe behaviours including children who are too inquisitive and are attracted to colourful objects; former soldiers who feel they don’t need to be taught of explosive ordnances because the items were part of their past lives; children who are living without parental care (street children) who cannot be found in schools or at gatherings during EORE sessions; Men and boys whose roles involve guarding the community at night and shepherding the flock by day; women and girls who fetch firewood in the bush and search for water points. EORE is therefore part of their gateway to their safety to minimize eventual casualties.

2 - Activities

The activities of this project in Duk and Twic East Counties will involve the following activities:1. Conduct explosive ordnance risk education awareness raising sessions to sensitize 50,000 (17,000 men, 17,000 women, 8,000 boys, 8,000 girls) of the communities in Terekeka, Duk and Twic East Counties. EORE awareness sessions will be conducted in schools, community gatherings, distribution sites, church congregations, IDP Camps, registration points for returnees among other gathering places.2. Printing of Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials with key EORE messages.The project team will concentrate on giving useful information about how to prevent death or injury and what to do if they encounter UXOs/ERWs and how to report the cases. The IEC materials will be a mix of leaflets, brochures, calendars, t-shirts with key EORE messages, branded jackets/raincoats, etc for programme visibility. 3. Establish/strengthen Child-to-Child (CTC) approach to learning and safe behaviours in schools.The CTC approach links children's learning (in or out of schools) with their lives (home and community) so that knowledge translates into safe behaviour and action. One child or a group of children can spread knowledge to other children; teach skills to same-aged child/children; demonstrate by example to a family/families; or work together with the community. 4. Conduct inter-sectoral EORE awareness sessions for 45 (30 men, 15 women) representatives of other humanitarian agencies in the county, local Government and CINA staff to improve coordination in service delivery of EORE activities. Coordination of EORE activities and Referrals will also be feasible with other agencies like ICRC and Humanity Inclusion (HI) for further victim assistance (VA) to the beneficiaries who are eligible for additional services.5. Strengthen 150 (30 women,120 men) members of Disabled Persons Organization (DPOs) through training on communication skills, Leadership skills, Effective EORE messages and safe behavioural change.The members of the DPOs will undergo the process of identification, registration, verification and training. The training received will therefore help enhance coping mechanisms of the members of the DPOs especially the persons with disabilities (PWDs), women, people living with HIV/AIDS among other minority groups. The DPOs will also carry on the activities of behaviour change after project closure.6. Conduct a 3-days training to 150 teachers/PTAs/SMCs (30 women, 120 men) on EORE messages to impart the knowledge in school and their homes. The members of PTAs/SMCs/Board of Governance and teachers will be trained and will help spread EORE messages verbally to their students, at home and in the community, even after project closure. 7. Carry out monitoring visits to the project location by senior staff to monitor, assess and mentor the field RE teams. The senior team will travel on a quarterly basis from HQ in Juba to Terekeka, Duk and Twic East Counties to do training, monitoring, mentor and gather lessons learnt which will inform the next phase of project cycle. 8. Conduct 3-days training to 90 (45 boys, 45 girls) adolescents Peer Educators and Community Focal points (CFPs) (2 men, 2 women in each County) on EORE messages to support in awareness raising in the schools and community. The CFPs will also help in dealing with issues related to PSEA, Community feedback and complaints mechanisms in the community.9. Conduct a 5-day training for 12 RE Officers and 3 Team Leaders on the standard operating procedures (SOPs) of EORE, weekly reporting to IMSMA, reading of GPS Coordinates, and adherence to different Org policies such as Code of Conduct and will be accredited accordingly by NMAA.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

CINA RE teams will ensure that the project adheres to the standards of Do No Harm framework. During all the project phases, the RE teams will ensure that they seek to identify and elevate the needs and voices of the most vulnerable and marginalized members. During implementation, community members including women and girls will be encouraged to participate freely and will be allowed to identify specific protection risks that different groups face, identify underlying causes and emerging concerns, assess the local capacity for response and solicit solutions from within the community itself. CINA has put in place an effective and anonymous complaints and feedback mechanism to allow and encourage individuals to report instances of SEA easily, confidentially and safely. Joint complaint and feedback mechanisms for any other matter will also be available and clearly communicated to all beneficiaries prior to any activity and involvement. Project implementation will draw heavily upon beneficiary's experiences of security, safety and risks through consultation to identify key areas of EORE programming. The RE teams will continue to teach the community members on how to do local markings in places where explosive ordnance have been identified and encourage them to stay on the safe paths.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The RE Officers provide the awareness sessions and enlighten the beneficiaries of what information need to be given to them. EORE sessions will include all community members. The DPOs and the persons with disabilities will be key project stakeholders, both in the planning and during implementation. Information dissemination is done through EORE banners with messages educating the individuals about the warning signs, how to report suspicious objects and incidents. Feedback mechanisms in form of Q&A sessions will be conducted after every session. They will be encouraged to report through the existing Community Structures/Community Focal Points. The children will be empowered through the Child-to-Child approach where they pass the information they have gained to their friends, school mates and those in other gatherings at church or community. Through the intercluster working group meetings, the RE Officers will receive feedback from other agencies working in the same location on the information required by the community members in their areas of operations. All the information gathered will be used to prioritize on the next activities to improve on service delivery.There will be help desks at each school where children will be reporting their complain or feedback regarding the project. They will be encouraged to share any fears they have or any myths they have had about explosive ordnance. These concerns will be addressed and information shared with them to know the effects of explosive ordnance. Those who will be able to reach the RE Officers will also be able to give feedback during focused group discussion or during awareness sessions. The views of the beneficiaries will be part of the lessons learnt for the next project phase or for modifications on where plans could have not incorporated some of the contextualized ideas.
