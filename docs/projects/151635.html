Project Summary


This programme has been designed to improve the immediate food needs for those affected by floods, cyclones, drought, conflict and diseases, leading to acute food insecurity in South Central Somalia regions. This is aimed at supporting them to protect their livelihoods, enable local food production and adoption of resilient strategies to mitigate against the recurrent shocks. The project conception is premised on that the beneficiary targeting will be determined based on the needs, which shall majorly be determined by IPC levels, and mostly IDPs, with some host community. The interventions will focus on emergency response for acutely affected households, and these will benefit from unconditional and unrestricted cash transfer, with the amount of distribution being MEB-based per region of target. Transitional interventions will also be implemented targeting communities that need to restore their lost livelihoods through building of assets while participating in conditional and restricted cash transfers. These include TVET for the youth, fodder production by the destitute agro-pastoralists, training and support on LEGS; and agriculture input to the riverine farmers. Lastly, the project seeks to build resilience of the vulnerable communities and households through IGAs to diversify their livelihood sources. Additionally, governance on natural resource would not only be enhanced through this project plan, but also gain transformative capacity thereby reducing conflicts in the use and managing of resources. DRM and Early Warning Systems will also be established by the communities to enable their preparedness to shocks. DKH/Partners will mainstream protection in its programming ensuring that only deserving cases are selected to gain in the project. In addition, gender will be a major consideration through determination of intervention participants. The Target number of HHs are 3,700; projected to benefit 22,200 people (11,322Male, 10,878Female).
The project will have the following project objectives.
Objective 1: To improve immediate access to food by the most vulnerable households among the targeted populations in South-Central Somalia.
Objective 2: To protect and restore livelihoods, related to food and income sources to the drought and conflict-affected communities in South-Central Somalia.
Objective 3: To support resilience of vulnerable populations against shocks.

Needs

FSNAU’s Post Gu 2018 assessment estimates 4.6 million people are acutely food insecure (IPC phase 2 or worse), including 2.5 million children, continue to require humanitarian assistance. Of these, 1.5 million are in crisis or emergency. Communities that lost most of their livestock during the 2016-2017 drought and those affected by conflict, as well as this year’s devastating floods and cyclone, will take longer to recover. Immediate access to food for populations affected by floods, cyclones, drought, conflict and diseases to address acute food insecurity and support to flood- and drought-affected populations to protect their livelihoods, enable local food production and prevent adoption of “crisis” strategies are necessary. Livestock herd depletion and degradation of agricultural land have reduced traditional male livelihood activities, whilst forced displacement into IDP settlements has further limited opportunities for income generation. Internally displaced are the most vulnerable and in dire need of immediate and long-term assistance. UNHCR estimates the total number of internally displaced Somalis to be 2.6 million. REACH (2018) reports overall loss of livelihood and income that drought-induced displacement caused major protection concerns within households, with respondents reporting loss of livestock and other economic assets. However, the adverse impacts of the 2016-2017 severe drought are expected to persist, especially among people who became destitute and got displaced due to the drought and among pastoralists who lost most of their animals. Those facing acute food security Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse need urgent humanitarian assistance and livelihood support. Livelihood support is also required for people who are Stressed (IPC Phase 2). Between August and December 2018 and onwards, an estimated 294 200 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished, including 55 200 who are likely to be severely malnourished.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This programme contributes to both resilience and Durable solutions. The action has conditional cash-based interventions which aim at building community assets which assure resilience of the communities to shocks. The cash-based schemes will target those activities that seek to prevent disasters like floods through building dykes a long river banks, desilting, excavating water pans/ dams among other contextual responses. The project also aims at diversifying livelihood options for the affected households and this directly contributes to the beneficiary households’ resilience to recurrent shocks, affecting the traditional livelihood sources. The project seeks to build the capacities of the beneficiaries in various approaches to production. While these, like LEGS may appear emergency at the initial stages, they are linked to both recovery and long-term. DKH/Partners will ensure participation of all cadres of the affected communities in the project interventions. Collective decision-making will be at the centre of the implementation processes to ensure ownership, which is a precise for sustainability. Skills development embedded in the programme is also an important strategy aimed at ensuring that vulnerable households or communities become resilient, thereby leading to sustainability. The DRM/EWS mechanisms, will also strengthen the communities’ preparedness to disasters, in addition to governance of natural resources like land, river banks, sand resource, among others, and these will also improve livelihood strategies for the affected communities. These efforts lead to resilience, while at the same time contribute to durable solutions in the long run. These activities will be targeted at the communities where residents got displaced due to either flood or drought. Having in place strengthened communities resilience and enhanced opportunities for productivity will attract those in displacement to return to their places of origin and be part of the improved situation, which is a durable solution having IDPs to return.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The risks of exclusion based on displacement are:1. Inadequate access to services 2. Being a minority in a settlement 3. Risk of eviction4. Risk of not being employedDKH and Partners has an elaborate beneficiary selection criteria that will ensure that all the deserving IDPs are selected to receive the services that they will be providing through this project. Vulnerabilities brought about by being minority will clearly be taken into consideration during implementation. Employment opportunities provided by through the cash schemes will also be made open to every vulnerable groups in the settlement. On the other hand, DKH/ Partners are members of the Somalia Shelter/NFI, and CCCM clusters and will ensure that all the IDP residents access emergency shelter and NFIs at the time of needs, information on the services available in their places of residence, in addition to coordinating with other service providers to reach out to those deserving cases to be reached with services in the various camps. DKH/Partners will ensure protection is a key component in its programming approaches to enhance inclusion.Increased cases of evictions of IDPs has been experienced in parts of the country, especially those in major cities who settle in abandoned houses. DKH/Partners have been part of the process ensuring that the IDPs are given dignified treatment even during evictions through advocating for the IDPs to be allocated longer land tenure, provision of enough notice prior to eviction. In Baidoa and Gaalkacyo, municipal authorities have made efforts to allocate land for the relocation of IDPs at risk of eviction. DKH/ Partners will further make protection a central component of site design and development for all other service delivery facilities, as well as the overall camp planning to ensure that any form of exclusion is minimized.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The risks associated with Gender and age are;1. Gender-based Violence2. Early marriage for girls3. Risk of recruitment into extremist groups for boys4. Risks of school-drop-out for schools-going-age children5. Exclusion from services for the female- and child-headed families, and the oldThe DKH plan has been designed to ensure inclusion of all the qualifying vulnerable groups, budget allowing. While implementing all the interventions in this action, female and child-headed households and old people will be prioritized for unrestricted cash transfer, men and representatives of the needy households and can participate in cash-related employment activities will be considered. SME will only target women and these will majorly be SGBV survivors to allow enable them access income for enhanced dignity. As a member of the CCCM cluster, coordination to ensure all the vulnerable groups are protected will be ensured. DKH/Partner will advocate for proper planning of facilities like latrines and water points in the camp so that accessing them at night by women is not a risk. The interventions in this project will be implemented from a rights-based approach i.e. that the target populations in need have rights to food, and that they have the right to a standard of living based on the Sphere standards. LEGS, for example will ensure that this is in place during it all processes. Everyone will be given roles equitably. The protection mainstreaming in this project will also ensure that people’s dignity are either restored or upheld. There will be a CRM in place to gather feedback from beneficiaries on their experiences with the project interventions. This will also encourage greater participation of the beneficiaries.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Conflict and violence has taken a toll on the project plan target populations and the design of this project consider reduction and mitigation altogether on the effects of conflict and violence. The project aims to build the capacities of the affected communities on Natural resource management. This will entail training on management of land, water and pasture resources, which are majorly the source of conflicts in Somalia. Restoration of community assets and diversification of livelihood sources will also reduce conflicts emanating from completion on scarce resources. In collaboration with other stakeholders, DKH/Partner will link the communities with partners who would be able to provide peacebuilding and contextualized management; in addition to psychosocial counselling to those who have been devastated by armed violence and conflict. Women particularly, will be used in most instances to play their traditional role of peacebuilding among the Somalia community. DKH/Partner will also seek to work with the local authorities, including community and religious leaders with the aim of enhancing harmonious coexistence among the target communities. It is also believed that targeting the youth for training on vocational skills to be self-reliant economically will also reduce the risk of their recruitment into the extremist groups. DKH/Partner envision that from the design of the project and rights-based implementation approaches embedded into it, the impact of armed conflict and violence will be reduced.
