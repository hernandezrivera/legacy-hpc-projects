Project Summary

Severity of drought and flood disasters and prolonged conflicts have worsened the living conditions of the populations, particularly internally displaced persons (IDPs) and impoverished households in Banadir and Lower Shabelle regions of Somalia. Most of the children in IDP settlements in these regions are currently out of school. There are challenges including funding gaps and insufficient humanitarian responses in these areas. The learning resources are in short supply and number of teachers left teaching work because of a lack of salaries or incentives. Some displacement settlements have no schools, and/or if there is one, it is a private school that charges tuition fees on children, that IDP households and low income families cannot afford. Instead, many parents tend to send their children (girls and boys) to the market and streets to do hazardous jobs such as collecting Khat endings (garabo), cleaning houses and washing clothes for other families to contribute to earning a sustainable living for their poor families.
The proposed program complements Somalia Humanitarian Response Plan 2019. It is intended to contribute to the Education Cluster Strategic Objectives. This project will focus on four main components with the purpose of improving access to protective and life-saving quality education for 3,000 school children (1,400 girls and 1,600 boys) in Banadir (Daynile and Kaxda Districts) and Lower Shabelle (Afgoye and Wanlweyn Districts) regions of Somalia. The first component will concentrate on increasing the number of the marginalized, out of school children (boys and girls) enrolled and retained in constructed/rehabilitated schools with gender responsive latrines and safe drinking water taps. The second component will put emphasis on the establishment or the renovation of primary schools with appropriate gender responsive sanitation facilities for out of school children (1,400 girls and 1,600 boys). The third component will be aimed at providing life-saving school feeding rations, supplies and learning materials to 3,000 school children (1,400 girls and 1,600 boys). And the fourth component will intend to develop teachers’ professional competence and CECs capacity to improve the quality of teaching and learning in the schools, to increase awareness on protection of children’s rights and also to promote school management and supervision skills for 95 teachers (30 female and 65 male) and for 75 persons of community education committees (45 female and 30 male) at primary schools in the target districts.

Needs

The impacts of repeated droughts and floods together with long-running wars have deteriorated the living conditions of populations in Banadir and Lower Shabelle regions of Somalia. The FSNAU latest reports indicate that 1.5 million people in Somalia still facing acute food security crisis or worse outcomes and 294 000 children are acutely malnourished in the countrywide. These have contributed to inflict suffering on whole populations or segments thereof, youth and children from internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host community. 19% of 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia anchored in Banadir region (UNHCR September 2018). The IDP children encounter different barriers to education and protection such as lack of adequate learning, denial of their human rights to education, exploitation and more. Education Cluster partners in Banadir reported that 18,852 school children 40% girls in Daynile and Kahda are out of education due to fund gaps while 19848 school age children (10,321 girls) are unreached in the same area. FENPS assessment conducted September this year, indicates that the schooling of 4,047 IDP children (1,863 girls) was interrupted by the closure of 19 temporary schools (9 in Daynile, 6 in Kaxda, 3 in Afgoye and 1 Wanlaweyn) that had been hosting them for reasons related to lack of funding. The team noted from respondents in the visited IDP camps that many children, especially girls went into labour to help finding livelihoods for their families as there were no schools for them to attend. The team also noted that many families send their children to wet feeding centres, 3-4 kilometres far from their camps to get food. Only 7.4% of the children are enrolled in government schools with the remainder in some form of private or community schools. National survival rates to Grade 5 across Somalia are 64%, suggesting high drop-out rates. The gender ratio in schools is also uneven with 44% of the enrollment being girls (Source: FEDERAL GOV. SOMALIA, MOE ESSP 2018-2020).

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project prepares safe and secure environment for the affected community through consultation with both direct and indirect beneficiaries in the targeted areas. Schools, classrooms and learning materials will be prepared in accordance with appropriateness and adequacy of children’s acceptance and protection of their human rights. FENPS designed the initial phase of the project with specific needs of the planned beneficiaries (children, youth, women and men,). It will ensure that all interventions will contribute both technical and material resources that are life-saving and life sustaining but also applicable to the recovery and durability of the services provided to the crisis affected people after the end of the project. The target affected communities (children, youth, women and men,) will be engaged in regular monitoring and consultation within the life span of the project, and gender dis-aggregated data will be collected and analyzed to compare planned outcomes with the achievements. The project will address issues related to protection concerns through mobilization and raise the awareness of the affected population to actively participate in all the activities of the project. Empowerment and participation of affected men, women, youth, and children will be considered in the different phases of the project. FENPS will create exist strategy, and will train school administrators and teacher-parent associations/community education committees in alternative methods that they would be able to handle, manage and run schools with their own skills and resources. These include allocation time and technical support to enable integration and/or relocation children to government schools where they will get sustainable education. FENPS will also train Community Education Committees in funding raising skills that they will be able to look for educational facilities and materials to run schools after the end of the project. Furthermore, this project is aligned with the Somalia National Response Plan, and then FENPS will coordinate all phases of the project with the Ministry of Education to incorporate its achievements into the outcomes of Education Sector Strategic Plan 2018-2020. The program will give highest consideration to advocate and lobby for inclusion of the most vulnerable categories of community such as children with special needs, unaccompanied children and single parent families in the direct beneficiaries of the project.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Gender based violence, shortage of hygiene promotion supplies, and lack of appropriate child friendly supplies in schools are paramount problems that hinder access to quality education at IDP camps in Banadir and Lower Shabelle regions of Somalia. The internally displaced persons (IDPS) always occupy overcrowded area in or out of cities where human basic needs and essential services are in short supply. Moral support is necessary with an emphasis on children, women, the disabled, and other marginalized groups in displacement camps, and FENPS will conduct timely assessment with dis-aggregate data to study how target beneficiaries are exercising and participating in project activities. FENPS will organize community mobilization campaigns to increase enrollment and retention of emergency affected school children (1,400 girls and 1,600 boys) in life-saving and protective learning environment in where hygiene promotion supplies and learning and teaching materials are adequate to the gender and needs for crisis affected community in the project area. FENPS will also consult with community education committees (CECs) and parents to encourage girls join in schools, while recruitment and selection of female teachers will be motivated. Safety and health school norms will be created through incorporation extra curricula of hygiene and sanitation promotion activities risk mitigation and other cross-cutting issues to learning sessions for 3,000 school children (1,400 girls and 1,600 boys). FENPS will train 95 teachers (30 female and 65 male) 75 CEC members (45 female) in gender mainstreaming and Do no Harm approaches to improve their capacity and help them practice positive school cultures. In addition, FENPS will consult with district commissioners to pass legislation and code of conducts that safeguards the security of displaced populations through advocacy with a view to integrating them into the wider society and protecting their rights to survival, education, health care.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

There are many but varied challenges that internally displaced persons face in the IDP camps. These barriers include shortage of adequate learning opportunities, gender based violation practices and insufficient hygiene promotion supplies. FENPS should, therefore, enlighten the general public consistently to change the socio-cultural norms and practices that treat females and the aged as less important than men and the young so as to have a society that has its own highly developed culture and way of life, where all have equal rights and opportunities. On this basis, FENPS should strive to play a vital role in providing access to life saving and protective quality education, reducing or eliminating all forms of discrimination among project beneficiaries through awareness raising programs now and in the future.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

In order to prevent or at least reduce the impact of armed conflict and violence, FENPS will educate IDPs and host community ( to whom it’s delivering services) about the importance of peace to life and development. It also needs to predict the probabilities of conflict and natural disasters in order to get prepared for it and/or to equip the beneficiaries with skills that enable them to find alternative solutions for their problems. To minimize the negative impacts of conflicts and natural disasters, it would be necessary to give proper care and treatment to all victims, while giving consolation and material support to the families of the individuals affected by, with special consideration paid to the safety and security of the places where large numbers of people gather e.g. schools, hospitals and the like to protect the physical and mental well-being of the innocent civilians by making it sure that they are not in close proximity with Gas stations, army checkpoints etc and that these sites have good evacuation mechanism in place.
