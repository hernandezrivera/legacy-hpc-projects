Needs

According to the assessment done by Whole of Syria food security sector 6.5 million people are estimated to be food insecure in Syria and a further 2.5 million people is at risk of food insecurity. Those who are categorized to be food insecure are reporting large food consumption gaps, depleted coping strategy and large food expenditure share. Those who are considered at risk of food insecurity are the worst of in the moderately food secure category and may be falling into food insecurity if their livelihoods are not supported. The main drivers of food insecurity in Syria are a set of interrelated factors related to the deteriorating economic environments as well as food insecurity stemming from massive displacements. Almost 40 percent of the surveyed households reported to be an IDP or returnee of whom 74.5 percent reported to be displaced for more than 12 months. Other key drivers include loss of livelihoods and reduced production capacity limited physical and financial access to food, high prices and inflation, reduced purchasing power and continuous livelihood depletion of the most vulnerable populations. The humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2019 and multiple actions are needed to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, utilization and stability of food are adequately addressed through lifesaving, life sustaining, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions. Emergency response at the early onset of any sudden population movement as well as targeted food assistance and lifesaving emergency agricultural assistance to meet the nutritional and kilocalorie deficit of the most vulnerable population as per food security indicators is crucial across Syria1. 1Food security sector narratives_HNO-Version_FINAL_circulation

Implementation of activities

This proposed intervention aims to provide vouchers to buy food items during 2019. The project will assist 12,000 Female HH families including men, women, boys and girls who are settled in Idleb and Aleppo. for 3 months, verified vulnerable beneficiaries will get vouchers value $50 and with those vouchers they will be able to buy (with their own choice and preferable) food items sufficient for the whole month for a family of Six. With this project we aim to target the most vulnerable groups and families affected by the ongoing conflict in Syria and recent escalating violence in the north west of Syria. We aim to reach 72,000 individuals directly with our intervention. Beneficiaries will be selected based on the identified vulnerability criteria, which include priority for the following categories:- Families that have lost their breadwinner or whose main breadwinner suffers from disability.- Lactating and pregnant women.- People with special needs and chronic diseases.- Elderly people.- Families with more than 5 children and hosting other families.The beneficiaries will be identified in coordination with the local committees and local councils in the targeted locations and according to multiple criteria’s including vulnerability, accessibility, economic situations and distribution areas. In addition, all the data will be verified before the beneficiaries are selected. The project will be primarily implemented by Human Appeal (HA) and its field staff with clear and concise planning and agreements with the local authorities, and clusters. HA will rely on its team on the ground as sub-office of HA and an E-voucher company and its assistance to implement the project directly, this includes monitoring the project to ensure that the objectives of the project are achieved alongside with a PDM.

Protection Risk Analysis

HA staff will participate in protection working groups, and child protection and GBV sub-clusters to take lessons learned for mainstreaming of protection in also FSL projects. Measures to protect the safety and dignity of beneficiaries will be contextualized to the political and humanitarian situation. In order to reduce risks of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence, considerations will be made with respect to the location and design of FSL distribution points in order to make sure that there is no sexual exploitation occurs through the implementation process and this issue is going to be managed and monitored through the complaint mechanism and roles of the HA MEAL staff that will always be available over the targeted locations. Moreover, HA team will be coordinating closely with cluster in order to develop a special tool for monitoring the issue related to this topic. HA will focus on protection of beneficiaries especially women, and children from sexual abuse and harassment in distribution points, camps, and communities. In this regard monitoring and reporting tools will be developed and implemented and during the project cycle. Finally. HA will focus on enhanced participation of women in project design, implementation and monitoring of activities. They have also been designed to be culturally appropriate, inclusive, and confidential. In this project, HA will ensure the safe access to services for all genders and age groups at all times. This measure will protect beneficiaries. Likewise, by installing complaint mechanisms in the targeted locations, which will be checked by HA staff during the time of the project, we will allow any protection issues related to this project to be known and seek immediate advice from the senior project staff in Turkey for a response.

Delays in project implementation due to the Turkish restriction on transporting goods inside Idleb, Hama, and Aleppo - Delays in project implementation due to the interference of the armed groups in procurement/ imposing taxes /selection of the project locations. - Overlapping of project activities and double agreement by local councils as a result of a lack of coordination. - Syrian regime offensive onto Idleb governorate (airstrikes, a ground offensive, chemical attacks…) -
