Project Summary

Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) will implement these proposed activities in Wau, Jur River, Maridi, Mundri East, and Mundri West Counties of South Sudan. Vulnerable groups including female-headed households and people with disabilities (PWD), whose vulnerabilities have been aggravated by the ongoing conflict, flooding, and economic depreciation in South Sudan will be assisted by this project to have access to prevention, mitigation and response interventions to increase their ability to withstand the impact of shock and optimize nexus opportunities reaching 14,750 beneficiaries (2,475 boys, 2,375 girls, 4,950 males, 4,950 female).
The goal of this project is to achieve the three strategic objectives of the protection cluster (SO1) Vulnerable crises affected people have reduced morbidity and mortality through equitable and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs; (SO2) Vulnerable crises-affected people’s protection risks are mitigated as humanitarians uphold a commitment to the centrality of protection in humanitarian action informed by communities’ priorities; and (SO3) Vulnerable people’s capacity to withstand the impact of shock is increased, and nexus opportunities are optimized.
We shall achieve the objectives by ensuring that vulnerable people in the project locations are identified and appropriate services provided for them to attain social stability, economic self-sufficiency within their communities, and psychosocial well-being that allow them to cope. Creating awareness among community members and local authorities on the importance of civil documentation and for the community members to start the process of acquiring national identity cards. The community members will be made aware of the process and the local authorities to lobby for the registrars to visit the nearby county headquarters to eliminate barriers to accessing the civil documents as some community members are unable to travel to Juba for the process. This will help them to access financial services e.g. in banks and have livelihood security as they will be able to save money and borrow loans as well as register for other services like telephone communication.
We shall provide group and individualized psychosocial support (PSS) and recreational services: we shall target 500 girls, 500 boys, 1000 women, and 500 men according to their individual needs. We shall include recreational activities targeting adolescent girls, boys, and women to connect socially with each other and create a platform where peer-to-peer support will be strengthened. Provision of basic livelihood skills like bread making, basketry, and needlework will avail an opportunity where participants will create income to care for their families and build their resilience to economic hardships.
We shall strengthen our compliance with Core Humanitarian Standards through producing and disseminating information, education, and communication (IEC) materials to inform the community on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) to ensure that the communities we work with can access timely, relevant, appropriate and context‑specific information about expected and prohibited staff behavior and the organizations’ measures and systems that address SEA by aid workers. The IEC materials will increase community awareness of gender‑based violence (GBV) and promote collective responsibility among humanitarian service providers toward PSEA.
The interventions undertaken will be based on the needs of the beneficiaries and will be delivered promptly according to the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS). This project will also build the capacity of community-based protection structures to embed a sustainability component for the structures to be fully engaged in enhancing protection in their communities.

1. Needs

An intersectoral needs assessment published in October 2023 in Wau indicated that around 33.7 percent of the survey respondents reported that they were in need of and tried to access protection services. Around 19.7 percent of respondents were unsuccessful and 14 percent were successful in accessing protection services. Around 23.3 percent reported that protection services were unavailable, however, 47.5 percent reported that MHPSS services are available, in addition to services relating to rape treatment and treatment of physical injuries (37.6%), legal aid services (18%) and livelihoods services for women and girls (14.6%). An inter-agency report on flood assessment in Mundri East and Mundri West Counties published in September 2022 revealed that people who were displaced are particularly fearful of the presence of armed thieves or criminals in the region who are likely to loot their properties and possessions left behind when they evacuated their homes to seek further shelter from the flood. People with disabilities lost everything during the flood, making it very difficult for them to afford to meet their day-to-day needs. According to South-Sudan-Humanitarian-SitRep-April-2023 published by UNICEF, over 13,500 people were displaced from their homes following armed attacks by cattle keepers in Ibba County, Western Equatoria State. This project seeks to address the needs of the people in Wau, Jur River, Maridi, Mundri East, and Mundri West Counties by drawing attention to their fundamental needs and building local capacities to prevent conflicts, mitigate the impacts of conflicts, and promote a greater degree of social transformation.According to the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023, child protection and general protection needs have significantly increased since 2022. Safety and security, lack of freedom of movement, HLP issues, discontinuation of child protection services due to access constraints, and lack of funding are the main drivers of increased protection needs. This project will meet the needs of such beneficiaries by creating awareness of protection for the beneficiaries to seek services and conducting referrals to inter-sectoral services for vulnerable people in need. This project has an activity that will build the capacity of men and women in the community to tackle conflict and promote peace on themes like conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation, and peacebuilding. The training will boost confidence in conflict resolution and create a sense of community ownership of the peacebuilding process leading to sustainability. The trained beneficiaries will be able to make valuable contributions in addressing the root causes and drivers of such conflicts while involving socially marginalized groups such as people with disabilities, widows, and IDPs to make an active contribution to the process of reconciliation. The proposed interventions will deliver PSS targeting men, boys, girls, and women to promote recovery among those who experienced shocks. Such shocks experienced by vulnerable persons cannot be overlooked, and require to be addressed by psychosocial support to increase their resilience and create an integrated development approach to promote their psychosocial wellbeing.The 2023 HNO further explains that persons with specific needs, including the elderly, people with disabilities, or single-headed households are among the most vulnerable and at high risk of exploitation. This project shall provide individual protection assistance to persons with disabilities to increase their independence and participation in community activities.

2. Project activities

We shall implement the following activities under PC1 to ensure the mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services :1) conducting protection awareness-raising through interactive mediums like community public rallies, sports, cultural dances peer to peer sessions to reach out to the community on protection issues to reach 13500 people (2250 boys, 2250 girls,4500 men, and 4500 women) (indicator- # of women, men, girls, and boys with protection concerns or specific needs reached with protection awareness raising messaging).2)providing assistive devices e.g. crutches, eyeglasses, walking sticks, white canes, wheelchairs, and tricycles to 900 (150 boys, 150 girls, 300 male, 300 female) to increase their participation for them to access services and opportunities within their communities (indicator- # of women, men, girls, and boys reached with individual protection interventions intended for durable solutions (other than CP, HLP, and GBV) including legal aid, access to documentation, reintegration and other protection support that can also be cash-based).3)creating awareness among 250 (125 males, 125 female) community members on the importance of civil documentation for them to start the process of acquiring national identity cards. The community members will be made aware of the process and the local authorities to lobby for the registrars to visit the nearby county headquarters to eliminate barriers to accessing the civil documents as some community members are unable to travel to Juba for the process. (4) carrying out 60 key informant interviews (KI) at the Payam level to identify the trends in child marriage, access to humanitarian assistance, access to justice, family separation, sexual violence, restriction of movement, HLP issues, safety and security concerns, explosive hazards, and child labor. (indicator-# of KI Interviews, at Payam level, covered by the Protection Monitoring System).We shall achieve PC 3 by promoting nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance through implementing the following activities: (5) strengthening 15 existing community-based protection structures through training religious groups, child welfare committees, women and youth groups on community-based protection to respond to violations against children, women, and vulnerable people in the community for them to champion people’s rights, safety, and dignity (indicator- # of community-based protection structures established or enhanced).(6) Training 600 women and 600 men in conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation, and peacebuilding for the participants to understand their roles in the process where broken relationships are healed and mended (indicator # of community-based protection activities implemented including resilience and PSS activities, PSEA, peace-building activities, and training through AGD approaches).(7) providing group and individualized psychosocial support (PSS) and recreational services: we shall target 500 girls, 500 boys, 1000 women, and 500 men according to their individual needs.(8) Producing and disseminating 200 information, education, and communication (IEC) materials to inform the community on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) to ensure that the communities we work with can access timely, relevant, appropriate, and context-specific information.
