Project Name

Child Protection and GBV Prevention integrated response among 25,000 Vulnerable Spontaneous Refugee Returnees, IDPs and Host Communities in Wau, Raja & Jur River of WBeGs and Gogrial East, Gogrial West, Tonj North and Tonj East Counties of Warrap State

Start Date

01/03/2021

End Date

30/12/2021

Project Summary

Through this intervention, LCAD targets at least 25,000 (13,000 women, 6,000 girls, 3,000 men & 3,000 boys) refugee returnees with enhanced returnees’ access to territory with dignity and respect for their rights and enhanced protection and access to lifesaving GBV, Child Protection, Psycho Social Support and Trauma healing Services and support to at risk vulnerable returnee and returnee routes/host communities to sustain their capacity to cope with protection significant threats.
Through border monitoring, returnees will be able to access the territory with dignity and their rights are respected, returnees and host community empowered and outreach strengthened and assistance provided to persons with specific needs. There will be prevention of and response to GBV, protection of children from neglect, abuse and violence, Psycho Social Support and Trauma healing services and durable and humanitarian solutions made available to returnees for a period of 12 months.

1 - Needs

LCAD bears in mind existing international and regional refugee protection mechanisms. LCAD is intent on furthering prevailing mechanisms in regards to issues of refugee returnees such as the Global Compact on Refugees which acknowledges that local partners have a role to play in measuring the impact of hosting, protecting and assisting refugees, in order to assess gaps in international cooperation. LCAD is a member of a network that aspires to the realisation of the Global Compacts on Refugees and Migration as well as ending Statelessness by 2024 and has build robust capabilities in indentifying, analyzing and seeking durable solutions to these persons of concern. The violence that started in 2013 and crimes committed have affected almost all families and the price paid incredibly high with loss of lives, infrastructure, assets, physical psychological abuse and loss of livelihood. People lost their homes and have stayed in temporary camps (PoC and IDPs camps), fled across the borders etc for years. Displacements coupled with challenges in ensuring physical protection have made the life difficult for many. Violence, deprivation and suffering have negatively affected the mental health of this vulnerable population, especially women and children.LCAD is planning to benefit from its diverse expertise and experience to provide a comprehensive response. Experience of violence and trauma has put large part of the target population, especially women in extremely vulnerable positions. The process of healing from a conflict is long, and the first emergency needs include support to cope with experiences, and ways to build resilience moving forward. Both individual and community psycho-social support is needed in displaced, returning and remaining communities. This complex situation requires a multi-sector response, which can be met by the common response from the LCAD. Open borders and access to the territory by South Sudanese returnees’ faces challenges. Notably, capacity to handle movements efficiently and in line with international principles needs reinforcement and capacity to register returnees rapidly must be built. In this regard, protection monitoring, protection interventions and up-scaled outreach are needed. Needs of vulnerable returnees need to be addressed in the delivery of protection services and community empowerment activities. Risks for women and girls are exacerbated by overcrowding, community tensions, and the limited opportunities of many households to meet their needs. Some have resorted to survival sex and early marriage. Medical and other support services for survivors of GBV are lacking. The signing of the 2018 Peace Agreement has seen to increase to spontaneous refugee returns. Children separation from families, child labour, and exposure to physical and psychological violence and limited access to child protection services is rife among spontaneous returnees. LCAD targets at least 25,000 (13,000 women, 6,000 girls, 3,000 men & 3,000 boys) refugee returnees with enhanced returnees’ access to territory with dignity and respect for their rights and enhanced protection and access to lifesaving GBV, Psycho Social Support and Trauma healing Services and support to at risk vulnerable returnee and returnee routes/host communities to sustain their capacity to cope with protection significant threats.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

LCAD Project staff are required to attend PSEA training, Project has built-in clear process for receipt and referral of complaints of PSEA, in accordance with IASC guidance, Project staff will directly or indirectly engage in the HCT PSEA Network

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

LCAD considers the specific needs and vulnerabilities of boys and girls of varying ages, as well of men and women, recognizing their roles in the communities, while trying to empower those who tend to be left out of power. To ensure meaningful access and participation; all groups (including people with disabilities) will be identified through outreach (including home visits), awareness raising sessions in the community and engaged in all project set about feedback and complaints mechanism. Community-based complaints and feedback mechanisms will be established for all services, and participant satisfaction assessments using LCAD tools and through quarterly focus group discussions with women, men, girls and boys (including individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate) will regularly inform the project of what’s working and what needs improvement and to be further tailored.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

LCAD recognizes global and local complain mechanisms procedures and standards and as such a Feedback and complaints mechanism is in place as part of the M&E process, in the form of a “letter box” and email address. Response is provided regardless of whether corrective measures are feasible to be implemented. One awareness session every 6 months is to take place in order to ensure that beneficiaries understand the mechanism function. All information kits written in locally understood language and with illustrations will be provided to beneficiaries at the project inception and throughout the project implementation cycle to inform as well as solicit feedback. At LCAD, all project staff abide and sign a code of conduct document, and receive refreshment training once per year so to ensure principled assistance is always maintained

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

LCAD through quarterly focus group discussions, home visits, satisfaction surveys with women, men, girls and boys (including individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate) will regularly inform the project of what’s working and what needs improvement and to be further tailored. LCAD team members will every month analyse and reflect upon feedback and complaints received to ensure regular reflection on the issues raised, solutions proposed and who reported them, and as such help LCAD to address these problems, and feed into strategic decisions and help to the program as needed. All groups of beneficiaries will be consulted to review existing feedback mechanisms, to express their views and provide suggestions on how to make those mechanisms more accessible. LCAD proposes to leverage suggestion boxes, satisfaction surveys, focus groups discussions and routine home visits as feedback mechanisms. Upon collection and receipt of complaints, responses and feedbacks LCAD will process the feedback, responses and complaints in line with LCAD Operational Guidance on Accountability to Affected populations (AAP).This engagement will include community groups discussions and awareness raising sessions, with the appropriate measures including personal protective equipment (PPE) put in place to mitigate the spread of COVID-19 and respect current restrictions. In all of these activities, LCAD will ensure a diverse participation of community members including girls and boy, women and men, adolescents, the elderly and people with disabilities. Data confidentiality and data protection will be ensured through all phases of collection, transfer, storage and communication.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

This project will contribute to quality, timely and inclusive protection services by providing practical ways to engage with all members of the community, respond to their differing needs and draw on their capacities in the most non-discriminatory and effective way. It will incorporate all emergency preparedness, prevention, response, reconstruction, recovery efforts, monitoring, evaluation and reporting to build inclusive and resilient societies in South Sudan.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

This project will decrease proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5 by replenishing household assets. This can be done by providing inclusive access to safe water which will help women and young girls reduce their long walk in search of water hence leaving behind the elderly people and children and their vital socio-economic activities. Provision of assorted tools, seeds and agriculture extension services will improve community productive capacities and there will also be counseling services to GBV survivours to help uplift their self-esteems

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will inclusively adopt both gender-sensitive and gender-transformative approaches based on the findings of a comprehensive gender analysis completed by HAS through previous programs in the target areas. The project will leverage all aspects of community engagement to promote gender equity, ensuring inclusive community consultation, building skills and expertise, creating spaces for leadership and decision-making and engaging women, men, persons with disabilities and the elderly persons in dialogues that advocate for positive social change that promotes greater equity, opportunities and respect for all persons. The project will strengthen women and persons with disabilities and empowerment by actively supporting their participation in leadership positions through the project. For example, at least 60% of water source and sanitation committees and lead farmers trained by the project will be women and persons with disabilities must be included.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Nearly half of all deaths in children under 5 are attributable to malrnutrition. Malrnutrition puts children at greater risk of dying from common infections, increases the frequency and severity of such infections, and delays recovery. This project will strengthen the coping mechanisms of the beneficiaries hence enabling them to provide vitamin, mineral and micronutrient supplementation to their children under 5. Through community-based nutrition education, the beneficiaries will be able to delay cord clamping after birth, provide kangaroo mother care, initiate early breastfeeding, provide dietary diversity, fortify staple foods and support school feeding programmes
