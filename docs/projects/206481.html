Project Summary

Following the fighting in Sudan on the 15th of April, there has been a progressive increase of people entering South Sudan from several border points, however the border that has received the most people from the North is Renk. So, 353,175 entered South Sudan, 166,236 in Upper Nile and 94,095 in Renk as of (02/11/23 Data tracking Matrix (DTM). The groups of people arriving are refugees from the North, other nationalities, and returnees from South Sudanese. From these persons, some travel onwards to settle in other locations with support from IOM, however there are many people sheltering in various locations in Renk. While the population are said to be in transit to the Refugee Camps in Maban, Adjongtok, and Wedwil and many returnees are moving onward to Malakal, Wau, Aweil, Bentiu and Juba. The humanitarian situation at the point of entry has deteriorated due to limited services such as access to clean water, food, shelter, and medical services, particularly for lactating mothers, children, PWDs and older persons. Consequently, protection concerns have heightened, as the population are exposed to harsh living conditions, and are unable to transit to their destination’s choices due to depleted contingency cash others might had or those in general had nothing to survive on. Current response capacities are overwhelmed due to the continuous influx of the population, in the face of those already stranded in Renk. The identified response gaps include the need to facilitate safe transit of the most vulnerable groups, particularly vulnerable women headed families, Persons with Disabilities and older persons receiving no support from caregivers, along meeting their immediate food, transport, health, and protection needs. Unaccompanied minors have been noted, teenage girls and female hygiene has been reported in many assessments as gaps. Most people lack basic hygiene materials and the ability to access health care or purchase food. In addition, most of these areas are already at IPC level 4 of food insecurity especially in areas of Upper Nile which is driven by climatic shocks, insecurity, population displacements, crops deficits and limited access to basic services with the cumulative effects of years of assets depletion and eroding households coping capacities and loss of livelihoods, the affected population, including the elderly, disabled and large numbers of unaccompanied children estimated 75% of the IDP’s are women and children with no access to food or cash, they are traumatized and have lost most of their assets. They come from an already compromised food security and nutrition position. The markets in the area are, however, operational and food items are available. A lack of sanitation facilities is resulting in widespread open defecation with women reportedly walking long distances to relieve themselves.
Therefore, the proposed action proposes the following: Outcome 1: The project aims to provide multi-purpose cash to the target groups in order to meet their immediate lifesaving needs, with options for health care, onward transport, and any basic needs. This will target 500 vulnerable households (3,000 people), the elderly, disabled, at risk persons and women. A cash value of £100 (as per the cash working group) will be provided using a service provider, however if risks are high, a cash voucher will be considered. Outcome 2: The project will as well provide 6 pit latrines of 5 holes using temporary materials. In this instance corrugated iron sheets will be used and plastic slabs. This design is proposed to ensure privacy and safety for users. Hygiene awareness will be conducted through the 15 hygiene promoters identified and trained from both the displaced population and host community. IEC materials will be developed to disseminate health and hygiene information, feedback mechanisms and safeguarding and accountability messages. 1. Needs

Food insecurity in the proposed areas is driven by the climatic shocks, insecurity, population displacement, crops deficits and limited access to basic services with the cumulative effects of years of asset depletion eroding households coping capacities and loss of livelihoods. Most of these areas are categorized in IPC 4 levels of food insecurity. In addition, following the outbreak of conflicts in Sudan on 15th of April 2023, there has been a progressive increase of people entering in South Sudan from several borders’ points of the country, however, the border that has received the most people from the North is Renk. So, 353,175 entered South Sudan, 166,236 in Upper Nile and 94,095 in Renk as of (02/11/2023 Data tracking matrix (DTM). The groups of people arriving are refugees from the North, other nationalities, and returnees from South Sudanese. From these persons, some travel onwards to settle in other locations with support from IOM, however, there are many people sheltering in various locations in Renk. While the population are said to be in transit to the Refugees camps in Maban, Adjongtok, and Wedwil many Returnees are moving onward to Malakal, Wau, Aweil, Bentiu and Juba. The humanitarian situation at the point of entry has deteriorated due to limited services such as access to clean water, food, shelter, and medical services, particularly for lactating mothers, children, PWDs and older persons. CA supported rapid inter-agency assessment conducted in Aweil West and Aweil North counties revealed there is high humanitarian need for food assistance and agricultural tools, according to 65% of respondent, while 21% respondent revealed that lack of clean water, shelter, and non-food items (NFIs) and 14 % respondent also revealed additional needs for primary health care and nutritious food or food supplement for the children. These needs are similar to the consultation meeting conducted in other project areas such as Renk and Fashoda that forms the basis for the design of this project. Multi-purpose cash (MPC) assistance was recommended as an immediate measure, distribution of seeds and tools for the returnees/refugees and host communities, establishment of demonstration farms, formation of VSLA groups and training of lead farmers on agronomic practices, climate-smart agriculture

2. Project activities

1.Identification, registration, and verification. The selection and registration of the beneficiaries will be led by the project management committee that will be formed and trained for this project. The selection process will involve the chiefs as well as the project extension workers supported by the project officers. Registration data will be desegregated by gender, age, village, etc. the data will be shared with Christian Aid and a database is established.2.Distribution of seeds and tools. Seeds and tools will be distributed to 27,000 beneficiaries (5,400 per each county) registered and verified households. The distribution centers will be determined through the safety audit report and the agreed location by the stakeholders and the beneficiaries. Distribution centers will be within reach of all the beneficiaries.3.Training of lead farmers on GAP, CSA, IPM, and Post-harvest. 5400 lead farmers from the five counties (1080 per county) will be trained in good agronomic practices, climate-smart agriculture, and integrated pest management practices. The lead farmers will support other farmers in the villages as they will have extra skills. Each trained lead farmer is expected to support 3 households at critical planting time, weeding period, and harvesting.4.Establishment of Demonstration Gardens. 22 demonstration gardens will be established in five counties, and each demonstration garden will be composed of 25 members total (550 members in the five counties) who will work to manage the demonstration gardens. These demonstration gardens will be used for learning purposes by the project beneficiaries as well as the community members. They will be established in suitable and accessible community locations where the general public can view. 5.Formation and Training of VSLA Groups. 20 VSLA established, and 500 members trained in VSLA methodology: each group comprising 25 members. The groups will be supported with the VSLA saving Kit (passbook, ruler, calculator, pens, saving boxes, mates, stamps, stamp pads, Padlock, and counter books)
