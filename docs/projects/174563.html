Project Summary

Maternal Newborn and Child Health (MNCH) remains the priority health agenda in the country. The Pakistan Demographic and Health Survey (PDHS) 2017-18 indicates that Neonatal Mortality is 42 deaths per 1,000 live births, Infant Mortality is 62 deaths per 1,000 live births, and Under-5 Mortality is 74 deaths per 1,000 live births. Despite 34% reduction in under 5 mortality in the past three decades and gradual decline in infant (from 86 to 62 deaths per 1,000 live births) and neonatal mortality (55 deaths per 1,000 live births for two decades) declined to 42 deaths per 1,000 live births in the most recent 5-year period), the progress remained insufficient.
Eighty six percent of women who gave birth in the 5 years before the survey received antenatal care (ANC) from a skilled provider (a 13-percentage-point increase from 2012-13), 51% of women had at least four antenatal care visits. Only 60 % mothers and newborns received a postnatal care check within 2 days of delivery. The Maternal Mortality Ratio has come down from 276 (PDHS2006-7) to 184/ 10,000 live births (PMMS 2020).
Despite some improvement in maternal newborn and child health status, the COVID 19 pandemic is said to have negatively affected the progress made in the past years. COVID-19 pandemic is leading to compromised MNCH services and quality of care due to various factors including redeployment and task shifting of medical personnel to respond to COVID-19 and large numbers of health care workers themselves becoming infected due to PPE shortages. Health facilities are getting overwhelmed by large numbers of COVID-19 patients needing management and treatment leaving little or no room for the vulnerable women, newborns and children to access routine MNCH services. The COVID-19 pandemic is already having adverse effects on the supply chain for contraceptive commodities by disrupting the manufacture of key pharmaceutical components of contraceptive methods globally, and by delaying transportation of contraceptive commodities. In addition, equipment and staff involved in provision of sexual and reproductive health services was diverted to fulfill other needs, shortage of reproductive health commodities and breaking of regular supply chain during lockdown resulted in discontinuity of the SRH services and reluctance of people to go to health facilities for sexual and reproductive health services.
The outbreak of COVID-19 in Pakistan is expected to continue all throughout 2021 with periodic increase in incidence/spikes. This will put strain in the provision of health services to the disaster affected population
In Sindh the situation worsen with recent flooding (August September 2020). Based on the data collected , it is revealed that in flood affected areas, an overall of 145 (85%) health facilities (HF) are functional while 25 (15%) of the health facilities are nonfunctional. The nonfunctional facilities are more based in the rural settings, compared to urban. Approach to the nearest health facility and type of health facility is also very important in addressing immediate health needs in floods. As per RNA findings, in urban settings, private sector (35%) is the most reached out facility for the health seeking followed by DHQ/THQ (22%). The nearest approach in rural areas is Basic Health Units (36%) followed by the DHQ/THQ (20%). The provision of round the clock (24/7) services in the functional health facilities of the flood affected areas is 82 (57%), while 63 (43%) have services availability only at morning times. Reproductive Maternal and Neonatal Child Health (RMNCH) services for pregnant women have also been affected in various districts, while 57% of the pregnant women still have access to the health facilities the remaining 43% do not have the 24/7 access.
Save the Children International did rapid assessment and team visited flood-affected areas in Badin. Badin has a population of 2 million; the 2020 floods affected 242,460 people (appr 50,000 families)

1- Project Activities

1. Targeted rehabilitation of selected health facilities in flood affected areas in Badin With initial coordination with provincial and district health department and PPHI, Save the Children will do health facility assessment to identify the facilities to intervene in and also assess the signal functions for EmONC services in the targeted health facilities. SCI intends to do minor repair and renovation in 10 selected health facilities with focus in improving the WASH and Infection Prevention. The interventions will cater in “Inclusion”, focusing the needs of special people (design of BOQs etc.). 2. Strengthening the EmONC services To strengthen the EmONC services, SCI will procure and deliver EmONC equipment and supplies and medicines to improve institutional delivers in the flood affected areas catering in the infection prevention aspect with regard to COVID-19 pandemic as well in 10 selected health facilities. 3. Training of facility and community based healthcare providers In order to improve the quality of services, training need assessment for community and facility based healthcare providers will be done in consultation with district health office. It will be followed by training of doctors, LHVs and community midwives (identified in consultation with district health office) on Basic EmONC and Infection Prevention. Meanwhile, 200 lady Health Workers/Community Health Workers will be trained on Community Case Management of Common Child Hood Illnesses including Diarrhea and Pneumonia. The facility staff will also be oriented and encouraged for efficient reporting on DHIS.SCI will assess the gap in DHIS and provide necessary assistance in terms of stationary and IT equipment required in this regard. Similarly the LHW-MIS system will be strengthened through bridging the capacity gap in this regard in the targeted area.Guidelines and protocols will be printed and displayed in the facilities to improve EmONC services. Similarly handouts will be printed for the lady health workers to share with women and girls. 4. Support activities of community mobilizers/LHWs Lady Health workers are the backbone of community outreach activities. They will be supported to conduct regular support group sessions in the community with women and girls raising awareness on MNCH and FP. Through targeted activities, the lady health workers will be oriented on screening and timely referral of cases to the health facilities. Meanwhile the referral systems will also be strengthened from first care level to the next level and beyond. The LHWs will also be oriented on infection prevention measures related to COVID-19 infection and through them the communities will be oriented in this regard. In this connection IEC material will be developed on COVID-19 infection prevention and printed to be used at community and facility level. 5. Monitoring and OversightStrong monitoring and supportive supervision in coordination with district health official will be ensured in the implementation area in Badin District. Meanwhile coordination with key government official at provincial and district level (from line departments) and implementing partners in the target district will also be carried out. Save the Children has a strong in house capacity for Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) that will be utilized to ensure the quality of data (age and sex desegregated) services in the intervention area. At least two case-studies will be developed in the project life to showcase the impact of interventions.
