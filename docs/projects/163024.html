Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in the North West/South West Regions of Cameroon started in November 2016 and gradually escalated into armed conflicts between the separatists’ fighters and government military forces in 2017. The situation of conflict-affected children in the South-West Region has worsened such that villages, schools and health facilities have constantly been under attack. 9 in 10 children have been out of school for three years (UNICEF, 03 Dec. 2019) and internally displaced people are facing high poverty levels with few prospects for short-term return.
Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) are critical for survival by the people in need especially children – who are more susceptible to illness and death from diseases that are often caused by lack of sanitation, inadequate safe water supplies and poor hygiene. The Environmental Protection and Development Association (EPDA) in collaboration with its local grassroots partners – Youth Development Centre (YDC) and Sustainable Development Initiatives Group Sustainable Development Initiatives Group (SUDEVIN) will work to ensure, protect and restore the right of Internally Displace Persons (IDPs) especially children, women, disabled and elderly to WASH as well as improve resilience of host communities to WASH services.
WASH interventions will include increasing access to safe water, facilitating proper sanitation through construction of sanitary toilets, hygiene promotion activities, WASH kits and waste management. EPDA and partners will be prioritizing IDPs in critically water deficient areas, in communities with high waste management issues as the ones with highest needs in the Fako, Meme and Ndian Divisions. In these selected areas and those to follow, EPDA and partners will ensure a protection risk analysis and mitigation measures have been put in place. Through the life saving WASH activities, EPDA will contribute to mitigate the risk of cholera outbreak but also ensure capacity to respond to the control of the already cholera outbreak in part of Ndian Division (official declaration by MINSANTE, 26th of November 2019). IDPs in some communities of the Fako, Meme and Ndian Divisions are in high need of WASH interventions. Protection interventions will be mainstreamed through the programme with WASH interventions designed including training of gender inclusive WASH committees encompassing women, youth and men. All will be trained in construction, maintenance, repair and management of the WASH facilities. Men, boys and youth will be involved in order to mitigate conflict around WASH facilities to actively reduce SGBV and in coordination with other humanitarian actors.
EPDA and partners will prioritize 1) improving sustainable access to basic sanitation and safe drinking water to vulnerable IDPs; 2) reducing the risk of poor hygiene-related morbidity and mortality of affected population in the crisis; 3) reinforcing capacities of host communities to improve WASH service and resilience of affected populations.
Our response will emphasize local systems strengthening, and linking humanitarian action, development and contextualised peacebuilding. Life-saving gender-sensitive services will be undertaken for displaced and host community IDPs to mitigate acute needs and enhance resilience. Gender-based violence will be addressed through preventive interventions and inter-agency referral mechanisms. WASH response will also focus in schools, health centres, etc, while reinforcing implementing partner capacities. Rapid Response Mechanism approaches will be expanded with the local partners. Security risk management capacities will be strengthened along with integrating post intervention monitoring to improve accountability and response quality.

Besoins / Needs

All WASH facilities will be designed taking into account disposal of used menstrual hygiene products, accessible to children and the physically disabled, sex disaggregated, and clearly marked. They will be locale from the inside and in the case of local communities will be provided with enough lighting to enable use especially at night. Staff of EPDA working on the project, WASH committee members and the general community will be trained on and informed about available referral pathways for confidential handling of SGBV cases, in coordination with the GBV sub cluster and other actors. The context in the South West especially Fako, Meme and Ndian (Bakassi and buffers) Divisions have shown to be unstable and requires EPDA/YDC/SUDEVIN to maintain flexibility to be able to support the most vulnerable population. For now at the priority is to ensure assistance to 70,000 people (approximately 32,000 at Fako, 30,000 at Ndian and 8,000 in Meme). As the situation is fluid and might changes rapidly, the project will remain flexible to reach the most vulnerable populations.
