Project Summary

This intervention will ensure access to basic education services to 10,000 most vulnerable IDPS learners ( 5,000 boys, 5,000 girls) across deep fields of Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe through awareness campaigns, teaching and learning material distribution, teachers capacity development and education system strengthening.

1 - Needs

The protracted crisis in the North East Nigeria has caused unprecedented consequences on men, women, boys and girls. The insurgency has led to waves of massive displacement with correlative disruption of livelihoods, WASH facilities as well as education infrastructure. This situation has negatively impacted learning of marginalized crisis-affected boys and girls specially children with disabilities with significant drop out from schools. According to Nigeria HRP 2022, some 8.4 million people in the North East states of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY States) will need humanitarian aid in 2022, only slightly fewer than a year ago. To this regards, 2.2 million are internally displaced persons, 1.5 million are returnees who lack essential services and livelihoods and 3.9 million are members of communities affected by their hosting of IDPs. The impact of the crisis on the education system has been severe. Since 2019, 611 teachers have been killed,19,000 teachers displaced, 910 schools damaged or destroyed, and more than 1,500 schools forced to close. As a result, an estimated 900,000 children have lost access to learning while 75 percent children in camps do not attend school. Deeply ingrained patriarchal values and cultural norms in NE Nigeria further compound the marginalization of girls and women. 70 per cent of girls of primary school age are out of school in Borno State - the highest percentage in the country (Education sector Multi-years needs assessment). Furthermore, the sand storm and recent flooding (June) also destroyed both schools in host communities, camps and schools. The rapid need assessment conducted by African Humanitarian Aid international (AHAI) in eight (8) schools and 50 households of Monguno, Pulka, Yola south, Madagali, Mubi North and Michika targeting parents/ caretakers of undeserved, marginalized children and children with disabilities. 40% female respondents reported not having resources to pay school levies (uniform, text books biros etc.), 40% adolescent girls declared using rags during menses. 5% declared using reusable sanitary pads, 70% of parents/ caretakers of children with disabilities reported limited access to specialized learning materials, 65% of assessed schools reported not having inclusive classrooms for children with disabilities; 75% assessed schools reported not having specialized teachers, 60% reported not having teaching materials for children with disabilities. 55% of out of schools adolescent girls and boys reported limited access to employability skills. The literature review carried by AHAI touching schools plans and polices reported that, teaching curriculum does capture inclusive needs and specialized response for children with disabilities. The Nigerian Government has been complementing the efforts of UNICEF, National and International partners to meet education needs of crisis-affected, marginalized learners including children with disabilities. However, the wave of Covid-19 and the crisis in Ukraine have impacted the funding flow so as to extensively meet the dire needs of beneficiaries. Despite the efforts of traditional humanitarian assistance structural and systemic educational barriers in schools' plans and policies have been retarding access to education for marginalized crisis-affected boys and girls of host communities including children with disabilities.

2 - Activities

through this program AHA and the implementing partners will ensure that learners have access to :- distribution of learning materials to 10,000 boys and girls ( text book, biros etc..) to ensure access to learning- distribution of teaching materials to teachers ( females and males) ( text books, registrar etc..) to ensure delivery of quality education. moreover, - teachers shall be provided with a capacity development package ( PSS, TARL, PSEA etc.) so as to improve teaching techniques and strengthen protection of learners in a safe and protective learning environment. Furthermore, education stakeholders' capacities shall be built in school plans and schools safety plans for potential emergencies.
