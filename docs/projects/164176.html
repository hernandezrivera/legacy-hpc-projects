Project Summary


This project will reduce Child Protection concerns including violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation by improving the access of at-risk girls and boys to responsive and preventive services. Those services include the provision of structured and non-structured psychosocial support activities, awareness-raising, parenting skills sessions, case management, and individual support, especially for children living with disabilities, through static child-friendly spaces (stand-alone or within learning spaces) or mobile units. In the same way, this project aims to enhance local capacity to provide Child Protection services through building the capacity of Child Protection teams and local community members on Child Protection themes. In addition, the project includes the establishment and support of local Child Protection networks and building their capacity on Child Protection.
The project targets about 41,100 affected girls, boys, women and men (including 18500 girls, 18500 boys, 2050 women and 2050 men) in 8 districts in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates through Child Protection services. Syria Relief SR will provide a secure and supportive environment. In addition, SR will build on existing structures and capacities within the local communities. The safe spaces will be within or next to the learning spaces and the mobile units will enable SR Teams to have flexibility in responding to newly IDPs wherever they reside. Syria Relief CP teams will deliver the Child Protection services directly and will receive technical, logistic and financial support from both SR field offices in Idleb and Aleppo in addition to SR Turkey Office.
This project will be implemented in eight sub-districts with oversight from the Child Protection field coordinator based in SR Kelly Office. The field staff will support the MEAL team who will be responsible for monitoring and accountability. A MEAL Plan will be developed by the Program and MEAL staff on the inception of this project, and before commencing the implementation to ensure that MEAL activities are well incorporated into the work schedule. SR’s MEAL will lead to the development of standard MEAL tools and approaches to be used during the implementation. Data quality is assured by triangulation of multiple quantitative and qualitative sources and use of electronic data collection software to minimize errors and allow for real-time data quality checks. Monitoring and evaluation tools include attendance sheets, registration forms, pre and post-SDQs, satisfaction surveys, and anonymized case trackers. On the other hand, SR will ensure the community engagement in monitoring the activities by setting complaint and suggestion boxes in each safe space and mobile unit and will circulate a WhatsApp number, Skype and email accounts to get the direct feedback from them in a transparent way; those complaints and suggestions will be addressed from the SR Turkey office.
As the tradition of most targeted communities does not allow men and women to sit together, SR will take that into consideration while delivering awareness-raising and parenting skills sessions. PSS sessions might also be delivered to girls and boys aged more than 10 years separately, for the same reason. Additionally, SR will hire gender-balanced teams to ensure equity of access to project services.
The Project mainly targets the younger age-group of children aged 6-11 as the adopted PSS curriculum suits this group. Besides, there will be awareness-raising sessions on Child Protection that will target adolescents and youth; SR will consult the adolescents and their parents while choosing the awareness topics to ensure the topics are culturally accepted.

4 - Needs

The prolonged conflict in Syria significantly influenced the smallest unit of community in Syria, leading to normalizing negative coping mechanisms affecting the lives of children. Child rights continued to be violated, where thousands of children were killed and injured due to the increasing level of violence. According to Syria HNO, 2020 draft; child labour, family separation, child marriage, child recruitment, and Psychosocial distress were the most reported CP concerns, jeopardising the lives of boys and girls. Besides, HNO 2020 highlighted the difficulties children living with disabilities and children who do not have birth certificates face in access to their rights and services. According to a rapid need assessment conducted by SR in November 2019, there is a continuing need to provide child protection services, where there are thousands of boys and girls, living at risk of different types of protection concerns, including lack/loss of personal documents, child labour, early marriage domestic violence, harassment, and the lack of psychosocial support services. Moreover, SR Case Workers in the current SR Child Protection Program identified tens of most vulnerable children including children who have got physical disabilities that have been preventing them to access different CP services. Overall, those risks affected the safety and well-being of girls, boys, women, and men in all areas of their lives from their homes to the wider community. Syria Relief proposes to provide CP services based on identified needs, gaps in the proposed sub-districts of Idleb, Jisr-Ash- Shugur, Harim, Badama, Darkosh, Janudiyeh, Dana, Salqin, Kafr Takharim, Armanaz, Maaret Tamsrin, Jebel Saman, A'zaz, Al-Bab, Jarablus, Afrin, Aghtrin, Mare', Ar-Ra'ee, Daret Azza, Atareb, Bulbul, Raju, Ma'btali, Sheikh El-Hadid, Jandairis, Sharan in Aleppo and Idleb governorates to reduce CP concerns, through continuous support to 105 safe spaces within/next to 105 learning

5 - Implementation of activities

Syria Relief will provide support to safe spaces within schools/TLSs and static child-friendly spaces which will carry out structured, non-focused, interactive and supervisedpsycho-social activities for children from 6 to 14 years old. Those activities will be conducted directly by trained Syria Relief PSS workers. SR will rehabilitate those SafeSpaces and CFSs in the first implementation period. SR will make sure those spaces are accessible by children living with disabilities. The activities will include the provision of “I Deal” curriculum for Psycho-social Support activities and life skills, play and recreational activities for children; awareness-raising on preventing violence and separation; how to recognize and avoid unexploded ordinance; and non-formal education for children (ensuring that CFSs are not competing for the learning spaces that provide formal education). Syria Relief will be utilizing the teams operating the child-friendly spaces, to provide community awareness-raising sessions for parents, community members, etc., on child protection related concerns including but not limited to child labour, early marriage, school dropout, lack of personal documentation, domestic violence, and risks of explosive remnants of war. During those awareness-raising sessions, Syria Relief's team will be discussing the reasons for these concerns, results and the prevention and response to these concerns. Syria Relief will provide parents/caregivers with parenting sessions dedicated to providing them with the skills they need to interact with their children, communication skills, problem-solving and decision making, non-violence discipline strategies, psychosocial support skills, children’s rights, children’s concerns, and positive parenting. Moreover, Syria Relief will support establishing child protection community committees/ networks. The community committees will inform members about available services and enabling access by liaising with relevant actions. Addit

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Girls, boys, women and men’s physical security is put at risk as a result of the violence associated armed parties; a lack of consideration for the safety and security to access, and lack of consideration of contextual information (e.g. aerial bombardment) and targeting of civilian infrastructure during and directly after service provision.Mitigation 1: Consider conflict patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions for establishing fixed-site service provision. Ensure safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (route and location) by boys, girls, women and men, elderly and populations with decreased mobility (due to physical, visual/hearing deficits, and psychological issues). Engage with local authorities on safety and security in the community. Risk 2: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services orinformation is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups.Mitigation 2: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what servicesare available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and serviceproviders for Code of Conduct and PSEA

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: the security situation is stable and helps to deliver the project services. Risks: Security situation deteriorates (airstrikes, shelling, inter-fighting between NSAGs) and control over the area of operation changes which might cause a delay in implementing the activities: Mitigation: SR may suspend some activities till the situation is stable. The staff is trained in and adhere to security protocols. SR employs program and monitoring staff from the area so they can move more freely. Strong community support will be maintained. If the control changes, Syria Relief will negotiate access for other areas. Considering the need of CP services for children across northern Syria, the service provider can be easily targeted to another location and SR may move to CP mobile units instead of static CFSs. All Syria Relief’s offices and field personnel implementing the activities have emergency contacts and receive updates on the security situation. SR informs donors and partners of relevant activities and security incidents. Risk: Staff and beneficiaries might be attacked or injured causing attrition or limits to the implementation of activities. Mitigation: All the activities that Syria Relief implements are based on the skills and competencies of the field staff inside Syria along with strengthened support from Senior staff based in Turkey. Access: Syria Relief maintains a good relationship with the communities it operates in allowing access and movement of staff from and to project location easily. Coordination: Syria Relief coordinates its project activities with NGOs, through UN-led clusters as well as through direct coordination with NGOs and local authorities. Risk: risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of case management if service providers are not adequately trained. Caseworkers will be trained on communication skills, CP SOPs related to case management, and PFA
