Project Summary

The FSNMS Round 28 and 2022 SMART survey results, estimated that a total of 2,175,546 people (1,469,919 in the host community, 315,503 returnees, and 390,125 IDPs), would suffer from acute malnutrition (AM) in SS in 2023. This figure includes a total of 1,437,735 children <5 years which 353,606 SAM; 1,084,129 MAM, and 737,812 AM-PLW). More children and pregnant and lactating women in SS suffered from acute malnutrition in 2023 compared to 2022 (increasing by 158, 201). Additionally, 26,555 (7.9%) out of a total of 337,120 refugees in 11 counties will need treatment for acute malnutrition, including 5,139 SAM children, 14,347 MAM children, 4,337 AM-PLW, and 2,731 patients with HIV or TB. In 2023, two of the WV’s 3 targeted states (Warrap and Upper Nile) and 8 out of 9 targeted counties have Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15%.
This project will implement lifesaving nutrition interventions across 3 states and 6 counties namely Warrap (Twic, Tonj North), Upper Nile (Renk, Melut, Fashoda) and Western Equatoria (Tambura); using CMAM and MICYN approaches and will be integrated into the ongoing health, WASH, protection and FSL activities in the above locations. WV will prioritize life-saving treatment services targeting children under five years of age, PLW, and chronically ill persons as the most vulnerable groups to acute malnutrition due to their increased biological and physiological needs. Prevention activities that address the immediate and underlying causes of malnutrition will be enhanced. Additionally, the response will integrate nutrition with other clusters to address key drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity, sub-optional childcare and feeding practices, morbidity, and lack of safe water and sanitation. Specific target locations will be within WV program areas where high prevalence of acute malnutrition, conflict and displacement co-exist. The project will enable WV to contribute to the nutrition cluster’s target of reaching 1.8 million children and women with emergency nutrition support and collaborate with food and livelihood, health, protection, WASH and Education clusters to provide multi-sectoral interventions. WV will be implementing the project in line with the 2024 cluster objectives.
Project implementation will be done through existing government seconded staff; and WV will Integrate/link all the interventions to the existing government health facilities for sustainability, WV will also ensure that reporting is done through the government systems that include NIS and DHIS2; and will make use of government data collection and reporting tools for sustainability purposes. In order to contribute to wasting reduction and prevention of malnutrition, WV will establish kitchen gardens in the community; Expand household food production (cassava, sweet potatoes, beans etc). WV will engage its FSL team to explore economic empowerment and transformation through a Savings for Transformation (S4T) model.
The HRP 2023 revealed that up to 866,340 persons will be in need of humanitarian in the 9 locations that WV has targeted for this response which include 2 (Twic, Tonj North) in Warrap, 1 in Western Equatoria (Tambura) and 3 in Upper Nile (Renk, Melut, Fashoda). Of those, the nutrition cluster aims at reaching 506, 031 (249,492 residents, 123,335 IDPs and 133,203 returnees). WV already has established static health facilities in Kodok, Melut and Twic and mobile clinics in Tambura to support the IDPs, returnees and will continue to contribute to reaching the cluster targets by implementing the integrated health, nutrition, WASH, Food Security and Livelihoods projects across the 9 target locations selected for this project because the affected populations have continued to suffer.

Children and PLW in SSD suffered from Acute Malnutrition in 2023 compared to 2022 (increased by 158, 201). 26,555 (7.9%) of 337,120 refugees residing in 11 counties in SSD will need treatment for AM, including 5,139 SAM children, 14,347 MAM children, 4,337 AM-PLW, and 2,731 patients with HIV or TB. 8 out of 9 targeted counties have (GAM) prevalence above the emergency threshold of 15% high AM level. According to HRP 2023 - 866,340 people will be in need of humanitarian assistance in the 6 WV target locations for this response is (Twic, Tonj North) in Warrap, (Tambura) WES and Upper Nile (Renk, Melut, Fashoda). The nutrition cluster aims at reaching 506, 031(249,492 residents, 123,335 IDPs and 133,203 returnees. WV already has established static health facilities in Kodok, Melut and Twic and mobile clinics in Tambura to support the IDPs, returnees and will continue to contribute to reaching the cluster targets by implementing the multi-sectoral projects in the 9 target areas for to assist affected people as they continue to suffer. In response to the Sudan Crisis WV is providing health, WASH, and protection services. By the end of Sep-2023, WV had reached 7,053 children without patient curative consultations. Children were screened for malnutrition, 143 referred to Renk OTP/TSFP for treatment. WV was called upon by MoH to integrate nutrition in the ongoing health clinic response at the Transit Camp in Renk and IDP camp in Melut. UNCHR/IOM and CCM have allocated WV new site where a facility is going to be constructed in a bid to provide complete service package (BPHNS). Under SSHF WV conducted outreaches in Tambura to serve peoples that were affected by the 2022 crisis that displaced over 80,000 people. Most have returned to their homes; a great number have settled within a 15km radius from the nearest health facility. The same locations (Yukusufu, Bavuru, Basanabaru, Kpotonoyo with total 6,506). WV provided mobile nutrition services to the affected communities by the conflict and the returnees have settled. WASH, protection, nutrition (MIYCN) services are provided by WV through other sources such as HPF, Global Affairs Canada, BHA and the Government of Ireland. WV's SSHF funded project recently ended (September 2023) on the backdrop of a swelling number of IDPs. WV manage to strengthen and contain all the nutrition activities in 10 IDP sites in Twic County, also providing treatment to all IDP children aged 6-59months, PLW-2,364 SAM cases without medical complications, 10,448 MAM cases treated, and 9,882 MAM cases of pregnant and lactating mothers identified, screened, admitted and treated across the 10 IDP nutrition sites. 6 OTP/TSFP shelters were constructed for service delivery. UNICEF, WFP continued to provide supplies for management of AM. A CNV network of 120 people and 10M2MSGs was set-up and trained. There is a need for continued support due to the growing needs of the IDPs, returnees and the affected community. This project will respond to the displaced communities, implementation will be complemented by existing nutrition, health and WASH project under WFP/UNICEF. Although 10 OTP sites are operational, there still remains some gaps that include: Lack of latrines, staff of shortage, transport funds for supplies, M2MSGs supplies in kind, CNV incentives, training given the high attrition of CNWs and seeds for vegetable gardening.

2. Project activities

To enable sustainability, WV will implement project through existing government seconded staff; Integrate/link all the interventions to the existing government health facilities; Strengthen the already established community systems such as Mother to Mother Support groups; Facilitate joint implementation with the county health department in terms of training, support supervision and quarterly review meetings; CHD capacity will be enhanced to continue implementation; Ensure that reporting is done through the government systems that include NIS and DHIS2; and Use of government data collection and reporting tools for sustainability purposes. To curb sales of nutrition supplies and equipment WV will facilitate continuous training, Work with law enforcers to routinely inspect markets, routine support and spot checks; efficient stock management; periodic CNWs rotations across sites. In line with the 2024 cluster objectives WV will implement the following activities:CO1; WV will: i) provide nutrition counselling to primary caregivers of children aged 0-23 months who received Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition counselling ii) Conduct cooking demonstration sessions at both the facility and community levels iii) Support Vit A supplementation to girls and boys aged 6-59 months during semester 1 & 2 iv) and Support deworming using albendazole for children aged 12-59 months in semester 1 &2, v) Support children 6-23 months benefiting from BSFP and PLW benefitting from BSFP. CO2: WV will i) Provide nutrition screening for acute malnutrition to boys and girls aged 6-59 months through CNVs in the community and CNWs at the nutrition site ii) Admit and provide SAM treatment to children aged 6-59 SAM who are identified as having malnutrition iii) admit boys and girls aged 6-59 months with MAM for treatment at health and nutrition sites. iv) Screen pregnant and lactating women for acute malnutrition v) Provide treatment to pregnant and lactating women who are identified as having acute malnutrition vi) Conduct programmatic visits to nutrition sites and reports submitted by members nutrition manager/coordinator from County Office vi) provide on job training and mentorship to nutrition staff at the health facility. vi) CNVs reporting on monthly basis VII) establish community feedback mechanisms at the nutrition sites. CO3: WV will: i) Conduct MYICN Barrier Analysis ii) Disseminate quarterly nutrition data dashboards at state level iii) Testing of girls and boys admitted with SAM at the nutrition site for malaria. CO4: To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2024: WV will i) Distribute LLITN to girls and boys admitted with acute malnutrition in nutrition sites to prevent malaria ii) Distribute LLITN to PLW admitted with acute malnutrition in nutrition sites for malaria prevention iii) WV will provide WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites, sensitize households on the correct use of aqua tabs through the CNVs, sensitize communities in the priority counties with hygiene and sanitation messages, iv) Provide safe and clean water to the target counties through rehabilitation of water points v) Establish kitchen demonstration gardens at the nutrition sites vi) Establish vegetable kitchen gardens in the community vii) integrate child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM ix) Train frontline nutrition staff trained on GBV basics, including how to safely and appropriately respond to GBV disclosures x) Conduct screening and or establish a referral mechanism for diarrhea treatment. xi) collaborate with Health in the Management of diarrhea in the health & nutrition sites and provide Zinc and ORS to children with diarrhea at health and nutrition sites.
