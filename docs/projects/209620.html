Project Summary

The principal objective of this intervention is to improve protection of civilians through engagement and advocacy among armed actors, de facto authorities and civil society to increase knowledge and respect of international humanitarian norms. This project will work directly with the duty-bearers for international humanitarian law (IHL), both as armed groups and local authorities. The organisation will focus on addressing the most urgent protection risks faced by civilians as result of the behaviour, policies and actions (or failure to act or pass policy) of armed groups and de facto authorities. These risks include restriction of movement, forced displacement, child recruitment, Housing, Land, and Property (HLP) rights violations, arrest and detention, gender based violence etc. The organisation will also advocate with authorities and armed groups on the conduct of hostilities to try to reduce risk of harm to civilians at such times. A key area of advocacy will be around the removal of restrictions of movements currently placed on IDPs by the 'Expatriate Card' system. The organisation will also work extensively with duty bearers on the issue of prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse and the prohibition of gender-based violence and discrimination.
This intervention is primarily targeted to support individuals at risk of violence and violations. This includes IDP populations in NES that are affected by discriminatory movement restrictions, IDP populations in camps that are exposed to a wide range of protection risks including GBV, civilians in areas of conflict that are exposed to harm during periods of conflict, those at risk of arbitrary arrest and detention which includes children.
To this end, the NGO will actively engage all armed groups and de facto authorities in the Northeast. This engagement takes many forms including private and public advocacy, awareness raising sessions on humanitarian norms, technical trainings on IHL legal issues, monitoring of violations.
In addition, the NGO will work with civil society organisations (CSOs), and community-led groups, such as Women-led and ethnic-minority led CSOs, tribal leaders, and religious leaders to bolster communities’ resilience and their capacity to safely engage duty bearers on their protection concerns.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

All our staff have signed Code of Conduct and have been trained for prevention of PSEA violations. PSEA action plans are discussed in our regular meetings, and as well while we are meeting with local authorities’ officials, we discuss those preventions as well. We will also organize capacity building sessions with local authorities, and other potential actors involved in this project, especially those in contact with IDPs, and make sure they have received PSEA training, signed the Code of Conduct and are aware about SEA and reporting potential violations. Our programme Coordinator for NES is also the PSEA focal point and has been trained for PSEAH investigation so he will be leading on the implementation of this project. Our orgainsation is actively membered in the PSEAH Network and Protection Working Group in NES, so we regularly participate in the regular and ad hoc meetings, report potential violations in the areas of our operation and as well are actively involved in developing and promoting the related policies and procedures either in host communities or the last resource sites. Operational risks related to our staff, local authorities, affected people, and those related to the processes, or existing systems, which could negatively impact our daily activities of the project are identified, assessed, evaluated, assign corrective actions, and recorded for keeping monitored.
