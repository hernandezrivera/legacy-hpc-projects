Project Summary

The prolonged humanitarian crisis in Syria combined with the challenges brought on by the COVID-19 pandemic and its economic impact has severely affected the health system in Syria and increased the risk of potential infectious diseases outbreaks. Since the onset of conflict in 2011, the country has suffered from deterioration of living conditions and environmental conditions, breakdown of control measures, collapse of the health system and decrease in health workforce, and inadequate surveillance and detection of cases, as well as difficulties in access to health facilities and to affected populations. The Early Warning and Response System was established in response to the Syrian Crisis, with the objective of addressing the increased potential for communicable disease outbreaks. EWARS has been considered as one of main source of data for monitoring and response to disease outbreaks in Syria. The number of sentinel sites increased and reached to 1360 in 2021 and covered public and private health facilities across the 14th Syrian governorates. Although EWARS has become well established in the country and had largely replaced the main national routine surveillance system, however, new concerns about the spread of infectious diseases, especially emerging diseases such as COVID-19 or pandemic influenza, have emphasis the importance of enhancing the early warning and rapid response surveillance system in addition to the existing routine surveillance system. This project aims to improve the availability of descriptive disease epidemiology data, outbreak early detection and response, provide data required for assessment of disease prevention and control interventions, in addition to improve the information system of surveillance by using a web-based interface with integration of other vertical surveillance systems to detect unusual events that might signal an outbreak. The project also aims to enhance the country’s capacities for timely laboratory confirmation, as outlined in the international health regulations (2005), and to facilitate the transfer of safe and appropriate diagnostic technologies, practices and training to laboratories in the zones of emergence of these diseases. The successful detection, characterization and tracing of transmission and the subsequent management and control strategies of these disease outbreaks require accurate laboratory confirmation. However, there are major gaps in basic laboratory diagnosis for common epidemic-prone diseases and in biosafety and biosecurity practices in the public health laboratories.
Proposed activities include the support prevention and control of epidemic prone diseases through the provision of lifesaving medicines for communicable diseases. Activities also include the support in enhancing laboratory capacity for timely diagnosis and response to potential epidemic of high thread pathogen, expand the EWARS coverage in hard to reach and remote areas, and develop electronic application for routine surveillance system.
The project will contribute in reducing the burden of infectious diseases and will cover cost effective interventions for the prevention and early detection of outbreaks. This project will be implemented through close coordination and consultation with MoH, health directorates at the governorates and district levels. the project will also contribute in strengthening the capacity of health partners through capacity building and technical support activities for surveillance and lab officers. and will ensure the availability of reliable disease surveillance data, and secure treatment and case management of Infectious disease.

4 - Needs

Infectious diseases pose a major and continuing public health threat to Syria, which continues to face recurrent outbreaks of emerging and re-emerging diseases with a significant impact on health and economic development. In 2020; COVID-19 was recognized as a global health emergency which has may negatively affected reporting of non- COVID -19 events. On the other side, waterborne diseases posed an increasing risk as a result of interrupted access to safe water supplies and damages in water network. Outbreaks of acute diarrhea were reported in three locations in Rural Damascus due to E-coli contaminated water as follows; January 2020 more than 1000 acute diarrhea cases reported in Wadi Barada and Tal Makan. And in October 2020 more than 9500 cases reported in Al-Moadamieh area in Rural Damascus. Hepatitis-A outbreak was also reported in Homs Tal Kalakh area with 60 cases detected mainly among children. Leishmaniasis is prioritized as one of main public health concerns in Syria. Moreover, there is an urgent need to enhance capacities for timely and accurate laboratory confirmation and facilitate the transfer of appropriate diagnostic technologies, practices and training to impacted laboratories. The public health laboratories at governorates have been affected extremely as result of crisis. public health laboratories are no longer functioning in most governorates due to damages of infrastructure, unavailability of laboratories supplies and devices, in addition to insufficient lab staff at governorates. only the Central Public Health Laboratory in Damascus is capable of testing for cholera, measles bacterial meningitis. Most laboratories have old and inadequate equipment which requires maintenance and calibration.

5 - Implementation of activities

- Upgrade surveillance system for infectious hazards including early warning system and EBS and strengthen further involvement of the private sector, private hospitals, NGOs, university and academic hospitals in EWARS through providing operational support for NGOs and community workers for participation in COVID-19 cases contacts tracing and follow up in addition to raising awareness activities. The activities will focused mostly in remote areas and IDPs camps at NES and will cover incentives, transportation, PPE.- Monthly meetings for the central national surveillance officers to coordinate and planning timely response. and analysing of surveillance data (6 meetings for 10 officers).- Support central public health laboratories with lab reagents and supplies. The quantities will cover the needs of CPHL in addition to peripheral laboratories at the governorates.- Support COVID-19 laboratories with reagents, supplies, and devices. Currently there are seven labs for COVID-19, MoH plan to increase the number of lab to 10 during 2021. - Support measles lab with reagents, supplies and PCR machine. - Activating of public health laboratories in Aleppo, Hassaka and Deir Ezzor. - Capacity building activities for lab staff. - procurement of medicines and vaccines includes the procurement of following items: Cholera kits, ORS, brucellosis medicines, leishmaniasis medicines, NTDs medicines, vaccines : influenza (100,000 dose), hepatitis B (60,000 dose for adults) , rabies (36,000 dose), yellow fever (1500 dose), Pneumococcal Polysaccharide (1500 dose), Human rabies Immune Globulin (16,000 dose).- Strengthening the capacity of RRTs: - update and print field guideline and SOPS for the surveillance including COVID-19- M& E.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. There is a protection risk for surveillance workers who may be exposed to disease infection, physical security/ violence during data collection from EWARS sentinel sites and investigations of alerts, including laboratory specimen collection in insecure areas due to conflict dynamics and the presence of actors on the ground. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium Mitigation measures: a) Assess and analyze the security situation of the concerned areas before planning field visits/investigations, b) Ensure the use of secured transportation routes and vehicles for field investigations, c) Build relations with local communities to increase acceptance of humanitarian workers, d) support the surveillance and rapid response teams with needed personal protective equipment PPE. 2. Disclosure/mishandling of patient names and information leads to stigma and discrimination against patient/family/community. Likelihood: low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation measure: use unique codes for cases to ensure the confidently of data, training for RRTs. 3. Nature of RRTs/lab workers jobs could lead to stigma/discrimination. Likelihood: low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation measure: engagement of community leaderships to advocate the tasks of RRTs. 4. Relatedly, for RRTs and laboratory staff: burn out/stress. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation measure: Increase the number of trained RRT and lab personnel, activate the RRTs at district level, involvement of community and health partners In surveillance activities mainly In hard to reach areas.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Difficulties in conducting verification of alerts and case investigations. Mitigation measures: a) Coordinate with other partners and communicate with WHO focal points. b) Expand sentinel site network and field focal points to increase info access. Risk 2. Unavailability of complete information that could undermine epidemiological analysis of outbreaks and timely updates. Mitigation measures: a) Consult with Ministry of Health and other health authorities on info sharing. b) Coordinate with other involved partners and communicate with WHO focal points on reporting and data sharing. Risk 3. Difficulties in confirmation of alerts due to unavailability of laboratory facilities. Mitigation measures: Liaise with WHO collaboration centres for lab sample analyses requiring advanced testing. Risk 4. Limited access to impacted areas due to conflicts, actors on the ground, and border closures. Mitigation 4. a) Pre-position medicines and supplies in key regions for rapid mobilization/ distribution. b) Coordinate with other involved partners to timely response to outbreak and information sharing. Risk 5. Delay in shipping medicines and distribution for outbreak responses. Mitigation 4. Ensure preposition of medicines and medical supplies in key regions of the country for immediate mobilization and distribution. Risk 6. Risk in delay of official correspondence with the Ministry of Health. Mitigation measures: a) Ensure consultation and close communication with Ministry of Health and other health authorities on activity implementation. b) Lobby Ministry of Health and other health authorities for appropriate and timely implementation. Risk 8: Community behavior of hiding cases . Mitigation measures: raising awareness.
