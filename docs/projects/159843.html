Project Summary

Somalia is a country whose public health system has significantly underperformed for many years because of longstanding conflict and instability and is further overburdened by the impact of climate shocks such as drought and flooding. Somalia has the world’s highest child mortality rate. The risk of childhood mortality is highest between birth and one month, accounting for approximately 45 per cent of death among children under age five. Nearly 54 per cent of health facilities do not provide an ‘Expanded Programme on Immunization’ services; driving coverage below 50 per cent, leading to a rapid accumulation of children susceptible to vaccine preventable illnesses and exposing communities to the risk of communicable diseases. Surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2019 indicate the persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices.
Due to the dry and harsh Jilaal (January March) season and a significant delay in the start of the 2019 Gu season rainfall, Moderate to Severe drought conditions prevailed in most parts of Somalia between March and mid-May. Accordingly, the 2019 FSNAU Post Gu nutrition assessment findings showed marginal decrease in the Median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) of 13.8% down from 14% of post Gu 2018 and 17.4% of post Gu 2017. The corresponding median SAM prevalence are: 2.3% (Gu 2019), 2.2% (Gu 2018) and 3.2% (Gu 2017).
The FSNAU projects the GAM in Badhaadhe district at 11.9 % and SAM at 1.7 % while the prevalence of GAM and SAM in the neighbouring Dobley IDP, Kismayo Urban and Kismayo IDP are 14.4%, 11.7% and 10.5%; 2.7%, 1.1% and 1.5% respectively. GAM and SAM rates Lasanood (Sool) are at 9.8 and 2.8 respectively while Dollow IDP in Gedo is at GAM 18.6 and 3.4 SAM slightly higher that of pastotal and riverrine Gedo populations which showed GAM rates of 16.4 and 17.9 respectively.
Population groups classified as Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse require interventions aimed at reducing food consumption gaps, eradicating acute malnutrition, saving lives, and protecting and saving livelihoods. Gedo, Riverine Gravity Irrigation of Middle and Lower Shabelle and Juba, Bay-Bakool Low Potential Agropastoral, Sorghum High Potential Agropastoral of Bay and Shabelle, Southern Agropastoral and Southern Inland Pastoral (SIP) of Hiran and Gedo all have a Critical GAM prevalence, indicated by a weight-for-height z-score of 15-29.9% or by a Mid-Upper Arm Circumference below 125 mm of ≥10% and are population of concern needing urgent interventions.
ADA is well positioned to implement the management of severe Acute Malnutrition both with and without medical complication since it already has well established four health facilities which will host static nutrition centers and also the outreach nutrition teams. actively participates in nutrition cluster coordination meetings at regional level and closely co-ordinates with individual nutrition partners in referring the active cases. ADA also has currently been working closely with the community through community health workers who will take lead in ensuring timely identification and referral of Malnourished cases from the IDP camps.
The project will target 17618 direct beneficiaries and 60,000 indirect beneficiaries from the community who will benefit indirectly from the project from improved services and improved nutrition and hygiene practices.

Mitigation Measures

Volatile risks in Somalia context with service delivery has been very fragile. ADA will focus of the humanitarian response and provision of protection services has been accessible thus forcing IDPs to move to find assistance or in drought impacted communities in accessible, less conflict prone areas. Areas with active conflict, will be benefiting from all measure of the health humanitarian response, have seen minimal protection engagement through the efforts of some highly motivated individual at the community. ADA will engage further in conflict affected areas through various actions including but not limited to engaging the various gatekeepers and the local authorities and the IMAMs. ADA will put in place feedback mechanisms that protect the whistle bowers and at the same time create openness to discuss the issues raised. ADA will work with communities in order to increase access to the disabled persons and ensure that persons with disability will receive necessary intervention without discrimination and stigma.
