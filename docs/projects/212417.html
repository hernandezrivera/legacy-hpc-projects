Project Summary

The proposed action aims to reduce risks, mortality, and morbidity among children under 5 (CU5), pregnant and lactating women (PLWs), and Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) through safe access to integrated lifesaving nutrition services, which will be provided by activating: 12Rapid response teams (RRTs) and 2MBA (mother baby areas) in Dana, Salqin and Marret mesrin subdistricts to deliver nutrition responses to the most affected vulnerable populations (CU5, PLWs) and PWDs according to their nutritional needs.project's teams will target areas with 3,4 severity in NW of syria.
The RRTs staff will distribute IYCF awareness messages for mothers and caregivers, Family MUAC Approach (FMA) will be activated in the targeted locations to enable mothers and caregivers to support screen and refer malnutrition cases among their children, CMAM activities will be implemented through screening malnutrition cases among CU5 and PLWs, and admitting those cases to OTP, TSFP to be treated in case supplies are available or refer them to the nearest health facility in addition to referring complicated SAM cases to SC to be treated, which will contribute to achieving integration between health and nutrition. Also, RRTs will conduct a hygiene promotion campaign in the camps to raise awareness of the importance of good hygiene practices. Throughout the campaign, the importance of good hygiene in preventing the spread of disease and promoting overall health will be emphasized, in addition to screening GBV cases among CU5 and PLWs and referring them to specialized centers.
On the other hand, two MBA (Mother Baby Areas) will be activated to protect and promote safe and appropriate IYCF practices and to mitigate/minimize the risks associated with challenging and difficult circumstances for caregivers and young children in NW Syria, where two nutrition technicians and one protection worker will support mothers, babies, and caregivers in each mother-baby area. One-to-one and group sessions will be conducted, including activities of IYCF initial rapid assessment for mother and baby pair and supporting complementary feeding of child 6-59 months.
Need assessment:
According to the HNO Syria2024 The prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has surged threefold in the past five years, escalating from 1.7 per cent in 2019 to 4.7 per cent in 2023 across Syria. In a recent SMART Survey, the prevalence of GAM WHZ increased to 4.1% in 2023, The child's anemia remains at 37%, which is considered Serious/ Severe. Anemia in WRA and PLWs remains at >50%. The prevalence of stunting rate remains at the same level at 22.4% with a very slight increase compared with last year at 22.3%,

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Nutrition service will be provided in targeted areas within the camps to provide preventive and therapeutic nutritional services for CU5, PLWs including PWDs. Treatment for cases of malnutrition will be provided if therapeutic supplies are available, or they will be referred for treatment to the nearest health facility if supplies are not available through coordinating with health and nutrition partners. Coordination will be done with camp management to obtain necessary support and key community persons such as the mosque's imam and teachers. Each rapid response team will provide services to 60 -90 beneficiaries per day according to the protocols approved by the nutrition sector. Protection Risk AnalysisThe project design is built on robust need assessment and targets the most vulnerable groups in the targeted areas without discrimination or exclusion. all the project’s staff are aware and acknowledged of detection and referral of GBV to other service providers. especially tow protection workers are delivering services in tow MBA and conducting group awareness-raising sessions targeting PLWs and caregivers of CU5. Before starting to implement the project activities, the project team is being trained on GBV core principles. RISKS and Mitigations:•Risk: lack of nutrition supplies•Mitigation: malnutrition cases will be referred to other nutrition facility supported through other partners to be treated•Risk: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) between service delivery personnel towards beneficiaries.•Mitigation: All UOSSM staff have undergone training on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and have signed Terms of Reference (TOR). They are also aware of the internal PSEA policy. and the organization has appointed a dedicated PSEA focal point to address any reported incidents promptly RISKS and Mitigations
