Project Summary

Of the total estimated 2.8 million internally displaced people in South Sudan, as of October 2024 there are 934,069 individuals (155,678 HHs) living in 123 formal and informal IDP sites across the country. Self-settled, unplanned IDP sites are the least visible and most underserved, placing this already vulnerable population group at an even higher risk of exclusion from access to basic services. Moreover, based on CCCM Cluster data, it is estimated that up to 15% of the returnees, mainly from Sudan, settle in existing IDP sites across the country.
The project will focus on assisting displaced people living in the most underserved sites, in locations prioritized by the CCCM Cluster, applying area-based, roving and mobile response approaches to reach informal sites, working through localized response and coordination structures that will allow access to hard-to-reach areas, identification of common needs among sites within a geographic proximity, where possible linking those site populations with existing, available services in the area. Mobile response will remain the emergency, time-bound modality to respond to new displacements. Mobile CCCM teams will work closely with identified local partners on the ground to build their capacity and ensure the sustainability of the response. Additionally, Since the start of the Sudan crisis in April 2023, UNHCR has been providing life-saving services in the transit and reception centers across the border and will continue to do so in 2025.
A key component of the project will be to support community-based structures to enhance participation in planning and decision-making, in particular seeking empowerment of communities. This will be done with a focus on vulnerable groups, PSNs and PWDs so that their views are received, and acknowledged and service delivery meets the specific needs of all groups. To this end, protection will be mainstreamed into all CCCM activities to respect and promote the rights of the most vulnerable. Site care and maintenance improvement projects will be implemented though community engagement and will be aimed at minimizing risks at site level.
UNHCR will put in place mechanisms to strengthen site/area level coordination and monitoring, improving the wellbeing of displaced populations whilst providing some level of dignity and equitability by promoting AAP. IDPs’ engagement in the delivery of services in an equal and needs-based manner will be possible through regular feedback and dissemination of information for example through community-based networks and complaints and feedback mechanisms.
In line with the CCCM Cluster Strategy 2023-2025, UNHCR will leverage the pivotal role of CCCM, linking service providers, IDP and host communities, and government authorities, as catalyst for effective and sustainable solutions. In all locations, UNHCR will support the regular roll-out of the Comprehensive Site Profiling Tool, which makes available site-level information highly relevant for solutions programming, for a variety of partners across the HDP nexus. The project will also explore phase-out and transitional strategies for managed sites, advocate for, and support increased responsibility of the government in the management and administration of displacement sites. UNHCR and partners will ensure coordination for solutions for the IDPs in sites – this may include return, safe and dignified camp exit, go and see visits, community engagement and consultations, identification, and implementation of self-reliance activities in IDP sites, etc.
Additionally, UNHCR will continue to ensure static CCCM coverage at the transit and reception sites receiving new arrivals from Sudan. Minimum CCCM interventions in these locations include activities corresponding to the HPC 2024-25 Strategic Objective 1, related to facilitating safe access to life-saving services and protection.

1. Needs

By end-2025, over 9.2million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance in South Sudan. Of the CCCM Cluster 2025 target of 1,126,886 IDPs and returnees living in displacement sites, UNHCR aims to reach 484,347individuals. Of these, 401,169 under the regular CCCM response and 83,178 new arrivals from Sudan, assisted in transit and reception centers. South Sudan continues to struggle with the impact of multiple displacement emergencies and protracted displacement situations. Ongoing localized violence and economic challenges severely hinder people's access to basic services. Frequent conflicts over land, resources, and power among various stakeholders add to the country's fragility, while extreme weather events are more frequent and intense, with recurring floods and drought-like-conditions disrupting agriculture, threatening food security, and displacing populations. IDPs continue to face life threatening risks and violations. In IDP sites, living conditions are characterized by overcrowding, poor infrastructure quality and sanitary conditions. According to data collected by the CCCM Cluster through the Comprehensive Site Profiling and the MasterList September 2024 tools, 89% of IDP sites are at risk of flooding, outbreaks of infection diseases, and localized violence outbreaks. 81% of IDP sites are self-settled, collective centers, spontaneous sites, which are the most severely underserved, placing heightened risk of exclusion from access to protection and basic services on the most vulnerable IDP population groups, including elderly, women and children (80% of site population), and persons with specific needs (20% of site population). Economic instability and limited livelihoods are continuing to impact household purchasing power, creating greater economic barriers to access to services, and forcing site populations to resort to negative coping mechanisms, while protracted displacement and limitations in achieving durable solutions contribute to increasing psychosocial trauma and vulnerabilities of people living in displacement sites. Most IDPs living in sites lack regular income and social safety nets to seek better and safer housing. Moreover, due to the protracted displacement, coping strategies of IDPs are significantly eroded, and unresolved security and livelihood obstacles lead people to remain vulnerable and in need of humanitarian assistance. This critical situation will extend to the percentage of South Sudanese returnees who will have no other option than to settle in existing IDP sites. Most returnees from Sudan are arriving in South Sudan in extremely bad health, nutrition, and physical conditions, and most have demonstrated food insecurity and the inability to meet basic needs. In addition, they face uncertainties related to security, HLP rights, and access to livelihoods. They require economic support, accessibility to services, improved social cohesion, safety, and security.CCCM interventions are vital for ensuring that displacement sites are safe and secure, assist displaced populations to meet their basic needs and facilitate solutions through coordinated comprehensive data collection that informs integrated response and transitional programming.

2. Project activities

In 2025, UNHCR intends to implement CCCM activities through an area-based approach, reaching a total of 484,347people across six states. Project activities are aligned with the CCCM Minimum Activity Set as set out by the CCCM Cluster, and promote the active involvement of displacement site residents, including through cash-for-work where feasible, with a particular focus on vulnerable groups, through participation mechanisms that will be set in at all stages of the project life cycle. CCCM activities will entail mapping, monitoring and coordination of services at site/area level, referrals, data collection and sharing, systematic and meaningful engagement and participation of displacement site residents, feedback mechanisms, establishing and supporting self-governance and community mobilization mechanisms, support for durable solutions, and basic site care and maintenance with mainstreaming of environmental considerations. The activities will seek to strengthen inclusive and functional CCCM structures through engagement with different members of the community in decision making and assignment of responsibilities, particularly the women, girls and persons with disabilities who have taken up additional burdens in the crisis to care for the basic needs of their families. UNHCR and partners will establish coordination mechanisms, reinforced through regular site or area-level coordination meetings engaging service providers, community representatives, and local authorities. UNHCR will continue its efforts for CCCM capacity building of different stakeholder groups, with a particular focus on community self-governance structures and government authorities, in support of phase-out and transitional strategies. This aims to increase communities’ ownership of the overall site management, strengthen their coordination with authorities and service providers and ensure integration of protection principles. In areas affected by conflict, the project will integrate community cohesion activities, establishing strong joint host-IDP community networks and leadership structures, and community joint watch-groups trained to support each other in a coordinated manner and improve the overall security around the sites. Additionally, UNHCR will support community-led projects to respond to gaps identified by the communities themselves. Community-led projects supported by UNHCR will promote engagement of women-led and IDP/returnee-led organizations, and the activities will aim to reduce tensions between communities, reinforcing a sense of dignity, reducing vulnerabilities, and helping build local capacity while reinforcing coping strategies in times of crisis. In reception and transit centers UNHCR and partners will continue to provide CCCM services including site management or site management support, community engagement and CwC, mapping, monitoring and coordination of services, headcounts, management, and upkeep of site infrastructure.UNHCR CCCM will measure the success of indicators through regular FGDs and meetings with the affected and targeted communities, inclusive of all groups. Rights-based and AGD Approach principles are applied throughout the program cycle to ensure that the voice, needs, and feedback of persons of concern are taken into account, and that gaps, challenges, and solutions are identified. UNHCR area-based approach CCCM activities will support solutions platforms and the Flagship Initiative through people-centered, context-specific and results-oriented interventions, putting participation and AAP at the center of the response, contributing to the development of medium to long term plans to address the many issues facing people living in displacement sites and the identification of durable solutions.
