Project Summary

There are at least 569,431 vulnerable children with severe needs in Jonglei State. 160,625 children are in Ayod and Bor South Counties are in need. There are 66,816 children in Ayod with extreme to catastrophic needs. In both Ayod and Bor South Counties, at least 50% are girls and 15% children with disability (CWDs). Insecurity and recurring inter-communal violence pose a significant challenge to school functionality in Jonglei and the Greater Pibor Administrative Area. Although insecurity and safety has been widely cited as the main barrier to access in the greater Jonglei area, however, school functionality has been severely impacted by flooding in this region as well.
SAADO is therefore seeking funding to address the most urgent educational, safety and psychosocial needs of children and youth in Ayod and Bor South in Jonglei state. This proposed intervention will target at least 7,300 children (5-17 years)(boys;3450;girls 3,850) of which (Host Communities 3,500; Returnees 2,500; IDP's 1,300) 50% will be girls across the two counties; 4,380 in Bor South and 2,920 in Ayod County. This project will largely target the most marginalized children, children with disabilities, out of school children, children from IDPs and Returnees households, and teenage mothers. In addition, this project will directly target 160 teachers (40% female) 60 PTA/SMC members and 20 CED officials. While this project purposes to primarily focus on the above-mentioned school age children, SAADO will also target at least 850 children (3-5years) with early childhood education interventions. Overall, at least 15% of the total target number of children will be children with disabilities. (CWDs). Reflecting on the different needs assessments’ findings and through analysis of the operational context, the following key areas will be the focus of this project: Access to education and retention of boys and girls including out of school children, IDP’s, Returnees, refugees, children with disabilities and teenage mothers is enhanced: Barriers to accessing education, such as lack of learning materials, inadequate learning spaces, WASH facilities will be reduced through provision of learning materials, renovation/construction of TLS, provision of WASH facilities, and provision of assistive learning materials for CWDs; The quality of teaching and learning is sustained: Through the capacity of teachers to adapt teaching to challenging conditions, psychosocial support for children, provision of teaching and learning materials for learners and teachers; Children are safe in schools and can be able to build resilience and positive coping mechanisms to face shocks related to conflict, crisis and disasters, and responding to security and safety, and violence against children within school environment; Improved capacity and systems of the Ministry of Education and community-based structures for service delivery, quality education and leadership strengthened.

1 - Needs

As evidenced from various assessments and analysis the current and evolving needs in relation to education services in South Sudan, which identified as existing educational challenges and inequities that have been exacerbated by broader social-economic hardships and the after- effects of the COVID-19 pandemic(HNO,2022). There are at least 569,431 vulnerable children with severe needs in Jonglei State. At least 28.2% of these children in need are in Ayod and Bor South Counties. In addition, 50% of the children in need are girls and about 15% are children with disabilities (CWD).Jonglei state still remains one of the leading regions that is difficult for children to have access to education (IRNA Report Jonglei state,2021). Insecurity and recurring inter-communal violence are the main impediment to access to education services for children and youth. These two factors have had significant impact on school-to-school functionality, resulted in high dropout rates and contributed to a great extent on compromising the quality of education in the region. Jonglei state has also been severely impacted by floods of the last few years. Many schools have been closed, submerged, or destroyed due to floods according to Education briefing note, (UNICEF South Sudan Jan 2022). Furthermore, barriers to are even extreme for the marginalized groups in Jonglei state. For girls, forced marriages and other negative traditional practices have had a Signiant impact on enrolment, retention, and transition to secondary school for the girls. Children with disabilities face more worse challenges in relation to access to education services. Stigma from the community, lack of accessible learning materials, school infrastructure that are not responsive to their needs and lack of trained teachers are just among some of the barriers. In overall, following COVID-19, and subsequent closure of schools in 2020, the barriers to access to education services for the children were exacerbated according to (COVID-19 Situation Report 3 March 2022). The pandemic also deepened the already existing social economic hardships that have had very significant impact on access to education services. At the school level, the barriers include lack of clean water and WASH facilities, inadequate learning spaces, low teacher/student ratio, and lack of trained teachers. Barriers are amplified for the adolescent girls and teenage mothers. Lack of menstrual health and hygiene and washrooms for girls in some schools has resulted in irregular attendance or even drop out of girls from schools. Jonglei state also is a home of a significant number of IDPs and Refugees in Bor and other counties. Children in IDP/Refugee camps are finding it very difficult to gain access to education services due to several social-economic reasons. In addition, the fees associated with education is the greatest barrier to access to education. Most of them are likely to miss out on education due to these challenges.

2 - Project activities

1. Establish/rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms in affected areas (in line with INEE-Minimum standards for education in emergencies). A community based approach will be adopted through the establishment/rehabilitation of TLS/classrooms activities. School management committees and local authorities will be consulted including negotiating for support in terms of materials, labour, space and technical support. Integrated schools improvements will include rehabilitation of latrines to ensure separate latrines for girls and boys, kitchen, hand washing points and fence around school. 2. Establish and rehabilitate gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion. Hygiene and sanitation in-formation and communication materials will also be provided to schools. Adolescents girls and youth will also be provided with dignity kits (ICRC standard) and corresponding education materials and orientation sessions. 3. Conduct back to school, community mobilizations and sensitizations campaigns to encourage enrolment of children and adolescents (3-17yrs) to PPE, Primary and Secondary classes. This will include messaging that targets enrolment of children living with disabilities, girls, IDPs, returnees and children from host communities. 4.Training and Sensitization of children clubs/Peer clubs, Teacher, and SMC members on prevention and response strategies of COVID-19. Behaviour Change (BCC) strategies will be employed to reinforce best practices and prevention of the spread of the virus among teachers, learners and the community. The schools will also be supplied with education information and communication(EIC) materials, masks, hand washing soaps, posters and hand washing points. 5. Provide school aged children with fortified school meals/take home ratio. PPE children from selected ECDs will also be provided essential vitamins and deworming tab-lets. 6. Provide selected ECD centres will be provided with learning (ECD kits) and play materials (recreational kits). The play and learning materials will be procured, packaged to an ECD kit and then distributed to the selected ECD centres. Parents and teachers will be encouraged to participate in developing additional affordable locally developed learning and play materials. 7.Training of teachers on EiE, basic pedagogy, subject matter and learner centred methodologies including psychosocial support, gender responsive teaching methodologies and child safeguarding and protection.8. Training for community members (SMCs & PTAs) and Ministry of Education authorities (Payam & County Education Officers) on psychosocial support, school monitoring , quality assurance and administration, facilitating gender responsive teaching/learning environment in schools and promotion of community based mechanism for child safeguarding and protection including protecting of girls from abuse. These trainings will be carried out mostly during school holidays to avoid affecting teaching and learning in schools. The trainings will also include other modules on the role and mandate of SMCs/PTA’s, aspects of school management, promotion of out of school children particularly girls, children with disabilities, IDPs, returnees and refugees to be enrolled in the school system. The training will be carried out by SAADO staff using the MoEST training manual for PTA’s. Additional materials will be developed to cover crosscutting and emerging issues. 9. Procurement and distribution of teachers and learners kits for ECD , primary/secondary schools and Accelerated Learning Program (ALP).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries’ feedback is very important in identifying the gaps in the proposed project design throughout the project cycle to facilitate project learning. In addition, beneficiaries feedback will be used to improve the project process, ensure the implementation goes as per the planned project plans and that it will informs the realization of the project objectives and outcomes.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

A core component of SAADO’s proposed intervention will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members through encouraging the community to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Protection and child safeguarding is at core of this proposed intervention. The report/referral pathways of sexual exploitation/abuse and GBV will be established in schools and also through community based mechanisms to ensure appropriate such cases are reported to the appropriate authorities in schools and the community

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members through having tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions made by the project in the community and will ensure that the school administrations, PTA's and SMC's are trained on feedback mechanisms; In addition, consultations with pupils, teachers, women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard),as well as consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs using confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) which will be used.
