Project Summary

The project “Promoting the realization of women and girls’ rights to inclusive, gender responsive Health education and livelihood towards preventing and coping with COVID19 crisis in conflict-affected state of Borno in North-East Nigeria” is a timely intervention that contributes to accelerating humanitarian relief, sustaining already achieved impacts, and ultimately protecting the lives of populations already bedeviled by armed conflict. This is imperative as COVID19 spread is now reaching conflict affected zones where; resources are mostly unavailable or strained at best; access to clean water, soap and other hygiene materials remains poor; overpopulated camps provide avenue for easy spread of the virus; basic primary health care is barely fit to provide basic services to only a few; local economies have been destroyed with limited livelihood options for families; and gender-based violence risks are at its highest. At the onset of sensitization about COVID19, communities misconceived the reality of the virus, subsequently when cases of the virus were confirmed in Nigeria several disinforming myths emerged making it harder for communities to adopt preventive practices. Albeit the reality of the virus is widely accepted, mixed perceptions coupled with religious and cultural norms still pose significant threats to people’s ability to adhere to preventive measure. This project supports in decreasing the deterioration of human assets and rights, social cohesion and livelihoods as it also protects, assist and advocate for children and caregivers particularly vulnerable to the pandemic.
As part of the projects objectives, this project aims to preserve the ability of the most vulnerable and affected people to meet the additional food consumption and other basic needs caused by the pandemic, through their productive activities and access to social safety nets and humanitarian assistance, ensure the continuity and safety from risks of infection of essential services including health (reproductive health, psychosocial and mental health, gender-based violence services), water and sanitation, food supply, nutrition, protection, and education for the population groups most exposed and vulnerable to the pandemic. This project also aims at Advocating and ensuring that the fundamental rights of children and caregivers who are particularly vulnerable to the pandemic are safeguarded, and that they have access to information and assistance. The ultimate goal of this project is to protect the lives and rights of children and caregivers who have been affected by the protracted conflict in northeast Nigerian and who are most vulnerable during pandemic outbreaks.
The project will be implemented in partnership with the State government, Ministry of Health NCDC and NOA over a 7 months period in 5 Local government areas and 6 implementing locations namely, Bama, Banki, Damboa, Dikwa, Mafa and Ngala. The project targets 42000 individuals across the 6 implementing locations.

Implementing Partners

CHAD is not a stand-alone implementing partner. In implementing this project, CHAD will work in close collaboration with the state government, Ministry of Health, Nigeria Centre for Disease Control, National Orientation Agency, Ministry of women affairs and social development, community structures and other humanitarian partners working across project locations. CHAD will work closely with the state government to obtain operational clearance to allow staff carryout project activities even in the face of a lock-down. This clearance also serves to keep the state government completely in the know of CHAD’s intervention as weekly updates will be shared bilaterally. CHAD will also work in tandem with the state’s ministry of health, also fully informing the ministry about the project and building a strong referral partnership through which incidences or cases can be reported for further actions. Experience show that as gatekeepers, community structures play vital roles in facilitating the participation of community members in project activities and as such, CHAD will work closely with all established community structures (e.g. community leaders, religious leaders, community influencers, community-lead organizations) in mobilizing the participation of communities in project activities.

1 - Needs

The COVID19 pandemic is an unprecedented public health emergency affecting all countries worldwide. There are currently 3145 confirmed cases of COVID-19 recorded in Nigeria as of 02:01, 7th May. 534 cases have been discharged with 103 deaths recorded. A cumulative total of 12,356 persons of interest (POI) were identified and being monitored, out of which, 99 per cent have exceeded the mandatory follow-up period of two weeks. In Borno State, a confinement order in form of a total lockdown commenced on the 22 April and was to extend over a period of two weeks while contact tracing and testing is being conducted. On 22 April, Governors of the 36 states of the Federation agreed on an inter-state lockdown for two weeks from 27 April to mitigate the spread of the pandemic from state to state. The effectiveness of these measures, however, greatly depends on the strength of the health systems and the ability of Governments to enforce policy decisions.In northeast Nigeria, the protracted armed conflict which is in its 11th year has given rise to a complex pre-COVID19 humanitarian needs crisis. Confinement measures are impacting the delivery of humanitarian assistance by limiting the movement of goods, aid workers and beneficiaries, and disrupting transportation services and domestic and international trade. These effects are highly problematic as conflict and other disasters are not stopping for the disease, and humanitarian assistance remains imperative. Majority of communities with ongoing humanitarian response have weak health systems and governance combined with poor basic service delivery. This will severely constrain their ability to prevent the spread of the epidemic and provide health care to infected people, as well as sustain health services to the general population, including sexual and reproductive health services, mental health and psychosocial support, and overall management of non-communicable diseases. Given this reality, the capacity of the region to contain and respond to an outbreak especially in camp settings remains diminutive, humanitarian actors can at best support the prevention of the spread of the virus viz empowering local communities towards the provision of hygiene materials, sensitization on preventive practices and support to monitoring and adherence in local communities while strengthening protection programs as the lockdown and restrictions might further expose women, girls and boys to further violence, exploitation and abuse. The impacts of COVID19 will be exacerbated by pre-existing drivers of humanitarian needs such as conflict or civil unrest, disaster, poverty and inequality – including gender inequality and discrimination – environmental degradation, food insecurity, malnutrition, poor health, water and sanitation infrastructure and services, low education levels, limited social safety nets or social assistance, unequal access to information and others.Children whose families are affected by the virus will require specialized care arrangements and case management services as isolation will separate them from their caregivers. Psychosocial support services are also required to mitigate misinformation, fear and anxieties arising from the COVID19 pandemic.More so, in the Borno state context, the lockdown presents an underlying opportunity for GBV on women and girls as men who normally go about daily activities are stuck at home with wives where heated altercations could lead to physical abuse, adolescent girls who should be in school or skill acquisition programs are idly confined to their neighborhoods, exposing them to the risk of sexual exploitation.

2 - Activities

Child protection messaging targeting children and caregivers and child friendly messaging on COVID 19: this activity aims to provide children and caregivers with key information on COVID19, its myths, symptoms, mode of transmission and preventive practices. key messages will be printed on fliers to be distributed using a tent-to-tent sensitization approach, messages will also be shared remotely using tele-communication devices and mass media. Child protection case management including referral to specialized services for existing caseloads and newly identified vulnerable children: the COVID19 pandemic heightens protection risks for unaccompanied and separated children who will require specialized case management services. Other children with parents are at risk of separation due to isolation measures and will require case management. Case management will include identification, documentation, support and follow-ups amidst the pandemic situation. Provision of mental health and psychosocial support services to children and caregivers: Given high levels of misinformation, skepticism and psychological distress arising from socio-economic impact of the virus, it is imperative to prevent and mitigate the anxiety and stress arising from the pandemic and its related control measures. Psychosocial support activities will range from boys’ and girls’ clubs, audio-visual display of resilience building plays and infographics on COVID19, tele-counselling of caregivers and airing of PSS related radio jingles. Support to caregivers to better care and provide for children amidst confinement measures: As a way of addressing GBV aspects of the COVID19 pandemic, this project will empower caregivers and older adolescents to engage in small scale income generating activities to keep them productively busy at home and out of random altercations either with husbands or other men which may result to physical abuse, this will also reduce their vulnerability to dependence and exploitation during confinement. Caregivers will be empowered to sensitize other women and children on hygiene practices to prevent the spread of the virus. As part of the income generating activities, the project will empower women to locally produce hygiene materials such as soap and face masks which will be sold in communities to help prevent the spread of the virus, this also supports livelihood initiatives to a large extent.
