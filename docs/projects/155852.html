Project Summary

The objective of the project is that the Children displaced due to the emergency situations and who have returned in to the five merged districts North and South Waziristan, Khyber, Orakzai and Kurram Agencies) of KP benefit from the child protection services that are in line with the UNICEF’s Child Protection Core Commitments in emergency.
The target beneficiaries of the project include:
Children (4-17 years): 74,004 (35,522 girls and 38,482 boys)
Adults (18 – 59yrs): 13,761
Total Beneficiaries: 87,765
UNICEF has been the only agency who has been providing Child Protection services to the children in the merged districts. During 2018 UNICEF supported the Social Welfare Department erstwhile FATA in the provision of protection services through the child protection Vans intervention in two merged districts namely; Khyber and North Waziristan. The interventions designed to focus on strengthening, at the community-level, children’s self-protective behavior, as well as contributing to enhanced knowledge and skills among families, gatekeepers and community members in support of preventing the abuse of children. Additionally, practical skills and knowledge was imparted to the children of various age cohorts and their families / care-givers through the medium of an existing training tool entitled – ‘Sticks and Stones’. In this way, the active participation of children in their own protection was encouraged.
During 2018, a total of 32,562 beneficiaries in the targeted 23 villages were reached and were involved in various activities related to self-protection including MRE, and Birth registration
The vulnerability Assessment conducted in 2017 indicated that women and children are highly vulnerable to mine risks when collecting firewood, fetching water and grazing livestock, the outreach of mine risk education to women remains far lower (8%) than to men (24%). Considerably fewer women (5%) than men (14%) have access to mobile phones and thus rely more on word-of-mouth for information. There has been a continuous increase in the cases related to Mine risk. The children are also facing many psychosocial issue, as they have lost their family members and friends, and even the conditions of their houses in the areas of the return adds on to their stress.
Multi-Cluster Indicator Survey (MICS) is another data source that suggests that rates of birth registration are is very low at below 1% in Merged districts of KP. UNICEF has been assisting the government in strengthening the formal system and associated mechanisms and processes for the registration of child births.
The project has two major outputs which are as under:
Output 1: Government and informal community based structures have improved capacities, i.e. knowledge, and material and financial resources to provide psychosocial support to returnee children affected by displacement.
Output # 2:
74,004 children and 13,761 number of parents and community members understand the risks pose by land mines and UXO and know how to prevent these risks and parents understand the importance of registering the birth of their children and act accordingly

Needs

UNICEF has been the only agency who has been providing Child Protection services to the children in the merged districts. During 2018 UNICEF supported the Social Welfare Department erstwhile FATA in the provision of protection services through the child protection Vans intervention in two merged districts namely; Khyber and North Waziristan. The interventions designed to focus on strengthening, at the community-level, children’s self-protective behavior, as well as contributing to enhanced knowledge and skills among families, gatekeepers and community members in support of preventing the abuse of children. Additionally, practical skills and knowledge was imparted to the children of various age cohorts and their families / care-givers through the medium of an existing training tool entitled – ‘Sticks and Stones’. In this way, the active participation of children in their own protection was encouraged.During 2018, a total of 32,562 beneficiaries in the targeted 23 villages were reached and were involved in various activities related to self-protection including MRE, and Birth registration Returns continue to the Merged districts, with 455,290 (97% of IDPs) families returning from 2010-2018. Although the Government initially planned for all people to return by the end of 2016, conditions within the merged districts, including the difficulties of returns during winter, meant that 15,836 families remained displaced and are yet to return. The vulnerability Assessment conducted in 2017 indicated that women and children are highly vulnerable to mine risks when collecting firewood, fetching water and grazing livestock, the outreach of mine risk education to women remains far lower (8%) than to men (24%). Considerably fewer women (5%) than men (14%) have access to mobile phones and thus rely more on word-of-mouth for information. There has been a continuous increase in the cases related to Mine risk. The authorities are also requesting for an increase in the scale of the awareness raising related to Mine Risk. The children are also facing many psychosocial issue, as they have lost their family members and friends, and even the conditions of their houses in the areas of the return adds on to their stress. Multi-Cluster Indicator Survey (MICS) is another data source that suggests that rates of birth registration are is very low at below 1% in Merged districts of KP. UNICEF has been assisting the government in strengthening the formal system and associated mechanisms and processes for the registration of child births. As a result of which the parents and care givers are taking keen interest in the registration of the births of their children. UNICEF believes, the significance of birth registration is that of “passport for protection” which on the one hand empowers government with updated child population for better planning and delivery of necessary services while on the other hand provides illegibility to children and their primary care-givers to claim their entitlements from the government and other service providers, thus, generating real-time demand for an stronger, resilient and responsive child protection system and accountability of the state towards the affected population and citizens in general.Informed by such a context of affairs, the proposed project intends to mobilize relevant technical, administrative and material resources for further enhancing the scope of the already existing Child Protection interventions by strengthening the relevant structures and mechanisms to be more resilient and responsive for the protection of children from significant harm and abuse in the areas of return of Merged districts.

Activities

Output # 1: Government and informal community based structures have improved capacities, i.e. knowledge, and material and financial resources to provide psychosocial support to returnee children affected by displacement. Activities:• Provision of recreational activities to the children (both girls and boys) below the age of 5-17 years• Children 5-17 years know and understand the protection risks and know how to prevent those risks through the conduction of the activities related to the stick and stone manual, Life Skills Based Education etc.• Formation of the Child Protection Committees• Capacity building of the Child Protection Committees, enabling them to prevent and respond to child protection issues at the community level.Output # 2: 74,004 children and 13,761 number of parents and community members understand the risks pose by land mines and UXO and know how to prevent these risks and parents understand the importance of registering the birth of their children and act accordingly • Organization of the awareness raising events though community meetings for adults and play games and other sports activities for children etc on the mine risk and birth registration. • Facilitate the parents and care givers through the child protection van in filling up the birth registration forms and linking them with the birth registration staff of the Local government department for the registration of the births of their children
