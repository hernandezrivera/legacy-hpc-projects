Project Summary

In 2023, Shafak will continue to deliver nutrition lifesaving services in northwest Syria. It plans to support 106639 individuals including 71481children,35867 F,35614 M under five, and 35153 pregnant and lactating women including people with disabilities.
The project targeted locations and intended activities are outlined as follows:
- Aleppo,Jebel Saman, Atareb:Activities will be implemented through six Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), one Outpatient Therapeutic Program Center(OTP),and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program through WFP supplies-TSFP).
- Idleb, Idleb, Maaret Tamsrin: Activities will be implemented through one Stabilization Center(SC) with capacity of 10 beds to admit theSAMcases with medical complications as it is the only referral center in the subdistrict also admitSAM cases withCholera referred fromCTCs, oneOTPCenter,7 RRTs, and SFP.
- Aleppo,Afrin, Jandairis: activities will be implemented through one RRTs. - Aleppo, Azaz, Aghtrin: activities will be implemented through one RRT.Aleppo, Azaz, Suran: activities will be implemented through one RRT. - Aleppo,Azaz,Mare: activities will be implemented through one RRT. - Idleb,Harim,Dana: activities will be implemented through 3 RRTs. - Idleb,Harim,Armanaz:activities will be implemented through twoRRTs.Shafak selected the locations of this proposal according to its current existing supported health facilities and nutrition teams funded by other donors. Through the proposed intervention, Shafak intends to achieve the following objective, which aligns with nutrition Strategic Goals to ensure continuity and increased coverage of nutrition services for prevention and treatment of acute malnutrition (SAM and MAM) among children aged 6-59 months and among pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in the supported Shafak health facilities and through RRTs.Shafak's Nutrition response is being implemented under operational guidance that stipulates adaptations in the context of Cholera Outbreak and COVID-19 to mitigate the risk of infection while ensuring continuity of life-saving nutrition services, mainly services for treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition, micro-nutrient supplementation, and IYCF-E. Also Shafak will implement CVA activities Under the NC's last recommendations and guidelinesThe RRTs’ activities require the list of supplies on a monthly basis which Shafak receives from UNICEF regularly through PAC warehouses in northwest Syria. The supply monthly needs will be calculated and submitted to UNICEF and the nutrition cluster through the established channels.WFP will secure supplementary feeding supplies (plumpy sup.) to Shafak nutrition teams and centers. WFP will support 3 Rapid response teams in the same areas of Shafak FSL project funded by WFP (General Food Assistant-GFA). This partnership with WFP will complement Shafak's intervention by applying 2 approaches of the CMAM program: Blanket supplementary feeding program and targeted supplementary feeding program in the same areas of proposed RRTs.
In 2023, Shafak is planning to apply most of the recommendations shared by Nutrition Cluster depending on the last results of SMART Survey conducted in 2022: -Scale up the provision of CMAM services including SAM and MAM treatment.-Accelerate Scale up the mother MUAC approach to strengthening early identification and referral of acute malnutrition among children and PLWS -Develop and implement an agreed IYCF package at different levels of care (facility, community, and individual levels) among all sector partners -Work with FSL, WASH, and Health to harmonize an integrated malnutrition preventive package focusing on the first 1,000 days with clear referral pathways and criteria -Scale up cash for nutrition in hotspots and underserved areas -Strengthening capacity of health and nutrition staff for anemia detection and treatment as well as investment in equipment for testing anemia and ensuring adequate supplies andsupplements for appropriate treatment and prevention

3 - Needs

Based on NWS Situation Report Last updated: 1Nov2022, an82 percent funding gap is putting the lives of2.5 million people in NWS at risk especially this winter.HNAP recorded 19,545 new internal displacements in northwest Syria in September – the highest record in 2022 - primarily driven by the deteriorating economy. Northwest Syria is faced with a triple burden of malnutrition- high chronic malnutrition depicted by high prevalence of stunting, increasing levels of acute malnutrition and emergency levels of anemia among children and women of reproductive age. Aggravating factors promoting malnutrition include food insecurity, elevated food prices, devaluation of the Syrian pound, scarce and unsafe water use, sub-optimal access to primary health care and disease spread and loss of household income (UNICEF Reports).According to the SMART Survey results, TheJuly2022NWS SMART survey report survey indicates a deteriorating nutrition situation with Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels of 5.0 %. The Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) prevalence SAM rate by MUAC doubled from 0.8% in 2021 to 1.3% in 2022, indicative of a worsening nutrition situation. This translates to 50,823 children with SAM, with 4,620 expected to suffer from SAM with medical complications and require hospitalization. SAM is the worst form of malnutrition; children having SAM stand a 10-12% imminent risk of malnutrition-related deaths, based on Surveillance report the MAM rate in the targeted areas is 15% the SAM2%

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Shafak has a zero-tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse. Support will be provided for PSEA measures to protect beneficiaries and carers, including technical assistance with safeguarding and PSEA policy review and development. At the time of recruiting, all staff members sign the Shafak PSEA code of conduct, and a protection expert gives orientations and regular PSEA training sessions on child protection policy and the PSEA code of conduct. Risk Identified1: the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse by the project personnel on project BNFs and women staff. Mitigation: 1- provide awareness raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA 2- train the new staff on PSEA, CSG and to have refresher trainings to the old staff. 3- Distribute banners, PSEA reporting mechanism including the suggestion box, WhatsApp number...etc. Risk 2: No reporting from SEA survivors: Mitigation: 1- Raise awareness between project BNFs about PSEA and reporting mechanisms. 2- Apply various reporting channels that are accessible to all BNFs. 3- Emphasize on the principle of confidentiality in dealing with SEA cases. 4- Assign gender-balanced PSEA focal points to encourage women and girls to report.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Assumption: Borders remain open and favorable to reach to the communities.Risk: the border between Turkey and Syria becomes closed, or the project location becomes a conflict zone, and thus the project may be subject to delays and complications due to expected escalation.mitigation: Shafak may need to secure additional amounts of supplies to be stored inside Syria for easy access for when the fighting escalates. Shafak need to account for time delays due to fighting. Emergency protocols, contingency plans and resources must be put in place alongside drills to ensure the safety of the staff and the project. Security assessments need to be conducted regularly and the project should be adapted accordingly to ensure the safety of the staff and the beneficiaries. Compensation plans will be adopted in the case of staff injury or loss on duty. 2-Assumption: staff chose to remain at the facility or RRTs locations.Risk: Lack of skilled professionals in these locations due to prolonged crisis and nature of the conflict. mitigation: Shafak have resorted to recruiting health and Nutrition workers from neighboring villages and areas and recruiting lesser-skilled workers. 3- Assumption: Cholera&COVID-19 disease is contained and no infections reach project staff; Risk:project activities,staff and beneficiaries get infected with the COVID-19 disease.mitigation actions: RRTs:will activate protocol shared by the nutrition cluster to implement nutrition activities during COVID-19.
