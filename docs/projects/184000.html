Project Summary

The project seeks to deliver quality life saving and life sustaining, inclusive and gender
responsive interventions to 143,000 crisis affected populations in need, across East and
South Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan States. This will be achieved through the
following thematic areas;
• Provide crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to basic water services.
• Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to basic/limited
sanitation services
• Provide and enable crisis-affected and vulnerable people access to hygiene services
• Provide Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) affected population access to minimum WASH
for Nutrition package
• Provide outbreaks affected and ‘at-risk’ population access to minimum WASH for Health
package
The response targets five localities in East Darfur ( Abu Karinka, Bahr Al Arab, Elfirdos
Shia'ria and Yassin), Seven localities in Blue Nile (Al Tadamon, Baw, El Damazine, Al kurmuk, Wad El mahi
Ar Rusayris and Geisan), four localities in South kordofan (Abu Kershola,
Ar Rashad, Al Leri and Um Durein) and eleven localities in South
Darfur (Beliel, Buram, Damso, Al Radoum, Kas, As Salam, Mershing, Nitega, As Sunta, Kubum, and Um Dafoug)

1 - Needs

According to the FY22 HRP for Sudan, the Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) situation has deteriorated due to a worsening economic crisis, non-functional or aging WASH infrastructure, decreased/insufficient revenue collections, poor budget allocation, and increased operation maintenance costs attributed to an increase in fuel prices in 2021. IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable residents are the crisis-affected populations at significant risk of epidemics, food insecurity, malnutrition, COVID 19 pandemic, floods, and new resource-related conflict. The country continues to go through a fragile transition period, amidst a myriad of multipleshocks. High inflation, rapid currency depreciation, fuel shortages and a strained supply of basic goodsand services characterized 2021. This was further compounded by the post-effects of COVID 19 pandemic, with 40,084 cases and 3,038 deaths confirmed in the country by 21st October 2021. Pockets of conflict especially in the Darfur region and South Kordofan led to displacement of people from their homesto rescind in IDPs. Flooding and its associated impacts affected over 88,000 people in 13 out of Sudan's 18 states, according to the Humanitarian Aid Commision (HAC). According to OCHA Sudan flash update No. 10 released on 2nd Sept. 2021, more than 12,700 homes were damaged and over 4,800 homes destroyed. Most of the families affected by the heavy rain and flooding were forced to shelter with relatives and move to government buildings. This put more pressure in an already bad WASH situation in those areas.These multiple shocks severely impacted livelihoods, escalatingthe vulnerabilities of the crisis affected populations. This response project targets 28 localities with a severity ranking of mainly 4-5 in East and South Darfur, Blue Nile and South Kordofan states where thebulk of the people in need rescind. These four states were in protracted conflict for about17 years and therefore also host majority of IDPs in Sudan. According to the HNO 2021vulnerability mapping, less than 7.5% of households in majority of the targeted localitiesin these four states have access to hand washing with soap and water. Similarly, less than7.5% of households in targeted areas of East Darfur and large areas of South Kordofanhave no access even to limited sanitation while in most targeted localities of South Darfurand Blue Nile states, access to limited sanitation was found to be between 25-50%.Access to basic water services as better but still low ranging between 35-65% across thetargeted localities in the four states. Status of WASH in schools was also found to bestressed or severe in these localities according to a study done by the WASH cluster.Protection risks abound, especially for women and girls who bear the burden offacilitating access to WASH services to their families. This is more pronounced amongdisplaced populations in camp settings who face unique challenges with regard to accessto services. Access to dignity kits among adolescent girls in IDPs and other remote andhard to reach areas is a major challenge which often forces to use old pieces of cloth,sometimes leading to infections. When not available, some girls end up missing schoolevery period cycle during the menses days. Water quality challenges have led to increasedSAM cases among U5s and increased WASH related morbidity among affectedpopulations over the years. Faced with these multiple shocks, eighteen million people arein need of humanitarian assistance to access WASH services across the country. Thesector has targeted 4.7 million people in need of urgent WASH services, among whichWorld Vision proposes to provide life saving and life sustaining interventions to reducesuffering, improve health status, dignity, safety and overall wellbeing for 160,000 vulnerablepeople spread across the four states targeted.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Uncoordinated use of natural resources can have detrimental impacts on theenvironment further contributing to climate change. Climate change has become oneof the major issues threatening the sustainability of WASH Infrastructure developmentin the world. Changes to weather patterns, including the reliability and predictability ofseasonal rainfall and the impacts of extreme events, now place unprecedentedpressures on water resources, especially in flood- and drought-prone contexts like Sudan. Climate change threatens water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) facilities andservices, as these are intimately linked to the water cycle and are vulnerable tochanges in the quantity and quality of available water resources. Floods and droughts,which pollute and reduce water delivery respectively, have now become a perennialissue to deal with in Sudan. We witnessed the destruction of WASHinfrastructure when flash-floods were experienced across many states in Sudan in recentmonths.One of the Five Principles of Sustainable WASH is the element ofenvironmental sustainability which entails placing WASH interventions in the widercontext of the natural environment and implementing an approach of integrated andsustainable management of water resources. This is the approach proposed to be used inthis response, to protect watersheds and over-exploitation of ground water. ThroughIWRM, WV will ensure harmonization of water use with land use to enhance theresilience of water and sanitation services to disasters as an effective strategy toreduce disaster impact, enhance ecosystem services, minimize pollution hazard,minimize over-pumping of groundwater aquifers, promote the sustainable use of naturalresources for WASH infrastructure, and source materials as sustainably as possible.Borehole sitting will be done through geophysical surveys accompanied withEnvironmental and Social Impact assessments to ensure correct siting. Through aparticipatory approach with the target beneficiaries, potential environmental impactsfrom proposed interventions will be identified during ESIA processes and mitigationplans developed, implemented and monitored. Green energy solutions like solarpumping and gravity schemes will be optimized as much as possible. Ground rechargetechnologies like sand dams will be adopted where feasible. Use of locally availableappropriate materials will also be promoted for sanitation facilities as opposed totransported brick and mortar.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection will be mainstreamed into planned activities. Theproject will ensure that each activity will not expose vulnerable groups includingwomen and children to protection risks. Community consultation will be conductedthroughout all the project cycle. Community feedback and complaint mechanism willbe set up. The feedback mechanism will ensure anonymity and confidentiality toprotect the reporting person. In cases of GBV or child abuse cases, support includingreferral to other service providers will be provided and the survivor will be linked toprotection agencies (Government entities or other organizations dealing with protection issues) to prevent exposure from further harm. To possible extent, communitydiscussions will be held separately between women and men to create safe space foreverybody to participate equally. Discussions for children will always be heldseparately from the ones for adults. The project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerablegroups (like minority groups, PLWD, among others) within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. Selection of villages will be done in consultation with line ministries, HAC and other relevant agencies such as UNFPA, department of welfare in the Ministryof social welfare to ensure needs of people with special needs are taken care of. Thesafety of beneficiaries, other project stakeholders and WV staffs is not negotiable andproject will ensure implementation activities will not put beneficiaries, WV staf orproject stakeholders on security risks. Activities for children will be done during the daywith the present of caregivers or other responsible adults from the communities. While theproject will prioritize female and child-headed families to participate in projectactivities or benefit from the assistance, it will also ensure such inclusion does notcreate another type of exclusion. Discussion will be held to inform communities onbeneficiaries’ selection criteria. The discussion will involve different groups within thecommunities including women and children. To adhere to Humanitarian AccountabilityStandard, feedback mechanism will be set up using suggestion boxes or other meanspreferred by the communities. Program staff will use current safeguarding andaccountability standards and policies. Orientation to new staff will include Code ofConduct and safeguarding principle to prevent exploitation of the beneficiaries. Sanitation facilities in schools will ensure gender segregation, be located in well litsafely accessible spaces especially for girls and be designed and constructed to offersafe and private dignified access to boys and girls. Care givers will be educated toensure that children only carry age appropriate jerricans when they have to help infetching water.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability to beneficiaries will be two-fold; to direct beneficiary groups andaccountability to host community leadership. Accountability to direct beneficiaries willbe through transparent engagement, communication and dialogue sessions with group members and regular joint review of progress.Accountability to host community leadership will be through MoUs that WVIrepresented by ‘Village Focal Points’ - will sign with community representatives. TheMoUs will spell out the roles of the organization as well as the role and contribution ofthe communities. In addition to being accountability tools; MoUs enable WVI tocommunicate decisions such as criteria for selection of beneficiaries and projectlocations. MoU are reference points for issues that arise during implementation. MoUshave enabled WVI hold communities accountable for commitments they make at theonset of projects. Participation: This program will be designed in consultation with IDPand host communities leadership and government entities. In addition to regularconsultation as part of ongoing interventions, IDP , host communities and governmentleaders were informed of this project and involved in selecting beneficiaries andprioritizing interventions. At the project onset, kickoff meetings at the village level willbe facilitated by project staff to further publicize the program and communicateopportunities for participation. Feedback mechanisms: The program will institutefeedback mechanisms such as ‘suggestion boxes’; quarterly feedback forums and‘hotlines’ where feasible. The ‘hotline’ approach has been piloted successfully inDarfur. The project staff will be tasked with mobilizing communities – particularlywomen – to participate and will be responsible for investigating factors that hamperparticipation. These factors will have come through normal feedback mechanismsand / or through or discovered through interactions. The project staff will worktowards ensuring that beneficiaries are aware of their rights and entitlements, ensuringthat have timely access to relevant and clear information and they are satisfied withthe opportunities they have to influence the response. The program staff will consultwith beneficiary communities’ on the design, implementation and monitoring ofcomplaints-handling processes. Program staff Welcome and accept complaints, andcommunicate how the mechanism can be accessed and the scope of issues it canaddress. The complaints will be managed in a timely, fair and appropriate manner thatprioritizes the safety of the complainant and those affected at all stages.
