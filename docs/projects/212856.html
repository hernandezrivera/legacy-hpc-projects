Project Summary

Over the past 12 years, the delivery of health services to over 4 million displaced persons living in northwest Syria has been provided by a wide range of relief and non-governmental agencies, both national and international. To ensure a common standard of care delivery and enable coordination among these various providers, the WHO Gaziantep office has developed an Essential Health Services package in 2016-17. The package describes a standardized priority care package, including specific human resource, medicine, equipment and infrastructure requirements as well as standardized operating procedures for priority health areas (including reproductive maternal neonatal and child care, non-communicable diseases and mental health among others).
The implementation of this package in Idlib governate was promoted through an integrated service delivery network in Harim District, which has been piloted since October 2017. Having evolved over nine phases, UDER has currently including 53 facilities within phase 11 of the Harim network (with a potential further expansion during this phase) providing different levels healthcare in terms of complexity and capacity (PHCs, hospitals, specialized centres and mobile units). The approach aims at integrating the operations of health facilities and ensuring a coordinated and comprehensive delivery of care services, with an ultimate aim of increasing access to health services equally for the displaced and host population in the region and ensuring efficient service delivery.
The project relies on WHO's health system building blocks to introduce a transition from the emergency service-based health response in NWS to a more robust health system through a phased approach which introduces upgrades to PHC networks in each of the six health system building blokes. It established local governance/coordination bodies at the field and management levels to oversee the implementation of these upgrades with the overall aim of reestablishing a robust system that connects and mainstreams healthcare services across all health facilities in NWS.
UDER has been leading this project over the past three years, and succeeded in expanding the number of participating facilities and the partners to 13 in the 11th phase. The project was independently evaluated and was praised for its impact and partner satisfaction.
The plan is to continue this project and expand to 70 facilities covering most of districts of Harim, Jisr Ash-Shoghour and Idleb and west Aleppo.
