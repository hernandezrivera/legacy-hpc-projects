Project Summary

The continued bombings, killings, kidnappings and the destruction of property by Boko Haram have become of great concern to the Nigerian government and the international community. The activities of Boko Haram have negative effects on the economy and the people. Similarly, The COVID-19 pandemic has resulted in over 1.4 million confirmed cases and over 83,000 deaths globally. It has also sparked fears of an impending economic crisis and recession. Social distancing, self-isolation and travel restrictions forced a decrease in the workforce across all economic sectors and caused many jobs to be lost. Schools have closed down, and the need of commodities and manufactured products has decreased. In contrast, the need for medical supplies has significantly increased. The food sector has also seen a great demand due to panic-buying and stockpiling of food products in response to this global outbreak.
Food security partners already spent longer time in the distribution of food and cash‐based support due to the need to maintain social distancing. This raises additional challenges, especially in high-risk security areas. Such challenges could lead to desperate measures like the outright sale of assets as a negative coping strategy; Critical access to justice, infrastructures, livelihood support activities, access to healthcare amongst others has been partially or completely destroyed. This has put pressure on the already limited local coping strategies thus increasing the demand on resources to respond to their needs.
Among the State with Covid-19 Confirmed cases, Adamawa State, recorded 17, Borno State, 185 and Yobe State 13 confirmed cases respectively. It is obvious that the containment measures (like the lockdown) by the Government will be tightened thus putting more pressure on livelihood support structures of the affected population in the three States.
As a result of the fast spread of Covid-19 the Federal and State Government imposes lockdown in most of the affected State, the lockdown, however, prevents many Nigerians working in informal sectors from traveling to work or conducting their business. Local food vendors and traders have expressed fears over their ability to feed their families during the lockdown, with their daily earnings their only source of sustenance. An increase in food prices as a result of the lockdown also means that many cannot stock up on necessities.
“The vast majority of people outside of the formal system are hit devastatingly by the lockdown. Zireenza Support Foundation believes that with supplemental support for household income and livelihood activities, social distancing will become more effective. Moreover, while others are still living in denial of the existence of the virus, there are others with misconceptions about the prevention and mitigation of COVID-19 such as traditional beliefs in the intake of some herbs as effective treatment and containment of the Virus may pose a severe danger in the health of those people that takes uncontrolled herbs.

1 - Needs

The long-term effects of COVID-19 crisis will be felt by those who are most vulnerable in North East Nigeria. There is an urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods, especially for the most vulnerable families and female head of household in Host Communities, along with people from the Host Communities. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions.In addition to its immediate impact on health outcomes and, tragically, on lives, it is now clear thatthe coronavirus (COVID-19) outbreak is likely to have long-lasting economic and social impacts on the stemming population, from the direct and indirect effects of illness.Without addressing this urgent need for livelihood restoration, a premature return of IDPs (or refugees) could lead to secondary displacement & deepen IDPs’ vulnerability to even a greater extend. Already now, the deprivation from Covdi-19 and the lockdown has led to societal tensions between the vulnerable community members in Adamawa, Borno and Yobe States. Targeting recovery or establishment of small enterprises for vulnerable Head of household, especially female Head of household is essential to address negative coping strategies which many households have already resorted to. These include buying food on credit, selling of productive assets, borrowing cash from others and more importantly there is severe protection concerns including early marriage, child recruitment, child labour, trafficking, and sexual exploitation and abuse, which Zireenza support foundation is working hard to mitigate through other projects. Cash- based programming has been described as a fast, efficient and dignified way of delivering humanitarian and development assistance in protracted conflict situation and as an alternative source of immediate income or complementary in-kind assistance where it’s feasible. To ensure that the most vulnerable groups are reached, Sector partners will adhere to following principles: Ensure that physically and socially vulnerable groups including female-headed households, elderly and people with disabilities are provided with alternative means of assistance through cash grants.

2 - Activities

The proposed projects seek to support three ER&L Strategic Priorities which are: 1. Awareness Creation on Covid-19 2. Addressing Hunger and 3. Training on Livelihood Skills. The zireenza Support Foundation shall conduct the following Activities to address the aforementioned. 1. Advocacy meetings with community stakeholders (Traditional/religious leaders, youth leaders, Women Leaders, etc) on promoting gender equality and participation in the management and recovery process of COVID-19, Community Development Planning Process and other community engagements to strengthen and Support social cohesion, while respecting the social distancing protocols across the project locations in the Propose three states. 2. Mapping of host communities to identify households hit by drastically reduced incomes, especially the most vulnerable families and female headed households with unstable incomes.3. Selections and training of 300 Male and Female for awareness creation and sensitization on COVID-19 and conducting house-to-house COVID-19 prevention campaign by the 300 selected team members in the Propose LGAs of the three states.4. a. Conduct an assessment on the basic needs of the targeted host communities in order to provide basic Skills and strategic support to the select 1,500 beneficiaries in areas of basic business principles, finance management, bookkeeping and savings b. Profiling and selection of 150 Male and Female for three-days training in their preferred livelihoods skills and Procurement and distribution of start-up kits to trained beneficiaries in the Propose three states. 6. Provision of cash transfer to 1,500 women and girls headed households for investment in income generating activities to enable them recover from COVID-19 pandemic and Profiling and selection of 600 Male and Female for cash for work activities targeting environmental hygiene promotion in the host communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will collect feedback from the most vulnerable members using (e.g. surveys,games, focus group discussions, interviews, etc.) and through which communicationchannel(s) will engage the constituents (e.g. social media, WhatsApp, in person, phonecalls, community meetings, etc.). Both decisions should be determined based on what isappropriate and accessible to the diverse set of people that make up the primary constituentgroup.Zireenza Support Foundation has already set clear goals and objectives for accountable feedback mechanism. In the collection phase ensure that the objectives and the purpose of the exercise clearly explain in simple term to the constituents involved. This should happenUsing simple and clear language, including when they can expect to hear back from ZSF about findings, conclusions or changes, If at all possible, “NGO terms” like“Accountability mechanisms” or “closing the feedback loop”; should be avoided, technical language can not only sound intimidating, but will likely confuse the constituents and potentially undermine the quality of the feedback you collect. The project will create an enabling environment of trust thereby giving the primary constituents a room to express their feelings, mitigate cultural biases too, because sometimes trust is not enough!unfortunately, in certain cases creating safe spaces and building trust with constituents is stillnot enough to collect honest feedback. Cultural biases may also be at play, particularly inSocieties where speaking your mind openly or giving constructive feedback is seen asdisrespectful or inappropriate.The project will be deliberate but allow the primary constituents to raise their own questions as collecting feedback has to be a balancing act between getting information you need and will act on, while also having an approach that is flexible enough to let the constituents raise the points that are important to them.
