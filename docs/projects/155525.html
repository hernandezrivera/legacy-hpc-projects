Project Summary

The project aims to reduce the exposure of displacement affected population to WASH related illnesses through improved access to potable drinking water, appropriate sanitation facilities and strengthened hygiene practices. NRC will strive to reach the most affected and in need in rural areas including bushes security permitting. Specific attention will be paid to women and girls are the crisis has forced them to lose their female dignity due to lack of dignity material, using leave and other plants related material as indicated by women in a gender analysis recently conducted by NRC in the Fako division.
To achieve this objective, the following activities will be implemented in partnership with local NGOs that have better access to communities:
1. Assessments on the water points conditions
2. Formation and training of hygiene promoter groups of water user’s committees
3. Capacity building of WASH actors and partners in emergency WASH and related issues
4. Promotion of safe hygiene and sanitation practices among IDPs and vulnerable host communities
5. Distribution of Wash Kits
6. Distribution of menstrual hygiene kits to vulnerable women and girls of childbearing age coupled to sensitisation on menstrual hygiene management.

Needs

The displaced population in the North West and South West regions rely on surface water from polluted rivers. Consequently, continuous water treatment is needed to avoid an outbreak of waterborne diseases;In addition to overcrowding, those who have fled to the bush are living in unhygienic conditions. According to the ERP, IDPs in both the North West and South West regions are experiencing a deterioration of living conditions and lack access to essential services, including clean drinking water (both in quality and quantity), and basic sanitation services (i.e., inadequate and unsecured latrines; open-air defecation; lack of menstrual and hygiene products for women and girls). The populations living in the bush have poor knowledge of water and vector-borne disease transmission. As they find themselves in unfamiliar and overcrowded areas, there is an urgent and critical need to address hygiene related concerns in order to prevent the spread of disease. A recent gender analysis conducted in the SW region (Dec. 2018) revealed that girls and boys living in areas such as like Munyenge, Kembong, Muea, Muyuka and Nkambe travel long distances to access potable water for the household and the consequence is that children sometimes go late to school or do not go at all because they have to bring water back home. In order to avoid the long queues and conflict that are faced during the day, some girls and boys choose to fetch water at night and thus the risk of sexual harassment/rape for girls is increased as they are more vulnerable in the dark. There is need to assess the water system in the region for improvement and repairs where needed. Displaced women and girls reported acute needs for menstrual hygiene materials. They said that in the course of displacement, some of them spent over a month in the bushes wherein they used plant/tree leaves, old cloths and underwear in the place of sanitary pads, thus exposing themselves to diseases. Due to inadequate income and limited resources, a majority of women (67%) and girls (70%) said that they do not have access to hygienic sanitary materials and now largely rely on the use old cloths, old pants, towels, toilet tissue or the few sanitary pads they receive from relatives/humanitarian organisations to meet their needs in menstrual hygiene management. NRC will ensure that the women and girls of childbearing age receive menstrual hygiene kits and are trained on good menstrual hygiene management and the use of menstrual hygiene kits. An adequate system will be put in place to collect sex and age disagregated data and monitor gender sensitive results and indicators presented in the Logic Model and Performance Measurement Framework.
