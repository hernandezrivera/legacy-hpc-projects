Project Summary

This project will contribute to the immediate life-saving needs and development of sustainable services to gain and maintain access to WASH services using multi-sectoral approaches among IDP, returnee and affected host community. This project will be mindful of the differing needs of men, women, boys, girls and people with specific needs, in the states of East Darfur, South Darfur, South Kordofan, and Kassala. The WASH projects will be integrated with other multi sector projects including Nutrition, Health and FSL.

1 - Needs

The IDPs camp, returnees and vulnerable host communities in South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan and Kassala states are getting assistance through CARE and are currently receiving 15 L/p/d, in line with SPHERE standards. However there is a need to sustain the provision of these services through further construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of facilities. . Around 300,000 individuals in the Jebel Mara region are newly accessible and in vital need of basic WASH, health, and nutrition support. Across Sudan, 7.6 million people are in need of humanitarian WASH assistance, exacerbated in East and South Darfur by cyclical flooding which frequently damages WASH infrastructure and destroys household latrines. The worst flooding for a century hit Sudan in August-September 2020, affecting at least 41,655 in East and South Darfur (OCHA, 22 Sept 2020), and resulting in the declaration of a 3-month state of emergency. In the new targeted areas the average is less than 10 l/p/d and comes from unprotected sources. The same multi-sectoral needs assessment has indicated that adequate water supply is one of the biggest challenges for IDPs and affected host communities including Kassala as not receiving safe and sustained access to water. According to FGD, participants reported that the water is pumped only in the morning and in the evening, which limits their access to adequate supply. 50% of the host community members still remain without latrines.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

CARE promotes environmental friendly and risk avert mechanisms in all its water, hygiene and sanitation interventions, as they have impact on the health of the public, environment and natural resources. Interventions to preserve and protect the environment will be included from the design phase, implementation and monitoring of each program intervention. The mitigation measures could include but not limited to: analysis of the potential impact of key interventions and discussion with community leaders and sector experts; regular joint water and sanitation facilities monitoring and supervision; emphasis on environmental education and capacity building for increased environmental awareness among beneficiaries and implementing partners; solid and liquid waste management; actions to avoid water pollution due to excavation, backfilling and concreating, hauling and damping of earth materials; controlling formation of stagnant water pool to avoid soil erosion and breeding mosquitoes. As well as conducting refresher training to WASH experts and community volunteers. Youth, women, men and others will be organized into water users committees, hygiene promoters, natural resource management groups, and tailored training will be provided to contribute positively to the management of natural resources and peaceful co-existence. Environmental impact assessment will be carried out if interventions require to do so and will be decided with sector ministries.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE will take extra caution in analyzing the protection of at risk groups, including children and girls. Efforts will include: no creation of new risks, address discrimination, analysis of the most vulnerable, safety and dignity in service delivery, orientation and familiarization on humanitarian principles and rights, and promoting empowerment. Furthermore, CARE and partners will exercise principles of protection mainstreaming: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of the communities about the best way to deliver the services and considerations for vulnerable groups, accountability mechanisms and Field visits. CARE and partners will empower communities by training & capacity building & awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consulting them in decision making process, identifying volunteers and establishing and training community committees. Strengthening community protection mechanisms and improving access to services through information dissemination on available services, good selection criteria and identified action of the most vulnerable and referrals will be prioritized. Do no harm analysis will be carried for each sector and the activities will be screened not to enhance risks for all especially women, girls and children. Facilities such as latrines and water points, income generating activities and trainings will be analyzed and discussed with at risk groups to ensure that the beneficiaries are protected and that the services are easily accessible and safely usable by all. Protection risks faced by women and girls, and in particular S/GBV risks, will be the specific focus of capacity building for staff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. Staff will also be oriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm and that they are able to respond safely and appropriately to protection cases when they are reported, using a confidential and survivor centered approach to disclosures of violence. An internal mechanism for reporting complaints and ensuring that required measure are in place will be promoted throughout the implementation period.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARE and its partners will take into consideration information sharing of the project and will get feedback of beneficiaries in the design of the activities and make deliberate efforts to incorporate their views and concerns throughout the programming cycle. To ensure accountability within the project, the staff and local government experts working under each proposed sector will be trained on accountability to program participants and to community leaders. CARE’s recent efforts to employ different accountability mechanisms such as a toll free number, rapid community review and reflection, and community score card represent important steps towards the project’s success as the beneficiaries will be able to provide direct feedback to service providers and suggest solutions. CARE and partners staff. The communities will receive orientation and familiarization sessions on CFRM in general, and on community meetings and forums such as women’s groups, men’s groups, Water User Committees, other project structures, Community Committees, nutrition and health workers’ meetings. Local communities and beneficiaries will be consulted on the design, implementation and monitoring of complaints-handling processes. Local committees in each project location will have two CFRM focal points (a woman and a man) to lead the initiative. This will enable beneficiaries in the targeted areas to safely report any concerns or provide feedback on the project. The participation of affected populations throughout the project period is fundamental in the programmatic approach.
