Project Summary

PUI’s FOCOS strategy programming continues to be undertaken in a multi-year strategic way, with 2022 representing the ninth year of implementation of PUI’s integrated-area based approach (IABA) in Syria. Through this approach, PUI make sures to create synergies between the different interventions implemented in areas in need in order to respond to the needs of the Population of Syria as a whole and to help them to go back to their pre-crisis standards of living, to enhance self-sufficiency and resilience in the long run and to promote social cohesion.
In line with SO1 and SO2, this project aims at meeting the basic WASH needs of the conflict affected population in targeted locations by enhancing their access to safe and adequate water supply and to adequate sanitation services, while fostering sustainability through the rehabilitation of community infrastructures.
Therefore, the plan for 2023 includes implementing the following interventions:
- 18 Water network rehabilitation interventions benefiting 150,200 persons (89320 females and 60880 males, 73866 children, 56011 adults and 20323 elderlies) of different community categories.
- 19 Sewage network rehabilitation interventions benefitting 172,600 persons of different community categories (102760 females and 69840 males, 90114 children, 57816 adults and 24670 elderlies.
- 2 WASH emergency responses benefitting 2000 returnees (1200 females and 800 males, 744 children, 1048 adults and 208 elderlies).
- 8 Hygiene promotion campaigns benefitting 4000 returnees (2400 females and 1600 males, 14800 children, 2096 adults, and 416 elderlies).
It is worth mentioning that PUI has not yet secured any of the required funding but has several proposals and concept notes under discussion.

3 - Needs

After 11 years of the protracted crisis, Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency where access to WASH services is compromised by prolonged decay of the existing systems, caused by damage over years of conflict, wear from long-term functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance, continuous drain of technical staff and poor natural resource management. According HNO 2023 draft, it is estimated that 13.55 million people in Syria will require WASH support in 2023, which represents a 2,6% increase over 2022. Almost half a million more people are in acute need and 10% more subdistricts are now in severe need. To better understand the needs on the ground, PUI obtained information from primary and secondary resources in targeted locations. It was observed that many basic WASH infrastructures are out of service due to destruction and lack of local municipalities’ support to secure spare parts, and people are relying on expensive water trucking or unsafe water sources for water consumption to meet their needs. Sewage networks are also partially damaged, and people are using septic tanks to dispose wastewater, and are collectively renting tanks that draw the water from those septic tanks to a proper disposal location. This is not only very costly for vulnerable people but those tanks are also flooding in the streets, exposing the population to health hazards and the spread of insects and rodents, in addition to the health risk of a mix between sewage and potable water.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Unequal distribution of services (Likelihood: medium, impact: high). Mitigation measures: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Organize joint planning culture and agree on minimum standards of vulnerability criteria; Include community and authorities in the design and implementation of projects.Risk: Health risk associated with construction works and exposure to chemicals. Mitigation measures: Ensure staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working with raw sewage wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff/partners/contractors.Risk: child labour among contractors, (Likelihood: High, impact: High) Mitigation measures: to ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ labourers; add zero tolerance disclosure on child labour in the contracts; check and register civil documentation.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: beneficiaries exposed to SEA harm (Likelihood: low. Impact: High) Mitigation measures: PUI will raise the awareness on existing service providers where victims can seek help; Enforce policies with/through all partners to prevent beneficiaries’ exposure to harm; Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train staff on code of conduct; address any SEA complaints. PUI has a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse, and is committed to implementing the six principles adopted in 2012 by the Inter Agency Standing Committee Task Force on preventing and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions; No curfew or lockdown due to pandemic or outbreak, stable security situation, consistent humanitarian access, no major fluctuation of Syrian Pound, no mass movements of populations, availability of material in the local market and acceptance of community.Risk: shortage of Fuel (Likelihood: medium, impact: high) Mitigation: rely on private transportation means, more activate remote management and decrease the site visits, Risk: Quality/quantity of water not adequate (Likelihood: High, Impact: High) Mitigation: to ensure provision of safe water and monitor water quality including water source, water trucks Risk: Potential exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) (Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High) Mitigation: to work closely with Mine actors and conduct works in cleared areas only; to conduct safety training with staff and contractors - Risk: Theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials by armed groups and used in conflict activities ( likelihood: low, Impact High ) Mitigation: to sign MoUs with local ministries to ensure local ownership of materials & facilities to promote cost recovery systems for longer terms maintenance and to promote community ownership. Risk: AWD/Cholera outbreak, Mitigation: hygiene promotion campaigns to ensure health awareness and best practices. Risk: Stakeholders face operation and management challenges upon completion of rehabilitation. Mitigation: PUI will train O&M units and provide them with the needed tools and equipment.
