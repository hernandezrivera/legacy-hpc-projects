Project Summary

IMC will implement this emergency nutrition response project in five districts, Bubi, Bulilima, Lupane, Tsholotsho and Gwanda. The intervention will cover around 73,044 people. The proposed intervention will address gaps in the provision of infant and young child feeding support and the management of acute malnutrition at primary care facilities. Under the Infant and Young Child Feeding intervention, International Medical Corps will provide technical support to build the capacity of health care workers in providing counselling and support for pregnant and lactating women. Under the integrated management of acute malnutrition intervention, International Medical Corps will be provide technical support for early case detection and improved case management which will result in improved treatment outcomes for malnourished children. The project will support facility-based services as well as community based services to improve reach through interventions such as community based counselling, community based screening and referrals as well as campaigns to raise awareness and uptake of services. IMC will support the response to seasonal surges in cases of acute malnutrition as well as waterborne diseases like diarrhoea in all targeted areas. Health facilities will be prepared with necessary capacity to enable them to identify and manage cases of acute malnutrition while supporting and promoting breastfeeding as a protective measure in times of acute food insecurity. IMC will be implementing proven high impact interventions such as the capacity building on IYCF-E, capacity strengthening on growth monitoring, promotion of an improved nutrition information system for early warning and surveillance. Under the integrated management of acute malnutrition, IMC will support SCs & OTPS run by the Ministry of Health and Child Care to provide optimum services by building capacity in case identification and management. IMC will support active and passive screening to eliminate treatment delays by quickly determining the children that should be enrolled for treatment. Treatment will be provided based on the national guidelines for the integrated management of acute malnutrition (IMAM). Children 6-59 months found to have SAM without complications will be treated in the OTPs and those with medical complications or children below 6 months will be transferred to SC where they will receive medical and therapeutic nutrition.
To promote optimal IYCF practices, including exclusive breastfeeding, timely complementary feeding, and dietary diversity, individual counselling and group sessions for caretakers of children 0 to 59 months will be provided. Nutrition, health and hygiene education will also be provided for caregivers of all children admitted in the IMAM program, women attending antenatal and post-natal services, and when immunization services are provided. Key messages will be on recommended feeding and child care practices, improving dietary diversity using locally available foods, and improved water, sanitation, and hygiene practices. Community outreach activities will be supported through capacitation and monitoring of community health workers. This will result in improved timely access to treatment, program coverage, and community engagement and contribute to the prevention of malnutrition. Community Health Workers will be trained in pertinent related modules such as participatory hygiene and health education and the community module on integrated management of childhood illness to ensure they offer a comprehensive service in the community

1 - Activities

The GAM prevalence in Matabeleland South and North was last reported in June 2019 at 3.1% and 4.1% respectively whereas SAM prevalence was 1.1% and 1.6%. These will likely increase particularly as the lean season progresses. IYCF practices in the districts are also far from optimal with less than 10% of children consuming diets of minimum quality while less than half the women consume diets of adequate quality. Poor diets along with the deteriorating economic situation place the populations in the two provinces at risk of malnutrition. IMC proposes the strengthening of the case detection as well as supporting appropriate Maternal and IYCF practices as a life-saving intervention in the proposed communities. Emergency situations can create severe stress, trauma or psychological difficulties for populations, which may particularly affect pregnant and lactating women (PLW), caregivers, infants and young children. This negatively affects feeding and care practices. Universal coverage of optimal breastfeeding could prevent 13% of deaths occurring in children less than 5 years of age globally, while appropriate complementary feeding practices would result in an additional 6% reduction in under-five mortality. Therefore, activities proposed by IMC are to protect, promote and support appropriate IYCF practices.The following capacity building activities will be conducted for health workers; Training of health workers on the integrated management of acute malnutrition (incl. case identification, case management, stock management and data management), defaulter tracing, WHO Growth Standards, IYCF-E. Health workers will be trained using nationally developed manuals and through the cascade methodology (ToT followed by Training of health workers). The trainings will ensure that health facilities are equipped to identify and manage cases of acute malnutrition. Health workers will also have the capacity to protect, promote and support breastfeeding which is an effective shield against the effect of food insecurity. The project will capacitate a pool on mentors who will be responsible for ensuring the trained health workers are offering high quality services. The mentors will be trained in the following areas;IMAM surge support, on-job coaching and mentorship of service providers. Mentors will conduct facility visits to monitor project implementation and will be drawn from the provincial pool of mentors within the ministry. The project will also build the capacity of community based cadres who are the first line of contact between the community and the health services. They will be trained on the following; active case finding and IYCF-E, establishment of mother support groups.
