Project Summary

The goal of this project is to protect the rights and uphold the dignity of the most vulnerable in Aweil East and Aweil North, South Sudan. The main objective is that risk of GBV is reduced and quality of response is strengthened for 16,000 (11, 200 Women and 4,800 Men) rights holders in Aweil East and Aweil North. The key outcomes include IDPs, returnees , Host communities mostly women and girls have improved access to existing services for GBV survivors through a secure and confidential referral and reporting system, increased community participation in GBV prevention and response activities. The intervention aims to achieve these outcomes through provision of individual and group psychosocial support, raising awareness amongst rights holders on GBV concepts, addressing stigma that GBV survivors are subjected to, capacity building of service providers on effective response to GBV survivors a as well as empowering and strengthening community based structures (CWG/women groups) in GBV prevention and response. Through this intervention, NCA will identify and address barriers to reporting with survivors and how they feel service provision can be enhanced. NCA will use an interagency approach in the implementation of the project to ensure that comprehensive services are accessible to survivors.

1 - Needs

The high incidents of GBV remain a major challenge in South Sudan particularly in Northern Bahr el Ghazal. Women and girls in Aweil East and Aweil North mostly IDPs, adolescent girls and returnees are exposed to many forms of GBV and threats including forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, rape, sexual assault, physical assault, intimate partner violence, emotional abuse, discriminatory practices within the legal system and harmful practices (South Sudan GBV Cluster Strategy 2019). According to data collected by the Gender-based Violence Information Management System (GBVIMS) in South Sudan in 2018 show that approximately 97% of reported GBV incidents affected women and girls. Widespread GBV constitutes a significant impediment to women and girls participation in recovery and development initiatives in Aweil East and Aweil North. The prevalent violence against women and girls in Aweil East and Aweil North are due to the gender inequality exacerbated by the prolonged crisis in South Sudan. All forms of GBV continue to be under reported due to stigma and customary laws which are strongly embedded in Northern Bahr El Ghazal society particularly in Aweil East and Aweil North. A recent survey conducted by NCA show that physical violence by husbands to their wives is widely accepted and is due to various reasons including refusal of sex and incomplete household chore. Presently, there are information and knowledge gaps on GBV as communities lack adequate platforms to discuss and address GBV issues. Lack of Women and Girls Friendly Spaces in Aweil East and Aweil North deny women safe spaces to discuss GBV issues affecting them and response strategies. In addition, women and girls in Aweil East and Aweil North walk long distances to access markets, services and fetching firewood and water which exposes them to further risks and threats such as attacks. Dependence on wood as source of fuel further expose women and girls to attacks during firewood collection in the bush. Girls are at risk to violence due to traditional practices and cultures including early and forced marriage. Women, girls continually face high incidents of trauma coupled with very limited opportunities for social and economic opportunities. Furthermore, due to food insecurity and lack of livelihood opportunities women and girls engage in negative coping strategies such as transactional sex and child labour. Women and girls of reproductive age face serious health risks and only about one in five childbirths involves a skilled health care worker. Survivors of gender-based violence lack access to adequate services. The family’s ability to provide sufficient clothes, shoes, menstrual hygiene products, and access to medications are also affected by their economic situation. Thirteen per cent of the population has access to hygiene products including soap and a jerry can. Menstruation can also undermine the school attendance of adolescent girls with the cost of menstrual hygiene becoming prohibitive as a result of the current economic crisis. Adolescent girls show a lack of knowledge on sexual and reproductive health rights (SRHR) but express an interest and desire for information and accessible SRHR services, which drives adolescent pregnancy which cause extreme health risks including fistula, HIV infection, and a higher rate of infant and maternal mortality.

2 - Activities

1) Train community leaders, youth, women and men groups, law enforcement agencies, PTAs to address issues of GBV particularly early child marriages and girl child education 2) Train peer to peer groups in schools on the negative effects of GBV in 30 schools in Aweil East and Aweil North. 3) Provide professional skills training on case management/PSS for NCA staff (counsellors)4) Provide specialised individual and group psychosocial support and case management services to GBV survivors, women and girls, men and boys of all ages. 5) Provide material support to extremely vulnerable GBV survivors, women and girls of all ages 6) Distribute 1500 dignity kits to GBV survivors in Aweil East and Aweil North. 7) Strengthen referral pathways for local community-based groups, grassroots women groups and conduct workshop to share and identify available resources within the GBV clusters 8) Hold advocacy meetings with Ministry of Education and Ministry of Gender to develop GBV polices in schools and in the community. 9) Hold six radio talk shows on GBV prevention in Aweil East and Aweil North . 10) Organise four awareness consultative sessions with Ministries of Gender and Social Development, Education, and schools administrations in Aweil East and Aweil North. 11) Support eight women groups to carry community awareness outreach on harmful practices of GBV in the community (rape, early child marriage, assault, domestic violence, etc). 12) Establish two Women and Girls Friendly Spaces in Aweil East and Aweil North13) Participate in 16 days of activism by conducting Community campaign, awareness raising and advocacy on GBV consequence and available services and risks.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

NCA has a rights-based approach and all rights holders rights are legally recognised.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

One of the main target groups of the project is displaced women and girls, including creating social bounds between host and displaced women, girls, men and boys. Displaced groups will be consulted during the planning and implementation phases, to ensure that the interventions are based on their actual needs, and to ensure that our response is locally-led.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

NCA has good relationships with local government authorities and community systems in Aweil established over the years as a result of NCA presence in the area. For example, NCA has good working relationship with County WASH departments in Aweil and local church structures which are spread in Aweil which are critical for implementation of this project. NCA will deepen its relationships with state ministries of gender and social development, education, Water and Health which are essential for the success of this project. NCA has a strong WASH programme which has components of GBV which will be linked to the project in the long term. Secondly, NCA will also work closely with community structures to ensure that that they take care of GBV survivors and vulnerable women and girls after emergency phase of this project. Referral pathway will be strengthened to ensure that GBV survivors are linked to other service providers in the community.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with national objectives on social protection such as the South Sudan National Social Protection Policy Framework developed in 2011 to address different vulnerabilities affecting South Sudanese. NCA intends to provide material support to women and girls and GBV survivors in the form of dignity kits and other support that will be identified with the GBV survivors during the implementation of the project.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

As the protection environment in Aweil East and Aweil North is not yet safe, the project will protect and safeguard the rights and entitlements of vulnerable populations especially women, girls and GBV survivors of all ages by providing targeted case management support using community structures such as churches, women groups and family units which are relatively safe and stable. To ensure stability and safety of GBV survivors and women and girls exposed to protection risks such as armed conflicts, distance to fetch water and firewood, cantonment sites, food security, NCA will strengthen community structures such as women groups, family units and church structures to provide case management and referral services in their locales. Families at risk of food security will be linked with UN agencies and other NGOs working on food security to provide longer term support as part of durable solution. Girls at risk of early and forced marriages will be linked to social support entities such as churches in close cooperation with Ministry of Gender and Social Development. NCA will lead advocacy efforts to improve protection environment by lobbying for removal of cultural barriers which prevent girls to access education. Anti GBV clubs will be formed in schools and in the community as social structures for increasing community awareness on GBV. Women and Girls Friendly spaces will also be established in Aweil East and Aweil North to provide safe spaces for women and girls.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

To ensure sustainability, NCA will strengthen the capacity of civil society structures and duty bearers to ensure continuity of the programme after completion. The co-localisation of NCA programmes in targeted areas will increase the sustainability of the programmes, as complex and multi-sectoral rights deficits will be addressed holistically. NCA will strengthen partners institutional and technical capacities, so they are able to co-lead development interventions, supporting the localisation agenda. Most importantly, NCA will encourage partners to coordinate their activities at sector-level to enhance peer support and cooperation.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

NCA has just concluded a gender analysis for its 2020-2024 strategy which articulates comprehensive approaches for addressing gender inequalities in decision making including holding trainings and outreaches on UNSCR 1325 which underscores participation of women and girls in decision making processes at all levels. NCA has also prioritised implementation of UNSCR 2250 on youth and so will ensure that the public and target population in Aweil East and Aweil North level of awareness on youth participation in decision making is promoted. In addition, a awareness raising and training on code of conduct and set up Complaints Reporting Mechanism, at NCA/partners' field offices will be provided to enhance downward and upward accountability. Throughout implementation period of the project, NCA will carry out conduct trainings on human rights standards for NCA staff and partners , awareness raising on human rights for women, girls, men and boys to understand their rights and entitlements of this project and other NCA programmes in Aweil.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

NCA has a Complaints Response mechanism at the country level which highlights communication modalities with communities. The CRM has contact details of focal points at project, country and HQ level where complaints are handled. There is a working email and telephone number for sensitive complaints. In areas where communication by telephone and email is not possible, NCA CRM focal points will consult with communities on best alternative channels for communication. Before projects are started or implemented, communities will be provided with information about the project, details about its scope, funding, objectives, beneficiaries including how the targeting of the beneficiaries of the project was derived. Implementation methodologies including entitlements and rights to complain will be discussed with the communities.
