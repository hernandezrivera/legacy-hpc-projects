Project Summary

"IRC Bangladesh is one of the crucial health partners of Health Sector in Cox’s Bazar in Rohingya response and providing Maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health (MNCAH), Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH), Non-Communicable Diseases (NCD) including MHPSS and Communicable Diseases (CD) health services from 03 Primary Health Care Centers (23 and 25; Camp 8E is in construction stage), 02 BEmONC Centers (1 in serving in Camp 26; another one is on the process), 17 Referral Hubs (12 active and 5 upcoming), 39 Women Friend Space (WFS) (23 active and 16 are in planning stage), 05 Women and Girl’s Safe Spaces (WGSS) and one (1) 60 beds SARI Isolation and Treatment Center at the camp level (Camp 23).
Barriers faced by women and girls in seeking care, especially BEmONC and SRH services, will be addressed through outreach activities. The services include the ANC, PNC, FP (Both long- and short-term methods), NVD, and emergency referral to Secondary Health Care Centers. IRC Community Outreach officers will work with Community Health Volunteers to increase their capacity to both increase knowledge related to SRH and how gender norms influence health seeking behavior. Male engagement activities in family planning (FP) and SRH will be periodized. Additionally, needs of children, adolescents, youth and old in all their diversity (gender, age, disability, ethnicity) will also be addressed. IRC will focus on Sex, Age and Disability Disaggregated Data (SADDD) for program quality improvement.
The Referral Hub (RH) project of IRC is to ensure patients with emergency obstetric conditions and other medical emergency cases will be able to access to PHCC/BEmONC facilities through rapid referral services. This project is fully funded by UNFPA
Besides, IRC is working in Host Community to support Upazila level health facilities at Teknaf and Ukhia upazila of Cox’s Bazar through logistic and Human Resources. The support is dedicated to support ITCs in these hospitals (30 beds Ukhia and 34 beds in Teknaf; total 64 ITC beds). "

1 - Identified Needs

"UNFPA estimated in October 2019 that around 1200 mothers were at risk of experiencing obstetric complications in the following three months’ time. The recent SRH working group bulletin showed that the health care facilities supported 7,446 facility-based deliveries and out of them 2,211 obstetric complications were reported between January and June 2020 despite COVID-19 situation. The majority of reported obstetric complications were a result of Postpartum Haemorrhage (10%) followed by Severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia related cases (6%). Other complications contributed the remainder of 84% of the obstetric complications. The same bulletin also highlighted the decline trend of ANC as well as PNC visit. Though the ANC visit is increasing steadily, but no improvement on PNC visit was observed so far. The recent MSNA reported that during the survey time only 51% pregnant women were enrolled in an antenatal care (ANC) programme. Due to their cultural and social norms, restricted mobility, hard geographical terrain and difficulty in transportation to health facilities, pregnant Rohingya mothers face barriers in accessing services resulting in a preference for home deliveries (47.6%) Deliveries are conducted by local traditional birth attendants in unsafe and unhygienic conditions. The health sector partners are running 97 health posts in the Rohingya refugee camps along with 38 primary health centers (PHCs) providing 24/7 services. However, there is lack of availability of 24/7 Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EmONC) services.Health Sector Bulletin 10 reported increased uptake of short-acting contraceptives (oral pills and injectable) over long-acting and reversible contraceptives (LARC) and 64,143 women of reproductive age (WRA) received family planning contraceptive methods. The most preferred family planning method was the Oral contraceptive pill (56%) followed by injectable contraceptives (33.6%) and condoms (8.7%). Uptake of FP methods found lower in Teknaf sub-district compared to Ukhiya. The prevalence of STI seems high in Rohingya camp and a qualitative study by Population Council found that Rohingya adolescents and youth had limited knowledge regarding STI and HIV, and have misconceptions regarding spreading of such diseases.Average family size of 4.6 members indicates the congested living condition in the tiny house which can be breeding ground for different infectious diseases. Humanitarian assistance is the main source to cover the basic needs for most of the refugees, and many have settled in hilly, formerly forested areas that are prone to landslides and flash-flooding during the monsoon season. In addition to those, the global COVID-19 pandemic and associated control measures have severely restricted access and service delivery to the highly aid-dependent refugee communities since March 2020, likely exacerbating levels of need.The ongoing pandemic deteriorated the health seeking behavior from bad to worse due to various reasons including the fear of contracting COVID-19 from Health Facilities. Increased difficulties accessing health services have been reported during the pandemic, which increased the feelings of distrust, dissatisfaction and misinformation in both Rohingya and Host Communities. The recent MSNA again highlighted that a significant number of people both from Rohingya refugees and host community are seeking health services from local pharmacy despite having free NGO supported health centers that provide health services free of costs. Cox’s Bazar is one of the poorest 20 districts among the 64 districts in Bangladesh. At the time of writing, there are around 4500 confirmed COVID-19 cases in the district and it has the eighth highest composite risk of exposure to the virus . While there are government health facilities throughout the district, they lack human resources, appropriate water sanitation and hygiene facilities, and medical equipment to service patients and visitors.
