Project Summary

Chad continues to face a protracted and multidimensional humanitarian crisis with a critical refugee displacement in the East coming from Sudan , internal displacements of population due to armed conflicts in the South and West, food insecurity, malnutrition, floods, and disease outbreaks throughout the country. As of 08 October 2024, 677,422 new refugees from Sudan were registered. Given the persistence of conflicts in Sudan and the constant influx of new refugees and returnees, the Chadian government estimates that the number of refugees and returnees could reach 910,000 in Chad by the end of 2024 .
According to recent figures produced by UNICEF, refugees represent 5.8% of Chad's population and 37.3% of the population of the 4 provinces affected by the Sudanese crisis as well as refugee SAM U5 children (39,589) represent 7.6% of the whole SAM U5 children caseload (523,381) nationwide and 33.5% of the SAM U5 children caseload (118,136) of the 4 provinces (Wadi-Fira, Ouaddai, Sila, Ennedi Est).
According to the needs analysis based on SMART 2022, 15 out of 23 provinces are considered priorities for SAM treatment. Three of these provinces have prevalences above the emergency threshold of 15%. These are Wadi-Fira (17.7%), Borkou (16.2%) and Ennedi Ouest (15.7%).
Securing RUTF and other related products is a real challenge and a reputational risk for UNICEF every year. Consequently, the planned pipeline for life-saving nutrition commodities, particularly Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) was put under stress by this increase of children requiring treatment. The situation was exacerbated by a delay in receiving In-kind RUTF cartons and lack of funds to purchase additional RUTF cartons. In addition, the operational capacity of the SAM treatment units in the affected health districts remains a major challenge, with low capacities in terms of human resources, materials, and equipment, particularly for the management of severely acutely malnourished children with medical complications. The inadequacy of human resources in health facilities hinders the holistic treatment of children with SAM, resulting in more child deaths. More health facilities, particularly inpatient facilities, continue to depend on human resources provided by UNICEF and other NGOs.
In order to respond to the populations affected by the so-called "Sahel and Health" crisis, UNICEF proposes to strengthen national capacity to improve access to treatment for 385,447 severely acutely malnourished children in existing nutritional treatment units (OTP) and inpatient units (Sc). Preventive nutrition services will be strengthened at the community and health facility levels. At the community level, the actions will integrate the early detection of children under 5 years of age suffering from acute malnutrition and their referral through mothers and caregivers, while strengthening the capacity of the latter on essential nutrition actions, including optimal feeding practices for young children, in order to prevent all forms of malnutrition. Thus, 1,257 community health workers will be trained on the Mother-MUAC approach as well as on interpersonal communication and awareness techniques and will be responsible for providing coaching to mothers and caregivers on MUAC screening while ensuring their follow-up. The capacities of health facilities with health personnel gaps will be strengthened with competent human resources. The Ministry of Health will be supported to strengthen the coordination mechanism at the national and provincial levels as well as the monitoring and evaluation of interventions through joint supervision missions including End User Monitoring.

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

The nutritional situation remains worrying throughout the country. This worrying nutritional situation, which has lasted for years, is exacerbated by multiple recurrent crises, notably floods, droughts and armed and intercommunity conflicts, with major population movements in the South (CAR crisis), East (Darfur and CAR crisis), Chari Baguirmi, Ndjamena (Cameroon crisis) and the Lake area (GANE attacks, including Boko Haram). Already in 2024, Chad was severely impacted by various crises, leading the Chadian government to declare a food and nutrition emergency throughout the country by decree N°0098 of February 15, 2024. Malnutrition remains a concern in 15 of the 23 provinces, which have exceeded the 10% malnutrition alert thresholds: (Wadi Fira (17.7%), Borkou (16.2%), Ennedi Est (15.7%), Ennedi Ouest (14.4%), Salamat (14.3%), Batha (13.8%), Bahr El Gazal (12.9%), Kanem (12.2%), Ouaddaï (12.1%), Tibesti (11.6%), Sila (11.1%), Lac (11.0%), N'Djamena (10.7%), Guéra (10.6%) and Hadjer Lamis (10.5%). Among these provinces, three (03) have reached and exceeded the very high (Urgency) threshold of 15%. The provinces of Barh El Gazal, Wadi Fira, Salamat and Sila recorded prevalences of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in excess of 2%. The survey revealed that SAM affects children aged 6 to 23 months more significantly (13.3% [11.9-14.9]) than those aged 24 to 59 months (6.0% [5.4-6.8]). This situation can be observed in most provinces, hence the need to reinforce malnutrition prevention actions based on various innovative strategies (1000 days) and focused on this segment of the population. Several other factors are also contributing to the deterioration in the nutritional status of the population in Chad. The most immediate of these are inadequate food intake, disease and poor feeding and care practices in households. Nutritional practices remain poorly adapted, with a negative impact on the nutritional status of children under 5. According to the SMART 2022 survey, one-third (33.8%) of children aged 6 to 23 months have a minimally acceptable diet, and only 7.3% of children under 6 months are exclusively breastfed. Still according to the SMART 2022 survey, 43% of children suffer from acute respiratory infections and 38% from febrile illnesses. This morbidity predisposes them to acute malnutrition, as do recurrent epidemics such as measles, in a context where the vaccination rate (75%) is below the recommended standard (90%). This heightened vulnerability, combined with the absence of health services to treat SAM (less than half of functional health structures treat malnutrition), has irreversible consequences for children, compromising their future and even leading to death. More than 8.4 million people will be affected by food insecurity (phase 2-5) during the lean season (June-August 2024), including nearly 3.4 million in severe food insecurity (phase 3-5), according to CH projections of March 2024. The analysis of refugees in the projected situation (June-August 2024) in Chad shows that of the 1,235,273 people analyzed, 297,544 are in crisis phase (Phase 3) and 122,290 in emergency (Phase 4).Nutrition Cluster projections for Chad show that by 2025, there will be around 1,808,285 children under 5 suffering from acute malnutrition, including 496,499 cases of SAM and 1,311,786 cases of MAM in need of nutritional rehabilitation. There will also be around 270,551 pregnant and breast-feeding women suffering from acute malnutrition. UNICEF will target 496,499 children suffering from SAM (i.e. 100%) in 18 of the country's 23 priority provinces.Most of the provinces located in the Sahelian strip are particularly affected by this situation, exposing children under the age of 5 and women to high vulnerability. An emergency nutritional response is becoming a priority to address unmet needs and prevent associated mortality and morbidity. Activities or outputs- Procurement and timely distribution of 308,358 ready to Use Therapeutic Foods and essential medicine.- Training or refresher training of 2,782 health professionals on the national protocol for the treatment of acute malnutrition and supply management system and on IYCF counselling including accountability for beneficiaries- Provide technical support in provinces with low performance indicators through 8 national nutrition consultants- Conduct SAM screenings through mothers MUAC strategy for early detection and treatment- Provide 99 additional health staff to all the gap of inadequate human resources- Support 9 monthly coordination meetings as a forum to review program implementation and identify issues and challenges at national and provincial levels- Organize 17 quarterly joint monitoring and supervision visits to the nutrition units in collaboration with local health authorities including 6 fields visits from the national level.- Provide communication materials and tools to the supported health facilities (anthropometric equipment, training materials, stock management tools)- Train 1,257 CHWs on interpersonal communication and sensitization.- Sensitization of mothers and caregivers, including men, on essential nutrition actions including best practices of IYCF. Results - 385,447 children suffering from severe acute malnutrition received quality treatment- 1,275,491 Children under five are screened for malnutrition- 2,782 Health centers have improved capacity on IMAM protocol, IYCF and accountability for beneficiaries- Seventeen (17) quarterly joint monitoring and supervision visits to the nutrition units in collaboration with local health authorities are organized including 6 fields visits from the national level.- The national and sub cluster coordination mechanism is reinforced - Communication materials and tools to the supported health facilities (anthropometric equipment, training materials, stock management tools) are ordered and distributed to health facilities.- 1,257 CHWs have improved their capacities on interpersonal communication and sensitization- 631,594 women and caregivers including men of children under 5 years of age improve their knowledge of essential nutrition actions including best practices of IYCF.
