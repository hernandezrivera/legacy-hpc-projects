Project Summary

The general objective of this proposed intervention is to contribute to a comprehensive protection system of vulnerable communities that are exposed to occupation related coercive environment and forcible transfer practices.
This intervention will target Area C Bedouin and herder communities exposed to occupation related critical events, with a specific focus on demolition and forcible transfer practices, Israeli settler violence and excessive use of force by the Israeli Security Forces (ISF) in the North West Bank (NWB) governorates of Nablus, Salfit, Tubas-Northern Jordan Valley (NJV) and Qalqilya and Ramallah. Area B and A hotspots (communities) of the same targeted governorates exposed or directly impacted by incidents of Israeli settler violence and ISF excessive use of force will be also targeted within this intervention.
The MdM intervention will focus on 3 main outcomes as follows:
1- Mitigate the impacts of occupation related violations (settler violence, demolitions and forcible transfer practices, excessive use of force by ISF) on the mental health and psychosocial (MHPSS) wellbeing of Palestinian communities living in the NWB including the NJV and Ramallah Governorate.
2- Strengthen the resilience and positive coping mechanisms of Palestinian communities living in NWB including the NJV and Ramallah Governorate.
3- Third States Parties are mobilized and encouraged to take systematic measures toward occupation practices which negatively impact the health and protection of Palestinians and their effective access to services in the West Bank (WB).
Within this action, MdM will contribute to the Protection Cluster Objectives 1 and 2, both related to the Strategic objective 1 of the HRP.
In relation to Objective 2 of the Protection Cluster “Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence”, MdM intervention will focus on the provision of MHPSS services including MHPSS Emergency Response (ER)psychoeducation, and mainstreaming protection, with a focus on child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV), Protection cases to be supported whether the case is related to occupation or not. MdM will address the health and protection needs of people with disabilities through the adaptation of interventions to their needs, accompany and follow up, and ensure that they are reaching the necessary services. To address community stress and anxiety related to COVID-19 pandemic and to limit the spread of the virus, awareness raising and MHPSS related to COVID-19 will be also mainstreamed within this action.
MdM advocacy efforts will also contribute to Objective 1 of the Protection Cluster “Increased respect for International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL) and accountability for violations”. The advocacy contribution of MdM will be through field briefings to diplomats and international media, production of periodic MHPSS related reports and factsheets to reflect the impact of the violations on the mental health of the Palestinian communities, in addition to a lobby tour with targeted diplomats in Paris and Brussels. MdM will feed and contribute to all advocacy initiatives of the Protection Cluster and its Areas of Responsibilities (AoR) and other forms (Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), AIDA) covering the MHPSS field.
MdM interventions within this action will be provided directly and through an outreach program to all targeted communities, keeping community participation and coordination as one of the main pillars of its intervention. As a medical organization, MdM is working with Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) related to COVID-19 that enable teams to safely implement this outreach program. MdM team members are also trained and prepared to provide awareness sessions on the mental health and psychosocial impact of COVID-19 if needed.

1 - Needs

Through direct outreach, MdM is providing MHPSS (ER) to victims of critical incidents related to occupation since 2012 targeting Nablus, Tubas- NJV, Salfit and Qalqilya governorates in addition to hotspots in Ramallah Governorate in close cooperation and coordination with local authorities, local MHPSS services providers, village councils, Protection Cluster and MHPSS AoR. The impact of coercive environment is significant in terms of fostering a prolonged environment of fear and intimidation, accompanied with waves of different violations related to occupation, including Israeli settlers' violence, demolitions and excessive use of force by ISF. MdM identified a clear increase of critical incidents within the first 9 months of 2021, where MdM intervened in 370 incidents (187 Settlers related, 90 ISF related, 93 Israeli Civil Administration (ICA)) compared to 282 incidents (178 Settlers related, 19 ISF related, 85 ICA) in 2020 within the same area of intervention. There was also a clear increase of community members in need of MHPSS ER. From January to September 2021, MdM provided MHPSS ER to 2465 beneficiaries (792 females, 1031 males, 271 girls and 371 boys), compared to 1,752 beneficiaries receiving MHPSS ER in 2020 (552 Females, 817 males, 185 girls & 198 boys) (MdM MHPSS data, 2021) within the same period and targeted area. Despite the COVID-19 pandemic, the number of incidents has continuously increased and never decreased. Nablus Governorate is still the most affected by incidents (209 incidents), followed by Salﬁt (69) then by Tubas - NJV (48), Qalqilya (24) and Ramallah (20). In 2021, the MHPSS needs increased in oPt likely in link with the long-term nature of the pandemic and the increase of conflict-related violence, including the May 2021 escalation in both Gaza and WB. Communities remain under constant stress, with high rates of anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). Although MHPSS has affected both genders, women are more disproportionately affected than men (UN Women Gender Alert, 2021). Based on MdM data between July 2020 and February 2021, symptoms related to trauma (ﬂashbacks, avoidance and intrusive feelings) were more common among women than men. Crisis situations tend to reinforce patriarchal norms and traditional perceptions with high expectations on the roles of women and girls (maintaining family cohesion, household chores), coupled with a decrease in freedom of movement, already impacted by the occupation. Children and adolescents are also affected by those situations. According to a 2021 study (WHO and Juzoor) on the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the mental health of adolescents, it was revealed that more than 60% of them reported being stress after having self-rated their current level of stress on 4 or above on a scale from 0-6, 6 being maximum stress and 0 being no stress. Marginalized women and girls (including women with disabilities) in Area C are particularly at risk of violence and suffer from higher incidents of GBV. They struggle to have a source of income to cover their basic needs increase their vulnerability and generate complex protection threats including heightened risks of GBV, intimate partner violence, sexual abuse and forced and early marriage (UN WOMEN-Gender alert). Moreover, these communities are generally far from the basic services and tend to have limited availability and access to multi-sectoral protection services. Addressing those needs, MdM will ensure a safe referral system and a close follow up for GBV, Child Protection and People with Disabilities cases. MHPSS ER is important tool to mitigate legitimate distress and diminish the chances to develop severe disorders and is a key intervention in the Palestinian context. In a context of an escalation of critical events, the provision of MHPSS ER is crucial to both minimize the acute impact and to strengthen resilience of communities. A basic principle of MHPSS interventions (IASC guidelines) is to develop a layered system of complementary actions that meets the needs of different groups. MdM promotes access to basic services and safety through referrals, promotes social support and provides direct support through MHPSS interventions. The well-being of victims impacted by critical incidents should be protected through both addressing the basic protection and MHPSS needs, which are equally important. In complement to the above actions aiming to prevent the development of psychological issues, complementary activities aiming to strengthen the resilience and coping skills of individuals and communities are needed. In line with MdM Swiss experience in south WB and the HNO 2022 highlighting the need for training among WB care workers, CBOs and community members on disaster response -including respond to the MHPSS needs of victims during critical incidents, the provision of the first layer of MHPSS services (IASC pyramid) is effective and sustainable. To cover MHPSS needs at community level, CBOs staff are in need of capacity building on PFA, protection minimum standards, child protection, GBV, PSEA and persons with disabilities’ needs. Reinforcement of CBOs staff capacity through training and supervision, material and financial support provided by MdM will enable targeted CBOs on acting as community level MHPSS actor Psycho-education sessions play an important role, as there is still a lack of information and knowledge about how critical incidents, gender-based violence, limited access to education, disabilities, and now COVID-19 pandemic have a heavy impact on mental health and daily functioning of individuals. As reported by Bedouin and herder community of the North WB in 2021, 84% reported stress and fear from the COVID-19 pandemic. To alleviate this stress and to limit the spread of the virus, MdM will integrate COVID-19 MHPSS impacts topic (based on MoH, WHO and CDC latest updates) within the awareness sessions.

2 - Activities

Output 1: Enhanced access to immediate MHPSS and to a holistic protection response for the victims of occupation related critical events. The objective is to mitigate the psychological and psychosocial impacts of the critical incidents related to occupation with a specific focus on Israeli Settlers’ violence, demolition and forcible transfer practices and excessive use of force by ISF on individuals, families and communities. These cases are identiﬁed by MdM from different sources including direct contact with the community, OCHA, CP /MHPSS AoR and other Protection actors. The main activities will be: (1) Support visit within 72 hours, including Psychological First Aid (PFA) for those in need. (2) 2nd Support visit including a Psychosocial Assessment within 10-15 days to those who required individual support in the first visit. (3) Basic and protection needs, child protection, GBV-related needs, the needs of people with disability, will be assessed, referred, documented and followed up. (4) Low intensity psychological interventions for individuals affected by mild psychological problems such as anxious or depressive complaints will be directly provided by MdM team. (5) Referrals to MH specialized services of people with moderate or severe mental health complaints, including in cases of suicide attempt or ideation, in need of advanced support to specialized MHPSS care through local services. Referrals to be done to other MHPSS specialized service providers under the umbrella of the MHPSS AoR or MoH based on the service directory per each geographical area. The Operational SOP and MHPSS ER framework recently developed by MdM serve as the roadmap for standardized intervention during critical events, and this will be updated within this project to address the change in context and needs and to benefit from periodic feedback on its utility by field workers. The validation phase of the SOPs allows review of case management protocols, service directories and referral system and expansion of service delivery if and when the context calls for it. Within all the above-mentioned activities, awareness sessions related to COVID- 19 and its impact on the wellbeing of the individuals will be mainstreamed according to the SOPs currently in use. Awareness about protection risks including child protection, GBV and PSEA will also be included in those sessions. Output 2: Enhanced Resilience capacity of the communities through Community Psychoeducation and MHPSS preparedness at community level: (1) Family/household-based Psychoeducation: the main aim is to make the communities aware about the possible impacts of traumatic events on their wellbeing as well as the resources and services available to alleviate it. These sessions usually address critical incidents impacts on women, men, girls and boys and the potential ways on dealing with it. Moreover, awareness will be raised and mainstreamed on MH, GBV, child protection and speciﬁc needs of people with disability, and COVID-19 related awareness whenever is relevant. Speciﬁc cases will be assessed within MHPSS sessions, identiﬁed and referred accordingly. In order to assure a universal access to MH support, and enable more men to access MdM services (a group that is currently seeking less support in the locations of activity) focus groups will be organized involving key communities’ leaders to assess their needs and shape together with them the most adapted MHPSS response. Households targeted will be identified in cooperation with the village councils, community leaders and CBOs. (2) MdM will map Community Based Organizations (CBOs), 12 CBOs to be selected, 3 per governorate (3 in Nablus, 3 in Tubas, 3 in Qalqilya and 3 in Salfit). Ramallah governorate was excluded as still and new governorate targeted by MdM within 2022. MdM to provide capacity building on provision of PFA, protection minimum standards, child protection, GBV, PSEA and addressing persons with disabilities’ needs. Following the trainings, CBOs trained members will be able to provide Psychological First Aid (PFA) to victims of critical incidents and will ensure a safe referral pathway to MdM secondary line MHPSS support (counselling and PM+), and also external referrals for other services providers. Local CBOs and training curricula will be identiﬁed in cooperation with the CP/MHPSS AOR, and a speciﬁc assessment will be conducted with the CBOs staff before the implementation of this activity. With the capacity building, close follow, material and financial support, the CBOs will be MdM local partner at community level providing the first layer of MHPSS. Output 3: Advocacy initiatives that aim to enhance the accountability for violations of HRL & IHL and the engagement of Third States Parties.MdM collects quantitative and qualitative information to raise awareness of public opinion and mobilize EU decision makers to effectively act according to HRL and IHL standards. The activities will include: (1) Production documentation, reports and testimonies about the impact of critical events on MHPSS wellbeing, awareness and access.(2) Contribution to joint reports (Protection Cluster, MHPSS AoR, AIDA, UN mechanisms).(3) Organization of ﬁeld tours and contributions to decision-makers brieﬁng. (4) Lobby tour(s) in Paris and Brussels MdM MHPSS interventions will be under the umbrella of, and in close coordination with, the Protection Cluster and CP)/MHPSS AoR and in close coordination to local MHPSS services providers as MoH MH unit, the school counseling department of MoEHE, and the child protection unit of the MOSD. All activities conducted by MdM-F and its partners will ensure that staff involved in providing MHPSS ER follow risk reduction measures to prevent the spread of COVID- 19 in the communities and adhere to the procedures to protect themselves and their colleagues from COVID-19 infection following SOPs develop based on WHO, CDC and MoH standards.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

All the implemented activities rely on the modality of assistance “service delivery”. MdM team will directly provide MHPSS emergency response, build the CBOs capacity to provide MHPSS ER and psychoeducation sessions through outreach approach. Based on more than 10 years of field experience in the NWB, this has been identified as the best modality to ensure the access of victims of critical incidents to MHPSS ER services and reinforcing the local community coping mechanisms to better cope and protect themselves from critical incidents through psychoeducation, taking into consideration the different access barriers (stigma, lack of public transportation and closures, check points, areas close to settlements and scattered distribution of communities in area C). Through the mentioned modality of assistance, the impact of critical incidents on the communities will be mitigated and the community resilience and positive coping mechanisms to deal with critical incidents will be reinforced.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

When feedback or complaints including PSEA complaints are received through any of the mechanisms in place, an immediate reply will be provided to the user with a reference number so the user can track the process. The complaint will be processed by the MEAL oﬃcer who will categorize the feedback into 2 main categories: regular feedback/complaint and sensitive feedback. Regular complaint is a non-sensitive complaint which relates to program design and implementation/service delivery, such as changes in location of provided service, working hours of MdM outreach, waiting time to receiving the services, unavailability of service, inclusion of a certain service, etc. Sensitive complaint includes issues related to abuse of authority by MdM staff, SEA related, derogatory/immoral behavior involving verbal abuse/physical assault, discrimination based on race, gender, sexuality or religion, harassment of beneﬁciary or any act that might harm MdM’s reputation, ﬁnancial corruption, misuse/fraud, child abuse and neglect, threats, blackmail. Based on that will take up the feedback to coordination level, if feedback is considered relevant according to MdM criteria, MdM will proceed with further actions, a review ethical committee will be formed (in which members of the community involved will take part) to analyze the case and make decisions. The committee will gather information and conduct hearings and visits. The user(s) who provided feedback will be continuously updated about the process through email or phone, and ﬁnal decisions and actions will be oﬃcially communicated through oﬃcial mechanisms (letter). All complaints will be registered anonymously in a speciﬁc database, periodic analysis of these feedback/complaints will be done and shared with MdM coordination and headquarters to be taken into consideration within program and strategy.MdM MEAL is usually analyzing the data/ feedback and share it on a quarterly basis with the managers, to adapt and improve the activities

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

MdM is currently using its Feedback and Complaint Mechanism (FCM) with all its beneficiaries, community leaders, stakeholders and suppliers, all are usually updated and reaching MdM through the distributed FCM card. The FCM cards are leaflets which highlights that feedback provided by any service user, whether positive or negative and SEA related, will all be addressed by MdM independently from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive services in the future. FCM cards explain the different feedback modalities including a specific mobile and WhatsApp number to receive calls or text messages, a specific email address to receive online feedback, the cards clarify that feedback could be also received through verbal feedback to the field team or by visiting MdM office. The cards specified the working day and hours to submit feedback. Same channels used within the FCM will be used as channels for vulnerable community to address SEA complaints. MdM teams distribute the FCM cards and explain it to the beneficiaries targeting all the individuals who received the MHPSS ER, psychoeducation, village councils and community leaders with whom those activities were coordinated. The different modalities used, enabled the access of persons with vulnerabilities and access barriers as children, women, people with disability, older persons and LGBT to provide feedback and PSEA related complaints to MdM if needed. Following the provision of MHPSS ER, team members are introducing the satisfaction survey to the targeted beneficiaries and obtain their consent to be contacted after the end of the intervention. Within 10-15 days, MdM MEAL team are contacting the beneficiaries, explaining to them again the objective of the satisfaction survey, confirming their consent and applying the survey. The Psychoeducation satisfaction and feedback questions are usually implemented at the end of sessions.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The MdM team, providing MHPSS to victims of occupation-related incidents and vulnerable community members, is a well-trained, supervised and capable to identify and address gender and age diversity including working with disabled persons, the team is updated about the cultural, religious, context and security situation in MdM operational area of coverage including towns, villages and Bedouin communities. MdM interventions are provided through outreach to the households of the victims in order to reduce the exposure of the victims to harm and provide equal access, including to people with disabilities. MdM security department and team are continuously assessing the locations of the activities conducted in the field to ensure safety and security of beneficiaries and MdM team. When MdM team receives a trigger of critical incident from OCHA, Protection cluster, community members, etc, the team ensures gathering the proper and enough information about the incident from different sources to avoid the repetition of the story by the victims and avoid further exposure to trauma. Furthermore, MdM team is coordinating the activities with the village councils and community leaders and cooperates with stakeholders to avoid community tension. Confidentiality and privacy of MHPSS interventions is a crucial principle applied by all MdM team. When conducting interviews, data collection and referring beneficiaries to other services, MdM follows a clear referral protocol developed by MdM and MoH in 2015. MdM is always ensuring that individual sessions are conducted in privacy, including from same family members. MdM is using digital data collection tools and using a barcode system to anonymize the data collected and ensure beneficiaries data is protected. MdM MHPSS team is programing the interventions based on gender needs, experienced female team members to intervene with women and girls and experienced male members to intervene with men and boys. Moreover, they are reaching community members in appropriate time, intervening with women within the morning hours, with children after the school time and with men after working hours. In massive incidents, MdM MHPSS ER team could be mobilized within the evenings and during weekends if needed. Within its case studies and advocacy documentation, MdM is protects the identity of victims by not using identifiable information to avoid the identification of cases. Written consent is received from the beneficiaries for use of testimonies in published materials (even when remaining anonymous). Within its psychoeducation activities, MdM is reinforcing the local community’s positive coping mechanisms and mobilizing the community resources towards community protection. MdM is counting on a functional feedback and complaint mechanisms, beneficiary satisfaction survey and working closely with beneficiaries, health authorities, local MHPSS service providers and community representatives involving them in the design, implementation and monitoring of the action. MdM team is sharing the protection concerns of the individual and communities targeted within its interventions with the relevant protection actors, and in close coordination and cooperation with Protection cluster. Gender based violence and child protection are mainstreamed within MdM outreach activities. In addition, MdM is ensuring all of its staff are sensitized and oriented on our policy on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, sexual harassment, moral harassment and abuse of authority.All activities conducted by MdM-F and its partners will ensure and monitor staffs involved in providing MHPSS ER follow risk reduction measures to prevent the spread of COVID- 19 in the communities and follow the procedures to protect themselves and their colleagues from COVID-19 infection following SOPs developed based on WHO, CDC and MoH standards.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Impartiality, non-discrimination, gender and inclusion of persons with disabilities are adopted by MdM. Within MHPSS ER activities, whenever MdM team receives a trigger about a critical incident, they gather all the related information about the community members affected, communicate with the beneficiaries and set an appointment for the intervention. All of MdM MHPSS ER activities are implemented through outreach approach reaching the community members in their households. Female members of MdM team intervene with women and girls while male members intervene with men and boys, respecting the culture and traditions of the local community and visiting them in proper timing, such as visiting the mothers in the morning hours and fathers after working hours. Using the outreach approach, MdM is ensuring the access of all community members including females, elderly and people with disability to the MHPSS ER services and reduce the risk and community security concerns while moving to obtain the services in other locations. Within its psychoeducation activities, MdM is conducting separate sessions for males and females reaching them by having 2 team members attend each household, 1 female team member to work with females and 1 male team member to work with males. The content of the session is adapted based on the age and gender of the targeted community members visited in their households. Within 2021, MdM reinforced the capacity and supervision of MdM field staff on gender, GBV and inclusion of persons with disabilities. A specific SOP was developed to address the needs of GBV and people with disability cases identified and ensure proper referral, follow up and documentation of these cases. Moreover, identification of child protection, neglect and abuse was reinforced within this intervention. A clear referral system for child protection is established with the Child Protection Networks and other NGOs under the umbrella of the CP/MHPSS AOR. MdM is addressing the needs of the local community with a gender-based approach (gender specific health), for instance, developing different IEC for women, men and children, developing different protocols based upon gender and also addressing all the barriers preventing people with disability to access services. By its outreach program MdM is able to reach the majority of the victims affected by the critical events, by the support provided by a well-prepared logistic department. MdM team is able to reach the scattered Bedouin communities, farmers and shepherds in their communities and place of work. To ensure basic and protection needs, MdM is using several referral approaches through the Protection cluster and its working groups, by liaising with the local authorities or by mobilizing the community resources in cooperation with CBOs and village councils, ensuring access of community members in need for such services. By having an outreach program, MdM MEAL process is taking into consideration the monitoring and documentation of needs per age, gender, vulnerabilities and targeted locations. All beneficiaries referred to any external service are documented and tracked accordingly.
