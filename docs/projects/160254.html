Project Summary

The project “Hygiene, Nutrition and Winterization as Cash as well as Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People in need along the Contact Line, Eastern Ukraine” (14 June 2019-13 June 2021) aims to improve food security, hygiene conditions, preparedness to winter and psychosocial well-being of conflict-affected populations in the 5-km zone along the 'contact line' in Eastern Ukraine (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts).
Whereas the project’s food, hygiene and winterization components are implemented by ASB Country Office Ukraine, the psychosocial support (PSS) is provided by ASB’s local partner, Samaritan Union of Ukraine (SSU).
The project is funded by the Federal Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany.
The winterization component of the project aims at addressing the most urgent winterization needs of the conflict affected populations residing in the 5-km zone near the Line of Contact in Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts (GCA) by providing winterization cash grants to 500 households for the winter season of 2020-2021.

1 - Needs

The conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues to have a negative impact on the socioeconomic situation in Ukraine, particularly in the areas adjacent to the ‘contact line’. Thus, out of 3,5 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in Ukraine, 2,7 million people reside within the 20-km-zone on both sides of the ‘contact line’ (2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO)). The current humanitarian crisis in Eastern Ukraine particularly affects older people, with 30% of the affected population being over the age of 60. According to the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster (FSLC), 1,1 million people in Ukraine are food insecure and/or require livelihood assistance. It is estimated that 13,000 people in GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts are still severely food insecure (2019 HNO Ukraine). The highest proportion of moderately or severely food insecure population (15%) is in the 5-km zone near the ‘contact line,’ compared with 10% within 5-20-km zone and 9% beyond the 20-km zone further away from the ‘contact line’ (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2018). As demonstrated by the WASH Cluster Study 2019, the 20-km zone has a significantly higher proportion of HHs in the poorest category: whereas 22% of HHs within the 20-km zone report they cannot always afford food, this is true for 10% of HHs in the GCA as a whole. For the 20-km zone, food is the main household expenditure, with the costs for heating and utilities being the second largest category of expenses (REACH Economic Security Assessment., March 2019). Household vulnerability increases in winter, when high heating costs negatively affect food security of particularly vulnerable HHs (FSLC’s Guidance Note on Winterisation 2017-2018). In the urban areas, 38% of HHs in the 20-km zone (GCA) rely primarily on mains heating (REACH Economic Security Assessment, March 2019). Therefore, increased heating costs during the winter months pose a serious challenge to vulnerable households, especially food insecure ones. In the 20-km zone along the ‘contact line’ (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblast), income per capita in female-headed HHs is 23% less than in male-headed HHs (REACH Economic Security Assessment, March 2019). In turn, since older people tend to spend most of their income on medicine (55.8%) and food (26.2%), their ability to pay for utilities during the heating season is significantly strained (2018 HelpAge Baseline report). The HelpAge study also demonstrated that 19.8% of older people (73.1% of them women) had debts, including arrears on utilities. Thus, female-headed HHs and older women are particularly in need of winterization assistance. As identified by the Shelter/NFI Cluster partners, winterization assistance is needed by approximately 30,000 vulnerable HHs in both GCA and NGCA. According to the Shelter/NFI Cluster, for the winter season of 2019-20, winterization assistance is required by some 5,531 of vulnerable HHs (or 13,385 people) in GCA. Overall, 50,200 HHs (or 121,485 people) in GCA have need for winterization assistance in 2020. It is estimated by the Shelter/NFI Cluster that only 30% of the people in need of winterization assistance receive state subsidies. Given the importance of covering a gap in preparedness for winter of the vulnerable HHs in the conflict area, the Shelter/NFI Cluster identified winterization assistance as funding priority for the second half of 2019. Therefore, ASB will continue to support vulnerable populations in the communities within the 5-km zone along the ‘contact line’ with cash for winterization. By providing winterization cash grants, the proposed project will contribute to Objective 1 the of the Shelter/NFI Cluster: “Address gaps in essential shelter and NFI needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict affected population through monetised/in-kind assistance and contingency.”

2 - Activities & outcomes

Purpose: to meet winterization needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations in Eastern Ukraine Output: vulnerable households along the ‘contact line’ received cash for winterization Outcome: target households improve their preparedness to winter. Activity 1. Household identification for cash for winterization The beneficiary assessment will be conducted in October-November 2020 to identify 500 particularly vulnerable households (approximately 1000 beneficiaries) in the 5-km zone near the line of contact (GCA). It will take place primarily in Avdiivka, Toretsk and Bakhmutskyi raion in Donetska oblast as well as in Novoaidarskyj and Popasnianskyi raions in Luhanska oblast. Beneficiary selection will be undertaken in coordination with Shelter/NFI Cluster, local authorities and humanitarian agencies operating in the area. ASB will target primarily beneficiaries who meet vulnerability criteria identified by HRP 2019-2020 (i.e. older persons, people with disabilities, female-headed households, IDPs). The vulnerability assessment of beneficiaries in the food and hygiene caseload will help to identify households who are most in need of winterization assistance. Given the multisectoral nature of the project (food, hygiene, winterization), the beneficiary selection methodology is based specifically but not entirely on food security profile of the households. Since caloric requirements increase during the cold season and many food products become more expensive in winter, food insecure people are generally the least likely to afford high heating costs in winter. Therefore, ASB would partially draw beneficiaries of winterization assistance from the caseload of beneficiaries of cash assistance for food and hygiene, thus strengthening synergies between different components of the project. The rest of vulnerable households will be identified based on the referrals from local authorities, Shelter/NFI Cluster and other humanitarian agencies. Activity 2. Distribution of winterization cash grants to cover critical winterization needs The winterization cash grants will be disbursed to 500 households in December 2020. ASB will follow current recommendation of the Shelter/NFI Cluster (as per 2018-2019 Winterization Guidance). 500 vulnerable households will be provided with winterization cash grants to cover the costs for heating and utilities for the period of 3 months (340 USD per household). Due to the exchange rate fluctuations, ASB may reduce number of beneficiaries and/or revise cash value. Within the framework of the previous project “Emergency Assistance to Conflict Affected Populations in Eastern Ukraine” (2018-2019), which was implemented by ASB in cooperation with ACTED and SSU, 99% of winterization beneficiaries reported satisfaction with the cash modality of assistance. For the population in the 5-km zone near the line of contact (GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts), the most preferred modes of delivery for cash assistance are bank account (48%) and post office (32%) (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2018). Thus, the modes of cash disbursement used by ASB (Oshchadbank and UkrPoshta) are in line with the preferences of the affected populations. Due to the home delivery of cash by Ukrposhta, older people and persons with disabilities would not need to travel to bank to withdraw their cash grants. Post Distribution Monitoring will be undertaken to measure the utilization of the aid provided. A hotline feedback mechanism will be available throughout the project to ask questions, request assistance, provide feedback and lodge complaints. All complaints will be recorded in the Complaints Registration Database for follow-up. All assessments, including PDM, are designed to allow age, gender and disability disaggregation.

3 - Indicators & targets

The main indicators and targets are the following: • 85% of beneficiaries are satisfied with the aid provided; • 85% of beneficiaries report that the assistance was easily accessible; • 70% of beneficiaries report that they are aware of the complaint mechanisms; • 500 vulnerable households receive cash for winterization; • 60% of program beneficiaries are female; • 30% of beneficiaries are older people; • 10% of beneficiaries are persons with disabilities and/or homebound.
