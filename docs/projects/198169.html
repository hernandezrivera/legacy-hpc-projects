Project Summary

The North West and South West regions have been experiencing serious socio-political crisis that have stirred up humanitarian needs. The crisis has had serious negative effects on water, hygiene and sanitation with mostly women and girls highly affected, as they are the ones who access WASH facilities often. The breakdown of the WASH systems and extreme hardship in the crisis affected regions plays negatively on their access to portable drinking water, better hygiene and sanitation services and affects their dignity and safety. This breakdown of WASH systems and the inability of these vulnerable population to afford basic WASH services reduces the regional and global contribution to achieving Sustainable development Goal 6, which aims to achieve universal access to water and hygiene for all by 2030. According to OCHA SITREP as of November 5, 2021, insecurity in the North West and South West regions displaced over 4,724 people in the NWSW to the West and Littoral regions, and 573,900 IDPs within or displaced from NWSW. These displaced persons in the West and Littoral regions lack meaningful and inclusive access to WASH facilities and services. This is because lots of humanitarian support target mostly the North West and South West, while a greater population of the displaced persons in the Littoral and West are not reached with humanitarian WASH response services. The humanitarian WASH needs are further compounded with the health crisis of COVID 19, cholera outbreak and monkey pox which have hit the already crisis affected communities in the North West and South West regions. The year 2022 have been the second most affected year by cholera for the last 25 years with more than 13,000 cases and 284 deaths of cholera reported. It has affected 8 out of 10 regions including refugee camp and IDP sites (HNO 2023),
The 2023 Humanitarian Need Overview report reveals that, insufficient investment of Cameroon Government in basic social services and the insufficient response provided by Humanitarian actor in the Northwest and South west regions continue to exacerbate water and sanitation needs of affected population including the neighboring regions (Littoral and West regions). This report reveals that 55% of assessed village collect water from an unimproved water point. Access to safe sanitation remain a major concern, although open defecation is low (2.4%), utilization of pit latrine without slap is predominant (78.8%) among assessed villages. This represents a high risk of contamination of surface and groundwater used by population in these localities. It is therefore imperative for a good WASH system to be developed in the communities with poor and insufficient WASH facilities and services so as to prevent cholera and provide equitable and inclusive access to water, hygiene and sanitation. The project in its response will prioritize the safety and dignity of the targeted women and girls, as well as affected host population and will reduce the risk and exposure to gender-based violence and other violence related to accessing WASH facilities. The project will be implemented in the South West (Limbe 1, Manfe, konye and Kumba 1 subdivisions), North West (Tubah, Santa, and Bamenda 1 subdivisions), and Littoral (Douala 4 and Djombe/Penja subdivisions). The project targets 24000 vulnerable women, girls and host population with hygiene promotion messages, 6900 families with complete WASH kits through all modalities, 4000 vulnerable women and girls with menstrual hygiene kits, 20 water points will be constructed/rehabilitated in communities with difficult access to safe drinking water, 1800 women, girls and host population will be sensitized on how to access humanitarian assistance, provide feedback and complaints to humanitarian actors. The HNO 2023 report reveals 1.08 million people in NW, SW, Lit and West region are in need of humanitarian assistance. This project will provide WASH assistance to 24000 vulnerable women, girls and families.
