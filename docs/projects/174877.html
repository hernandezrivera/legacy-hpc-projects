Project Summary

Tdh is proposing an 8-month intervention in Dikwa and Pulka LGAs,Borno State to respond to child protection needs. The goal of the project is to reduce the multiple protection risks and violations girls and boys in north-east Nigeria continue to face. The threats and risks posed by these protection risks have been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic which has not only negatively affected delivery of life-saving child protection services but has also weakened the capacities of families and communities to care for children (Child Protection 2021 Humanitarian Response Plan in northeast Nigeria). These services include 1) providing comprehensive case management services for girls and boys facing protection risks, including adolescents and children with disabilities; 2) provision of quality mental health and psychosocial support services and information on childcare and protection for conflict-affected children, including adolescents and children with disabilities, and; 3) strengthening community-based services and prevention actions benefit conflict-affected boys, girls and young people.
Tdh is recognized in the NE as a major CP actor with strong expertise and field work experience in Maiduguri, Monguno, Dikwa, Mafa and Pulka Through its work, Tdh has developed excellent working relationships with local authorities, and is well accepted by community members. Tdh will use this leverage to tailor this intervention and maximise its impact and beneficiary reach.

1 - Needs

Pulka (in Gwoza Local governmental area) is located 15 km east of the Cameroonian border. Pulka comprises of a combination of the host community, IDPs and returnees. The estimated population of Pulka is 67,487 people (56% IDPs and 44% host community). According to an assessment carried out by TdH, the protection/child protection needs are high. In Pulka, there are four camps with only two out of six actors delivering Child Protection interventions. From locally conducted Focus Group Discussion, hawking is perceived as the biggest difficulty faced by children in this location, preventing them from accessing education. Children spoke about the trauma from witnessing Armed Opposition Groups killing people, the sight of dead bodies and violence, often running for their safety. In such instances, there is an identified need for PSS activities to provide children and adolescents with safe environments. Dikwa is an accessible area that was previously a stronghold of Armed Opposition Groups. Returnees are centered around three camps and mainly come from neighboring LGAs and the wider Maiduguri area. 65% of IDPs live with host communities, putting additional strain on already difficult living conditions. In terms of (Child) Protection, there are very high needs, with many cases of forced recruitment, including children, girls forced to marry Armed Opposition Group members and killings as a result of the conflict. Based on recent Focus Group Discussions with local community members, the biggest perceived difficulties faced by children were hunger and lack of drinking water, particularly because of the high number of children in the location. Safety also presented as an issue with children recalling instances of abduction and 'luring' by Armed Opposition Groups. However, they identified CFS facilities as safe spaces in which they can congregate within their communities. Also, many returnees report forced returns, family separation, maltreatment, harassment, or physical assault, leading to a high need for PSS and (Child) Protection in general

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project Tdh will prioritize the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries throughout all stages of the project cycle in line with the Do No Harm principle and assisting the most vulnerable. Both vulnerability and concerns of the affected population (men, women, boys and girls), have been analysed and taken into account in the program design phase and will be considered during implementation ensuring that activities proposed are specifically designed to meet the needs of different age and gender groups. Beneficiary criteria will be discussed and disseminated within communities to ensure that vulnerable children with specific needs are reached. The proposed intervention will mainstream protection by addressing protection risks and the needs of children, strengthening community-based child protection mechanism to build community preparedness, and mitigate protection risks. The project will also emphasize the safety and dignity of beneficiaries by maintaining confidentiality and ensuring their participation and involvement in project implementation. The project will also promote inclusion through the involvement of all children, adolescents and caregivers in project implementation as per the Core Humanitarian Standards: which stipulates the necessity of receiving assistance that is appropriate and relevant to needs; at the right time; not negatively affected; communities knowing their rights and entitlements; have access to information and participate in decisions that affect them. The project will ensure access to information and build the capacity of children to enhance their knowledge on their rights, child protection issues, where to seek help. As it has been highlighted, the highest risk faced by adolescents are gender based violence, according to their caregivers. Tdh will implement this project, following the guiding principles of the GBV subsector.Tdh will honor safety and security, by ensuring the safety of the child survivor, and will maintain the awareness of safety and security of people who are helping the survivor. Tdh will also respect the confidentiality of the survivor, child, and family at all times. Relevant information on the case will be shared with other humanitarian actors for helping purposes if and only if the survivor agrees to it. Tdh will indeed seek his or her consent before engaging in any response or sharing of information. If the survivor is not able to read and write, he/she will provide his/her consent verbally. Tdh will also comply with the respect of the individual: he/she will be provided with information on options available to them and Tdh will maintain a non-judgment behavior. Tdh will provide its support to GBV cases without discrimination based on age, sex, religion, ethnicity, language, etc.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Tdh will ensure the active participation and involvement of beneficiaries in all stages of the project. Tdh will ensure accountability to affected populations using various consultative processes (community meetings, suggestion boxes, interviews, focus group discussions). Monthly project review and feedback sessions will be organized with beneficiaries to inform them on the progress and to collect their views about project implementation. These review sessions will be strengthened within the existing Community Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) in each community. Tdh shall ensure the intervention is inclusive ,, gender-sensitive and that the project addresses the protection and psychosocial needs of both girls and boys, including those with disability, and contribute to address systemic challenges to girls and women inclusion that is prevalent in the NE. Tdh gender-balanced teams that speaks the local languages shall ensure the information about the project is disseminated through community leaders. By doing so the target population will receive clear, relevant and timely information ensuring effective participation in project activities. Information on relevant services including referral pathways will be shared in coordination with the Protection team and CP teams. This will be realized through regular consultation and information sharing with beneficiaries. .
