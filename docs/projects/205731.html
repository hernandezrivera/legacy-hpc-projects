Project Summary

This project is for twelve-month and will be implemented in Awerial, Pibor, Yei, Lainya, Kajokeji, Renk, Malakal, Mayendit, Rubkona, Leer and Yirol East counties designed to provide quality education to children especially adolescent girls and young women including those living with disabilities, affected by floods, food insecurity and conflict. The interventions will target 20585 as direct beneficiaries (1000F,1020M and 9282G, 9283B). The men and women targeted by this project will be within the age range of 18-60 years while the school age going learners targeted are those between 3-17 years and out of this target, at least 15% of adults, youth and children will be People Living with Disabilities (based on the cluster recommendation). The intervention will mainly focus on ECD, Primary and Alternative Education Program and secondary school levels of learning in all targeted locations. The project is mainly designed to create access of learners to schools and inclusive learning centers while ensuring that barriers like poor access to schools, lack of scholastic materials, school retention for girls and WASH facilities for children's education are addressed. This project will also improve the quality of education by building the capacity of teachers through continuous professional development tailored to increase their teaching skills. In addition, the capacity of local education authorities such as County and Payam Education Supervisors and Inspectors will be strengthened by training them on better skills of school supervision, report writing and how to develop strategic plans alongside equipping their offices. This will improve the already ravaged system of education in the counties and Payams and ensure delivery of quality education services to schools is attained. Also the project shall distribute teaching and age-appropriate learning materials to both teachers and learners targeted since a lack of school supplies have been identified as one of the factors causing poor attendance and learning outcomes in schools. Moreover, the project intends to create better learning environments in schools through construction of temporary inclusive learning spaces to cater for all learners including CWDs. Additionally, the program plans to renovate those schools that have been destroyed by conflict and floods to enable learners access education in targeted locations and also ensure that gender segregated latrines and WASH facilities are established in schools lacking such facilities to address issues of girl’s hygiene while attending classes. Also distribution of sanitary materials to adolescent girls alongside training them on MHM and SRHR issues will be targeted by this intervention such that girl’s attendance and retention in schools is improved. The project will further establish Roll Models in the community who shall engage community members on importance of education of children especially the girl child. This will be done in close coordination with the PTAs/SMC who will be trained on school development as well as community outreach activities to engage parents and guardians of school children and youth on education related issues

1. Needs

The HRP 2023 estimated 3.4 million school children lack access to quality education services. Children & youth aged 3-17 years represent more than 40% of the population of which most are girls. The current trend in girls’ enrolment in schools is alarming because parents prefer boys to attend school which increases the risk of girls ending up in early and forced marriage. In addition, schools have limited learning kits, learning spaces as well as qualified teachers posing a challenge in accessing quality education. The closure of all learning institutions due to C19 in 2020 had devastating effects on the education of children and jeopardized critical learning gains made over the years. The South Sudan C 19 Preparedness and Response Plan 2020 highlighted that the school closure affected over 4 million children from accessing learning institutions adding more challenges to the already fragile education system in the country. The IRNA & UNHCR reports 2023 highlighted that Upper Nile and Unity state regions will be flooded with returnees and refugees from the Sudan conflict. The reports estimate 600,000 people will require humanitarian assistance and out of this number, school age children including girls and women are part of the population in need of support. Unfortunately, the initial response by partners to the victims of Sudan conflict targeted protection, NFIs, health and water interventions leaving out education services. This means, there will be huge need to invest in education in response to the population from the war-ravaged Sudan. To make matters worse, schools have been sub-merged by water in Unity state according to the Rapid needs assessment conducted by Plan International coupled with insecurity. According to the IRNA report 2021 in Lake state, Awerial continue to accommodate IDPs from Jonglei state as a result of floods and this additional population has stressed the available education facilities pausing further challenges to the already inadequate education services in the area. In addition, Yirol East also experienced similar IDPs from Unity state due to floods which has increased the need of school infrastructure, WASH in schools as well as school supplies.The IRNA report of 13 Sept 2023 reveals that 2038 (1100F 938M) children of school age are displaced in Southwest of Kajokeji due to land dispute between the neighboring Aringa tribe of Uganda and Kajokeji residents. The land wrangles started in 2022 and the two governments held diplomatic discussions to resolve the issue. Unfortunately, The Uganda community was not satisfied with the outcome and as such, the Aringa people fully occupied parts of Bori Boma displacing thousands of Kuku people of South Sudan. The report highlights that the school going age children displaced lacked education services including school supplies, learning spaces and qualified teachers. In addition, a multi sectoral needs assessment conducted by Plan International in Dec 2021 reveals that Yei, Kajokeji and Lainya counties had experienced continued conflict which affected children to access education services which composed of more than 15909 returnees of which over 50% are children of school going age and women who were returning from Uganda and Congo putting pressure on education needs. The assessment stipulated that over 40% of the schools are not functional hence limiting children's access to education facilities especially girls. The Greater Yei Humanitarian Monitoring Report Aug 2021 indicates that inclusion remains an issue as CWDs are less prioritized for education and regarded as curse in families. Education quality is affected by unskilled teachers in all the eleven counties and yet teachers are poorly remunerated by the government demotivating them further coupled with MoGEI limited capacity to supervise teachers (UNESCO 2021). Additionally, most schools do not have functional sex segregated latrines and yet the WASH facilities are not friendly for CWDs.

2. Project activities

The project activities and objectives are aligned to the needs on the ground but also based on the HRP 2023 as well as those of education cluster. In addition, this project will be implemented and monitored using United Nations Girls Education Initiative (UNGEI) gender-based toolkit to enable girls' barriers to education be addressed. Overall Objective: To increase access to quality, safe and inclusive education for IDPs, returnees and host community children and youth affected by hunger, conflict and floods in Awerial, Pibor, Yei, Lainya, Kajokeji, Renk, Malakal, Mayendit, Rubkona, Leer, Yirol East counties of Jonglei, Lakes, Unity, Upper Nile and Central Equatoria states as targeted by the education cluster. Objective 1: To strengthen community engagement and support in fostering children access to quality education through enhanced community participation and management of learning institutions. Objective 2: To increase enrolment of learners especially girls and CWDs in all schools of Awerial, Pibor, Yei, Lainya, Kajokeji, Renk, Malakal, Mayiandit, Rubkona, Leer, Yirol East.
