Project Summary

The intervention aims to target vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs) (displaced, evacuated, or returnees) and host community in Northwest Syria (NWS). The overall objective of this project is to ensure that vulnerable children and their families, who have been forced to flee inside Syria are able to meet their basic needs, and are protected. Save the Children (SC) will scale up the acute response to continuous displacement as result of the active conflict in NWS. Through the rapid response mechanisms, SC and partners will deliver critical services through different modalities (cash or in-kind). Note that based on the needs, Multi-purpose cash grant (MPGs) and/or Ready to Eat Rations (RTERs) could be distributed in camps and different IDP sites hence this is to be also consulted with Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Cluster. The MPGs 1,400 (FMoFA) and 2,100 (Danida), and 1,300 (DRA) and 2500 (ECHO) alongside the RTERs: 7,300 one-off is distributed to recent and most economically vulnerable IDPs HHs who are in need of urgent assistance, and 2,500 HHs receive one-time food assistance in the form of RTERs. Specific activities include: monitoring displacements and returns; coordinate food assistance needs with the Food Security Cluster; conducting a rapid market assessment to ensure no negative unintended consequences with a cash distribution; conduct rapid needs assessment to determine appropriate mechanism; registering beneficiaries; refer Child Protection (CP) cases to appropriate services; distribute relevant and appropriate support (e.g. MPG, or RTER) to beneficiaries; conducting Post-Distribution Monitoring; and training staff and community members on child safeguarding, safe identification and referral; CP Case Management supervision and coaching; identifying and refer most vulnerable children to CP team and other services.
The activities under Sector Objective 1 aim to give access to livelihood opportunities to vulnerable households (HHs), while at the same time improving local infrastructure relevant for Agriculture and, more broadly, the livelihoods sector. Activities include the provision of short-term labour opportunities to skilled and unskilled workers, supporting 400 HHs with small business grants and/or opportunities. These activities are complementary to the Agricultural- and Livestock-based activities included in the submission for the FSA sector.
Temporary labour opportunities will involve the completion of essential works, both those prioritised by the local council and benefitting the wider community in the target locations, particularly in relation to agricultural infrastructure (e.g. seed storage, agricultural roads, livestock water points, etc.) as well as providing increased income to the households participating in the Action.
To enable targeted f/m youth to rebuild their livelihoods and strengthen the capacity of local markets to build strengthen community assets and infrastructure, SC will carry out a gender responsive, disability and age appropriate training for Syrian youth and adolescents that increases their capacity to make their own livelihoods decisions and enables them to increase their self-reliance and improves their opportunity to participate in safe income generation opportunities to meet their basic needs and thus avoid reliance on negative coping mechanisms for survival. These trainings will occur in the youth centers, and the increased livelihoods opportunities will be uniquely tailored to girls and boys youth and adolescents alike based on the findings of the assessments.
SC will carry out livelihoods/market assessment and provision of life skills, financial literacy and management entrepreneurship trainings that will support youth and especially young women to runincome generating activities which will improve their income and hence contribute to meeting their household basic needs.

4 - Needs

Since 1st December 2019, more than 312,000 people have fled their homes in southern Idlib, moving further north to seek refuge from escalating violence. It is estimated that over 150 people have been killed – including at least 36 children – and hundreds more have been injured. The recent bombardment of Idlib has devastated communities, as homes, schools, hospitals and mosques have been damaged or destroyed. Ma’arrat An Nu’man and its countryside is almost empty, while thousands more from the Saraqab area fled in fear of hostilities extending to their area. Many families had to leave their homes in the space of hours, without any belongings. Others carried with them what they needed to survive, believing they would not return. In some areas, sustained airstrikes and shelling mean children and families have not eaten or slept for several days. They urgently need food, water, shelter, winter clothes and healthcare. With hundreds of thousands of families already forced from their homes since April 2019, this latest escalation has pushed the number of displaced people in North West Syria to over 700,000. As camps and nearby communities are already overcrowded, many children and families have been left with nowhere to go. The winter weather is adding another layer of urgency to the situation. Many families are seeking shelter in unfinished buildings, leaving them vulnerable to harsh weather conditions, and camps and informal settlements are being damaged by frequent flooding.In NE, Hassakeh governorate's subdistricts primarily face severity needs of 4 out of 6. SC in this sense will target host communities and displaced camp populations based on the severity in the subdistricts. This is also the case in NW Syria, where Idleb and Aleppo subdistricts will be determined based on the scale and severity of displace Syrians' needs

5 - Implementation of activities

MPGs provide relief to households most economically deprived and at increased protection risk. SC's experience in NWS indicates that unconditional MPGs is an effective way to assist newly displaced people to meet basic and urgent needs, while preserving dignity. SCI will target newly displaced families through ONE-OFF MPGs based on the vulnerability and eligibility criteria set by the cash working group. Also, SCI has a standard operating procedure in place to guide the selection and distribution process. Households are unique and beneficiaries will not include those who have previously received cash grants from SCI. The assistance will provide immediate stability to households in a cost-efficient manner as the families themselves can make informed choices. However, the modality of the cash programming will be determined according to the findings from rapid assessments. The response will align with the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) value adopted by the Cash Based Response Working Group, USD 120 per household in Idleb and Aleppo for supporting a six-person household for a month.The RTER response provides food relief to recently displaced households with deprived resources, through the distribution of dry and canned food. RTER packages do not require cooking and can be consumed straight away. For this reason, RTERs immediately support newly displaced populations. In line with food security sector standards, each RTER provided contains 2,100 calories per person per day and will include: canned chicken, meat, tuna, beans, dried thyme, dates, olive oil and cake designed to be sufficient for five to six family members for five days. Following the identification of the specific target locations,SC's partner assessment teams will conduct household (HH) assessments. The eligibility criteria aims to identify families that are displaced, having no productive assets, with no income and affected by the shock, and faces multi-sectoral vulnerabilities and needs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. To mitigate tensions in the communities, it is important with clear communication about the objective of the programme, the necessity of targeting the most needy and vulnerable amongst the affected population, having clear targeting criteria that can be understood by all, while also being open for dialogue and input and maintaining an effective complaints- and feedback mechanisms. 2. SC and partners continuously monitor the security situation and puts emphasis on selecting the right time and sites for distributions, distributes tokens (value vouchers) to recipient households and does not handle any cash as such. Value vouchers are, after verification against ID and beneficiary list, exchanged for USD by the Money Transfer Agent at the distribution site. 3. SC and partners have put in place a system of checks and balances ensuring an ascertainable registration- and verification process and where payments are supervised by independent parties. 4. SC monitors exchange rates but some value fluctuations must be expected under the circumstances. In situations with large fluctuations, in-kind might be a better response modality than value vouchers. 5. With the large escalation in IDP numbers, lack of overall cash availability is an anticipated risk that is best mitigated through cash distributing agencies aligning their response plans, under the guidance of the Cash Working Group, to reduce the pressure on the money transfer system.Regularly, SC interviews partners and staff to identify areas for increased risk mitigation and develop action plans. SC works with partners to strengthen their internal controls. An initiative was launched by SC in conjunction with OCHA to provide external training on international humanitarian law and negotiation with armed actors.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(S&S) SC’s partners engage with relevant stakeholders and receive timely updates. SC works on a common response to unacceptable conditions. Airstrikes SC supports partners to immediately report security incidents to be analysed and to develop and implement comprehensive safety plans. Loss of access SC and partners conduct thorough security assessments and maintain updated contingency plans. If necessary, SC will suspend programs and work to resume activities. If this is not viable, SC would assess possibilities for relocation. Only in a worst-case scenario would project termination be considered. Work permits Measures are in place to mitigate the Gov. of Turkey (GoT) legislative environment hindering SC’s ability to operate, I.e. using a third-party HR company to overcome work permit challenges. Diversion of resources to terrorist/ proscribed groups In 2018, interviews were conducted to identify areas for increased risk mitigation; an action plan was developed. SC launched an initiative with OCHA to provide external training on international humanitarian law and negotiation with armed actors. Partners have their own systems of anti-terrorism vetting checks for all contracted suppliers and SC’s Syria office vets against the third-party database for procurements $750+. Attacks by armed groups Security assessments are conducted on potential project sites. Proximity to military locations is monitored and a back-up vehicle is used in high-risk areas. Staff transit must be pre-approved and take place during daylight. Disruption to cash transfer operations In NWS, NGOs have seen temporary suspension of Bab Al Hawa and have faced challenges in money transfer due to GoT restrictions. Assumptions Qualified staff available for recruitment Cross border access and maintenance of one-year extension of UNSC
