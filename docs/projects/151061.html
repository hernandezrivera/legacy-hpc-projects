Project Summary

Overall Objective: Addressing protection concerns of the most vulnerable conflict-affected populations and strengthening protection systems and local capacity The project outcome are: 1) Capacity of conflict-affected communities in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblast GCA is strengthened on identification and response to protection concerns through integrated community-based psycho-social support (CBPS) and improved community cohesion; 2) Protection concerns of at risk women and girls, survivors of Gender-based violence (GBV) in Donetsk Oblast NGCA, are addressed and risk factors reduced through the provision of tailored protection assistance and awareness-raising; 3) Access to protection and to resilience mechanisms of the most vulnerable conflict-affected women and men, boys and girls in Donetsk Oblast NGCA is ensured through a holistic approach and targeted protection assistance; 4) Humanitarian accountability to affected populations is enhanced.

Needs

The conflict in Eastern Ukraine has entered into its fifth year. The affected population along the contact line continues facing physical dangers as well as lack of access to basic services and essential goods. Exposure to continuous shelling with no inadequate psychosocial support has serious psychological implications on personal health and may lead to more sustained psychosocial problems if not addressed timely and systematically. Availability of relevant services is limited and those available have been severely weakened and cannot absorb all people in need of assistance.With coping mechanisms depleted and broken support networks, the conflict-affected populations risk of becoming even more vulnerable. International and national actors have been continuously providing protection assistance. However people keep facing various protection issues and concerns, due to multiple reasons including insignificant attention to the protection issues and funding. Psychosocial distress is widely reported among all age and gender groups on both sides of the contact line, and psychosocial support remains one of the most requested forms of assistance from conflict-affected people from both sides. Similarly, In NGCA, civilians residing in the proximity to the contact line similarly continue facing physical insecurity as well as acute material needs such as food, shelter, medicine and hygiene. Unemployment and absence of prospects for the working age population, specifically in NGCA results in exhaustion of coping mechanisms and savings, contributing to their extreme vulnerability. The protracted crisis and linked with it posttraumatic stress disorders force many to resort to negative coping mechanisms. Use of psychoactive substances and alcohol has drastically increased in NGCA, predominantly by men leading to aggressive behavior, gender based and sexual violence. According to data from VARIANT, the TGH partner, 83% of men interviewed by the organization practice aggressive behaviors, of which 65% cases were caused by use of psychoactive substances and alcohol. 87% female respondents and 42% teenagers reported having suffered from violence without realizing it. GBV awareness is low and GBV cases are not properly responded to. Furthermore, tailored training is required to enhance capacity of service providers in identification of GBV cases and their referrals. Currently, no organization in NGCA is dealing with the gender-based violence issues.Furthermore, several assessments conducted by “Variant” demonstrate that key protection concerns for the affected population in NGCA remain challenges related to the receipt and restoration of identity and civil documents, access to social benefits and issues linked to housing, land and property rights. Continuous support is required to support vulnerable people including men and women, boys and girls in addressing their multiple protection concerns, negative effects of the conflict on their health and well-being.

Activities & outcomes

Outcome 1. 1. Building and capacitating the community CBPS experts: Workshops for community activities on the concept of CBPS and protection. Selection of target communities based on TGH, partners, and cluster assessments and recommendations. Selection of CBPS community experts. Gender balance will be prioritized in the selection. ToT with focus on in-depth knowledge about CBPS; Do No Harm approach to CBPS, tailoring CBPS activities to the needs of communities, care programmes for humanitarian staff, prevention of professional burnout; two reflective workshops and regular mentoring and supervision of CBPS experts. 2. Assessment of community needs in psycho-social support, prioritization against jointly agreed criteria, ensuring inclusion of the most vulnerable groups. Mobilization and capacity building of target communities in CBPS: Forming initiative groups and trainings on community on protection principles, the concepts of psychosocial well-being, positive stress and trauma coping mechanisms, gender and conflict sensitivity, do-no-harm principles, basic communication skills, etc. Sensitivity to gender, age, vulnerability and conflict issues will be stressed in the development of community action plans for CBPS. The CBPS activities may include community events, restoration works and livelihoods initiatives, basic psychological support, trainings for children, counselling support for parents, referrals to professional services, etc. 3. Implementation of target communities’ action plans: Community action plans will be implemented focusing on addressing protection issues and building and restoring confidence and trust within and among communities. Provision of grants to IGs (500-1000 USD per community (total of 20 communities)) for implementation of community action plans including community activity, festivals, groups discussion, etc. 4. Promoting CBPS best practices: Review meetings will be conducted with the participation of community members, CBPS experts, relevant authorities to discuss and document CBPS best practices and lessons learned. Outcome 2. 1. GBV awareness campaign and referral pathways: Capacity building of medical staff in identification of GBV cases and provision of psychological support through series of initial training. GBV awareness information materials developed and distributed in 20 medical and social protection departments. Referrals to multidisciplinary project support and/or relevant service providers. 2. Multidisciplinary care for GBV survivors: Care will be provided by the qualified project team comprised of medical specialists, psychologists, and social workers. Development of individual cases; including group and individual counselling. Provision of motivation package (Food and/or Hygiene). Multidisciplinary care (individual and group counselling) of children affected by family-based violence. 3. Regular focus group sessions for 600 men practicing aggressive behaviour including among ex-combattant staff and persons in detention facilities displaying high levels of aggressiveness. Individual consultations will be provided as required. Provision of motivation package (Hygiene items). 4. Referral of children affected by family violence. (Care will be provided by the qualified project team comprised of medical specialists, psychologists, and social workers). Development of individual cases, including individual and group counselling. Provision of motivation package (Food and/or Hygiene items). Outcome 3: Addressing protection concerns, legal, documentation and PSS support to 600 most vulnerable and excluded individuals affected by the conflict from Donetsk, Debaltsevo, Dokuchaevsk, Gorlovka, Enakievo, Zhdanovka, Kirovskoe, Snezhnoe, Torez, Shakhtersk, Yasinovataya. Provision of motivation package (Food and/or Hygiene items). Outcome 4: Feedback mechanisms operational. Affected beneficiaries participate in all stages of the PCM. Trainings on the Core Humanitarian Standard.
