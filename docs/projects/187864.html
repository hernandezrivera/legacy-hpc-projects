Project Summary

SO1. This project aims to contribute to increased food security for vulnerable displaced and host community households in Idleb and Aleppo Governorates. It has 2 main components Household Food Security and Bakery Support. Within the Household Food Security component NGO X targets an average of 55,656 HH/ 278,280 food-insecure individuals per month with food kits, cash for food, or value vouchers. The regular food assistance is to the value of 70% of the food SMEB (Survival minimum expenditure basket) or 70% of the Sphere minimum standards of 2100 kcal/p/day.
The Bakery Support component provides 601,655 individuals living in supported bakeries’ catchment areas with bread at a reduced and stabilized price. This is achieved through subsidising up to 40% input (flour and yeast) of bread production thus supporting local bakeries to produce a stable quantity of bread that meets local needs. This support is conditional upon the selling of this bread in the local markets at a reduced and stabilized price agreed with and monitored by NGO X’s Cash and Markets, and Monitoring Teams. Approximately 33 bakeries are targeted to benefit from this assistance.
SO3. Also, NGO X will be conducting a rehabilitation activity for 5 bakeries in both of Idleb and Aleppo, the component will be used as an emergency response to the contracted bakeries under the clustering objective of SO3.
NGOX will implement the proposed project in partnership with 4 NGOs. Please note that these 4 NGOs will input into the OCHA HPC separately under this project. This decision was agreed between all parties and submissions have been coordinated to ensure there is no duplication with regards to either budget or beneficiary targets.
NGO X has secured 52% of funding for 2022 for this project, and initial approval for the remaining 48%, however, contracting is not yet complete. No funding is yet secured for 2023, but with high likelihood of doing so. NGO X has permission to operate in all proposed locations.

4 - Needs

Macro-economic challenges in 2021, combined with the continued impact from the COVID-19 global crisis, and on top of the catastrophic impacts of more than a decade of conflict, have had a major impact on food security in Northwest Syria. The Syrian pound has undergone a 70-fold depreciation since 2011, while the Turkish lira (TRY), a commonly used currency in NWS, lost over 40% of its value last year, hitting its lowest level against the USD in December 2021. This has led to significant price increases for everyday food and non-items, many of which are imported from Turkey – including vegetables, flour, sugar and fuel. Food prices are 128% higher than the same time last year. The price of bread has also increased significantly in the past 4 months. In September 2.50 TRY could by 700g bread, while in December, 3.5 TRY only buys 500g bread. The average daily salary is 20-30 TRY which is not enough to cover basic needs. People have few options other than to resort to negative coping mechanisms, often disproportionately affecting women and children, including borrowing money, selling of productive household assets, early and forced marriage, child labor, or selecting less expensive or preferable food - all of which increase protection needs and/or reduce households’ capacity for self-sustenance in the future.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project is comprised of two main components: community food security and household food security. SO1. The household food security component will support food security for HHs through CVA or food kit distribution. Each HH will receive 8 rounds of assistance. NGO X plans to continue to transition households from kits to vouchers & cash as conditions allow, scale up its use of electronic vouchers. NGO X will continue to apply its household selection vulnerability criteria. NGO X will provide blanket distributions in IDP camps and informal settlements due to high vulnerability of the target populations. The second component is bakery support, which will advance the following outcomes: households have access to diverse foods in adequate quantities; and communities have access to bread at affordable prices. NGO X will support 33 bakeries, providing required flour and yeast inputs to ensure high-quality bread which will be made available to the bakery catchment population at an agreed affordable weight and price (up to 38% reduction of the current market bread price). Routine monitoring including distribution monitoring and post-distribution monitoring will be conducted for all distribution-based activities. For the bakery component, bakery feedback surveys, shopkeeper satisfaction surveys and bread beneficiary survey will be conducted.Additionally, under SO2, NGO X also will be conducting rehabilitation activity under bakeries business support component for 5 bakeries.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R 1: Deteriorated security situation making access more difficult M 1: NGO X has established a remote monitoring mechanism, with multiple whatsapp rooms, including bakery owners, bread distributors, local councils etc. This mechanism provides NGO X with sufficient monitoring data to ensure bakeries can continue to produce subsidized bread. Assumption 1: NGO X & partners can maintain long term access and if access is restricted that it is temporary with the above mitigation measures are employed during a short term access issue. R 2: Syrian pound depreciation against USDM 2: Vouchers and cash distributions are provided in USD, which is the most stable currency in circulation which protects the transfer value provided to the beneficiaries. FGD are carried out with community representatives regularly to understand the impact of currency fluctuations. Assumption 2: That sufficient USD notes are in circulation in North West Syria to distribute at the scale NGO X & partners intend to. USD is allowed to be distributed by local authorities in NWS. R 3: COVID19 Pandemic M 3: NGO X redesigned its food security programme to be adapted to C-19 and mitigate risks of transmission between staff and beneficiaries. These measures include social distancing, hand sanitation, mask wearing disinfection. Assumption 3: the vaccinations will not be spread for a large scale in NWS in 2022 and assumed that COVID19 will continue to be a risk during 2022.
