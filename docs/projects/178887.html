Project Summary

In 2020, SARD’s conducted a YNA (Yearly Needs Assessment) which observed the needs in various sectors including Food, Non-Food Items, Shelter and Livelihood sectors. Findings showed that 47% of households reported that their food consumption decreased in the last month. Furthermore, the main challenge relating to food access pertained to the high price of food items in local markets, as reported by 95% of households. Additionally, safety and security concerns in markets, particularly for women, were reported as an impediment to access.
To address the wide range of multi-sectoral needs, SARD’s intervention is comprised of the following: emergency and life-sustaining food assistance, NFI and shelter assistance (described in the SNFI document), and ultimately livelihood restoration for affected families.
SARD is committed to support affected populations in North-West Syria, prioritizing communities based on severity of need. With a budget of $6,325,000, this intervention will address the immediate needs of approximately 82,500 individuals over the course of a 18-month period through the provision of life-saving and life-sustaining food and resilience assistance.
Through a protection lens, SARD’s intervention is designed to ensure that newly displaced and vulnerable families have access to dignified and timely assistance through a robust response mechanism. To ensure that marginalized groups such as female-headed households, elderly and persons with disability (PwD) have safe and secure access to assistance, specialized and gender-sensitive activities have formed an integral part of SARD’s response strategy
Based on the individual family’s situation as well as the market and security situations, emergency food assistance will be provided through the in-kind modality (cooked meals or RTE) 60,000 individuals.
Monthly life-sustaining food assistance will be provided during a period of up to 8 months to 17,500 food-insecure individuals (IDPs and host communities) through e-voucher or cash modalities based on the best option for the family. The estimated budget of 2,240,000 USD is based on 80 USD per family (of 5 to 7 members) per month for a duration of 8 months.
Support for livelihood restoration will entail provision of cash grants to eligible families, prioritizing those in the agriculture and livestock sectors, targeting 5,000 individuals with a budget of 1,000,000 USD. The intervention is through cash grant (of average 1,000 USD per initiative/family)

4 - Needs

In SARD’s recent needs assessment, 95% of households interviewed reported the high price of food items in local markets as a significant impediment in their access to food. In order to cope with the lack of food / money to purchase food, households tend to resort to stress and crisis coping strategies such as decreasing spending on non-food essential items and on productive assets, purchasing food on credit, borrowing / sharing food or selling assets. For example, 47% of households reported a decrease in food consumption in the last month. Additional barriers of access were reported for women or persons with disabilities were cited, as the security and safety in the markets remains a concern. Thus, food continues to rank as the main priority for affected households, followed by shelter, NFIs, hygiene items, and healthcare.With regard to income generation and restoration, lack of job opportunities in the local market, lack of financial means to start a business along with lack of technical qualifications were reported as the main challenges faced by affected households. On average, 68% of households interviewed relied on income-generating activities for sustenance while 16% had no cash sources available.

5 - Implementation of activities

To address the multi-sectoral needs as described above, multi-sector assistance will be provided and range from emergency food assistance to restoring food production livelihoods. SARD will ensure newly-displaced families have dignified access to life-saving assistance through provision of emergency food through the delivery of cooked meals (CMs) with bread for one month to populations in needs, in close coordination with the FSL and CCCM clusters. Emergency activities will take place as follows: Identification and targeting of newly arrived IDPs, registration and distributions , monitoring and post-monitoring of the emergency support, and close coordination with the relevant clusters including the sharing of the 4Ws matrix. In addition to this, SARD is committed to also provide life-sustaining food support to most vulnerable families (MVF). This action provides 6-months food assistance for selected MVF, including IDPs and host communities. Vulnerability is identified and prioritized through a selection and scoring mechanism. Assessment on the security, locations, tenders and markets’ surveys will enable SARD to determine the modality of response (cash, voucher, or in-kind). Among the most vulnerable families, SARD will identify families eligible for small-scale food production opportunities, in order to offer them a sustainable exit strategy from food assistance. The selection will be based on eligibility criteria to gauge the family’s capacity. A BoQ (Bill of Quantities) will be signed with selected families and followed by cash grant dispersion. Following the action, review and satisfaction survey are conducted by the M&E team to verify implementation and measure the impact of the action.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Women and girls being excluded of the program design, implementation and evaluation of activities. Mitigation: As per our protection mainstreaming policy needs of women and girl are constantly considered, with specific dedicated staff, questions and action/response targeting the most vulnerable and the gender sensitivity. Risk: Boys and girls (under 18 years) are excluded of the design, implementation and evaluation of activities. Mitigation: Needs of boys and girl are constantly considered, with specific dedicated staff (part of SARD child protection mobile team) with adapted assessment tools and action/response targeting the young and the gender sensitivity. The CRM is adapted for under 18 years, ensuring the feasibility of a safe and secure feedback mechanism, as well as adapted follow up to address the issues. Risk: Older persons and persons with a disability (PWD) part of the design, implementation and evaluation of activities. Mitigation: Program staff is regularly trained inclusion and adequate program response tailored made for PWD. Risk: Increased sensitivity between host communities and IDPs. Mitigation: Based on a community approach, social activities are conducted based on communities’ initiatives, ensuring an inclusive participation of all groups. SARD ensure the involvement between the host community and internally displaced persons once the project (specially in Shelter and infrastructure project) starts in order to mitigate any tension within the community. Risk: Program staff unintentionally perpetuate harm or increase stigma of data confidentiality is not respected due to lack of training. Staff were trained on data confidentiality, psychological first aid and on communication skills.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:-Locations of project activities are secure and accessible. -Markets are functioning with the availability of food on the market-People of concern (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) are aware of selection and prioritization criteria implemented by SARD-No Covid-19 cases reported in the IDP sites/camps.-SARD staff and targeted communities are aware of Covid-19 threats, risk, and mitigation measures.Risks and Mitigation:-Insecurity conditions result in accessibility problems and exposure to risks to staff and beneficiaries: To prevent these risks, safety precautions will be taken such as alternate routes, changing the warehouse and office locations. Should the situation become volatile, SARD can use a mobile kitchen.-Lack of supplies. To prevent any shortages, SARD will identify alternative options in nearby areas. SARD's warehouse in Syria has 1-2 weeks food provision for the kitchen, and SARD has secured access for additional food supply in Turkey.-Implementation of gender and protection-sensitive measures during activities, including additional technical support when required.- Risk of tension due to selection criteria for the livelihood component. SARD will assess in advance and adopt mitigation measures aiming at adequate distributions of chances-Activities in the context of the Covid-19 pandemic: Standard operation procedures were developed and being implemented by SARD’s teams. The procedures include establishing Covid-19 task forces as part of SARD's Safety Team, disinfecting all of SARD’s facilities including offices, warehouses, utilities, and surfaces daily, at the opening and closing-SARD Syria staff members have completed trainings in crisis management in the context of Covid-19.
