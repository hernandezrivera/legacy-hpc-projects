Project Summary

In November 2017, tensions in the North West and South West regions turned into violence with dramatic humanitarian consequences.
The Poor WASH conditions continue to be reported in the regions with the displaced population mostly affected compared to the host population. 30% (NW-42% and SW-15%) of the IDPs cannot access sufficient water for household use and 26% do not have sufficient access to functional/improved sanitation facilities.
In addition, conflict has triggered extensive destruction and looting of WASH facilities. Access to water is often far, forcing children to walk more than 60 minutes to the nearest water source. The large influx of IDPs into urban areas has also led to the over-utilization of existing water and sanitation facilities and forcing people to rely on contaminated water sources that are prone to outbreak of diseases. Reaching these people with WASH programming is an essential element in reducing young child and maternal mortality through addressing their rights to WASH.
The proposed action aims to reduce the spread of disease and improve environmental health conditions in Fako and Manyu divisions (Southwest) and Mezam and Ngo-Ketunjia divisions (Northwest) by increasing access to essential water, sanitation and waste management facilities and by promoting proper waste disposal, maintenance of water facilities and hygiene techniques.

1 - Besoins / Needs

In the North West (NW) and South West (SW) regions, the situation remains volatile. The "Non-State Armed Groups" (NSAG) are increasing the attacks against the government and its symbols and against the populations and suspected sympathizers. The central government's response is the deployment of a repressive military apparatus. A deployment which has led to an intensification clash with consequences for civilians at the start of 2021, the crisis went beyond the limits of the two English-speaking regions with an attack in the West region, the second of its kind.The persistence of insecurity threatens local populations, exposes them to risks affecting their physical integrity and leads to population movements, disruptions and losses of their goods and livelihoods, thus reducing their access to drinkable water.The conflict has triggered extensive destruction and looting of WASH facilities. Access to water is often far, forcing children to walk more than 60 minutes to the nearest water source. The large influx of IDPs into urban areas has also led to the over-utilization of existing water and sanitation facilities and forcing people to rely on contaminated water sources that are prone to outbreak of diseases. Reaching these people with WASH programming is an essential element in reducing young child and maternal mortality through addressing their rights to WASH.Activities:DEMTOU Humanitarian envisages to carry out the following activities;1. Capacity Building/Training: Training and education will be fundamental to establishing better sanitation facilities and hygiene practices within the community. Targeted community participation and participatory activities will be led by trained Community Health Volunteers (CHVs) to educate the community about the benefits related to improved WASH practices and services2. Conduct a water source assessment in Fako, Manyu, Mezam and Ngo-Ketunjia divisions3. Rehabilitate targeted water infrastructure as identified by assessment (16 targeted water infrastructures in Fako, Manyu, Mezam and Ngo-ketunjia divisions)4. Training of hand pump mechanics, water committees and care takers5. Conduct 50 hygiene promotion and education campaigns in the project area using IEC and other materials like aquatabs, soaps, posters and t-shirt6. Hygiene and sanitation promotion sessions in schools, including school competitions and teacher training7. Household hygiene monitoring and sanitation surveillance campaignsResults:1. 10,000 people in bush/hard to reach and peri-urban areas and host communities have access to basic sanitation facilities and safe drinking water;2. 10,000 people are knowledgeable and do practice good hygiene and environmental health
