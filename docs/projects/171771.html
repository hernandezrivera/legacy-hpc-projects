Project Summary

In 2021 humanitarian response, Plan International will reduce food insecurity and negative coping for IDP and vulnerable populations. With the multifaceted crises of drought, locus, and COVID19, 5.1 million people across Somalia face food consumption gaps or acute food insecurity by December 2020, with 849,900 children under the age of five likely to be acutely malnourished by August 2021. Through this project, Plan and partners will effectively reduce food insecurity and promote dignity and choices among project participants by increasing purchasing power with unconditional cash transfers. By targeting vulnerable families with children and delivering this intervention in a gender sensitive manner, Plan strives for secondary outcomes of improved the protection environment and reduced rates of girls dropping out of school.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

For Plan International whenever appropriate and possible, cash or voucher assistance should be the preferred form of aid over in-kind. In markets contexts where supply is adequate or prices are not dramatically inflated, providing cash or vouchers will save lives. Distribution team must investigate the gender considerations in order to ensure decisions made for CTP prioritize improving wellbeing of women and girls. From our 2020 response, we found multipurpose cash assistance to be good practice and an effective conduit to assist people in IPDs in Somaliland, and it boosted the economy, protecting the dignity while reducing social gatherings.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

In consultation with the Cash Working Group and other sectors in Hargeisa, Plan will ensure sufficient assessment of market functionality. This will determine whether target population would be able to procure and meet basic needs with what is locally available or if there are supply gaps, issues of inflation, or another reason to provide in-kind.
