1 - Needs

While the conflict in Ukraine enters its sixth year and is considered as protracted by many observers the violations of the ceasefire still happen from time to time resulting in newly damaged or destroyed private dwellings on a monthly basis. This, in combination with the fact that neither the Ukrainian Government, Donors, International Organisations nor INGOs have been able to meet the complete shelter needs generated from the start of the armed conflict and, consequently, has resulted in unmet shelter needs in eastern Ukraine. During the latter part of 2018, and the first six months of 2019, NRC’s Shelter team conducted damage assessments in both Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts, specifically in the communities along the Government-controlled side of the ‘Contact Line’. The assessments were initially informed through key informant interviews with representatives of local administration in the conflict-affected communities, with the data (in the form of property lists) followed up with address-level technical assessments in all locations were access was possible. Both reports identified a baseline of 3,189 damaged properties across the two Oblasts, however, as field research started in 2018, this number has diminished considerably through the shelter interventions of the past 12-months, leaving a significant, but achievable end goal to ensuring that persons of concern, who have had property damaged or destroyed as a direct result of the conflict are able to restart their lives in adequate housing with dignity. Thus for Luhansk region the remaining of approximately 700 houses in need of Shelter intervention is estimated and for Donetsk region this number is around 1,200 houses. In general the majority of the remaining houses require light, medium and heavy repairs while certain percentage requires full reconstruction. In addition to the direct damage of shelters as an impact of the conflict, eastern Ukraine experiences long and harsh winters, below 0°C from end November to mid March, with an average low of 10°C and down to 20°C in the colder areas. It is recognized within the humanitarian community that winter represents a major concern for IDPs and conflict affected populations, especially in the most exposed communities along the contact line. Most houses along the contact line do not have full thermal insulation, and with increased costs for fuel and electricity, this leaves families at risk of exposure, or having to choose between food security or heating. The Shelter programme provides assistance to conflict affected, vulnerable groups of all ages and gender. In our area of operation, the ratio is approximately 60% Female and 60% Elderly. This is not by design, but rather the census of the population who are unable to move away from the line of contact.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Within the frame of its response plan for 2020, and based on a 2019 damage assessment, NRC will aim to provide shelter assistance and provide heating and insulation solutions to the remaining gap of households residing in conflict affected area of Luhansk and Donetsk oblasts (GCA).NRC will implement a project focused on different categories of responses, such as, Light & Medium repair works, multi-storey buildings repair, thermal enhancement, connection of essential utilities:(i) Light & Medium Rehabilitation work to conflict affected households whose houses have been partially damaged; this will target communities along the contact line and where possible multi-storey buildings. (ii) Where possible, and identified as a barrier to occupancy, assistance in connection to sustainable utilities, specifically targeting Heavy Repair and Core shelter assistance recipients from 2016-2019. (iii) Providing thermal enhancement (insulation material) of houses for residual populations in order to ensure protection from the winter cold, reducing costs, and energy demand and organizing of informational/awareness sessions for communities along the contact line to increase participation in state energy-efficiency programs.(iv) Contribution to the restoration of connections to basic utilities on household and community level in cooperation with LFS department.In an effort to contribute to families’ ability to live in safety and dignity, NRC will use a beneficiary-driven approach for all repair and construction activities with technical assistance provided throughout the entire process from assessment and design through construction. Furthermore, the team will work proactively to positively impact labour and price inflation. The beneficiary-driven approach is effectively the equivalent of the self-managed construction of homes, the common practice before the conflict. Community involvement in the programme through integrations with livelihoods opportunities will serve to inject capital into the micro-economy and increase overall community ownership of their own recovery. Due to its strong multi sectoral presence in Luhansk oblast, NRC will strive to undertake integrated programming, creating synergies between its shelter, legal aid (ICLA) and food security/livelihood programmes. Specifically, potential beneficiaries for shelter assistance who have lost their ownership or tenancy documentation, will receive support from NRC’s legal aid programme in order to apply for extracts from the official administration records. As well NRC will focus on increasing the cooperation with state authorities namely SES who continues its operation in the affected areas.Where feasible, the above project activities will be aligned with relevant GoU strategies (e.g. IDP Integration Strategy, State Targeted Recovery/Peacebuilding Programme, Regional Development Strategies, etc.) and will create synergies/linkages with the activities of recovery/development actors (HDN) operating in eastern Ukraine.Gender analysis demonstrates that needs for both gender groups are the same, but due to higher percentage of women, especially among the elderly, the assistance will be provided accordingly, and beneficiaries will be selected in close consultation with relevant stakeholders and municipal authorities and committees.

3 - Indicators & targets

Light and medium repair in Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts: 200 affected households supported with shelter solutions; Multi-storey buildings: up to 10 buildings (~120 households) will receive repair of common-space infrastructure including (but not limited to) heating systems, roofing, other essential services; Winterization (thermal insulation) in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts: 40 affected households supported with thermal enhancement solutions; 10 communities (~750 households) in hard-to-reach areas supported through community engagement/awareness programmes. Rehabilitation of household and community linkages to WASH and utility networks in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts: 1,300 affected households supported with utility connections. The geographic focus for NRC shelter interventions will be in the front line districts of Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts (GCA), within 5 km from the contact line. All components will be planned in close coordination with other operational actors in order to avoid overlap and to ensure gaps are addressed. Targeting will focus on a range of vulnerable household categories: elderly, disabled people, single woman headed household, pregnant and lactating women, large number families.
