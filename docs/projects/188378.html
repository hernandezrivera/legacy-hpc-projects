Project Summary

Ten years of crisis have made all population groups vulnerable as 14.1 people are in need of protection (49.1 % female) (HNO 2022-protection). Besides, the socio-economic influence of COVID-19 has added, and continues to add, enormous pressure on the economic conditions, which implied devastating consequences for already vulnerable populations across the country, and mainly those who are in acute need of humanitarian assistance, particularly women and girls. The current socio-economic situation is causing some of the most challenging humanitarian conditions experienced in the past ten years of crisis.
Women and girls are particularly vulnerable to protection risks, mainly GBV and sexual and reproductive health, “Women and girls continue to be disproportionately affected by GBV. The prevalence of GBV, impunity of perpetrators, and the absence of functional institutions that guarantee women’ and girls’ rights and safety, negatively impact women and girls limiting their freedom and eroding their resilience.
Inequitable gender norms relegate women and girls to positions of subordination and are used to justify the use of violence against them across Syria. Not all women and girls can access the job market, for instance, still constrained by social norms and family restrictions.” (HNO 2022)
Girls and boys continue to experience persisting violence including physical, emotional, sexual and domestic violence. Insecurity, economic hardship and COVID-19 exacerbate child protection concerns. These fueled harmful coping mechanisms, and increased child labour and child marriage, putting adolescent girls at particular risk. Child Protection issues are spread across all 14 governorates at generally increased levels. Kidnapping/Abduction was reported as occurring in 26 per cent of communities; Child labour in 84 per cent of communities; child marriage is an issue for adolescent girls in 71 per cent of communities; family separation in 36 per cent of communities. (HNO 2022).
As a result of the prolonged crisis, schools and hospitals were wrecked by weakened protection, security and justice systems. Thus, women and girls lack access to safe spaces and services where they can learn about their sexual and reproductive health rights (SRHR), discuss concerns and issues around GBV, early marriage, harassment and other topics without the fear of social and peer stigma, and receive psychosocial support as needed.
AKF aims to:
Provide quality and integrated protection services, with a focus on community-based approaches through:
1.1 Awareness-raising sessions to 415 people.
1.2 Provide 300 individuals with assistance for specific protection needs (including PSS, referrals, and material assistance) through community-based protection services
1.3 support 16 community-based initiatives and community-based protection structures.
2. Enhance strategies to empower women and girls, with a particular focus on most at risk groups through:
2.1 providing GBV prevention and empowerment activities to 837 women, men, girls and boys.
3. Promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response through:
3.1 Training and sensitizing 122 non-GBV humanitarian workers on GBV risk mitigation

4 - Needs

Salamieh District has a population of 223,935 people, (40.8%) of whom are youth (=18<)(49%) female youth). According to a study conducted by AKF in 2021, (98%) of households (HH) are food insecure, and vulnerable IDPs and residents also report high reliance on assistance to meet basic needs. The unemployment rate is particularly high among females (75.3%) compared to (28%) males The imbalance between HH income and expenses contributes to negative coping mechanisms adopted by households. About (22%) of children (age:0-18) in Salamieh are out of school due to the high cost of education (SYP 13,900/month/person(46475 SYP/month/family (Among families with children in school or university)) while 6.6% of all children in Salamieh are engaged in child labour. In 2019, AKF consulted with girls and female youth, parents, NGOs and community leaders to identify the social, cultural, economic, and structural barriers to women and girls’ empowerment. The evaluation determined the following: lack of decision-making ability; high risk of SGVB; personal safety in and around the school; lack of safe spaces and networks where they can share their concerns without the fear of social and peer stigma; the absence of trusted resources about sexual and reproductive health.

5 - Implementation of activities

(1) AKF will establish PWDs-friendly 7 girl clubs in sub-districts of Salamieh as a safe space for women and girls to learn about healthy lifestyles, improve life skills and discuss issues related to SRHR, GBV, and MH. This includes training a team of female facilitators to mobilize women and girls, facilitate sessions, monitor progress and identify and refer beneficiaries to health facilities as required. 2 facilitators will lead each club.(2)AKF will establish 5 Community Management Groups in sub-districts consisting of NGO members, Community Based Organizations (CBOs), MH experts, and community leaders to present the perspectives of the community, support with awareness-raising campaigns and provide feedback on the project. This will include developing promotional materials in collaboration with these groups. Also, 4 boys clubs will also be established in target areas to sensitize boys, including PWDs, and reduce stigma about MH, SRHR and GBV.(3)AKF will train facilitators to identify and prioritize approximately 300 women and girls, including those with disabilities, requiring PSS. GBV cases will be referred to MH services, namely AKHS’s (AKF sister agency) Health Counseling Center (HCC) – the only facility in Salamieh District delivering MH services. Processing and outcome monitoring project’s activities will include baseline and end-line assessments of the target group, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries and Focus Group Discussions with project stakeholders.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Girls face discrimination or negative judgments.Likelihood: medium (M).Impact:M. Mitigation: AKF will train facilitators on dealing with such cases and establish rules to create a cooperative atmosphere among girls. AKF will mobilize LNGOs to boost awareness of project objectives and significance. Regular monitoring will identify cases of exploitation and take action. Facilitators accused of bias in relations with girls. Likelihood:low (L). Impact:M. Mitigation: facilitators will sign a code of conduct. AKF will monitor adherence to the code and interfere when needed. Leakage of beneficiaries’ information. Likelihood: M. Impact:high(H). Mitigation: Facilitators will sign a confidentiality agreement and hold private sessions for special cases. Safety and Security concerns to create safe and accessible spaces for youth, including PWDs. Likelihood: L.Impact:M. Mitigation: AKF will apply its policies to ensure a safe and secure environment with the availability of WASH facilities. Lack of access to targeted girls due to the sensitivity of the subject. Likelihood:L. Impact:H. AKF will mobilize LNGOs to promote the services and utilize its database that includes girls in need for such services. Financial sanctions. Likelihood:M. Impact:H. AKF will follow the sanction clearance process before the signature of the proposal. Approval of authorities. Likelihood: L. Impact: H. AKF will coordinate with authorities through its HQ in Damascus and ensure that required approvals are secured.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk 1: There is a risk that the programming directly impacting youth, particularly adolescent girls and PWDs, may lead to safeguarding issues. Mitigation: • Train all project staff on safeguarding policies • Ensure all implementing partners adhere to AKF's Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct • Ensure all partnership agreements clearly outline the reporting process to AKF • Develop gender-responsive referral pathways and ensure project beneficiaries and vulnerable groups are aware of safeguarding policies and can access reporting mechanisms • All data collection will consciously abide by robust ethical standards, guided by AKF’s Codes of Conduct and Safeguarding Policies. • Conduct an assessment of safety aspects related to the internal environment of the club spaces and the surrounding environment. • Equip clubs to meet safety standards for all beneficiaries with special attention to the requirements of PWDs. •Conduct awareness-raising campaigns and activities such as Information Education and Communication (IEC) materials - among targeted groups, partners, and larger communities, which includes information about GBV and safeguarding. AKF will work closely with key community members and groups to establish a community-based complaint mechanism for reporting and feedback to AKF and the established referral system. •AKF will ensure to take necessary measures to make services available and friendly for PWDs.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1: Resistance from the local authority and community leaders due to the sensitivity of GBV and SRHR in the local culture. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will establish high-level coordination with local authorities and community leaders to explain the objectives and secure support before the project launch.2: Lack of local GBV and SRHS experts. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will coordinate with other INGOs and national actors to use their resources, develop a healthcare referral pathway and explore the possibility to provide a legal one.3: Lack of community interest in GBV, gender, and SRHR subjects might affect female adolescents and youth participation and activities reach. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will conduct community mobilization through LCBOs and NGOs, and ensure engaging community leaders and decision-makers as supporters of the project.4: Lack of Community Management Groups’ long-term commitment to expand the project objectives. Likelihood: low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will conduct meetings and adopt a participatory approach to ensure community ownership More, AKF will provide incentives such as appreciation letters for the most committed members.5: Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact on the project.
