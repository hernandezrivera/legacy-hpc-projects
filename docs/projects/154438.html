Project Summary

The general context in South Sudan is summarized, based on the Real Time Review (RTR) for the South Sudan Joint Response 4 report 4th October 2018, as that the humanitarian crisis has continued to intensify and expand. As a result of the compounding effects of widespread conflict and violence and a deteriorating economy, 7 million people – more than one in two across the country – need sustained humanitarian assistance. For most of the counties in Western- and North Bahr El Ghazal and Warrap, according to FewsNet (http://fews.net/east-africa/south-sudan), the IPC level in October-December 2018 and projected January to March 2019 is 4, indicating emergency levels of food insecurity. A significant increase in the provision of humanitarian assistance, above planned levels, is needed to prevent more households from falling in to Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5). This is particularly the case in Payam Greater Baggari in Wau County in Western Bahr El Ghazal as the security situation prevented till now much needed assistance. The other proposed areas include Jur River County in Western Bahr El Ghazal, Akon North Payam in Gogrial West in Warrap and Aweil West and Aweil North in Northern Bahr el Ghazal.
The population figures for the proposed areas, as per National Bureau of statistics March 2015 for 2019 estimate, are: Wau County: 230,919 (111,176 Male and 119,743 female). for Jur River County 194,806 (101,017 Male and 93,790 Female), Gogrial West: 196,925 (76,480 Male and 80,445 Female), Aweil West: 244, 667 (117,967 Male and 126,700 female) and Aweil North:196,014 (94,502 Male and 101512 Female). Save the Children reported in the Gurtong newspaper on 18th October 2018 that more than six million people currently need urgent food assistance, including more than one million children. Near-famine conditions are predicted in four of South Sudan’s states, a rapid and worrying increase from 2017, in which famine was only declared in one state. Western Bahr El Ghazal is among the 4 States showing the highest levels of food insecurity. Anticipating improved security in Payam Greater Baggari, Dorcas proposes a WASH project to assist the suffering people in the area. Similarly WASH intervention are proposed for Jur River County, Akon North Payam, Aweil West- and Aweil North Counties. The objectives for WASH sector in the proposed areas are: provide access to safe and sufficient water, adequate sanitation facilities, waste disposal solutions and increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. Missing details in this proposal will be filled in during the full proposal stage.

Needs

The general context in South Sudan is summarized, based on the Real Time Review (RTR) for the South Sudan Joint Response 4 report 4th October 2018, as that the humanitarian crisis has continued to intensify and expand. As a result of the compounding effects of widespread conflict and violence and a deteriorating economy, 7 million people – more than one in two across the country – need sustained humanitarian assistance. Hunger and malnutrition have reached record levels, and 1.75 million people are now on the brink of a catastrophe. More than 1.1 million children under the age of five were forecast to be malnourished in 2018, including nearly 300,000 severely malnourished and at a heightened risk of death. This makes 2018 the toughest year on record. A crop assessment estimates South Sudan’s cereal production in 2017 at 764.107 tons, which is a decrease of about 8% from 2016 and is 14% below the five-year average – the lowest output since the start of the conflict. Nearly 4.3 million people – one in three South Sudanese – have been displaced, including more than 1.8 million who are internally displaced and about 2.5 million who are in neighboring countries. People who have been displaced multiple times, are more vulnerable to threats to their safety, health and livelihoods. For most of the counties in Western- and Northern Bahr El Ghazal, according to FewsNet (http://fews.net/east-africa/south-sudan), the IPC level is estimated to be 4 for January to March 2019 and for Warrap the estimation is IPC 3, indicating emergency- and crisis levels respectively of food insecurity. A significant increase in the provision of humanitarian assistance, above planned levels, is needed to prevent more households from falling in to Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) for Western- and Northern Bahr El Ghazal or to emergency (IPC 4) in Warrap. The situation in Payam Greater Baggari in Wau County need special attention as due to insecurity not much assistance could reach this Payam. Akon North and both Aweil West and Aweil North are challenging for logistical reasons (far from Wau and at times due to flooding). Jur River County is relatively save and easier accessible. Save the Children reported (Gurtong on 18th October 2018, Posted in: Home, Humanitarian) that more than six million people currently need urgent food assistance, including more than one million children. Near-famine conditions are predicted in four of South Sudan’s states, a rapid and worrying increase from 2017, in which famine was only declared in one state. Western Bahr El Ghazal is among the 4 States showing the highest levels of food insecurity. As the lean season (when food stocks are low) begins, 270,000 children in South Sudan are severely malnourished (SAM) and at risk of starvation. Some 20,000 could be expected to die from extreme hunger before the end of 2018. “Malnourished children have substantially reduced immune systems and are at least three times more likely to contract and die from diseases like cholera and pneumonia than healthy children,” says Deidre Keogh, Save the Children’s Country Director in South Sudan. “Without urgent funding to increase and maintain humanitarian services, many children are in danger of dying.” The revitalized peace agreement signed in September provides hope for millions of children to be protected and thrive if implemented effectively. The level of emergency malnutrition is in addition to insecurity and related limited access, understood to be attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets and poor sanitation and hygiene. Dorcas will provide adequate context-, needs- and beneficiary information during the full proposal preparation phase.

Activities

WATER SANITATION AND HYGIENE 1: Access to safe and clean water through repair and drilling 2: Hygiene Promotion activities 3: Sanitation and waste disposal. Activities will be implemented in close coordination with the communities through Community Based Participatory Planning and implementation. This will be done through mobilization of Community leaders, Farmers, both men and women and local Government representatives i.e. RRC, Local Chiefs, County Agricultural Department etc. Given the high insecurity in Payam Greater Baggari, Dorcas will involve Mary Help, a local church organization, in the project implementation in this area as this organization has a good cooperation with the various parties involved. In Jur River, Akon North and Aweil West Dorcas is currently implementing projects and staff is fully aware about the needs of the people and challenges of the implementation. If projects in other sectors will be approved, Dorcas will implement all the projects in an integrated manner.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Access for humanitarian partners to Payam greater Bagari areas have been so irregular due to insecurity as a result of the fighting fractions of the SPLA/IG and SPLA/IO limiting access for humanitarian interventions to needy communities. Even after the 12th September 2018 signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) fighting continues in parts of Bagari limiting access to deliver humanitarian aid. As a result, currently Greater Baggari is a "no go" area for Dorcas staff. The project will monitor barriers to access for different population groups. Mary Help, a local church organization, has access to Bagari even when Dorcas staff cannot access the location and they have implemented interventions on behalf of Dorcas before and such options will be explored further in situations of restricted access to the area. Details will be provided at full proposal preparation stage. For Jur River County, Akon North, Aweil West and Aweil North more information is available which will guide the staff to overcome all type of barriers
