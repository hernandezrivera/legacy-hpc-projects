Project Summary

The program aims to promote eco-friendly micro-entrepreneurship & alternative livelihood opportunities for the targeted marginalized population, especially women and other excluded people, in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upzaila of Cox’s Bazar district. This will be done by creating increased and sustainable income opportunities through different on-farm activities such as Raising nursery, Fisheries and Aquaculture, Crop Farming and Gardening, Poultry Farming, livestock farming and any other on-farm IGA as per choice of participants , which will ultimately contribute to peaceful conflict resolution. ACF will provide support to 975 beneficiaries in particular women and youth for Eco friendly and alternative livelihoods opportunities. Among the 975 beneficiary, at least 70% of the beneficiaries will be women and 7% will be the PWD and or other disadvantaged groups with a special focus on female-headed households and beneficiaries with age between18-35. The project participants will be selected from the most vulnerable households, including those headed by females, households with persons with disabilities, orphan-hosted households, and households with elderly individuals. ACF has a beneficiary targeting tool which generate vulnerability score for each of the households (HH). PRA techniques such as transact walk, wealth ranking and community consultation will be done to get initial list of the beneficiaries. The higher the score indicated the higher level of vulnerability. ACF will ensure training, input and market linkage support on Raising nursery, Fisheries and Aquaculture, Crop Farming and Gardening, Poultry Farming, livestock farming for the targeted host community as per their choice. ACF will adopt Climate smart agricultural technology to cope with multi hazards like tidal surges, floods, salinity, drought and others. Workshops on market linkage and income generating activity opportunities will also be facilitated for the host communities. Finally, awareness will be raised among the project participants for social cohesion between the host and refugees' communities as tensions between these communities appear as an obstacle to stability and maximization of opportunities. These activities will allow the participants to acquire marketable skills that will create opportunities for them to engage in self-sustaining and life-enhancing activities, notably through income generation. The new skills of on-farm IGA and income-earning opportunities created by the proposed project will enable the beneficiaries to build-up on the support they receive from aid agencies and government, and at the same time improve their well-being and regain of self-esteem. ACF's MEAL team will develop tools based on the project's output and outcome. The MEAL unit will conduct periodic surveys, focus group discussions, and participatory development meetings with the targeted population. In the project implementation area, an effective complaints and feedback mechanism will be established to ensure community participation, transparency, and accountability. Additionally, a contingency plan will be included in the project to address any disasters that may occur during the project period, and all staff will be equipped with the necessary tools and protective gear to respond to emergencies within 72 hours. ACF planned for the most vulnerable affected population by the Cyclone or any disaster in host community of Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila and two refugee at camp (Camp 1w & camp 11) through Emergency Support Program (ESP) of SIDA RRM. This program targeted for emergency support for 3400 refugee households and 5880 host communities. The proposed project will adhere to the Food Security Sector (FSS) policy and guidance.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Bangladesh hosts around one million FDMNs, most of whom fled for their lives from Myanmar in 2017. The country set an admirable example of solidarity with the refugees despite its resource constraints, especially in Cox’ Bazar region, where most refugees are located. However, in the host communities is a perceptible shift from this rather unconditional solidarity to an anti-refugee sentiment. Over the years, solidarity with the plight of FDMNs evolved because of economic instability as a result of the wage fall and price hike, unequal access to humanitarian aid and uneven distribution of resource opportunities created through substantial donor support to the FDMNs. Because of the disrupted economy that comes with hosting so many refugees, the tension between host communities and refugees increase. To address these tensions, creating increased and sustainable income opportunities of host communities, which will ultimately contribute to peaceful conflict resolution. According to REVA-6, in the Bangladeshi community, insufficient food consumption saw a slight decrease to 34 percent in 2022, down from 38 percent in 2021. However, this still represents a decline from 21 percent in 2019, indicating ongoing challenges faced by Bangladeshi communities in meeting their food consumption needs since the onset of the pandemic, and their slow recovery from job and livelihood losses. Both Rohingya and Bangladeshi households are grappling with difficulties in providing enough food, often resorting to negative coping strategies. Approximately 65 percent of households in the camps and 55 percent in the Bangladeshi community turned to less preferred or lower-cost food options as their most common coping strategy. The economic vulnerability of Bangladeshi community households increased to 35 percent in 2022, up from 32 percent in 2021, already surpassing the pre-COVID-19 level of 26 percent in 2019. This decline underscores the challenges households face in light of the ongoing global food crisis, the continued economic impacts of the pandemic, and the 2022 price increases of essential commodities in Bangladesh. In the Bangladeshi community, the proportion of households not employing any coping strategies to secure food increased to 29 percent, marking an 11 percentage point rise from the previous year (18 percent), yet still 10 percentage points lower than the base year of 2019. The percentage of Bangladeshi community households experiencing income deprivation has decreased to 18 percent. As per the MSNA 2023 report, approximately 36% of households in the host community faced diverse challenges in accessing income-generating prospects. Moreover, 29% of households indicated zero income (in Sabrang Union, this percentage was 37%). In the 18-25 age bracket, 58% of individuals were employed out of the total population that reported working, with only 8% being female. According to RIMA II-2022 report, In comparison to 2021, the resilience index of Ukhia and Teknaf has significantly increased. Besides, a negative impact of shock specially landslides and price hike was observed on the resilience capacity of the host community. The households not affected by shock were found having a higher resilience capacity (37) compared to those affected by shocks (32).

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Action Against Hunger (ACF) is a global humanitarian organization that takes decisive action against the causes and effects of hunger. ACF has been supporting communities in Bangladesh since 2007 and intervening in Cox’s Bazar District since 2009, where it is supporting both host communities and the Rohingya communities through its high-quality interventions. ACF thus has many years of contextual experience and acceptance built through strong engagement of host and Rohingya communities, other organisations, authorities - including at camp level, and other relevant stakeholders. ACF has a strong technical expertise in Food Security and Livelihoods, including DRR, ensuring food and nutrition security as well as resilient and climate adaptive livelihoods. Throughout its long experience in the country, particularly in Cox’s Bazar, ACF has promoted numerous innovative ideas in the field of Food Security and Livelihoods in order to strengthen the resilience of both host and refugee communities through actions similar as proposed in this program. ACF is also the Assessment and Information Management Technical Working Group Lead, Nutrition and WaSH Sector SAG member and member of different clusters, sectors and working groups, including the Food Security Sector, basing its humanitarian support on its guidelines. ACF boasts a wealth of experience in partnerships with UN agencies, including UNHCR, UNICEF, IOM, WHO, UNOPS, and WFP. Additionally, ACF has collaborated with local NGOs such as Mukti Cox's Bazar, FIVDB, Sushilan, Friendship, SHED and SARPV etc. Throughout the project cycle, ACF will reinforce coordination with WaSH, Health, Shelter, FSS, GBV, and other actors. ACF is an active member of the PSEA network, and all staff members will receive further training on safeguarding policy and PSEA guidelines. The proposed interventions under this project align seamlessly with the Food Security Sector priorities and the strategies set forth by the Bangladesh Government.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

ACF is a member of PSEA network and have an assigned focal person. ACF will conduct PSEA training for all staffs and volunteers involved in project implementation. The project is committed to fostering inclusive participation, specifically targeting diverse groups, including individuals with disabilities, while steadfastly adhering to the "Do No Harm" principle. ACF will establish Women-friendly and Inclusive Spaces (WFIS) as an integral component of this project. The construction of WFIS will prioritize the safety, security, and unique needs of women, person with disability and persons with special needs. These spaces will serve as venues for meetings, awareness-raising sessions, and other activities aligned with the project's objectives. ACF will install complaint boxes at the community level within WFIS. Additionally, ACF will ensure the dissemination of a hotline number through leaflets, stickers, beneficiary passbooks, and other mediums at various project sites. A conflict-sensitive approach will be consistently applied throughout, incorporating risk and security assessments to mitigate potential harm. This proactive strategy aims to prevent interventions from escalating tensions or compromising the safety of both staff and beneficiaries.

3.3 - Age & Disability

ACF maintain Sex, Age and diversity Disaggregated Data (SADDD) and Washington Group Questions (WGQ) for targeting beneficiary in order to address the specific needs of the each beneficiary group. ACF abide by the local laws and by applicable international instruments, including the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child and International Labor Organization conventions. The activities will be enabled for age group 18+ under the project. ACF provides priority for person with disability in selection process and implementation of activities. ACF will provide further training for staffs on SADDD and WGQ. ACF will ensure referral mechanism to ensure rehabilitation services for every person with disabilities and establish facilities after conducting accessibility assessment.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project beneficiaries will be actively engaged in the assessments conducted by ACF like post distribution monitoring (PDM), food security surveillance survey (FSS). The beneficiary selection will be done by community consultations and the affected people will decide to select beneficiaries as per criteria of project interventions. Household with PLW, person with disability, children under 5 and elderly are prioritized in the criteria of selection of beneficiaries. Communities also will provide their feedback on ACF led project activities formally through the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) using hotline number, complaint box and email. And informally through meetings and individual interactions based on which actions will be taken to rectify the project activities if needed. Apart from this, ACF will prepared a Project Monitoring Committee (PMC) by the local community, who will provide feedback and learnings.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

ACF is committed to the mainstreaming of the PSEA principles and has own policy. This entails embedding a robust framework that prioritizes the safety, dignity, and well-being of all participants, particularly those who may be vulnerable or at-risk. ACF will take initiative to aware community people, staffs, beneficiary and all stakeholders involved, equipping them with the knowledge and skills to recognize signs of vulnerability and report any incidents promptly. ACF have clear reporting mechanisms, including a confidential hotline, complaint box and email to encourage open communication and facilitate immediate response to any concerns raised. Additionally, ongoing monitoring and evaluation will be conducted through a PSEA lens, ensuring that interventions remain aligned with best practices and uphold the highest standards of protection for all individuals involved in the program.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, ACF have been implementing disaster risk reduction activities in Cox’s Bazar since 2009. ACF facilitating to develop and review of CRA (Community Risk Assessment) and RRAP (Risk Reduction Action Plan) of Union Parishad. ACF always take initiatives on small scale mitigation measures based on CRA and RRAP. ACF developed DRR taskforce on early warning, search & rescue, evacuation and first aid by the support of Cyclone Preparedness Program (CPP). ACF organized disaster dill by CPP and fire drill by fire Service and Civil Defense as per community sensitization. Capacitated UDMC and WDMC members based on updated SoD. Through this program, ACF will facilitate for organizing community development voluntary action by Women Friendly and Inclusive Spaces (WFIS) through campaigns such as awareness on early warning, search & rescue and evacuation, hygiene practice, tree plantation, cleanness of area, anti-use of polythene, anti-drug and livestock vaccination as per identified problems by the community. WFIS will be oriented and linked with CPP volunteers for assisting DRR activities. Youth female CPP volunteers will get priority as a member of WFIS. ACF will develop and review contingency plans to ensure the safety and well-being of project participants in the event of a crisis. All the participants under the training activities will receive disaster preparedness environ and climate change mitigation topics. This project will introduce climate smart agriculture for homestead gardening such as vertical garden, sac/bag/hanging gardening, use of compost pit for organic manure by using household waste etc. This project will introduce pheromone trap, bio-pesticides at beneficiary level.
