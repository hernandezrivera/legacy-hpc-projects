Project Summary

The aim of this project is to enhance and sustain emergency food production through provision of complementary and integrated agricultural crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools, fishing kits and agricultural extension support. The other objective of the project will be to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that builds the resilience of households. It seeks to enable vulnerable IDPs and host communities to recover from crises, with a focus on seeking more durable solutions and building the capacity of communities to cope and withstand recurrent shocks and strengthen resilience to future shocks. It will build on interventions that CAFOD and its implementing partners Caritas Diocese of Malakal (CDoM). The communities have endured food insecurity caused by a complex interplay of climate variability induced factors such as cycles of droughts and floods. These have been compounded by intensified inter-ethnic conflict, livestock raiding and worsening economic situation across the country. This project will provide integrated streams of livelihood inputs including crop and vegetable seeds, farming tools including ox-ploughs as well as provision of fishing kits. The project will continue to support seed fairs to support local market where feasible, as informed by assessments that CAFOD and its partners will continue to carry out. This will be accompanied by a comprehensive farmer training package on sustainable agroecological farming practices and climate smart agriculture including soil and water conservation techniques. The project will be implemented through a network of farmer extension services using recruited and well trained and equipped community based extension workers. The project will also strengthen capacity of lead farmers. Seed selection, preservation and storage will also be supported to strengthen seed security. The training will also include improved fishing practices, hygienic handling of fish and fish preservation for fisher folk. The project will reach 12,000 individuals IDP, returnee and host communities) in Dethwok and Lul payams of Upper Nile State. The community will be involved in targeting and a clear vulnerability criteria will be agreed. As part of beneficiary selection CAFOD will also deploy its SADI (Safety, Access, Dignity and Inclusivity) Framework to ensure inclusion of vulnerable and often hard to reach groups such as female headed households, disabled and elderly persons. To realize the inclusion of persons with disability, the project will ensure that at least, 10 percent of the benefiting households have a person/s living with disabilities. The project will collaborate and work with other agencies working on Food Security and Livelihood intervention to achieve synergy and avoid duplication, utilize their expertise, work with health and nutrition actors and Ministry of Health to support households with malnutrition cases with a basket of interventions. This project will build on the previously implemented CTP projects in Fashoda and will address gaps not met on the current programming in Fashoda County

1. Needs

Fashoda County in Upper Nile is one of the areas most affected by localized conflicts. Renewed fighting between armed factions in several locations, including Padit in Fashoda County, Upper Nile State, displacing thousands of people to Kodok town and other locations. Intercommunal dynamics in Fashoda County have been strongly shaped by fighting during the most recent civil war. In the early stages, Fashoda was a site of contested control between SPLA – IG and SPLA – IO forces. Fashoda County is levelled as Priority 1 areas in UNOCHA HRP 2024. Given the high levels of acute food insecurity in the country, immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance is needed to save lives and prevent the total collapse of livelihoods in the affected areas, particularly those with a high share of populations in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Urgent action is also required for populations in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) to protect their livelihoods and reduce household level food consumption gaps. Lul and Dethwok payams of Fashoda County in Upper Nile State were heavily affected by the violence in Upper Nile. The population in Lul payam fled the area amidst killings, torturing and rape. Severe gender-based violence violations were committed. Dethwok payam was not directly reached by the violence but the inhabitants fled when fighting came close. After a military intervention by the Central Government in December 2022, fighting stopped in Fashoda County. At the beginning of 2023 the local population gradually started to go back to their home areas in Dethwok and Lul payams. However, the returnees face several challenges. Dethwok Payam of Fashoda has 10 Bomas (Ashak, Coshang, Alel, Dethwok, Othurshok, Pabiew, Borbak, Malo, Abenyai, and Atar) with an estimate population of 800 HHs (estimated 4,800 individuals) after recent displacement. According to the community leaders and local authorities in Dethwok Payam, most of the community was displaced to Kodok town and some to Melut County. Currently, the community is voluntarily returning to Dethwok Payam. The area is surrounded by floodwater, with Dethwok St. Ambrose catholic church is on Island where there are no sanitation facilities or protected water sources for people to use. Lul payam falls in the southern part of Kodok town in Fashoda County. Lul payam has 13 Bomas. They are Nyingar, Bol, Boodh, Pabur, Beew, Yony, Lul, Abaneem, Agot, Thworlong, Kwom, Wadman and Agwor. The Estimate Population of Lul payam is 24,360 individuals (4,060HH). Some of these people got displaced to Malakal and Kodok during the recent clashes in November and December 2022. However, many of them have started to return to their villages and they are in dire need of support to rebuild their lives and livelihoods. The returnees are facing numerous challenges related to food insecurity and rebuilding of their livelihoods to earn and diversify their income. They have practically lost everything during the forced displacement as the result of the conflict. They lost their properties and their houses and are, therefore, faced by difficult living conditions resulting to serious malnutrition, especially among children, pregnant and lactating women and the elderly. The conflict has worsened pre-existing gender inequalities and poverty among women. The situation has been compounded by the influx of returnees from Sudan due to the war that broke out in April 2023 that has exerted pressure on the existing scarce resources. The current CAFOD project in Fashoda is reaching 1000 households (6,000 individuals); this proposed project aims to reach 12,000 individuals.

2. Project activities

The project will assist crisis affected communities’, households, and individuals with lifesaving food and support them to produce enough food sufficient to meet their food requirement for a year or until next harvest season. This will be achieved through provision of locally adaptable, short maturing and high yield crops and vegetable seed varieties and fishing equipment. The quantity of seed will be based on the UNFAO crop and vegetable seed kit standard. Farmers will be provided with farm tools and implements such as ox ploughs. This support will be accompanied by contextually tailored extension support to increase uptake of sustainable and improved agroecological and climate smart agricultural practices. The project will support Agroecology training for extension workers, followed by model farmers who train farmer groups, building on traditional knowledge supporting regeneration of the environment, enhanced biodiversity, reduced erosion, improved groundwater infiltration, supporting increased productivity and production of diverse nutritious foods. Farmers will also be trained on seed selection and preservation techniques of local high yielding varieties and will be supported to establish seed banks. The project will also support the establishment of tree nurseries at schools and in gardens, to support farmer managed regeneration and reclamation of the environment. Training will also cover appropriate seeding levels, soil and water conservation, mulching, ridge farming, pest management and post-harvest handling. The project will provide comprehensive and tailored agricultural extension services to the beneficiaries in order to improve productivity. The project will deploy agricultural extension staff and volunteers recruited from the local community. CAFOD and CDoM will collaborate with state and county agriculture department to educate and train farmers targeted under the programme. Opportunities will be explored for collaboration with WFP and FAO through the voucher or cash for assets to support poor resourced farmers to plough, plant and weed farmlands. The intervention will be implemented in ways that support local markets through seed fairs based on seed market assessments. Given the high levels of malnutrition in most of the project areas. CAFOD training package also includes training on preparation and consumption of nutritious foods given especially mothers and those with malnourished children. This will involve training on how to prepare various dishes using locally available foods and vegetables. Households affected by malnutrition will be prioritized for support with vegetable seeds and assorted hand tools to enhance food dietary diversity of that household. As a result, the project will provide training on fishing activities, along with this, will also provide recommended fishing kits according to UNFAO fishing kit standards. Among the training package, will be how to make and repair nets, hygienic handling of a catch of fish and preservation methods such as smoking, salting and fish storage.
