Project Summary

Given the ongoing protection crisis in South Sudan, DRC will continue to work with populations affected by conflict and displacement to enhance the protective environment of individuals and communities, and identify, prevent and address protection risks. The main objectives of the project, aligned with the Protection Cluster Humanitarian Response Plan objectives, are:
- Provide immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach areas
- Prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience
- Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other affected populations
The project is designed to alleviate suffering of the most vulnerable individuals, and ensure safety and well-being of conflict-affected communities are prioritised and achieved. The project will focus on three main components:
- Protection analysis and advocacy: DRC will focus on protection data collection and analysis, for the purpose of ensuring an in-depth understanding of the protection environment, allowing for appropriate prevention and response activities. In parallel, DRC will ensure protection concerns are captured in protection information products, such as assessment reports, monitoring reports, and protection snapshots, allowing for appropriate and relevant advocacy with the clusters, humanitarian partners, and other main stakeholders. DRC protection programming will ensure evidence-based interventions, at the individual and community levels, ensuring appropriate and principled action is taken to prevent and respond to protection risks.
- Protection risk prevention: DRC will continue to work closely with communities, particularly existing community structures, and vulnerable groups and individuals, to identify protection risks, coping strategies, community and individual intentions, and resilience among the affected population, to ensure preparedness and resilience of communities and at risk individuals are enhanced. DRC will work with community-based protection networks, facilitate focus group discussions, awareness raising, trainings on human rights and protection mainstreaming, and establish community-driven action plans, aiming to prevent and mitigate identified protection risks.
- Protection response: DRC provides tailored protection responses to individuals at risk and communities affected by violence and displacement. The responses include protection monitoring, individual protection assistance (IPA), through case management, and mobile responses, identification and support to vulnerable individuals at risk, psychosocial support, service mapping and referrals, community based protection networks, and other necessary interventions based on needs in various locations, such as housing land and property (HLP) and legal aid.
DRC’s approach in South Sudan is both through static presence (mainly in Upper Nile and Unity States) and mobile teams, who rove throughout the country based on new shocks and movements of populations. In Unity State, DRC forms part of a group of agencies implementing the “Beyond Bentiu Response” strategy, which aims at assessing locations beyond the Bentiu POC, and providing services to hard-to-reach communities in different counties. DRC adopts an integrated approach, linking general protection with sexual and gender based violence (SGBV) prevention and response, food security and livelihood (FSL), shelter and non-food items (SNFIs) and camp coordination and camp management (CCCM). DRC will continue to engage with other humanitarian partners and the relevant clusters to coordination and ensure aid effectiveness, as well as focus on mainstreaming protection throughout its programming. DRC will put ample focus on the centrality of protection, ensuring safety and dignity of persons of communities affected by displacement are prioritised and a do-no-harm approach is mainstreamed during all aspects of the project cycle.

Needs

According to the 2019 Protection Cluster strategy, approximately 5.7 million South Sudanese women, men and children will face protection risks and violations in 2019. These risks are a direct result of the continued violence and conflict affecting civilians since 2013. In spite of the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan, R-ARCSS, on 12 September 2018, fighting and displacement have continued to affect the civilian population, and is an indication that political peace may not necessarily lead to an end of the ethnic conflict. Displacement, caused by conflict and rights violations, continues to be one of the main protection concerns, with 2,486,562 South Sudanese refugees currently in neighboring countries, 298,881 refugees in South Sudan, and 1,903,650 internally displaced persons, of whom 198,202 are living in PoC sites. Lack of access to food and basic services are some of the challenges IDPs face, while South Sudanese refugees in neighboring countries face harassment and different types of violations, sometimes leading them to return to South Sudan, in turn exposing them to additional protection risks. The South Sudanese population continues to be subject to different human rights violations, including deliberate attacks, deliberate destruction of property and assets, forced displacement, conflict related SGBV, abductions, forced recruitment, restrictions on freedom of movement of civilians, and family separation, among others. Parts in Upper Nile State and Unity State are affected by these attacks, as well as Central and Eastern Jonglei, Western Bar-al-Ghazal, and Yei. With operational presence in Upper Nile and Unity States as well as three roving mobile protection teams, DRC has been able to obtain a strong understanding of the general protection environment. It has become apparent that protection needs remain high, and will continue to be high throughout 2019. As such, there is a need to continuously monitor and analyse the protection situation and needs of the affected population, to inform humanitarian responses, and adhere to Do No Harm and protection principles. DRC has identified the need to continuously mainstream protection into all humanitarian programming, and ensure protection principles are adhered to. There is also a high need to continuously advocate for improved access to services, particularly for persons with specific needs, the most vulnerable, and displaced persons, focusing on remote locations. DRC emphasizes the need to work closely with affected communities to identify their needs and empower them to become more resilient, based on their protection risks and needs. For humanitarian programming to be principled and priorities the safety and dignity of the beneficiaries, it is important to provide the assistance where the communities are, and ensure their intentions and concerns are well understood and captured. DRC will therefore continue to have a combined mobile and static approach to its programming based on occurring needs and new shocks. DRC also recognizes that displaced individuals are identifying solutions to their displacement situation; these solutions include local integration, return to their area of origin, or moving to a different location they deem safe. Men, women, boys, and girls may have different interpretations of their situation, and make decisions based on which solution may seem best for them, and what risk they may face. It is therefore important to provide assistance to IDPs and returnees based on their informed and voluntary decisions and with high regard for their safety and dignity. Needs identified for these concerns are related to housing, land, and property, legal aid, access to justice, and peaceful coexistence.

Activities

DRC’s intervention in 2019 will focus on addressing the main protection needs as identified by the cluster, and DRC’s needs analysis. Protection activities will focus on protection analysis (evidence-based programming), protection risk prevention and response, and will target a total of 221,000 individuals. Based on these needs and in line with the protection cluster’s strategic objectives for HRP, DRC’s proposed activities are as follows:Under strategic objective 1, DRC plans to implement mobile and static protection assessments, including in POCs, DRC will implement protection monitoring. This protection data collection will be processed, analysed, stored, and shared with relevant actors, following the protection information management principles (people-centred and inclusive, do no harm, defined purpose, informed consent and confidentiality, etc). Through its data collection and monitoring activities, which include monitoring visits, FGDs, KIIs, observation, population data gathering, protection desk management, and community based protection networks (CBPN), DRC will identify vulnerable people at risk and will provide appropriate responses, include case management, IPA, referrals, and legal awareness and counselling. Under this objective, DRC aims to reach approximately 15,000 persons at risk, and persons with specific needs. Under strategic objective 2, DRC will implement community based protection risk mitigation and support activities to reduce vulnerabilities and enhance the resilience and coping mechanisms of communities facing protection risks. Activities under SO2 include CBPNs, awareness raising on mitigation of protection risks, information dissemination, and strengthening community resilience through risk mitigation strategies and community engagement. DRC operates at the community level, conducts FGDs and interviews to inform its protection programming, and supports communities to advocate for more services to avoid exposure to threats on the way to access services, and increasing household and community level resiliency through income generating and PSS projects. DRC will also target specific beneficiaries who require income generating or livelihood activities in order to mitigate protection risks. Under this objective, DRC aims to reach approximately 185,500 persons affected by conflict and displacement. Under strategic objective 3, DRC will engage with communities to understand their intentions and provide support for individuals seeking durable solutions. DRC will support through identifying HLP needs in specific locations that are deemed stable, and provide appropriate awareness and counselling on housing land and property concerns. In addition, DRC will work with communities consisting of different ethnic groups, displacement backgrounds, and social situations, to ensure their durable solutions needs are met, including peaceful coexistence and inclusivity. The activities under this objective will only be implemented in static responses, in Upper Nile and Unity states, and will target approximately 20,500 people. In addition, DRC will provide training on protection mainstreaming to humanitarian partners, trainings for community based structures on human rights and protection risks, and advocate with main stakeholders on protection concerns, with the aim to improve the overall protective environment of the affected populations. All activities, interventions and analyses will be gender, age, and diversity disaggregated to ensure inclusion, participation and response to the needs of the most vulnerable groups. DRC is the co-lead for the Protection Cluster in Upper Nile State, and coordinates closely with the state-level protection cluster in Unity State, ensuring all activities are clearly coordinated with other partners and avoiding duplication at all levels. DRC also closely coordinates with the Protection Cluster at the national level to ensure complementarity and avoid duplication.

Consultations with affected people

Yes

Provide breakdown by women, men, boys and girls

Approximately 5,000 people (2,400 women; 2,100 men, 250 boys, 250 girls) have been consulted over the months of September and October 2018. This consultation took place during regular monitoring visits, FGDs with men, women, boys, and girls, protection assessments conducted on mobile missions, PSN identifications, community based protection networks, etc. In general, DRC consults communities about their needs, the risks they face, their displacement history, their intentions, their safety situation, as well as identifies specialised protection risks (SGBV, child protection and mine action). These consultations take place on a daily basis, and are captured in DRC’s monitoring reports, protection assessments, context snapshots, monthly reports, and camp management satisfaction surveys and information products. DRC engages with communities on a daily basis to understand their needs, concerns, and analyse the protection environment. This project design is based on these discussions and observations within the communities, including separate discussion with women, men, boys, and girls, persons with disabilities, persons with special needs, and community leaders.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

DRC has put in place global organisational policies and procedures to safeguard against harmful behaviour; DRC is in full agreement with the UN zero tolerance policy relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. Violations constitute acts of serious misconduct and are grounds for disciplinary measures up to and including dismissal, as described in sections 3.7 and 3.9 of DRC’s Code of Conduct (CoC). DRC implements awareness raising in project sites on PSEA, provides training to staff on PSEA and CoC, and has complaints and feedback mechanisms in place in all operational areas. Moreover, DRC also commits to organisational diversity and gender equity through adherence to the DRC Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) Vision and Standards policy.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes, the project measures access of beneficiaries by conducting regular protection mainstreaming FGDs to ensure and verify beneficiaries have access to services. DRC also monitors this through its M&E assessments and evaluations, such as post distribution monitoring (PDM), satisfaction surveys, etc.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes, the project measures safety of beneficiaries by ensuring safety questions are mainstreamed into FGDs and PDMs. DRC also ensure safety of beneficiaries by monitoring the protection environment, having regular discussions and meetings with the community based protection networks, talking to women, to children, to men, and to community leaders. When safety concerns are identified, DRC raises these issues with specific agencies who can respond adequately (safety providers or authorities).

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes, the project measures dignity of beneficiaries by ensuring continuous community engagement and feedback and ensuring safety and dignity of beneficiaries are prioritized throughout the intervention. Through its activities, DRC will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centered-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols.
