Project Summary

UNHCR will undertake the following activities: 1) Community-based protection, including community empowerment of a network of Protection Focal Points (PFPs); 2) Protection monitoring for evidence based and data driven programming and advocacy; 3) Protection services for persons with specific needs, including case management, psychosocial support and legal assistance; and 4) Institutional support with a particular focus on the Ministry of Gender, Child Protection and Social Affairs, the National Institute for Disaster Management (INGC), the District Infrastructure and Planning Service (SDPI) and the National Directorate of Civil Identification.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

UNHCR will undertake the following activities: 1) Community-based protection, including community empowerment of a network of Protection Focal Points (PFPs); 2) Protection monitoring for the purpose of data analysis and advocacy; 3) Protection services for persons with specific needs, including case management, psychosocial support and legal assistance; and 4) Institutional support with a particular focus on the Ministry of Gender, Child Protection and Social Affairs, the National Institute for Disaster Management (INGC), the District Infrastructure and Planning Service (SDPI) and the National Directorate of Civil Identification.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The main objectives of the project is to prevent, mitigate and address the protection risks of affected populations in priority areas. To achieve this, UNHCR will undertake the following activities: 1) Community-based protection: i) Community empowerment: UNHCR will expand its network of Protection Focal Points (PFPs) and youth human rights advocates who will be trained to identify and respond to protection challenges in their areas through a community-based approach. This includes: i) referring persons with specific needs to case management providers and specialized services; ii) disseminating information on human rights, GBV prevention and response, child protection, protection of persons with specific needs and referral pathways; and iii) organizing community-led initiatives. ii) Community outreach: UNHCR will expand its mobile protection teams who will be managed by UNHCR or partners’ staff to provide individual counselling to affected populations, including legal support and assistance on MHPSS and GBV related matters, and conduct identification and referrals of persons with specific needs. 2) Protection monitoring: UNHCR and its implementing partners will collect data at household, group and individual level with the aim of identifying protection gaps and incidents, undertaking analysis of protection and displacement trends, and conducting advocacy with Government and/or clusters. 3) Protection services for persons with specific needs: UNHCR will undertake case management of persons with specific needs, including children at risk, persons with disabilities, victims of violence, GBV survivors, and will provide specialized services such as psychosocial support, legal assistance (specially for legal documentation, birth registration and HLP). 4) Institutional support: UNHCR will strengthen cooperation with the Ministry of Gender, Child Protection and Social Affairs, the National Institute for Disaster Management (INGC), the District Infrastructure and Planning Service (SDPI), the National Directorate of Civil Identification and provincial and municipal authorities, in order to improve the protection of IDPs. UNHCR will provide trainings on international and regional legal framework for internal displacement, GBV prevention and response, child protection, disability inclusion, protection of persons with specific needs and PSEA, among other topics. UNHCR has supported and will continue to support the rehabilitation of infrastructure of Government offices and donate office supplies and IT equipment to them for the civil documentation project in Cabo Delgado as well as in Nampula and Niassa provinces. Finally, UNHCR will continue supporting the domestication of the African Union Convention for the Protection and Assistance of Internally Displaced Persons in Africa (Kampala Convention).

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

UNHCR staff and its partners are trained on humanitarian principles, standards and key-elements of the centrality of protection: do no harm principle, safety and dignity, effective access to services and assistance, accountability to affected populations and community participation and empowerment. In some districts of Nampula province, UNHCR has already started delivering, in coordination with INGD, trainings on protection of IDPs to local authorities in places where IDPs have arrived. These trainings will be extended to the remaining districts of Nampula province that have not yet benefited from these sessions and, therefore, to Niassa and Cabo Delgado Provinces. UNHCR has developed, improved and strengthened protection monitoring tools for identifying risk profiles and collecting data on protection incidents, with indicators of age, gender and diversity (AGD). In the face of any immediate and identified threat to security, an emergency protection or assistance service must be activated. Protection interventions focus on saving lives and guaranteeing the rights to safety and physical integrity of affected populations. In addition, in order to guarantee the dignity of people, it is planned to continue providing timely and quality services taking into account the background of the individuals, the respect for their culture and religion and the survivor-centre approach, while respecting their freedom of choice.UNHCR has developed SOPs for humanitarian assistance and has adopted the Protection cluster vulnerability targeting criteria based on the specific needs of individuals. UNHCR also uses a global toolkit for classifying the level of risk of individuals (Heightened Risk Identification Tool) to assess the level of risks according to which protection and assistance services are provided.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

UNHCR places the affected communities at the center of its programming and conducts frequent consultations with members of community structures to involve them in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of community activities. The main pillar of UNHCR country strategy is the community-based protection (CBP), which includes a section on the enhancement of accountability to affected people (AAP) mechanisms, including community engagement, communication with communities (CwC), and complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFM). In 2022, UNHCR will strengthen the organization’s community complaints and feedback mechanisms to allow people affected by the crisis to have access to information and participate in decision-making processes that affect their lives and be part of projects developed by UNHCR and its partners. Communication with communities will also be strengthened through mobile protection teams and desks set in key-intervention areas, as well as by remote protection monitoring through protection focal points and helplines.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

In Cabo Delgado, UNHCR has provided individual protection assistance based on needs, taking into account the targeting vulnerability criteria agreed by the Protection cluster and the standard specific needs criteria, which include persons with disabilities, prioritizing interventions based on the most common and urgent needs. This approach should be extended to Nampula and Niassa provinces in 2022 to enable harmonised and consistent protection interventions and approaches across the operation. UNHCR protection assessments include disaggregated data on the specific needs considering age gender, diversity and disability approach. UNHCR also conducts specific consultations with persons with disabilities on a regular basis in order to assess their specific protection issues and provide personalized assistance and material support. UNHCR has conducted disability inclusion assessment to understand gaps and challenges of persons with disabilities in accessing available services and based on the results of the assessment, protection program activities will be designed.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

UNHCR takes environmental impact into account in all its operations, given that the environmental damage left by displaced populations could be a major concern. As part of its community-based protection strategy, UNHCR ensures that the communities are sensitized and engaged on the protection of the environment, emphasizing their responsibility for the local environment where they settle. As part of the community initiatives that will be supported in 2022, UNHCR will consider joint eco-friendly projects with IDPs and host communities.
