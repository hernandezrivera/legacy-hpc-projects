Project Summary

In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of the 16.7 million people in need, 5.5 million are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites. After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. The erosion of basic service capacity has continued, with water and sanitation systems, and public health services under immense strain, in a context of barely any development investment. Recurrent disease outbreaks, waterborne diseases, a prolonged drought and water crisis, vaccine-preventable illnesses, and food insecurity are contributing to rising mortality and morbidity.
The main purpose of NCA’s WASH program is to reduce health-related and protection risks among conflict-affected populations by providing sustainable access to safe water, adequate sanitation, and improved hygiene. This will address the need for long term prevention and control measures for maintaining public health, and for promoting socio-economic development among the target communities. The involvement of local stakeholders is important so as humanitarian crises are not used to reduce what needs to be maintained as a public service. The targeting adheres to sector needs and priorities, as identified and reported in the HRP and HNO 2024, this being the best guarantee of a truly good outcome. Activities include the use of renewable energy (solar power) for pumping and distributing water; monitoring and optimising groundwater extraction using remote sensing; operation and maintenance training and tools for pump operators; and the support of vulnerable groups with the distribution of essential hygiene items and awareness that meet family needs and are gender appropriate. In terms of geography, the targeting is informed by the needs as expressed by the severity scale for HNO 2024, with priority given to sub-districts with overall score of 4 or 5. Overall score of 3 is also considered under some conditions, e.g., if corresponding overall score for protection/GBV sectors is high. Relevance with recent, ongoing, and planned projects implemented through NCA directly, and through local partners is also considered.
The selected governorates and potential locations at the sub-district level are:
(i) Damascus (Damascus)
(ii) Rural Damascus (Haran Al'awameed, Kafar Batna),
(iii) Aleppo (As-Safira, Jabal Saman, Al Khafsa),
(iv) Hama (Shat-ha, Oj, Kfar Zeita)
(v) Idlib (Khan Shaykun)
(vi) Lattakia (Lattakia, Al Qardaha)
(vii) Tartous (Sheikh Bader)
(viii) As Sweida (As Sweida)
(ix) Dara’a (Dara’a)
(x) Homs (Hawash)
(xi) Ar Raqqa (Ath Thawra)
In summary, the project will address:
a) Access to safe water (also related to public health and protection risks)
b) Access to essential hygiene items to most vulnerable groups (women, girls, disabled, elderly, etc.)
c) Resilience of public water systems (O&M and environmental protection)
d) Access to safe sanitation facilities
NCA has established office in Damascus since 2021 and has a team consisting of program and support staff. Furthermore, NCA has secured core funding for its direct implementation of WASH activities in selected areas in 2024. This will allow NCA to coordinate with all stakeholders and targeted communities and in close coordination with the WASH Cluster. Activities will be reported in the 4Ws by NCA's program/MEAL team
For this project, NCA will target total of 100,510 people (29,148 W, 26,133 M, 20,102 G,25,127 B), and has secured approx. 56% of requested budget for 2024 activities
