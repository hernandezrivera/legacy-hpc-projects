Project Summary

The emergency nutrition response is targeting children under five years, adolescent girls and women most especially pregnant and lactating mothers from internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities in Akobo County with chronic emergency nutrition needs. The planned nutrition intervention activities will as well be integrated with Health, WASH, Education, FSL and GBV prevention interventions by creating synergies and coordination with Health, GBV and WASH, FSL and Education clusters through Health, GBV and WASH, Education and FSL implementing partners so as to prevent and reduce malnutrition related morbidity and mortality by reducing the prevalence of global acute malnutrition in children under five years and pregnant and lactating mothers (PLWs) in Akobo County.
Therefore, the proposed project activities and targets will be including prevention of malnutrition through BSFP intervention for 6,000 children 6-23 months and 1,500 PLWs, provision of MIYCN counselling to 12,000 caregivers of children under five years, provision of micro nutrient supplementation (Vitamin A) to 15,000 children 6-59 months, deworming for 10,000 children 12-59 months, malnutrition screening for 15,000 children 6-59 months and 8000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW), conducting 12 monthly monitoring and supportive supervision visits, provision of capacity building through on job training and mentoring for staffs, provision of hygiene and sanitation messages to the target beneficiaries, establishment and maintaining of demonstration kitchen gardens at 5 nutrition sites, conducting GBV, conducting cooking demonstration sessions at the nutrition site and community, supporting 60 community nutrition volunteers to undertake home visits, provision of long lasting insecticide treated mosquito nets to 13071 SAM and MAM children and 3000 PLWs with acute malnutrition among others in the project period.
The project intends to support nutrition needs of 25,000 beneficiaries from most vulnerable (IDPs), returnees and host community beneficiaries of Akobo County majorly children under five years, adolescents and women mostly pregnant and lactating mothers. This project comes at a time when Akobo County is struggling with continuous inter communal conflicts causing internal displacements worsening the vulnerability evidenced by high rates of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) due to food insecurity caused by lack of enough food stocks among the internally displaced and returnee community increasing the vulnerability among the host communities and high food prices due to poor access to markets and inflation. The situation has impacted negatively on the already limited basic health, nutrition, WASH and FSL services in this location. Therefore, this project will address the nutrition needs of the most vulnerable women, children (6-59) months and adolescents integrated with GBV risks mitigation, WASH, Health, Food security and livelihood interventions.

1. Needs

Following the post-civil war chronic crisis and intermittent communal conflicts causing internal displacements and natural disasters like floods coupled with the post COVID-19 pandemic global inflation in food and fuel prices and influx of returnees in South Sudan that has deepened the country into chronic humanitarian crisis. The communities of Akobo County have experienced unprecedented chronic humanitarian crisis which has been heightened by the recent influx of returnees from Ethiopia and Sudan, seasonal flooding that has led to loss of harvests, destruction of farmlands and livestock that has plugged this county into severe food insecurity that has led to high prevalence of malnutrition. According to Humanitarian needs overview (HNO 2023), the severity of humanitarian conditions and people in need in Akobo County are in extreme levels due to continued conflict, seasonal flooding, deepening food insecurity, high food prices and inadequate access to primary health care services which has been recently worsened by influx of returnees from Ethiopia and Sudan. According to people in need (PIN) by the nutrition cluster, the GAM rates for Akobo County is 27.9% which are higher that the WHO minimum standard of 15%, a confirmation that this county is facing nutrition related morbidity and mortality. This is worsened by the fact that this county is already struggling with food insecurity, poor primary health care services, inadequate WASH facilities and protection concerns which continuously keep GAM rates above the WHO minimum standards. TADO proposes to bridge this gap by establishing nutrition facilities in these locations mostly in the payams of Bilkey, Gakdong, Nyandit, Dengjok with high population of IDPs, returnees and inadequate health and nutrition services where people walk long distances to receive nutrition services. These payams lack adequate preventive health and nutrition services since the partners supporting curative health and nutrition services do not fully support preventive nutrition services in these communities. TADO will utilize its operational and static presence in Akobo County with field offices where it’s a health implementing partner providing preventive primary health care services mainly routine and ZERO dose children vaccination in five health facilities of Chaan, Kier, Meer, Wechboth, Wechgoak Primary health care units which are mostly not supported by nutrition and health implementing partners meaning they lack basic primary health care including nutrition services but with our supported vaccinators and community mobilizers trained on Boma Health Initiative guidelines, this can create noticeable impact. This vast static presence and experience with community mobilisers and vaccinators already present in Akobo County supported by TADO will be utilized to make sure equitable access to quality preventive nutrition services in in Akobo County are offered to the vulnerable population. This synergy will easily contribute to improve cluster objectives by reducing acute suffering and prevalence of global acute malnutrition in this county. TADO will utilize the strong coordination with the county, state ministry of health authorities and the WASH, FSL, protection health and static nutrition partners in the respective county to make sure quality preventive nutrition services both at community and nutrition facility level are offered to the vulnerable population.

2. Project activities

TADO will address the nutritional challenges of the vulnerable population in Akobo county though the following activities:1. TADO will conduct early detection of malnutrition through nutrition screening at the community level. 2. TADO will provide micronutrient supplementation (Vitamin A) to children (6-59 months) during the national immunization days (NIDs)3. TADO will provide behavioral change messages to the mothers and care takers of malnourished children using maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling cards to improve the nutrition at household level.4. TADO will conduct monthly cooking demonstrations to the beneficiaries so as to improve on the nutrition diversity, meal preparation and nutrition status at household level.5. TADO will carry out deworming for children 12-59 months with albendazole tablets to prevent iron deficiency anemia due to worm infestation during NIDs.6. TADO shall provide WASH kits to the caregivers of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) children to improve on their hygiene and sanitation conditions at household level to reduce the prevalence of diarrheal diseases among these children.7. TADO shall establish and maintain demonstration kitchen gardens at every nutrition site for demonstration to the mothers and care takers of malnourished children so as to improve the availability of kitchen gardens at household level hence improving food security and dietary diversity.8. TADO shall sensitize households on the correct use of aqua tabs and this shall be done by CNVs at community level. 9. TADO shall sensitize communities in county with hygiene and sanitation messages to reduce hygiene related diseases at community level.10. TADO shall coordinate with health facilities to make sure all children with diarrhea are managed.11. TADO shall provide BSFP services to children 6-23 months and PLWs.12. TADO shall make sure AAP feedback mechanisms in the community are established.13. TADO shall support community nutrition volunteers (CNVs) to conduct community nutrition screening and home visits.14. TADO will routinely collect data and from the community and nutrition sites and routinely report to the nutrition cluster County health department and state ministry of health.
