Project Summary

Over May 2023 - January 2024, INTERSOS conducted Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessments and livelihood-specific analyses in multiple sub-districts in Hama, Idleb, and Rural Damascus. Identified needs and gaps include: low access to income generating opportunities due to lacking technical and soft skills (especially among women, youth and PwDs); high socio-economic vulnerability due to depleted livelihoods and inability to afford productive assets. This has been exacerbated by persistent insecurity in many areas, protracted displacement and slow returns (linked with poor reconstruction), economic decline, and earthquake’s impacts. In 2024-2025, INTERSOS will continue integrating its livelihood programme with protection (HSYR24-PRO-212371-1), education (HSYR24-EDU-212411-1), and health (HSYR24-HEA-211951-1) to ensure a holistic response to the increasingly complex needs of local populations. Targeting Hama, Idleb and RD - possibly expanding to Deir-ez-Zor, INTERSOS will primarily focus on reducing the income-expenditure gap of vulnerable individuals, reinforcing HH self-reliance, and reducing the risk of adopting negative coping mechanisms. Focus on vulnerable and at-risk individuals/HHs (e.g. parents/caregivers of OoSC or children at risk of school drop-out, PwDs and their caregivers, female-headed HHs, etc.) wants to mitigate their heightened exposure to protection risks and negative coping mechanisms (e.g. school drop-out, child labour, early marriage, etc.). INTERSOS livelihood response will include vocational and soft skills trainings and on-the-job trainings based on participatory labor market assessments and followed by distribution of start-up kits or productive assets, while providing small grants and business management trainings to promising micro- and small-enterprises. Discrimination/exclusion of females from male-dominated productive/employment sectors (Likelihood: M; Impact: H) will be mitigated through participatory assessments, community engagement, and promoting evidence-based and risk-informed programming. Exclusion of PwDs (L: H; I: H) will be avoided by ensuring their participation across programme cycle, GAD reporting, and reasonable accommodations. Tensions between population groups due to perceived unequal assistance (L: L; I: H) will be mitigated through conflict sensitivity analyses and community consultations. Child labor risks (L: L, I: H) will be prevented through clear selection criteria, regular monitoring, and training on CP and protection mainstreaming. INTERSOS has global PSEA/CP policies applying to all personnel and suppliers, incl. trainers. A country-specific strategy ensures SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP declaration and attend PSEA/CP induction/refreshers (PSEA/CP trainings are also provided to partners). In-country PSEA Referent and 6 PSEA FPs are appointed to receive reports about alleged SEA acts and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Reports can be made in person, to a dedicated email address, by phone or through INTERSOS CFM boxes. Complaints are then referred to the PSEA Referent and investigated. Programme risks are mitigated through robust monitoring and reporting mechanisms, capacity building for staff and partners, and clear engagement protocols with SARC/line ministries. Safety and access risks are mitigated via security assessments, capacity building on relevant protocols, and close coordination with key actors (OCHA, SARC, line ministries, other partners, community networks). Close supervision of the compliance with internal SOPs, rigorous monitoring of financial expenditure and financial planning, frequent due diligence and pre-qualification processes, regular market research and procurement in stocks reduce financial and procurement risks. INTERSOS Contingency Planning Process details risks and mitigation measures for each target governorate.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Promoting integration between Livelihood and Protection, Health and Education responses, INTERSOS will implement the proposed action through 2 modalities: in partnership with SARC or directly under sub-MoUs with MoSAL, MoE or MoH, depending on target locations and best modality to ensure integration with other Sectors. Proposed activities include: 1. Context analyses: to investigate demand and supply of labor/skills in the target locations, identify key barriers to access employment opportunities, and build relationships with local businesses and potential employers. Participatory approaches (FGDs and KIIs with community representatives, DoSAL officials, private sectors and potential employers, etc.) will be applied. 2. Unskilled and semi-skilled individuals will be targeted with market-relevant vocational and soft skills trainings, providing them with start-up kits or productive assets to promote self-employment. 3. On-the-job training sessions (complemented with the provision of soft skills trainings and productive assets) will target semi-skilled individuals to enhance their employability and support viable micro-enterprises. 4. Business training will be delivered to promising micro and small entrepreneurs on topics such as financial management, project development and management, product development, marketing, etc. Upon completion of the training and development of a business plan, these local entrepreneurs will receive small grants to recover/improve their business; in turn, this will support the recovery of affected/impoverished value chains and will generate further job and livelihood opportunities. Beneficiaries will be identified through: 1. Internal referral: INTERSOS teams working in the same locations in complementary projects (Protection, Education, Health) are all trained about safe identification and referral. Vulnerable individuals who may be found to be eligible and interested to be referred to livelihood services will be internally referred to the Livelihood team; 2. Referral from external partners: INTERSOS regularly conducts service mapping in each area of implementation, coordinating with Sectors and partners to share information about available services and defining referral pathways; 3. Referral from community groups established in the target sub-districts will be sensitized about available services; 4. Self-referral: individuals who are interested to participate in project activities will be able to register themselves in the static protection services or with the community groups. In addition, INTERSOS will rehabilitate public facilities such as school and health facilities, providing them with solar-powered systems to promote safe and quality service provision (targets reported under Education and Health responses as well). In 2024-2025, INTERSOS plans to reach 7,890 individuals with 3,122,600 USD budget requirement, including 3,545 individuals (1,439,300 USD) in 2024 and 4,345 individuals (1,683,300 USD) in 2025.
