Project Summary

In the context of a large-scale humanitarian crisis in South Sudan, with severe needs and limited funding, effective aid prioritization becomes an urgent priority. To help fill critical information gaps, REACH will deliver common services for the humanitarian response in South Sudan, providing information and analysis through the Area of Knowledge (AoK) and Port and Road Monitoring (PRM); multi-sector rapid assessments and ensuring that information is accessible and relevant to decision makers by feeding findings into the humanitarian coordination structure through REACH's role as co-chair of the Needs Analysis Working Group

Needs

In the context of a large-scale humanitarian crisis in South Sudan, with severe needs and limited funding, effective aid prioritization becomes an urgent priority. Evidence-based needs analysis is required to ensure assistance is effective and allocated according to needs. However, accessibility and security issues within South Sudan have impeded systematic data collection efforts – limiting the effectiveness of humanitarian planning and implementation inside the country. As the South Sudan crisis continues, it is becoming increasingly important to fill such information gaps in a systematic manner to promote more effective humanitarian response and planning.

Activities

Area of Knowledge remote monitoringThe Area of Knowledge (AoK) project conducts remote data collection from areas inside South Sudan where access to direct regular primary data collection is not possible. The overall objective of the project is to inform aid planning and enhance the understanding of humanitarian context within South Sudan; improve humanitarian access to vulnerable groups; and indirectly monitor the direct outcomes and impacts of large-scale humanitarian interventions. Humanitarian information is currently collected at the settlement (village/neighborhood) level in areas identified with ongoing violence or deepening food insecurity, through participants in static IDP Sites, who have recently visited a remote settlement, or phone calling to participants in remote settlements. As of October 2018, AoK data is collected from 15 field locations, assessing 9 out of the former 10 states (see below for more details). In 2019, REACH plans to continue providing detailed information and analysis on hard to reach areas by continuing data collection in these 15 bases, and expanding to new bases to cover new areas. The data collected will be used to produce monthly factsheets on FSL, WASH, CCCM, NFI and Shelter, and Protection needs, as well as quarterly situation overviews providing multi-sectoral analysis at the state level. Port and Road Monitoring REACH will continue to conduct the Port and Road Monitoring project. Since 2017 has been conducting monthly Port Monitoring exercises since 2017 in Akobo, Jonglei State; Nyal, Unity State; Yambio, Western Equatoria State; Kapoeta, Eastern Equatoria State; and Renk, Upper Nile State to better understand the movement of South Sudanese across the borders of Sudan, Ethiopia, Kenya, and the DRC, in addition to movement into and out of key areas of concern within South Sudan. The outputs from this assessment provide an indicative understanding of the origin and destination of migrants, push and pull factors, and intentions of permanent resettlement in destination areas. In 2019 REACH plans to consolidate and expand the PRM activities to provide a more comprehensive overview of cross-border movement in South Sudan. The data gathered will be used to produce monthly factsheets detailing scale of the movement, demographics and push and pull factors. Multi-sector rapid assessmentsSince 2016, REACH has deployed over 20 teams to conduct rapid assessments in locations such as Baggari, Raja, Pibor, Canal/Pigi, Yirol West, Mundri’s, Moyo and the Kapoetas. Utilizing REACH’s remote monitoring system and engaging with external partners, REACH identifies critical information gaps or locations which have indicative reports of worsening humanitarian needs. To verify these needs, REACH conducts rapid assessments to remote locations to identify the severity of needs and which sectors are worst affected. In 2019, REACH will develop, in close collaboration with the ICWG an agreed upon multi-sectoral rapid needs assessment tool, which can be deployed by individual agencies, to ensure both REACH and other partner assessments are using the same tools, in order to enable comparable needs analysis from country-wide assessments.

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

REACH conducts quarterly community feedback sessions, in which information collected is shared back by REACH teams in 10 of our field sites. REACH further shares all information products online making them available to the wider public.
