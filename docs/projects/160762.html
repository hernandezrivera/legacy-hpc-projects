Project Summary

In alignment with the Humanitarian Response Plan 2020 and the United Nations Strategy on Mine Action 2019-2023, UNMAS will field four Quick Response Teams (QRT) with embedded Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) Officers and Community Liaison Officers (CLOs) to provide the following services: explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) spot tasks, survey and clearance of confirmed hazardous areas (calculated in square meters, as well as conduct Explosive Ordnance Education (EORE). UNMAS intends to deliver EORE session to 45,000 beneficiaries disaggregated as follows 13,500 boys, 13,500 girls, 11,250 women and 6,750 men. During the EORE sessions, further information will be gathered by the EORE and CLOs on suspicious hazards, which will then be shared with the clearance teams for surveying, marking and clearance. EORE alerts communities on how to identify, avoid and report explosive hazards they may encounter to reduce their threat of injury or death. Additionally, the clearance teams will survey and clear 600,000 square meters of land.
The four clearance teams will be highly mobile in order to respond to urgent and prioritized tasks throughout South Sudan. The teams will provide survey and clearance of explosive hazards as well as provide risk education to women, men, boys, and girls to mitigate the threat of injury or death from explosive hazards. During the quick response tasks, the EORE component of the team will also deliver risk education to the surrounding communities.

1 - Needs

As of October 2019, there are approximately 467 hazardous areas recorded in the Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) database, which are either suspected or confirmed to be contaminated by landmines or explosive remnants of war (ERW). However, the total extent of contamination across South Sudan is unknown and particularly in areas ongoing conflict as illustrated in the Greater Equatoria Region. Therefore, there is a need to survey and locate unreported or suspected hazardous areas as well as to conduct clearance and removal of ERW. The legacy of a protracted and multi-dimensional conflict has resulted in civilians living in close proximity to landmines and ERW. Specifically, there are seven counties: Canal, Juba, Magwi, Morobo, Terekeka, Torit, and Yei, with the highest recorded confirmed hazardous areas; moreover, an additional 22 additional counties are contaminated with hazardous areas. These explosive hazards threaten the lives of civilians, particularly those fleeing active armed clashes. It further inhibits the return of displaced persons, the resumption of livelihoods, and the delivery of emergency and life-saving assistance. During the removal and clearance of explosive hazards, concurrent provision of EORE will ensure that communities and individuals are able to develop strategies and coping mechanisms to reduce the threat of landmines and ERW. The teams will be highly mobile and able to rapidly deploy to situations which need immediate or emergency response in priority locations to enhance protection needs, support the returns of internally displaced persons (IDPs) or refugees, as well as support other humanitarian agencies delivering aid to vulnerable populations.

2 - Activities

At the technical level, UNMAS ensures that all mine action activities are conducted in accordance with the International Mine Action Standards (IMAS), the International Ammunition Technical Guidelines and Standards (IATG), the Strategy of the United Nations on Mine Action 2019-2023, and the National Standards and Technical Guidelines (NTSG).Survey, clearance and risk education staff are trained and accredited by UNMAS annually. UNMAS will ensure effective prioritization of tasked locations (in coordination with key stakeholders, including relevant cluster partners in addition to Protection, WASH, and FSL). Technical and non-technical surveys to locate the presence or absence of explosive hazards will commence. Confirmed hazardous areas (CHA) will be recorded, marked and cleared. The surrounding communities will be informed and made aware of the status of the hazardous area. Once the hazardous area has been cleared, the land will be handed over to the community in liaison with the local authorities and the National Mine Action Authority (NMAA). The IMSMA database will record clearance progress and update maps accordingly on monthly basis and the updated information will be shared with stakeholders. CL Officers will provide EORE tailored to the needs of the audience (internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, host communities, children, men and women to receive targeted messaging and teaching methods).

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Further consultation between the relevant clusters (Health, Child Protection, etc.) to ensure where possible pathway referrals for survivors of explosive hazard accidents are shared with the community. The purpose of this project is to respond to contamination by explosive hazards and remove them, so that civilians are able to enjoy a safe and secure environment. Due to the location of confirmed hazardous areas in South Sudan, the majority of which are in the Equatorias, there is a potential for the ongoing conflict to hinder or block the ability of UNMAS to access those areas and provide assistance.
