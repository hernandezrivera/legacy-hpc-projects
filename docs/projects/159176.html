Project Summary

According to FSNAU-FEWS-NET- Post-Gu-Technical-Release – Sept 2019, over 60% of the population in Bay region (i.e. 485,000 people) will most likely face Acute Food Insecurity through December this year, of which 267,000 are facing IPC 3 and 4 and Qansahdhere is not exceptional. Therefore, URRO proposes an integrated drought response intervention providing immediate lifesaving child and maternal focused Health and Nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups including IDPs in Qansahdhere district with the intention of reducing morbidity and mortality related to acute malnutrition. Malnutrition remains the major problem persisted over time in Somalia with high levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) well above emergency threshold of 15% (NCC – Detailed Input for HNOHRP 2019_Revised) and the humanitarian situation continued to deteriorate because of the poor rains in Gu, causing widespread food insecurity, deepening health, nutritional and water related issues and exacerbated by large-scale movement of people in Bay and Bakool regions.
About 20,875 of the Qansahdhere population are estimated to be U5 (i.e. 20% of UNFPA 2014 total population in Qansahdhere) and equal or more others of pregnant and lactating women (PLW) as well as a good number of men (fathers etc) are expected to benefit from the project in one way or the other. Therefore, over 50,000 population in Qansahdhere from all livelihood zones including IDPs and returnees will indirectly benefit from this program, while, targeting 16,000 drought affected boys, girls, women and men in the district – 6500 children under the age of five (U5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) are among the project direct beneficiaries who will equally benefit from both components of the proposed drought response program (Health and Nutrition)
The proposed project intends to increase access to an essential package of health services for drought affected under five children and pregnant and lactating women including inpatient maternal, newborn and child health (MNCH) services, antenatal (ANC) and postnatal (PNC) care services, skilled birth attendance and basic emergency obstetric neonatal care (BEMONC) services, Health Care Workers training on standard case management protocols, health education and awareness raising campaigns that encompasses a full range of sexual and reproductive health services and on sexual and gender-based violence response protocols. The program will also train community health workers (CHWs) on health promotion, diagnosing common diseases, referring patients to health facilities, gender-sensitive health promotion and behaviour change communications (BCC) to drive demand for services. To achieve all these, project will purchase of very important equipment for URRO MCH in Qansahdhere.
The provision of life-saving maternal and child health care services can be effectively succeeded when complemented with nutrition section, thus, the proposed intervention will also Increase access to gender-sensitive nutrition interventions aimed at preventing, identifying and treating acute malnutrition among children under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women. This will include: Outpatient Therapeutic Feeding (OTP) services for children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM); Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) services provided to pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and children under five years with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM); Community Volunteers, especially women, trained in the identification, referral and follow-up of children with SAM and gender-sensitive information, education and communication (IEC) materials with infant and young child feeding (IYCF) messages produced and disseminated.
