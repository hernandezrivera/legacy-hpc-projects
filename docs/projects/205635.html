Project Summary

This project is seeking to respond to the critical integrated lifesaving, Nutrition, health and food security needs of the IDPs, returnees and host communities in Renk, Twic and Ulang Counties in Upper Nile and Warrap States targeting a total of 103,985( Renk= 7,772, Ulang= 19,659 and Twic = 76,554)
Beneficiaries. Of these total targets MAM under 5= 48,163 and MAM PLW= 32,745 and SAM under-five 23,078. All three counties have a GAM rate above the WHO 15% percent thresh hold (Renk= 25.1%, Ulang= 26.4% and Twic 25.3%. all three counties are categorized as IPC phase 4. The main objective is proving critical lifesaving nutrition support to vulnerable populations including women and children with a specific focus on reducing malnutrition prevalence rates amongst children 6-59 months and pregnant women (PLWs)
This will be implemented by GOAL as an integrated package of the Nutrition, health, and the Food security and livelihood interventions. This will contribute to the over objective of the nutrition sector, is aligned with the South Sudan ministry of health strategi plan. This project is seeking to create a nexus between emergency, transition, and sustainable nutrition outcomes.
The proposed response aligns with the HRP 2024 priorities for Nutrition food security and livelihoods, and health cluster.
Strategies and the interventions will meet both immediate and lifesaving needs among affected populations while strengthening local capacity to deliver high quality service and
contributing to reducing dependencies on food assistance as well as fostering longer term behavior change outcome.

GOAL currently have nutrition grants in Renk, Ulang and Twic and is seeking to build on those to reach more facilities with an integrated package of interventions that will help to address the root causes of undernutrition. The plan is to integrate our health interventions in the same area. GOAL will undertake formative research to help inform its intervention and improve the impact.
This project will be implemented in all Payams in the Ulang, Renk, Twic and Kajo keji Counties targeting all the payams.

GOAL is going to work closely with the County Health Departments in all counties and their respective State Ministry of Health making sure the capacity of these institutions is built as part of the system building resilient and sustainability. Key activities will include providing integrated preventive and curative nutrition services targeting the most vulnerable groups including responding to severe and moderate acute malnutrition and improving maternal infant and young child feeding practices. leverage on support of other sectors to provide a package that is not only cost effective but also high impact.

1. Needs

According to the 2023 INFORM Risk Index, South Sudan is ranked as the second most vulnerable country globally to the impacts of natural hazards, including droughts and flooding.Malnutrition remains a persistent problem in South Sudan, with millions of children and women still suffering.0ver 50 % of children are sick with diarrhea, malaria and upper respiratory infections and require treatment, while health services coverage remains low in the country. The food security situation remains very bad with 62 % of the population are food insecure. The WASH and sanitation situation is bad shape as over 50% have no safe water or sanitation.According to the nutrition cluster updates,1.5 million children are at risk of wasting and require urgent lifesaving treatment poor Infant and Young Child Feeding Practices. 0.6 million women are at risk of wasting Children with severe wasting are 12 times at risk of dying if no treatment is provided Children with moderate wasting are 3 times at risk of dying if no treatment is provided 74% of young children do not get minimum acceptable diet for them to grow and develop well 62 % of the population are food insecure . Malnourished women of reproductive age have a higher chance of giving birth to smaller babies, continuing the circle of malnutrition in future generations South Sudan has one of the highest maternal mortality rates in the world—2,054 deaths per 100,000 birthsSouth Sudan continues to face overlapping crises, including floods, conflict and food insecurity, which contribute to persisting high levels of malnutrition. Preliminary results from the FSNMS Round 28 and the 2022 Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) surveys show that children between 6 and 59 months suffering from acute malnutrition remain at an average of 16 per cent in the lean season, surpassing the emergency threshold of 15 per cent and thus considered very high. According to the Nutrition Cluster PIN 2024, the average GAM rate in the three counties (Renk, Twic and Ulang) is above 25 which is way above what is projected as the national average during the lean season and far above the WHO thresh holds for emergencies. High food insecurity, lack of hygiene, sanitation, health and environmental services and inadequate maternal and childcare continue to drive malnutrition levels higher, negatively affecting the health and well-being of women and children.About 2.5 million people need nutrition services alone,65% children under5 and 35 % Pregnant and lactating women with over 143,000 in extreme need in Twic, Renk and Ulang Counties The three counties targeted for this project, Renk Twic and Ulang are among the worst affected areas in the country. Renk and Twic are along the Sudan border with high influx of people crossing from Sudan. This is putting more burden on an already over stretched counties. The population is affected by a multitude of complex challenges, shocks, and stresses, because of the prolonged impacts of conflict, inter- and intra-ethnic disputes, climate-related hazards (such as flooding, and unpredictable rainfall), and economic shocks. The impact of these shocks and stresses continuously undermines development, frequently resulting in disrupted agricultural production and loss of harvests; loss of livelihoods, property, and assets; mass displacement; inter community tensions; destruction of infrastructure, dysfunctional markets, and disrupted trade routes. Cyclical conflicts and climate-induced disasters are becoming more frequent and more intense. The populations suffer from chronic lack of access to food and sustainable livelihood options, forcing households to resort to negative coping mechanisms detrimental to health, well-being, and long-term development.

2. Project activities

The project activities are aligned with the nutrition cluster objectives, and the ministry of health priorities include: The project activities are aligned to and contribute to the cluster nutrition cluster objectives and they include: GOAL will undertake a formative research through qualitative assessment, to understand the specific practices that are affecting these emergency impacted populations and talk to communities about what could be done to address the poor practices = MIYCN emergency assessment GOAL will seek to adopt and adapt existing counselling cards and other communication materials to fit the context of the population based on findings from the formative research Conduct MIYCN counselling seasons in all the nutrition sites using IYCF charts provided by MOH/UNICEF/WHO/WFP to caregivers and engage community gate keepers through inter-generational dialogues sessions informed by the findings of the formative research. Train care givers on family MUAC screening. Conduct community or household level/Family MUAC screening of children 6-59mnth for acute malnutrition and refer to nutrition sites. Support community outreach, community screening for malnutrition for early identification and referral. Build the capacity of nutrition providers to provide integrated management of malnutrition(IMAM) and sessions not covering not only the generic training on IMAM but also on the simplified approaches.Admit and treat all children with SAM in IMAM program Treatment of Moderate acute malnutrition in children 6-59month. Admit and treat all children with MAM in CMAM program. Conduct training to all the nutrition staffs on CMAM program. Support to VASD campaign, additional budget to supplement VASD NIDs campaigns. Treat Moderate acute malnutrition in Pregnant and Lactating women. Conduct GBV safety audits in all the nutrition sites in in the three Counties. Conduct training on protection/GBV to 130 nutrition staffs in across the three counties. Conduct malaria testing using RDT for children with SAM and MAM in all the nutrition sites. Integrate child stimulation and early childhood development services in the provision of care for children with acute malnutrition. Collaborate with health cluster to ensure prevention, screening and treatment of malaria and management of diarrhoea at the nutrition service delivery. Work with the WASH Cluster to ensure provision of WASH kits to children with acute malnutrition and sensitize communities with hygiene and sanitation messages- soap and improvement of WASH and IPC in Nutrition facilities.Deliver blanket supplementary feeding programmes to vulnerable childrenSupport micronutrient supplements to young children and women. Work with the Protection Cluster to ensure mainstreaming of services and referral of child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV) cases identified in the nutrition sites.Collaborate with health cluster in the management of diarrhoea, including providing Zinc and ORS to children with diarrhoea.Undertake community engagement activities during the design, implementation, and monitoring of nutrition services to strengthen Accountability to Affected Populations.Conduct regular program and field monitoring and joint supportive supervision to strengthen evidence-based decision-making.Support nutrition coordination meetings with the government, implementing partners, and donors. This project will be integrated with Health,, WASH, Food Security and livelihood Cluster. With health, the project will seek to integrate with ongoing health programs that GOAL is implementing in the same area but also trying to implement nutrition activities in facilities where health is implemented. Specifically, we will integrate with EPI, maternal and child health intervention, deworming, SRH especially looking for opportunities to integrate family planning intervention during nutrition activities. With FSL, we will integrate on the establish kitchen garden
