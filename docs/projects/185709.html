Project Summary

CARE proposes an integrated approach to gender-based violence and sexual and reproductive health that will reach 26000 people living in Camps 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 and Palonkhali and Jaliapalong Host Community in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. The project activities are informed by CARE’s current rapid gender analysis and operational expertise and are adapted to meet the differing needs of men, women, boys and adolescent girls with and without person with disability. Women and girls will be provided with life-saving GBV services, resilience building and empowerment programming which will include Case Management, Structured Psychosocial support services (PSS), skill building of the women and girls, capacity strengthening of the community activists and service providers, etc. Additionally through community mobilization women, men, boys and girls, will be reached with proven strategies, to address negative social norms that fuel GBV among the population and COVID-19 awareness.
The project will take special consideration of COVID-19 context and high chances of infection. COVID-19 prevention practices will be adapted and COVID-19 prevention messages will be integrated in awareness raising activities. CARE will coordinate with CiC, protection actors, community leaders to promote social cohesion while implementing the project. The project will pay attention that while implementing activities it will not contribute in destroying environmental aspect.
CARE will continue assessing how well the program is meeting quality and standards, accountability commitments as well as the adaptation of programs based on learning. CARE will ensure robust M&E system to assess program quality and evidences by collecting and analyzing both qualitative and quantitative data. Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) is central to all CARE programs and services and in connection to that, a responsive feedback and complaint mechanism will be functioned which will be safe and accessible for all members of the community including women, men, girls and boys. The target communities will be engaged and consulted throughout the intervention through community meetings, focus group discussion, different assessments and routine M&E activities. There will be continuous and timely sharing of information and communications with communities about rights and entitlements and mechanisms to share feedback and concerns. Based on the feedback and views of the community, the program design will make necessary adaptations.
A systematic process will be followed to capture learning and reflections. Programming will be implemented, informed and driven by current sex, age and disability-disaggregated data, ensuring activities meet the differentiated needs of direct and indirect beneficiaries. Based on the trends identified, the programmatic emphasis will be placed on offering services, support, and resilience building tools for women girls, men, boys, PWD and gender diverse population. Activities will be undertaken in accordance with CARE’s protection policies and will address the unique needs of vulnerable populations.
CARE will continue it's strong coordination with GBV SS, GBV IMS, Case Management working group and other relevant working groups to ensure harmonised, quality and collaborative GBV services.

1 - Identified Needs

Gender-based violence (GBV) in humanitarian contexts represents a global issue of grave concern, disproportionately affecting women and girls. According to the inter-agency Gender Based Violence Information Management System (GBV IMS) 2021 second quarter reports, intimate partner violence (IPV) has been identified as the overwhelmingly dominant form of sexual & gender-based violence reported in Cox’s Bazar. Intimate Partner Violence (IPV) remains a large percentage of all reported incidents. The average of reported IPV incidents in 2021 April to June recorded 84%. The increase in percentage of reported incidents in survivors’ residences (90.5%) and public toilets/latrines. The report illustrates the need to strengthen the security and safety of these locations through awareness and prevention mechanisms. According to JMSNA 2021, there is 34% sectoral Living Standards Gaps (LSGs) under protection. 10% of households reported that the safety and security situation has deteriorated in their area of residence. According to Inter Agency research report 2020 by UNHCR, CARE & Action Aid, Rohingya girls and young women are particularly vulnerable to child marriage, sexual abuse and trafficking. Women and girls, particularly those living with disabilities, and Hijras, are vulnerable to physical, psychological and sexual harassment and abuse. Due to refugees’ heavy reliance on aid, there is a risk of SEA by humanitarian workers in the camps. Women and girls in polygamous marriage situations are particularly at risk of experiencing IPV.Married girls are at higher risk of IPV than their unmarried counterparts in the camps. For instance, 17% of older (15 to 19 years) married girls have experienced violence in the past 12 months compared to 4% of the older unmarried girls. Mainly women and girls lack adequate access to information, coupled with insufficient provision and institutional barriers for SGBV prevention and response. Inadequate SGBV outreach activities and social stigma result in under-reporting of sexual abuse, and IPV cases. There is a gap in understanding the concepts of protection and gender among some key humanitarian actors, including a lack of regular and systematized sensitization on rights-based and refugee rights issues. Overall coordination on gender-specific protection issues in the response still requires improvement. There is inadequate understanding of the Rohingya culture and local context among humanitarian actors that acts as a barrier for undertaking culturally-sensitive interventions. For instance, the economic empowerment agenda mostly targeted women, excluding men to some degree, which led to SGBV incidents being perpetrated against women. According to RGA – COVID-19(ISCG, Gender hub, CARE, OXFAM), safety and security risks have increased during the pandemic for all, but pre-existing gendered risks are exacerbated Since the COVID-19 crisis. 43 % women & girls and 53% men & boys has reported Safety and security risks have increased during the pandemic outside the house and 20% women & girls and 18 % men & boys has reported Safety and security risks have increased during the pandemic inside the house in the host community. 42 % Rohingya women and girls and 20% men & boys reported the Safety and security risks have increased inside the house and 25 % women & girls and 51 % men & boys have reported the Safety and security risks have increased outside the house during the COVID-19 crisis. (Rapid Gender Analysis’20 on COVID-19). The UN Secretary General called the dire GBV situation as the Shadow pandemic. However, GBV and protection needs were listed as non-essential humanitarian response during COVID-19 restrictions in Cox limited programs to address SGBV. Movement restrictions and suspension of GBV services during COVID-19 exacerbated the barriers to report GBV cases.

2 - Activities or outputs

1.Implementation of GBV primary prevention methods such as Girl shine, EMAP: Women and girls and women and girls with disabilities will be engaged through Girl Shine and Women Lead in Emergencies (WLiE) approaches, and men and boys, men and boys with disabilities will be engaged using EMAP module to address GBV and discriminatory gender social norms. 2.Training and engagement of Community Outreach group which will comprise of men and women, PWDs and gender diverse population in GBV prevention strategies using tested social changes approches in the camps and targeted host community3.Outreach activities on GBV & COVID-19 and sharing the GBV services related information targeting men, women, boys, girls, PWDs and gender diverse population at host and refugee communities. 4.Sensitization session with community & religious leaders, CiC to change harmful social norms and practices. CARE will put proactive effort to engage women group and women leaders engage in these sensitization sessions. 5.Providing survivor centered GBV case management services to women and girls and referrals to appropriate need basis services to survivors. CARE will coordinate with CP partners to support child survivors. CARE will upgrade the 6 WGSSs to improve safe access by PWDs. 6.Providing psychosocial support to women and girls and women and girls with disabilities and gender diverse population: CARE will provide individual and group PSS support and engage GBV survivors in recreational activities to overcome trauma, stress, terror associatedwith GBV incidents.7.Dignity Kits & MHM kits distribution: MHM kits to CARE GBV team will coordinate with SMSD and WASH team to distribute dignity kits and MHM kits as part of contingency plan. CARE will also mobilize GBV team to conduct group counselling services and awareness raising and information dissemination on GBV, referrals and PSEA. Reproductive age group women and girls will be supported with dignity kit to promote menstrual hygiene and SRHR.8.Skills development and empowerment training for women and girls in the targeted rohingya and host community9.Conducting training to GBV service providers and other humanitarian staff on protection, GBV pocket guide and GBV IASC Guidelines, in the camps and targeted host community10.Awareness raising campaign, day celebrations (IWD, 16 Days of Activism): 8300 women, men, boys, girls, PWD and gender diverse population will reach out throughcommunity awareness campaigns and day celebrations through IEC materials, public address system. Comprehensive educational campaigns will be conducted through clear and specific messaging in GBV and COVID-19. 11.Conduct training on GBV case workers and Response Officers case management, GBVIMS, CMR, GBV IASC Guidelines.
