Project Summary

Needs: The 2024 HNO Summary states that there are 13.6M people in need of WASH assistance. Furthermore, there are 125 districts that are categorized as severity score 4 or 5 for WASH needs. ZOA has also conducted their own needs assessment in the target locations, which confirm the need for urgent WASH activities, especially in terms of rehabilitation of WASH infrastructure to improve access to adequate safe and clean water. The selected activities are either based on the needs that ZOA team came across during the (evaluation and monitoring of) activities implemented during 2023, or recommendations by local population, WASH sector and authorities, which ZOA all takes very seriously. Therefore, before the final design of any project ZOA will consult the concerned ministry, local authorities, WASH sector and organizes FGDs with different age groups of the community, so that the activities really serve their needs. During 2022 ZOA has signed an MoU with the Ministry of Water Resources, which facilitate and speed up approvals.
Implementation of activities: ZOA plans to provide an integrated package of WASH activities. The overall objective is to provide support to water and sanitation/sewage systems to ensure regular services for vulnerable households in areas with high severity of needs. The activities are aligned with WASH sector objective 4: ‘Support and restore water, sanitation and solid waste management systems to ensure continuity of services for most vulnerable affected people in Syria’. ZOA’s intervention will contribute to improving access to water for both domestic and agricultural use, as well as restoring sanitation infrastructure and promotion of improved hygienic practices. Activities include: 1) Rehabilitation of water infrastructure, including water pumping stations, pipelines and filtration stations, 2) Emergency water trucking (temporarily, to cover period of system repairs), 3) Provision of HH water tanks, 4) Rehabilitation of sewage systems, 5) Community level Hygiene promotion campaigns, 6) Provision of hygiene and/or dignity kits. Since electricity is a major hurdle ZOA has taken into consideration the provision of solar energy systems wherever possible. Upon completion, ZOA will hand over the completed verified work to the relevant authorities. Manager of Program Quality supervises MEAL officers in-country. ZOA will monitor its interventions based on the logical framework indicators & activities with sex, age & disability disaggregated. Field level monitoring will be key in quality assurance, tracking progress, improved accountability & for demonstrating impact.
Risks, assumptions and mitigation measures
1. Changes in exchange rate & increasing inflation: ZOA closely monitors market prices, and contractors are committed to their offers for a specific period to avoid big changes in pricing due to exchange rate changes or inflation.
2. Lack of fuel availability: ZOA has contracts with several transportation service providers, allowing us to conduct the necessary trips without major constraints.
3. Lack of skilled labor: ZOA already has contracts with experienced local skilled laborers.
4. Delayed approvals: ZOA has an MOU with MOWR to facilitate faster approvals.
5. Security risks affecting implementation in the field: ZOA continually assesses the situation in the field and coordinates wit INSO, UNDSS, local authorities and community leaders.
6. Fraud and corruption: ZOA has clear policies preventing and mitigating risks of fraud and corruption including segregation of duties, and regularly trains its staff on these topics. All financial transactions are documented, filed and monitored and regular spot-checks and audits are conducted. Communities and stakeholders are well informed about their entitlements using multiple channels and good reporting channels are in place.
7. Protection risks: Please refer to protection risk analysis and PSEA strategy under Project summary extension in the response plan.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis: The project will adopt an inclusive and non-discriminatory approach so that all groups can safely and equitably access assistance. Target populations are at the center of decision-making processes throughout the project, for example consulting on the eligibility for assistance, which are based on context specific needs, preferences and vulnerabilities. This project considers the safety and dignity of beneficiaries to avoid creating or exacerbating protection risks for individuals, households or communities. (i.e. safety of beneficiaries, social tensions, intra-household dynamics). Project related protection risks include potential abuse, exploitation or other unacceptable behaviors by contractors, suppliers or staff within the community during the implementation of the works or during distributions. Therefore, all contractors, suppliers and ZOA staff/ volunteers will sign ZOA’s Code of Conduct. Also, contractors are required to provide a safe and secure work environment for their workers. Multi-channel feedback mechanisms are in place and ZOA staff knows how to deal with different types of feedback, including referrals for psychological and protection services and support. All staff including volunteers are regularly trained on protection, PSEAH, ZOA’s CoC and child protection. PSEA Strategy: All ZOA staff, volunteers and contractors sign a Code of Conduct informing them of their obligations as well as consequences of involvement in sexual exploitation and abuse. Staff is regularly trained on PSEA and knows how to make safe referrals to relevant services for people identified to be in need of specialized protection services in coordination with the Protection Sector. The project will put in place and inform beneficiaries about several accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries, staff and partners, that include a variety of confidential channels, eg. hotlines, social media, complaints box. Please note that the activities mentioned in the summary are covering two years (2024-2025). However, the targets and budget is covering only 2024. Additionally, for 2025, ZOA aims to support an estimated 700,000 beneficiaries with a budget of around 4.5M USD
