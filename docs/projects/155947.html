Project Summary

The project will be implemented in three phases as following:
First phase: Beneficiaries’ selection from host community and IDPs is according to these criteria:
1- Youth and girls (Jobless).
2- Female-headed households.
3- GBV women survivor.
4- families with > 6 members and have no fixed income.
5- Women which were released from detention.
6- people which owner in difficulties project.
At this phase, 450 IDPs and host communities (350 women +100 youth) from each region will be selected according to the selection criteria above.
WSA will depends on the principle of points in the selection of beneficiaries.
Second phase:
Training all the beneficiaries on the mechanism of study and analysis of local markets and feasibility study of projects and budgeting and sustainability of projects.
Third phase: The design which will be implemented as following
- 450 Beneficiaries in three regions will take the lead of conducting Market Assessment to determine what are the elements, gaps and the opportunities for livelihoods, and interview employers through employers' survey, so the beneficiaries themselves study and analyze local markets in order to determine what is suitable to them and to acquire sense of ownership of these projects).
- Distribution of beneficiaries to groups in each region as following:
The first group is 300 beneficiaries who do not have small projects where each two of the beneficiaries (IDPs and host community) will submit a small project proposal
The number of small projects to be received is 120.
The second group is 120 beneficiaries, who have small in difficulties projects where they will prepare study for their projects and the reasons for their in difficulties projects.
The number of small Stumbling projects to be received is 120
- Receiving project proposals from the beneficiaries and forming a committee in HQ to select acceptable small projects according to criteria (economically viable projects - sustainable projects - income-generating projects - projects that achieve optimum utilization of natural resources-Possibility of projects for the employment of the jobless……).
- Funding acceptable projects as following:
Funding 20 small projects in each region for $ 1000, Which meet previous criteria.
The number of small projects funded in the three regions is 60

Funding 20 small in difficulties projects in each region for $ 500 Which meet previous criteria.

WSA will integrate gender-based violence prevention activities into project activities through the development of an action plan that will incorporate the principles of protection during project implementation stages.
Throughout the WGSS: Women and Girls Safe Spaces which WSA is managing as sustainable community centers in Khan Shaykun and Bennsh and Madiq Castle. They are called SALAM center 1 in Khan Shykun and SALAM center 2 in Madiq Castle. (SALAM center 3 is in Madiq castle).
Those centers are providing GBV (Prevention and response) services for women and girls. The project is funded by THF 7012 now and then by THF 9297 afterwards. Needs

• The large-scale population movements; the widespread destruction and contamination of agriculture related infrastructure and value chains such as markets and bakeries; depletion of productive assets and savings, increasing debt; and limited economic opportunities have all contributed to socioeconomic hardship and the disruption of livelihoods. This has led to high levels of poverty across Syria, with 69 per cent of the population estimated to be living in extreme poverty. As a result, the coping capacity of many people in the most affected communities in Syria has been nearly exhausted. Households are resorting to harmful coping strategies that disproportionately affect the most vulnerable segments of the population, specifically children, youth and adolescents. These mechanisms include cutting back food consumption, spending savings and accumulating debt. Such coping mechanisms are not only negative and unsustainable but, once exhausted, prompt people to resort to increasingly exploitative and hazardous activities such as child labor and recruitment, early marriage, and engagement with armed groups 18 Increased efforts to support the ability of households and communities to withstand current and future shocks are therefore essential (HNO 2108).• WSA has gathered information through the continuous visits from the women and the girls to its GVB centers during the last two years and through the focused group discussions with women, girls and the young people in addition to the local councils.• The results showed that the economic situation for the women is miserable especially for those who lost their husband and they had to work very hard jobs in order to feed their hungry children, or to wait for the food basket aid.• the results have shown that there was a neglected group, who are the women who were released from the regime prisons, those women are living under disastrous situations after their husbands left them alone or they face unacceptance by the community at the time they are in very great need of support to start a new life which might help them to overcome what did they face in the prison.• the general feeling of the young people is they were forming a new pressure on their families who already suffering from the general bad financial situation, which led the young men to join the armed groups for the sake of money and push the girls into leaving the schools and getting married in an early age.• On the other hand, the recent movement of IDPs from Homs, rural Damascus and recently Dar'a , had very bad impacts on both the IDPs and the host community, and these impacts have affected the economic situation, the movement of the cash a lot with lack of job opportunities • WSA believes that the best investment is the investment in the human resources and especially the young people, and according to this WSA has designed this project on the basis of strengthening the resilience of this young group, which is the cornerstone of Syria's future.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1:Some people and dominant groups in the work area do not accept the idea that there are particular centers for women and girls especially that our our center is the ﬁrst one of its kind in the area. Risk Level : High Mitigation Measure: External awareness sessions to introduce the center's activities. Risk 2: Distinguishing between residents and displaced citizens Risk Level: Average /Medial Mitigation Measure : Full survey of the percentage of the displaced people in the region and implementing external activities for them and inviting them to the center. Risk 3: Distinguishing between residents of the city and the residents of the neighbourhood areas Risk Level: High Mitigation Measure: Increase the external activities in the neighbourhood villages more and more.Risk 4: threats of kidnapping the workers in organizations that have been widely spread in the Syrian inside Risk Level : High Mitigation Measure : district of safety and security rules in terms of mobility and not to declare the dates of mobility and not to paste any banners of the association logo on the cars.Risk 5: The inability to meet the needs of all women in the region that we work in. In terms we are the lonely center that is specialized in the service GBV Risk Level : Average / Medial Mitigation Measure : Prioritizing the women according to their needs and provide them the services they need. Risk 6: Fear of the inability to keep the conﬁdential and secret documents safe due to the emergency security incidents. Risk Level: High. Mitigation Measure: Archiving all the documents on confidential electronic system and destroying the paper documents periodically as soon as they are completed and done.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks are divided into security and program risks Security risks: A- Shelling / It is one of the major threats in Syria, especially with the escalation of military conﬂict in northern Syria in this period. The shelling can be direct or indirect. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Choose response centers which they are far from military locations. 2- Coordination and continuous communication with the S&S assistant and local authorities to ensure the safety of staff and beneﬁciaries. 3- Store relief items in small quantities and multiple locations. 4- Suspension of work temporarily in case of intensiﬁed shelling 5- Daily Risk Assessment- movement clearance for staff 6- Evacuation plans 7- Avoiding the crowding of beneﬁciaries during activities. B- Unforeseen Changes in the Conﬂict / This risk including that staff of organization cannot safely access locations, staff get besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Continue to gather information from range of sources. 2- Develop contingency and evacuation plan to work in alternative location. 3- Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space. 4- Training to the staff about contingency and evacuation plan in addition to Information Security 5- Duty of staff C- IEDs/VBIEDs/PBIEDs/ Can be direct or indirect. It is one of the major threats in Syria. Solution/ Mitigation measures: 1- Choose centers far from military locations 2- Avoiding the crowding of beneﬁciaries during activates. 3- Commitment to all humanitarian principles, especially neutrality and impartiality to avoid Targeting. 4- Continuing coordination with the Security and Safety department during staff movement D- The intervention of Armed groups in humanitarian action/ Its common risk, especially extremist groups, to obstruct humanitarian action (blackmail) Solution/ Mitigation measures:1- The organization's obligation not to provide any information about the donor to any military faction interventions and other related information. 2- WSA policy is clear, do not to make a concession. 3- Coordination with civilian local authoritiesE- Flocculation of Dollar rate and issues of money transfer, Mitigation:1- WSA is using OWNDA &QUICK BOOKS tools of cash monitoring and follow-up.2- Sending money to Syria by PTT (Turkish government postal and telegraph organization)
