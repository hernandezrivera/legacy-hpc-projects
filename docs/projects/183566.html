Project Summary

Project Objective:
To enhance the capacity of agricultural assets of vulnerable farmers in the southern governorate of the Gaza Strip who are subjected to humanitarian crises to restore livelihoods.
Special objectives:
1. To rehabilitated the greenhouse of 100 farmers in the targeted area ( at least 5 female farmers their age is between 20 -65-year-old and 95 male of the headed household farmers theirs age is between 20-65-year-old).
2. To rehabilitate agricultural water wells and install 75 kW solar power for three wells in the targeted area to improve water access to 300 dunums. (at least 6 female farmers their age is between 20-65-year-old and 54 male of the headed household farmers their age is between 20-65-year-old).
3. To rehabilitated 100 poultry farms and install 100 kW solar power for 100 farmers in the targeted area ( at least 5 female farmers their age is between 20 -65-year-old and 95 male of the headed household farmers theirs age is between 20-65-year-old).

The project activities will be implemented in Rafah, Khan Younis, and the Middle Area.

All project activities will be implemented by the parallel in all regions and completely coordinated with the Ministry of agriculture and the Food Security Sector.
The estimated number of direct beneficiaries is 200 farmers (At least 10 female farmers and 190 males' headed households) while the total number of the farmers and their families is 1100 members.)286 male, 264 female, 286 boys, 264 girls) with 22 PWDs.
The project is designed to urgent support to protect and restore livelihoods for vulnerable farmers who are affected by humanitarian crises, blockades, and recent hostility on Gaza.
The project will be based on the Do No Harm framework with a gender transformative approach and all targeted households will be selected according to protection-related and socio-economic vulnerability criteria.
The project's objectives match the Food Security Sector (FSS) objective #(1) The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security, and
productive capacity restored/enhanced.
Moreover, project objectives aligned to the HRP 2022 oPt, Strategic objective country #( 3): The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued, through Implementation of project activities that will contribute protect and restoring livelihoods for vulnerable farmers.

1 - Needs

Gazaain is witnessing a continuous decline in their living conditions; the level of food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Gaza as well as the COVID-19 pandemic which has already had severe socio-economic consequences. During the10-21 May 2021 has seen the Israel hostilities on Gaza, worsened the already poor humanitarian situation, undermined by years of blockade, the internal Palestinian divide, and recurrent escalations. This has been compounded by the measures imposed to contain the COVID-19, including recurrent lockdowns and, a reduction of commercial activities (Escalation -OCHA Report#1).The livelihoods of many farmers, breeders, herders, and fishers have been negatively impacted by destruction or damage to their land, productive assets, and farming infrastructures. In addition to their inability to access such resources, during the Israel escalation, including those headed by women, the older people, those with specific needs, and people in poor health conditions ( Flash Appeal OCHA- May 2021). According to World Bank (June 2021), the agricultural sector suffered direct damage of USD 45 million, while the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) estimated it was accounting for USD 55 million. More than 26,000 dunums of orchards, vegetable crops, and greenhouses, and more than 450 livestock and poultry farms, were fully or partially destroyed. According to the Palestinian Ministry of Labor, it is estimated that more than 19 thousand workers were affected by direct damages to active facilities, 37 percent of them working in the agriculture sector. By the hostilities ended, during May and June 2021 Al Nakheel has investigated humanitarian needs using several methods in KhanYouis, the Middle area, and Rafah Four focus groups discussions were conducted (10 female,27 male farmers) in the three targeted areas Their age was between 20 to 65 years with different marital statuses, and Five Semi-structured interviews executed during the field visits in the affected area were to some owners of the greenhouse, open-field land farmers, poultry farmers, livestock farmers, and water wells owner. With females and male farmers (random samples). The main finding: -100% of the attendee's farmers stated that they are affected directly by the hostilities and have no access to irrigate their land crops care, and harvest during the hostilities. -92% of the attendee's farmers stated that they suffer from the high cost of agro-input as well as they suffer from the low selling price of most produced crops. - 86 % of the attendees determined that there is a lack of irrigation water as well as a lack of power to operate water wells. -100 % of the attendees stated that they are in need of cash assistance to replant their lands and to pay some of their debts. -100 % of farmers the sales were low and the poultry breeders were affected by the hostilities and COVID-19 the market access was limited and sporadic amidst the restoration of movement restriction.-100 %of attendee's female farmers illustrated their needs to maintain their livelihoods to reactivate their production and plan for the next production cycles. -100 % of the attendees' farmers stated needs that humanitarian assistance should be delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable, and participatory manner. To conclude, there are no differences between the reported needs of female and male farmers. And the farmers have horrible damage and their needs have never been changed since 2019, and there increase in the number of affected and vulnerable farmers of which they accumulated debts. The intervention will be built on the understanding of gender-specific needs and expectations as well as socio-economic factors and cultural sensitivities and raised PSEA awareness as well as.

2 - Activities

This intervention aims to protect and restore the agricultural assets hold by male and female farmers who are vulnerable to humanitarian crises in Rafah, Khan Younis, and the Middle area.The activities are designed in coordination with MoA and FSS to eliminate the duplication of the beneficiaries from other agriculture projects, and Implementation of the activities will be taking with health measures to prevent the outbreak of COVID-19. The selection beneficiaries criteria are based on the identified needs, gender equity, sensitivity without any bias, and Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. to ensuring equal benefit. Although traditionally most farmers are male, attention will be paid to targeting farms headed by women or those that employ women, This project will target women farmers who are the head of household, hold agriculture assets, and actually practice agriculture by themselves. Al Nakheel and their partners will encourage women to apply to benefit from the project activities In addition to Al Nakheel will announce the priority will be for the female farmer and the female beneficiaries will get additional marks when evaluating the needs and selecting the beneficiaries on other hand the legal opinion will be consulted to ensure minimum risks on the implementation is likely to happen. Furthermore will be verified by the local committees to ensure that the selected 10 female farmers having the support documents that proving the female farmers and these agricultural assets are holding it. In addition, the final list will be developed and passed to the MoA to get their feedback on the selected beneficiaries. Activity # 1: Rehabilitation of greenhouses for 100 farming households (at least 5 female farmers headed households and their age is between 20-65- year-old). The project will be protected and restore livelihoods for greenhouse vulnerable farmers, one dunum greenhouse for each farmer to rehabilitate 100 greenhouses ( 30 Rafah, 40 Khan Younis, 30 Middle Area), through unconditional and restricted cash assistance,3000 NIS per one dunum (equal 950 USD ) for the agricultural inputs for one dunum (1000 m2) greenhouses ( nylon, irrigation system, Beechwood, Wood gutters, seeds, seedlings ). this amount which should cover to maintain the livelihood one dunum greenhouse was estimated at 3000 NIS (equal to 950 USD) based on market survey, previous experiences. Output #1: 100 greenhouse rehabilitated and of high productivity soil and at least 1200 tones of vegetables. this Activity will be done in three installments as follows: - 40 % first installment - 30 % second installment - 30 % final installment. The cash distribution will be through a standing order to the bank include the names of beneficiaries, ID number, the amount value of installment in each installment. Activity # 2: Rehabilitation of water wells. Partial Rehabilitation for operating three agricultural wells to improve the efficiency of discharge water and installing renewable energy for the agricultural water wells. (1 Khan Younis, 2 Middle Area) each well will serve 20 farmers; this activity will be provided water for 300 dunums. Outputs#2: improving water access to 300 dunums at a low cost. Activity # 3: Rehabilitation of 100 poultry farms and install renewable energy. The poultry farms will be rehabilitated according to determining the needs of each farm and determining the schedule of quantities included installing renewable energy( 30 Rafah, 40 Khan Younis,30Middle Area ) at least 5 female farmers headed household and their age is between 20-65-year-old and 95 males of the headed household farmers. Outputs # 3: # poultry farms are rehabilitated and provided. Three local committees will be formed one for each area will ensure community participation in beneficiaries’ selection and identification of needs. the committees will be At least 35 % of the members will be women. and representing people with disabilities ( PwD) by Local NGOs representatives. The local committees will participate in planning, implementing, and monitoring, and evaluating the project activities. This will ensure the good participation of women in the project cycles. Al Nakheel partners will be responsible for implementing activities just in the Rafah area, in addition to Al Nakheel will be responsible for the project in general and the project activities will be implemented in an overlapping manner to ensure the completion of all projects activities within 12 months.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Protection mainstreaming is a key driver to maximize positive impact. The proposed project mainstreams protection, including Al Nakheel, follow the Code of Ethics and Conduct ensuring that the beneficiaries are treated with respect and dignity, and preventing abuse. The Partners through its nature of work understand the concept of "do no harm" and consecrate their effort to provide the received an agricultural asset to the local community to strengthen peace among them. Therefore, the staff is highly recommended not to use their power during the project implementing stages and not to be an element of the conflict. And not to use this agricultural input to support their sides in the conflict to prevent harm to the community. Partners follow the Rights-Based Approach Manual as it deals with beneficiaries as rights holders rather than service recipients and empowering them to protect and claim their rights. Partners use an effective complaint system so that beneficiaries can give feedback on their treatment and quality of services Partners follow the humanitarian principle of DO Not Harm through preventing and activities that may cause negative outcomes on the target beneficiaries. The project will be adequately announced in the targeted communities using various outreach tools to give equal opportunities to benefit from the project. The targeted groups will be selected based on transparent selection criteria that ensure humanitarian assistance is delivered without bias, and with a view to alleviating existing inequalities and vulnerabilities. The selection of beneficiaries will fulfill the transparent and clear criteria for the project to be reducing suffering, maintaining human dignity, and reach the most valuable people. Gender has been strongly incorporated into the design of the project. the partners adopted “Zero Tolerance” regarding any action causing GBV, all the staff members signed and trained on GBV and harassment prevention policies PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse). The gender focal point in Al Nakheel will be responsible for monitoring and follow up on any Gender-related complaints. The gender risks facing project implementation are protection risks associated with cash programming in Gaza including an increase in domestic violence and/or intra-household power imbalances (i.e. who controls and spends the cash) and the exclusion of women or individuals with specific needs in the process of awarding the cash (e.g. due to secondary social status and restrictions on movement) Mitigation measures include: - use of female staff members to make female household members more comfortable. - in-person visits if households are not able to collect cash. - a beneficiary orientation that informs male and female household members about the potential risks especially for female-headed households as well as an introduction to the Community Accountability and Reporting. - on a case-by-case basis, staff will conduct household visits for orientation. - training staff on the detection of potential protection risks.
