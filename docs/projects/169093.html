Project Summary

For the Education Response, UNOPS proposes minor and major rehabilitation of the damaged schools, in coordination with UNESCO. This project will target 11,215 students. UNOPS remains flexible to work on locations and gaps which will be identified and assigned by the Education Cluster and UNESCO, therefore the project is not restricted to any specific locations or schools. The budget is based on a target of a minimum of 25 schools. See further details in "Activities". UNOPS has been coordinating with UNESCO in this regard.
All procurement needed will be carried out under UNOPS Emergency Procurement Procedures in order to ensure speedy delivery in under 3 months.
Estimated costs and target values are based on the available needs assessment results, and costs of similar projects implemented by UNOPS in the region. UNOPS has extensive experience in infrastructure-related projects, including large-scale school rehabilitation in Jordan (120 institutions).
UNOPS is submitting a multi-sectoral response, with proposals in HEALTH, SHELTER, and EDUCATION. Final costs are contingent upon confirmation of the final budget of the 3 proposed responses.

1 - Needs

According to the Statistical Bulletin (2018-2019) of the Center for Educational Research and Development (CERD), there are 177 schools in the Beirut governorate (31 percent public and 66 percent private), and 494 schools in Mount Lebanon and the suburbs of Beirut governorate (CERD 20193). These schools were serving over 300,000 students prior to the Beirut Blast, that is, -more than one-quarter of the country’s total student population. According to OCHA (Sitrep #6, as of 21 August) the Ministry of Education and Higher Education (MEHE) released the results of the rapid needs assessment, recently conducted with the technical support of UNICEF. Findings indicate that 159 schools were affected by the explosions, affecting the learning of 85,313 children. Out of the 159 affected schools, 107 have moderate to severe damages, impacting the learning continuity for around 48,000 children. An estimated $22.6 million will be required to rehabilitate the damaged schools, including almost $10.5 million for publicschools. According to the Rapid Damage and Needs Assessment of the World Bank (RDNA), 60 percent of rehabilitation could be done in the first 3 months requiring 9.2 million USD. The MEHE set up the MEHE Beirut Blast Response Committee, and UNESCO is coordinating partners engaged in the rehabilitation works.

2 - Activities

The proposed activity under this project is the rehabilitation of a minimum of 25 moderately to severely damaged schools, to be assigned by UNESCO based on the identified gaps in the response. For each school this will include an in-depth technical assessment, an engineering design review (in case of structural damages), minor and/or major rehabilitation and procurement of materials and equipment.In case of heavy structural damages which could not be rehabilitated in 3 months during the emergency phase, only the first phase (technical assessment, design review and minor repairs) will be undertaken under this project. The second phase (rehabilitation of structural damages and/or partial reconstruction) will however fall under the early recovery and reconstruction phase.
