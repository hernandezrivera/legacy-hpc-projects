Project Summary

With the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS), the security situation in the country continues to stabilize. Due to this, the country is experiencing voluntary returns of the population who had fled to neighboring countries like Ethiopia, Sudan, Uganda, Kenya and DRC. This has put pressure on existing WASH facilities. This has occurred in an atmosphere where WASH coverage is also still very low at 41% coverage with basic water supply, 11 % coverage with basic sanitation and with 63% of the population still practicing open defecation (OD) according to the UNICEF/WHO 2019 Joint Monitoring Programme. This scenario is putting peoples’ lives at risk of contracting water borne diseases
With this changing scenario, there is need to ensure that the returnees, host community and IDPs have access to clean and safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene practices. This project will be implement by NSDO to provide life-saving WASH services aimed at meeting the critical emergency needs of Returnees, Host Communities and IDPs in Lire and Nyepo Payams of Kajo Keji County; Terekeka, and Juba Counties of Central Equatoria State.In addition, the organization will undertake similar intervention in Pageri payam of Magwi County in Eastern Equatoria State.
Through the project, NSDO will focus on timely and appropriate access to WASH services for returnees, IDPs and host communities and specific WASH interventions for GBV mitigation, famine risk mitigation, and WASH-related diseases risk reduction for outbreak control.
The project will begin by jointly planning and reviewing the needs with beneficiary members of each activity site, technical assessment of target WASH facilities suggested by beneficiary members for repairs. Hand pumps will be rehabilitated by flushing (cleaning up of the well by air compressor and treatment of the borehole water with chlorine, disinfection) and replacement of worn out parts. In addition, hygiene promotion campaigns, training of water user’s management committees will be trained on pump maintenance and repair, which will be complemented with distribution of repair kits. The project will serve at least 35,000 beneficiaries (17,850 females and 17,150 males); 39% of the populations are boys and girls.

1 - Needs

Since the signing of revitalized peace agreement it has been received with mixed reactions by the population with majority holding on the hopes of sustainable peace. This has led to voluntary returns of majority of the population who had fled to neighboring countries like Ethiopia, Sudan, Uganda, Kenya and DRC. This has put pressure on existing WASH facilities. This has occurred in an atmosphere where WASH coverage is also still very low at 41% coverage with basic water supply, 11 % coverage with basic sanitation and with 63% of the population still practicing open defecation according to the UNICEF/WHO 2019 Joint Monitoring Programme. This scenario is putting peoples’ lives at risk of contracting water borne diseases In the WASH Cluster severity mapping , Juba county in Central Equatoria State is mapped at severity level 4, whereby the area is marked as Cholera hot spot in addition of presence of other diseases such as diarrhea. Cholera is endemic with frequent seasonal outbreaks. Poor access to clean drinking water, sanitation and hygiene practices are the main drivers for cholera. According to the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System for 2018 (July/August), in the entire country only 29% of the population has access to a borehole, tap stand or water yard within a maximum 30-Minute distance without facing protection concerns. The current people in need of safe drinking water and provision of hand washing facilities are 3.3 Million. According to UNICEF's August, 2019 WASH briefing note, construction of the water supply systems for urban and peri-urban communities of Juba is currently at 30% completion. To survive, families may be forced to drink dirty water, putting them at risk of waterborne diseases such as cholera and diarrhea, which remain leading causes of death among children in South Sudan. The situation is further aggravated by poor hygiene practices, where 75% of the population reported that household members had been self-diagnosed with a waterborne illness in the previous two weeks. This is further worsened by the fact that only 10 per cent of the population have access to improved sanitation , With more returnees arriving from IDP and refugee camps, majority of the population continue to share existing facilities. Though concerted efforts by health, WASH partners have brought the cholera that had threatened the lives and health of thousands, support must continue to ensure health, sanitation and other vital services continue to be provided to those in need of them most. The priority groups to be assisted by this project are the conflict affected people, people at risk of water borne diseases and people facing food insecurity/ poor nutrition. These include children, as well as displaced people with limited access to safe water and improved nutrition. The project area will be Terekeka, Kajokeji and Juba Counties in Central Equatoria State and Magwi County in Eastern Equatoria State. Through this project, NSDO will provide timely, equitable access to safe and clean drinking water, sanitation and hygiene for 6,650 IDPs, 19,775 host Communities and 8,575 returnees. In addition, the following will be carried out:• Mitigate WASH-related gender based violence,• Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package, • Integrate WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level,The summary of the beneficiaries per location is as follows: a) IDPs: Juba, 3,500; Terekeka, 700; Kajokeji, 1,750 and Magwi, 700; Total =6,650b) Host Community: Terekeka, 6,300; Kajo Keji 9,625; Magwi, 3,840; Total =19,775c) Returnees: Kajo keji, 6,125; Magwi, 2,450; Total =8,575 NSDO is well placed to implement this project since it has field operations in Juba-Rajaf, Kajo Keji and Nimule with a clear understanding of the dynamics and context of these locations, given the past experience in implementing SSHF funded projects, in the areas of WASH

2 - Activities

To reduce morbidity mortality and suffering from protection threats and incidences of most vulnerable in severity levels 3 & 4 (SO1), the organization will integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package by undertaking the following activities •Provision of safe drinking water at appetite test for SAM/MAM in 20 Nutrition Centres, 4 per payam/locationConduct water qulaity monitoring in 20 nutrition centres •Provision of WASH/hygiene kits to 100 children, 20 per location admitted and discharged for SAM treatment from functional OTP/SC• Provision of safe water to 100 children, 20 per location admitted for SAM treatment at OTP/SCIn order to reduce excess morbidity and mortality rates from epidemic prone diseases such as malaria, diarrhea, ARI, ,measles in priority areas, SO1 and to integrate WASH in health response to control outbreaks at wider community level, (WASH CO2 & CO3), through the project NSDO will undertake the following activities: • Holding of 5 community consultation meeting, 1 per location (disaggregated by sex/age) before designing/operationalizing the program/project• Technical assessment of boreholes and flushing of selected boreholes to remove turbidity and treat borehole to improve water quality• Drilling of 20 boreholes, 4 in each of the 5 payams • Rehabilitation of 40 boreholes , 15 in Terekeka, 15 in Kajo Keji, and 10 in Magwi Counties• Construction of 8 mini water yards, 2 in Juba (Don Bosco), 2 in Magwi, 2 in Terekeka and 2 in Kajo keji• Establishment of rain water harvesting systems in schools through provision of water tanks & gutter to 5 schools per payam, total 25.• Provision of hand washing facilities in 25 schools In addressing SO2 and to provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees, CO3 and to mitigate WASH-related gender-based violence, CO4. NSDO will ensure that WASH services addresses the vital needs, public health and protection issues through an AAP community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross-sectoral evaluations and analyses. In this regard, the organization will undertake the following activities:• 20 VIP latrine construction, in schools and health facilities, 5 per payam, secured and gender appropriate sanitation facilities.• Hygiene promotion campaigns targeting 500,women, 300 men, 250 girls and 200 boys reached• Provision of MHM kits to 250 vulnerable women/girls, 50 per location of menstrual age in schools and targeted community. • Training of Community health promoters/workers, WASH focal points, 20 per location; pupil ambassadors 50 per location on appropriate hygiene practices at their household level• Training of 50 project staff on Protection and GBV guidelines• 5 Protection assessment exercises, 1 per location conducted in by female staff with women, girls and children through FGD for safe and accessible WASH facilities with community input • Rehabilitation of 12 stances of 3 blocks of latrine after risk analysis incorporating gender appropriate sanitation facilities in each block, 2 per payam in schools and health facilities, • 5 GBV risk analysis sessions with target beneficiaries conducted, 1 per payam with female and male beneficiary members at each project site To enable target population recover from crisis, like displacements and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses and in order to support coping capacities and livelihoods of the affected population, SO3, CO3, NSDO will undertake the following activities:• Train community members/water user management committee (20 women, 30 men, 20 girls and 30 boys per location) on maintenance of water points• Distribution of 80 standard and special repair tool kits to 80 Water Management committees • 5 Construction of protected water points, in Terekeka

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project will ensure that WASH services are provided in safe and secure locations; and facilities are gender sensitive and accessible by people with special needs. Special attention will be given to protection mainstreaming in all WASH activities including design, Implementation, monitoring and evaluation. The needs of special groups including the disabled, children, girls, women and the elderly will be considered during designs and implantation

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

NSDO has a sustainability plan for all its WASH projects, as part of the project sustainability, all completed WASH facilities will officially be handed over in a ceremony with all stakeholders invited including project beneficiaries. During the handover ceremony, a detailed MoU will be signed between NSDO and user committees with specific details on roles and responsibilities of the beneficiaries and user committees with regard to operation and maintenance. NSDO will ensure the user committees are formed and trained at each project site on operation and maintenance. NSDO will continuously coordinate with local leaders to ensure safety of the rehabilitated and renovated facilities and to curb cases of vandalism. The water user committee comprising of pump mechanics will be trained on operation and maintenance and will be provided with tool kits and spare parts from the cluster core pipeline. The community hygiene promoters will also carry sustainability messages to households to inculcate the culture of taking individual responsibilities of all WASH facilities by the target beneficiaries

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

To reduce the risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services, NSDO will ensure that the activities conducted in secure and accessible places.In addition, the organization will seek support from other partners to support delivery of services where and when needed. To avoid communities leaving their habitual place of residence for the purpose of getting assistance (humanitarian assistance as a pull factor), the organization will ensure that its activities are localized. This is addition of the assistance act as a pull factor also ensure that community members, especially women and girls are closer to their homes/residence thus reducing the incidence of sexual abuse/GBVPeace building messages will be incorporated in projects' activities so as to ensure beneficiaries risk exposure to violence during registration/distribution is minimized. e.g. increasing the number of water point will also go along way into ensuring protection of women and reduced cases of GBV since women will not have to travel long distances in search of water thereby exposing themselves to cases of sexual assault. In addition of emphasizing peaceful coexistence within the community, the organization will ensure adequate security arrangements and crowd control measures in place by incorporating local security apparatus.
