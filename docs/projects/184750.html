Project Summary

The intervention is geared toward preventing and responding to eviction, monitoring of threats to evictions by assessing potential eviction sites, monitoring, documentation and reporting to relevant authorities and relevant clusters to mobilize support for advocacy. The advocacy and campaigns will aim to ensure actors facilitate lawful eviction processes. Capacity development targeting settlement managers, local authorities on Housing, Land and proper protection monitoring through ensuring collection of information based on the methodology of the Somalia Protection Monitoring System (SPMS).
Protection, monitoring and response mechanisms with regards to land, housing and property is lacking in most of protection programming. Most often legal Aid services, land, houses and property rights are not prioritized hence leaving big gaps in the protection response mechanism. There is poor coordination among protection and response service providers especially on land, housing and property rights. Furthermore, the protection response mechanisms have limited or no provision of free legal aid services to IDPs, survivors of GBV and HLP.
Therefore, this project seeks to improve and strengthen coordination among service providers and to enhance legal protection and response mechanism for IDPs on land, housing and property rights. These will be achieved through, improved referral pathways, on spot monitoring and response to assist IDPs reclaim housing, land and properties, awareness raising, free legal aid and innovative and sustainable alternative dispute resolution mechanisms.
NoFYL has presence in Banadir, Galmudug, Lower Jubba and Gedo Regions and has built strong linkages with communities and other actors which will facilitate the access and smooth implementation of project activities. NoFYL has an excellent record in linking with beneficiary communities (returnee, IDPs and host) affected by disasters and conflict, enabling them to cope with life threatening situations and recovery, and have good working relations with the local authorities and camp leaders. The progress of project activities will be monitored by NoFYL field offices in Kismayo and Mogadishu office. NoFYL has developed monitoring tools to monitor the progress. According to the agreed work plan, each activity will be monitored using participatory, results-based feedback mechanisms.

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1.1: Assessment monitoring threats of eviction by landlords, authorities and settlement managers, the assessment will also cover land tenure use, status of rental agreements, and forced evictions. This activity involves undertaking a rapid assessment of the current ownership and tenure rights of IDP communities, and a detailed study of property state, legal documentation and tenure rights.Activity 1.1.2: Protection of IDPs against forced eviction with a focus on sites at high risk of evictions in target sites and beyond. In Mogadishu, Kismayo and Galmudug districts, small-scale evictions occur on a daily basis and IDPs constitute the largest and most vulnerable of those affected. NoFYL will intervene to prevent eviction of IDPs from their sites. NoFYL will intervene upon alerts received of imminent eviction threats against IDP sites, or in sites assessed to be at high risk of eviction. Prevention interventions will include: negotiating with landowners, gatekeepers or local authorities for the continuation of tenure by the IDPs, with the support of District Commissioners, negotiation of extended notice, consultation on alternatives, alternative accommodations and non-discrimination.Activity 1.1.3: Provision of Post-eviction response package. This is a one-off minimum financial assistance to evicted households as an immediate relief after an eviction. This support will go in to support transportation of household items during relocation to new sites, purchase or repair of broken household items during forced evictions and payment of rentals during the transition period before full settlement. Each household will receive a one-off amount of 200 USD target 3000 evictees or people at risk of eviction in the target locations.Activity 1.1.4: Through Lawyers and paralegals NoFYL will establish one (1) legal aid clinic in each of the project locations for the provision of legal advice, counseling for cases related to tenure insecurity: The paralegals will facilitate negotiations on tenure security since evictions are highly due to tenancy agreements are either not in place or are not properly negotiated. Counseling will entail the provision of one-to-one advice to displacement affected persons on how to overcome obstacles to durable solutions, and local remedies that are available to assist them address protection concerns with which they are faced, including forced eviction and other tenure insecurity issues. Activity 1.1.5: Establish community information Centers in each of the target locations that will serve as a communication centres to create local systems that will reduce forced evictions through an enhanced commitment of authority towards HLP issues and through improved local knowledge and practices related to HLP issues including legal frameworks and rights on rental and contractual procedures and arrangements. Activity 1.1.6: Raise public awareness on HLP issues: this will involve the development of IEC materials that will be shared with targeted communities through the information Centers and community outreaches. increase local awareness on HLP issues and legal rights on land rental and contractual procedures and arrangements. Activity 1.1.7: Social cohesion and dispute resolution by building cohesion between IDPs and host communities. This will be conducted by establishing Peace-building community committees to support the prevention of conflict and response efforts to enhance peaceful coexistence. (Weekly dialogue sessions with community leaders (Women Group, Elders, Religious Leaders, and Youth Group) on social cohesion and peace-building. The dialogue sessions will cover topics on protection and promotion of HLP rights, forced eviction, women access to HLP rights. The sessions will be covering information on HLP rights, remedies, and entitlements.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Some of the project activities will involve cash disbursement/distribution. e.g. The provision of cash-based post-eviction response package assistance to evicted households which serves as an immediate relief after an eviction. This cash-based assistance will support transportation of household items during relocation to new sites, purchase or repair of broken household items during forced evictions, and payment of rentals during the transition period before full settlement.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The current needs and gaps in Housing land and property (HLP) in Somalia point towards the need for cash-based post-eviction response package assistance to evicted persons in IDP sites as well improving tenure security. Furthermore, NoFYL having worked in the proposed target locations previously has assessed the prevailing market conditions to determine the likely impact that cash transfers could have on the local economy. Cash transfers were thus deemed to be beneficial as the local markets are well stocked with common basic goods that beneficiaries could use the money to purchase. Besides, this increasing purchasing power will stimulate an increase in demand and supply which will stimulate local economic growth.
