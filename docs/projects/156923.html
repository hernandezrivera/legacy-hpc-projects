Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining food and livelihoods assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb.
One-off multi-purpose cash transfers
Where money transfer agents are available and functional markets are accessible, one-off MPCT will be provided allowing HHs to determine and prioritise their most immediate needs. MPCT will be based on the survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) rates of each location, which includes food assistance. MCs Women Cash Pilot in Dar’a highlighted that when women in female-headed HHs are provided with cash, they retain the spending power within the HH and according to our findings (see Annex 10) were able to more effectively determine how to use the cash to meet the priority needs, as opposed to when cash was provided to male-headed HHs, or men within the HH.
In-kind food
Where cash and vouchers are not feasible, MC will distribute Ready to Eat food rations for newly displaced HHs or a food basket for HHs with access to cooking facilities.
Repeat assistance to food insecure and underserved communities
Primarily targeting out of camp populations, MC will focus on vulnerable populations, such as IDPs, returnees and people of concern with repeat assistance. This will typically be provided for 3 months through MPCT, voucher or food assistance. This action will consider all HHs as potential beneficiaries and will target the most vulnerable regardless of their status. Where repeat cash or in-kind food is a viable and effective form of assistance for IDP camps, this option will be explored; with particular attention to potential protection concerns and pull factors.
Livelihoods
MC provides vulnerable households with temporary income through CFW and contributes to the restoration of productive assets through promoting adapted techniques for food production at household level and distributing agricultural inputs.

Needs

NES: Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence is signaling a reduction, with the potential to destabilize a delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. Across Syria, one in three households is now headed by women, which highlights different needs. Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) and large-scale destruction pose a serious risk to the physical safety of civilians, housing, land and property (HLP) and for the restoration of basic services.According to HNO data, most of NES is either critically or severely food insecure, particularly in southern Al-Hasakeh governorate, Ar-Raqqa and Sabka Districts (Ar-Raqqa governorate), and along the west and south of DEZ. MC’s post-harvest study highlights that low rainfall has led to 44% of farmers failing to produce a harvest despite receiving agricultural assistance. Over 99% of farmers did not generate an income, with insufficient harvest for domestic consumption. The consequences of the drought will most likely impact the rest of the country as the NES governorates combined generate over 60% of the national wheat production (FAO), leading to fears of an emerging food security crisis. NWS:With multiple waves of IDPs, humanitarian conditions deteriorated in 2018. The establishment of the demilitarised zone (DMZ) in Sep 2018 saw the numbers and frequency of displacement subside, however, inter-AOG conflict in January 2019 has the potential to further de-stabilize the area. According to the HAT’s Dec 2018 Report, “The DMZ Agreement”, there is likely to be a partial or complete breakdown in the political process if key provisions are not fully implemented, leading to a surge in IDP movementAll of MCs target areas are either critically (Dana) or severely food insecure (Marat an-Nu’man, Atereb, Daret Azza) (HNO 2018). MC assessments show 77% of HHs limit portions at mealtimes, 60% restrict adults’ consumption for small children to eat, and 73% of HHs reduce the number of meals/day, for an average of 4 days/week.

Implementation of activities

1: Emergency response to crisis affected vulnerable people with short term food assistance through appropriate modalities and 2: Monthly food assistance for the assessed food insecure people through appropriate modalitiesMC will identify conduct multi-sectoral needs assessment to verify needs, priorities and gaps, determine level of severity and identify specific vulnerable groups within communities. To ensure the most vulnerable households are selected, MC conducts a vulnerability assessment scoring exercise based on aggregated vulnerability scores. the modality of response is determined by market assessments included in the rapid / multi-sector needs assessment. Market assessments identify if markets are functioning, what types of products are available, cost of products and if markets are accessible. If markets are functional, MC prefers to use cash or voucher modalities. In cases where markets are not functional, inaccessible or unaffordable, MC selects in-kind support. Food assistance will be provided for a period of up to three months (partly through multi-purpose cash assistance based on SMEB rates per locations), with the most vulnerable households qualifying for multiple months of assistance. Regardless the modality that will be employed for food assistance, the ration (food basket) will be designed following the SPHERE standards of 70% of the 2,100 kCal per person per day with 10-12 percent of total energy provided by protein and 17 per cent provided by fat with inclusion of required minerals. In Ein Issa specifically, Mercy Corps will provide regular cash assistance as fresh food top-ups to long-term IDPs and/or refugees living in camps to complement the monthly food ration provided by WFP GFRs. While the latter only covers 1,500 kcal/person/day, the top-up will ensure that these vulnerable populations have access to dietary diversity and are reaching the recommended daily intake of 2,100 kcal/person/day, by complementing their diet with diverse and nutritious food. Relevant tools such as NutVal will be used in the design of the ration. In case of cash, the amount will be determined based on the actual prices of the food basket items that are identified and in coordination with the cluster or the relevant available coordination body. The percentages of the food need (dietary need) that will be met is determined based on the cluster recommendation as well as the assessment results that MC or the partner is carrying out to the target population. Each distribution will be followed by post-distribution monitoring (PDM).3: Distribution of agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment; and related training and 4 Support to asset building and asset protection (small livestock and animal feed distribution) including winterization activities; and related training.Targeted agro-pastoralist households in NES have improved access to required agricultural and livestock inputs, water, infrastructure and extension services resulting in improved production, improved household consumption, and increased household income. MC will provide farming households with 1) the needed agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizer, labor, access to water and machinery), coupled with improved knowledge and practices to restart planting and improve productivity; 2) restock essential livestock and related inputs for livestock holders, including improved knowledge and practices, to improve productivity and processing of by-products

Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps Syria has rolled out a protection risks analysis tool for staff and partners since 2018, as part of the protection mainstreaming strategies. Noting that the Mercy Corps submission is reflective of protection programming incorporated into several donor grants (often multi-sector), each project and key interventions will have a protection risk analysis. The principle of risk analysis and protection assessment is a corner stone to Mercy Corps protection mainstreaming. Mercy Corps staff and partners will be trained on key principles, staff will be gender balanced and complaints followed up on. The following illustrates the main protection risks associated with this program, and mitigating measures that will be taken by Mercy Corps. Mercy Corps acknowledges that there are a significant number of protection risks that individuals and communities can face as a result of poor programming or programming in a difficult environment. This does not capture them all.1. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and Mercy Corps has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. Mercy Corps is committed to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Child Safeguarding through appropriate policies (Code of Conduct) which are enforced through on-boarding and training to all staff, suppliers and partners. Distribution are carefully planned to ensure they are done at times and in places that are safe and accessible to women and girls, as well as the elderly and people with disabilities, and that any barriers to access as a result of gender norms or physical ability are taken into account when planning for the distribution.2. Programming exacerbates community tensions due to perceived discrimination/favouritism: Mercy Corps upholds a conflict sensitive and DNH approach throughout programming. MC utilizes appropriate and transparent targeting techniques, prioritising the most vulnerable and those at risk, ensuring services will be delivered without discrimination, ensuring inclusion of minorities.. Mercy Corps has invested heavily in the tools and capacity to understand the conflict and power dynamics of where we work through regular analysis at the of communities through to regional geopolitical politics to ensure all interventions are conflict sensitive and in line with a DNH approach. Our approach is grounded in analysis and, accountability (ensuring transparent and inclusive targeting, participatory design, and transparent feedback and complaints mechanisms. Mercy Corps staff and partners are representative of the demography in each area and have close community ties which facilitates community acceptance and aids accountability. Staff and partners are trained to ensure conflict sensitivity approaches are mainstreamed across interventions.3. Local authorities restrict activities of partners due to lack of support for protection interventions. Similarly, where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will endeavour to engage with local actors to increase opportunities for operating. 4. Persons without documents refused assistance: As part of a protection mainstreamed intervention, Mercy Corps does not require beneficiaries of protection or non-protection projects to possess national ID documents. In line with protection cluster guidelines, information can be triangulated using local verification processes. Protection interventions can increase stigma and risk to beneficiaries. Mercy Corps support project designs which mitigate risk to beneficiaries. Mercy Corps also is supporting projects targeting youth and adolescents and persons with disabilities who are historically excluded from target interventions.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps closely coordinates our contingency planning alongside the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) which is hosted by Mercy Corps. The establishment of the demilitarised zone (DMZ) in Sep 2018 saw the numbers and frequency of displacement subside, however, inter-AOG conflict in January 2019 has the potential to further de-stabilize the area. Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence is signalling a reduction, with the potential to destabilize a delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. This has meant that there is questions of operational viability across northern Syria in 2019. Mercy Corps remains in close coordination with our donors and partners for appropriate contingency planning to enable and adaptable programming.
