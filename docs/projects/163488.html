Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to provide life saving services to children under five, pregnant and lactating women and other vulnerable persons desplaced and their hosts following violence in Kumba, Mamfe, Fontem and Mezam health districts. Due to violence most of health personnel have fled and community mobilization services are almost inexistant. IMC will seek to revitalize different health facilities by filling gaps in health personnel and procuring essential medicines and basic equipment for target populations. IMC will seek to provide free of charge health care to vulnerable communities regardless of their status (IDPs, refugees or host communities). As per different assessment reports, children under five, pregnant and lactating women present higher vulnerability risk. IMC will seek to support at primary level the provision of minimum services package including prevention and curative activities, in all targeted health facilities. Free of charge services will include curative consultations, ante and post natal consultations (ANC and PNC), deliveries, SAM without medical complications cases, rapid laboratory tests, expanded programme immunization (EPI). IMC will also support OTP in each target health area by providing logistic support for Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) transportation, reporting, and technical support (monitoring, supervision). In all target areas, IMC will strengthen community health system by identifying CHWs who will be trained, equipped and paid incentives. In health areas with high IDPs/host population ratio, IMC will seek to cover human resources gaps to reduce the strain put on limited existing health personnel and improve beneficiaries' access to continuous global and quality health services. In Mamfe, Kumba and Mezam District Hospitals ,3 medical doctors with surgery background will be recruited . To ensure that drugs provided by IMC will be managed with high quality to avoid rupture and worse used, IMC will deployed 1 nurse in each health facility. These staff will be trained and coached by Pharmacist doctor on drugs management. In addition, according to the check list established for each health center , IMC will provide support for volunteer Nurses, Aid Nurses and Midwives to complete the need. These staff will be recruited locally when possible and will be trained on key topics and paid salary through the MoH staff national standard salary grid. This strategy has been successfully experienced in East, Adamaoua, North and Far North rgions by IMC and it has an added value in sustaining local health system and contribute to affected communities resilience and rapid ownership of interventions. IMC will seek to build the capacity of locally recruited staff on different topics including IMCI and Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal care. For patients in need of further investigation or appropriate care referred to secondary level, IMC will cover transportation, feeding and medical bills for targets groups. IMC will contribute to the establishment of a functionning and effective referral system. Community surveillance of disease of potential epidemic will be assured through CHWs network under the leadership of MoH.

Besoins / Needs

The results of different Needs Assessment conducted in both South West and North West demonstrate that there are important gaps to be filled, in particular, in primary and secondary health care in affected health districts.Access to health care remains a concern due to insecurity, geographical and financial barriers. Most of health facilities were closed due to prevailing violence. A large part of this dis-functionality is due to the conflict. In fact, due to the conflict, qualified MoH staff left, monitoring and supervision are not conducted regularly, and population does not have access to health care because of increased insecurity, having an economic and social impact. EFSA conducted by WFP in January 2019 revealed proxy Global Acute Malnutrition rates of 4.4% and 5.6% for the NW and SW regions respectively. Despite early harvests of maize and groundnuts in the NWSW as reported by FEWSNET, the food security among IDPs and poor host households in rural areas remains at stress levels (IPC Phase 2). As per Nutrition Sitrep (OCHA Sept 2019), the overall nutrition situation in the NWSW regions remains of great concern despite lower proxy Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates for the NW (4.4%) and SW (5.6%) regions (EFSA January 2019) as the underlying factors to malnutrition persist (food insecurity, poor WASH and limited access to health care). The food security situation continues to be a concern due to reduced production, reduced prices in rural areas, increased prices in urban areas and decline in food consumption as a coping strategy adopted in most households. The deterioration of household livelihoods and food access, particularly in urban centres, and the low coverage of assistance, expose internally displaced persons (IDPs) and poor host populations in urban areas to poor living conditions and acute food insecurity Crisis (IPC 3). More so, preliminary findings from the MSNA conducted in September 2019 indicate a worrying WASH situation with over 55% of the assessed communities reporting that more than half of the vulnerable groups do not have enough water for their needs and more than 40% of the assessed communities practice open defecation. The coverage of nutrition interventions in both regions is low. Only 14% out of the total estimated Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) caseloads for 2019 (2,500) have accessed treatment for SAM. The above elements have an impact on the evolving nutrition situation.
