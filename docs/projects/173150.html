Project Summary

This project is designed to ensure activities and interventions address the needs of the affected population. The project target communities based on age, gender, and disability, considering vulnerability, risk, and magnitude of the problems. Infants (<6 months), based on risk, are targeted for inpatient management, IYCF, and C-MAMI interventions. Children 6-59 months are targeted in multiple nutrition interventions. Adolescent girls will benefit from feeding counseling and micro-nutrient supplementation (IFA). ACF will target PLWs for treatment (TSFP) and prevent undernutrition (IYFC/BSFP/IFA). Chronic patients, like TB cases, will benefit from OTP/ BSFP. Moreover, newly arrived refugees will be welcomed and offered a hot meal at the transit point.
The project's ultimate goal is to contribute to the improvement of the nutritional status of the camp and host communities and increase access and coverage of nutrition services. The treatment program includes SC, OTP, TSFP, and prevention services that include; BSFP, Growth monitoring, and promotion (GMP), IYCF-E, C-MAMI, community mobilization/outreach, and micronutrient supplementation. The internal referral will be enhanced, and external referrals will be limited to SAM with medical complications in SCs and linking with health facilities (e.g., ANC, EPI, etc.). Apart from that, ACF will provide a hot meal service in the Transit Center kitchen for new arrivals temporarily residing in the transit center, awaiting relocation into the camps. In the host communities, ACF will collaborate with MoH and SHED to ensure that nutrition activities carried out at Health Facilities are strengthened and technically supported, implement School health nutrition programs targeting all adolescents, and with community mobilization activities. ACF will also continue to conduct regular SMART/SENS/SQUEAK surveys to monitor the population's nutritional status and place a Nutrition Surveillance System to enhance the program monitoring further.
ACF will do the necessary emergency rapid response preparedness to support the target population and mitigate risks associated with extreme natural events. Notably, ACF will preposition the nutrition program required/relief items and equipment to respond to the need, formulate and train the mobile nutrition team, and prepare the caregivers on early detection and referral of malnourished children (family MUAC/MLM).
ACF has factored in community participation in identifying barriers and boosters in nutrition service utilization. Findings from community focus group discussion (FGD) and in-depth interviews are considered in this project design. This project follows a community-based approach, and encourage refugees involvement at all stage of the project intervention through meaningful engagement and consultation of refugee committees in all camps.

1 - Identified Needs

Despite the efforts and gains of the previous years' response, SMART Survey conducted in the refugee camps from 25th of September to 23rd October 2019 indicates the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) is 10.9% (8.3-14.0 95% CI). The prevalence of GAM in Kutupalong-registered camp has significantly reduced from 24.3% (19.5-29.7%, 95% CI) in October 2017 to 12.1% (8.9 - 16.1%, 95% CI) in October 2019. The same survey showed that SAM prevalence in the camps has slightly reduced from 1.1% in November 2018 to 0.9% in October 2019. According to WHO classification, the prevalence of malnutrition among all children 6-59 months is severe (10-14%). The nutrition surveys conducted indicates that nearly half of children 6-23 months suffered from anemia (Hb<11.0g/dL), representing a severe public health problem according to the WHO threshold (40%). Coverage of SAM and MAM treatment services in the camps is classified as moderate (SAM: 77.7% (95% CI; 72.8-82.4%), MAM: 61% (95%CI: 57.5-64.6%) according to the coverage survey (SQUEAC) conducted by ACF in November 2019. Coverage of SAM has made significant change increasing by 43% from last year’s survey (27.4% [95%CI; 22.5-32.9%]), and MAM services coverage increased by 21% compared to November 2018 survey (34%). However, this is still below SPHERE standard (Refugee coverage >90%).Data from a two-week recall of diarrhea, acute respiratory infection, and fever are concerning, indicating a high disease burden among children under five. The prevalence of Diarrhea has remained the same at 28% in 8 camps, 9.9% in Kutupalong registered camp, while a slight increase was recorded in Nayapara RC from 25.2% in 2018 to 28.5% in 2019. According to October 2019 SMART survey result, the prevalence of Acute respiratory tract infection is 9.4%, 5.1%, and 7.1% in 8camps, Kutupalong, and Nayapara RC, respectively. The same survey finding indicates that the fever rate without cough has significantly increased from 38% to 47.7% in camps and from 33.6% to 52.1% in Nayapara within one year between the two surveys. Findings of the IYCF-e monitoring exercise indicate that most children are breastfed; however, exclusive breastfeeding is low with 64% of children 0-5 months exclusively breastfeeding, considering context and aggravating factors, particularly in the makeshift settlements. All three surveys indicate that less than 30% of children achieve a minimum acceptable diet for their optimal growth and development (Makeshift only 7.3%). Besides, 45% of infants in the makeshift camps and 24% in NRC are not timely introduced to breast-feeding immediately after birth thus are deprived of colostrum that would contribute to their immunity enhancement. Findings on the assessment of nutrition status of PLWs aged 15-48 indicate that 5 % has MUAC<210mm (MAM). The highlighted needs above only focus on the data around a limited number of nutrition and health indicators and the under-nutrition situation resulting from numerous aggravating factors. The most acute needs identified where ACF can make a meaningful impact will be on;1. Access to malnutrition quality treatment services2. Access to malnutrition prevention services; including community mobilization services3. Access to information on young infant and complementary feeding and C-MAMI services4. Micronutrient supplementation for children, adolescent girls, and PLWs5. Monitoring of the nutrition situation

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Running of the community outreach program that includes community sensitization on nutrition and health including COVID-19 prevention, screening and referral, home visit, and absent and defaulter tracing. Activity 2: Treatment of SAM ( with or without medical complications) and MAM under-five children and PLWs, and provision of growth monitoring and promotion (GMP) service. Activity 3: Provision of Blanket supplementary feeding service provide for children under five and PLWs, and Other at-risk groups (for instance sick and elderly persons). Activity 4: Implementation of community management of at-risk mothers and infants (C-MAMI) through detection and enrollment of children 0-6 months with breastfeeding and medical problems and support for mothers. Activity 5: Enhanced facility and community-based infant and young child feeding and child care practice in emergency (IYCF-E) services. Activity 6: Provision of micronutrient supplementation (MNP, IFA & Vit. A) for children, adolescent girls & PLW. Activity 7: Conduct SMART/SENS surveys to monitor the nutrition status in all the camps Activity 8: Building the skills and technical capacity of nutrition workers, community volunteers, partners, and ACF nutrition project staff.
