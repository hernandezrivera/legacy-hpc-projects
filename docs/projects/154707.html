Project Summary

There is still extensive explosive hazard contamination across Iraq that poses dangers to vulnerable individuals, inhibits the safe return of displaced persons and impedes the provision of humanitarian assistance to vulnerable populations. The objective of the project is to address the needs of the most vulnerable conflict-affected people and enable safe and sustainable returns through the provision of Risk Education, Survey and Clearance. The project will focus its activities in Ninewa governorate, and will target the most vulnerable people in the project area. The majority of the project budget will be allocated for costly but life-saving survey and clearance activities in Mosul, Telafar, Tilkaif and Sinjar, with parallel awareness raising and risk education activities.

Needs

The conflict against ISIL has resulted in complex, dense, and extensive explosive hazard contamination. Particularly alarming is the variety of improvised explosive devices (IEDs), many of which were used by ISIL as defensive weapons prior to their withdrawal. Widespread contamination in newly retaken areas continues to have a severe impact upon people’s safety, and particularly that of men and boys. Contamination also continues to impede safe and sustainable returns, as well as humanitarian access. According to the IMMAP incident database, from December 2017 to October 2018, a total of 2,453 explosive incidents have taken place in Ninewa, Mosul corridors, Hawija and western Anbar, with more than half (56%) involving IEDs. Without safe and sustainable returns, there can be no rehabilitation and recovery. The need for Risk Education, Survey and Clearance is acute. The project will specifically focus on Ninewa governorate.

Activities

* Deliver Explosive Hazard Risk Education to communities in areas of displacement and in areas or return/origin: Train and deploy mixed-gender teams to provide age and gender tailored RE sessions to at risk populations, specifically targeting children and IDPs returning to highly contaminated areas. The RE sessions will provide the targeted population with knowledge and skills to adopt safer behaviours with regards to Explosive Remnants of War and Improvised Explosive Devices. RE sessions will be tailored to the specific needs of boys, girls, men and women, focusing on the risky behaviour most usually associated with each group, with a specific emphasis on boys and men as they continue to be the highest number of victims. The RE sessions for men and boys will focus on the risky behaviours carried out by these groups, and encourage safer alternatives. The RE sessions for younger children will use simplified and repetitive messaging, to assist with memory and understanding, and will use a variety of songs and games. The RE sessions for women will also include advice and suggestions for sharing safety messages in their homes, with their children, in a sustainable manner.* Deliver Explosive Hazard Risk Education training sessions to service providers, including first responders, Community Focal Points (CFP) and Youth RE Ambassadors (YREA). The training of CFPs and YREAs will ensure the project impact will continue beyond the end date of the project and the sharing of RE messages will be sustainable, following peer-to-peer methodology. Both male and female CFPs and YREAs will be trained, helping to ensure important safety messages are shared with both genders. CFPs and YREAs will be selected by the community members during community meetings and focus group discussions.* Conduct Survey and Clearance of explosive hazard contamination in areas of return/origin. Mixed gender mobile teams will conduct Non-Technical Surveys, during which they will gather information from boys, girls, men and women about levels and types of contamination. These surveys will also include information about the impact of the contamination, and will help to prioritize the project clearance activities based on humanitarian needs. In addition, the project will capacity build, train and mentor a local NGO to conduct survey and clearance activities in accordance with International Mine Action Standards. Male and female youth will be especially targeted in the training programme for the NGO. The structured and sustained training provided to the male and female youth in Ninewa governorate will not only increase local (national) clearance capacity in Iraq, but will also provide vulnerable youth with viable mid-long term livelihood opportunities within the general mine action sector. * Train public service personnel, including representatives of communities and authorities on aspects related to mine action. Training may include topics on explosive hazard awareness, search, and explosive ordnance disposal.This project is inline with the Iraq HRP 2019 Strategic Objective 1: Increase access to basic services: for the most vulnerable conflict-affected people and ensure responsible transition to durable solutions for those with prolonged humanitarian needs. It is also in-line with both with the Protection Cluster Objective 1: Provide specialized protection assistance, and community-based support, to vulnerable conflict-affected individuals, thereby enabling them to secure their rights, access humanitarian assistance and public services, and overcome barriers to achievement of durable solutions; and with the Protection Cluster Objective 2: Support and advocate with authorities, humanitarian and recovery actors to address protection issues in accordance with protection norms and applicable legal standards, thereby contributing to a favourable protection environment. This project will contribute to strengthening national capacity in survey and clearance through structured and sustained training and mentoring programme designed to enable a local NGO to conduct clearance activities in Iraq.

Protection Risk Analysis

1: Safety and security risks project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries.2: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members or partner staff members. 3: Community threshold of acceptable risk increases as a result of living in contaminated areas for a longer period of time, thus causing more accidents.4: Once marking of contaminated areas begins, communities mistakenly assume any area not marked must be safe.

Mitigation Measures

1: DCA and implementing partners have designated security focal points who actively monitor the security situation in project locations, set up security SOPs and procedures, and conduct regular security-related training for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required (informing all relevant authorities). Locations for training, awareness raising and risk education activities are selected and regularly reviewed based on security assessments and local conflict dynamics.2: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff and partners on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Complaints Handling Systems will be set up at the start of the project and beneficiaries will be informed of means of submitting feedback and complaints. Confidentiality is maintained with regards to sensitive complaints to ensure beneficiaries are not exposed to harm. 3: Project staff will ensure that RE sessions include topics on effects and consequences of risky behaviour leading to explosive hazard accidents. Together with community members they will discuss safer alternatives and encourage behaviour change. Peer to peer educators will also encourage positive behaviour change. Awareness raising and risk education messages and methodologies will be reviewed regularly and adapted as required.4: During Community Liaison activities, project staff will inform community representatives and members that areas may also be contaminated with explosive hazards, even if they are not marked. RE sessions will include the same message also.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Following Core Humanitarian Standards, the project will endeavour to be fully accountable to affected populations, and at the start of the project a feedback and complaints mechanism will be set up and regularly monitored throughout the project period. Beneficiaries will be informed of means of submitting project related feedback and complaints. Regular consultation with community members and community representatives throughout the implementation of the project will inform beneficiaries of project plans, services and progress. Surveys (NTS) conducted by project teams related to contamination will include consultations with community members (including boys, girls, men and women) and representatives where community needs for clearance as well as concerns related to suspected hazardous areas will be recorded. The information gathered during these surveys and during focus group discussions be used to prioritise the areas for clearance activities. In this way, the affected population will be involved in the prioritisation of clearance activities, based on their needs.
