Project Summary

This proposed action aims to urgently respond to the current rising critical needs among severely impacted households (HHs) to enhance their access to food and other basic needs and restore their livelihood capacities.
This project will build upon Mercy Corps’ current Cash Programme under the umbrella of Gaza Protection Consortium (GPC), which responds to continually increasing needs amongst targeted vulnerable groups, including those whose situation has been severely exacerbated by COVID-19 and recent hostilities.
Through unconditional cash assistance, this project will identify and select the most vulnerable households (HHs) in two groups of acutely vulnerable HHs from Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah governorates: i) vulnerable farmers and breeders in close coordination with Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) and ii) vulnerable HHs from the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) unserved or underserved waiting lists of Social Safety Net (SSN) with focus on Female-Headed Households (FHHs).
Through this project, Mercy Corps and its local NGO partners – Gaza Culture and Development Group and Al Foukhary Association for Country Development – will target 1,125 vulnerable HHs (6,188 individuals), broken down as follows:
1) 300 eligible vulnerable Farmer and Breeder HHs identified through combined HH vulnerability assessment and livelihood technical assessment. Each selected Farmer and Breeder HH will receive an average amount of $1,500 in three installments. Each installment comprises $500 (~ ILS 1,600) restricted cash for livelihood assets restoration.
2) 825 Highly vulnerable and crises affected HHs will receive a total amount of $1,300 over a period of four months (ILS 1,046 monthly installments in line with the Cash Working Group (CWG) endorsed MEB 2.0 Transfer Value).
This critical cash support will allow vulnerable HHs to have flexible, reliable, and timely financial support to address their urgent needs, restore their livelihoods, build resilience, and restore agriculture production capacity. Cash installments will be distributed through Electronic Debit Cards (e-cards) or via Cash over Counter through Bank of Palestine, which has extensive geographic coverage and is highly accessible through ATMs and Branches in North Gaza and Gaza.
By utilizing cash as a modality for direct assistance, beneficiaries have greater power to determine how to meet their own urgent needs, empower them, and maintain their dignity while reducing their vulnerability and contributing to the restoration of their food security. Additionally, in the Gaza Strip, local markets are functional, and vendors continue to have adequate stock available; therefore, cash assistance is an appropriate humanitarian response modality to meet critical basic needs.
Through well-established evidence-based vulnerability and technical targeting, the project will use the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF), and Technical Vulnerability Assessment Tool (TVAT) to identify, assess and verify selected vulnerable HHs to receive cash assistance, monitor activities, and ensure full coordination and engagement with the community and primary stakeholders. Mercy Corps will be the focal point of coordination with MoSD, and local partners will coordinate closely with MoA to cross-check names of affected households and affected Farmers and Breeders.
Additionally, Mercy Corps will promote community ownership, accountability, and participation throughout the project through baseline assessments, orientation sessions, Post Distribution Monitoring, and its Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanism (CARM), while also mobilizing key stakeholders to more deeply engage with identified vulnerable HHs.

1 - Needs

The overall socio-economic and humanitarian conditions in the Gaza Strip have continued to deteriorate. However, the drivers of this deterioration are multiple and more complex this time. The restrictions on movement and access to goods continued for 14 years, which has crippled the already weak economy. On the other hand, the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic has put an extra burden on essential services and access to employment. These conditions have deepened poverty and increased unemployment rates that reached 45% in the second quarter of 2021 (PCBS, 2021). The pre-elementary findings of the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) show that Gaza Strip relies heavily on humanitarian aid, with 73% of households (HHs) reporting that they received humanitarian aid in the past six months. In addition, the MSNA shows that 53% of HHs reported a decrease in their income due to the Covid-19 pandemic. A Mercy Corps and Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) baseline vulnerability assessment (Nov 2020) found a short-term unemployment rate of 74%, increasing to 82% in Khan Younis governorate. In addition, 80% of the 115,000 wage employees working in the private sector in Gaza receive an average monthly wage of ILS 600 ($ 182), far below the deep poverty line and less than the ILS 1,450 ($ 439) minimum wage (PCBS, 2020). This situation has rendered the majority that almost 62 % of Gaza’s population is food insecure (Flash Appeal, 2021).The Gaza Strip witnessed a devastating escalation in hostilities in May-2021 resulting in massive destruction of residential buildings, critical infrastructure, and livelihoods. The agricultural sector incurred enormous damage; the Ministry of Agriculture estimated the cost of direct damages incurred to the sector to be $ 38.7 Million (MoA, 2021). In addition, the World Bank estimates the cost of damage to the agriculture sector to be between $ 40 - 45 Million (World Bank, 2021). According to MoA, 11,000 dunams of open land vegetables were damaged with an average loss of $ 1,500 per dunum. Furthermore, 1,500 greenhouses were partially destroyed with an average cost of $ 2,000 per greenhouse (MoA, 2021). The situation has exacerbated the need to support affected livelihoods, especially farmers urgently, and restore their productive capacity after the latest escalation.The following assessments and the highlighted needs by Mercy Corps and partners inform this proposed action:1- Recent Escalation Impact: Mercy Corps has conducted a rapid emergency assessment as part of the baseline of the following projects:● A rapid emergency assessment part of the baseline for a current USAID-funded Cash Project (Mercy Corps, Sep 2021) indicated that among the 2,322 HHs assessed, 42 percent were directly impacted by the latest escalation, by either damage to shelter, loss of assets, or both. The loss is estimated to be approximately $ 1,300 per HH, with 9 percent of HHs reporting losses of more than $ 4,000. ● A baseline assessment of Farmers and Breeders under the current Cash Programme (Mercy Corps, Sep 2021) has revealed that 98% of the farmers and breeders have been impacted by the recent escalation of hostilities that took place in May 2021, with average livelihood losses of $ 3,400 that included land, irrigation networks, water catchment, and equipment. 78% of farmers prioritize land rehabilitation and material input like seeds, seedlings, fertilizers, and pesticides. The average cost needed to help farmers restore their livelihoods and agricultural activities is $ 2,275.2- Vulnerability Assessment: Mercy Corps has conducted the following assessment studies: ● A baseline vulnerability analysis conducted by Mercy Corps and Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) in the third quarter of 2020 on 2,241 prospective MPCA recipient HHs found an extremely high rate of indebtedness (90% of HHs) with an average per-HH liability of over ILS 10,000 ($ 3,114), which would take six years to repay without assistance.● A baseline vulnerability analysis conducted by Mercy Corps in Sep 2021 for a current USAID-BHA cash project assessing 2,322 HHs indicated that 95-97 percent of HHs reported being unable to fully meet their income, food, health, education, and other basic needs. It also showed that only 8 percent of the HHs had acceptable food consumption and 17 percent of the HHs reported being at risk of eviction. The baseline showed that the most common negative coping strategy used by 81 percent of HHs, is purchasing goods on credit or taking loans. This aligns with MSNA findings that show that 75 percent of the surveyed HHs have been buying food on credit. In addition, this BHA vulnerability assessment showed that 86 percent of the HHs were in debt, with an average total debt of ILS 3,000 ($ 934). ● The vulnerability assessment of a current HF project showed that 89% of the assessed HHs (712) had accumulated debts with an average of $1,500. HHs reported using several coping strategies (top used: Buying goods or services on credit or using borrowed cash 91%, sending HH members to eat elsewhere 69% and reducing expenses on health 57%). 71% of HHs used to reduce the number of daily meals and consume fewer food portions at least three times a week, and 58% used to borrow food from relatives at least three times a week. 94% of the HHs were with high rCSI (high coping >10), and 44.4% were with poor or borderline FCS. 95% of the HHs were below the deep poverty line or fell between the two poverty lines. 3- Gender ● Mercy Corps used the Gender Analysis Framework tool to help identify the gender-specific information needed to design and deliver smart, targeted project activities that meet the needs of both men and women. The tools help to analyze gender around five domains 1) Laws, regulations, and institutional practices; 2) Cultural norms and beliefs; 3) Gender roles, responsibilities, and time used; 4) Access to and control over assets and resources, and 5) Patterns of power and decision making.

2 - Activities

Mercy Corps and its local partners (Gaza Group and Al Foukhary) will target the most vulnerable and food-insecure HHs in two major groups: Group 1) Farmers and Breeders, and Group 2) vulnerable HHs and FHHs on the Social Safety Net (SSN) waiting list. The project will be implemented in Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah. The identification and selection of Farmers and Breeders (Group 1) will be carried out through an open application process and based on two assessments and scoring systems: 1) The Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool (UVAT) endorsed by the Cash Working Group. Based on the data collected through UVAT, Mercy Corps will apply the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF) scoring system. The PMTF will be used as the standardized selection criteria for targeting all the project’s beneficiary HHs. It is a methodology that helps to identify and target the most in need of humanitarian assistance programs. It uses multivariate regression to correlate specific proxies, including HHs’ characteristics, employability, assets, and other vulnerabilities, including Food Consumption and Coping Strategy Index. 2) The Technical Vulnerability Assessment Tool (TVAT) developed by Mercy Corps is based on established selection criteria. Technical selection criteria for farmers will include: i) dependency on agricultural productivity as their sole source of income; ii) ownership of small land – 1 to 5 dunums – that are currently being used for planting; iii) have incurred losses to their livelihood because of the economic decline; and iv) have lost their agriculture production or on the verge to losing their agricultural production due to increases in operating costs. Likewise, identifying unserved or underserved, vulnerable HHs and FHHs (Group 2) will be carried out by obtaining lists through the established partnership with the MoSD. The selection will be based on a 100% vulnerability assessment (UVAT) and applying the PMTF. Under direct and close technical and administrative guidance and capacity building from Mercy Corps, Gaza Group and Al Foukhary will conduct vulnerability assessments through hired and trained enumerators and assistant enumerators using mobile data collection tablets. The data will be integrated into Mercy Corps’ database (CommCare), which will automatically score the applications based on the integrated scoring system. During the door-to-door vulnerability assessment, any protection concerns or gender-based issues will be further investigated for any additional support required, including women, children, older people, and persons with special needs. The most eligible SSN HHs who are Extremely Poor (below the national Deep Poverty Line) will be prioritized to benefit from cash assistance. All final selected lists of beneficiaries will be cross-checked with the agencies providing similar assistance, and line ministries. Mercy Corps and Local Partners will provide each selected beneficiary household with an Electronic Debit Cash Card (eCard) that they will use to access the cash from ATMs or purchase directly from shops of their selection. Before cash distributions, Mercy Corps and Local Partners will provide printed orientation material packages to all selected beneficiaries. The orientation materials will provide all project information and communication with Mercy Corps and will include information about the project and activities; clear instructions on using eCards for the first time; bank and ATM locations in Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah governorate; project Monitoring and Evaluation; and Mercy Corps’ CARM system channels of communication. Additionally, in the locations where program activities will be conducted, posters will be displayed describing the CARM and providing a Mercy Corps’ Call Center Free Number and other channels for beneficiaries (or any other interested party) to contact program staff with concerns complaints or questions. Local partner NGOs are also required to adhere to Mercy Corps’ CARM and Safeguarding policies when implementing program activities. The cash transfer top-up amounts will be distributed for each group of beneficiaries in multiple installments: Group 1) Farmers and Breeders (Restricted Cash and Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance): Each of the selected 300 Farmers and Breeders (60 Gaza, 42 Middle Area, 108 Khan Younis, and 90 Rafah) will receive an average amount of $1,500 in three installments. Each installment comprises $500 (~ ILS 1,600) restricted cash for livelihood restoration in line with technical assessment and materials inputs that need to be decided by the Farmers and breeders. Group 2) SSN FHHs and HHs (Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance): Each of the selected 825 SSN FHHs and HHs (352 Gaza, 147 Middle Area, 200 Khan Younis, and 126 Rafah) will receive a total amount of $1,300 over a period of four consecutive months. The monthly transfer value of ILS 1,046 (~ $ 325) is in line with the Cash Working Group (CWG) endorsed MEB 2.0 Transfer Value. Finally, Mercy Corps’ comprehensive monitoring combined with a well-established CARM system and implemented PSEA and safeguarding policies allow program staff and partners teams to have real-time access to information on the ground. This also gives program teams and managers the ability to quickly adapt programming as necessary and maintain our strict and highest quality standards.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Mercy Corps Palestine has a robust Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanism (CARM) system backed by a global system. The system includes a Mercy Corps hosted and a dedicated call center with a free hotline through which a trained female operator can address simple project-related inquiries from beneficiaries and other community members. All CARM complaints or feedback are registered on an online tracking database in six categories. The six categories are i) Request for information, ii) Requests for assistance, iii) Minor dissatisfaction with program activities, iv) Major dissatisfaction with project activities or the behavior of Mercy Corps Team Members or Partners, v) Fraud or corruption by Mercy Corps Team Members or Partners vi) Sexual exploitation and abuse, child abuse, sexual misconduct, human trafficking by Mercy Corps Team Members or Partners. The first four categories (i, ii, iii, and iv) are sent to respective Mercy Corps members as per CARM policy, who prepare a response and communicate feedback back to callers promptly. The program team immediately adopts corrective actions, and report back to community members who submitted information requests or complaints. Data from the CARM tracking database are collected and analyzed regularly, and an internal report is produced in line with Mercy Corps Global CARM Minimum Standards policy. Other categorized sensitive complaints (v, and vi), which include fraud, corruption, child abuse, or sexual misconduct, are processed through a well-established dedicated safeguarding committee trained by Mercy Corps under direct supervision from both the Country Director and Mercy Corps’ Headquarters Integrity Team. Mercy Corps will give beneficiaries all possible means to convey their feedback about their concerns and specific needs, not only during assessments but also throughout the project life through direct contact, surveys, PDMs, and CARM.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

To appropriately address identified needs, Mercy Corps proposes a high-impact project that expands on the success of the current Mercy Corps Cash Programme to urgently meet the basic needs of highly vulnerable HHs in these community groups to help them cover their basic needs, reduce their use of negative coping strategies, avoid incurring further debt and restore their production capacity, resilience, and food security. In designing this project, Mercy Corps built on the success and learning of the current cash programming and the extended capacity under the Gaza Protection Consortium in partnership with the Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC), in addition to its standard procedure of gender and protection mainstreaming. Thorough risks, protection, and gender analyses were conducted to inform the design of activities, management of the project, mitigation plans, and safety and security measures for both participant beneficiaries and project staff. Mercy Corps provides refresher training regularly to all Mercy Corps team members and local partners on Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanisms (CARM), Safeguarding policies, including the protection of program participants, and code of conduct. Likewise, all program participant beneficiaries and community members will be oriented on approaching Mercy Corps and providing feedback or complaints using CARM. Each project beneficiary receives an orientation package that includes information about the project, CARM, and safety guidelines on cash handling. Mercy Corps collect and share data about beneficiaries using the Data Minimization Approach, Beneficiary Privacy, and Data Security Protocols: i.e., Mercy Corps gathers only the data that is adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary to determine the eligibility of humanitarian assistance and required to measure the impact and to cross-check to avoid duplicate assistance. Beneficiary Data are collected, synced, and stored on Mercy Corps’ highly secured CommCare database, which is only accessible to authorized MEL staff. Mercy Corps will distribute cash assistance in this project through Electronic Debit Cards (eCards) from the Bank of Palestine, which has extensive geographic coverage and is highly accessible by beneficiaries through ATMs and Branches in Gaza, Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah. This will ensure that cash assistance will be accessible by beneficiaries even outside regular banks' work hours. If local authorities impose movement restrictions due to COVID-19, Mercy Corps and local partner NGOs will coordinate with authorities to distribute cards to participants door-to-door and consider all health safety measures to both teams and beneficiaries.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through well-established evidence-based vulnerability and technical targeting, Mercy Corps and local partner NGOs will use the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF), and Technical Criteria to identify, assess and verify selected vulnerable HHs to receive cash assistance, monitor activities, and ensure full coordination and engagement with the line ministries (Ministry of Social Development [MoSD], and Ministry of Agriculture), the community and all relevant stakeholders. The identification of eligible beneficiary farmers, breeders will be carried out through an open and transparent application process using established vulnerability criteria and mobile data collection tools. Social Safety Net (SSN) vulnerable households will be identified from the MoSD unserved waiting lists of those who lost their source of income and livelihood from Gaza, the Middle Area, Khan Younis, and Rafah. The potential beneficiary's data will be synced on the Mercy Corps database (CommCare). Home-to-home vulnerability assessments will be conducted on all beneficiaries to confirm their eligibility according to established selection criteria for each beneficiary group. The PMTF will be used as the standardized selection criteria for targeting all the project's beneficiary HHs. It is a methodology that helps to identify and target the most in need of humanitarian assistance. It uses multivariate regression to correlate specific proxies, including HHs' characteristics, income, assets, coping strategy index, food consumption, expenditures, debts, and others. For farmer, or breeder beneficiaries, the selection will be based on two assessments: The vulnerability assessment of farmer HHs using the PMTF, combined with a technical vulnerability assessment tool developed based on established selection criteria. Final lists of selected HHs will be cross-checked with the FSS member’s database, INGOs providing similar assistance, and local authorities to avoid duplication. People with specific needs (older persons, pregnant and lactating women, people with chronic health conditions, disabled) and their caregivers will be consulted during the life of the program to understand their safety and dignity concerns. Mercy Corps and local partners' teams will identify the people with specific needs during the household assessment surveys. Mercy Corps will provide printed orientation material packages to all selected beneficiaries, share information, educate the beneficiaries on the project, and clarify the best possible ways to benefit from the assistance. Beneficiaries with specific needs or have difficulties due to illiteracy, or cultural reasons (e.g. needing a male relative to accompany a female household member) will be assisted to identify and select trustee "Helpers," such as first-degree family members, to assist them in receiving their cash/cashing out from ATMs. During the implementation and monitoring of project activities, the Mercy Corps program team and local partners teams can go to the home of people with specific needs to collect feedback and ensure they received the assistance.
