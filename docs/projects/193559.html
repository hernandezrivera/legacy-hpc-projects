Project Summary

The project aims at decreasing the high morbidity and mortality rate across Sudan by providing life-saving access to scarcely available services with an integrated approach covering multiple sectors. The access to free primary and secondary healthcare for 60,000 children 0-14 will be ensured through the provision of high-quality paediatric services in all EMERGENCY (EMR)’s facilities. Access to free maternal & reproductive health services for women will be guaranteed to 8,700 women living in underserved areas through in-hospital and outreach consultations. Free regular malnutrition screenings & supplementary feeding cycles will be provided for 5,800 hospitalized malnourished children in EMR’s wards. Free cardiac screenings & life-saving surgery will be ensured to patients affected by neglected heart illnesses leading to premature death such as Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD), to decrease the prevalence of chronic cardiac disease across the Country. Finally, health & nutrition education will be provided to promote positive behaviors to reduce both malnutrition and the risk of vector borne and waterborne diseases, which account for the majority of illnesses diagnosed in EMR’s Paediatric Centres.
The Centres in Port Sudan and Nyala are secondary healthcare facilities with integrated services. A total of 15.000 and 23.000 patients respectively will be triaged and receive an outpatient consultation, receiving a pharmacological therapy, vaccinations, blood investigations, X-ray or blood transfusion whenever needed. 4586 children will be screened for malnutrition through MUAC screening & WHZ, and RUTFs will be provided for 3300 SAM & MAM children. Depending on their level of micronutrient deficiency, iron, folic acid, calcium/vitamin D and zinc supplementation will be provided. Patients with medical complications will be hospitalized; those who will not meet the admission and treatment criteria will be stabilised and referred via ambulance to public facilities and feeding centres that are part of EMR’s network of collaboration with local stakeholders. In Port Sudan, a comprehensive set of SRH services such as ANC, PNC and FP for 1200 women, plus EPI services for newborns, will also be performed within the premises of the Eskandaria governmental PHC, in agreement with the local Midwifery Department.
In Mayo, PHC and SRH services will be offered to 22.000 children 0-14 and 7.500 women of the catchment area. In the Centre, children will be triaged and receive an outpatient consultation; those mainly affected by dehydration and respiratory tract infections will be stabilized, given free pharmacological therapy and referred to public facilities. Blood investigations & vaccinations will also be performed. All children will be screened for malnutrition and receive RUTFs and vitamin/iron supplementation if needed. SAM cases will be referred towards Stabilization Centres nearby if no further medical complications are found, and towards higher-level health facilities in case of medical complications. EMR has also developed well-structured cooking classes three times per week for the caregivers of SAM and MAM cases run by experienced Health Promoters (CHPs). Additionally, EMR will ensure SRH services addressed to women of reproductive age, like in Port Sudan.
With the aim of responding to the endemic prevalence of RHD in Sudan, EMR cardiologists will also perform ad hoc cardiac assessments, and children in need of further specialized consultations will be referred to EMR's Salam Centre for Cardiac Surgery in Khartoum where they will be further evaluated in OPD, and potentially admitted in ward for surgery (all free of charge).
Health and nutrition promotion sessions in all EMR facilities and in outreach will ensure long-lasting behavioral changes at local level. On top of this and to ensure long-term sustainability and retention, the intervention will also focus on capacity building of national health staff in all EMR facilities.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan remains complex, with 15.6 million people – about a third of the population - estimated to need humanitarian assistance in 2023. High rates of inflation, the economy underperforming, the below average agricultural season of 2022 and the war in Ukraine draining imports of wheat from Ukraine and Russia all contribute to worsening living conditions and an increase in the health and nutritional needs of the population. Local communities are consequently forced to adopt harmful coping mechanisms, such as a reduction in health expenses and in food variety and quality. According to figures from the preliminary phases of the 2023 HNO, the global acute malnutrition (GAM) prevalence among children under-five continues to remain at elevated levels of 13.6%. According to EMR’s 2022 data, the MAM-SAM prevalence in targeted areas is the following: in Mayo (Angola & Mandela areas) 19% MAM, 11% SAM. In Nyala town 14% MAM, 13% SAM (higher during the rainy season). In Port Sudan (Shager area) 12% MAM, 11% SAM (higher during the lean season). On average 15% of all children who receive a consultation in our facilities are MAM and 12% are SAM. In addition, around 50% of all pregnant women examined in Mayo and Port Sudan (where we operate in very vulnerable areas) present some degree of malnutrition , with MUAC <230mm . When it comes to healthcare, Health Cluster data highlights that there are major gaps in the provision of health services in all States across Sudan. Paediatric and maternal health services, as well as medicine supply, are deemed insufficient, including the provision of essential drugs, vaccinations, malnutrition screenings and referrals. In Mayo, the EMR Paediatric Centre is the only free paediatric facility available, and public hospitals in the area (which are not free) are often understaffed and devoid of necessary equipment (ex: oxygen, lab reagents etc.); women’s pregnancies are often followed by community midwives, who don’t have specialized training and charge for their services. In Nyala there is only one public hospital (not free) whose staff is often on strike for weeks on end, so our facility often bears the brunt of an increased influx of paediatric patients who have already lost precious time to access treatment. In Port Sudan most hospitals are located in town, while the peripheral area of Shager/Eskanderia where our facility is located only avails of one (not free) PHC with very little equipment and personnel. Moreover, before our SRH activities, there were no free midwifery services in the area and the MOH midwives only worked twice a week for 3 hours, so virtually no PLW ever reached the four minimum ANC visits recommended by the WHO. Because of gaps in public service provision, most people rely on the private sector, which increases the population’s out-of-pocket expenditure, which underlines the importance of offering free integrated healthcare services, as well as referrals. Despite public healthcare supposedly being free of charge for children under-5, parents often find themselves having to pay for auxiliary services such as blood works and medicines. Finally, the concerning burden of heart disease is also to be mentioned, RHD being the most common cause of cardiac morbidity and mortality among young people in Sudan according to official data, with an incidence of 1 per 1,000 inhabitants. If neither prevention nor early detection programmes are conducted, specialised surgeries are needed to treat it and a very low percentage of the population can afford them, resulting in avoidable premature deaths.Brain drain also has a detrimental impact on the health sector: the current level of health personnel staffing is only able to cater to 17% of Sudan’s population (according to World Bank data, the N. of health personnel per capita is only 0.3), as many trained and qualified health staff leave the country in search of better income opportunities.

2 - Activities/Outputs

For the Health sector, the chosen response modalities will be: facilitating access to basic services for vulnerable population; outreach services for the underserved areas; supporting primary health services; ensuring availability of essential medicines; provision of MISP for RH to support reproductive, neonatal and child health; expansion of EPI program. For the Nutrition Sector, under HRP SO1.2: minimum package for prevention of malnutrition among under 2s and 5s; early detection through MUAC; micronutrient supplementation; dissemination of nutrition information with cooking demonstrations and SBCC; growth monitoring; referral strengthening. In particular:1. Children 0-5 have access to primary & secondary health services: OPD activities (triage, visits, medications & lab services). Patients identified with RHD symptoms will be referred to the Salam Centre for Cardiac Surgery. In case of other medical complications, they will be transferred to a public hospital (for Mayo) or admitted in EMR’s wards in Nyala and Port Sudan. CHPs will ensure follow-up. EPI will also be provided. 2. Increased access to maternal & SRH services in Mayo and Port Sudan: EMR will target PLW and support maternal healthcare with comprehensive ANC visits. Ultrasounds for fetal heart rate measurement & TT vaccinations will be carried out. PNC will be provided to all newborns & mothers ( hospital & household level). Women who receive ANC services will be advised on the importance of PNC. FP services and counselling will be ensured. 3. Children 0-5 have access to nutritional services: Malnutrition screenings will be performed on all patients through MUAC and WHZ systems (hospital & outreach level). Following the PM2A approach, a preventive nutrition package will be offered with facility-based screening & age-appropriate behaviour change communication on nutrition, health & hygiene practices for parents. RUTFs will be provided for SAM and MAM children; children with medical complications will be admitted in ward (Nyala and Port Sudan) and treated with a TSFP; those with no complications will be referred to SCs. Micronutrient supplementation will be ensured. Facilitated access to nutrition facilities for children with disabilities, treatment & referrals will be granted through training of CHPs and health staff in the identification of CWDs. Simple cooking classes will be carried out with caregivers in Mayo on how to prepare nutritious food with affordable & locally available products; once more room is available they will also be organized in Nyala & Port Sudan. 4. Health education sessions are carried out: Health promotion will advise the local population on good health and hygiene practices and encourage caregivers to comply with prescribed treatments for their children and with scheduled follow-up consultations. They will focus on critical areas such as communicable and non-communicable disease prevention, hygiene practices and nutrition. Specific messages will target PLW. The sessions will be held at health facility level every day and in outreach in the catchment area. 5. Health staff is regularly trained: EMR’s Centres are accredited as Paediatric training facilities by the Sudanese Medical Specialization Board, and are periodically assigned residents. Theoretical and on-the-job training sessions will be carried out for all medical staff and CHPs on topics such as integrated management of childhood illnesses, maternal and child health, disease surveillance & reporting. 6. Equitable coverage and accessibility to quality referral services are ensured: EMR will strengthen its referral network, both towards public facilities and to the Salam Centre for Cardiac Surgery for patients with RHD. All health promotion & training activities & relative budget will contribute to resilience:20% of health activities.40% of nutrition activities.30% of SRH activities.100% of health promotion activities.100% of training activities.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

EMR in all its projects tries to have the least possible environmental impact, and is committed to reducing energy consumption for heating, cooling and for the daily activities of its hospitals through the installation of renewable energy systems and the use of local resources. The adiabatic filters installed under the hospitals of Nyala and Port Sudan constitute an innovative cooling system that allows the clinic to be air-conditioned using a natural water ventilation method inspired by the tradition of the northern Sahara, thus dramatically decreasing the energy expenditure for air conditioning. Proper medical waste management is also ensured to protect the patients, the communities and the environment. In addition, to avoid paper waste all toilets are equipped with a stockpile of small fabric towels, which are discarded in a laundy bean after each single use so as to be washed and reutilized.Additionally, following the floods in Darfur in 2022, the State observed a further deterioration of the environment situation in Nyala and surroundings. The lack of a waste management system and the widespread presence of stagnant water provides a perfect breeding ground for disease and mosquitos, which in turn are at the centre of the recent outbreaks of chikungunya and dengue fever. This can aggravate the endemic/epidemic potential for outbreaks of vector-borne diseases, such as malaria, and communicable water-borne diseases contracted through direct contact with polluted waters (such as amoebiasis and AWD). Therefore, during health promotion activities EMR will promote hygiene messages that can raise awareness on proper sanitary behaviours that will reduce the potential for these risks.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As per foundation chart, EMR respects and encourages diversity, equality, inclusion and gender balance across all interventions, and is committed to ensuring access to free-of-charge healthcare in compliance with the principle of non-discrimination (both applied to medical practice as such, as well as to crosscutting policies e.g. hiring policy). This principle, which is taken into consideration during all phases of project implementation, seeks to guarantee that health assistance as a fundamental human right is “exercised without discrimination of any kind”. All international and national staff members within EMR comply with the principle, and base their day-to-day medical practice on the EQS (Equality, Quality, Social Responsibility) Model outlined in the Manifesto for a Human Rights-based Medicine (accessible on EMR’s website). Collection and analysis of Sex and Age Disaggregated Data (SADD) allows EMR staff to assess, monitor and evaluate service delivery, treatments, achievement of targets and intended outcomes, with the aim to better capture the needs of different beneficiaries and adjust programmes according to observed changes. To enhance access to healthcare services for women, female staff members are constantly recruited in various roles. Their presence ensures not only high professionalism, but also the maintenance of a culturally sensitive, safe and peaceful environment for female beneficiaries and their children to seek healthcare assistance. The access needs of persons with reduced mobility have also been taken into account, as all facilities are equipped with dedicated infrastructure such as ramps.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In regards to the complaints mechanism, EMR operates on the belief that beneficiaries are best equipped to voice their concerns; therefore, EMR liaises regularly with community focal points and local authorities to get feedback and to ensure the acceptance of the intervention. EMR also has an internal Code of Conduct (or Code of Ethics), which outlines the commitment of the organisation to humanitarian principles, excellence, transparency and accountability. The Code, as well the specific internal document on PSEA, condemns sexual exploitation, abuse and discrimination in all its forms. All policies respect sexual integrity in all our programmes and activities, and prohibit gender harassment, sexual exploitation and discrimination. The internal Child Protection Policy also condemns child exploitation and abuse in all its forms. All policies, programmes and activities respect children, their well-being and development.As per feedback and complaint mechanisms, dedicated complaint and suggestion boxes will be set-up for beneficiaries to provide their anonymous feedback on the services provided. They will be sensitized about the existence of the complaint mechanism, and their feedback will be analyzed and collected, to keep on adjusting services accordingly. Dedicated focal points will be available for beneficiaries to confidentially discuss issues face to face. The focal points will provide reports to EMERGENCY HQ on the complaints received. EMR also regularly holds training courses for service providers on AAP in order to ensure that our staff behaves responsibly towards affected populations.
