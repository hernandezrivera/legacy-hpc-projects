Project Summary

Aweil East, Aweil North and Aweil South counties are among the counties that are expected to experience high levels of food insecurity and malnutrition as per the November 2024 IPC report. The three counties are IPC Phase 4 counties in September to November 2024 and are expected to remain the same till June 2025. Malnutrition level in the three counties is classified as critical between September to November 2024 and is expected to remain the same through to June 2025. This is an indication of the poor living standards and dire livelihood needs among the people in these counties. To minimize the food insecurity levels in these counties, FADM will initiate activities that will equip the vulnerable household with crop inputs and skills to be able to produce vegetables and crops for household consumption thus improving their food security level. FADM will additionally equip fisherfolks with fishing gears and skills that will enable them to carry out fishing activities helping them to improve their diets and sell the excess fish caught to earn cash for other household use. To achieve this, FADM will secure and distribute crop inputs to a total of 3,000 households in Aweil East, Aweil South and Aweil North Counties with 1,000 households targeted in each county. FADM will train a total 300 lead farmers to support the households that will be provided with crop inputs. FADM will also initiate dry season vegetable production among 1,500 households with 500 households targeted in each of the three counties. FADM will set up kitchen gardens at nutrition centers to teach mothers whose children are admitted at the nutrition centers how to grow nutritious vegetables in kitchen gardens with the activity initiated in 36 nutrition centers in the three targeted counties. The kitchen gardens will also act as demonstration centers for other farmers. FADM will additionally provide fishing kits to 1,500 fisherfolks in the three targeted counties and train the 1,500 fisherfolk on good fishing practices..

1. Needs

The people of South Sudan are still confronted by formidable challenges marked by conflict in 2013 and 2016, intensifying humanitarian, economic, social and political crises. The country grapples with dire economic conditions marked by institutional fragility, economic policy distortions and limited diversification. Inflation, currency depreciation and limited diversification are significant economic challenges in the country. In addition, global trends, including pandemic-induced supply shortages, rising United States dollar (USD) denominated commodity prices and a stronger USD has impacted the economy significantly. The effects of conflicts deepen extreme poverty and hamper private sector prospects and livelihood improvements. Additionally, the country faces severe climate vulnerabilities, ranking as the second most vulnerable country globally to natural hazards according to the 2023 INFORM Risk Index and stands among the top five most climate-vulnerable countries yet an estimated 95 per cent of the population depends on climate-sensitive livelihoods, increasing their risk to face food insecurity due to climate change. These conditions have made the acute food insecurity and malnutrition to continue to deteriorate in the country with an estimated 6.3 million people (47% of the population) classified to be facing IPC Phase 3 or above between September and November 2024. Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical level of food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 4 and a further 41,000 people facing catastrophic level of food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 5. Compared to the same period last year, this indicates an increase of approximately 500,000 people in Phase 3 or above. During the same period, 11 counties across the country and an estimated 126,000 (20 percent) returnees are in Phase 4 and 62 counties are in Phase 3. (IPC report of November 2024). Data from the same report indicates that an estimated 6.1 million people (45 percent of the population) will likely experience Phase 3 or above. Of this total, 1.71 million people are projected to be in Phase 4, and 31,000 South Sudanese returnees will likely experience IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) in the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025. During the lean season of April to July 2025 an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4, and 63,000 people likely to be in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The nutrition situation in the country has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. Aweil East, Aweil North and Aweil South counties are among the counties that are expected to experience high levels of food insecurity and malnutrition as per the November 2024 IPC report. The three counties are IPC Phase 4 counties in September 2024 and are expected to remain so till June 2025. Malnutrition level in the three counties is classified as critical between September to November 2024 and will be so till June 2025. This is an indication of the poor living standards and dire livelihood needs in these counties. Vulnerable community members especially women are the most affected as they are the ones that bear the most burden of providing for their family in such situations and are susceptible to other forms of exploitation as they fend for their children. Thus, the need to initiate this project’s activities to improve the living standard of people living in these counties and include other cross cutting issues such as protection when doing so.

2. Project activities

Inflation, currency depreciation and limited diversification are significant economic challenges. The COVID-19 pandemic exacerbated these issues, causing a sharp oil price drop, income loss, severe currency devaluation and soaring inflation, which the country has yet to recover from. In addition, global trends, including pandemic-induced supply shortages, rising United States dollar (USD) denominated commodity prices and a stronger USD impact the economy significantly. South Sudan's reliance on imports for consumer goods faces a persistent balance of payments deficits and limited hard currency access beyond oil exports. As of 31 August 2023, the South Sudanese pound (SSP) had depreciated against the USD by 50 per cent since the beginning of 2023. 1. Distribution of main season livelihood kits.FADM will secure from UN FAO and distribute agricultural inputs to farmers in the three counties. FADM will target 1,000 households in each of the targeted county. The kits will include maize, sorghum, groundnuts, cowpeas, pumpkins, onions and water melon seeds respectively. This will enable the targeted household to grow and harvest agricultural products during the rainy season thus helping to improve their access to nutritious food. FADM will also procure and distribute hoes to the targeted household in order to be equipped to able to tilt their lands and grow the seeds that will be provided.2. Initiate dry season vegetable production.FADM will target 500 households in each of the three counties with dry season vegetable production. The beneficiaries will be grouped into a group of 20 people and each group will be equipped with 20 watering cans, 50g of kales seeds, 50g of tomato seeds, 50g of eggplant seeds and 50g of onion seeds. The groups will be supported to set up vegetable gardens along the rivers and next to water ponds in order to grow vegetables from the seeds that will be provided. The seeds will be secured from FAO. FADM will also procure and distribute hoes to each of beneficiary that will participate in vegetable production. This will enable the targeted households to produce nutritious vegetables for both household use and for sale with the money from the vegetables sold used in supporting their households.3. Distribution of fishing kits FADM will target 500 households in each of the county that will be targeted and all the three counties will be targeted with this activity. FADM will secure hooks and monofilaments from FAO and distribute to households who carry out fishing activities. The beneficiaries will be taught how to make nets from the monofilaments to support them in fishing. The provided fishing gears will help the targeted households to harvest fish that will be used at the household level and some sold for cash for household use. This will help in diversifying the household nutrition needs and improve their wellbeing. 4. Train farmers on good agricultural practices and fisherfolk on good fishing practices.FADM will train 300 lead farmers in each county targeted. The trained lead farmers will then be deployed to support the targeted farmers. The lead farmers will be trained on good agricultural practices such as nursery bed preparation, transplanting land preparations, weeding, thinning, mulching, post-harvest handling among others. Additionally, FADM will train 1,500 fisherfolks on good fishing practices such as selective fishing to allow the young fish to grow and multiply, fish preservation methods, proper fish storage methods and how to make different products from fish.
