Project Summary

Toward 2024, BRAC's Child Protection programme aims to strengthen the child protection system and services across the Rohingya and host communities for the well-being of the children and adolescents. BRAC Child Protection programme objective aligns with the objective and target of the CPSS. BRAC has proposed child protection intervention in 14 camps and 7 unions of the host community. The programme will target 77,502 (59% female, 41% male) children, adolescents, community members, and stakeholders.
The J-MSNA 2023 study shows that 28% of Rohingya refugee children aged under 18 are involved in marital unions, whereas 70% are girls. The determination of child labour is a significant issue due to economic limitations. According to the protection monitoring report, child labour has increased approximately 3% in Q1 2023, with male children having a higher prevalence. This programme will ensure the continuation of existing services, e.g. unstructured Psychosocial Support (PSS), structured PSS, case management services, adolescent support activities, e.g. life skills-based sessions, set-up adolescent clubs, parenting sessions, community-based child protection mechanism, e.g. Community-based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC), awareness message, Early Childhood Development (ECD) activities, social cohesion activities, and maintain sectoral coordination with a priority focus on integrated services for children with disabilities. The gender-diversified children groups and Children with Disabilities will be supported through this programme’s activities. The referral pathway system and support for the target children from all gender, cultures, ethnicities, and other backgrounds, including Children with Disabilities, will be ensured to enhance effective and efficient programme support. The intervention will engage and encourage the adolescents in various activities, e.g. skills development and dialogue within the community to promote empowerment of children, adolescent girls, boys, and Children with Disabilities in the target communities. Therefore, the programme will adopt a comprehensive approach catering to 0-18-year-old children and adolescents and above 18 years (boys and girls in different camps and host communities) will be reached with the CP services as well. A holistic approach will be applied to provide child protection services, e.g. case management, structured psychosocial support, community-based child protection mechanism, early childhood development, life skills, and service provision for Children with Disabilities
BRAC will consider the most vulnerable population in this programme and prioritise safety, dignity and meaningful access and participation of children, adolescents and community to ensure the integrity of the programme.
BRAC will maintain strong coordination with the Child Protection Sub-Sector (CPSS) and Case Management Technical Working Group (CMTWG) by attending the different meetings and workshops and providing technical support where required. This CP programme will also coordinate with the Protection Working Group (PWG), Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) Working Group, and ECD Working Group as well as with the government, e.g. Department of Social Service through active participation and contribution to ensure effective programme exposure and intervention. BRAC will make sectoral inputs to the situation report, 5W report, and need-based report, including the situation monitoring report.
BRAC will apply its Safeguarding policy, CP Code of Conduct, to ensure the safeguarding and Protection From Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) related cases of the staff, volunteers and programme participants in alignment with the ISCG guidelines. The Minimum Standard For Child Protection (CPMS) in humanitarian action guidelines will be followed while implementing the interventions to ensure “Do no Harm”.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Among 947,230 Rohingya people living in Cox’s Bazar, 52% are children who are mostly dependent on humanitarian support (UNHCR-Govt. Population factsheet September 2023). Children living in Cox’s Bazar (camps and host communities) are still facing various and complex protection risks i.e. child marriage, child labour, neglect, etc. and challenges that reflect their different vulnerabilities, needs, strengths, and capacities depending on a number of intersecting factors, such as age, gender, disability, and other relevant diversity. According to the J-MSNA 2023 report, 2% of Rohingya refugee children aged under 18 years reported being married, and most of them were girls (70%). According to Joint Protection Monitoring Report 2023, the issue of child labour continued to be a matter of concern for many reasons to overcome the economic constraint of their family. During the specified time frame, there was a 3% rise in reported instances of child labour in comparison to Quarter 1 in 2023, with a greater occurrence seen among male children. Based on the data, it has been shown that around 28% of children were involved in the worst form of child labour. The phenomenon of child marriage persists with a notable gender disparity, as seen by 92% of reported occurrences affecting female children, while the remaining 8% is attributed to male children. Mental health and psychosocial distress is also important child protection concern in this setting. Unaccompanied and separated children need special attention as they are more prone to different forms of protection concerns. The Rohingya households reported that at least one child was engaged in child labour, which was also a great concern in the host community as a significant number of working children were involved in hazardous work. The REACH report showed that 12% of male and female individuals were identified as Persons with Disabilities, and the prevalence rate of disabilities was found to be increasing, ranging from 2% among 2 to 4 years old and to 51% among older persons (Source: REACH, May 2021, “Age and Disability Inclusion Needs Assessment-Rohingya Refugee Response). The scenario was almost the same for the host communities as well. Infectious diseases, such as Scabies, Diarrhea, Dengue, etc., affected the child protection scenario differently. These diseases’ accompanying containment increased their exposure to the risks inherent in daily life in the camps and surrounding host communities. Sometimes, the unstable situation of the camps, such as internal conflict and gunfights, generates anxiety, aggression, depression and various sorts of psychosocial stress, which need to be addressed and treated properly through comprehensive and structured Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS). Besides, there is a lack of sufficient ECD programmes. In addition to this, out-of-service young children aged below 4 in the camps accelerated the above issues. On the other hand, the limited scope of pre-vocational skills-related activities for adolescents has also exacerbated these issues. Moreover, it is observed that there is a lack of social cohesion between the host and Rohingya communities, which results in tension among them.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Through ISCG, the Child Protection Sub-sector (CPSS) has already initiated the rationalisation process. To comply with the rationalisation process, BRAC has started adopting an integration approach so that child protection services can be enhanced with the multi-sectoral camp-based activities. BRAC has already received the allocation of camps through a rationalisation process where BRAC will be working in 11 camps, but the programme proposed an additional 3 camps (1E, 1W and 9; based on the CPSS Rationalisation Tips Sheet) along with the existing one. As a part of the contribution towards host communities, this programme has proposed implementing the intervention in the 7 unions where BRAC already has its ongoing CP interventions.In this regard, BRAC will adopt integrated protection services, including an early childhood development programme. Referring to the Rationalisation principle (principle 3), a holistic approach will be taken to ensure all child protection services to children and adolescents. Following the rationalisation guidelines CPSS, BRAC has already begun the process and started implementation. BRAC has a solid fundraising team to work for funding opportunities and, as a result, has confirmed funds from other non-UN donors, which will be utilised to continue the service.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The Child Protection programme will closely work with the general protection and Gender-based Violence (GBV) programmes to ensure the relevant services for children, women, Children with Disabilities, etc. This programme will provide integrated services along with general protection and GBV. As part of this, BRAC will also consider protection mainstreaming through awareness-raising activities, where all protection messages will be disseminated to the target children, adolescents, and community members to enhance the protection mainstreaming mechanism. GBV-related cases received under the Child Protection programme will be referred to the respective sectors (e.g., MHPSS, GBV). This programme will also provide orientation to the Community-Based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC) on GBV, protection and service mapping. BRAC will also adopt the protection principle during the programme implementation.As a PSEA network member, BRAC has compliant policies prioritising protection and safeguarding with an emphasis on 'Do No Harm' to prevent unintended adverse effects of any programme.

3.3 - Age & Disability

BRAC will remain actively engaged in ensuring the equal participation and fair distribution of humanitarian assistance without discrimination, abuse, neglect or exploitation of any population group. This programme will ensure gender equality and disability inclusion through awareness sessions, parent meetings, community consultation, Community Based Child Protection Committee, etc. Besides, BRAC will emphasize Gender and Sexual Orientation diversity in its programme design by integrating and involving people from diverse backgrounds through different activities of the intervention. As part of this, the elderly people will be reached through awareness-raising activities, Community-Based Child Protection Committees, parent engagement and community consultation. This programme will ensure access of the target Children, adolescents, youth, and adults, including Persons with Disabilities, to the CP services equally. This programme activities are aligned with ISCG guidelines and the integrated approach.BRAC will be imperative to ensure that protective measures are not only culturally sensitive but also provide equal access to essential services and opportunities for all members of the Rohingya and host communities. BRAC has a commitment to creating a more just and equitable environment for all Rohingya individuals, where the vulnerabilities of age and disability are addressed comprehensively, ensuring that no one within the community is left behind in their pursuit of safety, dignity, and well-being. This holistic approach prioritizes preventing harm, promoting resilience, and providing support regardless of age and disability. To make bridging through multi-level child protection activities and minimize the gap among the target community, particularly for the elderly and Persons with Disabilities. Reasonable accommodation, need-based tailoring and person-specific programme activities will be in-built and incorporated into the programme design and service points.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to affected population (AAP) is an active commitment to use power responsibly by taking into account giving account to and being held to account by the people humanitarian organisations seek to assist. BRAC will provide project participants from diverse communities with a meaningful influence over decision-making in inclusive, non-discriminatory arrangements. Besides, BRAC will also put individual feedback mechanisms and innovative approaches to joint feedback mechanisms that can reinforce transparency and mutual accountability and have a positive impact. Project information will be shared with the beneficiaries effectively and transparently.The designated programme is accountable to the affected population, e.g. boys, girls and youth. Under the pillars of AAP, BRAC will ensure the inclusion of persons with disabilities and special needs through the programme activities, which include the participation of the persons with disabilities and taking opinions during the designing and planning of any activities in the programme and ensuring the transparency of the programme. However, in all stages of the interventions, the programme will ensure the effective participation of the community following CPSS AAP guidelines. The programme will continue to receive feedback through established feedback help desks in all facilities to address opinions and suggestions of the community to strengthen the protection services following BRAC's procedures. Following the BRAC's guidelines (with technical support from CPSS), feedback and complaints will be addressed. On the other hand, to make the programme sustain, CBCPC, youth group, adolescent group and community volunteers will be oriented and guided on how to take responsibility.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

BRAC is a member of the PSEA network and has policies, procedures, and mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse. BRAC will ensure the safety and well-being of programme participants, staff, and the community at large. BRAC has strong compliance and policies, including BRAC Safeguarding and BRAC child safeguarding policies, to ensure immediate action in response to any PSEA and safeguarding-related incidents. To ensure BRAC’s accountability towards the affected population, a feedback mechanism such as complaint boxes in service points, 24-hour hotline numbers for PSEA focal, sensitization on right-based assistance in all existing information provisions, train community volunteers, vendors as well as consultants on PSEA matters to ensure BRAC’s commitment to AAP and Child Safeguarding, protection and GBV principles and do no harm will be in place. BRAC will replicate the materials produced by the PSEA network to raise awareness among Rohingya and host communities. While BRAC will follow the Inter-Agency SoPs for SEA complaint referral in the Rohingya Response in Bangladesh and acknowledge that reports of sexual exploitation and abuse are mandatory, under penalty of termination of this agreement, the investigation, follow-up, and potential disciplinary action against SEA offenders will be based on the BRAC’s internal procedures. The programme will ensure that the target Children, both in the Rohingya and host communities, can easily raise their voices through the CFRM box, BRAC helpline (24/7) and community consultation meetings. An autonomous body which is built to maintain the CP programme and BRAC team will be operational in addressing the issues by following and adopting all the protection principles and privacy and ensuring the required support. BRAC will closely coordinate with the national network and conduct reference checks about new recruitments to ensure that SEA offenders are not employed or re-hired.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC HCMP has already adopted an Emergency Operation Centre (EOC) and Emergency Response Team (ERT) structure with representatives from all programmes to respond in an integrated way in emergencies. During any emergency, all programmes work jointly in the affected areas by conducting primary assessments and analyzing the situations. This programme will work in a holistic approach, considering the disaster risk reduction plan to ensure all the facilities are kept safe from disaster and make sure as a temporary shelter. It will provide essential disaster risk management and emergency response training to the programme staff and volunteers, including community volunteers, to respond effectively during an emergency. The BRAC's DRR and CP programme team will collaborate closely with Site Management, Camp in Charge (CiC), Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP), and the Upazila Nirbahi Officer (UNO) to organise awareness sessions on hazards. It will help in preparing this intervention to mitigate the risks associated with potential landslides, cyclones, and floods. An ERT will be established to respond to during any emergency. Evacuation of identified Persons with Disabilities will be ensured through the ERT in case of any issues raised during the disaster. On the other hand, BRAC will contribute to protecting the camp and host community environment by planting trees in the child protection facilities and raising awareness of environmental protection at the community level. The target community children and adolescents will also be oriented on the environment and climate change, which will help them understand environmental conservation. In case of any child or adolescent’s needs, special care during an emergency or crisis, one-to-one counselling at these centres will be available. Regular assessment-based precautions will be taken to address the DRR issues.
