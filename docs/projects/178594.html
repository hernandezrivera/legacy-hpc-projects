Project Summary

BRD will providing lending services for both men and women on favorable terms.
This project aims at firmly establishing work culture and principle of self-reliance in addition to providing job opportunities for people in need.
This project is directed towards providing an opportunity for those who have the skills, capabilities and willing to establish a private business.
BRD offer them liquid cash required to accelerate and promote their activities. This will help in creating new jobs and increase beneficiaries’ income.
BRD providing financial inclusion and lending services for 200 persons.
Returned installments that are paid by those persons will be given to other ones.
BRD aim to number of beneficiaries will reach 120 different from the 200 persons/borrower who supported before by the end of the year, using favorable loans system to be the total 320.
The persons who got the financial support will again this persons support other people by supporting them to reach the total number of 320 people.
More than 76% of the Syrian citizens suffer from extreme poverty, 35% of them suffer from extreme poverty.
Each person, income ranging from 0 to 1.9 dollar daily as per definition of income line issued by world bank.
On the other hand, number of evacuees from south of Idleb have reached more than 520K (CCCM cluster) evacuee from DEC2019 to JAN 2020
Most of those evacuees are settled in north of Syria in Idlib, Afrin and Euphrates shield.
This project is offering means and tools of revenue generating. Moreover, those projects will emerge people from poverty, improving the quality of living and enhancing resilience.
Total number of beneficiaries 1500 Individual
Men 700, young youth male individual 50, Woman 700, young youth female individual 50. 50% IDPs and 50% HC, 10 PwDs
BRD value the participatory approach in engaging stakeholders and we always make sure to apply this approach at different stages of the project.
In the implementation phase: During the implementation of the project, the partners will ensure that the project is introduced to the local community and local authorities including goals, activities, duration, location, and the criteria of selecting beneficiaries. To ensure well introduction process, it will include: consultation sessions with local authorities, seminars with the local community, sessions with the beneficiaries, flyers, social media posts, road posters, and banners. Several project activities, for example: community assessment and community initiatives, are designed to be beneficiaries an essential part of the outputs and implementation.
In addition to - Micro-grant committee is involved in PMA (Output 2.1) to avoid supporting projects that can make the poor much poorer, PMA contributes to Do No Harm principle because it define the real gaps in the market and avoid projects that have no real potential in the market, it studies the competition in the market, and the bottle-neack in the value chain that can cause lack of resources or create more supplies than the demand in the market. PVCA, PRA, and PMA are going to guide the secio-economic intervention to avoid the projects that can harm the whole value chain.
- Creating referral system with clear selection criteria based on the priorities of the community to avoid creating competition among the workers by studying the wages scale that can not disturb the wages in the future.
Grant programing is taking in consideration the social cohesion among IDPs and HCs to avoid creating any perception of unjust distribution of grants, giving fund to a certain group (e.g. IDPs, as they lost their assets after displacement) this can cause more harmful reactions from HCs who has the perception that IDPs are taking their opportunities.

4 - Needs

Many Syrian citizens strive to stay alive after their whole life was destroyed due to the conflict for nearly ten years (HNO 2021 ) Millions of people are exhausted from adaptation strategy. Nearly 69% of citizens suffer from poverty and nearly 35% suffer from extreme poverty characterized by severe deprivation from foodstuff required for survival. The potential of having essential services stopped almost completely, including medical care, clean water and education. Some families resort to unsustainable and unsafe means to survive, including compulsory and/or early marriage, child labor and child soldiering to survive. On the other hand, increase of prices and decrease of currency value in 2020 obliged Syrian families to struggle to earn their living as well as the Unavailability of fuel for heating ....etc. Earn living opportunities decreased more than before; also loss of income affected millions of dependents. Nearly 85% of the Syrian citizens are below poverty line, while 69% of them suffer from poverty as 35% of them lives in extreme poverty. Those who suffer from extreme poverty are not able to afford essential needs for their families. It is likely that, family income may confront more pressure especially after the rise of fuel prices. This reality has created the need for operational solutions instead of temporary rescue solutions. Those solutions proved to be less effective over the long run, in addition to its negative psychological effects on the affected people desires to work and secure their life. Women, Men are willing to improve their skills to find new sources to secure their lives expenses, while no bodies supporting them.
