1 - Needs

The decade of conflict has had a devastating humanitarianconsequence in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states, however, in2023, the security incidence and fatalities has improvedcompared to the peak of the conflict in 2014/15, though, conflictand violence continue unabated In some part of the region.Therefore, humanitarian needs remain high. With the stateGovernment commitment to close all camps in the state by 2024,the closure may rush internally displaced persons (IDPs) back toareas not fully stabilized. Without adequate planning, thesereturnees risk facing a shortage of resources and potentialsecurity threats, further exposing them to more risks. As seen in2022, many of the relocated IPDs became homeless, scatteredacross the host communities and limited access to essentialservices. In terms of food security in BAY states, the October2024 CH analysis indicates that an estimated 4 million people inare food insecurity during the lean season and need assistance toavoid resorting to negative coping strategies.The currentpopulations in Emergency (Phase 4) residents in the BAY isestimated as 191,839 (20.9%) (CH October 2024). The increase inthe number of vulnerable population is driven by the lingeringinsecurity which is driving increases in displacement andlivelihood disruptions in food 4 producing communities. Theabnormal spikes in food prices and high inflation rate, spurred bythe removal of fuel subsidy and its resultant effect on cost oftransport and overall business operations; continues to directlyand indirectly impact negatively on the outcomes of stable FNI.The lingering impact of Russia/Ukraine war on the prices of farminputs (fertilizers and agrochemicals) is also incapacitating morefarmers from fully utilizing available cropland for food production.Considering the above key drivers of food insecurity, an estimated5million people will face crises and emergency (IPC 3 and 5) levelof food insecurity in the critical lean season of June- August2025. NSAG activities and natural disasters like flood and droughthave increased displacements and impacted food production inthese LGAs with the majority struggling to meet their food needsdue to inability to access lands. The situation is furthercompounded by excessive flood witnessed in Sep 2024 whichdestroyed crops, farmlands and limiting volume of foodproduced. Galloping food inflation (37.52% in Aug 2024) has alsoimpacted the purchasing power of vulnerable IDPs, returnees andhost community members, who are unable to access food andother essential items in the market and leaving them at the mercyof humanitarian assistance. Pregnant, Breastfeeding Mothersand U2 children are particularly affected as they face morevulnerabilities. These populations need food assistance or facethe risk of further deteriorating to worse levels of food insecurityand engaging in negative coping strategies. The reasons behindthese needs include factors like poverty, lack of income becauseof insecurity leading to limited access to land for cultivation, anddisplacement among other challenges. These have limitedopportunities for HHs to engage in meaningful livelihoods suchas agriculture, which is the main source of livelihood for thesepopulations. According to the NBS, the Nigeria’s inflation raterose to over 28 years higher in June 2024 (July 2024, newsgroupreport).

2 - Activities

YOBE STATE: ACF proposes to reach 600HHs (12,000 individuals) cash transfer month which is tailored to the National and State Cash Transfer Programme transfer value implemented by the National Cash Transfer Office and the National Social Safety-Nets Programme implemented by the Federal government. The following proposed breakdown of target beneficiaries across the yobe: Nguru 300 HHs (6000 individuals) and Machina: 300HHs(6000 individuals) LGAs of Yobe state. These vulnerable households shall also be reached with IGA(50), livestock cash grant (50) and 50 husbands of the PBW will receive agricultural cash support. BORNO STATE: Action against hunger proposes to reach 10000 households (600000 individuals) IDPS and very vulnerable host community members in Borno state with 3 roundof food assistance during the lean season with the following proposed breakdown: Gubio (3000) and Magumeri (7000).Meanwhile, 7,731HH (46,386 beneficiaries) will receive 3 rounds of off lean season MPCA. As anticipatory action, ACF plans to support 1000 newly displaced IDPs in Magumeri and Gubio with three (3) rounds of MPCA. In Mobbar LGA of Borno State, ACF will support 300 PBW with CVA to meet their food needs, during and off lean season. In an effort the improve the resilience of conflict affected population in MMC, Jere and Konduga, ACF proposes to support 4800 IDPs and host community members with agricultural livelihood cash and trainings, livestock and livestock market support. ACF will also support vulnerable HHs with temporal employment through cash for work activities and the rehabilitation of valuable livelihood community assetsADAMAWA: ACF will support Agriculture/Livestock Markets and market System across Borno and Adamawa states through policy development, enhancement and training of value chain actors.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The proposed intervention shall take into considerationgender concerns by prioritizing the minimum standards ofprotection and gender to ensure referral of anyprotection/GBV concerns received through the FCRMmechanism. The collection of age and sex-segregated dataduring targeting and registration will ensure that the intendedbeneficiaries are included and supported with nodiscrimination on any basis. The project activities shall beshortened in duration to allow women to have time to care fortheir babies and engage in other productive work. Provisionshall be made for child/baby friendly spaces in locations andvenues of demo sites, meetings, trainings and distributionpoints. The distributions shall take place near to the locationsof the beneficiary homes to avoid having the PLW walkinglong distances. To ensure the safety of beneficiariesespecially women and girls, vendor locations shall be sited inareas considered to be safe by the community and separatelines for PWDs, elderly and most vulnerable PLW made forpriority service. To diffuse possible gender tensions, womenand men shall be represented during community meetingsand joint decision making on the assistance shall bepromoted gender sensitive way that empowers women without causingGBV and household tensions. ACF will ensure that allindicators that could have gender aspects will be age,disability and sex-disaggregated in data collection, analysis,and reporting. To ensure safe and accountable programming,ACF will ensure protection/GBV issues are well assessed,prevented and mitigated both among beneficiaries and amongstaff. Protection risks at the community level include possibletension between host population and IDPs. ACF projectactivities shall ensure that beneficiaries are not put at risk dueto movement of project beneficiaries from one location to theother. Targeting of beneficiaries will ensure strict inclusion ofonly households that are residents in the location ofintervention. Risk of community transmission of COVID-19may add to the vulnerabilities of people in the locations ofintervention. To mitigate this risk, ACF shall conductsensitization to beneficiaries and community stakeholders onthe pandemic and promote the use of COVID-19 protectionprotocols such as physical distancing, the use of masks,handwashing and sanitizers by beneficiaries, vendors, staffand stakeholders. The collection of age gender-segregateddata will ensure the inclusion of women, children, the elderlyand PLW and efforts will ensure that no discrimination on anybasis. Furthermore, the project will mainstream protectionthroughout the project cycle by ensuring that protectionprinciples guide the humanitarian assistance ACF is providing.This includes: ● Ensuring access for affected populations tohumanitarian assistance based on needs and promoting non-discriminatory principles (Action Against Hunger Charter)within the program, which is also explicitly outlined in theCode of Conduct, which is signed by all Action Against Hungerstaff. ● Monitoring access of the most vulnerable populationto humanitarian assistance. In the process of validating targetcriteria, the M&E team is encouraged to crosscheck lists andminimize exclusion errors. In addition, ACF will reportinternally when check points or blockades are preventinghumanitarian assistance. Special measures will be taken toensure equality of access for affected people in remote orinaccessible areas. ● ACF will reinforce the concept of “rightto receive humanitarian assistance” within its own staff andwhen communicating with stakeholders (NEMA, SEMA, etc.).● ACF will focus on IDPs, returnees and host communitiespromoting authorities to recognize and identify the vulnerability andneed.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACF will ensure that all formal and informal communicationsfrom persons of concern, both positive and negative, informprotection, assistance and solutions to programming, and thatcorrective action taken as appropriate. To achieve this, ACFwill: • Establish and maintain effective feedback systems(including comments, suggestions, and complaints), using avariety of communication channels that are accessible to allpersons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitiveand non-sensitive feedback; • Allocate human and financialresources to ensure that feedback from persons of concern issystematically collected, acknowledged, assessed, referred,and responded to in a timely, confidential, and effectivemanner; • Collaborate with partners in feedback referral andresponse processes (wherever appropriate). Action AgainstHunger including Food Security and Livelihood sector, plans andprograms. It also provides a range of rapid and accessiblechannels of communication with affected populations anduse them to inform affected populations about proceduresand processes that affect them so that they are able to makeinformed decisions and choices.
