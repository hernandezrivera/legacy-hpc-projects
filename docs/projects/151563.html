Project Summary

The repeated impact of climatic extremes, such as floods and droughts, has had profound direct and indirect effects on Somalia’s economy. Floods and droughts are among the most dangerous and costly of natural disasters. The project objective is to reduce flood and drought impacts through the use of FAO’s early warning information and foster a better understanding of the Juba and Shabelle river basins in Somalia through detailed river surveys that will see durable and sustainable measures to reduce frequent occurrence of floods. The project will aim to scale up the use of modernized early warning systems and climate information to save lives and enhance livelihoods in vulnerable communities through expansion of hydro-meteorological monitoring network that will generate data which will be used in turn in the production of early warning information for early action. To sustain this, FAO will strengthen institutional and indigenous capacities for use of Early Warning Systems (EWS) and Climate Information (CI) in preparedness for response to climate related disasters. The project will also undertake river survey and morphological study for sustainable flood risk management of the Juba and Shabelle river basins. The study results will be used to build institutional and indigenous capacities to mitigate flood risks along the two rivers.

Needs

Somalia has faced a number of climate-induced disasters whose frequency has increased in recent decades. These hazards have disrupted food production, leading to community displacement, loss of life and assets, bringing an overall reduction of community resilience. The country has faced seven major droughts, eight major floods, and three tropical cyclones impacting several livelihoods circa the last decade. The Juba and Shabelle rivers, which constitute key agricultural zones, are prone to recurrent floods. The 2015 El Nino floods and subsequent droughts of 2016 and 2017, led to severe food insecurity across the basins both inside Somalia and the Ethiopian highlands. Lack of management along the two rivers has undermined emergency and humanitarian interventions efforts to reduce flooding. Sedimentation, human activity and low capacity in river management has led to degradation, frequent floods and infrastructural damage. SWALIM has long collected climate data and provided services for Somalia together with disaster agencies in Puntland, Somaliland and Mogadishu, as well as line government ministries. The project has developed a rich hydro-meteorological database, the backbone for interventions in climate related disaster risk reduction. SWALIM has also developed flood and early warning systems for both floods and droughts. The data and existing systems can be used to support flood management activities that will see a halt to recurrent hazards especially along the two perennial rivers. This enabling programme proposal seeks to use the existing database and systems to scale up modernized early warning systems and climate data to support vulnerable people. The proposal further seeks to address flood risk management via the study of the morphology of the Juba and Shabelle rivers and continuous use of high resolution images to monitor them. This study will enable early determination and forecasting of risk prone areas to save communities and protect their assets.

Activities

1. Scaling up the use of modernized early warning systems and climate information to save lives and enhance livelihoods in vulnerable communities:i. Expansion of networks that generate climate-related data to save lives and safeguard livelihoods from extreme climate events,ii. Development and dissemination of products and platforms for climate related information/services to communities engaged in agriculture based livelihoods,iii. Strengthening institutional and indigenous capacities for use of Early Warning Systems (EWS) and Climate Information (CI) in preparedness for response to climate related disasters, 2. Undertake river survey and morphological study for sustainable flood risk management of the Juba and Shabelle river basins:i. Continue monitoring river breakages and flooded areas during the Gu and Deyr rains using satellite imagery and field verification,ii. Carry out a river morphological study to plan river training works, support flood management and flood forecasting along the two rivers,iii. Strengthening institutional and indigenous capacities to mitigate flood risks along the two rivers.Indicators1) Number of telemetric (online) monitoring stations supplied and installed in selected areas;2) Number of flood and drought-risk and early warning products (SMS alerts, bulletins and radio messages) developed, disseminated and used to inform vulnerable communities;3) Online Flood Risk and Response Information Management System (FRRIMS) enhanced; 4) Reduction of acreage and number of people vulnerable to flood risks along the Juba and Shabelle Rivers;5) Number of flood maps produced during the Gu and Deyr seasons;6) One report of river morphology for Juba and Shabelle Rivers produced and disseminated.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The data and information products developed by FAO during the project period will enhance the foundation for technical and institutional support to the Somali government in the water and disaster management sectors. With technical backstopping from SWALIM experts, the project will support government agencies in climate data collection as well as the production and dissemination of early warning information. The Government-selected disaster agencies (HADMA in Puntland, NERAD in Somaliland and SODMA in the FGS) will be the key implementers of this activity. Further, FAO will partner with the Ministry of Water and Energy Resources to undertake river surveys and a morphological study for sustainable flood risk management of the Juba and Shabelle river basins.The achievements of the project will not be sustained without strong institutions that can effectively make use of the SWALIM system. Building and enabling institutional resilience will therefore be the core component of the project under the two activities. The management, optimization and dissemination of information, in conjunction with an institutional development strategy, will require an integrated capacity-building program for line ministries, together with institutional strengthening, to sustainably operate and maintain the pertinent sector entirely. This would include provision of necessary hardware and software to the ministry data processing centers and integrated technical assistance.Engaging government institutions and building the capacity of its people will enhance durable solutions to frequent floods and droughts. The study is also expected to reveal the underlying problems of both frequent flooding and hydrological droughts in the recent past. This will contribute to identifying long lasting solutions to riverine flooding along the two Juba and Shabelle basins.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The ultimate beneficiaries of the SWALIM early warning information generated through this project are vulnerable communities. In times of floods there is a high risk of the vulnerable communities being excluded from receiving early warning information due to displacement. This project aims to scale up efforts to reduce disaster and climate risks. It also aims to strengthen the resilience and adaptive capacities of people at risk of displacement by investing in early warning for early action information systems and increasing institutional and indigenous capacities to mitigate flood risks along the Juba and Shabelle Rivers. Furthermore, the project will establish capacity for preparedness and community-based early warning systems in order to prepare for safe and timely evacuations if displacement becomes unavoidable, thus better safeguarding the protection of all displaced persons.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women and children are often the most affected during seasons of floods and droughts. The early warning information generated during this project will help local, humanitarian and government interventions to anticipate natural disasters such as these, benefitting the most vulnerable people, particularly those who are children and women. Furthermore, as part of this project, the participation of targeted women in workshops to promote early warning information will be encouraged.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Humanitarian access to some areas within Somalia is limited due to conflict and security threats. FAO is sensitive to the variable impacts of armed conflict and responds in a number of ways, including closely monitoring the security of local staff and partner organizations with guidance from the United Nations Department of Safety and Security (UNDSS) in Somalia. FAO’s team comprises of Somali staff familiar with security concerns and with strong networks in their respective areas. The local staff can access most field locations to supervise, give guidance to implementing partners and create community awareness. However, in areas with limited access, SWALIM, as part of this project, will scale up the use of modernized early warning systems and climate data. SWALIM’s use of Very High Resolution (VHR) river survey satellite images for example, will be emphasized in areas with limited access to ground truthing. Some are high-risk spots due to conflict, particularly along the Juba and Shabelle Rivers, therefore access is largely limited. FAO’s VHR imaging provides details in near real time without physical presence necessary at location. Incoming images, remotely acquired, are examined by in-house experts to analyze and generate customized information. Moreover, part of this project requires engaging local institutional and indigenous communities for information exchange. Bringing local interested parties in a given area together and equipping them with knowledge around causes of disasters, combined with a platform for dialogue, increases familiarity and common understanding. This exchange contributes to alleviating the impacts of local-level tensions, which can escalate to conflict. Considering SWALIM’s exposure to different security, situational and technical knowledge informs the ability to avoid areas whereby any project intervention can heighten insecurity, placing vulnerable communities at further risk.
