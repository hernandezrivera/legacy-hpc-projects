Project Summary

The 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that 16.6 million of people will be in need of WASH services in Syria. Nearly two-thirds of water treatment plants, half of all pumping stations, and one-third of water towers have been damaged due to the conflict as per the August 2022 Water Crisis response plan. In camps across Syria, the drinking water is still mainly provided by trucks with only 21% of all camps connected to water networks, leaving the majority of IDPs in camps dependent on costly and unsustainable supplies of safe drinking water. The proposed project aims at enhancing access to water, sanitation, and hygiene services for conflict-affected populations across Northern Syria, with emergency, life-saving WASH assistance mostly provided in camp settings, and longer-term, resilience focused assistance mostly provided off camp. Acted will provide a variety of assistance modalities toward this aim. Off-camp, Acted will rehabilitate, through contractors and in coordination with all relevant stakeholders (including WASH Cluster, WASH Working Group, and local councils and technical departments), numerous assets, including water pumping stations, water treatment stations, water towers, etc. Acted will also install new assets and/or facilities to fit existing assets, in areas where the need for water is highest. Acted will look to hand over these assets to relevant local stakeholders while providing operation and maintenance support, and capacity-building trainings, in order for WASH-related basic services to remain operational through these assets after the completion of the intervention. In Northwest Syria, Acted will conduct repairs and rehabilitation of WASH facilities in health centers. Acted will also provide emergency water trucking out-of-camp in Northeast Syria. In-camp, Acted will provide life-saving water supply, sanitation services including wastewater and latrine management, and solid waste management services, and water quality monitoring to several camp clusters in Northwest Syria (NWS), as well as Washokani and Areesheh camps and multiple informal settlements in Northeast Syria (NES). Additionally, to further enhance sanitation conditions and in effort to increase access to water Acted plans on piloting household-level grey wastewater treatment and recycling systems to support wastewater management on camp. Complementary to this assistance, Acted will provide hygiene promotion and awareness sessions to all targeted camp residents, and distribute WASH NFIs to highly vulnerable displaced persons. In total, Acted expects its activities to reach 875,348 individuals under the WASH sector in 2024. Acted will seek synergies with its -saving assistance off-camp, particularly water trucking. With regards to in-camp interventions, Acted will work through targeted communities in camp, notably through community-based structures, to build the sustainability of the WASH activities conducted. In NES, this include involving Water Committees from the resident population to participate in water quality monitoring, whilst informing camp residents on how to raise maintenance and water supply issues. In NWS, Acted is also evaluating the prospects of cost recovery mechanisms. The approach considers social and economic dimensions, including the enabling environment, willingness to pay, and financial considerations such as fixed and variable costs. Active participation in the Cost Recovery in WASH Infrastructure Technical Working Group at the WASH Cluster will further promote informed decisions are taken. All Acted staff are trained on the Code of Conduct and its 8 policies, including the PSEA and Child Protection Policy. As part of every training, the staff is trained on recognizing sexual exploitation and abuse situations and concrete steps to follow for reporting any concerns or suspicions.
This project will target 875,348 beneficiaries.
