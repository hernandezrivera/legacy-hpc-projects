Project Summary

The integration of children with disabilities in Education, requires a special approach facilitated by technical specialists, appropriate educational programs, individual assessments, and an educational plan that includes supporting services such as family guidance plan, community sensitizing and awareness raising, capacity building for the education personnel, infrastructure development, and activating the case management and referral system. Therefore, to achieve such integration of children with disabilities in the local formal and non-formal education, Sened focuses on children and youth with mild and moderate, mobility and mental, disabilities, through several levels as per the elements below:
The child with and without disability, the Family, the educational personnel, and the infrastructure through providing special education program and family guidance.
Sened's interventions will be implemented in an existing schools that provide formal and non- formal education to support children with and without disabilities with tailored activities which focus on removing the barriers and the negative attitudes along with raising the awareness of their families about their needs given that such steps need to be implemented gradually through the room of resources which provides Children with disabilities with initial assessments that identify the type and severity of their disabilities and their educational skills which contributes to including them in formal or non-formal classes while children without disabilities will be assessed after attending ASER test which measures their educational level as a prior step towards referring them to formal or non-formal classes along with training them on implementing joint activities with their peers with disabilities at school under close supervision of Sened's technical staff. The first step that Sened takes into consideration is identifying different types of barriers through conducting a needs assessment that covers the project's proposed areas.
Sened will implement the planned interventions through the following 8 components as follows:
1.Provision of formal & non-formal education service for Children with and without disabilities.
2.Provision of life skills.
3.Capacity development for Teachers and Education Personnel:
4. Rehabilitation of learning spaces.
The implementation of those components will take place by Sened's PSS & Special Education teams who will be trained on dealing with persons with different disabilities. Regarding the protection interventions, they will be provided through a protection project implemented by Sened in the same locations.
Sened will make sure to share the targeted beneficiaries through filling the EDU 4W on monthly basis.
The total target of those interventions is 1200 BNs.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2021, ninety-four per cent of all school-aged children are living in areas with severe, extreme or catastrophic education conditions mainly concentrated in Aleppo, Idleb and Rural Damascus Governorates, an increase to last year’s 86 per cent. Due to the compounding impact of the 2020 situation many students are either behind in their learning, finding it difficult to return to school or at risk of dropping out. Younger children, adolescents and children with disabilities face further challenges accessing learning services due to limited availability of Early Childhood Education (ECE) centres,300 secondary education services and vocational training opportunities. Across all learning levels, education facilities that can provide inclusive education for the estimated 15 per cent of school-aged children with disabilities remain extremely limited. The national self-reported basic education attendance rate during 2019-2020 academic year was 81 per cent with nearly all children (96 per cent) attending formal education and learning in classrooms (97 per cent).303 Self-reported attendance, however, does not capture the impact of school closures and other factors that impact student retention and learning. IDP children were less likely to attend school than resident children (with rates at 72 per cent and 84 per cent, respectively). Looking between governorates, Damascus, Hama, Homs, and Tartous had primary school attendance rates of 90 per cent or higher.

5 - Implementation of activities

SENED is proposing to directly implement Education interventions that aim to address the educational challenges as follows: 1- Provision of F/NF education service for Children with and without disabilities, both IDPs and host community. The target group will include children and youth with mild and moderate disabilities who are out of school and with learning difficulties. This service will be provided by conducting three courses: Literacy (Arabic), Numeracy, and accelerated education, children with mild and moderate disabilities will attend this course in the room of resources.2- Provision of life skills for children and youth with disabilities with opportunities to develop capacities and resources that help them deal with the emotional, social, and practical challenges that help them integrate into formal education system.3- Capacity development for Teachers and Education Personnel: the trainings will cover topics like: Inclusive education, dealing with CwDs at schools...etc. 4- Rehabilitation of learning spaces which is essential for increasing the access of CwDs.As part of Sened’s accountability mechanism, the CRM enable SENED’s stakeholders to safely raise a concern or make a complaint in respect of its work and to be assured that appropriate action is undertaken. The CRM channels have to do with complaint phone number, Sened’s FB page, complaint box...etc. Sened shares with beneficiaries a brochure that includes all details about the complaint mechanism.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Protection Risk/ThreatLearning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school.Mitigating MeasuresThreats and associated risks may be multiple and varied. They may be influenced by sex, age, geographic area, disability, time of day, time of year, events and other factors. These should be identified and to the extent possible measures taken to reduce the risks for each population group. Depending on the threats and risks, Sened will take the measures below :1. Reducing time/distance travelled by decentralizing learning facilities.2. Considering sunrise and sunset times when setting learning hours.3. Providing students, their caregivers and school personnel information on safer routes/times to travel.4. Providing students, their caregivers and school personnel information on what to do and what not to do if they face different types of threats.2.Protection Risk/ThreatTeachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students, students participate in bullying or act violently towards other students and/or to teachers.Mitigating MeasureLearning facilities should have an enforced code of conduct against all forms of violence and unacceptable conduct, including bullying....etc.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sened protects the privacy of beneficiaries and vulnerable groups by adapting and securing their data, adhering to the "Do-No-Harm" concept, and maintaining their dignity. Beneficiaries are informed on how to use effective feedback and complaint method. This mechanism comprises established reporting procedures and channels for sexual exploitation and abuse. The PSEA idea is also considered in the awareness-raising themes provided for children and adults in order to raise their awareness of their rights and safety, as well as the safety measures to report such misconduct.Risk: Affected populations and Sened staff, are placed at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, including harassment. Mitigating measures: Training will be provided toSened staff PSEA Policy and Code of Conduct. which would be provided by our specialized staff in protection to other staff in Sened 1. SEA by humanitarian workers male-dominated, gatekeepers, and potential other beneficiaries. 2. Risk of violence from beneficiaries among themselves/with humanitarian workers when huge onset occurring (crowd control)Likelihood: High. Impact: High in terms of reputation, trust. mitigation measures: 1. Establish a feedback mechanism 2. Recruit female staff and train them. 3. Train staff on code of conduct 4. All staff contracts to include PSEA 5. Community committee with equitable representation of women 6. Train staff on the Inter-Agency Coordination in Community-Based Complaint Mechanisms.
