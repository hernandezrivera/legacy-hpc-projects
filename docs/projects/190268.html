Project Summary

In Somali Region, over 3.5 million people are facing critical water shortage, 131 out of 448 boreholes are non-functional (29%) & an estimated 3.675 million people are facing food shortage due to failure of the past two consecutive rainy seasons. A total of 1,123,111 livestock died in 77 woredas of the region due to the current drought. According to the regional government report, 47,725 HHz are internally displaced in 37 woredas due to drought & others also displaced triggered by internal conflict. In March, the drought-affected schools are 432, out of this 137 are primary, & 226 ABE schools whereas 34 ABE & 35 primary Schools are partially closed in March 2022. A total of 130,830 students were affected by the drought. In March, 403 & 413 schools have no safe water & separate latrines respectively in 6 zones of the drought-affected areas. According to the regional health bureau report, a total of 23,467 SAM cases were reported between Jan to feb-2022 with monthly average of 11,736. On Feb-2022 total new SAM cases reported were 12,665 with the RR% of 97.9%. When compared both the previous month & last year of the same month the SAM admission trends increased 14.71% & 26.48% respectively. Due to poor Livestock body condition the availability of livestock product (milk) is very low & the price of milk were very high. There is no livestock disease outbreak reported in Liben zone. However, based on the assessment made in the zone, there are significant number of deaths of cattle & camel due to with unknown diseases & also other above normal endemic diseases are reported by the zone emergency task force on March 10, 2022. The shortage of water & pasture may further aggravate drought related livestock diseases such as Goat & Sheep diarrhea, CCPP, CBPP, pasteirolsis, Trypano & MCF. The project is planning to implement in Liben zone of Filtu Woreda, which is one of the zone highly affected by the drought & found the largest number of IDPs settlement sites. CISP is currently working recovery project in the zone & this project is design to complement the existing & try to bring sustainable durable solutions to the affected communities.
To improve the equitable access & quality of education, access to WASH & food security for emergency-affected people in Liben zone the project is targeted 47,969 individuals (24,608 w & 23,361 m or 39,008 host communities & 8,961 IDPs) living in the project target areas. The project is focused on three sectors, which constitute WASH (38%) % of the total budget, education 35% & agriculture (27%) as per the regional priority1. In the WASH sector the project targets 32,389 (14,196 w & 13,476m) people affected by the drought through rehabilitation of water supply systems, provision of water treatment chemicals, conduct trucking, provision of water tankers, hygiene & sanitation promotion & local capacity building activities. The education activities emphasize reopening of closed schools/back to school campaigns & promoting equitable, safe & inclusive education in the targeted areas. The project reinstitutes the damage classrooms, construction of semi-permanent learning spaces, provision combined desks, water facilities & segregated latrines by sex & CWD friendly. It also supports students through provision of scholastic materials, school feeding to needy children and sanitation pads adolescent girls. Totally, the project will support 8,982 students & teachers. In the agriculture sector, the project is planning to support the government planned vaccination campaigns against the prevailing livestock diseases in the area. The project also support communities that lost their livestock by restocking small ruminants & protecting their animal from the prevalence of diseases by supporting the local government to improve vet treatment services & vaccination campaigns & refresher capacity building to CAHWS & support vet equip’t. In the sector the project will address 6,598 HHs (12,315 people) directly

1. - Projects activities

Projects activities WASH: To address the above mentioned situation, the project will rehabilitate 5 non-functional water sources (1 borehole, 2 ponds, 2 underground water tanks/birka) in Mesajid, Biyoley, Dahyelo, Helebon & Kulay Kebeles & Filtu town. The solar powered borehole that can serve large number of people & use a source of water trucking. The main problems of the boreholes are damage of the electromechanical equipment (pumps & generators), damaged on the pipelines, distribution points & water reservoirs in Mesajid kebele. The Birkas will be used for water trucking dropping points & will harvest appropriate volume of water during rain as durable solution. The ponds will serve to lessen the livestock water shortages after rehabilitation. • Provision of 6 water tankers to serve water collection/drop off point for water trucking, • Conduct water trucking to serve 15,375 people with 3 trucks for 60 days until the next rainy season starts & the damaged water supply systems maintained, • Distribution of 950 Thousand sachets of water treatment chemical to 5937HHs to serve for 120 days, • Conduct community based hygiene & sanitation promotion, including safe disposal of animal Carcass that died due to the drought & in schools to address at least 23,765 people, • Constructing of 28 blocks (224 seats) of semi-permanent communal latrines for 4480 IDPs & the host communities that has no access to latrines & defecate in the bushes, • Provision of sanitary materials like soap to schools, health institutions & poor families in Filtu IDPs, Kualy site, • Community & local government capacity building activities. Education: Equitable emergency response learning space services will be provided both the IDPs & the Host communities, • Community-based gender sensitive & inclusive enrolment campaigns involving PTSAs members, kebeles & religious elders, • Conduct emergency school feeding program, • Undertake back to school campaign involving model women, PTSAs, community representatives & school leaders by house to house visits & discuss with drop out children’s families & the students, • Provision of scholastic materials to 4,384 children (at least half of them will be girls) of very vulnerable to students to continue their education, • Conduct school performance review meeting with stakeholders including teachers, PTSAs & community representatives participated, • Provide reusable sanitary pads & underwear to 734 adolescent girls, • Provision of school furniture & rehabilitate school buildings that have been damaged age & used as shelter by the IDPs during their arrivals in Bangile, Haji-Abdi & Quruglas primary schools in Filtu town, • Construct semi-permanent/temporary learning space 3 IDPs settlements that will provide improved learning space for 456 students of 222 boys & 234 girls, • Cleaning & Sanitation Materials items were distributed to 28 schools of ECW intervention woredas. Food security/Livestock • Livestock restocking for those lost their livestock assets due to drought (8 shoats per HH) to 600 poor HHs, • Provision of concentrated Livestock feed for 250HHs, • Support the government animal Vaccination campaign through logistical & perdiem support to host communities & IDPs in vouchers system, • Supply of veterinary treatment drugs & equipment to the government clinics, • Training to the government health workers on disease surveillance & reporting, • Capacity building & refreshment trainings to community animal health workers including provision of vet kits to CAHWs.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The project will be implemented by CISP directly, in collaboration with community and Woreda and Zone Education, Livestock and Water, Energy and Mineral offices. The regional bureau will provide continuous coordination and monitoring throughout the project implementation period. In addition, Liben Zone Water, Energy and Mineral Office will mobilize its experts and service rig for the installation of the pumps and jointly facilitate training of community operators. The education and Livestock development bureaus will be appraised the project and also responsible to monitor and coordinate interventions implementing in the region. Similarly, the Women and (Children) Social Affairs will monitor the participation of women, children and people living with disabilities. It will also make sure that the systems are children, women, and disabilities friendly. CISP will be also responsible for direct implementation of the project, supply project inputs, provision of logistic and overall coordination. The organization will provide all staff necessary for the implementation of envisaged activities. CISP works in the area in partnership with local and International Humanitarian Organizations implementing emergency response projects and during the implementation will be applied the usual principles of the of coordination, complementarily, synergy and harmonization with other development actors operating in the area. Intervention kebeles of this project have been systematically selected in such a way that there will not be duplication of resources/efforts rather complementing with their interventions, for example, the rehabilitation of the water systems will shorten the distance of water source not only for this project but also other partners (Save the Children and the government) working water trucking. CISP and the government are working on nutrition project in the operational areas for host communities and IDPs. CISP will also complement with its own similar projects implementing Liben zone for complementarity and liking the emergency with development. Hence, our intervention complements the already existing interventions, and no activities will overlap activities of other partners. Particularly, the project will integrate with nutrition projects supplying water to health centres. Basically, the major coordination mechanism in Ethiopia is the cluster system at different levels (Federal, Zone and Woreda). All the emergency interventions in the zone are coordinated by the zonal emergency task force led by zone administration while sector based clusters are coordinated by the zone respective sector offices (Water, education and livestock offices). CISP is an active member of the zone emergency taskforce as well as of the federal and regional WASH taskforces

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The project will consider the Feedback and Compliance Mechanism (FCM) as accountability for the affected populations (AAP) and the donor to deliver the project objectives to the best satisfaction of end beneficiaries/clients through client response programming approach (CRP). The CRP will systematically listen to the perspectives of our clients and use their feedback to make decisions and communicate and explain to our clients how their feedback has informed our actions. The mechanism involves people at every stage and has been developed in a participatory way with representatives from all stakeholder groups including the most vulnerable and excluded. This was done through involving the community in the process of assessments, program design, monitoring and evaluation and then set up complaints and response mechanisms including listening exercises; perceptions studies; community score cards; story-telling, mobile phone calls or text messaging (SMS), SOP and others. In case that a project receives complaints or feedback that fall outside our remit, we will have a system in place to refer the feedback onwards to someone who is in a position to deal with the issue (known as referral pathways). The mechanism will handle sensitive and non-sensitive complaints with the proper procedures to handle both types. The mechanism for handling complaints will also take into account specific contexts and safety to recognize potential dangers and risks to all stakeholders involved in each process, including staff, complainants, witnesses and others. The mechanisms will include ways to prevent harassment, injury or harm to those wishing to raise an issue or complaint. The system will ensure the confidentiality of program participants when managing their complaints. This will assure that any information given is restricted to a limited number of people on a need-to-know basis and is not disseminated more widely. Feedback and complaints will be handled objectively and with fairness. All users will be made aware of the procedures; so that they understand its purpose, have sufficient information on how to access it and understand how it works. It will also be accessible for all groups within the community, including the most vulnerable and those with disabilities; this will be done through multiple channels to ensure everyone can be reached. Staff will be trained to ensure that standards are met in receiving, processing, reviewing, ensure everyone can be reached. Staff will be trained to ensure that standards are met in receiving, processing, reviewing, responding and resolving complaints. The system will be guided by policy and procedures that set out the key characteristics of the mechanism, such as who is responsible for receiving and handling complaints, how feedback will be recorded, reviewed and actions communicated, who has access to what information. Response time. The policy or guidelines will also clearly articulate, or signpost the process for managing sensitive complaints. The FCM will also be complemented by post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to measure the clients' feedback and satisfaction on items distributed by the project
