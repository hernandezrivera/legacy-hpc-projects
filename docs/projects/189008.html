Project Summary


IMCs provide comprehensive, integrated primary and secondary health care services through static health facilities complemented by regular mobile outreach to increase and expand fair coverage and access, especially for displaced or hard-to-reach populations with intermittent periods of stability and weather-constrained access. Aligned with the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for South Sudan, IMC's health care interventions are designed to (1) Improve equitable access to lifesaving essential quality health care services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations to approximately 550,000 conflict-affected, displaced and vulnerable host population in Juba, Malakal, and Wau displacement camps, as well as in Canal/Pigi, Akoka, Balliet and Panyikang Counties (2) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by improving the capacity of the country's health system for early detection preparedness and response with COVID-pandemic and other disease outbreaks, (3) Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. IMC will continue to provide Primary and Secondary health care services integrated with Nutrition, SGBV and MHPSS across its 26 supported health facilities in the states of Central Equatoria (Juba IDP Camps, Khor Williang PHCC), Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau IDP camp), Jonglei (Canal/Pigi County), and Upper Nile (Malakal PoC and County, and Akoka, Balliet and Panyikang Counties). These services will include Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness (IMNCI), outpatient consultation for communicable and non-communicable diseases, endemic diseases, trauma, injuries, community-based education at the facility and in the community. Provide the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) at static health facilities and outreach; Provision of Sexual and Reproductive health services across Juba (IDP and Khor Williang health facilities), Malakal (PoC and Malakal Teaching Hospital), , Wau IDP, canal PIGI sites for vulnerable populations, especially women of reproductive age (15-49 years). IMC will focus on health facilities, Mobile outreach teams, and community-based intervention to ensure access, availability, and utilization of appropriate reproductive health services. Access to treatment and care of chronic disease for patients as well as trauma and injury related services will be provided in all IMC project proposed sites. Provision of comprehensive integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, including psychiatric emergency services for in- and outpatients will be done all static health facilities through skilled MHPSS Workers with the guidance of MHPSS Specialist. The facilities will also be supported with essential psychotropic drug supplies. IMC will provide Community health education and social-behavioral Change communication helps for disease prevention and health promotion activities,facilitate referrals of pregnant women to a health facility for antenatal, delivery and postnatal services.IMC currently provides advanced emergency obstetric and newborn care, trauma care, trauma surgery, and rehabilitation for major injuries and basic surgical care at the Operating Theatres. These OTs will serve as final referral points for general surgery and obstetric emergencies in the displacement camps and beyond, including the nearby counties/states. All of IMC's interventions will have a particular focus on vulnerable persons, such as women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women, children under 5, and people with particular needs and vulnerabilities, adolescents, people living with disabilities, survivors of sexual abuses, minorities, and marginalized groups and Adults, displaced people, and host communities.

1 - Needs

Access and use of health services remain limited in South Sudan. It is estimated that only 44% of the population in South Sudan live within reach of health facilities and have consistent access to primary care services. According to the 2022 HNO, insecurity is the main barrier to access health services in South Sudan and according to the multisectoral need assessment conducted by IMC, ACF and Care in December 2021, flood and long distance to access health services remain a challenge in Canal Pigi where IMC remains the only organization providing health services for the whole county. Still, there is a substantial health services gap in IMC operational areas including unavailability of some health services in remote area, limited referral options, poor drug storage facilities and shortage of essential medicine and basic equipment. These gaps have made it difficult to meet additional health needs posed by the influx of returnees and flood displacement. According to the IDSR data, malaria, acute respiratory tract infections, and diarrheal diseases among others, remain the leading causes of morbidity, which indicates highly prevalent communicable diseases are continued burden of the community and its productivity. Based on IMC Semi-Annual data (April 11, 2021-Sept 30, 2021), number of outpatients consultations increased by 17% mainly because of IDP influx from flood affected area and increased number of host communities seeking health services within IDP clinics in Malakal and Juba respectively. The morbidity attributed to common diseases, Malaria (31%), Diarhea (8%) and Acute Respiratory Tract Infection (27%) remains top needs for seeking healthcare in Malakal and Juba IDP camps. In addition, according to WHO Immunization data portal, South Sudan still report extremely low immunization coverage with 49% Penta 3 coverage. Canal Pigi (in Jonglei), Balliet and Panyikang (in Upper Nile) are part of location where low coverage is reported. In IDP camps, WASH situation is alarming following the downsizing of WASH activities due to budget cuts, which constitute public health concerns in IDP sites. MHPSS: Despite concerted efforts by the limited actors to provide MHPSS services in South Sudan, mental health conditions remain widespread and largely untreated in the country. A combination of factors that include the prevailing conflict situation and other socio-economic situations such as lack of employment opportunities, Covid 19, and climate-related issues such as flooding further worsen the situation for mental health in South Sudan. The World Health Organization has recently stated that in such cases, the percentage of people experiencing common mental health conditions can double to 22% while those with preexisting and severe mental health conditions remain vulnerable and still cannot access mental healthcare, it will not be doubtful that South Sudan is in such a situation. Additionally, the limited numbers for qualified human resources for mental health (South Sudan has only three psychiatrists for the entire population of 12 million) further worsens the situation (OCHA 27. March 2020 situation report). Additionally, based on IMC data, in 2021, IMC provided various MHPSS services, including clinical mental health consultations totaling 2,411 in the targeted sites with epilepsy reported up to 71% of the assessed cases, followed by psychosis at 18% and depression at 7%, which is a significant jump as compared to 2020 when 1,538 mental health consultations were conducted. All of IMC's interventions will address needs of vulnerable persons, such as women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women, children under 5, and people with particular needs and vulnerabilities, adolescent people living with disabilities, survivors of sexual abuses, minorities, and marginalized groups and Adults, displaced people, and host communities.

2 - Project activities

Through static and mobile teams, IMC’s trained health workers will provide health care services which will address the following core areas of health response: communicable diseases, reproductive health, non-communicable diseases, emergency preparedness and response, mental health and psychosocial support, and community health activities.Curative and Prevention of communicable diseases: IMC will provide outpatient services in all IMC-supported health facilities and mobile outreach to treat all communicable diseases, especially the most common leading communicable diseases, including Malaria, diarrhea, and acute respiratory infections. Using the Integrated disease Surveillances (IDSR) IMC will closely monitor any public health disease outbreaks through community outreach. Data collected will be analyzed and shared with MOH, WHO and the health cluster on a weekly basis and IMC team will participate in early investigation and response of any potential outbreak. Non-Communicable Diseases: IMC will carry out consultations for NCDs, prepositioning and distribution of essential medicines for NCDs in accordance with MOH clinical guidelines, training of healthcare providers on the prevention and management of NCDs as per national guidelines,24/7 clinical care for people with priority mental, neurological and substance use disorders in the static health facilities, and referrals of MMUs to health facilities, as well as provision of consultations and care for trauma-related injuries. Sexual and Reproductive Health: Provision of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) of life-saving sexual and reproductive health services, including Antenatal Care, BeMONC, CEmONC, health facility delivery, postnatal care services, family planning services, and STI prevention/protection and management; upgrading of maternity and operating theatres for CEMONC interventions at Khor Williang PHCC; distribution of clean delivery kits to all pregnant women in their third trimesters during mobile units outreaches; provision of CMR services; prepositioning of PEP Kits; provision of FP services with special focus on family planning counselling, and reproductive health outreach through CHWs.MHPSS: Provision of integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, including psychiatric emergency services for both in- and outpatients in all static health facilities through skilled MHPSS Workers IMC’s MHPSS workers trained on mhGAP-HIG will conduct regular field visits to follow up on all cases treated in facilities to ensure quality of case management. IMC and MoH clinical staff will be trained on selected priority mental, neurological and substance use disorders, and will be provided with routine supportive supervisions. The facilities will also be supported with essential psychotropic drug supplies.Community Health Services: IMC will provide community health education and social behavioral change activities ,including training community health workers to deliver awareness raising on various health topics at community level. Emergency Preparedness and Response: IMC has an IDSR solid system on the ground to early identify any epidemic; IMC will increase the community health workers and also form an Emergency Response Rapid Team (ERRT), that can quickly respond to health emergency/potential outbreaks, including Cholera, AWD, Measles, COVID-19, Ebola, and conflict-related injuries/wounds, as well as to natural calamities, such as flooding. The Team will be recruited and will work for IMC's static health facilities when an emergency is not reported.The Team will be trained on Emergency Better Building Response Mechanism, Infection Prevention and Control, Case Investigation and Case Management. They will do initial investigations and treatment of suspected cases through sample collections, contact tracing and isolation, while coordinating with the health cluster and authorities to mobilize a full response.

3 - Project indicators

IMC will adopt the indicators set by the Health Cluster and as appropriate in this proposed project. To measure the achievement of the expected outcomes and ensure high program quality, IMC has developed a robust system for monitoring, including setting up patient tracking systems within health facilities, registering daily community health activities, and conducting routine quality control interviews with targeted beneficiaries. Impact-level indicators will inform more specifically the Change and extent to which high impact and high-quality essential medical services for vulnerable populations and community engagement services have become available as a result of the project. IMC will disaggregate and centralize monthly activity-level indicators and track output/outcome-level indicators. The project will use performance data gathered through the monitoring of activities implemented.IMC will ensure close monitoring and reporting of project implementation to assess progress and data quality, identify challenges during implementation, and arrange contingency measures, as appropriate. IMC will utilize a range of monitoring tools, including the Project Management Tool (PMT) to ensure that project activity are implemented as per the plan and resources are utilized efficiently. In addition, a MEAL Plan and an Indicator Performance Tracking Table (IPTT) will be developed to assess progress of indicators against targets monthly. The analysis from the IPTT will be shared to the program team and used to inform the extent of the achievement of results and objectives and improve project implementation. With the technical support and guidance from IMC's medical and MEAL team, the field team will be guided by detailed implementation plan to ensure timely execution of program activities and review.Health program gender and disability disaggregated data will be captured routinely using HMIS tools specific for various serviced delivery points, Disease surveillance data will be reported weekly to the health cluster and as defined in health cluster guidelines including the disability monitoring report mechanism. IMC has a well-established monitoring and evaluation system. At the field level, the Program Managers use the developed work plan that will guide the program's implementation. At Juba level, the Technical Coordinators, working closely with the M&E Coordinator, will provide technical support to the field teams on data collection, reporting and analysis. Using the existing reporting tools program training reports and supplies consumption reports will be shared internally and externally with the Health Cluster, MoH, donor and other stakeholders. The MEAL Coordinator will carry out quarterly monitoring visits to support Data Quality Assessments (DQA), to improve the data collected and used to assess program performance. The DQA process will include MEAL officers and program staff at the field. During the project quarterly review and planning meetings, findings will be discussed with program teams. During the monitoring visits, Program staff will continue to receive on-the-job training on collecting quality data and analyzing and using the information to produce positive changes during the implementation period. Data collected will be disaggregated with a particular focus on vulnerable persons, such as women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women, children under 5, and people with particular needs and vulnerabilities, adolescents, people living with disabilities, survivors of sexual abuses, minorities, and marginalized groups and Adults, displaced people, and host communities.IMC will ensure accountability to affected populations during the implementation of this project. Mechanisms such as community feedback and complaints handling systems will be in place to ensure accountability to the affected people.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Feedback and Complaints arising from the communities will be processed, analyzed and discussed during program management meetings. The meetings will develop responses and action plans to address the complaints and feedback raised. These responses will be provided through the AAP committees in each project location. In addition, the information will be keyed into a complaints and feedback response database for analysis and learning. During monthly/quarterly program review meetings, the information from the database will form part of the meeting reflections and will inform necessary programmatic corrections

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IMC will further strengthen existing CBFRM mechanisms that will ensure the most vulnerable members of the community are catered for. IMC ensures accountability to affected population is incorporated from the design phase by consulting affected communities, with equal representation from women, girls, PWDs, elderly, etc., on their specific needs and vulnerabilities, that should be adapted by the project. During project implementation, the project will use different channels, such as AAP monthly meetings, suggestion boxes, hotlines, house-to-house visits, etc., to inform affected communities on their rights and entitlements, and mechanisms available to them to share feedback and complaints. All feedback and complaints channels are determined as a result of community consultations, including FGDs and KII conducted with different groups within the communities. These groups, including vulnerable and marginalized populations, are asked about current practices, preferences, and barriers to receiving information, sharing feedback, and raising complaints. Whenever possible, information is shared through trusted pathways, with messages using simple and easily understood terms and language inaccessible media and formats. The project will also consult communities on their preferred feedback modalities and support the set-up of these channels where they are most functional. Feedback and complaints coming from the various channels will be analyzed, and patterns identified to inform program adaptation where necessary. In addition, regular community consultations will be conducted to ensure participation of communities in the ongoing implementation and MEAL activities. These will include assessing the level of satisfaction with services provided, unmet needs, identification of lessons learned and recommendations for project improvement.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IMC in South Sudan has developed country-specific guidance to protect Beneficiaries from exploitation and abuse in humanitarian crisis. This Guidance complements IMC’s global safeguarding policies. All IMC employees have to undergo training on PSEA and sign a pre-employment commitment against sexual exploitation and abuse, as well as report any suspected cases. IMC will conduct regular awareness raising sessions with the community on PSEA through QAAPCs and CHWs. The project team will incorporate PSEA messages into CBFRM activities, especially during monthly AAP meetings with the community. The project team will also ensure posting/distribution of IEC materials at strategic locations in the community, including how to report PSEA cases. Regular sessions at the health facilities and other services point will be done on a monthly basis. Community focal points will be empowered through training to be able to pass on key PSEA messages, jointly with IMC project teams. For sensitive cases that arise, communities will be sensitized to report to the field site manager/designated focal point. The field site manager is irresponsible to log in the complaints into IMC reporting system, Ethics point, for further investigation. Communities will be sensitized to report any sensitive cases directly to ethics point reporting systems for investigation. Ethics point contacts will be shared during community sensitizations.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IMC utilizes community-based feedback and response mechanisms (CBFRM) to solicit feedback and receive complaints. IMC’s CBFRM procedures provides guidance on: (1) effective community consultations – building upon community preferences on how to raise concerns and provide feedback; (2)major steps on managing feedback and complaints and how to explain our actions and decisions to the communities (including vulnerable and marginalized people); (3) Clear informed consent - IMC will provide all necessary information on how to manage feedback and complaint process, explain people’s rights and entitlements, as well as key steps that IMC will take to keep confidentiality of sensitive complaints, and (4) Routing and management of sensitive complaints, including SEA and safeguarding, including, protection measures and investigation pathways. CBFRM is aligned closely with the organization’s broader safeguarding and accountability policies; together, these procedures help ensure the systematic use and management of the feedback and complaint handling process across all field locations and programs. Building on the existing feedback and complaints mechanism, IMC will promote community participation on management and use of the feedback and complaints through community learning meetings, community Help desks, Post distribution Monitoring, AAP meetings and fi eld registers as a safe and user-friendly channel to collect, process and respond to the complaints according to IMC and community' preferences, as well as disseminate information about the project.During program implementation, IMC will continue to enhance these already existing community accountability structures in the interventions guided by IMC South Sudan CBFRM country local procedures. IMC will share relevant program information including information on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure meaningful participation. Regular information on the program performance and monitoring results, staff code of conduct and PSEA reporting lines staff will also be shared. Communities will be sensitized on existing AAP structures as a means of sharing complaints and feedback. During these sensitizations, communities will also be consulted on the most context-appropriate and safe avenue to share complaints and feedback. The information will be used to strengthen the CBFRM in the implementation sites. Further, the existing AAP committees will be trained in receiving, handling and responding to complaints and feedback on the project interventions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

- The current security status of all IDPs and POC camps is calm, however since Juba POC changed to IDPs, the frequency of fighting among youths inside the camp was increased; thus, IMC will work closely with Community Watch Group and SS police jointly with the camp community leaders. IMC will continue alertness on the security situation of health facilities and outreach sites in all project locations, in close coordination with the camp/community leaders, local and national authorities, community watch group, and UN agencies. IMC will also maintain its 24/7 security guards across all its supported health facilities, which also help assess the daily security situation together with IMC Site Managers and Security Director. IMC also has Security Risks Analysis (SRAs), Standard Operating Procedures (SoPs), and Contingency Plans in all its project locations, updated bi-annually or as the need arises.- There are separate waiting areas, wards, restrooms, and WASH facilities in IMC's health facilities for women, men, girls, and boys. The CHWs and community volunteers go around the facility to maintain orderliness in queues and ensure that no untoward incidents of abuse and exploitation may happen to anyone accessing the health facilities. Giant banners about PSEA are also posted in different corners of the health facilities and the community. CHWs also disseminate key messages on the importance of reporting PSEA cases, including where and how to report such issues.- To ensure a continuum of care and provide high-quality health services, IMC ensures that multi-sectoral referral pathways are in place in all its project locations, especially for survivors of protection incidents, a number of cases referred from facility to facility will be recorded and reported. Moreover, monthly safety audits are being conducted, which results are shared with and discussed with relevant national and local authorities and other NGOs to draw timely, actionable solutions and provide targeted support to the survivors.- To ensure readiness to respond to an infectious disease outbreak, including COVID-19, IMC will ensure training of health workers on infection prevention and control, disease monitoring, reporting and response process. IMC will also implement standard precaution of IPC in supported health facilities including hand washing, waste management system, prevention of needle stick injuries, distribution of PPEs and regular health facility environmental cleaning.- IMC will continue implementing all planned activities in full equity, without discrimination based on race, color, origin, religion, age, gender, or disability. Particular attention will be given to ensure that children, PLW, vulnerable women and girls, and GBV survivors access quality health and protection services. Full access and preventive activities will also integrate persons with disabilities, the elderly, and the youth. IMC is doing special adjustments to accommodate the special needs of PWDs and the elderly, such as doing house-to-house visits/consultations to them, ensuring that health facilities are accessible to patients with movement constraints and physical disabilities, supporting rehabilitation where necessary such as ramps and grab bars, among others.- IMC will further strengthen the accountability of the affected population (QAAP) committees and CBFRM modalities. All feedback collected from the community through CBFRM will be analyzed, and feedback will be provided timely. For issues raised to the 3rd party, IMC will officially communicate the community concerns that need attention. IMC will also use the monthly coordination meeting forum as an opportunity to share community concerns and address them in a coordinated way.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

IMC incorporates protection principles in its all its programs and its daily work by prioritizing the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries served. Consultations and other activities will take place in secure areas that are also easily accessible for groups who are extremely vulnerable and/or with limited mobility. IMC healthcare workers will be trained with a focus on gender-based violence prevention and survivor-centered treatment core concepts (clinical management of rape), considering the challenging environment that poses high barriers to vulnerable people, especially women and girls, in terms of reporting GBV cases, as well to counter the general lack of information regarding this topic. IMC’s multi-sectoral health, nutrition and protection project will integrate GBV medical and MHPSS services to survivors, including counselling, management and proper referral for survivors of GBV, including rape survivors. All staff will be trained on protection, GBV Core Concepts and safe referrals. Service mapping and medical referral protocols are in place across IMC’s health facilities and project locations to ensure meaningful and safe referrals, including ensuring patient information shared with third-party service providers is only done so after informed consent of the patient is secured. Furthermore, IMC provides integrated mental health and psychosocial support services, including psychiatric emergency services for both in- and out-patients in all its supported health facilities. Other PSS interventions that IMC provides include psychoeducation, support groups and peer support sessions for the most vulnerable groups, including community leaders, women, children, GBV survivors and PWDs of the displaced and conflict-affected population in the target sites. These are conducted through small group discussions, tea sessions and community outreaches with the aim of bringing vulnerable individuals and group of people together, create social opportunities for their families and care givers of persons with psychosocial disabilities, and the wider community structures, which are tailored to ensure promotion of psychosocial wellbeing is achieved and the human rights of socially disadvantaged vulnerable persons are promoted. In addition, IMC will implement/pilot Self-help -plus (SH+) in all its project locations. SH+ is a stress management intervention based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) principles, for use by persons and group of people experiencing psychosocial distress and other stress-related conditions.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Through an integrated and comprehensive health, nutrition, FSL, WASH and protection services delivery, and through working with partners, IMC will ensure to have early warning system in place to reduce the flood related impact. With the support of the stakeholders/partners IMC will carry out a more in-depth needs assessment on flood risks in the selected operational areas, establish and strengthen early warning system and train community members to apply Community Based Disaster Risk Reduction (CBDRR) and Climate Change adaptation (CCA) initiatives in affected communities. Further, through these initiatives in the target communities, each Boma will have an operational Community Disaster Response Team. The trained local facilitators will accompany the communities develop the risk maps, setting up of the early warning system, contingency plan, and awareness campaign. IMC will ensure improved awareness to contribute safe waste disposal and mitigate blockage of drainage system

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

IMC will adopt a multi-sectoral approach in services delivery in all its target locations, integrating health, nutrition, FSL, WASH and protection services. IMC directly implements nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive interventions, as well as strengthening the enabling environment for nutrition to address the immediate, underlying and basic causes of undernutrition through provision of both curative and preventive nutrition services. In areas/facilities where IMC does not implement Health programs, IMC will closely work with other partners on the ground to establish a well-coordinated and coherent referral systems. Through this, IMC will contribute to decreasing Morbidity and mortality related to diseases
