Project Summary

Building on MdM experience implementing lifesaving humanitarian interventions since 2015, MdM will target 50,424 beneficiaries through provision of multi-disciplinary quality healthcare services for the most vulnerable people affected by the crisis in Eastern Ukraine (Luhansk and Donetsk Oblast, GCA and NGCA). The project will particularly target vulnerable women and girls affected by on-going conflict by ensuring
access to Sexual and Reproductive Health services and access to GBV prevention and response services. The project will also contribute to address the high level of mental health problems among internally displaced persons and host populations in conflict affected areas.
In order to achieve this objective, MdM will target the most vulnerable communities and categories of people affected by the conflict (such as women and girls, elders, people with disabilities, people exposed to conflict -related stressors):
- The outreach approach in remote locations of Luhansk and Donetsk oblast close to the line of contact will ensure access to PHC, treatment and referral to specialized care. Outreach teams will promote a
comprehensive approach by providing PHC, MHPSS and SRH services and will be integrated within the existing health facilities (ambulatories /feldshers’points).
- The local health system will also be supported through targeted donations of supplies and equipment and health staff will be trained (with a specific focus on SRH and MHPSS) to face current lack of capacity building opportunities in conflict-affected Luhansk and Donetsk Oblast. The training plan will be developed in line with the ongoing Health Reform and by creating synergies with public training institutes and/or hospitals.
- Building up on MdM experience in the area MdM will continue supporting local capacities to provide a comprehensive package of services to survivors of Gender-Based Violence including the most vulnerable populations, and raising awareness on health and mental health consequences of GBV, promoting the development of a sustainable referral pathway.
- MdM mental health and psychosocial interventions based on evidence-based methodology will be expanded and disseminated to support the needs of persons who deal with psychological problems such as: stress, fear, feelings of helplessness, as well as practical readjustment challenges related to conflicts within families, issues with communities and livelihood difficulties.
MdM advocacy efforts will also continue to ensure the integration of MHPSS into PHC in the framework of the current Health Reform. In NGCA, in so-called Luhansk People Republic, where MdM has been registered since October 2017, MdM will continue to support PHC facilities close to the contact line, which have been particularly affected by the conflict. MdM will pursue with donations of life-saving medical equipment to
Emergency departments of hospitals with focus on Children’s health and Sexual and Reproductive Health. A new project of distribution of maternity and new-born kits initiated in 2019 will be expended.

1 - Needs

Despite the conflict being now in a protracted state with peaks of violence and daily engagements or artillery exchanges, humanitarian needs remain at a high level on areas bordering the LoC. - The lack of health care providers in areas close to the LoC (0 – 20 km) is a major factor reducing the availability of health care in these areas: there is a shortage of 40%of doctors in Luhansk and 25 % in Donetsk GCA (HC, 7/2019) - The conflict has increased distances and travel times to primary health and maternity facilities in particular in rural areas. Previously frequented healthcare facilities in Donetsk and Luhansk city area are now inaccessible to populations west of the LoC. In GCA, freedom of movement of patients and health care providers is continuously hindered by insecurity, and the lack and expense of proper means of transportation. - Inaccessibility and unaffordability of health care and medications lead to the worsening of the health conditions and increased acute health events, in particular for patients with chronic diseases. Lack of treatment is life threatening for patients suffering from diabetes, cancer, and cardio-vascular conditions, including hypertension, which is extremely common among people older than 60 years-old (>65% of beneficiaries of MdM outreach teams). - In the current setting of conflict and displacement, aggression, violence, disrupted support social networks and incessant exposure to danger contribute to psychological stress and mental health disorders and deteriorate social resilience. Psychological stress and mental health problems have also been associated with poor lifestyle and more-risky behaviours (i.e. smoking, drinking alcohol and consuming drugs). Older people are often socially isolated and also need psychosocial support. Stigma associated with mental disorders often deters people from seeking help. Those who do seek treatment may not know where to turn and may seek help from family doctors who do not have the knowledge and skills to help or take medications without consulting with a professional. According to a rapid health assessment (Nov 2018, MdM, Luhansk GCA), the most critical health-related issue is a lack or shortage in medicines, consumables and basic medical equipment at the PHC level. A number of PHC facilities is not functioning at full capacity due to brain-drain of health staff. The lack of effective ambulance services or home visits by health workers is associated with understaffing issue and financial burden of facilities. Access to secondary and tertiary health facilities for curative or diagnostic purposes are limited due to inability of patients to cover financial cost of long travel distance, immobility of some clients and lack of financial opportunities to cover specialized services. In the town of Stanytsa-Luhanska which daily hosts thousands of NGCA residents arriving for short visits, MdM assessed MHPSS services (March 2019) and revealed that the major psychosocial stressors impacting wellbeing include grief and loss, separation from family members and distress of anticipation of potential escalation of the conflict, which result in high consumption of alcohol, self-isolation and aggression. The population within the 5km zone is disproportionately female (57%), retired (36%) and elderly (33%); 67% of those aged 60+ are women, 36% have a chronic illness (REACH, 2018). Older people are in need of more health services due to health issues, but due to different reasons they have limited ability to use them. Through the existing programme MdM found out that 60+ with disabilities experience substantial barriers to accessing health care. These are physically inaccessible medical facilities; lack of appropriate transport to enable them to seek medical or rehabilitation services; untrained personnel and inadequate staffing; harmful practices, particularly in relation to persons with psychosocial disabilities; denial of treatment on grounds of disability.

2 - Activities & outcomes

In line with the needs identified by MdM teams and in line with the HRP, MdM intervention will ensure the continuous provision of emergency life-saving activities to answer the most urgent needs of conflict-affected population while reinforcing the local capacities to improve the access to PHC, SRHR, MHPSS services in Eastern Ukraine. / Considering age and gender profiles, access to healthcare is essential to save lives and reduce the use of negative coping strategies and exposure to risk among the local population. MdM will reach out to most vulnerable communities affected by the conflict via its community health outreach teams, composed of a doctor, a midwife, a nurse and a psychologist with PHC, SRH, and MHPSS services (around 20 remote locations in Popasna and Stanitsa-Luhanska districts, Luhansk GCA, and in around 12 locations in Bakhmut district, Donetsk GCA, within 20 km from the LoC). / Outcome 1. Increased access to quality PHC, including SRH and MHPSS services for confict-affected population in Donbass region, Eastern Ukraine (Donetsk obl GCA, Lugansk obl GCA and NGCA). A1: Deliver direct gender responsive healthcare services through community outreach, including primary healthcare, sexual and reproductive health and MHPSS services in communities with little or no access to healthcare, provide free-of charge medications and facilitate referrals for patients facing barriers in accessing relevant specialized healthcare services (Luhansk GCA, Donetsk GCA) A2: Implementation of gender-appropriate awareness sessions on PHC, SRH and MHPSS at the supported health facilities and communities close to the the LOC (Donetsk and Luhansk GCA) A3: Distribution of maternity and newborn kits targeting mothers and newborns residing close to the LoC in Luhansk NGCA / Outcome 2. Improved capacities of the communities and local health system in the provision of comprehensive healthcare services (including SRH and MHPSS) for the vulnerable population in conflict affected areas of Donbass Region (Donetsk and Lugansk GCA, Lugansk NGCA) A1: Capacity building and technical supervision of primary health care staff and of community outreach workers on PHC, SRH, MHPSS and GBV topics (incl. GBV Response Focal Points and community representatives who are conducting home visits to disabled/elderly persons) A2. Support health and social facilities affected by the conflict through donations of medical supplies and equipment (Luhansk GCA + NGCA, Donetsk GCA) A3: Strengthening the local health system on the implementation of the health reform A4: Provide community based MHPSS evidence-based services (Donetsk GCA, Luhansk GCA, NGCA) A5: Conduct rehabilitation works in community based health facilities in Donetsk GCA A6: Community-based MHPSS activities in locations near the LoC targeting the local population in Luhansk GCA and visitors from the NGCA / Outcome 3. The awareness of national and regional stakeholders is raised through advocacy and evidence-based communication A1: Organize advocacy events at national and local levels, to ensure that the implementation of the health reform is in line with the needs of conflict-affected population A2: Participate in national and regional working groups to engage and contribute to the coordination efforts in Donbass Region A3: Support advocacy activities to advocate for the needs in MHPSS, SRH, GBV related services and comprehensive healthcare services for conflict affected populations to be addressed through raising awareness and informing target audiences on these issues /MdM intends to intervene on a pure needs-based approach and to activly coordinate with actors in the area through participation in the Health cluster and in working groups such as MHPSS and GBV (Protection), to avoid duplication of activities and locations and to gain complementarity. Bilaterally MdM will assure coordination with international and national actors (incl. WHO, ICRC, URCS, PUI, MoH, regional DoH and others)
