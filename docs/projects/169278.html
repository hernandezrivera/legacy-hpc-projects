Project Summary

On August 4, a large explosion occurred in Beirut Port, claiming 180 lives, injuring over 6000 and causing widespread material damages. The cause of the blast is pending investigation but has been attributed to 2,750 tons of ammonium nitrate which was inadequately stored in port warehouses. A number of sectors of Greater Beirut were affected, some of which are home to vulnerable, poor, migrant workers or refugee communities. Homes and businesses, including restaurants, bars, and hotels have been lost. Buildings were also largely damaged and glass was shattered at a wide radius in the aftermath of the immense explosion. Up to 300,000 people could have lost their homes.
Following the explosion, primary and secondary data collection identified widespread multisector humanitarian needs, notably in terms of shelter (in the form of support to rehabilitation), food insecurity, livelihoods and health.
In terms of food security, in the first few weeks after the explosion, locals and NGOs have provided ready-made meals, snacks and food parcels. In addition, non-food items have also been distributed. However, in-kind distributions have now generally been discouraged as most markets are functioning. In addition, while food stocks are reported as relatively low, no food shortages should occur. The challenge will be at the household level where the ability to afford food may impact quantity and diversity of food consumption.
The explosion takes place while Lebanon faces a deep economic crisis and many people had already exhausted savings and have resorted to negative coping mechanisms such as decreasing their food intake, especially of expensive items, before the blast. Overall, coping capacities are low. For communities located near the site of the blast, the urgent need for cash assistance to access basic services was identified.

1 - Needs

HI’s Multipurpose cash assistance program will support vulnerable households access to the following basic needs identified:ACCESS TO FOOD Food security was a cause of serious concern before the crisis. Since October 2019, hyper price inflation has adversely affected access to food for many households. The World Bank estimates that up to one million people are living below the poverty line, and 45 per cent of the Lebanese population at risk of sliding into poverty. The risk for refugees, migrants and other non-Lebanese communities is even higher.The COVID-19 lockdown further aggravated the impact of the economic crisis. The impact of measures taken to limit the spread of COVID-19 almost completely halted economic activity, with a direct impact on the unemployment rate across Lebanon. The latest WFP survey on impact of Lebanon’s economic crisis and COVID-19 on livelihoods and food security revealed that food has become a major source of concern. This applies throughout Lebanon, including urban areas, with more than half of Lebanese respondents expressing over the past month their concern for possibly not having enough food to eat. Of the estimated 300,000 explosion-affected population, it is estimated that up to half of these (150,000 people) may require immediate assistance; figure to be confirmed in WFP’s rapid food security needs assessment (including nutrition). SHELTER AND NFI Lebanon’s economic crisis, which wiped out 70% of savings for many Lebanese, will make it very difficult for people to fund repairs to their homes following the Beirut port explosions. Prices of construction and repair materials such as wood, aluminium and steel for windows and doors, as well as glass are likely to increase due to higher demand and their potential scarcity on the market. Even those that find equipment and can afford to cover the expenses face strict cash withdrawal limits imposed by Lebanese banks. As a result, they might also have to wait before their houses are habitable again. The most vulnerable households might face prolonged displacement or live in hazardous conditions without basic services such as water and electricity, or privacy [ACAPS Secondary Data Review, 12 August 2020].

2 - Activities

HI activities that align with FA Food Security Strategic Objective 1: Expand ongoing cash-based assistance in the Beirut area for up to 150,000 beneficiaries affected by the explosions:Activity 1 - Socio economic vulnerability profiling and monitoring of households, which include persons with functional limitation and/or psychosocial distress, impacted by the explosionActivity 2 - Unrestricted multipurpose cash transfers for a period of three months
