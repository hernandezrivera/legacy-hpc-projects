Project Summary

The Project is in line with cluster objectives 2023 with the purpose to contribute to the reduction in malnutrition related mortality and morbidity, and improve access quality lifesaving emergency nutrition interventions for the most vulnerable populations targeting SAM and MAM U5 children including pregnant and lactating women affected by floods in Jur River County.
Mary Help Association is implementing nutrition intervention with improved GAM rate is at 13% which is below the critical threshold as per IPC 2016 guidance for thresholds using MUAC proxy GAM (PRA Jan 2022). However, there is low coverage of antenatal care (ANC) first and fourth visits as well as deliveries by skilled birth attendants (SBA) are extremely low with ANC 1 at 40% and ANC 4 at 30% while skilled delivery is less than 40%. The Child Health indicators associated with low immunization coverage which is extremely poor with Penta3 coverage of only 30% (Facility register 2021) which is attributed to barriers due to insecurity limiting mothers and children to access and utilize health care services. There is high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (60%), diarrhea (20%) and pneumonia (10%) in U 5 years and other diseases (10%) which have a direct negative impact on women and young children resulting in increase in acute malnutrition.
These statistics show the significant need to step up efforts on provision of essential emergency nutrition services for the next 12 months; therefore, treatment of SAM and MAM for children below five years as well as PLW continue to be one of key need for saving lives of most at risk.
This project targets will be 60% of the total caseloads, therefore, 4,867 (M= 2,336, F= 2,531) SAM cases, and 8,386 (M= 4,025, F= 4,362 MAM cases for children of 6-59 month of age respectively, MAM PLW cases 7,747, MIYCN 18,353, VAS 41,294 and Deworming 38235 (PIN 2023).

1 - Needs

Jur River County is among the counties in South Sudan that is severely affected by intercommunal conflict and the economic crisis. On top of that the delayed rain and followed by the flood, affected the cultivation and the crop.The county is inhabited by an extremely vulnerable and traumatized community majority being women, girls, boys, and elderly people who are physically and psychologically exposed to a multitude of stressors. The County is under IPC Phase 3 with People in Stressed (FSNMS round 24) and insecurity in the area is interrupting access to farm lands causing deterioration in food security. Farmers are facing challenges in crop production which have adversely affected their ability to access food from own crop production. The depletion of food stocks and insecurity have cause agricultural disruption leading to increase in malnutrition cases. People are less likely to be able to be engage in traditional livelihood activities, such as cultivation and cattle keeping, and coping capacity.MHA intends to increase services to Roc Rocdong Payams not having any nutrition services due to barrier to access since they are located near the rebel control area with no nutrition services due to lack of infrastructure at the nutrition sites that would require establishment. In addition, only limited quantities of supplies could be pre-positioned at nutrition sites, due to inadequate secure storage facilities, hence limited storage capacity, and risks of looting. Therefore, acute malnutrition is a life-threatening condition that requires urgent treatment where most families and communities have exhausted their resilience and nutrition related copying mechanisms resulting into girls and boys and women resorting to activities that put children and women at increased risk. If this is not supported, pregnant women will have adverse birth outcomes due to malnutrition. Mary Help Association is implementing nutrition intervention with improved GAM rate is at 13% which is below the critical threshold as per IPC 2016 guidance for thresholds using MUAC proxy GAM (PRA Jan 2022). Most households with pregnant and lactating women are food insecure which leads to adverse birth outcomes due to malnutrition. The above statistics can increase due to low coverage of antenatal care (ANC) first and fourth visits as well as deliveries by skilled birth attendants (SBA) are extremely low with ANC 1 at 40% and ANC 4 at 30% while skilled delivery is less than 40%. The Child Health indicators associated with low immunization coverage which is extremely very poor with Penta3 coverage of only 30% (Facility register 2021) which is attributed to barriers due to insecurity limiting mothers and children to access and utilize health care services at Mary Help Hospital. There is high burden of preventable diseases caused mainly by malaria (60%), diarrhea (20%) and pneumonia (10%) in U 5 years and other diseases (10%) which have a direct negative impact on women and young children resulting in increase in acute malnutrition. These statistics show the significant need to step up efforts on provision of essential emergency nutrition services for the next 12 month; therefore, treatment of SAM and MAM for children below five years as well as PLW including people with disabilities continues to be one of key need for saving lives of most at risk in Jur river county. This project targets 60% of the total caseloads, therefore, 4,077 (M=1,957, F=2,120) SAM cases, and 8,386 (M=4,025, F=4,362) MAM cases for children of 6-59 month of age respectively, MAM PLW cases 7,747, MIYCN 18,353, VAS 41,294 and Deworming 38,235 (PIN 2022). These targets are focusing on the vulnerable people among internally displaced persons, returnees and host communities with limited access to nutrition interventions. MHA will link the nutrition services with other sectors like Health, WASH, Education, FSL and Protection.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

MHA is in compliance with Code of conduct on PSEA that Prohibit sexual exploitation and sexual abuse (a) Sexual exploitation and sexual abuse constitute acts of serious misconduct and are therefore grounds for disciplinary measures, including summary dismissal; (b) Sexual activity with children (persons under the age of 18) is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally. Mistaken belief in the age of a child is not a defense; (c) Exchange of money, employment, goods or services for sex, including sexual favours or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior, is prohibited. This includes any exchange of assistance that is due to beneficiaries of assistance; (d) Sexual relationships between MHA staff and beneficiaries of assistance, since they are based on inherently unequal power dynamics, undermine the credibility and integrity of the work of the United Nations and are strongly discouraged; (e) Where a JDF staff member develops concerns or suspicions regarding sexual exploitation or sexual abuse by a fellow worker, whether in the same agency or not and whether or not within the MHA system, he or she must report such concerns via established reporting mechanisms. Other types of sexually exploitative or sexually abusive behaviour may be grounds for administrative action or disciplinary measures, including summary dismissal, pursuant to the JDF Staff Regulations and Rules.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

MHA will strengthen prevention strategies to avoid protection issues will be duly taken into consideration by ensuring that the mobile response is close to the population so that they are not exposed to protection incidents. Gender balance will be ensured during recruitment of personnel. Health and Nutrition workers will be trained on the clinical management of rape and protection issues concerning women and child abuse. MHA will ensure that services are targeting beneficiaries including men, women, boys and girls, and groups such as older persons, persons with disabilities, persons belonging to minority group. Referral pathways will be identified between communities, health facilities and safe houses for the clients facing gender-based violence. Appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can receive the information they need to make informed decisions, provide feedback on the humanitarian interventions, share concerns and submit complaints. The project will ensure that information dissemination includes, as minimum, accessible and timely information on cluster strategies, targeting criteria, geographical focus, services provided and entitlements, programmatic changes, and community feedback and complaints mechanisms; The project will translate messages into local languages and deliver them through context appropriate, protection sensitive methods and channels such as, face-to-face meetings, notice boards, radio, mobile phones, and public fora, community outreach workers, religious leaders, training and awareness-raising sessions, social groups and community centers; Ensure that visible and accessible feedback and complaints mechanisms are in place, tailored to the operational context and the preferences of communities, which can appropriately handle complaints like violations of the law (such as sexual exploitation and abuse),institutional policies / codes of conduct; and quality and appropriateness of humanitarian program
