Project Summary

Under the project, UN Women will continue implementing GBV response, risk-mitigation, and prevention in both the Rohingya refugee camps and host community, in line with Inter-Agency standards for GBV in emergency programming and the IASC Gender Equality Policy.
UN Women pursues a focus on the most vulnerable groups within the camp and host communities. This includes gender diverse persons (GDPs), elderly women, and women and girls with disabilities. Activities include tailored support and services, and partnerships with specialized organizations. UN Women will strengthen its support to GDPs, adding peer-to-peer outreach and psychosocial counseling to existing activities focused on awareness and resilience building. UN Women prioritizes women’s rights organizations and will work to build their capacities on the intersectional needs & vulnerabilities related to GBV.
As a response to GBV, UN Women and partners provide GBV survivors with case management support including psychosocial support, counselling services, first-aid, and referral pathways through its 8 Multi-Purpose Women’s Centres (MPWCs), 5 in camps and 3 in the host community. The survivors are also referred to other MPWC services such as livelihood skills development and vocational training to enhance self-reliance and resilience. The MPWCs provide integrated services to survivors, including feminist literacy, livelihood skills development and vocational training to enhance self-reliance and resilience (under the LSDS).
The MPWCs are in close coordination with critical stakeholders, including CiCs and the Protection Emergency Response Unit (PERU), and report to them the needs of affected women and girls identified during emergencies. In addition, a referral mechanism for GBV is in place at all the MPWCs. In addition to community outreach activities by MPWCs, UN Women’s 6 Gender Field Officers (GFOs) and 65 gender volunteers (30 women; 30 men; 5 gender diverse persons) communicate with Rohingya women, men, girls and boys on a daily basis, assess the situations, refer GBV survivors to available support and services, including MPWC services and report cases to the CiC.
In addition, UN Women strengthens the capacity of GBV response and prevention by relevant actors, especially APBN for gender-responsive policing through training, coaching, mentoring, and peer support. UN Women monitors the activities of the (10) Women and Children Help Desks (WCHDs) at police stations in camps to ensure the WCHDs provide rights-based, survivor-centered services to women and girls in alignment with relevant international guidelines and standards, , and as outlined in the Handbook on Gender-Responsive Police Services developed with contributions from UN Women in Cox’s Bazar.
For risk mitigation, UN Women conduct awareness-raising activities on GBV issues and gender equality through the MPWCs, GFOs, and gender volunteers for the MPWCs visitors and broader community members, increasing their capacities to respond to GBV incidents in their communities and to support women's access to essential GBV services.
Furthermore, UN Women implements the GBV prevention programme of social norm and behaviour change, to address the harmful and discriminatory norms and practices that perpetuate GBV. The programme aims at not only women but men, and camp leadership including CiC, Majhis, and religious leaders/Imams.
UN Women has already secured the funding from three donors. However, it aims to mobilize more resources to increase the coverage of community members and stakeholders targeted for awareness-raising and social norm change programme.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Gender-based violence remains a significant issue among Rohingya and host community women and girls, underpinned by regressive and patriarchal gender norms and reflecting increased insecurities in the refugee camps in 2023. Overcrowding, limited privacy, unsafe WASH facilities, the lack of lighting, disasters and emergencies including cyclones and fires, are also known to contribute to GBV in the camps, which is mostly perpetrated by intimate partners. Decreased funding, evident in food ration cuts, further fuel the increase in GBV, polygamy, child marriage, and human smuggling and trafficking. Furthermore, the absence among women, gender diverse people, and people with disability to voice and decision-making within camp management and community structures, contributes to their vulnerability. While seeking support from Mahjis and CiCs remains the preference among Rohingya refugees seeking external support on safety and security issues (UNDP and UN Women analysis 2023), these positions are male dominated and Mahjis and CICs lack understanding of GBV services. This presents a challenge for survivors to access rights-based services and highlights the need for specialized services and work with duty-bearers to ensure gender sensitization and appropriate referrals. J-MSNA findings and GBV-SS reporting revealed that only just over a fifth (21%) of sectors and stakeholders in the camps have been trained on GBV mainstreaming and risk mitigation. Alongside the need to strengthen related capacities, there remains the need to strengthen the leadership capacities of women’s rights organizations and women leaders to play a stronger role in the response, to align more strongly with global commitments and the commitment to strengthen the capacities of local NGOs, as per the localization agenda. Taking these findings into consideration, UN Women in 2024 will continue increasing women’s and girls’ access to critical GBV services such as psychosocial support, counselling, and referrals, and raise awareness and address social norms and behaviors among the community members and leaderships, and strengthen GBV response and prevention capacity of sectors and stakeholders in camps and the host community.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UN Women is a member of the GBV-SS and coordinates its GBV programming with the members of the GBV-SS to ensure facility rationalization and adequate coverage of GBV service points across camps. UN Women has planned its proposed activities in 2024 on the needs identified by the GBV-SS in terms of activities and coverage.The UN Women GBV project activities are aligned with the GBV-Sub Sector objectives and the four activity groups, covering case management provided through MPWCs; capacity building for those involved in GBV prevention and response (including gender-responsive policing); awareness raising and risk mitigation (including sensitization of CiCs and their staff through UN Women GFOs and the efforts of women's networks and groups); social and behavioral change (including by UN Women gender volunteers). Of note, UN Women is planning to establish one new MPWC in Teknaf/Paurashava, to ensure adequate coverage of services there. Women-led networks and the self-oranized groups of women in the camps implement GBV activities and play a critical and complimentary role, while not delivering case management, which is not within their capacity. In line with the GBV standards for emergencies, their role is critical.All of UN Women activities are premised on active participation of the affected community. The activities also place emphasis on the capacity building of women-led organizations as part of UN Women's agenda for localization in line with Grand Bargain commitments, and empowering the community-led system to ensure sustainability.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The programme will ensure the safety, dignity, and rights of the targeted women, girls and gender-diverse population. The programme will implement do-no-harm principle. All UN Women implementing partners are members of the PSEA Network and have PSEA and Child Safeguarding Focal Points. They also have permits from government authorities to operate in Cox's Bazar (whether in refugee camps or hot communities). The MPWCs provide protection services, such as case management inclusive of psychosocial counselling, awareness-raising sessions on reducing GBV, child marriage, polygamy, and women's economic empowerment and leadership activities at one-stop, and referrals to relevant services, including health. These services will reduce the impact of physical and psychological harm that arises from violence, coercion, deliberate deprivation, and other threats. Thus, the operation of the MPWCs mainstream protection. Furthermore, the programme will assist training participants in claiming their rights through awareness-raising on women’s rights and gender equality, to facilitate access to appropriate services in a safe manner. The MPWC ensures providing a safe and open space for women, and thus the access by men is restricted.In addition, UN Women has 6 Gender Field Officers (GFOs) with around 65 gender volunteers deployed in 6 camps who engage with their community members, assess the protection situation and refer GBV survivors in need of support to the MPWCs. UN Women plans to expand the coverage of other camps in 2024, based on the gap identified by the sector. The GFOs provide gender volunteers with capacity building training on humanitarian principles and gender equality to ensure appropriate response to identified cases of GBV. They play a critical role in the GBV referral pathways and ensure coordination with the GBV sub-sector focal points in the camps.

3.3 - Age & Disability

"Leaving no one behind", UN Women has been working with Organisations of Persons with Disabilities to promote human rights, gender equality, and non-discrimination against persons with disabilities. All UN Women staff, partners, Gender Field Officers, and gender volunteers receive training from OPDs on disability and inclusion. Through its localization agenda, UN Women works closely with local women-led and women's rights organizations by providing capacity-building training on humanitarian principles, humanitarian coordination, and service delivery. UN Women works from an intersectional lens, including disability, to address their specific challenges. Some of the WLOs/WROs set a priority to target persons with disabilities and refer them to UN Women's interventions. UN Women ensures the safety and accessibility of people with disabilities to the protection and livelihood services provided at the MPWCs. The MPWC is called a "peace center" by the Rohingya women and girls, considering privacy, safety, fire, and weather resistance in terms of overall protection. The livelihood services set a specific target to reach at least 10% of refugee and host community women with disabilities or women with family members who have disabilities. Staff engaging with the services are trained on disability and inclusion before the service provision. Furthermore, UN Women ensures that a feedback and complaint mechanism system in place at the MPWCs is accessible for persons with disabilities, regardless of the type of disability, by providing various forms of complaint available.UN Women continue to support the WLOs/WROs that target persons with disabilities to participate in humanitarian coordination mechanisms and advocate for people with disabilities and intersectionality at the inter-agency levels, and establish a dedicated disability helper function within the MPWCs to support women and girls with disabilities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to women and girls is at the core of UN Women's humanitarian response. UN Women collaborates with communities to identify and address their specific challenges, emphasizing the meaningful involvement of crisis-affected women and girls in decision-making processes across the humanitarian program cycle, in alignment with the IASC Revised Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations and Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. UN Women emphasizes incorporating a gender analysis that considers intersecting identities throughout all phases of humanitarian programming. This approach aims to address those affected by the crisis, such as women and girls who may have diverse sexual orientations, gender identities, gender expressions and sex characteristics (SOGIESC), disabilities, and/ or migratory status. By preventing consequences that put women and girls at risk and addressing common gender biases in conflict analysis, UN Women provides a more accurate, comprehensive understanding of conflict's root causes, triggers, and drivers. UN Women's Gender Field Officers (GFOs) and gender volunteers across the camps are a conduit between communities, in particular women, girls, and gender diverse individuals, and the CiCs, camp management and wider response coordination. The GFOs and gender volunteers act as gender equality advocates; collecting feedback and complaints from their communities and addressing the issues through, understanding that the communities will feel comfortable and confident in sharing with them. This feedback is shared upwards as well into the response coordination through the GIHA WG. At the MPWCs, in addition, the feedback mechanism is in place with written and verbal forms available through the box or the hotline number.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UN Women prioritizes protecting individuals in all their diversity from sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) through robust governance frameworks and operations. With a survivor-centred approach aligned with the IASC’s core principles on SEA, it promotes compliance with humanitarian standards and advances for gender equality, women’s empowerment, and the protection of women’s rights in humanitarian action. UN Women is an active member of the PSEA network, Protection Sector, GBV Sub-Sector and GiHA Working Group and ensures that it is abreast of sector discussions and strategy development.All UN Women staff, partners, Gender Field Officers (GFOs) and 65 gender volunteers working in the camps and host community are trained on PSEA including reporting mechanism, and signed inter-agency code of conduct. UN Women PSEA focal point and reference points are linked to the inter-agency PSEA network, and the focal point information is displayed in the office as well as the MPWCs. During the field work, partners, GFOs, and volunteers closely assess the gender situation in the camps and host community, including PSEA, and report and escalate issues following the reporting lines. UN Women also established the PSEA feedback and complaint mechanisms at the MPWCs, available in written and verbal forms through the box or the hotline number, and ensures the client confidentiality and safety while processing the cases.Furthermore, UN Women strengthens the capacities of local women-led organizations and women’s rights organizations (WLOs/WROs) in humanitarian principles including PSEA, and promote their participation into humanitarian coordination spaces through the localization agenda. UN Women will continue to collaborate with the PSEA network coordinator in the provision of related trainings, and facilitate the active engagement of WLOs/WROs in the network.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UN Women strives for a resilient and empowered community, placing emphasis on vulnerable groups of women and girls in disaster-prone areas, conducting capacity-building at the community level to strengthen disaster preparedness through community volunteers and local women’s organizations. UN Women plans to renovate the existing MPWCs and Women’s Market, which function as cyclone shelters for women, children and the elderly, to be more fire and weather resistant, enhancing durability and accessibility to enhance access among persons with disability and the elderly to shelters in times of need. In concurrence of offering protection services, the MPWCs will provide 250 Rohingya refugee women and girls (50% women; 50% adolescent girls) and 300 Bangladeshi women and adolescent girls (50% women; 50% adolescent girls) with capacity building training on disaster risk management. In the Women’s Market, furthermore, solar panels and their installation, electricity connectivity, disaster preparedness equipment, and material relief items will be procured to enhance availability of much needed items for emergency relief and preparedness. UN Women also works on capacity building at community-level to strengthen disaster preparedness through community volunteers and local women’s organizations. Existing community volunteers (gender volunteers and GBV response volunteers) will be supported to develop skills in disaster and emergency preparedness and response.
