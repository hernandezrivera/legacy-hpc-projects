Project Summary

IOM operates a main hub in Maiduguri (Red Roof) and a total of additional seven7 field hubs in Gwoza, Bama, Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, Banki and Damasak in deep -field locations. Humanitarian hubs are providing humanitarian partners in Maiduguri and various deep field locations with safe and secure accommodation, office spaces, and support services enabling access of partners and delivery of services to the affected .population. The humanitarian base camp in Maiduguri currently accommodates 167 people at a time, and each of the seven deep field accommodates 15 people at a time. Under the proposed project, IOM will aim to support the maintenance of seven humanitarian hubs and one1 humanitarian base camp. IOM will also complete the establishment of the humanitarian hub in Rann, and initiate the establishment of two new humanitarian hubs in deep field locations (as directed by the Humanitarian Hub Task Force). Tented structures in Damasak, Banki and Monguno will also be upgraded to concrete structures, and the capacity of the humanitarian hub in Banki will be increased to 100 beds.

Needs

The North-East region of Nigeria experienced violence perpetrated by non-state armed groups (NSAG) since 2015 resulting to massive displacement of residents who fled from the areas affected by the violence. Several humanitarian agencies responded to the resulting humanitarian crisis but were met with several challenges. One of the major challenges faced by the humanitarian agencies was the availability of safe accommodation and office facilities for their teams. In response to the need, the International Humanitarian Partnership (IHP) through the Swedish Civil Contingencies Agency (MSB) partnered with IOM in setting up humanitarian hubs in Maiduguri and in newly accessible areas. In 30 April 2018, MSB ended their deployment in Borno State and handed over the completion of the building of the remaining hubs and operation of all the humanitarian hubs to IOM. As of 30 September, IOM operates a main hub in Maiduguri (Red Roof) and a total of seven field hubs in Gwoza, Bama, Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, Banki and Damasak in deep field locations. The Humanitarian Hubs Task Force also identified Gulak, Madagali in Adamawa State as another possible site where a humanitarian hub may be built. The site proposed by the Madagali Local Government Authority (LGA) was jointly assessed and endorsed by the Humanitarian Hubs Task Force and the United Nations Department of Security and Safety (UNDSS), Maiduguri. Red Roof has a total bed capacity of 167, while the field hubs each have capacity of 15 beds. In addition to providing accommodation, all hubs provide office spaces and meeting/training facilities for humanitarian agencies. In collaboration with the Emergency Telecommunication Sector (ETS), the field hubs also provide internet connectivity to humanitarian workers who are not residents of the field hubs. This collaboration however will be effective only until the end of 2018. ETS will handover to IOM, the operation and management of the internet connectivity in both Red Roof and the field hubs by January 2019. Despite the establishment of the seven deep field hubs, the continued insecurity in these areas continues to pose major challenge to humanitarian presence. The current design of the field hubs allow each field hub to accommodate up to 15 persons for a maximum of three nights. This capacity limitation has forced some humanitarian workers who need to be in the field for longer periods to seek other accommodation options, most of which are not complaint with UN Minimum Operating Security Standards (MOSS) and Minimum Operating Residential Security Standards (MORSS). The current funding for the Humanitarian Hubs Project is ending in 31 January 2019. IOM has embarked on a cost-recovery mechanism to raise funds through the revenues that would be collected from the operation of the hubs to help fund their continued operation in 2019. The cost-recovery mechanism includes rationalization of the staffing and operating costs to bring down the staff and office cost of the project to the minimum. Despite the cost saving measures included in the cost-recovery mechanism however, IOM expects to generate roughly 60% of the projected operating cost of the hubs for 2019. Additional funding is needed to cover the gap in operating costs and cover the establishment and maintenance of new hubs.

Activities

• Complete the construction of the humanitarian hub in Rann.• Initiate the construction of a humanitarian hub in Madagali. • Initiate the construction of one additional hub, as directed by the humanitarian hub task force• Complete security assessments, in collaboration with UNDSS, for the hubs in Gwoza, Ngala and Dikwa to increase the maximum number of nights the hubs may accommodate humanitarian workers from 3 to 10.• Convert the tented structures in the hubs in Damasak, Banki and Monguno to concrete structures.• Manage twelve humanitarian hubs including the provision of accommodation, food, internet and office services• Increase the total occupancy capacity of Banki Hub to 100 bed spaces.• Put in place the cost-recovery mechanism for the humanitarian hub project
