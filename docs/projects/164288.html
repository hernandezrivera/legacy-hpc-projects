Project Summary

UNRWA estimates that 418,000 Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance in 2020. However, UNRWA will be working towards targeting its assistance towards the most vulnerable Palestine refugees. The Agency will be providing NFIs (namely sleeping mats, mattresses and blankets, kitchen sets, and jerry cans) to 30,000 Palestine refugees. Those affected by acute emergencies, including newly displaced persons and those directly impacted by new or increased hostilities will also be offered tarpaulin sheets. Families and individuals spontaneously returning to newly accessible areas that were severely affected by the conﬂict and/or lacking basic services will also be eligible for in-kind support based on needs. The Agency will also create a contingency stock for 2,500 families in need that includes all main NFIs distributed by UNRWA. The Agency will also launch pilot initiatives in selected locations to support the most vulnerable refugees whose shelters have been damaged as a result of the conﬂict. Different implementation modalities will be explored, including a self-help approach used in other UNRWA ﬁelds of operations, directly involving the families whose shelters are being rehabilitated.

4 - Needs

As of the end of 2019, an estimated 40 per cent of the 438,000 Palestine refugees estimated to be in Syria continue to be displaced, with Yarmouk, Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps, largely destroyed by the hostilities. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns in 2020 will increase. According to UNRWA’s observations, many of those who have returned are suffering a secondary displacement, often forcing them to move in with extended family. The lack of privacy and limited available resources has a particularly negatively effect on women and girls. Many are also being forced to rent shelters, which creates a huge financial burden on an already poor and vulnerable population. The latest socio-economic assessment of living conditions conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in absolute poverty. It also found that households headed by older persons, females, and persons with disabilities are some of the most vulnerable Palestine refugees and suffer from high unemployment rates and poverty. This aligns with HNO 2020’s findings: “among affected population, female-headed households, youth, elderly and people with functional difficulties have reduced access to basic services including shelter, as well as reduced livelihood opportunities and income that supports access to NFIs.” Deteriorating socioeconomic conditions further undermine household resilience and coping capacities. HNO 2020 referred to the unaffordability of many life-sustaining, basic items and mentioned that NFIs continue to be essential and life-saving, particularly for those displaced or forced to relocate. Families' inability to meet those needs could force them to adopt harmful coping mechanisms, especially female-headed households and households with youth. To address this, UNRWA will be providing NFIs and shelter support to refugee IDPs, returnees, and those suffering from acute emergencies.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNRWA distributes in-kind NFIs in areas where access is restricted, acute emergency settings, and according to needs in newly accessible areas and to spontaneous returnees (based on need) reaching almost 30,000 individuals. Main NFIs distributed by UNRWA include blankets, mattresses, mats, tarpaulin, clothes, kitchen sets, Jerry cans, cleaning sets etc. UNRWA will launch pilot initiatives in selected locations to support the most vulnerable refugees whose shelters have been damaged because of the conﬂict.Supporting the rehabilitation of these shelters will contribute to protecting the most vulnerable while preserving their resilience and reducing dependency on aid handouts. Beneficiaries will be selected by the local community in consultation with UNRWA – Relief and Social Services Programme in addition to the Engineering Department. There will be a community-based monitoring for the rehabilitation of each household with support from UNRWA’s Engineering Department. A task force consisting of UNRWA's different sectors specialists will be formed to provide the strategic direction for the project. Cash will be transferred in tranches following the progress in the rehabilitation work. Out of 122,000 Palestine refugee families, only 1,500 families will be considered for piloting, which is only 1.2% of the total population. UNRWA also has a dedicated SFO based Programme Support Team. The Programme has a Monitoring and Evaluation system in place that closely follows up on and provides feedback and donor reports for all projects.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Beneficiaries face exploitation or abuse travelling to and from distribution points where they receive non-food items (NFI). o Likelihood: medium o Impact: medium Mitigating measures: - In order to minimize problems in accessing distribution points, UNRWA allows flexibility within the families as to who collects the assistance and a delegation system allows individuals of families to nominate a trusted person to collect the assistance on their behalf. Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of GBV and sexual harassment, particularly at locations with high number of beneficiaries. o Likelihood: medium o Impact: medium Mitigation Measures: - UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time. - UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming ensuring principles such as safety and dignity and access are incorporated - Risk: Explosive remnants of war (ERW) are recognized as a protection risk for Palestine refugees across the country, including when accessing UNWRA services. o Likelihood: low o Impact: high Mitigation Measures: - UNRWA has prioritized awareness raising and training to staff, community members, including children, on reducing exposure to risks related to ERW. - UNRWA sends text messages to beneficiaries to advise them on changes to services in order to minimize unnecessary movements.Risk : Increased risk of inter or intra-community tension due to varied assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. o Likelihood: High o Impact: mediumMitigation Measures: - Ensure beneficiary selection criteria is agreed upon with the government (GAPAR) and is clearly communicated widely within a community and amongst different groups. - Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community. - Establish complaint mechanism at community level. - Training to staff.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A mix of humanitarian and post-conﬂict interventions: Whilst Damascus, Rural Damascus and most of the major urban concentrations in Homs, Hama, Latakia, and Dera’a experienced relative calm throughout 2019, hostilities continued in other areas of the country and the overall situation remained tense and unstable. In 2020, the Agency anticipates that a mix of humanitarian and development approaches that are supportive of post-conﬂict rehabilitation will be needed to respond to the different needs ofPalestine refugees across Syria. High levels of humanitarian needs especially amongst the most vulnerable Palestine refugees: A The latest socio-economic assessment conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in poverty. Depreciation of the Syrian pound, rising inﬂation, increasing prices of basic commodities, destruction and damage of houses, and limited livelihood opportunities continue to undermine resilience and coping capacities, particularly for those most vulnerable. Increase in returns; a large proportion of Palestine refugees in Syria will remain displaced: the levels of displacement are foreseen to remain high among Palestine refugees, as access to Yarmouk is still restricted and the level of destruction there is signiﬁcant. Returns to Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps, which have sustained major destruction, remain slow, due to the level of damage to basic infrastructure and the lack of public services. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns from out of Syria in 2020 will increase, as will HLP and CD needs. Secondary displacements is also anticipated among Palestine refugees due to their legal status. Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to face serious protection challenges: It is anticipated that protection risks will persist and may intensify in newly accessible areas where spontaneous returns, both from within and outside of Syria, are expected to increase
