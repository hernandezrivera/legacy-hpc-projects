Project Summary

According to the 2024 humanitarian needs and response overview, the current conflict in Sudan has created massive displacement, with over 6.1 million people forced to leave their homes in search of safety. Of the 4.85 million people displaced internally, 54 percent are women and over half are children under 18 years of age .Sudan is among the top four countries in the world with the highest prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM), with an estimated 13.6 percent. The nutrition outlook is expected to deteriorate in 2024 due to ongoing conflict, food security decline, and compromised health and WASH services, and prolonged displacement.
Sudan is one of the top four hunger hotspots of highest concern globally with a third of the population soon becoming food insecure. Malnutrition rates among under-five children are among the highest in the world and are an increasing concern in all states for children and pregnant or lactating women, raising the risks of compromised immune systems, vulnerability to disease, and associated mortality.
A household survey conducted in El Fasher locality in North Darfur state in July 2023 revealed that 72% of the households struggled to access nutritious food for their consumption mainly due to lack of money and not having income sources and 93% of the households did not have diverse food intake (less than 4 food groups). Reasons given to explain difficulty in accessing food or difficulty in getting adequate and diverse diet included: lack of money (56%), insecurity due to the current conflict (28%), unavailable of food in the market (9%), and high food prices (7%). 69% of the households did not seek nutritional support or assistance for specific health conditions or deficiencies and 24% of household heads were not aware of the recommended breastfeeding practices for infants and young children. Malnutrition-related diagnosis mainly OTP/TSFP/SC accounted for only 6% among the different of diagnosis people sought at the health facilities.
RI will target and prioritize the 2024 nutrition response based on six areas: (i) Prioritizing the youngest and most vulnerable children with the highest mortality risk, (ii) Providing life-saving preventive and protective nutrition package, (iii) Treating SAM cases with medical complications, (iv) Treating SAM cases without medical complications among children under-five years, (v) Implementing non-food and food-based malnutrition prevention programs, and (iv) Generating evidence for planning, advocating for resources and decision-making. RI will improve access to nutrition care and services in 81 primary healthcare facilities (47 in ND, 12 in BN, 4 in Sennar, 4 in River Nile, 4 in Red Sea, and 10 in Al Jazirah). Through the proposed nutrition services, RI will build the capacity of 324 (M: 156 and F: 168) nutrition workers through formal and on-the-job training that will strengthen service delivery at both facility and community levels. Furthermore, RI will strengthen the referral mechanism and will ensure that complicated SAM have access to stabilization centers.
Through the proposed project, RI intends to reach 207,664 (M: 67,648 and F: 140,016) 7) and will provide quality nutrition lifesaving and recovery services to conflict- and natural disaster-affected IDPs and host communities in six localities in North Darfur (El Fasher - 52,539, Mellit - 19,225, El Tawisha – 7,886, Kalemindo– 3,094, Dar Al Salam – 5,757 and Al Malha – 27,575); four localities in Blue Nile (Al Rosaries - 5,620, Damazine - 4,794, Geissan – 5,720, Wad Almahi – 1,148); two localities in Al Jazirah (Wad Madani – 10,833 and South Al Jazirah – 11,176); two localities in Sennar (Sennar – 10,746 and Singa – 8,597), two localities in River Nile (Atbara – 5,970 and Shendi – 7,642) and two localities in Red Sea (Port Sudan – 10,985 and Suakin – 8,358).

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Gender Based Violence (GBV), a lack of AAP, and disability have detrimental effect on the nutritional status of under-five children and PLW most often contributing to the deterioration of the nutritional status as well as impacting their mental and psychosocial wellbeing. Moreover, persons with disabilities are at heightened risk of malnutrition and face barriers to accessing nutrition interventions. RI aims at promoting equitable access to nutrition services which ensures that the projects reach the most deprived members of the targeted communities. Nearly all the 2024 nutrition indicators are gender-disaggregated, and it is expected that over 50% of beneficiaries will be women. Special attention will be paid to mothers, female caregivers and PLWs during service provision. Privacy and confidentiality of beneficiaries will be respected and shall be ensured at all times of service delivery. For example, IYCF counselling and SAM cases consultation sessions with be done in a separate room to ensure privacy. RI will deploy nutrition workers of both genders who will be able to attend to the unique needs of both women and men in a comfortable, private and confidential way. The proposed project will aim at a 50:50 gender representation for community nutrition volunteers (CNV) who will be conducting active case finding to identify malnourished cases, absentee and defaulter follow up and home visits. RI will take a socio-inclusion approach and shall ensure that beneficiaries with special needs such as people with disabilities (PwD), persons with mental health challenges, those that are in hard-to-reach locations/ villages and those with special needs are able to access the needed nutrition services by working on both supply and demand sides. Service providers will be sensitized on rights and needs of PwDs/ those with special needs; and will be trained on inclusive nutrition services provision looking at the dimensions of gender, age, and abilities. RI will also promote the recruitment of inclusive teams, in particular for engaging with communities, and deliver awareness sessions on equality of rights, needs of PwD and promotion of equal access to nutrition services. Finally, RI will ensure to enforce zero tolerance for discrimination/ stigmatization and lack of respect towards PwD among staff.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

RI will organize face to face consultations with individuals (in their homes) through aid workers, face to face with individuals and community leaders (in other venues/locations) through aid workers and face to face and group consultations with aid workers in order to receive and give feedback. RI will support in the improvement of site-level services providers’ knowledge and awareness on AAP, GBV and disability through capacity building and referral pathways that will be organized in collaboration with the respective sectors. In addition, RI has set up other mechanisms such as a toll free hotline; email service and office drop in service for the community to provide feedback. RI has put up posters in all HFs showing the different CFMs which are public and available for reporting any cases of corruption, sexual exploitation, theft, quality of services, and theft. RI will also consider facilitation of gender segregated focus group discussions (FGD) for both men and women facilitated by staff of the same sex to collect feedback on project activities to continue improve service provisions.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Relief International has a standard operating procedure (SOP) that guides the proper implementation and management of the accountability to community principles including safeguarding issues such as PSEA and child abuse. RI is working to strengthen the accountability system to be able to capture the needs and voices of the program recipients at each stage of the project cycle management: need assessment, designing, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. RI has also rolled out its own Child Safeguarding Policy across the operation areas in order to reinforce existing policies such as Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) within RI and therefore increase and/or maintain a protective environment for beneficiaries including children attending all RI-supported services. All staff receives mandatory training on this policy. Adding to existing feedback and complaint mechanisms, RI also aims to strengthen its accountability system putting a mechanism which enables coordination with the inter-agency to facilitate learning on best practices, complementarity in complaint responses; and strengthening capacity of staff on management of complaints and feedbacks to inform project designing, improve service delivery, and, enhance program recipients participation in each state of the project management cycle, etc. As part of the existing accountability system, RI also documents community satisfaction on services which enable informed decisions on program design and adjustment, budget allocations, and future programming.
