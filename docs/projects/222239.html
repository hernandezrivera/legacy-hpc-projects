Project Summary


The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire, characterized by a protracted humanitarian crisis, widespread displacement, food insecurity, and limited access to basic services, including healthcare (UNOCHA 2023). The health system in Kapoeta East and Terekeka County is severely constrained due shortage of skilled health personal, flooding, intercommunal conflicts, lack of clean water leading to high morbidity and mortality.
The proposed project seeks to deliver a mix of static primary health care and mobile services to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for hard-to-reach populations. The interventions aim to improve access to low-cost, high-impact health services to the communities in Terekeka and Kapoeta Counties. The project will complement ongoing health interventions by impact health organization in these locations. IHO is currently the lead health partner implementing health system strengthening project in Terekeka County and also has HIV prevention project in Kapoeta East County. The project will provide lifesaving quality health service to 32,712 individuals (Boys 9999, girls 8160, men 6275, women 8278) with a budget of USD 985000
Impact health organisation will provide emergency lifesaving health interventions which will be integrated with nutrition, WASH and protection. Additionally, the intervention will focus on sexual gender-based violence (SGBV), clinical management of rape (CMR), mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS).
IHO will provide maternal and child health interventions, mainly skilled birth attendance, ante natal care, post-natal care, Sexual and Reproductive Health/ family planning service and Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI).
Impact Health organisation will ensure that people with disabilities and mental disorders have priority access to emergency health services.
The project seeks to decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from illnesses such as malaria, diarrhea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine-preventable illnesses like measles. This will be achieved through the management of illnesses including malaria RDT screening and treatment, as well as a measles vaccination campaign. The project will also provide access to life-saving essential healthcare, including mental health to women, men, girls, and boys, including ability-challenged persons.
To reduce the vulnerability of women, men, girls, and boys at risk of mortality, morbidity, and protection incidents/threats in priority areas, the project will undertake protection monitoring, advocacy, awareness raising, and risk mitigation.
To reduce the suffering of girls, boys, women, men, older persons, persons with disabilities, and other persons with specific needs at risk of, or who experienced violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect, including gender-based violence, the project will increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders, and the elderly.
IHO will strengthen disease surveillance by enhancing the Early Warning Alert and Response (EWAR) system, training healthcare workers in disease detection and reporting, improving data collection and analysis processes, and collaborating with local and national health authorities for timely outbreak investigation and response.
To strengthen the feedback mechanism, the project will consult the population groups targeted to receive assistance throughout the entire cycle of the response by establishing a community feedback mechanism. The project will strengthen coordination and contextual analysis of needs by working closely with the state ministry of health and county health departments.
These interventions will be in line with the South Sudan Ministry of Health’s policies and guidelines, namely basic package of health and nutrition services, Boma Health Initiative, sexual and reproductive health, immunization guidelines and health policy of the Republic of South Sudan.

1. Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains dire, characterized by a protracted humanitarian crisis, widespread displacement, food insecurity, and limited access to basic services, including healthcare (United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs , 2023). The country continues to face multiple emergencies such as disease outbreaks, floods, and conflict-related displacement. In 2024, an estimated 9 million people require humanitarian assistance, with 6.3 million in need health services (HNO 2024). Key health indicators remain alarmingly poor, with a maternal mortality ratio of 295 per 100,000 live births in 2017 (WHO, 2024) and an under-5 mortality rate ranging from 30 to 112 per 1,000 live births across different states (WHO, 2024).The food insecurity and malnutrition situation in South Sudan is deteriorating. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47% of the population analyzed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). This includes 1.71 million people facing critical levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and a further 41,000 people facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity or IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Acute malnutrition affects an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months, including 650,000 cases of severe acute malnutrition ( IPC published November 2024)IPC phase classification (December 2024- March 2025) puts Kapoeta East County in IPC 4 (Emergency), while Terekeka County in IPC 3 (Crisis) highlighting severe food insecurity and a critical humanitarian needs. Both counties face high prevalence of malaria, respiratory infections and diarrheal diseases. Additionally, frequent outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases, such as measles remain a concern due to low immunization rates (WHO South Sudan, 2023). Malnutrition is widespread, with children under five being the most affected (UNICEF South Sudan, 2022).The health system in the targeted counties is severely constrained due shortage of skilled health personal including midwives, leading to high maternal and neonatal complications. The counties have limited access to safe water and mostly rely on unprotected water sources, inadequate sanitation facilities contributing to waterborne diseases (South Sudan WASH Cluster 2023). Gender inequality and gender-based violence are pervasive, perpetuated by socio- clultural norms and intercommunal conflicts. Additionally, access to Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services remains insufficient in these in the targeted locations, leaving vulnerable groups without necessary care. (IHO Feb. 2024)Terekeka County faces additional challenges, including recurrent flooding and intercommunal conflicts which severely hinder access health services due to intercommunal conflicts and recurrent flooding. In September 2024, a rapid needs assessment ( IRNA) conducted by RRC and humanitarian partners reported that 44912 individuals were displaced by flooding, the most affected payams included Mangalla North, Bari- Arie, Nantiko, Buko, Wonki, Tukor, Lengi and Korshoba of Gemeiza . Flooding disrupted access to 4 out 0f 20 health facilities resulting to shortage of medical supplies and vaccines.In Kapoeta East County numerous cattle raiding and social cultural barriers have greatly disrupted health service delivery and limit uptake of available health services.The proposed project is seeks to deliver a mix of static primary health care and mobile/outreach services to increase and expand equitable coverage and access, especially for hard-to-reach populations. The interventions aim to improve access to low-cost, high-impact health services to the communities in Terekeka and Kapoeta Counties. The project will complement ongoing health interventions by impact health organization in these locations. IHO will provide quality health service to 32,712 individuals (boys 9999, girls 8160, Men 6275 and Women 8278)

2. Project activities

This intervention aims at improving equitable access to and utilization on health services through 4 static primary health care facilities and 4 mobile clinics. Impact Health Organization (IHO) plans to undertake the following key activities :1. Support reporting for communicable disease surveillance and Response Early Warning and Response. IHO will strengthen early Warning Alert and Response (EWAR) system in coordination with all stakeholders and ensure Outbreak investigation and early response.2. Establish and strengthen infection prevention and control in supported health care facilities. IHO will work to improve infection prevention and control (IPC) measures in 4 health facilities (2 PHCU and 2 PHCC) to ensure they meet WHO IPC minimum standards.3. Strengthen routine immunization and conduct EPI outreach in hard-to-reach areas. IHO will strengthen routine immunization services and carry out outreach to hard-to-reach areas through ensuring availability of vaccines and functional cold chain system reaching 1291(595M, 696F) children under one year with PENTA3. 4. Train and recruit community health workers. IHO will train and recruit community health workers to increase their density per 1000 people.5. Provision of psychosocial support services: IHO will provide mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services to those in need reaching 1296 individuals (540 M, 856 F) including those with disabilities.6. Provision of CMR services- IHO will training and equip health workers to provide CMR services to 5 survivors. Provision of rape kits and referrals to safe houses will be done.7. Provision of outpatient consultations. IHO will provide outpatient consultations 26,500 (11,540 M, 14960F) through mobile outreaches and static clinics.8. Conduct community consultative meetings to identify needs and participate in response. IHO will regularly engage communities to coordinate activities, share information, and address challenges. 8 community consultative meetings will be conducted.9. Provision of BeMONC services. IHO will encourage skilled birth deliveries through equipping BeMONC centers and training health workers. Antenatal and postnatal care services will as well be provided. The project will support 350 skilled birth deliveries.10. Provision of rehabilitation services for disabled: IHO will provide rehabilitation services to 1439 persons with disabilities.11. Establish community feedback mechanism through suggestion box, community consultative meetings and exit interviews.12. Distribute/pre-position medical supplies including interagency emergency health kits (IEHK0 to 4 health facilities and 4 mobile sites.13. Conduct reactive measles vaccination campaigns in response to measles outbreaks targeting children 6 months – 15 years. This is intended to reach 810 (395 M, 415 F)14. Conduct 24 community health events to promote behavior change among communities in the targeted counties.15. Conduct malnutrition screening for children under five years. IHO will carry out screening for children with SAM and MAM and conduct referrals to nutrition partners. This will ensure integration of Health and nutritional services.
