Project Summary

To address the wide range of multi-sectoral needs, SARD’s intervention is comprised of the following: emergency and life-sustaining food assistance, linked with livelihood restoration opportunities as an exit strategy as well as emergency MPC for newly displaced and emergency affected beneficiaries . The project will target 50,000 Individuals responding to 10,000 HHs and will take place in Aleppo ( within Jabal Saman and AZAZ districts ) and in Idleb ( Harim).
The project focuses on using e-card assistance to help beneficiaries address their immediate food and basic needs. It covres the food needs ( 70% of the SMEB) for up to 8 months for 20,000 individuals representing 4000 HHs, so that families are relieved with access to fresh food and specific items and have increased time and resources to begin rebuilding their livelihoods. The amount equivalent value of the e-voucher is based on the CWG Guidelines that is 100 $ per HH. It ensures that the beneficiaries have access to the wide variety of items in a safe and dignified manner.
The targeting strategy will focus on the following vulnerability criteria:
a) Female-Headed households.
b) Child-Headed households
c) Households headed by or including People with Disabilities (PWD), including Explosive Ordnance Victims (EOVs).
d) Households including Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) with children aged between 0-24 months. Due to the lack of Prenatal Health Care services in NWS, this criteria will place particular emphasis on households with infants aged between 0-24 months.
e) Households headed by a person living with underlying or chronic illness .
f) Households headed by older persons.
g) Newly displaced IDP households, defined as arriving in targeted settlements within the past six months.
Furthermore, to better rebuild their lives and secure the income needed to more consistently meet their basic needs, it caters for early recovery by starting income-generating activities in which the beneficiaries acquire skills to earn income, cash grant of 1200 $ will be given to 1,000 HHs, representing 5,000 individuals based on existing skills and capacities identified by SARD technical team .
Distribution of E-cards for repetitive food assistance and for IGA, SARD will use an electronic tracking system, making payments from Turkiye, to keep good management of funds for avoiding aid diversion, meeting compliance duties as per the audit standards. Each beneficiary will receive an e-card charged on a monthly basis and will receive the cash from the contracted financial service provider. The e-card for repetitive food assistance will be unrestricted cash. The method selection of cash was made in consultation with beneficiaries and in accordance with the right to dignity of affected populations to promote decision making and ownership over expenditures in accordance with HH priorities. Cash will allow the assistance to be more effective and efficient in areas with accessible markets. Additionally, cash also has a multiplier effect which is expected to boost and stimulate the local economy.
For emergencies and based on the context of the displacement, SARD will distribute one -off MPCA ( 150 $) when to 3000 HHs ( 15,000 Individuals) when markets are functional and products are available, this will be done through contracting with Hawala agents. If markets are either far to reach or not functional, RTEs will be considered in such emergency situations where in-kind is more effective than cash. SARD proposes distributing 2000 RTEs to 2000 HHs (10,000 individuals).
Through a protection lens, SARD’s intervention is designed to ensure that newly displaced and vulnerable families have access to dignified and timely assistance. To ensure that marginalized groups such as female-headed households, the elderly, and persons with disability (PwD) have safe and secure access to assistance, specialized and gender-sensitive activities have formed an integral part of SARD’s response strategy.

3 - Needs

Access to food remains one of the top three priorities for HHs across Syria according to 2023 HNO, with ¾ women led HHs reporting unmet needs. Furthermore, adult women and men are specifically strained and impacted by food insecurity as they frequently engage in adverse coping mechanisms to ensure that their children have enough food to eat.In 2022, SARD’s conducted a YNA (Yearly Needs Assessment) which observed the needs in various sectors including Food, Non-Food Items, Shelter, and Livelihood sectors. Findings showed that 25% of households reported that their food consumption decreased in the last month. Furthermore, the main challenge relating to food access pertained to the high price of food items in local markets, as reported by 78% of households, the low quality of available food (36%), and the lack of variety of goods in the local market (14%). On average, 68% of interviewed households rely on income-generating activities (IGA) for subsistence while 16% have no cash sources available. However, preliminary findings show variations based on households’ displacement status with 63% of IDPs having IGA and 17% with no cash sources compared to 87% and 10% respectively for host households. Lack of job opportunities in the local market, lack of financial means to start a business along lack of technical qualifications are the main challenges faced by conflict-affected households. Cash remains the most preferred aid modality for livelihood support as reported.

4 - Implementation of activities

To address the multi-sectoral needs as described above, this assistance will provide a different and context based food assistance as well as restoring food production livelihoods to those with capacities and skills. SARD will ensure newly-displaced families have dignified access to life-saving assistance through provision of one -off emergency MPCA or RTEs, to populations in needs. Assessment on the security, locations, tenders and markets’ surveys will enable SARD to determine the modality of response (cash or in-kind).SARD is committed to also provide life-sustaining food to most vulnerable families in the form of cash using E-card modality. This action provides 8-months of repetitive cash of food assistance. Vulnerability is identified and prioritized through a selection and scoring mechanism. Among the most vulnerable families, SARD will identify families eligible for IGA opportunities, to offer them a sustainable exit strategy from food assistance. All project activities will be monitored by MEAL team on an ongoing basis using the means of verification designated for each output to track progress against the project indicators. A comprehensive monitoring plan will be developed at the beginning of the project containing information on indicators, means of verification, timeline and products. Activities will take place as follows: Identification, registration, distributions , monitoring and post- monitoring of the emergency support, including the sharing of the 5Ws.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Under the current context, the most vulnerable categories, such as women headed HH, PwD, may be excluded from accessing the service due to the socio-cultural barriers.Likelihood: medium, Impact: HighMitigation Measures. a) SARD increase visibility for self-referral to ensure inclusion of the most vulnerable individuals. b) SARD applies a scoring system to all selected beneficiaries in order to categories them according to their vulnerabilities. Protection Risk: The high target of the project may lead in some cases to reduce the quality of the service.Likelihood: low, Impact: medium.Mitigation Measures: a) Prioritization of safety and dignity and avoid causing harm: in IDP sites/camps, the program and MEL teams constantly monitors the quality of the assistance delivered, ensuring that only quality activities are provided. b) SARD has a strong CRM in place that is regularly advertised to local communities, currently, due to Covid-19 prevention measures, beneficiaries, and non-beneficiaries can register any feedback/complaints via SARD’s WhatsApp number. Protection Risk: The blanket approach of the program limits the possibility of providing tailored service to person in Need.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: MediumMitigation Measures: SARD coordinate with protection actors to enhance referral pathways and ensure providing the service based on the needs of beneficiaries.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Due the nature of the project, household visits are required to assess the needs and deliver the services which may expose the most vulnerable individual to the sexual exploitation and abuse.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: Through SARD's a) MEL team, and established accountability and complaint mechanism, SARD insure a quick response in case on any incident. B) constantly check the efficiency of the reporting pathways and reviewing SARD’s PSEA policy; c) Employs staff aware of the PSEA policies and run regular refreshing training; d) conduct awareness raising activities to the beneficiaries of the program. Risk: The socio-cultural context in the area may impact the relationship between SARD employees which may results in abuse of power or sexual harassment.Likelihood: low, Impact: HighMitigation Measures: a) SARD value a divers and transparent working environment, therefore, it ensure all staff are aware of the PSEA and CoC; b) review the CoC and PSEA on regular basis and updates them accordingly; c) review the complaint mechanism and ensure all staff are aware of them by running refreshing training on regular basis.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: -Locations of project activities are secure and accessible. -Markets are functioning with the availability of food on the market -People of concern (beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries) are aware of selection and prioritization criteria implemented by SARD -No Covid-19 cases reported in the IDP sites/camps. -SARD staff and targeted communities are aware of Covid-19 threats, risk, and mitigation measures. Risks and Mitigation: -Insecurity conditions result in accessibility problems and exposure to risks to staff and beneficiaries: To prevent these risks, safety precautions will be taken such as alternate routes, changing the warehouse and office locations. Should the situation become volatile, SARD can use a mobile kitchen. -Lack of supplies. To prevent any shortages, SARD will identify alternative options in nearby areas. SARD's warehouse in Syria has 1-2 weeks food provision for the kitchen, and SARD has secured access for additional food supply in Turkey. -Implementation of gender and protection-sensitive measures during activities, including additional technical support when required. - Risk of tension due to selection criteria for the livelihood component. SARD will assess in advance and adopt mitigation measures aiming at adequate distributions of chances - Activities in the context of the Covid-19 pandemic: Standard operation procedures were developed and being implemented by SARD’s teams.
