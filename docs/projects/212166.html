Project Summary

A.Needs
IHR staff responding on the ground in Syria report that the quake-affected areas targeted in this proposal (Qourqeena, Badama, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Bennsh, and Ariha sub-districts) host a high number of families displaced by conflict. Many of the people impacted have already been displaced up to 20 times. Lack of access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, deterioration of shelter integrity (notable increase in damages to windows/doors), and lack of privacy are the top shelter adequacy issues identified in the previously mentioned areas.
Camps needs:
- Mountainous locations hinder infrastructure and amenities.
- Flooding and winter issues: Camps face flooding and heating fuel challenges.
- Gender-based challenges and limited empowerment.
- Needs to enhance shelter infrastructure to mitigate flooding.

B. Intervention and implementation of activities:
IHR aims to address critical Shelter gaps in response to the needs of Vulnerable people residing in 8 underserved camps in Rabeeta, Barisha, Kafr Aruq, and Kherbet El Joz communities (Qourqeena and Badama sub-districts) in addition to support sustainably repairing/rehabilitating housing for owners/tenants/returnees/host families in Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Bennsh and Ariha communities.
C. Analysis of protection risks:
Risk 1: The Physical safety of beneficiaries/communities is at risk due to the structural integrity of the building and/or the presence of ERWs in or near potential work sites.
Likelihood: High
Impact: High
Mitigating Measure:
1. Structural assessment of buildings by qualified personnel.
2. Repair/rehabilitate the buildings only if the building is found structurally safe.
3. Work closely with local authorities, communities, and agencies involved in ERW clearance to ensure that the building/area is free from ERW.
4. Orient contractors/workers on risks associated with the presence of ERW and ask them to report immediately report if a suspicious object is found and suspend the work until the suspected object is removed safely.
Risk 2: Eviction from shelters units, camps, transit sites, spontaneous settlement due to lack of approval from property owner/conflict in property ownership.
Likelihood: High
Impact: High
Mitigating Measure:
1. Work closely with local authorities to ensure that there is a written approval from owner for a specific period to use the property to develop the site.
2. Start developing the site only after written approval is produced.
3. Advocate with local authorities to facilitate approval for spontaneous settlement.
4. Advocate with local authorities to find alternative accommodations/sites for those at risk of eviction.
5. Work closely with population at risk of eviction together with other sectors and local authorities to find solutions and clearly communicate the options available.
D. PSEA Strategy:
This proposal upholds key principles set out in the HRP such as accountability to affected populations, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and do no harm.Reporting SEA Complaints: A confidential hotline is available for anonymous reporting.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

1- The first side of the project is targeting 600 shelter units in Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Bennsh, and Ariha Communities through the provision of housing rehabilitation to meet the needs of people through the provision of housing rehabilitation services for returnees & IDPs people for the affected beneficiaries whether they were tenants or owners depending on the actual needs of the targeted people which will be identified and verified by our field team after targeting communities in real needs for shelter assistance, where IHR will provide information and data of existing vulnerabilities related to Shelter, negative coping strategies and damage level as well.2- The second part of the project aims to provide comprehensive Shelter support leading to dignified and safer living conditions to enhance the resilience of IDPs in 8 camps in Rabeeta, Barisha, Kafr Aruq, and Kherbet El Joz communities, focusing on the provision of 350 dignified shelter support and protecting the IDPs from the harsh climate and potential flood risks. Also, we will provide insulation for 500 tents in sites where we cannot implement dignified shelter to ensure a warmer and more comfortable living environment in addition to upgrading the main roads and infrastructure within the camps and installing lighting poles as recommended by the cluster.Risks: 1.Risk of creation of spontaneous settlement in location where no management system is set-up and/or services are not provided.Likelihood: HighImpact: HighMitigating Measure:1.Constantly liaise with the SNFI Working Group on planned activities, attend the WG meeting and seek support prior to the implementation of the activities. 2.Carefully and periodically check and coordinate with Sites and Settlements Working Group to ensure that relevant management systems can be set-up3.Raise awareness of IP and staff on the dangers connected to incentivating (indirectly) the creation of unmanaged camps2.Not having an exit strategy might lead to transforming a temporary shelter solution (CSs) into a long-term accommodation, with the risks connected to tension with the host community, owners and eventually among beneficiaries families themselves.Likelihood: HighImpact: HighMitigating Measure:1.Ensure an exit strategy in place, with time-bound agreement with the owners and tenants2.Keep the communication lines with the authorities open to inform them of the rationale of the interventions and the aim of the programmeMEAL activities: MEAL Department will work effectively and efficiently towards achieving project goals.
