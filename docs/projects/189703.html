Project Summary

Delivering quality health service delivery is key in the reduction of mortality and morbidity and especially maternal and child Mortality rates. Over decades in South Sudan, delivery of quality health care services to meet the huge health care needs of the vulnerable populations in need has been met with strong resistance emanating from a host of factors; severe floods, acute food and livelihood insecurity, sub-national level violence and conflicts and the deteriorating economy as well as a legacy of political instability and conflicts. These factors have made it even worse for the affected populations to access health care. Health infrastructure has been severely hit and lack of essential medical supplies makes the situation deplorable.
As of 2021, South Sudan had experienced the 3rd wave of severe flooding with more than 850000 people in 33 counties mostly from Jonglei state affected by floods devastating populations and cutting down the delivery of essential services including health services. (Flood Snapshot issue 4 Oct. 2020).
The impact of the COVID 19 pandemic has severely hit the globe with south Sudan also having cases of the disease. This has great health service delivery with weak health systems and economic crisis in the country putting pressure on the inadequately equipped health facilities. More than 44% of the population in South Sudan travel more than 5 km to access health services and vaccination coverage remains less than 50% (UNICEF 2020).
The purpose of CAO's health response plan is to increase access and scale-up of emergency healthcare services to 30,000 vulnerable populations ( 21,000 host and 9,000 IDPs); 13,230 Men, 13,770 Women, 1,470 Boys, 1530 girls in Ayod and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State.
CAO is proposing an estimated HRP budget for the year 2022 at US$ 742,132. This budget will purposively support the following 10 health facilities based on the following level.
• 4 Mobile Clinics; 2 in Ayod County and 2 in Fangak County to reach 9,000 IDPs individuals.
• 2 PHCUs: 1 PHCU in Ayod and 1 PHCU in Fangak County to reach 18,500 host population and 2,500 IDPs/Returnees/Refugees in the two counties.
Therefore, in the 2022 HRP plan, CAO will seek to contribute to improving access to emergency and integrated health care services through provision of OPD consultations, Skilled birth attendant deliveries, immunization services, reproductive health services, GBV prevention and case management of rape, Nutrition services including referral to nutrition sites, mental health services, COVID 19 prevention
measures and management of cases health education and disease surveillance and alert reporting through timely reports. This will be done through mobile health services and static facilities with 4 mobile health facilities, 2 PHCUs, and 2 PHCCs across all the two target Counties.
Of the 30,000 vulnerable IDPs and host populations targeted for this plan in Ayod and Fangak Counties, CAO will target 1,439 people with disabilities.

1 - Needs

• In South Sudan, the humanitarian situation across Jonglei State continued to deteriorate as a result of a host of; natural, man-made, and macroeconomic shocks resulting in mass displacement and limited access to crops, livestock, and markets. Most counties in Emergency Phase 4 of IPC report. These shocks have simultaneously eroded the capacity of food-insecure households to employ coping strategies, exacerbating already widespread, severe, and multisectoral vulnerabilities. Moving forward, humanitarian food assistance will be essential to offset a continued and widespread deterioration that may result in health effects (REACH Assessment in Jonglei, Sept 2020).• The weakened health system compounded by multiple shocks will impact the health and well-being of sick people living in Lakes, Jonglei, and South Sudan at large in 2021. There is limited access to optimal health services due to ongoing conflict, flooding, and an underdeveloped health care system. According to South Sudan IDSR reports, Malaria remains the top cause of morbidity (64 percent) and mortality (45 percent). Between 9 and 15 November 2020, there were 89,403 reported cases of malaria. Measles outbreaks have been recorded in Fangak, Ayod, and Pibor in 2020 (WHO IDSR/EWARS Bulletin, 2020)• SMART surveys conducted in April & September 2020 by Action against Hunger put Duk County at a GAM Rate of 22%. Malaria was reported as the most common health problem for people in 67% of assessed settlements across Jonglei State. (SMART Survey, April 2020).• According to South Sudan’s HNO, 2021; the ongoing flood situation will further aggravate the health situation, the spread of existing outbreaks, and the likelihood of new outbreaks in 2021. This highlights the critical need to strengthen disease surveillance and response system to prevent, detect and respond to future outbreaks. The absence of basic health care including vaccination services has resulted in low immunization coverage in our proposed HRP areas for instance Ayod and Fangak, increasing the risk of vaccine-preventable disease outbreaks. Planned mass vaccination campaigns were disrupted by the COVID-19 containment measures in place, which worsened the vaccination coverage and related health risks (HNO, 2021)•Based on the identified health gaps, CAO is therefore proposing a health care plan to improve access, and scaling-up emergency health cares services to 30,000 vulnerable populations ( 21,000 host and 9,000 IDPs); 13,230 Men, 13,770 Women, 1,470 Boys, 1530 girls affected by floods in Ayod and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State. further, CAO will use an integrated approach to ensure that vulnerable populations affected by acute food insecurity, severe floods, and conflict can get health services by provision of mobile health services and static facilities thus reducing crude mortality and morbidity rates in the target Counties.

2 - Project activities

Under the health response, CAO is therefore proposing the following activities to 30,000 vulnerable populations ( 21,000 host and 9,000 IDPs); 13,230 Men, 13,770 Women, 1,470 Boys, 1530 girls in Ayod and Fangak Counties of Jonglei State for the year 2022; • Conduct awareness campaign on COVID-19 at households and community level• Conduct epidemic disease surveillance, prevention, and control diseases outbreak detection, investigation, and response in all the health facilities • Implement WASH in health facilities including building the capacities of health workers to prevent and control COVID-19, Measles, and cholera outbreaks.• Conduct Immunization against vaccine-preventable diseases and emergency antigens for immunization and cold chain commodities.•Provide BeMONC services in Fangak and Ayod PHCCs • Trained health workers including skilled birth attendants on ANC, and partograph• Conduct mobile and outreach health services in hard to reach locations• Request, transport, and distribute IEHK kits including major essential drugs for health facilities• Provide integrated MHPSS and SGBV including services in health facilities • Provide protocols, guidelines, and IPC supplies in all health facilities under CAO.• Conduct rapid needs assessment and AAP to get the view on communities on healthcare providers in all the counties under CAO• Conduct monitoring visits, and support supervision to ensure quality provision in health facilities.•Conduct health education for all patients and households living near a health facility•Implement integrated community case management of diseases including boma health initiatives for the smallest unit of government.• Provision of rehabilitation services and assertive devices for the disabled. • Conduct curative consultations for inpatients and outpatient services• Conduct MUAC Screening for all the under five years’ children and pregnant & lactating women.• Conduct mass distribution of mosquitoes nets including mass vitamin A supplementation for pregnant women.• Conduct Malaria screening, treatment, and tracking • Provide ANC, normal skilled delivery, and postnatal care services• Conduct family planning & other reproductive health services

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CAO will use Positive feedback to improve the project implementation. Negative feedback will be used as a measure to correct mistakes and improve programming as well as to the satisfaction of the beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CAO will embrace the use of disability inclusion facilitators who will be able to communicate with people with disabilities, where possible sign language interpreters will be used in order to get feedback and audio recordings for the visually impaired. CAO will employ local community members to be able to get feedback from the illiterate and women representatives to get feedback from the women and girls.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CAO will ensure that the communities are aware of the non-tolerance to sexual exploitation and abuse by CAO as an organization. During the project design and implementation, CAO will create awareness on what entails sexual exploitation and abuse through setting up posters and banners with a language understandable to the beneficiaries

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CAO will have community engagement meetings with the vulnerable and affected populations. during the engagement meetings, CAO will inform the beneficiaries about the project activities and their entitlements and form beneficiary accountability committees that will be responsible for giving feedback to the organization and vice versa. CAO will also put suggestion boxes for collecting feedback ad a designated hotline for complaints which will be acted upon as soon as possible.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

CAO is highly specialized in provision of emergency protection interventions with minimum package of prevention and risk mitigation of GBV in all the target locations for this project. Therefore, CAO will contribute to the collective outcome 2 by ensuring that GBV interventions especilly related to GBV awareness through sensitization events and mobilizations will be conducted in all targeted health facilities which this project will establish. CAO will train teachers on GBV awareness, risks and how to mitigate them. Further, patients enrolled into this programme will be educated on GBV taking consideration of their age. In this case, it is anticipated that by 2023, CAO will have contributed to prevention and risk mitigation of GBV in all its operational areas by atleast 80% coverage of the GBV risk communication and mitigation interventions in Ayod, and Fangak counties.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Throughout implementation of this project, CAO will use the education platforms to carry out whistle blowing and early warning communications that are geared toward conservation of sustainable environment not only for the current generation but also for the future including establishing tree planting clubs in schools, and also promoting climate sensitive interventions like afforestation awareness among all learning centers in Ayod, Fangak, Pochalla, and Akobo West. This way, it is anticipated that CAO will contribute to lowering of flood related displacements by less than 30% by the year 2025.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

During implementation of the project, CAO intents to use a multi sector approach to integrate food security and livelihood activities in schools. For instance CAO will establish kitchen gardens in health facilities and mobile outreach health facilities in Ayod and Fangak. CAO will also train health care workers and caretakers of patients on agronomic practices like vegetable production in school gardens. This way, its anticipated that CAO will contribute to the reduction of people in IPC phase 4 to below by atleast less than 20% by the year 2023.
