Project Summary

Inter communal violence continued to be the key drivers of humanitarian needs in Ethiopia, inclding the Oromia and Somali regions. The Ethiopia Crisis Response Plan (2022) of IOM states that, Ethiopia is also the second largest refugee-hosting country in Africa hosting over 823,000 refugees and asylum seekers. Most of the refugees are accommodated in 26 refugee camps with limited services and depend largely on humanitarian assistance.
The number of internally displaced people in the country increased from 3.2m in 2018 to 4.3m in late 2021. Insecurity and access restrictions hampered the relief operation and exacerbated the humanitarian crisis. According to Ethiopia CCCM master list updated in December 2021, 871,176 IDPs (about 52% female) are residing in 427 sites in the Somali Region. Out of these sites only 5 are CCCM managed by IOM, UNHCR and ACTED. While there are four camps (Darimi, Gabagabo, Sariirta and Tuliguled Town) in the Tuliguled woreda of Fafan Zone in the Somali Region, none of the camps are CCCM managed.
The coordination and camp management of the IDP centers in three rural kebeles and one transit center in Tuluguled town is run by government DRM. A total of 11,374 IDPs are targeted with the project.

The proposed interventions aim at providing effective coordination at camp level between the humanitarian assistance and protection programmes being delivered to the displaced population. The project will involve the local level partners such as government offices and NGOs working in the woreda through a regular coordination mechanism and will be tasked to plan, implement and monitor project activities in a regular manner. The project will be actively participating in the regional level CCCM cluster meetings, exchange of updates and ensuring that the CCCM activities are integrated into the local plans.
LWF will monitor risks to mitigate the possible impacts.
- The selected sites are located in conflict-prone areas. In the case of social tensions over selection of beneficiaries, the beneficiary selection committee will addressed the situation in consultation with the kebele Administration, woreda DRM and Women and Children Affair offices. The beneficiary selection committee will be selected from the IDPs and kebele leaders to oversee the targeting process. Beneficiary targeting criteria will be established in consultation with local government bodies.
- Drought. The current drought situation in the Somali region may disrupt the camp setting triggering second level displacement. LWF will work with the government and other humanitarian actors to make sure that lifesaving assistances are delivered to the affected communities in a timely manner. LWF will also work closely with the concerned government offices in exchange of early warning data.
COVID-19. Due to the congested settlement in the IDP camps, COVID-19 poses a high risk of transmission. LWF will work with the health office. LWF will also consider key COVID-19 prevention measures and protocols into its own activities such as meetings/trainings.
- Fraud and corruption. The possibility of fraud and corruption happening is minimum. LWF has a very good financial management and control system to protect such kind of risks. The LWF head office team will establish a system of close monitoring of financial transactions in the project area. The field coordination office has the necessary staff to ensure that the project is properly managed and run.
- Security and staff safety. LWF has a system of continuous monitoring and reporting of security incidents to deal with them on a timely manner.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

As a matter of best practice, LWF coordinates to a high degree and maintains strong relationships with local and regional government actors in all of its programming. Relevant woreda and zone government offices, including the agriculture, women and children’s affairs, and disaster risk management offices were consulted in the planning of this project. These offices would be involved in the implementation of the project through ongoing consultation, stakeholders’ meetings, and the selection and complaint handling committees.LWF attends all relevant coordination meetings, including those convened by the WASH and ES/NFI clusters, the agriculture task-force, CCCM, and the cash working group at the zonal, regional and federal levels and discusses on coordination and synergy, among other issues. Additionally, LWF is an active member of the Humanitarian International NGO coordination forum in Addis Ababa. In Fafan zone, LWF has been an active member of the Disaster Risk Management task-force consisting of NGOs and UN agencies operating in the region. LWF will continue to use these forums to strengthen information exchange, synergy and complementary with the other actors who are operating in the same geographical areas in Bale and East Hararghe zones. Besides, LWF has strong working partnership with the local government at regional, zone and woreda level. The regional coordination office based in Jigjiga town will be responsible for the project implementation in the zone and will support the project to enhance complimentary linkage using the existing expertise and resource apart from the new staff and resources to be deployed through the project. Additionally, the LWF team in the East will ensure that this project complements previous and current interventions in the same intervention woreda of the zone.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

There will be complaints boxes where complaints can be dropped at any time. LWF also provide telephone number and email address of its staffs whom they can call or send email to register their complaints. As part of accountability mechanism, the CRM will ensure that the community members can freely and confidentially share their complaints without fear. Besides, the project will set up complaint and response handling committee in each of the 4 IDP Camps. The committees will be composed of between 5 to 7 members. Their main duty will be to receive complaints and any form of feedback from the community. Some of the most common complaints expected are related to targeting of beneficiaries, activity timelines and quality of product and service delivery. Towards this, LWF will employ a wide range of means. There will be a hard cover note book at each site where the committees from the community members can directly record the complaints received or have someone to write for them and the feedback given. LWF will work with the PSEA network and the inter agency accountability working group (IAAWG) to exchange information and also improve the system for a coordinated response.
