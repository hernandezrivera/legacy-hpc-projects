Project Summary

RI will have a direct implementation approach to meet the reproductive health and protection needs of targeted communities in Northwest Syria (NWS), incorporating water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) measures to support these health outcomes. RI will look to support partners with reproductive health (RH) kits provided by UNFPA to meet the immediate needs of beneficiaries in NWS, and RI will increase access to sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and protection services by supporting 2 health facilities and 1 Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) through adjacent/integrated protection spaces providing protection risk mitigation activities. This integrated approach will facilitate confidential and protective environments for referrals to be conducted within. Health and protection activities will be integrated through the health staff visiting the WGSS to deliver SRH awareness and the presence of a Social Assistant (SA) in HFs to provide initial care and support to survivors and facilitate referrals to the WGSS. This program will enhance the quality of health service delivery through robust monitoring and supervision of 2 Women and Children Hospitals, which are Nour Hospital in Idleb Governorate in Taftanaz sub-district, and Abin Samaan hospital in Aleppo governorate Al Atareb sub-district. The intervention aims to provide safe access to integrated quality RH and GBV prevention and response.
RI will also improve WASH facilities and services to minimize sub-standard WASH conditions for individuals in NWS. RI selected 8 HFs (Wassim Hasino Hospital; Fadi Koshkosh Dialysis Center; Kafr Takharim Obstetrics/Gynecology; Males Primary Care Center; Armanaz Primary Care Center; Armanaz Hospital; Armanaz Dialysis Center; Armanaz Peer Primary Care Center) to improve hygiene services through health care waste management. The project will reduce the impact on the environment by establishing a waste management zone to strengthen medical waste disposal in NWS.

3 - Needs

Syria's decade-old conflict has trapped civilians where people’s rights to safety, security, and well-being are regularly violated. Protection issues disproportionately affect marginalized or socially excluded people and groups (HNO 2022). They face additional barriers to accessing the limited available services.RI will work in NWS to focus on an integrated health and protection program to ensure the following four key results are met: increased immediate access to relevant services, ensuring protection needs are met with dignified means, a protective environment is ensured through strong community engagement, and the local capacities of organizations are enhanced.The disruptions in assistance, particularly specialized protection services, lead to enhanced distress and increased risks of GBV and other protection issues (HNO 2022). Overall, the project will aim to increase access for conflict-affected individuals to integrated protection and sexual and reproductive health services in NWS. To support the RH outcomes, improved waste segregation and safe disposal of medical and non-medical waste are needed in NWS, as most HFs in NWS do not have a clear plan for medical waste. RI will strengthen medical waste management and support 8 HFs to reduce the overall rate of diseases caused by transmission diseases. RI will support a central medical waste zone NWS. Currently, there is no safe disposal of medical waste in the area.

4 - Implementation of activities

RI will implement the project in the following hospitals: Idleb Governorate, Taftanaz sub-district in Al Nour Hospital (G0700016 Nour Maternity & Pediatric Hospital), with integrated protection links through a nearby WGSS; and Akhtarin Hospital ( B0204070 Akhtarin hospital ) located in Aghtrin sub-district in Aleppo governorate, integrating GBV and PSS services through protection and GBV staff based in the hospital. Technical support sessions on RH from RI health team will be provided to HF’s SRH key staff and the supported HFs will be monitored by RI field health officers. Training on SRH protocols and SRH and protection referrals will be provided to the HF staff. Integration between GBV, PSS, and health services through the inclusion of a SA in the HF to strengthen referrals of GBV cases. Specialized maternal and SRH services will be delivered in the HFs including antenatal and postnatal care, family planning, vaginal deliveries, c- section, clinical management of rape (CMR) survivors, comprehensive abortion treatment and care, and syndromic management of sexually transmitted infections. RI will directly implement the WASH project which will include all the required support to the field staff, renting a vehicle and collecting the medical waste from the HFs to be disposed of in the waste zone area and equipment and logistics. This will include the procurement and distribution of healthcare waste management items, diesel, and needed materials.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

RI staff are exposed to risk of harm as a result of operating in a highly volatile environment. Country and hub security managers, area security coordinators, officers and focal points are in place for adequate context monitoring and analysis, engaging communities to understand acceptance and protection strategies.Some RI facilities are near the frontlines and/or armed combatant locations, while others are in areas of latent conflict. Therefore, risk thresholds are set up and accepted considering existing humanitarian needs and humanitarian imperative. Eg: Idlib is a highly contested area. If conflict levels become unacceptable, relocation and hibernation SOPs take place. Warden system, headcounts and addresses of staff are checked and updated on regular basis. Mitigations include: Contextualized staff training on areas as First Aid, personal security and security management; Further capacity building to local partners as part of our contractual agreements and Organizational Capacity Assessments; Outreach strategy when entering in a new community or when any issues arise; Discuss with all local communities and groups about humanitarian principles and the nature of work to avoid any challenges; Remote verification of transactions as per RI Remote Verification Framework; Individuals and entities with whom RI engages in any monetary and in-kind transactions are subject to ATC/CSI Watchlists screening; Continue advocacy and liaison with local authorities.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

RI staff or partners could perpetrate abuse against participants (in violation of PSEA and Child Protection policies) and/ or staff or programs could expose participants to harm. To mitigate against this: - Established feedback/complaints mechanism and following up on the feedback/complaints using feedback tracker. • HR orientation to all new staff and refresher orientation to existing staff on HR policies with mandatory signing of Code of Conduct and HR policies (this includes clear definition of PSEA and how to report it). • Regular staff orientation on incident reporting mechanisms. • Incident management team follows-up with a timely approach to all complaints according to severity rate. • Deploying a PSEA focal point to represent RI PSEA network, WoS Safeguarding Focal Point appointed to support strengthening safeguarding needs.• Regular communications on updated/revised PSEA policies/revised to the staff and trainings on updated policies are delivered in a regular basis. • Ensure that protection mainstreaming is a core component of RI programming, programs are based on key protection principles: accountability, meaningful access, and avoiding causing harm. • Develop awareness-raising materials for staff, contractors, and clients on PSEA topics.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

If RI’s registration status were to be revoked in Türkiye, RI will maintain Syria operations through remote management. RI would transition to increase procurement in Syria and improve control measures to assess commodities' quality if the border access is blocked.If HFs are rendered inoperable from airstrikes or any other security incident, RI will suspend or relocate the HFs as appropriate. In case of loss of access, RI will leverage existing community feedback mechanisms to understand sensitivities in delivering SRH-based activities and modify awareness-raising activities to reduce any community backlash through indirect staff from the community and community mobilizers that provides outreach activities.RI will maintain a high level of transparency with all local communities and groups about humanitarian principles. RI has developed a robust, context-specific fraud mitigation plan and continues to reassess and improve operations based on lessons learned to prevent fraud and corruption at the supported HFs.Actions will be taken to promote social distancing and each HF will have triage space for early recognition of COVID-19.Risks related to WASH activities will be constantly monitored and risks will be mitigated. The physical security assessment and upgrades will be taken into consideration for any new static location including but not limited to the proximity to the frontlines, military checkpoints, de-confliction, access, emergency exit, CCTV cameras, and a bunker.
