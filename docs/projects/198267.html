Project Summary

WASH conditions have become a significant concern as IDPs return to their original Local Government Areas (LGAs) in Northeast Nigeria, as conflict-affected families increasingly lack access to adequate water and sanitation infrastructure due to damage/destruction to facilities, poorly constructed and overburdening of WASH facilities in camps and host communities. Poor access to improved water and sanitation services, seasonal flooding, transmission of waterborne diseases such as seasonal cholera, typhoid and other related water borne diseases. According to the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) between 2021 and 2022, the BAY states experienced an increased number from 3M to 5M people in need of WASH humanitarian assistance, across internally displaced persons (+29%), returnees (+ 113%) and hosting communities (+95%). The highest WASH needs were even found in the catastrophic (very critical) situation for host communities and IDPs out of camp in some LGAs in Borno (Magumeri) and Adamawa (Demsa and Jada).
In 2022, access to safe water, dignified sanitation and hygiene awareness was impacted by heavy flooding, camp closures, and the movement of IDPs and returnees. This has led to negative coping mechanisms including drinking water from unsafe sources, increased open defecation and poor personal and household-level hygiene, resulting namely in an unprecedented cholera outbreak. Further displacement due to conflict has led to poor access to safe water, and congestion in camps has resulted in limited access to safe sanitation with partners resorting to manual dislodging facing heavy flooding, moreover with some challenging access to the dumping sites. Hygiene awareness and personal hygiene at the household level have been impacted by the high cost of living which has resulted in a lack of soap at the household level, leaving vulnerable populations, especially the elderly and children under 5 at high risk of diseases. Other problems include: lack of water collection and storage facilities; and hygiene items, poor hygiene practices in healthcare facilities, schools, Child Friendly Spaces and informal/temporary learning spaces, poor waste management in health facilities and the host community

According to the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) between 2021 and 2022, the BAY states experienced an increased number from 3M to 5M people in need of WASH humanitarian assistance, across internally displaced persons (+29%), returnees (+ 113%) and hosting communities (+95%). This has led to negative coping mechanisms including drinking water from unsafe sources, increased open defecation and poor personal and household-level hygiene, resulting namely in an unprecedented cholera outbreak. Further displacement due to conflict has led to poor access to safe water, and congestion in camps has resulted in limited access to safe sanitation with partners resorting to manual dislodging facing heavy flooding, moreover, with some challenging access to the dumping sites. Hygiene awareness and personal hygiene at the household level have been impacted by the high cost of living which has resulted in a lack of soap at the household level, leaving vulnerable populations, especially the elderly and children under 5 at high risk of diseases.In 2023, Save the Children (SC) will expand its multi-sector integrated approach to better prioritize the WASH-related acute vulnerabilities from a risk reduction perspective, through risk mitigation and case area targeted interventions, such as before the rainy season in the flood-prone areas and cholera hotspots. Considering the population movements and lessons learned regarding the cholera outbreak and historic floods, the sector will also reinforce the local capacities and prepositioning of the most cost-efficient common pipeline supplies. SC will continue to mainstream protection and mitigate gender-based violence (GBV) to ensure that access to WASH services is framed after systematic consultations with women and girls from accountability for affected population (AAP) perspective at the top level beyond the feedback mechanisms, in particular women and children can do so in a safe manner.

2 - Activities

WATER: SC will support life-saving assistance through the provision of safe water at the household level by ensuring emergency and sustainable water sources are available for the affected population. In camps and hosting communities, support for health and nutrition centers that will present acute water shortages will be prioritized. SC will increase responses in water quality through routine monitoring and chlorination (household and communal level). Additionally, the safety of beneficiaries to access water points will be increased by ensuring the functionality of water points, and improved ownership by the community to reduce incidences of theft and vandalism as reported in 2022.• SANITATION: Improve access to sanitation, by ensuring the availability of gender-segregated sanitation facilities to the affected population. Additional support will be prioritized to Child Friendly Spaces, Health, Nutrition centers (OTPs) and Temporary Learning Spaces which will be closely coordinated with respective sectors. Before the rainy season, SC will prioritize vector control campaigns in camps and scale up preparedness plans, including dislodging capacity support.• HYGIENE: Promote in local languages culturally appropriate hygiene messages for social behavior change, all along the key WASH NFIs distribution and monitoring to all target populations, namely the households with SAM children and/or cholera cases. Prevention and awareness messages will be aimed at breaking the transmission cycle of communicable diseases (especially cholera) which will improve food intake and health of children especially those under 5, significantly contributing to health and nutrition objectives. Priority will be given to Bama, Gwoza, Mafa, Monguno, Pulka, Jere & MMC, with key locations in Yobe (Damaturu, Potiskum) and Adamawa (Yola North & South, Mubi Axis and Michika, Numan and Lamurde). Further, SC will target women and girls with menstrual hygiene awareness items and messaging including improving the capacity to locally prepare produced menstrual hygiene items.In addition, SC will tailor the program to ensure Integration into its existing nutrition program to address issues of malnutrition, the outbreak of communicable diseases and improved access to schools by ensuring water and sanitation access for TLSs (temporary learning spaces), in line with the centrality of protection with guidance from Protection Sector. SC will prioritized and improved capacity of response for national organizations through capacity building and promoting partnerships. Strong national partners will be supported through the availability of documented approaches, such as technical guidelines to ensure the response is sustainable. Further, SC will continue to work closely with government agencies and agencies (RUWASSA, BOSEPA etc) to ensure complementarity.
