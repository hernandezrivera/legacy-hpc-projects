Needs

There have been multiple displacements in South Sudan, including a recent mass displacement in May -July 2017 in Nasir, Ulang, Longechuk, and Maiwuit as well as in February 2018 in Nasir, worsening situation of both IDPs and host communities. Hygiene practices, including open defecation, put people at risk of disease where the population is highly concentrated - as is the case in displacement sites. Access to water and sanitation is projected to remain a major issue in 2019, further aggravating the risk of malnutrition and disease relating to poor sanitation and hygiene. A lack of safe drinking water, in combination with poor hygiene practices, leaves a large proportion of South Sudan’s population at persistent risk of preventable waterborne diseases including cholera and acute watery diarrhea. RI has conducted several needs assessments and surveys in Upper Nile State throughout 2018, including an inter-agencies assessments conducted in Maiwuit county in May and in Nasir county in September and RI participated and lead the WASH, Health and Nutrition sectors’ assessments. The assessments of all counties revealed the following: • The majority of residences are in scattered, hard-to-reach locations very far from water points; distance travelled to water sources varied significantly across and within surveyed payams reaching between 10 minutes and four hours to fetch water. Far distances to fetch water, greater than 30 minutes, causes significant protection risks especially for women and girls, alos increases the likelihood of drinking stagnatna aand contaminated water from swamps and ponds. • Open defecation remains rampant. While RI has constructed household and institutiaonal latrines in the targeted areas, constructed latrines do not meet the need for those remaining without latrines. It was observed across the four counties that 90 – 93 percent of people were practicing open defecation. • Throughout the assessment, no handwashing facilities at the household level were observed. RI health facilities maintain handwashing facilities, however schools and other community structures are lacking proper handwashing facilities. Personal hygiene is also very poor, children were observed playing in stagnant, open water in many cases. • Most sanitation facilities in health and schools are not functional and not being maintained properly causing a worsening sanitation and environmental conditions for patients, healthcare workers and pupils. • There was limited proper medical waste management and incineration/disposal observed at health and nutrition facilities. In lack of proper environmental sanitation, medical waste is being thrown and dumped randomly, leading to infectious diseases.Poor hygiene practices further aggravate health issues and heighten the risk of disease outbreak (including cholera and typhoid). In addition, most households were using dried calabash plants and open plastic buckets without covers or lids for water storage. In some payams, households are rely on small, old jerry cans with a storage capacity of only five liters. Education and hygiene promotion is limited due in part to limited access by NGOs and community actors. In Upper Nile, RI is the main WASH partner in Nasir, Ulang, Maiwut, and Longechuk – often the only partner providing services to hard-to-reach communities. Increased awareness and education on water treatment methodology and the spread of water-borne illnesses is critically needed to prevent further spread of diseases. Through proposed WASH interventions in water supply (water point rehabilitation, construction, and water point disinfection), sanitation (latrine construction, waste removal) and hygiene promotion, RI plans to improve and strengthen ongoing WASH initiatives as well as enable expand into further hard-to-reach locations in Greater Upper Nile, Jongeli and Equatoria regions in order to address the WASH needs of IDPs, returnees, and host communities.

Activities

Assessments:  Conduct in-depth situation analysis, baseline survey and KAP survey to assess WASH needs and key trends in target communities. Conduct technical and sanitary surveys to all targeted water points / sources for construction / rehabilitation. Water Provision: Installation of water treatment plants in order to provide safe water to prevent cholera and other water-related diseases among IDPs, host and vulnerable communities - 2 Units. Construction of new boreholes by manual drilling to increase the water coverage among vulnerable IDPs and host communities with locations determined through consultations with women, girls, boys and men – 10 boreholes. Establishment / rehabilitation and installation of solar motorized pump with small scale water supply distribution systems- 6 water supply systems. Rehabilitate and repair 80 damaged and abandoned boreholes to ensure safe water provision to communities. Establishment / rehabilitation of damaged and abandoned rain water harvesting systems (RWHSs) in institutions (schools and health facilities) - 5 RWHSs. Form or re-establish 103 water users committees of 5 persons each (WUCs) with at least 50% female membership and at least two women in a position of responsibility/influence. Preposition spare parts for borehole rehabilitation/construction.  Introduce households to improved water treatment methods and educate households on the importance of safe water and water borne disease prevention. Conduct water quality surveillance to all water points, distribution systems in the project areas to ensure safe water provision to IDPs, host and conflict-affected communities in order to prevent cholera and other WASH-related diseases. Conduct refresher training for 103 pump mechanics and caretakers with key attention placed on the engagement of womenSanitation:  Construct at least 1,000 emergency/family shared pit latrines for host and IDP communities and mobilize community leaders to support hygiene promotion activities. Construct and rehabilitate institutional latrines to ensure safe and adequate access to sanitation services - 100 latrines. Construct / rehabilitate incinerators and waste disposal pits to ensure proper medical waste incineration and disposal at health facilities – 10 Nos. Introduce and promote household and community solid waste management through training, provision of tools and awareness campaigns.Hygiene Promotion : Train 150 community hygiene and sanitation promoters (40% male and 60% female)  Distribute hygiene kits in order improve hygiene practices – 6,000 HKs. Distribute WASH –NFIs kits in order improve hygiene practices – 10,000 NFIs. Distribute NFIs, aqua tabs, PUR to ensure safer water consumption and handling. Provide digging kits to affected communities for latrine construction.  Conduct hygiene promotions sessions and awareness program through trained community hygiene promoters. Conduct special women and girl’s menstrual hygiene education and promotion sessions to share good hygiene practices during mensuration  At least five (total - 25) hygiene promotion campaigns per project site, household visits and community discussions in targeted villages focusing on cholera prevention measures, hand washing, environmental sanitation. – minimum: 25 campaigns Prepare and implement cholera prevention measures through hygiene promotion campaigns, using IEC materials to promote safe water consumption and good hygiene and sanitation practices. Strengthen EWARS through integrated health programming for WASH-related diseases Ensure integration through hygiene promotion sessions at health and nutrition facilities.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Information on AAP will be provided during sensitization and mobilization prior to the project start date through consultations with community leaders - both formal and informal. Chiefs, project management committees, water user committees, and women/youth groups will be provided with AAP messaging through meetings. Additionally, prior to all distributions, AAP messaging will be shared through mass-messaging.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages to both sexes will be generic and easy to understand (including the use of pictorials). Messages to vulnerable groups (including women, girls, the elderly, PLWs), will be provided in a more discrete manner including at the household level and/or by female staff.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

PSEA is already mainstreamed throughout all of RI's policies and programming. Populations will be informed of reporting mechanisms, anonymous and non-anonymous through AAP messaging. SEA is unacceptable in RI, is considered gross misconduct and is grounds for immediate termination of staff.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Local leaders - informal and informal have been consulted on a regular basis throughout 2018 for feedback on 2019 programming through formal and informal meetings. Consultation with beneficiaries also has occurred through meetings with water user committees, at schools, and through hygeine promootion sessions at healthcare facilities. Beneficiaries of RI's 2018 programming are consulted on a regular basis for feedback on programming and quality of service to improve and enhance programming in 2019. Additionally, coordination meetings with national and international NGOs take place in Juba and in the field to review ongoing programming and plan for 2019.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Existing feedback and complaint mechanisms will be strengthened in 2019. Water User Committees receive feedback and complaints directly through the community, RI staff additionally conduct monitoring visits to field sites to consult with beneficiaries on their satisfaction of RI programming and methods of improvement. Additionally, at all distribution sites, a complaint desk is present (including membership from local authorities) to address issues raised immediately.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Monitoring and evaluation includes post distribution monitoring, site/household visits, as well as regular supervision by field and Juba staff. The community participates in post distribution monitoring through consultation during summarization of results and development of follow-up action plans to address feedback. Additionally, the community participates in monitoring through established complaints mechanisms, wherein they can immediately report to RI and local authorities any violations. Moreover, the community is consulted through one-on-one interviews and group discussions on improvements in programming for 2019.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project considers specific protection needs of all population groups in the following ways: - At all distribution sites a safe space for PLWs and mothers/caretakers of children will be established for breastfeeding, childcare, and socialization (promoting sharing of experiences). - Rights awareness messaging is disseminated prior to all distributions and through WUCs- Referral mechanisms to health, nutrition, and SGBV care are established and will be utilized for cases of illness/diseases, malnutrition, and SGBV - identified cases of illness and malnutrition will be immediately referred to the nearest health/nutrition facility. Self-reported cases of SBGV are referred to the main protection partner (ADRA, CMD) for care and case management. - Vulnerable groups including elder.y, households with malnutrition, PLWs, and the disabled are prioritized for programming.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Barriers to access include violence, and swampy.blocked roads due to rain and swamps. To address barriers to access, RI utilized community mobilizers as well as community hygiene promoters to reach the most vulnerable where RI may not be able to reach. Additionally, RI regularly engages in RRMs to reach IDPs and returnees - where static facilities may be inaccessible. Moreover, in all operational field sites RI maintains means of movement including boats, motorbikes, donkey carts, and vehicles to reach all targeted beneficiaries.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Ineqialities are addressed mainly through the prioritization of women in beneficiary selection as well as in decision-making processes. Women are prioritized for hiring, and are consulted on all decision-making procedures to ensure their voice is heard and considered. The threat of coercion and community tension is mitigated through involvement in programming by local leaders and beneficiaries, to ensure transparent and accountable mechanisms.
