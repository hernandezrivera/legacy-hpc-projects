Project Summary

The proposed education project purposes to ensure equitable access to safe, protective and inclusive learning opportunities for children and youth (3-17 yrs) affected by conflicts, Covid-19 pandemic and natural disasters in Jonglei State. This intervention will ensure access to education for all children including the disadvantaged, vulnerable and hard to reach regardless of their gender, disability or any other circumstance that have made them to be left behind. By adopting an integrated and inter sectoral programming as a cardinal rule in SAADO’s programming approaches, the proposed intervention aims at reaching out to 12,000 (51% boys,49% girls) children (3-17 yrs) from host communities, returnees IDPs(of which 13% will be children with disabilities, with EiE while ensuring integration of key thematic interventions namely; protection, gender and inclusion, WASH in schools, peace and conflict resolution and prevention. In addition, this project target to reach 390 teachers (M/F) and 300 SMCs/PTA in 30 schools (20 -Bor South & 10 Duk Counties) with training and supplies. The design and implementation of this project will be guided by key humanitarian standards that provides guidance on EiE such as IASC-INEE, SPHERE, Do No Harm, ACP, Covid-19 back to school response plan, IASC and GAM. The proposed project locations have been selected based on the existing dire needs (PIN/Severity), and that the interventions are intended to address both short term and long-term education and protection needs of target population groups. In addition, this intervention will leverage and complement SAADO’s over 10 years of experience of working in Jonglei State through other similar education interventions. SAADO is currently implementing a 5-year “Education and Peace building project” supported by Save the Children (SCI) with funding from NORAD in Bor South County and the Education Cannot Wait (ECW)supported by the Multi Year Resilience Programme(MYRP) in Duk County. The project will reach 20 schools, over 8,000 children, 200 SMCs/PTAs and 260 community based local authorities who have already demonstrated impressive results that have provided lessons that will inform the implementation of this project. SAADO has adopted integrated community driven approaches to programming to facilitate transformative changes to lives and communities to mitigate risks and vulnerabilities. This project will rip from the benefits of the already established community based networks and partnerships that SAADO has already established in the region. Inclusion and participation of all key stakeholders at all levels is at the core of all our programming. Through the proposed intervention, SAADO will involve all key stakeholders including people living with disability, the old and other vulnerable groups through the project management cycle. For instance selection of schools, establishment/rehabilitation of TLS and classrooms, negotiating access, monitoring and evaluation, and verification of key outputs and project learning. SAADO has put in place a robust MEAL mechanism with a dedicated team of MEAL staff at the field and main office that will ensure all project data, key outputs and outcomes are reported on time. SAADO will also coordinate with the Education Cluster , intersectional coordination forums and the MoE to share monthly data on enrolment , attendance and dropout rates to feed into regular 4W/5W database. The PM will be tasked with the overall responsibility of providing managerial and Technical leadership , with technical backstop of the Director programmes. A project coordination team comprising of the field staff and the staff at the national office will be established to oversee day to day implementation of the project. Regular project review will be conducted by the project team and lessons learned in the process will inform improvement of project efficiency and effectiveness.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan was further exacerbated by COVID-19 in 2020 according to the HNO overview report 2021. An estimated 1.9 million children and youth (3-17 years) who are affected by conflict and floods found themselves in further grave humanitarian situation as a result of COVID-19 pandemic according to UNICEF South Sudan situation report February 2021. This has worsened the situation and further increased their suffering and vulnerability, particularly for those affected by humanitarian emergencies from floods. The closure of schools in March 2020 in response to the COVID-19 pandemic spread across the country interrupted normal school actives and exposed the children and youth to further hardships according to UNESCO study on impact of COVID-19 released 2020. It also heightened the huge responsibility of the education actors to respond to the situation by ensuring availability of additional resources and capacities to continue responding to the learning needs and protect children and youth 3-17years of age, and education personnel from the COVID-19 pandemic related protection and life saving issues. According to the HNO situation overview report, Jonglei state is one of the most affected region with thousands of people living under grave humanitarian crises as a result from protracted violent crises between the communities and natural disasters such as floods. With an estimated population 1.7 million population and largely comprising of children and youth (3-17yrs), who account for approximately 60% of the population, the situation in not better there. To contribute towards the education cluster target of 805,951 children in dire need of protective and life saving education interventions, SAADO sets a target to reach 12,0000 children and youth (3-17) to access and attend 30 learning spaces in Bor South and Duk Counties, 3% of children with disabilities. Additional 460 teachers and 400 educational personnel will also benefit from this emergency intervention. The two counties of Jonglei State have an estimated population of 517,000 of which 54% are children and youth (3-17yrs), and 3% are children living with disabilities. An estimated population of over 400,000 people are in dire humanitarian needs for life saving and protective humanitarian interventions according to the IPC January to March 2021 report. SAADO’s humanitarian response prioritizes provision of safe and protective education and emergency services for children and youth affected by both natural disasters and conflict, IDPs, Returnees and host communities provision of inclusive education packages for the vulnerable children and youth. This intervention also endeavors to strengthen the response capacity of targeted communities and education authorities to mitigate the impact of the emergencies and COVID-19 on children and youth (3-17 yrs of age) and approximately 460 teachers, benefiting from training such as appropriate pedagogy and subject matter, life skills training and curriculum materials, child protection and safeguarding, ECD training and prevention and mitigation of the COVID-19. Communities , children and youth (3-17 years of age) and teachers will also benefit from information on protection and prevention of GBV, and COVID -19 prevention and mitigation.

2 - Project activities

1. Establish/rehabilitate temporary learning spaces and classrooms in affected areas (in line with INEE-Minimum standards for education in emergencies). A community based approach will be adopted through the establishment/rehabilitation of TLS/classrooms activities. School management committees and local authorities will be consulted including negotiating for support in terms of materials, labour, space and technical support. Integrated schools improvements will include rehabilitation of latrines to ensure separate latrines for girls and boys, kitchen, hand washing points and fence around school. 2. Establish and rehabilitate gender segregated latrines and water points and hygiene promotion. Hygiene and sanitation in-formation and communication materials will also be provided to schools. Adolescents girls and youth will also be provided with dignity kits (ICRC standard) and corresponding education materials and orientation sessions 3. Conduct back to school, community mobilizations and sensitizations campaigns to encourage enrolment of children and adolescents (3-17yrs) to PPE, Primary and Secondary classes. This will include messaging that targets enrolment of children living with disabilities, girls, IDPs, returnees and children from host communities. 4. Training and Sensitization of children clubs/Peer clubs, Teacher, and SMC members on prevention and response strategies of COVID-19. Behaviour Change (BCC) strategies will be employed to reinforce best practices and prevention of the spread of the virus among teachers, learners and the community. The schools will also be supplied with education information and communication(EIC) materials, masks, hand washing soaps, posters and hand washing points. SAADO also endeavors to engage the communities to counter misinformation and stigma linked to the spread of the virus. 5. Provide school aged children with fortified school meals/take home ratio. PPE children from selected ECDs will also be provided essential vitamins and deworming tab-lets. 6. Provide selected ECD centres will be provided with learning (ECD kits) and play materials (recreational kits). The play and learning materials will be procured, packaged to an ECD kit and then distributed to the selected ECD centres. Parents and teachers will be encouraged to participate in developing additional affordable locally developed learning and play materials. 7. Training of teachers on EiE, basic pedagogy, subject matter and learner centred methodologies including psychosocial support, gender responsive teaching methodologies and child safeguarding and protection. 8. Training for community members (SMCs & PTAs) and Ministry of Education authorities (Payam & County Education Officers) on psychosocial support, school monitoring , quality assurance and administration, facilitating gender responsive teaching/learning environment in schools and promotion of community based mechanism for child safeguarding and protection including protecting of girls from abuse. These trainings will be carried out mostly during school holidays to avoid affecting teaching and learning in schools. The trainings will also include other modules on the role and mandate of SMCs/PTA’s, aspects of school management, promotion of out of school children particularly girls, children with disabilities, IDPs, returnees and refugees to be enrolled in the school system. The training will be carried out by SAADO staff using the MoEST training manual for PTA’s. Additional materials will be developed to cover crosscutting and emerging issues. 9. Procurement and distribution of teachers and learners kits for ECD , primary/secondary schools and Accelerated Learning Program (ALP).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Protection and child safeguarding is at core of this proposed intervention. The report/referral pathways of abuse and GBV will be established in schools and also through community based mechanisms to ensure appropriate such cases are reported to the appropriate authorities in schools and the community.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

A core component of SAADO’s proposed intervention will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members through encouraging the community to provide regular feedback through monthly community consultation meetings facilitated by established project management committees. Information will be fed back to project management teams who will be responsible for adapting and iterating implementation strategies and workplans accordingly thereby ensuring field realities are reflected in monthly planning hence facilitating flexible and adaptive programming based on lessons learnt and emerging priorities and needs. In addition, information relating to the project’s response to feedback will be fed back to communities by the project management committees through scheduled consultation meetings, thereby ensuring ongoing two-way communication.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

SAADO’s proposed project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members through having tailored transparency and feedback mechanisms such as open meetings, verbal, written and pictorial notifications of decisions and actions made by the project in the community and will ensure that the school administrations, PTA's and SMC's are trained on feedback mechanisms; In addition, consultations with pupils, teachers, women and men (separately where necessary to ensure distinct gendered voices are heard),as well as consultations with different age groups and persons with special needs using confidential complaints mechanisms (such as a complaints box) which will be used.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries’ feedback is very important in identifying the gaps in the proposed project design throughout the project cycle to facilitate project learning. In addition, beneficiaries feedback will be used to improve the project process, ensure the implementation goes as per the planned project plans and that it will informs the realization of the project objectives and outcomes.
