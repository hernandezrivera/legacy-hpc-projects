Project Summary

As stated in the Humanitarian Needs Overview of 2022, more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, need humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021.Findings from the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview showed a sustained deterioration of people’s physical and mental wellbeing. In the 2022 HRP, ALIGHT proposes to undertake life-saving interventions to affected populations targeting IDPs and returnees living in IDP like situations while engaging the host community and more settled returnees in activities geared towards resilience building. Resilience building and preparing the community for takeover will be realized through capacity building and building water structures that serve both short-term response to emergency situations while building water and sanitation structures that last beyond the emergency phase. These will include boreholes that are solar powered with minimal operation and maintenance costs. In sanitation, ALIGHT has a positive experience working with communities in Kajo-Keji and Budi through CLTS to build household latrines that are of a more permanent nature. WASH needs will be identified through data collection and analysis in the course of implementation of the ongoing intervention and periodic assessments conducted jointly by UNOCHA and other partners. Over a period of four years, Alight has been implementing an integrated intervention among its sectors of WASH, GBV and Nutrition and will continue and strengthen this approach. This proposal targets geographical locations where Alight is implementing both GBV and Nutrition or either of the two. This makes it easier for implementation and integration of the three sectors (WASH, GBV and Nutrition) critical in addressing humanitarian situations resulting from drought, flooding, or displacements due to conflicts. ALIGHT promotes technical/ infrastructure solutions with the people from the communities, employing human-centered and interactive approaches in hygiene and sanitation promotion such as the PHAST, Wash’em and Super Momas; and gender balanced-community lead operation and maintenance practices. Involving the community at the design stage builds confidence, transparency and an atmosphere of trust and is a statement of Accountability to Affected Persons. In this HRP plan, ALIGHT Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) sector will provide services to men, women, boys, and girls IDPs, returnees and host communities in Kajo-Keji in Central Equatorial State, Budi and Kapoeta South in Eastern Equatorial State and Aweil West in Northern Bar Ghazal. Besides ALIGHT having presence in the selected areas, Kajo-Keji county where ALIGHT has its major humanitarian response is reported to receive the highest number of returnees in the past six (6) months as per UNHCR/RRC spontaneous return monthly bulletins of October 2021. ALIGHT, as the deep field focal point for UNOCHA in Kajo-Keji has funding support from BHA/USAID, UNHCR, WFP and UNICEF for GBV, WASH, general protection, and Nutrition. Services targeting IDPs, returnees and host community. Similar responses are on-going in selected counties of Budi and Kapoeta South in Eastern Equatoria for GBV and WASH. In Northern Bar Ghazal, ALIGHT is operating ten nutrition centers in Aweil West and one women center in Aweil Center with extensive outreach activities in the outlying villages. WASH has strengthened integration by working with women and adolescent girls on operation and maintenance of WASH facilities and promoting hygiene at the centers.
The WASH sector is responsible for the development, operation, and maintenance of WASH facilities in these centers. Covid 19 pandemic has brought challenges including limited community interactions. ALIGHT has been able to work with the community to introduce and encourage practices that limit the possibility of Covid- 19 spread. These include regular hand washing and social distancing. Use of masks during training has been enforced.

1 - Needs

According HNO 2022, ten years after independence and three years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continues to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. Their situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference. Public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 and climatic shocks has resulted in dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to education and water, sanitation and hygiene and health services. Protection concerns remain high, with people impacted by violence having limited access to justice and the rule of law. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, needs humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. Continued conflict and instability in the country combined with flooding have resulted in large-scale internal and cross-border displacement. At the same time, limited improvements in some areas have prompted some people to spontaneously return. In addition, the government, with the support of some humanitarian agencies - has facilitated returns to certain areas. Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. At least 90,000 returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. Conflict and economic shocks, as well as other household-level stressors, for example, gender based violence (GBV) and poor water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) services, are some of the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the country. In 2022, an estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May-July) as shocks appear to be intensifying. There are 13 counties with extreme levels of food insecurity in 2022, compared to 6 in 2021. In addition, an estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2022. Humanitarian access to essential services, including health care, education, water and sanitation, as well as protection and legal services, remains a challenge in an already complex context. Between January and December 2021, 591 reported humanitarian access incidents were recorded. These ranged from violence against humanitarian personnel and assets to operational interference. According to WASH Cluster Strategy 2022 2023,living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.9 million people were negatively impacted by inadequate access to clean water and sanitation and poor hygiene practices in 2021. A rough trend analysis anticipates a possible number of people in need1 (PIN) of 6 million per year for 2022 and 2023. The document also identified main drivers of WASH needs to include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites. An assessment coordinated by UNOCHA in the months of September/October 2021 in Kajo-Keji highlighted critical humanitarian needs including a large number of returnees and IDPs still struggling to settle and facing challenges of land ownership, lack of functional water points and overgrown vegetation in what was once their homes. Due to flooding in the northern parts of the country many cattle keepers have migrated into Eastern and Central Equatoria. This has created conflict between the cattle herders and the community who are mostly farmers.

2 - Project activities

Kajo-Keji County: ALIGHT is currently providing a range of humanitarian services to over 5000 IDPs in Logo in Liwolo Payam. This is part of the IDPs who were displaced from Korijo during the conflict that started in September 2020. Another smaller groups settled into the neighboring villages where ALIGHT continues to provide lifesaving services like rehabilitation of hand pump operated boreholes, providing sanitation tools to enable them to construct their own latrines and provide hygiene kits to the most vulnerable amongst them. ALIGHT is working with the RRC in Kajo-Keji Central and ROSS in areas under opposition administration to extend GBV outreach services to families that have settled in these areas after fleeing Korijo IDP sites. In this proposal ALIGHT plans Water Supply and quality testing and treatment: this includes • Rehabilitation of 8 more boreholes with hand pumps in settled areas of Logo, Pure and Merwa. 15 boreholes are planned for rehabilitation in Kajo-Keji central host community settlement using contractors who will be identified from the area. • Ensure regular chlorination of water points and provision of aqua tabs for use at households. Budi County: • ALIGHT has been supporting the County water department in Budi in operation and maintenance of 15 hand pumps operated boreholes. This will be continued to ensure access to safe water. In Kapoeta South ALIGHT proposes: • Rehabilitation of 5 boreholes and continued support through provision of spare parts and hand pump tool kits for repair and maintenance of hand pumps. Aweil West County: • Recovery, rehabilitation, and protection of at least 10 boreholes located in flood areas. Protection will include building of embarkments and sandbagging • Provision of water treatment reagents to people affected by floods • Provide new training/refresher training for Hand Pump Mechanics to provide repair and maintenance to hand pumps. • Conduct water quality testing including water chlorination and bacteriological testing, using water testing devices and supplies. Sanitation Activities: • Construct a total of 300 household latrines, which are gender separated and safely accessed and equipped with hand washing facilities in IDP/returnee in new settlements in Kajo-Keji central and Liwolo Payam, Aweil West, Kapoeta South and Budi through provision of plastic slabs, tarpaulin and local woods, and using sanitation groups to support with digging of pits and constructing of super structure. • Establish, train and support Sanitation Groups in IDP settlement in Kajo-Keji, Aweil West and in Budi communities, each group consists of 10 members (5M,5F). • Form Sanitation Groups to mobilize and organize cleaning campaigns. Hygiene Promotion: • Co-create the household and community level activities with the beneficiaries using the WHO’s Participatory Hygiene and Sanitation Transformation (PHAST) method. • Distribute hygiene kits including sanitary reusable pads for the most vulnerable adult women and adolescent girls in IDP settlement in Kajo-Keji IDP and host community in Budi, Kapoeta South and Aweil West • Distribute WASH NFI kits to communities showing high prevalence of GAM and high WASH vulnerabilities in Kajo-Keji IDP and host community in Budi, Kapoeta South and Aweil West • Training 10 Hygiene Promoters (5M,5F) in IDP camps in Kajo Keji , (5M,5F) in Budi and Kapoeta South and Aweil West • Establish, train and support Women Hygiene Committees each committee consists of 10 female. • Conduct hygiene promotion education campaigns activities in IDP, returnee and host community camps and settlements. • Undertake promotional activities in Kapoeta South, Budi, Kajo-Keji and Aweil West to prevent cholera outbreak due to flooding and poor hygiene practices. • Support to GBV and Nutrition Centers Technical design, supervision of construction, operation and maintenance of all WASH infrastructures within GBV and Nutrition Centers operated by ALIGHT.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ALIGHT uses beneficiary feedback mechanism as a monitoring tool to gauge beneficiary satisfaction with our services, where possible modify the project objectives and activities to best meet the needs of the beneficiary, to ensure maximum reach and also track any malpractices among people responsible for resource management. The affected people have the possibility to contact the field staff on daily basis to describe their needs. Further they can talk with specific Sector leaders in the camps, who are in contact with Camp management and ALIGHT field staff. ALIGHT conducts continuous Monitoring where the beneficiaries can express critics and concerns about their satisfaction with the project implementation. Further anonymous Mechanisms, such as Feedback Boxes on central places in the camps and communities are used. ALIGHT is eager to improve any feedback mechanisms, so the beneficiaries are able to articulate their needs independent from aid organizations. ALIGHT developed Feedback Mechanisms in all areas of operations to allow community members to voice their concerns about project design, implementation and evaluation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ALIGHT will be keen on the gender considerations and the unique needs for people with disabilities so that the feedback mechanisms are sensitive and responsive to their special needs. ALIGHT is fully aware that people with disabilities are the most knowledgeable about their own needs and are the best source of information on what works best for them. The impact of this project is likely to affect men, women, boys and girls differently and efforts will be made to ensure that their needs, priorities, suggestions and capacities are considered during project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation to avoid perpetuating gender inequalities and promote equality

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ALIGHT works to minimize the risks to vulnerable populations in its water and sanitation programs by developing an in-depth understanding of the community dynamics in relation to individual and group interests and sensitivities such as ethnicity, religious beliefs, economic status etc., in order to ensure equitable access for all. Through its WASH technical designs, ALIGHT strives to reduce the risks from exposure to GBV, create safe and friendly environments for women and children, and make the facilities accessible to individuals with special needs. ALIGHT adheres to the set of guidelines that are an intrinsic part of our program design paradigm. These include the IASC/ Global Protection Cluster Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action, WASH Thematic Area Guide (TAG), and the Handicap International guidelines on design of interventions for people with disability. Illustrative actions ALIGHT takes to ensure protection and gender are mainstreamed in WASH programming include: • Separate communal latrines for women and men constructed at suitable locations where health teams will provide their services regularly. • Positioning of latrines at visible locations in the village to discourage GBV perpetrators to commit act of violence in clear view of the community, while at the same time, ensuring dignified access to women, adolescent girls and other users through installation of partition screens between latrines used by men/ boys and by women/ girls, or positioning the block for women/ girls separately from the block for men/ boys where more suitable. • Solar lights and strong door locking system at each latrine cubicle. • ALIGHT communal latrine design includes positioning of baskets inside the latrine cubicles/ blocks designated for women and girls to dispose sanitary items in dignity, with clean water jerry cans positioned inside

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

As it is clearly stated in the WASH Cluster strategic document of 2022-2023,community engagement in WASH in South Sudan is a crucial dynamic process connecting thecommunity and stakeholders so that people affected by the crises have more control over theresponse and its impact on them. It is a priority for the WASH Cluster. Effective engagementlinks communities and response teams to maximize community influence to reduce public healthrisks, provide appropriate, accessible services, improve programme quality and establishaccountability. It explores the capacity and willingness of the community to manage andmaintain WASH system.Alight commits itself to collecting gender specific data on vulnerable populations. Our data collection tools are designed and developed in consultation with affected community members to capture robust data that can be disaggregated with respect to sex, age, ethnicity, female-headed households and geographic area when applicable. Alight will use a mixed methods approach to data collection that allows triangulation to ensure validity. All data collected by ALIGHT on its project beneficiaries is Sex and Age-Disaggregated (SADD) to ensure that ALIGHT’s responses appropriately and effectively meet the needs of vulnerable affected women, men, boys and girls. Data is collected on a routine basis in consultation with community members using the following approaches: Key informant interviews, Separate Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) for men and women, Post distribution monitoring 2 weeks after distribution activities are completed, with a focus on hygiene kit, waste management and hygiene promotional material distributions, Direct observation, Survey and Feedback and complaint mechanisms. To ensure personal information is documented and stored in an ethical manner, ALIGHT will introduce a secure GBV Information Management Service (GBVIMS) and continue to utilize established case management tools. ALIGHT will also analyze GBV data to monitor trends and to inform GBV programming design.
