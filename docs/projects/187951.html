Project Summary

The provision of Sexual and Reproductive Health services is essential in humanitarian setting, it is an effective approach for preventing deaths, diseases, and disabilities associated and resulted from unintended pregnancy, obstetric complications, sexual and other forms of gender-based violence, HIV infection, and a range of reproductive disorders. UNFPA jointly with partners, will ensure the provision of comprehensive SRH services in humanitarian settings.
The intervention primarily targets women, girls in reproductive age, youth and adolescents, and men and boys. Sudan HNO 2021 indicates total people in need at 8.1 million, health sector target is 5.7 million. UNFPA will target 1.6 million with more than 80% are women and girls in reproductive age. The geographical coverage follows the HNO identified locations with severity ranking three and above.
All SRHR intervention will be implemented simultaneously through coordinated actions with all relevant partners. The planned interventions prioritize and aim reducing SRH mortalities and morbidities through: timely provision of Emergency Obstetric care and save delivery services, timely referral to lifesaving services, support the availability of Family Planning services, improving Clinical management of rape for survivors of SGBV, availing HIV and STIs prevention and response, improving Antenatal and postnatal care, Post abortion care, and other SRH services. These will be achieved through strengthening the capacity of SRH care providers with emphases of midwifery services, timely availability of lifesaving SRHR supplies, timely deployment of temporary clinics and mobile clinics, supporting the referral system, and demand creation through awareness raising and educational activities. Maternal Morbidities such as Obstetric fistula will be given especial attention by UNFPA.
The implementation of activities will be conducted through NGOs and Government partners with the strong technical support from UNFPA, including maintaining the field presence.

1 - Needs

The Health sector Humanitarian Needs Overview for the year 2022, indicates 9.2 million individuals expected to be in need of humanitarian assistance and basic health service in Sudan which includes 2.2 million women and girls at reproductive age. Reference made to MISP calculator 2019; around 290,000 pregnant women are estimated to be in need for obstetric care and access for safe vaginal delivery. Around 88,000 pregnant women are estimated to either experience obstetric complication or require a caesarean section. It is also estimated that total number of 44,000 cases of sexual violence who will seek care during 2022. Sudan is characterized with a high maternal mortality rate (295 per 100,000 life births as per 2017 UN estimates). In reference to the 2020 Maternal death surveillance report, the most prevalent cause was Obstetric hemorrhage (33.1%), followed by hypertensive disorders (13.1%), then sepsis (12.8%). Moreover, it confirmed that (62.0%) of maternal deaths had no ANC before, and (68.2%) were attributed to limited accessibility (delay at home, or delay in reaching health facility). According to the 2021 multi sectorial needs assessment MSNA, only 13.4% of women across the country had their deliveries in health facilities during 2021 with 40% of the home deliveries were not attended by a qualified healthcare person. Significant gap in coverage with Basic emergency neonatal and obstetric care services is observed, with less than 37% of the need.Gaps in provision of CMR services are identified nationwide through the regular CMR mapping, at both supplies, and trained cadres, the latest mapping indicated less than 20% coverage.According to figures by the ministry of health, there is a very low ratio of health cadres per 1000 population, with 82% gaps from the recommended targets by WHO. Midwifery cadres as well were not classified as skilled cadres in view of the duration of the pre service training. During 2021 the availability of emergency medicines declined steadily to reach 43%. COVID19 case fatality ratio in Sudan stayed at 7.5% recording one of the highest globally. COVID-19 vaccination coverage is standing at around 2% of the population. Sudan remains prone to various risks and hazards such as floods, localized armed clashes and inter communal violence, displacement, climatic shocks and hazards, disease outbreaks, food security, malnutrition and protection risks. The deteriorating economic status worsened the situation. The protracted humanitarian crisis in Sudan continues to impact the already fragile health system and is reducing the capacity to provide basic health services and respond to the multiple emergencies affecting the country. The annual cycles of floods, disease outbreaks, civil unrest, coups, border conflicts, and ongoing economic crisis have further impacted the accessibility to health services.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Support the SRH coordination mechanism at states and national levels to ensure the coordinated provision and expansion of SRH response, ensuring integration of SRH within the existing health cluster and national mechanisms, as well as within the GBV subsector, and other relevant secters. 2. Strengthening national/ state contingency and preparedness focusing on Minimum Initial Service Package on RH in Crisis (MISP) and scaling up to comprehensive SRH, beside proper planning. This includes a) reflection of SRH within planning and preparedness phase, b) capacity building of health care providers on relevant lifesaving SRH services such as EMONC, FP, STI management, Clinical Management of Rape survivors and psycho-social support, etc. c) prepositioning of lifesaving SRH equipment and supplies, d) establishing a roving team for provision of lifesaving SRH services, and others. 3. Support referral system for SRH emergencies including maternal and neonatal emergencies, and survivors of sexual violence, through enhancement of locally initiated system, deployment of temporary mechanism as well as supporting community led initiatives, 4. Equipping health facilities that provide lifesaving and essential SRH services (delivery rooms, maternity wards, blood transfusion unit and theaters, outpatient and emergency rooms) 5. Establishment of integrated SRH temporary and mobile teams at states with fragile context, including procurement and deployment of mobile/temporary clinics/hospitals/PHC. 6. Deployment of roving SRH care providers and teams to areas with limited capacities. 7. Prepositioning and timely distribution of lifesaving reproductive health medicines and supplies, micronutrients, equipment and supplies. 8. Support transition to comprehensive SRH services starting from contingency and preparedness phases, 9. Support demand creation and awareness raising on reproductive health seeking behavior including addressing Youth/ adolescent RH needs focusing on their specific needs and vulnerabilities in humanitarian settings.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

UNFPA aims to make a positive contribution in the area of environmental impact mitigation. It’s work on sexual and reproductive health helps promote resilience among vulnerable populations. Access to voluntary family planning, maternal health care and quality education are key ways to empower vulnerable women and young people, making them better equipped to prepare for and respond to environmental -related crises. At the procurement, level the organization works with suppliers (International and national levels) to implement sound environmental impact provisions.Manufacturers working with UNFPA have active and documented waste-water programme and have agreed to reduce raw material usage. UNFPA also produced and disseminates a guide for safe disposal and management of unused unwanted commodities. Throughout the project, planning and implementation process UNFPA closely monitors and set the mitigation measures that needed to avoid, reduce or compensate for environmental impacts to enhance the environmental and community benefits of the proposal.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through all project steps, starting from planning, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation, the following principles will be adhered to 1. Avoid causing harm: UNFPA and actors will ensure that service and assistance delivery preserves the physical integrity of individuals and communities, their dignity. These packages have already been adapted to the context of Sudan, and are culturally appropriate and minimizes any harmful and unintended negative consequences. 2. Equality: UNFPA and the actors will ensure that assistance and services will be provided according to needs and not any other determinants such as ethnicity or nationality, in good quantity, within safe and easy to reach locations, are known by the affected individuals and are accessible by all various groups including disabled individuals. 3. Accountability to bene ciaries: UNFPA and the actors will ensure that the affected individuals and communities are engaged in the measurement of the quality of interventions serving them, including mechanisms for suggestions and complaints from the population. 4. Participation and Empowerment: Foster inclusive participation to decision-making processes, support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and empower themselves

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability is core in the UNFPA response and improved through continuous consultation with the affected population on implementation modalities as well as to their satisfaction with services provided. UNFPA is an active member of the national PSEA technical and coordination team and leads the survivor support task team and together is working with the team members to ensure that GBV referral mechanisms are accessible to survivors of PSEA. UNFPA will equip implementing partners and targeted communities with information regarding the PSEA, and how to report and access services including health, legal and psychosocial support services. UNFPA’s partners and beneficiaries play an active role in the decision-making process in regards to the planning, implementing, and evaluating of activities. This is done by conducting a focus group discussion with women and girls in women centers. The project will support the development of self-protection capacities and aim at improving beneficiaries’ daily lives through improved formal and informal protection mechanisms. This is considered the most effective way of tackling gender inequality as the root cause of GBV.
