Needs

Humanitarian needs in Gaza continue to deepen, with the recurrent escalations of violence exacerbating the effects of almost a decade of Israeli blockade, rendering Gaza increasingly unlivable place. Lack of electricity and fuel has resulted in power outages lasting 18 hours per day, with some areas of Gaza almost entirely disconnected. The electricity crisis has impacted the availability of clean water supply through the network which reaches most homes for just a few hours every 3-5 days, Women and children in rural areas bear the brunt of securing water in their households. The rapid assessment conducted by the WASH cluster in August 2017 has already found that the energy crisis has reduced the available water supply from 84 liters per capita per day to only about 54 liters per capita per day. Further, desalination plants are functioning at only 15 per cent of their capacity. The shortening or suspension of sewage treatment cycles has led to increased levels of pollution and contamination of groundwater sources and the Mediterranean Sea, leaving over 90% of water sources too contaminated for human consumption and more than 60% of the sea around Gaza contaminated with untreated sewage. On the other hand the health system in Gaza is under stress due to various factors including dysfunctional equipment, shortage of medical supplies and drugs all compounded by the energy crisis. Hospitals have been struggling to operate due to electricity cuts resulting in increased reliance on fuel powered generators l to maintain essential services. These services require a constant supply of electricity include patients being treated in intensive care; new-born babies in neonatal units; patients requiring emergency surgery; dialysis patients treated for kidney failure; and those needing emergency care. More than 4,800 patients in Gaza daily require access to lifesaving or life-sustaining health care that requires a constant supply of electricity. Of these, at least 300 are connected to life-saving medical machines such as ventilators, dialysis machines, incubators and anesthetic machines, where disruption or electricity cut-out puts patients at immediate risk of brain damage or death (OCHA, Sep. 2018). Fuel is also required for waste removal vehicles to undertake proper solid waste management. Waste water facilities and desalination plants are dependent on generators and scarce fuel, and sewage pumps are often unable to function due to the lack of electricity.

Activities

UNRWA will continue to provide critical emergency support for WASH interventions in Gaza, both directly, through solid waste management in camps, and indirectly, through public service providers. UNRWA will remove and safely dispose of solid waste from unofficial dumping sites in and near camps and in areas densely populated by refugees during emergencies. UNRWA will provide emergency fuel to maintain vital environmental health services, according to priorities identified by the Cluster and in consultation with other stakeholders, including CMWU and communities. However, the final percentage use per service will depend on several external factors, such as duration of electricity cuts (and subsequent use of generators), or flooding, which may necessitate the running of storm water pumps. UNRWA will provide approximately 270,000 litres of fuel/month for operating WASH municipal facilities such as water wells, water tanks boosters, desalination water plants, and sewage pump stations, waste treatment plants (138 municipal & 9 UNRWA WASH facilities). Furthermore, UNRWA will assist the municipalities and Joint service councils (30 utilities) with 110,000 litres /month of fuel to operate the fleet of solid waste management for primary, secondary solid waste collections as well as the machineries of final solid waste disposal at landfill sites (3 sites). UNRWA will ensure beneficiaries participation in the design and review of activities. To maintain current levels of essential health services and operational ability by providing support, in form of fuel supply, UNRWA will provide approximately 570,000 litres of fuel/month for public health services such as hospitals, emergency medical services and primary health centres including UNRWA. UNRWA will coordinate with other agencies and public utilities (PWA) throughout implementation. Identification of emergency priorities will be carried out based on demonstrated needs as identified by local WASH committees, UNRWA, CMWU, PWA and solid waste actors in consultation with UNRWA’s Protection Unit and other relevant actors to ensure the most vulnerable areas are prioritized. Activation of emergency responses will be coordinated with the WASH cluster, and UNRWA will monitor and control the activities through regular on site visits by its sanitation engineers, registration of fuel and disinfectant quantities provided to each installation, and logbook on fuel usage
