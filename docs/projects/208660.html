1 - Needs

The decade of conflict has had a devastating humanitarian consequence in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states, however, in 2023, the security incidence and fatalities has improved compared to the peak of the conflict in 2014/15, though, conflict and violence continue unabated In some part of the region. Therefore, humanitarian needs remain high. With the state Government commitment to close all camps in the state by 2024, the closure may rush internally displaced persons (IDPs) back to areas not fully stabilized. Without adequate planning, these returnees risk facing a shortage of resources and potential security threats, further exposing them to more risks. As seen in 2022, many of the relocated IPDs became homeless, scattered across the host communities and limited access to essential services. The country is grappling with a critical economic situation, primarily driven by a surge in inflation affecting both food and non-food items. The removal of the fuel subsidy on May 29, 2023, and the subsequent liberation of the national currency, the naira, have further intensified these challenges. According to the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) data from November 2023, the inflation rate has reached an alarming 27.33%, marking the highest in two decades. This economic turmoil has profound humanitarian consequences, notably in the form of displacement. Approximately 2.3 million internally displaced persons (IDPs) are now contending with precarious health conditions, a lack of reliable income sources, and intermittent access to humanitarian aid2. The removal of subsidies and currency fluctuations have compounded the difficulties faced by both consumers and businesses, contributing to the widespread economic strain. In essence, the confluence of surging inflation, the removal of the fuel subsidy, and currency challenges has created a multifaceted crisis, impacting the lives of millions and underscoring the urgent need for humanitarian interventions.

2 - Activities

YOBE STATE: ACF proposes to reach 3000HHs (18,000 individuals) in Yobe state with the following proposed breakdown: Fika 1500 HHs (9000 individuals) and Machina: 1500HHs (9000 individuals) LGAs of Yobe state. The vulnerable households shall be reached with IGA and unconditional cash transfer. BORNO STATE: Action against hunger proposes to reach 10000 households (600000 individuals) in Borno state with food assistance during the lean season and considering the camp closure with the following proposed breakdown: Monguno (2000), Mobbar (1500), Nganzai (1500), Magumeri (1500), Konduga (1500), Kaga (1000), Mafa (1000) and Gubio(1000) LGAs. The same beneficiaries will receive cash transfer aligned with the federal government cash assistant outside the lean season. The vulnerable households shall be subsequently graduated into different livelihood activities. About 3000HH (18000individuals) will be selected from Konduga, Kaga, Mafa and Monguno LGAs considering market availability of the area.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The proposed intervention shall take into consideration gender concerns by prioritizing the minimum standards of protection and gender to ensure referral of any protection/GBV concerns received through the FCRM mechanism. The collection of age and sex-segregated data during targeting and registration will ensure that the intended beneficiaries are included and supported with no discrimination on any basis. The project activities shall be shortened in duration to allow women to have time to care for their babies and engage in other productive work. Provision shall be made for child/baby friendly spaces in locations and venues of demo sites, meetings, trainings and distribution points. The distributions shall take place near to the locations of the beneficiary homes to avoid having the PLW walking long distances. To ensure the safety of beneficiaries especially women and girls, vendor locations shall be sited in areas considered to be safe by the community and separate lines for PWDs, elderly and most vulnerable PLW made for priority service. To diffuse possible gender tensions, women and men shall be represented during community meetings and joint decision making on the assistance shall be promoted through sensitization of men and women beneficiaries. Awareness messages shall be tailored in a gender sensitive way that empowers women without causing GBV and household tensions. ACF will ensure that all indicators that could have gender aspects will be age, disability and sex-disaggregated in data collection, analysis, and reporting. To ensure safe and accountable programming, ACF will ensure protection/GBV issues are well assessed, prevented and mitigated both among beneficiaries and among staff. Protection risks at the community level include possible tension between host population and IDPs. ACF project activities shall ensure that beneficiaries are not put at risk due to movement of project beneficiaries from one location to the other. Targeting of beneficiaries will ensure strict inclusion of only households that are residents in the location of intervention. Risk of community transmission of COVID-19 may add to the vulnerabilities of people in the locations of intervention. To mitigate this risk, ACF shall conduct sensitization to beneficiaries and community stakeholders on the pandemic and promote the use of COVID-19 protection protocols such as physical distancing, the use of masks, handwashing and sanitizers by beneficiaries, vendors, staff and stakeholders. The collection of age gender-segregated data will ensure the inclusion of women, children, the elderly and PLW and efforts will ensure that no discrimination on any basis. Furthermore, the project will mainstream protection throughout the project cycle by ensuring that protection principles guide the humanitarian assistance ACF is providing. This includes: ● Ensuring access for affected populations to humanitarian assistance based on needs and promoting non-discriminatory principles (Action Against Hunger Charter) within the program, which is also explicitly outlined in the Code of Conduct, which is signed by all Action Against Hunger staff. ● Monitoring access of the most vulnerable population to humanitarian assistance. In the process of validating target criteria, the M&E team is encouraged to crosscheck lists and minimize exclusion errors. In addition, ACF will report internally when check points or blockades are preventing humanitarian assistance. Special measures will be taken to ensure equality of access for affected people in remote or inaccessible areas. ● ACF will reinforce the concept of “right to receive humanitarian assistance” within its own staff and when communicating with stakeholders (NEMA, SEMA, etc.). ● ACF will focus on IDPs, returnees and host communities promoting the principle of impartiality. ● Assistance to IDPs will not be tied to special legal status, but encourage local authorities to recognize and identify the vulnerability and need.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACF will ensure that all formal and informal communications from persons of concern, both positive and negative, inform protection, assistance and solutions to programming, and that corrective action taken as appropriate. To achieve this, ACF will: • Establish and maintain effective feedback systems (including comments, suggestions, and complaints), using a variety of communication channels that are accessible to all persons of concern and that are appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback; • Allocate human and financial resources to ensure that feedback from persons of concern is systematically collected, acknowledged, assessed, referred, and responded to in a timely, confidential, and effective manner; • Collaborate with partners in feedback referral and response processes (wherever appropriate).Action Against Hunger (ACF), ensures that participation, feedback and complaints mechanism are integrated in all strategies including Food Security and Livelihood sector, plans and programs. It also provides a range of rapid and accessible channels of communication with affected populations and use them to inform affected populations about procedures and processes that affect them so that they are able to make informed decisions and choices. This is in line with IASC principles and commitment. ACF considers sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers as an act of gross misconduct and therefore perpetrators will face termination with immediate effect. Irrespective of any form of consent, if the sexual activity with children (anyone under the age of 18 years despite the customary or legal definition in the country) by humanitarian workers is prohibited regardless of explanation. Moreover, sexual exploitation as a mode of exchange for benefits are completely prohibited not only with beneficiaries but also within humanitarian workers.
