Project Summary

The present project aim to provide an improvement in WASH and Nutrition services to IDPs, returnees and host communities in Borno and Yobe states North east Nigeria. The project is timely given particular consideration to the plans of government to close all camps within the shortest possible time and open up normal life activities across all villages, towns and cities across the BAY states.
In the areas of WASH, in order to save the lives of those affected by the over a decade conflict in Borno and Yobe states, the proposed project will focus on providing WASH interventions and capacity building of stakeholders in affected areas in close coordination with state and local authorities. A total of 64300 men, women, boys and girls will benefit from the intervention through improved access to safe water supply, improved hygiene practices and distribution of basic hygiene kits. Access to water supplies will be targeted in the most affected communities through water trucking, construction and repairs on boreholes with hand pump, construction and rehabilitation of boreholes with solar pumping system, construction of new water points, and chlorination of water at household level, water storage, water sources and distribution of tool kits in targeted areas. Improved hygiene practices will be achieved by training community-based volunteers/mobilizers, construction of emergency latrines, rehabilitation of existing latrines, latrine dislodging and construction of hand washing facilities close to latrines. Environmental sanitation will be developed through the improvement of drainage systems, training of WASH committees on waste management, distribution of tool kits, massive hygiene promotion campaigns in communities, schools and TLS, provision of hand washing facilities in Schools, public places and TLS. Furthermore, a basic hygiene kit (bucket, jerry can, soap, tippy tap and ORS) will be distributed according to the needs to the most affected households. Gender main streaming and protection will be considered as a high priority during the project period.
In addition, this WASH programme will serve as a complementing support to exclusive breast feeding practices through strengthening community awareness, training, orientation on the role of traditional and religious leaders, and other activities to promote exclusive breast feeding (EBF). At community level, the project will promote, protect and support optimal breastfeeding practices through mother-to-mother support groups. The wider community will indirectly benefit from a range of activities, including awareness raising sessions, resilience building activities to support communities’ resilience to violence and promote a conducive environment for effective Health and well-being of the selected communities’ beneficiaries.
Finally, through this WASH project WASSI will also support peace initiatives through awareness-raising to sensitize the wider community to support the social cohesion and peaceful coexistence. The aim is to reach the newly identified direct beneficiaries over the 12-months project period through awareness-raising tools such as EBF and HP practices-focused IEC and communication materials to be disseminated through the programme will help reach a much wider audience.

1 - Needs

According to UNOCHA’s Global Humanitarian Overview; in 2023, a record 339 million people will need humanitarian assistance and protection – a significant increase from 274 million people at the beginning of 2022. North Eastern Nigeria has been under conflict between the government forces and non-state armed groups (NSAGs) for over a decade. The insurgency has resulted in the displacement of over 2 million individuals who found shelter in either IDPs camps or host communities. North-east Nigeria remains one of the world’s largest humanitarian crises, with an estimated 8.3 million people needing assistance in 2023. As at November 2022, there are 857,821 IDPs in Camps, 1154840 IDPs in Host Communities, and 1525636 returnees in North East Nigeria. Several LGAs in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa are affected by the conflict displaced or currently accommodating millions of internally displaced persons. In Borno state, there are 820062 IDPs in Camps, 820346 IDPs in Host Communities, and 607388 returnees. In Yobe state, there are 17603 IDPs in Camps, 133764 IDPs in Host Communities, and 262970 returnees.After 12 years of unstable struggles in the Northeast (NE) Nigeria working situation is ever complex and challenging for both government and other agencies. Humanitarian action is characterized by increasing access constraints, humanitarian corridor is becoming smaller, and growing needs in various hotspots. As a result, humanitarian partners’ ability to deliver lifesaving assistance to populations in hard-to-reach areas, is drastically hampered. Thus, there are more than 1.1 million people in areas currently inaccessible to international humanitarian actors. In 2022, access to safe water, dignified sanitation and hygiene awareness was impacted by heavy flooding, camp closures, and the movement of IDPs and returnees. This difficult operating environment increases the risk of further deterioration in both food insecurity and nutrition situations and multiply the number of emergency hotspot locations. In 2023, North-east Nigeria remains one of the world’s largest humanitarian crises, with an estimated 2.61 million people needing WASH assistance. According December 2022 OCHA report ‘Floods have affected more than 4.4 million people across Nigeria since July, with over 2.4 million people displaced’. Hence the need for intervention in this region. The armed insurgency by the non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in North East Nigeria has resulted in large-scale migration, disruption of agricultural activities, deterioration of the nutritional status of children and women, and the devastation of an already fragile public infrastructure, including health, food security, and water services.The government in the region are concerned with the people living in camps, their main concern is that this may lead to serious food security issues. The BAY states are always agitating for more support from international partners and donor agencies. This particular project’s main targeted populations are living in some LGAs of Yobe (Damaturu, Bursari, Gujba and Gulani) and in Borno (Bama and Konduga). Water Supply and Sanitation Initiative (WASSI) has operational presence in the mentioned areas in the field of WASH and Nutrition (Multi sectorial approach) in these locations. The organisation has recently implemented some emergency projects in Bama and Karasuwa with funding from UNICEF (PD Ref. No.: NIR/PCA2022314/HPD2022321). The proposed intervention has objectives to serve as part of UN’s preparedness action plan for responding to conflict-crisis situations which can be activated when such situations arise. It is expected that when activated, the project focuses on delivering lifesaving results in WASH and Nutrition/Health in the target locations across Borno and Yobe states.

2 - Activities

The proposed project is planned to achieve WASH strategy objectives 01 of Humanitarian Response Plan; ensure effective, sufficient and continuous provision of life-saving water, sanitation and hygiene services for people in needs in BAY states through the following activities.i. Every person in need has safe and equitable access to a sufficient quantity of water for domestic needs, as per the sector’s standardii. Every person in need has safe and dignified access to improved sanitation facilities, as per the sector’s standardsiii. Every person in need benefit from basic genderand age-sensitive hygiene items including top-ups as per sector’s standardiv. Provision/Increase of WASH facilities in public places (schools, markets, health care facilities) in host communities.In line with the above WASH activities, the following indicators are targeted• 5400 people having access to emergency safe water facilities and services as per sector’s standards.• 3000 affected people have safe and equitable access to a sufficient quantity of water for domestic needs, as per the sector's standards• 2500 women and girls have safe water access after AAP/GBV risk analysis• 10000 affected people have water systems' functionality supported by sustained operation and maintenance services• 1000 affected people have safe and dignified access to improved sanitation facilities, as per the sector's standard• 200 women and girls have safe sanitation facilities access after AAP/GBV risk analysis• 25000 affected people benefit from community tailored gender and age-sensitive hygiene messages aiming at hygienic behaviour and practices as per the sector’s standards.• 1200 people assisted with WASH NFIs (hygiene kits, replenishment hygiene kits, WASH cholera kits, WASH Nutrition kits, sanitation kits and MHM kits)• 1000 cholera affected people with WASH kit provided at HH level

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

WASSI recognizes the different needs of children, pregnant and lactating mothers and thus the proposed project will be implemented in an inclusive and gender-sensitive manner prioritizing children, pregnant and lactating mothers’ participation in project activities and ensuring that the gender-specific issues related to children, pregnant and lactating mothers are addressed. Applying learned lessons from the phases of the previous project, WASSI found that most of the project participants were less privilege children, pregnant and lactating mothers since they were more likely to be malnourished during these hard times. WASSI will address gender-specific challenges with the adult stakeholders engaged in the project by targeting women to participate in project activities and ensuring gender-related topics are included in the topics of the programs. The project will involve all partners, volunteers and stakeholders in a PSEA training using WASSI's PSEA training guidelines and other standard guiding principles. In addition, the toll-free line will be open to all participants of the project and be open for report of PSEA related issues. The organisation will also collaborate with the protection cluster through it's protection department to make referrals for beneficiaries who require protection.As this project will address factors contributing to health and malnourishment of vulnerable children, pregnant women and lactating mothers, WASSI will work to ensure that all these children, pregnant and lactating women have a better health in their communities irrespective of their gender, ethnicity, religion, or any other basis used for marginalization. WASSI will also engage the relevant Ministries, Departments and Parastatals through the continuation and strengthening of the Project Advisory Committee (PAC). In addition, WASSI will work with the State Primary Health Care Management Agency and Rural Water Sanitation Agency, Ministry of Women Affairs (SMWASD), Ministry of Health and Ministry of Youths, sports, social and community development in the project implementation process to ensure that activities complement their work and that they are able to continue relevant activities beyond the scope of the project.
