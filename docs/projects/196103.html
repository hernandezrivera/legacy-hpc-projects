Project Summary

NRC aims to increase socio-economic access of both Rohingya and the host community youth and adolescents by emphasizing on creating self-resilience and livelihood opportunities using training as a key tool. Two modules of market driven training packages will be provided to the beneficiary which will help them to acquire updated skills that aligns with current market needs. The training modules will be continuously upgraded and reviewed through using secondary research thus modules will always be aligned with current market. The first module will focus largely on theoretical and conceptual learning and the second module will focus on practical skills acquisition of the learners. Subject matters such as literacy, numeracy, life skills and business skills will be incorporated along with the 2 modules. Key focus will be on female learners and people of diverse gender groups. NRC will focus on 10 camps. 3% of the overall beneficiaries will be targeted for including persons with disabilities who then will be served as project beneficiaries through referral system.
There will be trainings for the volunteers and staff specifically focused on inclusion to conduct outreach and referral pathways for persons with disabilities. The referrals will be documented and forwarded to appropriate service providers. On the other way, to materialise the efforts on inclusion and mainstreaming, identified learners with disabilities will be provided customized and appropriate learning pathways based on their needs where physical access also includes. Apart from these, NRC will also include awareness building and sensitization sessions for the inclusion of diverse age group and person with disabilities through including relevant topics in teacher trainings modules and community sessions. NRC will also provide business and employment skills through trainings for all beneficiaries keeping to the needs of the market. Content for this will be cater to the learners in consultation with partners. After the training the semi-skills learners will be put through a re-tooling process where they will get the opportunity to practice and further sharpen these skills and re-learn previously mistaken purposes.
Participants will also have the access to input in the Youth Working Group advisory meetings promoting youth empowerment through engagement. Youth led community clubs will be created to support this process and will also be promoted DRR activities when needs rise. Moreover, preparedness will also be a core regular part of this activity. Building social cohesion through learning and employment activates for both communities and advocacy initiatives will be integrated with overall education programming that will integrate education programme with NRC’s other core competencies. Education programme will also focus on self and collaborative welling exercise opportunities through community projects that ensure meaningful engagement within peers and community people.
NRC will implement the proposed activities with the coordination of other humanitarian actors and government as well. NRC will coordinate specifically with local and national government throughout the project cycle by taking feedback, supporting in different emergency situation through preparedness and preventive measures taken by government and involving them in the projects through meetings and field visits.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In the Rohingya Humanitarian Crisis, which affects over 1.3 million people, over 900,000 refugees and over 450,000 host communities in Bangladesh's Cox's Bazar district need assistance. In the fifth year of the crisis, there are still urgent needs in every area of humanitarian assistance. And perhaps most significantly, the majority of Rohingya are now totally dependant on aid and risky negative coping mechanisms. Adolescents and young adults make up nearly 200,000 of those impacted (JRP2021), and 89% of them lack access to any kind of formal education or training in practical skills. Boys report that they do not think that subjects are relevant to them or that they cannot prioritize education over pursuing the meager livelihood opportunities within the camps, and high rates of child marriage result in adolescent girls bearing caregiving responsibilities. Cultural barriers and the role of women and girls within the home also contribute to the gap between girls and boys accessing services. Therefore, in 2022, the Livelihoods and Skills Development Sector will place dedicated focus on the development of skills for the beneficiaries of the host community and refugees. Through the Youth Working Group and in close collaboration with the Skills Development Technical Working Group, the Sector will oversee the activities related to its skills development framework.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

NRC will continue to work closely with the CiC office, site management, and education sector and camp level focal points so that services can be tailored to reflect camp needs, sharing updates in order to avoid duplications and create synergies, including the continuous development of efficient and effective referral mechanisms. In addition, NRC`s presence and provision of services would enable NRC to understand the Livelihoods and Skills landscapes at large, protection risks, needs and gaps in services` provision in areas where it works. This information will be collected, analysed and used for evidence-based advocacy and further programme development and will also be shared externally with other actors and stakeholders within relevant coordination mechanisms through Livelihoods and Skills Sector, and Sub-Working Groups such as DRMWG, YWG and the Inter-Agency Advocacy Group as appropriate. Moreover, NRC will make available its global expertise in adoelscents and youth skills development initiatives and the experience gained in Bangladesh to technically support sectors and sector partners and relevant stakeholders on these thematic areas. In particular, NRC plans to conduct specialised technical assessments/researches on verified gaps and supports related to specific topics and technical areas, with special focus on adoelscents and youth related issues. These may include for example an assessment on youth needs, as well as related practices and barriers, for both refugees and host community beneficiaries.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Four Protection Mainstreaming principles – a) safety, dignity, no harm, b) meaningful access, c) accountability and d) participation & empowerment will be promoted throughout the project implementation; while the Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (hotline for complaints and feedback) will be constantly upgraded in line with the NRC CFM Policy, procedures and the NRC CFM Handbook. NRC has also engaged the PSEA Network to train camp volunteers during inductions, and NRC has a designated PSEA focal point for reports concerning NRC staff as part of needs assessment and risk analysis, project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluations.

3.2 - Gender

All activities and reports will include sex, age and disability disaggregated data (SADDD). Safe and Inclusive (SIP) programming principles and confidentiality will be promoted throughout the project implementation.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

To address the needs of the affected communities according to the sex and age disaggregation, gender and disability marker, targeted the individuals with wide range of criteria are included in the project. These includes the individuals specific focus on adolescents and youth, with majority focus on increasing female participation and engagement, of Adult Females (18-59 years), Adult Males (18-59 years) and unknown Sex and Age and Differently abled persons in forms of volunteers enagegemtn who acts as teachers, sensitization and engagement with guardians etc.

3.4 - Diversity

The contexts presents with specifics risks for women, girls, and persons with specific needs along persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority groups and gender diverse populations, continued to face heightened barriers in education services. Meaningful participation will be addressed the issue by inclusion of the diverse groups to increase participation of women, girls, and persons with specific needs along persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority groups and gender diverse populations through this project.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

In alignement with the Accountability to the Affected population (AAP) principles, targeted objectives will be achieved through the provision of services delivered by taking information sharing, communication and feedback mechanisms and transparency into place, while ensuring participation, egnagement and inclusion through gender diverse lences through out the humanitarian project cycle phases. Learning, feedback then wll be reflected through necesssary process and pathways cosnidering the contexts and needs in order to reflect accountability towards the beneficiaries.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

NRC is part of DRMWG and will continue to be an active participant within interagency disaster preparedness efforts for disaster and emergency preparedness responses and in addressing the challenges brought on by COVID-19, seasonal and climate related affects. In coordination with PERU through NRC's overall operation teams during any emergency to support prevention, mitigation, response and overall phases. Staff, volunteers will be part of emergency response team, coordinated by NRC's emergency response team, where the participants will receive emergency capacity building training. They will share disaster risk reduction and disaster preparedness messages at community level. Mitigation, Preparedness, Response, and Recovery measures as identified through overall emergency plan, and aligned with sector. Reduce, reuse, recycle concept will be included in project delivery to reduce environmental impact. Youth will be trained on the prepared/curated modules as per alignment with activities.
