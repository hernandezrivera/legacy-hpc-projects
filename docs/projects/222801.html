Project Summary

The program aims to provide high-quality protection services by adhering to GBV minimum standard guidelines and integrating GBV response, prevention, and risk mitigation strategies, along with Child Protection (CP) principles and sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services. Targeting high-risk and marginalized populations, it will reach 44,000 individuals in Camps 11, 12, 15, 16, and the Jaliapalong host community in Cox’s Bazar. CARE, in collaboration with Mukti Cox’s Bazar, operates seven Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) and one multipurpose centre, serving both camps and the host community. This program will continue in existing locations and expand based on rationalization and 2025 needs. The strategy is based on findings from the Australian Humanitarian Partnership (DFAT) end-line evaluation, CARE’s Rapid Gender Analysis, and CARE's expertise, adapted to address the needs of men, women, adolescents, persons with disabilities, and gender-diverse groups.
Comprehensive services will continue through WGSS, MPC, and outreach activities to address protection and GBV issues for women, men, children, and other at-risk groups in camps and the host community. A survivor-centred approach will prioritize the rights, needs, and safety of survivors. GBV case management services will include child sexual survivor support and multi-sectoral referrals, ensuring confidentiality and ethical data practices via GBVIMS. Integrated SRH services will provide clinical management of rape (CMR) and timely sexual violence response within 72 hours to support recovery and address sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) will promote resilience and aid healing.
The project will implement structural prevention approaches to change social norms and prevent GBV, using evidence-based curricula like EMAP, Girl Shine, SASA, and Social Analysis Action (SSA). Vocational training for women and girls, positive parenting, and community care initiatives will improve protection from violence. The Women Lead in Emergencies (WLiE) approach will empower women, removing barriers and creating leadership opportunities. Religious and local leaders will be engaged to facilitate cultural shifts, and men and boys will be involved to reduce violence.
Capacity building will include interagency GBV case management training, GBV minimum standards, CP guidelines, and multi-sectoral knowledge to enhance staff skills and well-being. The project will strengthen GBV response systems, adhering to international standards and boosting the capacity of community leadership and stakeholders. Non-protection actors in sectors like health, education, WASH, legal, and security (APBN/Police) will be trained in GBV prevention and response. Strengthen and build the skills of the volunteers by GBV guiding GBV principles and developing key safety messages protection measures.
Community-level awareness will be enhanced through strategies such as community-based outreach and peace and security campaigns. Engaging men, youth, and boys will be crucial to reducing the stigma around seeking GBV support. These strategies will support local initiatives, empowering the community to identify and solve local problems while influencing strategic decisions related to community safety and security. The project will ensure accessibility for marginalized groups, including people with disabilities and gender-diverse populations. It will also promote campaigns and joint initiatives, such as International Women’s Day, 16 Days of Activism, Mental Health Day, and Child Rights Day, to combat harmful practices. Infrastructure will be improved for accessibility, with accommodations, assistive devices, and rehabilitation services provided.
CARE will maintain strong coordination with entities like GBV SS, Protection sectors, GiHA, and relevant task teams to ensure harmonized, quality, and collaborative GBV services.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Rohingya crisis, now in its eighth year, remains one of the most significant humanitarian challenges in Asia, centred in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. The ongoing influx of people fleeing violence in Myanmar has further strained living conditions, with Bangladesh currently hosting approximately 1,010,298 Rohingya in Ukhiya and Teknaf upazilas including new arrivals of people. This population places immense pressure on local host communities, affecting 568,298 people. According to the ISNA 2024 report, 848,096 individuals are in need, including 20% girls, 21% boys, 18% women, 4% men, 2% older people, and 8% persons with disabilities.Heightened risks and ongoing violence, both within and surrounding the camps, have exacerbated protection concerns, particularly for women, girls, and marginalized groups. Fear of gender-based violence (GBV), compounded by inadequate lighting and unsafe conditions at night, restricts the mobility of women and girls, limiting their access to essential services. The ISNA 2024 findings reported that 46% of refugees feel unsafe while walking, especially at night in the camp. Cultural norms further intensify these barriers, making it difficult for women to travel alone or seek help. Additionally, marginalized groups, including gender-diverse populations, the elderly, and people with disabilities, face mobility restrictions that lead to underutilization of services. In this context, there is an urgent need for safe shelters for at-risk gender-diverse individuals, males and male survivors including gender-specific services, especially for urgent cases that require an immediate response mechanism. [Reference from GDP WG brief]The escalating conflict between violent groups has also increased protection risks, especially for children, who are vulnerable to abduction, forced recruitment, abuse, beatings, psychological abuse, and extortion. [Reference from UNICEF & child protection sub-sector] According to recent monitoring data, 68 documented incidents involving child rights violations were committed by armed groups subject children, particularly those aged 14-17(UNICEF/CRMM).Food insecurity remains a critical issue, disproportionately affecting single and female-headed households, who often resort to negative coping mechanisms. [ISNA 2024). Research highlights that 45% of married girls and young women experience GBV in their marital lives, often linked to child marriage, cultural practices, and socio-religious influences. (GAGE study). The ongoing armed conflict has intensified concerns over forced and child marriages, particularly when male family members are absent. Recent data shows that 74% of GBV cases involve intimate partner violence (IPV), with additional cases of physical assault (53%), psychological abuse (25%), and denial of resources (19%). [GBVIMS Q2 report]Health risks are also prevalent among married adolescent girls, who often face forced sexual initiation and unintended pregnancies, increasing the likelihood of complicated pregnancies and sexually transmitted infections, including HIV. Gender-diverse populations, meanwhile, have limited access to healthcare and psychosocial support, leading to unaddressed mental health concerns and exclusion from community decision-making processes.On a positive note, integrated services have shown effectiveness, as evidenced by the 91.1% of rape survivors who received timely clinical management of rape (CMR) services within 72 hours, leading to better recovery outcomes. This highlights the need for continued integrated care and training for healthcare providers in both SRH and GBV response. The 2024 ISNA report suggests that future livelihood interventions must include activities related to food assistance, such as agricultural and income-generating programs, to reduce dependency and enhance community resilience. GBV service providers, including volunteers, are also at risk concerns identified in the community and referred to GBV cases.(ISNA'24)

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will align with the humanitarian protection principles, and actions to respect equal dignity and be tailored to the specific protection needs of the most vulnerable Rohingya refugees and host community members. Throughout the project cycle, women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, and the LGBTQ population will be consulted through baseline and end-line assessments, regular focused group discussion (FGD) and community sessions. Special attention will be given to distribution points to ensure gender-sensitive arrangements.Protection risks will be monitored through timely service audits, and the project will train community volunteer networks in GBV and protection, including trafficking, child marriage and other security concerns. Awareness-raising activities will include the 16 Days of Activism campaign and the dissemination of IEC materials on PSEA and GBV Pocket Guide for a safe referral system. Additionally, CARE will conduct capacity-building training for (PERU) members. At the GBV sub-sector level, CARE, in collaboration with other agencies, will continue working with camp authorities and actors to ensure adherence to GBV referral pathway procedures and standards. To ensure the effective mainstreaming of protection, non-protection sectoral staff and volunteers will be trained in GBV principles, and handling cases using a 'do no harm' approach. This will require a fully functional and inter-operational community feedback mechanism that facilitates community-based solutions and enables rapid referral and response. Regular feedback and complaints will be collected, with necessary actions taken to ensure safe and dignified service delivery.This project will uphold PSEAH and child safeguarding policies by being sensitive to the local culture, gender, and age power dynamics and addressing child protection-related risks. In line with CARE’s localization strategy, the project will strengthen the institutional capacity of partners SEA process.

2.3 - Age & Disability

Preliminary results from a comprehensive survey using the Washington Group Questionnaire (short set) by ISNA 2024 indicate that at least 12% of people in the Rohingya communities have some form of disability (11% female and 13% male), 79.21% of people with disabilities face significant challenges in accessing humanitarian assistance, primarily due to their impairments, inaccessible service facilities, lack of accessible information, and limited representation and leadership opportunities.This project emphasizes the inclusion and active participation of people with disabilities in GBV programming, ensuring they have meaningful and equal access to services. The program uses a twin-track approach to disability inclusion, recognizing that disability impacts people of different ages and genders differently. It builds on previous experience in adapting physical infrastructure and GBV service facilities to be more accessible, particularly for women and children. To support access to protection services, people with disabilities in the project areas will be provided with assistive devices and reasonable accommodations. Equal access will be monitored through disability- and sex-disaggregated data collection.The project will establish Disability Support Committees (DSCs) and Self-Help Groups (SHGs), which will receive capacity-building training and be integrated into governance structures to promote equality and universal design principles. For instance, the Golden Citizen Card for persons with disability ( host community) will be promoted as a means of recognition, offering priority access to government and social services, health benefits, economic opportunities, and decision-making processes. The use of the Washington Group Questions will ensure comprehensive disability data disaggregation to better identify and address the needs of people with disabilities in both the camps and the host community.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure accountability to the affected population (AAP) in all interventions, CARE Bangladesh has implemented a robust Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) across all humanitarian projects in Cox’s Bazar. This system involvement will prioritise the needs in all stages of the project including design to implementation to effective feedback and response. CARE has ensured that all members of the affected community—regardless of sex, age, disability, race, caste, or colour—have equal access to this safe and responsive CFRM system. Practices related to CFRM channels are harmonized and standardized, with consolidated reports on feedback trends shared with the AAP Technical Working Group (TWG), donors, and relevant projects. CARE follows Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) for CFRM and uses various platforms and tools for data collection, including KOBO, ODK, MODA etc. Messaging is standardized and disseminated in four languages, available in both audio and visual formats to maximize accessibility. CFRM box will be established and accessible to all iGBV service facilities in WGSS and MPC. The project will continue to use established feedback mechanisms, such as feedback hubs, household visits, face-to-face communication, suggestion boxes, and hotline numbers to gather community complaints and feedback. It will also work to strengthen the capacity of volunteers and community leaders, including Majhis, religious leaders, women, youth, and persons with disability. Advocacy efforts will be maintained with the CiC office and community leaders to ensure complaints and feedback are referred to the appropriate channels.CARE will provide comprehensive training to staff, community-based volunteers and partner NGOs on CFRM protocols, emphasizing the importance of timely and respectful feedback resolution. MEAL team will conduct Post-Distribution Monitoring using appropriate methodologies and ensure that feedback is incorporated into project improvements.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

The project upholds CARE’s commitment to Prevention of Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment (PSEAH), ensuring a safe and accountable working environment for all gender groups, with a special emphasis on creating women-friendly and gender-equal service facilities. CARE’s Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy adheres to international standards, demonstrating a zero-tolerance approach to sexual exploitation and abuse and a strong commitment to addressing safeguarding issues. All CARE staff, partners, and volunteers will receive comprehensive orientation on PSEAH and Child Safeguarding policies.To effectively integrate PSEA into the project, CARE will ensure that any SEA allegations are properly investigated, and necessary actions are taken according to standard operating procedures (SOPs) for SEA complaint referrals within the refugee response framework. Reports and investigations will be conducted in a timely, fair, safe, confidential, and survivor-centered manner. CARE will uphold a mandatory reporting system for all allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse, in alignment with the CARE Safeguarding Policy and PSEA SOPs. The project will work to drive transformational change within social structures, reinforcing a zero-tolerance policy for sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse.All staff, volunteers, and partners will receive training on PSEAH using CARE and ISCG modules, incorporated PSEA messages throughout the program, culturally and gender sensitive. Project managers and leaders will actively work to prevent PSEA and maintain a safe environment. Disseminate PSEA hotline numbers, key messages, and complaint mechanisms to beneficiaries. Audio-visual materials will be used to enhance understanding and awareness of PSEA principles.The project will ensure that implementing partners are active members of Cox’s Bazar PSEA Network and PSEA activities will be implemented to align with the Cox’s Bazar PSEA Network’s priorities

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Climate change and natural disasters significantly impact women and girls, increasing their risk of violence and exacerbating gender-based inequalities. Due to traditional gender roles, women and girls often carry the burden of household responsibilities, managing limited resources and facing food scarcity, while also having limited influence in decision-making processes. This lack of awareness and preparedness leaves them particularly vulnerable to climate-related hazards, putting both lives and property at risk. To address these challenges and reduce the protection risks faced by women, this project will promote green skills cultivation near their homes, encourage homestead gardening using the CARE Climb project intervention and approach, such as hydroponic green skills, mushroom cultivation and vermicompost etc to support sustainable lifestyle practices. This project will also engage women in planting trees, and management of solid waste in the community. These initiatives will drive behaviour change, empower women, and create livelihood opportunities. By developing green skills, Rohingya women will gain consciousness, confidence, self-esteem, and aspirations, enabling them to make more sustainable choices and improve their quality of life.Although women's roles in homestead gardening and aquaculture production are often framed as 'supporting,' they are, in fact, critical 'performing' roles in addition to their extensive household duties. This project will work to elevate these roles and enhance women's leadership in disaster preparedness through the Women Lead in Emergencies (WLiE) groups. The project will provide training and capacity-building opportunities, empowering women to take active leadership and decision-making roles before, during, and after disasters. Community-women groups will be trained in disaster response including psychological first aid and early signs of disaster reduction of the risks of calamity.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

is project is dedicated to ensuring robust emergency preparedness and response to crises and natural disasters. We are committed to supporting search and rescue/DRR volunteers, GBV/Protection workers in the camps, and individuals and communities affected by sudden hazardous events, with a strong focus on their mental health and overall well-being. The project will lead the integration of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support Services (MHPSS) to build resilience before crises occur and strengthen the capacity of the Disaster Risk Management (DRM) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) sectors. The DRR staff and volunteers will be trained to GBV PG and provide Psychological First Aid (PFA) as first responders in the crisis. This will help reduce trauma and uphold the safety and dignity of survivors. An emergency fund will be secured to ensure rapid response and dignity kits will be distributed to support the mobility of women and girls while reducing protection risks. A Rapid Gender Analysis will be conducted to gather gender-segregated information and identify specific needs within 48 hours onset of a crisis. Additionally, GBV service facilities will be secured and reinforced ahead of the monsoon season to mitigate the impact of hazards. In the emergency response plan, alternative or tentative service facilities such as a Tent will be stored for the continuation of the GBV support if facilities have occurred and damage significantly. To reduce the vulnerability of extremely vulnerable groups of people, all GBV service facilities including WGSS and MPC will be accommodated as safe shelters for the affected people. In terms of disaster risk reduction, the project will focus on identifying, assessing, and monitoring risks. Regular GBV emergency flash updates will be shared in a timely response to highlight the protection risks with GBVSS.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

This project aims to address the needs and priorities of all age and gender groups, with a specific focus on youth (ages 15 to 24). GBV service accessibility will be ensured for youth groups, available for females in WGSS and MPC centres for males. The project will engage youth in activities that strengthen life skills, using the Girl Shine and Adolescent curriculum for adolescents aged 15 to 18. These programs will increase their knowledge of preventing gender-based violence (GBV) and child marriage.Additionally, the project will provide dignity kits and menstrual hygiene management (MHM) kits to women adolescent girls including youth promoting safer mobility, protection, and a dignified life. Youth groups, referred to as community outreach groups, will include both male and female members and will receive training to build their basic numeracy and literacy skills. They will also develop negotiation and leadership skills, equipping them to raise awareness about the impacts of the climate crisis and GBV. Through anticipatory action training, these groups will gain the knowledge and skills needed to address these critical issues.The project will also promote youth involvement in leadership and decision-making roles, empowering them to contribute actively to their communities. Youth will be encouraged to organize and participate in community campaigns and events, such as the 16 Days of Activism and International Women’s Day (IWD).

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The program aims to enhance resilience and self-reliance among Rohingya and host communities through initiatives such as homestead gardening and the development of green skills. These environmentally friendly practices will improve nutritional intake and food security. The project will provide women and girls access to training at WGSS centers, where they will learn sewing and other skills from experienced trainers. By equipping women and girls with the knowledge and tools needed to generate income, the project seeks to break the cycle of poverty and promote self-reliance. Additionally, the project will integrate elements of the CARE CLIMB initiative, focusing on sustainable green skills to ensure food security, promote a healthy environment, and support low-cost cultivation using natural resources. These efforts will contribute to women’s empowerment and long-term community resilience.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

This project will be implemented in the host community as part of a nexus development initiative. The intervention will take place in Ukhiya Upazila, specifically in Jalipalong Union, covering Wards 2 and 8, and will address the needs and priority activities identified in the JRP 2025 through the GBV program. According to the 2024 ISNA report, there are 25,219 people in need in Jalipalong, and CARE aims to reach 7,649 individuals through two WGSS centers and extensive outreach activities in these wards. The project will adhere to interagency and government guidelines, allocating 30% of its resources to development initiatives. The project also prioritises the needs of host community people to address Gender-based violence to respond, prevent and mitigate strategies with the proposed plan and intervention. Collaboration of Coxsbazar DC, UNO and Interagency joint initiative to prevent GBV will be well structured in the proposed project such as 16 days activism, and IWD event campaign.
