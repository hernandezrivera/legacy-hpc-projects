Project Summary

4,800 households, equivalent to 28,800 individuals are targeted through the proposed intervention in the area of Old Fangak and New Fangak. The action is intended to support households affected by recurrent shocks, catastrophic flooding and most recently conflict and displacement. Agricultural activities as well as fishing are currently the most significant forms of livelihoods. Agricultural production was severely affected with reported loss of harvests, followed by long periods of flooding, making it impossible to cultivate anything and more recently by conflict which forced people of New Fangak to displace to Old Fangak and surrounding areas of New Fangak.
Beneficiary selection will be primarily informed using the Food Consumption Score (FCS) and the Reduced Coping Strategy Index (rCSI). Nevertheless, the use of other criteria of vulnerability such as female headed households, or heads of household that are elderly, people with disabilities (PwD), child headed households etc. will be considered, of course considering the availability of household members that can be engaged in manual labour such as cultivating and fishing.
The agricultural calendar will be considered. The rainy season in the target area is unimodal, starting in May and running until October. During this period, beneficiaries will be engaged in farming activities especially for crop seeds (maize, sorghum). During the lean season, and while awaiting harvests, beneficiaries will be engaged in vegetable farming. This will improve the dietary diversity of supported households but will also be helpful in protecting crop seeds from being consumed in place of being planted. All through out, fishing activities will be supported since the target area is next to the Nile River, which is a rich source of fish. This will compliment farming activities to ensure a constant availability of food in supported households. Cash distribution will be a preferred modality of support, if the local markets have sufficiently recovered from the conflicts.
Activities will be closely coordinated with other humanitarian actors in the area. FCA occasionally chairs local coordination meetings on a regular basis. The same will be done at national cluster level and through the 5W reporting matrix.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2022, climate change has had an impact on various sectors and most notably, on agriculture. The target area has suffered from recurrent, above average rainfall over the past few years which has negatively agricultural activities, availability of food and ultimately food security. It is estimated that 835,000 have so far been affected and mostly in Jonglei and Upper Nile States. According to the South Sudan HNO 2022, Jonglei State and in particular Fangak County is among the most affected counties as a result of the flood. This is comparable to the situation in 2021 in Pibor County. Fangak County is among the counties that experience year round water logging from the Nile River, surrounding streams and swamps affective livelihoods, trade and supply of basic and essential supplies and services. Water borne diseases have also been reported. Humanitarian actors on the ground have been struggling to meet the humanitarian needs occasioned by these recurrent shocks on the local communities. Conflict has also been sporadically reported, lastly in August 2022, when it last broke out in New Fangak and Tonga forcing all the residents to seek safety from the surrounding areas. Residents of Tonga fled to Malakal and New Fangak residents sought safety from the surrounding areas of New Fangak and from Old Fangak. People and humanitarian workers have slowly started to return to New Fangkak, but the situation is still precarious. The conflict continues in Tonga and the town in currently occupied by armed groups. Moreover, Fangak is classified as ICP4 with approximately 10,000 people in phase 5. The HNO places Fangak County as having one of the lowest resilience capacities at RCI<11 which confirms that affected households are engaging in negative coping strategies to address food insecurity in their households. Additionally, the area has suffered from the global rise of prices limiting access to food even further.

2 - Project activities

The project will respond to the following priority cluster objectives linking to proposed activities: CLFSC/CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 3, 4 & 5). Project participants will receive agricultural inputs (vegetable, crop & fishing kits). The following livelihood kits will be distributed to the most vulnerable participants:a) Fast maturing vegetable seeds: Okra (20g), Onion (20g), Amaranth (20g), Tomato (20g). b) Crop seeds: Maize (5kg), Sorghum (3kg). Nevertheless, crop seeds will only be distributed if the prevailing conditions favour cultivation. In the event of flooding, this might not be possible, in which case, FCA will adapt the kit by dropping cereal seeds and in place, doubling the quantity of vegetable seeds provided i.e. an extra 20g each for okra, onion, amaranth and tomato. c) Agricultural tools: Digging hoe (1pc), weeding hoe/maloda, pedal pumps (1pc per group of 25 to support vegetable farming/irrigation during the dry season). d) Fishing gear: Fishing twines (2 spools), monofilament (1coil) and fishing hooks (2 boxes). CLFSC/CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (implemented across all 78 counties). Project participants will benefit from training, asset building and market support. Training will accompany distribution activities, mainly based on better agronomic techniques such as seedbed preparation, transplanting, pest control, irrigation as well as post-harvest management and control. Specific training will also be conducted for the fishing component to complement existing tradition fishing methods in order to increase productivity. Beneficiaries will be accompanied and closely mentored on agricultural activities with the active participation of County Agricultural Extension Workers in collaboration with the FCA FSL team. Cross cutting issues (1) Gender: Project advisory committees, comprising of 50% representation by women will be formed to ensure that gender issues are always considered. These committees will also act as a local complaints feedback entry point and the link for feedback from beneficiaries to FCA and vice versa. Female headed households are particularly vulnerable in the context of Fangak County. As a result, they will be purposefully targeted for this intervention. The project seeks to identify at least 70% of primary beneficiaries as women. This will contribute significantly to their participation in project activities and eventually, their empowerment. (2) Environment: FCA will make use of existing tools and policies such as the FCA Climate tool developed (2020) as well as the FCA environmental protection guidance package (2020) to inform the environment analysis before activities begin. Environmentally friendly agricultural practices will be encouraged e.g. control and prevention of soil erosion, promoting the use of animal manure and compost for fertilizer, the use of mulching for conservation of soil moisture, etc. (3) HIV/AIDS and COVID-19: The target communities will continue to be sensitized on HIV/AIDS and COVID-19 since there is a risk still persists despite a general lack on emphasis of the existing dangers presented by both diseases. (4) Disability and inclusion: The project will purposefully target households, who have members with disability taking into account the availability of household members with the ability to engage in agricultural activities. Targeting, registration and data collection regarding beneficiaries will include disaggregation based on disability to better understand the dynamics in the target area. There already exists sufficient data on families with children living with disabilities and this will also be taken into account.

3 - Project indicators

4,800 households (28,800 individuals) will benefit from training, asset building and market support. This will be recorded through training reports, attendance lists, pre and post tests, post harvest as well a household survey. 4,800 households (28,800 individuals) will receive agricultural inputs (vegetable, crop and fishing kits). This will be recorded through distribution lists, post distribution monitoring reports and household surveys. Impact level indicators, in line with the cluster objectives will also be monitored and reported against in the final reporting phase.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The following mitigation measures will be used to address the risks identified above: 8.1. Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. This will be mitigated by having in place a well function project advisory committee and a complaints handling mechanism that addresses arising issues. The relevant duty bearers will be involved to ensure an equitable repartition of beneficiaries e.g. between IDP, returnees and host communities. 8.5. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. Culturally, people living with disabilities are normally hidden away from society. As a result, they risk being left out during the beneficiary selection process. A door to door campaign will ensure that such beneficiaries are not left behind. 8.6. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. To mitigate against this, the existing complaints and feedback mechanism will be re-evaluated to ensure that beneficiaries are comfortable with it and that it is contextualized to the existing realities. Regular stakeholder meetings will ensure that issues are openly discussed and addressed. 8.9. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area. With flooding, a lot of agricultural land was rendered unusable. The little available land may be subject to disputes. However, the use of kitchen gardens will ensure that this available pieces of land are put to good use. For crop seeds, the local and traditional authorities will be involved in identifying communal land where this is feasible. The project will also distribute fast maturing vegetable seeds, which will allows project participants to diversify their diets during the times when water recedes . Furthermore, as Fangak has experienced extreme flooding from 2019 due to climate change and therefore the risk of flooding is considered high. Should this continue to happen, the project will expand its fishing activities (delivery of fishing gear and training) with the permit of the donor. Since the extreme flooding has largely prevented planting and harvesting, the people of Fangak have resorted to fishing and collection of waterlilies. Before flooding fishing was practiced mainly be fisherfolks, but since harvesting became very difficult, people fish to supplement their diets. Therefore, the project's focus will shift to supporting fishing.
