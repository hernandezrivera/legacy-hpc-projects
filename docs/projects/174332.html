1 - Needs

The Covid-19 pandemic has deteriorated the humanitarian situation in (BAY) states of northeast Nigeria. The number of people in need increased from 7.9 million to 10.6 million within 4 months’ time due to the Covid-19 pandemic. With 4.3 million people in need before the COVID-19 crisis, Borno already is the worst affected state by the protracted crisis as indicated in the number of people targeted in the Humanitarian response plan(HRP) addendum. Dikwa Local government area of Borno state is one of the worse hit amongst many others with recently reported attacks from Non-state armed groups which has to lead to the highten presence of security forces, Continuous actions against the non-state groups have also lead to the displacement of many who now reside in camps and within host communities, with supervised movement by the military in and out of the LGA. Dikwa remains inaccessible by road except through the humanitarian air services. Due to the above limitations, the most vulnerable population has almost zero sources of livelihood, food and water this has been worsened by the Covid-19 pandemic, The most recent assessment conducted by REACH in hard to reach LGAs one of which is Dikwa and Gwoza FGD participants listed food as a high priority need. To cope with insufficient food, reducing the number of meals consumed was reported by most assessed settlements in Dikwa (80%) and Gwoza (76%). Furthermore, 15% also reported skipping entire day without eating The number of meals reported to have been consumed per day aligned with the reported use of reducing meals as a coping strategy, with Dikwa and Gwoza having the highest proportion of assessed settlements reporting most people ate one meal a day (70% and 83%, respectively 70 % also reported having no sources of livelihood. CRUDAN will provide food security and livelihood, dependence on humanitarian assistance is very high. In other to reduce the risks/exposure to the covid-19 and to meet the identified needs within the community, Through this project, CRUDAN will build on their ongoing efforts in strengthening the resilience of the affected population through an integrated. According to the HNO Humanitarian Need Severity Levels and Presence of Humanitarian Actors 2020, Dikwa was classified to be extreme and thus prioritized the estimated population of Dikwa LGA is 113,902 that includes 75,470 internally displaced persons (IDPs). 81% of the IDPs live in formal camps, while 19 per cent live with the host communities. 38% of households are female-headed in the formal camp and 23 per cent in the host community.CRUDAN’s needs assessment conducted in Dikwa and Gwoza in June 2019, and in addition with other secondary information, identified the frequent influx of new arrivals, as a result some of the major needs identified, food needs is considered most critical, as those who are having access to food assistance, would have to share with unregistered/new arrivals, which made it inadequate and difficult to meet up with the food needs. For others that still have access to food support, such where not complemented with condiments. It was established that most of the new arrivals hardly comes in with items or any belongings, and among the critical things the need is a cooking stove and utensils that will help them prepare their food, because this many have to take the risk of going into the bushes to gate firewood, causing harm to the environment, and tendencies to fire incidences as the put-in fires in open space with stones given it the tripod stand. Another source of concern in the findings is selling off items by the beneficiaries at a devalued price in other to buy what the actually need and are happy with. Some beneficiaries felt lack/or limited support for livelihood made them continues dependent on humanitarian aid, which the felt that if they are supported to use their previous skills in reviving their livelihood base, they will be less dependent.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CRUDAN will target the most vulnerable households with their interventions including child-headed households, women-headed households, large households with children elderly without family support, PLWs, PWDs, chronically ill people, households with low food consumption scores, and people with low coping strategy index scores. In both LGAs targeted, both host communities, Internally Displaced People (IDPs) and returnees to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach. With the FSL and MPC activities, partners are targeting households who have little access to food or sources of income such as child-headed households, PWDs or the elderly. The target beneficiaries often include new arrivals of IDPs and returnees who are the most vulnerable. The intervention will apply a community-based (Participatory Approach) selection process to identify the beneficiaries. CRUDAN conduct a community entrance meeting at the start of the programme to explain amongst others the beneficiary selection criteria. In collaboration with community members, the most vulnerable beneficiaries are then identified and registered.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

CRUDAN in all the LGAs will conduct a joint community entrance meeting during which community members were informed about the project objectives and activities and beneficiary selection criteria. Furthermore, they will inform the affected communities about the roles and responsibilities of the different stakeholders involved, their rights and entitlements and they were provided with information on the different complaint and feedback channels available including the use of a toll-free line, visiting the help desk or speaking with a staff member, community leader or community volunteer. Throughout the project duration, CRUDAN will conduct community feedback meetings where community stakeholders are informed about progress to date in terms of implementation, challenges encountered and areas where community support is required. Furthermore, the available complaint and feedback channels will be reiterated. The CRUDAN ensured that the community representatives present during the entry and feedback meetings represented all groups in the community, including women and PWDs. In addition, program staff and community volunteers share project information during community meetings, training, and other activities such as distributions

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

CRUDAN will establish several channels for the affected population to share complaints and feedback including suggestion boxes, hotlines and help desks which are set up during distributions. Furthermore, the partners actively solicited feedback during the community feedback meetings in which women, youth and PWDs participate. For most partners, these feedback meetings are held in the form of focus group discussions and are gender and age disaggregated. CRUDAN will establish a committee comprising of community representatives which they consult. In addition, feedback is also received during post-distribution monitoring exercises and field visits. The different feedback channels are explained during the community entry meeting.Issues arising and feedback provided by the community are addressed by CRUDAN or shared with other Other partners working in the same location or at cluster level depending on the magnitude of the issue/feedback. Whatever transpires during the project is also shared within the larger humanitarian community through the UN Local Coordination Group (LCG) meetings. Sensitive feedback such as PSEA complaints is handled with full confidentiality. If suggestions are not applicable, people are duly informed, and reasons are provided as to why their feedback is not being addressed
