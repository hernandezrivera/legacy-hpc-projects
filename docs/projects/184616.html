Project Summary

According to the 2021 Somalia Humanitarian Needs overview, 3.5 million Somalis face emergencies and crises including food security conditions in 2021 with 60 percent of children constituting of those in need in Somalia contributing to the worst malnutrition rates in the world. About 1 million children are acutely malnourished with about 162,000 under 5 suffering from life-threatening severe malnutrition. Huge food and nutrition gaps remain a common challenge among poor and marginalized communities. The proposed action will scale up the provision of Nutrition services in Benadir, Galmudug, Nugaal, and Hirshabelle regions for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and vulnerable host communities. This action will target 36,125 children and pregnant women through supporting outpatient therapeutic feeding (OTP) and targeted supplementary feeding (TSFP) to manage Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) respectively through Integrated health services as well in a stabilization center in Galmudug region. While the IRC currently supports integrated healthcare and nutrition services in the regions, quality nutritional services will be scaled to increase access especially among under-five children and pregnant or lactating women in host and IDP communities.
The proposed action will support 2 static facilities and 4 outreach mobile teams with the community health workers promoting Infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices and scaling up hygiene response in the community. Additionally, current and immediate needs from the spread and effects of COVID-19 in the supported region will be addressed from the continued infection prevention and control (IPC) practices, creating awareness using media and training, among others. Through creating demand and access to nutrition services the facilities and mobile outreaches will include receiving nutrition supplies (RUTF, RUSF) and PPE supplies. As well as nutrition promotion, community engagement for IYCF promotion will contribute to improving the nutrition statuses of the vulnerable and marginalized communities in the supported regions. Similarly, community outreach components will strengthen the roles of CHWs in providing targeted health and nutrition education messages through community engagement sessions including MUAC screening and referrals at the community level while also ensuring reduced COVID transmission through social distancing principles. Further, the IRC will train CHWs to enable them to provide a rapid, effective response in cases of health emergencies or disasters for instance the COVID-19 pandemic.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The strengthened IRC client response mechanism will provide enough room for community engagement to prevent the lack or poor inclusion of women, children, people with special needs/disabilities, and marginalized groups in the project. As well, regular community outreach activities and awareness at the community will improve community role in the project activity implementations. Moreover, robust inclusive selection community engagement encourages representation in community committees and groups emphasizing women, children, people with special needs/disabilities, and marginalized groups including women, girls, IDPs, and people with disabilities. The IRC implements and monitors programs through the inclusion of clients and other stakeholders in the feedback mechanism on its delivery performance and avenues for clients will be given to reporting any IRC-related misuse of resources, lack of effectiveness, timeliness, or responsiveness. A communication structure is in place in collaboration with Complaints Response Mechanism and includes guidance on how to handle complaints and report them to the IRC Management. The community health committees established will serve as forums where feedback on program implementation can be shared and ideas on further improving activities generated. An exit interview will be conducted within the project period to get feedback from the community The main barriers to accessing healthcare in these areas include lack of quality health services including lack of emergency obstetric and neonatal care and minimal staff capacity. In addition to the above, the recurrent conflicts have disrupted routine health programs leaving a large sector of the population without access to health services. Hence IRC will recruit and engage qualified service providers to provide services and outreach responses that create more access to health services. The proposed project supports the gender theme in that it promotes health service provision to all sections of the population including men, women, girls, and boys, without discrimination building strong working relationships with the local authority and line ministries to ensure no potential influence that can alter the project design and resource allocation among target areas. close collaboration and working relationships with the IDP and host communities will be built to ensure fair project resource distribution based on participative selection criteria that inform the vulnerabilities.
