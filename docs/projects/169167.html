Project Summary

Project includes TWO activities:
1) Provision of Cash for Rent for 800 vulnerable families threatened with eviction or who have lost their shelters damaged in Beirut Port Blast.
2) Immediate shelter assistance (sealing-off kits) to 165 affected families in Karantina, Mar Michael and Burj Hammoud (Activity completed, reported here for financial tracking purposes).
These activities are based on long-standing PCPM operational presence in Burj Hammoud and surrounding areas that included:
a) provision of Cash for Rent assistance to vulnerable families living in those districts since 2016 (Polish Aid funding). PCPM implemented Cash for Rent programs in Lebanon since mid-2012 and is the chair of the inter-agency technical committee on Cash for Rent.
b) UN Habitat-PCPM Neighborhood Upgrade program in Maarach (Burj Hammoud), implemented by PCPM thanks to UN-Habitat since early 2019 (still ongoing).

1 - Needs

Hundreds of buildings are either destroyed, partially destroyed, or are structurally unstable (at risk of collapse). According to UN-Habitat, at least 800 families - probably much more - are in urgent need of relocation. PCPM rapid assessment in Karantina district found out that many residents are unlikely to return even this year. According to the LRC needs assessment, over 55% of residents affected by the Beirut explosions were not owing but renting their apartments. The explosion affected housing situation of the vulnerable families in various ways: 1) those who lived in their own apartament were forced to relocate for a definite period of time (until completion of rehabilitation works) and have now rent fees to pay as an additional significant cost in their expenditure basket; 2) those who lived in rented apartments need to first make an informed decision whether they want to return to the same apartments or rent undamaged accommodation elsewhere. In either case, they are in need of Cash for Rent assistance due to high vulnerability, loss of assets and livelihoods, as well as general socio-economic crisis in Lebanon. Residents who remained in their apartments require urgent, even temporary, repairs to close window and door openings damaged by the blast.The Beirut explosion damaged among others districts that were already highly vulnerable, such as eastern outskirts of Beirut City and Burj Hammoud. In Burj Hammoud several public buildings were damaged, with windows and internal partition blown out. Streets and public spaces suffered from falling debris and window frames. Electricity connections were affected, water tanks ruptured. Small businesses which experienced material damage and lost their storefronts have low chances of quick resumption of business activity, further endangering livelihoods, already affected by the socio-economic crisis and massive loss in purchasing power.

2 - Activities

PCPM project includes the following activities:1) Provision of Cash for Rent for 800 vulnerable families threatened with eviction or who have lost their shelters damaged in Beirut Port Blast.Cash for Rent for the persons affected by Beirut Blast will be done in accordance with the latest update of the Shelter Sector guidelines, adjusted to this particular emergency (through the currently ongoing CFR TTC), among others in terms of length of assistance. Cash for Rent aims at supporting the household by covering part of the rent (in some cases - all rental costs) and ensuring the tenure security thanks to tripartite lease agreements between the implementing agency, the tenant and the landlord. Cash for Rent assistance will be paid through ATM cards and subject to regular monitoring, as per the Cash for Rent guidelines and cash group recommendations. THE ASSISTED HH WILL BE EITHER REFERRED TO MINOR REPAIRS/STRUCTURAL REHABILITATION PROGRAMS OF THEIR ORIGINAL SHELTERS OR TO FURTHER ASSISTANCE BEYOND THE SCOPE OF THE FLASH APPEAL. This activity falls under the shelter strategic objective 1: "Reduce immediate protection-related shelter needs of the most vulnerable households"2) Immediate shelter assistance (sealing-off kits) to affected families in Karantina, Mar Michael and Burj Hammoud. Target: 165 families. Provision of sealing-off kits delivered from Poland onboard of two Boeing 787 flights and distributed within one week of the disaster (Activity completed, reported here for financial tracking purposes). This activity falls under the shelter strategic objective 1: "Reduce immediate protection-related shelter needs of the most vulnerable households" The project targets 4343 persons: 3600 (800 HH) for cash for rent, 743 (165 HH) for immediate shelter assistance.
