Project Summary

The program will strengthen the overall protective environment and resilience of the most vulnerable internally displaced people and host community members in Northwest Syria. Our organization will provide integrated protection services, including specialized gender-based violence and child protection case management, advanced and focused, and/or those who are survivors or at risk of gender-based violence and abuse – especially women and children. The program will target at least 26,025 beneficiaries. Attention will be given to appropriate entry points such as existing women’s groups and support networks. Areas of operation are selected in coordination with Clusters and health directorates. Case management services will be tailored to beneficiaries’ individual needs, taking into consideration family and caregivers. Psychosocial support will help beneficiaries to improve their coping mechanisms, manage stress and find solutions to their challenges. Group work will provide space for beneficiaries with similar issues, problems or circumstances to come together for mutual support. Outreach services will spread information on positive mental health and protection risks, as well as assist in reaching the most vulnerable in communities and making appropriate referrals to services. Training will target both community members and front-line service providers to increase their capacity to help and support others. Awareness-raising activities aim to decrease stigma around accessing services through encouraging people to be more aware of protection risks faced. Protection services will be integrated with health and physical rehabilitation services under other programs in order to improve access, ensure strong referral mechanisms and identify those most at-risk. Activities will be accessible by all and include: psychosocial support, community-outreach, group work, awareness-raising activities, training for service providers, community members on mental health and protection topics, and integrated mental health services for the most vulnerable people, including people with disabilities, people facing mental conditions, psychological distress and trauma. Activities will also target children and women assessed to be at high risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect or violence, children found to be associated with armed forces and armed groups, unaccompanied or separated children, persons with mental disorders or psychological distress, the elderly, chronically ill, and people with disabilities. All activities will be based upon the principles of the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings, Minimum Standards for Child Protection, and Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action. Our organization will work to improve the availability of holistic response for the most vulnerable by conducting training targeting a range of humanitarian actors in order to strengthen response with a survivor-centered approach.

3 - Needs

According to the CP HNO 2022, Girls and boys continue to experience persisting violence includingphysical, emotional, sexual and domestic violence, with children at risk of being killed or injured, recruited, and used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted, and sexually abused. GBV is a dominant aspect of the Syrian humanitarian crisis, affecting the lives of millions of women and adolescent girls across the country (Voices from Syria 2022). As noted in NGO1a's 2021 MSNA, 92.2% (319) of the HH respondents mentioned different dangers facing children where the top three dangers facing children cited by respondents were kidnapping 56.4% (195), dropping out of school 55.8% (193), and and neglect 35.8% (124). NGO1a's 2021 MSNA shows that protection services which include integrated (CP), GBV and PSS remain a major need across NWS, with expansion needs. Based on data collected by NGO1a's current GBV implementation, psychological abuse is the most prevalent type of GBV case (40%), followed by physical assault (28%), denial of resources/opportunities and services (18%), sexual abuse including rape (8%), and early and forced marriage (7%). Economic deprivation, lack of livelihood opportunities, discrimination, exclusion, and socio-cultural norms have led to the continued normalization of GBV and severely impacted the psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights, including those with disabilities.

4 - Implementation of activities

Continued specialized GBV, CP, and MH Case Management will be provided in Idleb and Aleppo in WGSSs, CP centers, PSS centers, through mobile delivery community-based activities, and integrated into PHCs to respond to lack of social cohesion, shared stressors, and difficult living conditions and aiming to promote resiliency, limit psychological effects of crisis, reduce protection risks and stigma and provide an avenue for support. Awareness sessions will be conducted using the GBV sub cluster awareness raising toolkit. Empowerment and support groups for women and girls will be conducted in a safe environment where women can interact and socialize with other women coming from similar backgrounds. Focused-non specialized PSS program will be provided to adult women. Community-based child protection PSS group activities target children through psychosocial, recreational, conducted in CP Centers. YEP provides for youth includes life skills, and skill-building and psychosocial activities. Parenting skills sessions provide to parents, to strengthen their abilities to contribute positively to childhood development Capacity Building: NGO1a will provide training to service providers, humanitarian staff stakeholders, community members to build capacity, raise awareness, improve referral mechanisms and strengthen coordination Community Committees under CP and GBV will be established and will include active community members like parents, teachers, health staff, etc.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

• Our organization has prepared a safeguarding action plan to promote and make sure safeguarding measures are in place.• A Safeguarding Policy is being distributed for all staff to read and acknowledge.• Our organization delivers MAST training which includes SEA training for new hires and as an annual refreshers for all staff. • Our organization has safeguarding focal points for the staff to revert to in case of any concern, question, or complaint. • Our organization has safeguarding posters placed in all of our facilities along with the several complaining channels that staff or beneficiaries can use. • Our Protection Team delivers training sessions for all staff for the safeguarding and the referral pathways. • Our Protection Team does a safeguarding risk assessment for all facilities • Our organization has established the CBRFM, taking into consideration community awareness. •


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: NGO1a maintains access to targeted locations; Security situation remains stable; Non-interference by groups in control of program locations Risks: Security situation is volatile where destabilization negatively impacts staff and beneficiaries; Delays in procurement and delivery of essential pharmaceuticals and medical consumables and equipment essential for health facility rehabilitation and operations; leading to delay in start-up of services for beneficiaries or cause of stock outs and shortage of medicines; Program locations are taken over by GoT, GoS or allied forces; leading to restrictions in implementation of activities without specific approvals (in the case of GoT) or through cross border operations (in the case of GoS) Mitigation Measures: The physical security of NGO facilities to be regularly assessed and upgraded as needed; Close security monitoring. Security training for staff which to limit/lessen exposure; Support to evacuation/withdrawal of local staff to alternative locations to continue managing program or revise targeting; NGO1a will consider supporting affected staff through Duty of Care, including financial compensation; NGO1a uses joint efforts of procurement and legal staff to clarify details of hosting countries regulations; NGO1a applies for appropriate certifications, licenses and permits to import and/or use bonded transportation and warehouses for humanitarian assistance; NGO1a will explore supporting LNGOs registered in Turkey.
