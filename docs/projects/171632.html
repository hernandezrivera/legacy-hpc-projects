Project Summary

According to FSNAU-FEWSNET Sept release Due to the combined effects of widespread and severe flooding, Desert Locust infestation, socioeconomic impacts of COVID-19 and the cumulative impacts of previous shocks, up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2020 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. In addition, 849 900 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished through August 2021. Sustained and large-scale humanitarian assistance and government support are currently preventing more severe outcomes in many areas. Desert Locust continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2020. The 2020 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly. However, weather and Desert Locust hazards threaten to increase the acutely food insecure population in late 2020. The consensus climate forecast released in late August by the Greater Horn of Africa Climate Outlook Forum (GHACOF56) indicates an elevated likelihood of below-average to average rainfall across Somalia during the October to December 2020 Deyr rainfall season. Damage from Desert Locust could exacerbate the impact of a below-average 2020 Deyr season. As of September, In summary, 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher) outcomes between now and December without sustained humanitarian assistance. An additional 3 million people are expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 5.1 million. Humanitarian assistance must be sustained through December 2020 to prevent Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes for 2.1 million people. Livelihoods support is also required for people that are Stressed or worse (IPC Phase 2 or higher). And hence this appeal by KDO
This project intends to help deal with climate-related shocks and stresses, that threaten people and communities compounding existing poverty and vulnerability and undermining long-term development, encourage communities that have previously relied upon one or two rainfed harvests of staple food and to diversify with a variety of crops with more frequent harvests through solar panel irrigation system horticulture production. Diversification activities along with training and awareness raising activities on early warning mechanisms, water retention, reducing soil erosion, improving soil fertility and planting trees that can lower temperatures, to allow other crops to be grown nearby and some varieties that produce fruits to improve HH nutrition and income.
The project will be anchored on a social protection concept (SP) to help beneficiaries anticipate and cope with climate-related shocks and stresses, such as droughts the outcome being to gain long term resilience and sustainable outcomes. KDO has paid attention to gender roles and relations between women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities in particular, how they affect intra-household decision making and bargaining, time poverty, and women’s reproductive unpaid care work. KDO is informed by the fact that HH are not homogenous units, but are sites of ‘bargaining’, in which women and men have different abilities to decide and control how the transfers are pooled back into the household.

Project Activities

Create awareness on Covid19 safety and prevention by providing and advocating the use of PPEs as required by WHO guidelinesDesert locust, pest and diseases control through a well coordinated program effort through training of control of the desert locust, pest and diseases and the safe use of pesticides targeting 1500 small holder farmers within • Improve 250HH’ immediate access to food for 4 months through provision of conditional cash transfers @100 USD per month (1500 individuals) through severity and vulnerability analysis of target HH, gender, age, disability and willingness to participate in livelihood diversification for food and income, natural resource management, sinking of 15 shallow wells for solar irrigation and 3 community dams. • Target and provide 250HH with unconditional transfers @ 100 USD for 3 months. The CT will be dispensed on a MEB basis and the targeting criteria will include gender, disability, age, and vulnerability analysis of target HH • Establish, restore community-driven afforestation nursery, natural resource management, disaster risk management, and preparedness and training • Provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs (seeds and tools) through Cash vouchers and technical support to 18,500 host communities and 15,650 IDPs target beneficiaries(5,691 HH) Markets in the project target areas are functional, through structured dialogue with the community beneficiaries they indicated that they would prefer Cash transfers, both conditional, unconditional and voucher system modalities as this helps them purchase what they need most and through this transfers, local economy growth is enhanced since the money is spent locally within their village markets. they also cited mobile money transfers as efficient timely and easy to monitor since the transfer is always accompanied by a deliver message which has helped minimize malpractices and other logistical issues associated with relief, in summary, they reported high efficiency in the mobile-based cash transfers. KDO will coordinate and collaborate with the CWG for guidance during implementation of the cash-based interventions.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

KDO has been implementing Cash based programmes within Nugaal. According to an assessment done by KDO on the effectiveness of the modality to its beneficiaries they reported that cash based interventions have flexibility and choice on what the HH needs are according to needs and priorities, Dignity in allowing the beneficiaries to have control, felt recognized in providing for their own families, support to local trade whereby most of the beneficiaries do trade among themselves by selling some items in the market, increased income and savings and improves local economy and peaceful coexistence between community members.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project considered market functionality by observing that where cash based interventions are targeting, there are thriving markets Other factors considered by KDO were:-• Is the market functioning? • Are basic items available? • Are there government policies that restrict the movement of goods? • Is the market competitive? • Are markets integrated? • Will traders respond to an increase in purchasing power, and how quickly? • Is there a risk of inflation in the prices of key commodities?

Mitigation Measures

Community-based approach – The participation of the displaced and the host community will be ensured by the targeting criteria which will include gender, disability, age, and vulnerability analysis of target HH who will be drawn from HH in IPC phase 3 and 4, essential to create a sense of ownership and ensure that activities are adapted to the local cultural and social context, Local community resources will be identified, mobilized and used to implement the project. The community will participate in the design, implementation and monitoring of activities and form part of the decision-making process at all stages. KDO will liaise with local authorities local leaders, national and international aid agencies to have access to displaced populations and obtain first-hand information on the overall conditions and most pressing protection gaps to avoid duplication of activities and risks of exclusion based on displacement. Sensitization that the cash transfers/vouchers provided is for the family groups and not only for the person whose name is being registered for distribution. At a minimum, registration will be based on family groups and will include the names of all male and female adults in the family. All data will be disaggregated by age, sex, place of origin, and background. Maintain complaint referral mechanisms and mailboxes in the camps or collective settings for the population to communicate their concerns KDO will strive to identify who are the most invisible groups in the community, the power relations, and the existing specific needs of any group according to age/sex. Please provide further explanation

KDO was established in the year 1994 with the aim of improving Food Security and Social development in Nugal region. KDO has experience in the implementation of livelihood projects in PL state. The NGO is well known in Nugaal region with good relations with the local communities and authorities as a key active NGO in the implementation of food security and livelihood projects and has high reputation. Since 2009 KDO has supported 68,086 households in difference phases of relief intervention our on-the-ground presence gives us a base in places of high priority in terms of need our ability to facilitate the various stakeholders to ensure greatest impact on the ground. This facilitation is a key component in how we operate as an organization working in partnership with the target communities to plan projects that have long term durable solutions to enhance HH resilience to future shocks. KDO will in this particular project ensure to provide general guidance on the process and conditions necessary for achieving a durable solution; and assist in determining to what extent a durable solution has been achieved through the creation and restoration of sustainable community and HH assets, by opening up of HH farms that will cultivate drought tolerant early maturing crops, community afforestation and fruit tree programmes for food and income and combat climate change and desertification, training and awareness raising activities on early warning mechanisms, water retention, reducing soil erosion, improving soil fertility providing cash conditional and unconditional cash transfers community works employment during lean season’, as an alternative to poor households having to sell their productive assets or take their children out of school to buy food, Communities group coping mechanisms and solidarity networks to mitigate the impact of food shortages encouraged and thrive enhancing durable solutions and resilience.
