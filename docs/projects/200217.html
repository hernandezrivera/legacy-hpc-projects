Project Summary

TIn 2022, Lebanese residents and Syrian refugees continued to adopt a wide range of strategies to cope with a lack of food and/or the means to buy it, including relying on less preferred/less expensive food, reducing the number of meals eaten per day, and restricting consumption by adults so children can eat. In particular, the findings of the REACH-led 2022 MSNA revealed that the percentage of Lebanese households (HHs) that had to rely on less preferred and less expensive food to cope with a lack of food or money was 53.7% in Tripoli, 44.9% in Baalbeck, and 33.8% in Akkar. As such, since the beginning of the crises, HHs’ average weekly frequency of consumption of animal proteins, dairy products, and fruits and vegetables has decreased significantly.
The Action has both food security and nutrition components which complement and reinforce each other, therefore it is necessary to summarise below the food security components. The Action is divided into two sections:
A: Climate-Smart FS solutions at the HH level
A1:Food Security Component: The Action aims to provide food assistance at the HH level through the provision of training, tools, and in-kind grants to develop climate-smart permagardens at the HH level, thereby contributing to the restoration of vulnerable populations’ autonomy and resilience.
A2: Nutrition component: This part of the Action will improve nutrition at the HH level, targeting households with PLW and children between the age 0-23 months, recognizing the importance of fully supporting growing children with good health and nutrition during the first 1,000 days of their development, the critical window from conception to 2 years of age. The component will involve nutrition awareness raising sessions and gender equality training for both men and women. This responds to Objective 1 of the Nutrition Sector Response Strategy: ‘Young children and their caregivers have access to and demand for services aimed at prevention of all forms of malnutrition and the associated developmental risks (0-5 years of age)’ and Objective 3: Women have enhanced access to and demand for services aiming at prevention of all forms of malnutrition-particularly during pregnancy and lactation. 618 households (2,627 individuals) will be targeted in this component.
B. Distribution of School Lunches
B1: Food Security component: Distribution of nutritionally rich meals at the school level (public schools (children 6-12 years) will be prioritized) with 6 schools selected across the three locations, covering children’s essential food needs. The lunches will be sourced locally and will be selected by the nutrition expert hired for the project.
B2: Nutrition component: Awareness raising sessions for parents, students and staff on how to prevent acute malnutrition will be provided in the schools. Healthy and well-nourished schoolchildren learn better. This is in line with Objective 2 of the Nutrition Sector Response Strategy: Objective 2: School age children and adolescents have access to and demand for services aiming at prevention of all forms of malnutrition-particularly among girls. The action will target 5,000 children across the three targeted areas (Akkar, Tripoli and the Bekaa).
Beneficiaries will be split geographically between agencies (ACTED: Tripoli and Akkar, and SI & LOST: Baalbeck). Each agency will cover families in its current operational areas, building on previous experience, field knowledge and relationships with stakeholders as well as affected communities
Two nutritional experts (One for Bekaa and one for Tripoli and Akkar) will develop the material for awareness raising sessions, including a section on nutrition counselling to PLW. The experts will also oversee the delivery of the sessions in the locations which will be delivered by consortium partners.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Risk of increasedin income-generating activ tensions at the household/community level, as a result of engaging women in the activities: one of the changes that the project seeks to promote is making the productive/economic role of women expanded and recognised within households and the wider society. This will be possible through engaging groups of men stakeholders, in ‘softer’ components of the project, tailored to challenge preconceptions about gendered roles in society. Through open discussion and awareness raising, the program aims to fully engage men in project activities.During the Action's activities, child protection and GBV cases could be identified. The consortium will debrief the project teams and provide a PSEA and child protection training. Partners will collaborate with relevant child protection partners on the ground to make referrals as necessary.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

ACTED will promote the use of best practices in accountability and transparency of its interventions through formal beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM). The partners’ CRM includes a hotline (phone and WhatsApp), email, and communicating directly with MEAL or program staff. Each partners’ CRM modalities accommodate all, including women, the elderly and people with disabilities, by providing modalities to contact the partners in-person or by phone, and confidentially. The project team and the MEAL/Accountability team will make sure that the CRM hotline is widely communicated to beneficiaries and showcased in relevant visibility material. Dedicated staff will be responsible for receiving, logging, reporting, and following up on complaints, ensuring this process occurs in an effective manner. This will ensure a direct and accessible feedback mechanism for all beneficiaries and wider stakeholders to register feedback whilst underpinning accountability to affected population
