Project Summary

In this project, CEFOCSS is proposing to distribute combined vegetable, crop and fishing kits to 45600 individuals in Pigi/Cana, Fangak South,Uror l Counties of Jonglei State and Panyikang County of Upper Nile State , South Sudan
This will be followed by provision of training on improved agronomic practices as well as fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics to 32000 farmers/fishermen in Fangak South , Atar3/Diel and Tonga of Panyiang . Within the onset of the project, CEFOCSS is proposed to trains the selected 2000 per each location with the best agriculture practices and also selected 1000 fishing men per location with the fish handling training.
The trained farmers/fishermen will be expected to pass on their knowledge to the remaining farmers/fishermen. The knowledge gained from these trainings will be of importance in building the community resilience to future shocks eg floods and famine. These activities are aimed at improving production of vegetables, crop and fish hence contributing towards reducing malnutrition rates. The vegetable, crop and fish products will go a long way in restoring the nutritional status of the affected population, through increased food consumption and better dietary diversity score.
The project will reach the most vulnerable affected by the ongoing communal conflict and the flood respectively.
We are planning to support the small group of farmers especially the returnees with vegetable seed and other tools for cultivation.Women play an integral part in agricultural production, as subsistence farmers, cash crop growers, food processors, and livestock owners, among other roles. It follows that empowering women will impact the agricultural markets overall. Recently, many development organizations have begun to integrate gender into their agricultural development projects. While this new generation of projects are too recent to provide evidence on the long-term impacts of targeting women in agriculture. CEOFCSS seeks to identify strategies that are most effective in targeting women and that have the potential to economically empower women in the agricultural sector.

1. Needs

Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) report (November 2023) indicates that 7.1million South Sudanese (56%) of the population will face severe food insecurity (IPC 3+). In total, 98,000 (over 89%), 28,000 (over 94%), 44,000 (over 85%) and 26,000 (over 87%) of the people in Fangak, Pigi-Canal, Uror and Panyikang Counties respectively will face severe food insecurity of IPC 3+ conditions. The main causes of food insecurity are climate related shocks, manly floods which have destroyed farms, livelihoods and resulted in displaced populations. Over three consecutive years of flooding have been experienced in these counties. Crops were destroyed before harvesting and in storage. Livestock died through drowning and starvation as grazing grounds were flooded. Conflicts witnessed at both sub-national levels and at regional level including spillover effects of conflict in Sudan that has increased pressure on humanitarian needs. Coupled with flooding, are intensified sub-national violence arising from political instability, cattle raids and revenge killings. Violence causes instability limiting access to land for cultivation but also results in deaths of productive members of the House Holds. Agricultural productivity has been persistently low and coping capacity of these populations has been eroded. At the Macro-economic level, depreciation of the local currency and effects of international Wars in Ukraine and Gaza have made food more expensive and scarcer. Coping strategies of affected households have been exhausted and household assets eroded. Without timely humanitarian intervention, this situation could worsen and plunge populations into famine situation.The goal of this intervention is to support the vulnerable populations in these selected counties with livelihood kits and training so as to help them restore and sustain their livelihoods. This intervention will be guided by two cluster objectives (CO). The second CO which is to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. The third CO which is to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. It is expected that through this intervention, target populations will be able to supplement food provided through general food distribution which has proved to be irregular and therefore unreliable but also increases dependency to external support systems. The intervention will also contribute towards reduction in flood related displacements in the target counties. This will be achieved through disaster risk reduction strategies that will build the community capacity on early warning signs and mitigation measures including construction of dykes to control flow of water. The project will also ensure minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.The intervention will run from January to December of 2024.The conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that erupted in Sudan on 15 April has killed and injured thousands of people and displaced other to the neighboring Countries including such as South Sudan. This situation has deeply affected the economy of the South Sudan. Displacement of population has led to increase in influx of IDPs adding pressure to the little resources within these counties. However, there are more than 30000 returnees from Sudan and other 15000 IDPs integrated in Uror,Fangak and Pigi Counties. The last year conflict between the IO and Agwelek contributed to the displacement of the neighbors' counties such as Tonga, Fangak and Pigi .More displaces has occurs where the livelihoods of the people have become dire. in both affected locations.

2. Project activities

Project activities will be structured around the three cluster objectives. Objective one: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 & some 3). Under this objective, the main activity will be construction of community assets through conditional food assistance. Intended activities will include construction of dykes around farms to prevent perennial flooding, excavation of drainage channels, construction/rehabilitation of access roads, de-silting water pans to collect water during the rainy season, excavation of shallow wells. FFA will help communities establish communal assets, mitigate effects of flooding on food production and enhance preparedness and response to flooding which has become a yearly occurrence in the target counties. Cluster Objective two: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock. Under this objective, main activity will Emergency food production focusing on main season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fishing, vegetable & crop kits. Dry season activities will include Emergency food & livelihood support through provision of fishing kits/ vegetable kits; and at least three variety of fast maturing vegetable seeds. Dry season farming will focus on water efficient farming methods to produce sufficient and nutritious vegetables for consumption and possibly sell for some cash. This is to ensure increased availability of food even during the dry season where availability of vegetables becomes a challenge and therefore complicates the food insecurity and malnutrition situation. Main season activities will concentrate on crop production specifically maize, sorghum and legumes. Fisheries activities will cut across the main season and dry season as there are sufficient fishing grounds for both seasons. Cluster Objective three: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes. The main activity will be Training and capacity building on both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production targeting, farmers through field schools and on demonstration plots as well as the agropastoralists. All activities will be implemented so as to establish a nexus between Humanitarian response, Peace building (conflict sensitivity) and Resilience programming. A strong emphasis will be laid on accountability to affected population to ensure the do-no-harm principle is observed. There will be a strong coordination at county level with other partners to reduce duplication and ensure efficiency and wider coverage. The project will emphasis integration with other sectors to achieve greater impact in meeting household needs. For instance, the project will support establishment of kitchen garden within Nutrition centers and target mothers and caregivers of children enrolled in nutrition program with training and input support. This is to ensure that those children discharged from nutrition program do not relapse but have sufficient and nutritious food at home. The project will also incorporate WASH messaging at home, especially with reference to preparation and utilization of food, human waste disposal and provision of WASH items including water containers and purification tablets, cooking utensils and food preservation containers from other partners. To improve access to milk and household income, the project will provide small ruminants to caregivers of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) children within the Nutrition centres. This will be implemented through the mother-to-mother strategy and Care groups for both distributions and training. The project will carry out regular monitoring with participation of Boma management committees, and will develop and share project reports on monthly, quarterly and annual basis.
