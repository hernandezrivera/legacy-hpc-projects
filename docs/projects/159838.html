Project Summary

According to HRP 2019, the situation of people affected by displacement and disasters remains precarious with respect to physical safety and dignity. As a result of destitution, women, men, girls, and boys, are exposed to particular and various forms of violence, exploitation and discrimination. Frequent protection risks reported on the ground include GBV, forced evictions, exposure to explosive hazards and child rights violations, such as child recruitment by armed groups and early marriage. Displacement increases the risk of family separation, and currently, there is an estimated caseload of 8,000 unaccompanied or separated children in Somalia. A recent Somalia child protection assessment conducted by UNICEF in June 2019 asked respondents whether they were aware of children who have been separated from their usual caregivers, a majority (30.4%) reported knowing between 1-5 children separated from their usual caregivers. 20.6% were aware of 6-10 children, 15.9% were aware of 11-20 children, 8.0% were aware of more than 100 children, 7.1% were aware of 51-100 children, 6.1% were aware of 21-50 children and 5.1% could not approximate the number. 56.4% of the respondents stated that there were more boys than girls separated, with more than three quarters (88.5%), stating than separated children are mainly over 14 years.
The Gender-Based Violence and Child Protection project is targeting 28,300 vulnerable IDPs and host communities in Banaadir and Galmudug Regions, Somalia to reduce the suffering of women, girls, men, and boys. Training of Health care providers on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) will be conducted to respond to reported cases of rape, and PSS. Dignity kits will be distributed, solar lamps will be bought and distributed to the identified women and young mothers where their security is deemed threatened at night. The trained community on psychosocial support and rights of children will be there to support SGBV survivors and issues affecting children in the community through Community-based approaches, therefore the trained frontline service providers will be able to respond and offer psychosocial support to Sexual Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) survivors, unaccompanied and separated children will be gathered for by the community through identification of caregivers within the community in line with survivor-centered approaches and using the existing referral pathways. Identified unaccompanied and separated children will be registered and tracing, follow up and reunification will be arranged through the support of the cluster and other actors. SGBV survivors will be supported through service accessibility and livelihood project. Gender-Based Violence Information Management System will be used to inform actors on the different types of GBV occurring in the community and it will help in GBV programming.

Project Activities

Outcome 1: Separation of girls and boys from families is prevented and addressed, and family-based or alternative care is provided. Activity 1.1: Identity, document, trace and reunite Unaccompanied and separated minors with appropriate caregivers. CISP will further strengthen mechanisms for registration and receiving information and for the active tracing of immediate family members and relatives through capacitating child protection committees. Case management procedures for follow up of children who have been reunified with their caretakers will be strengthened and IDTR coordination improved. CISP is the CP AoR and co-leads in coordination of child protection interventions ensuring coordinated prevention and response efforts. Activity 1.2: Provide interim care for UAM, or other vulnerable children through foster care, interim caregivers or safe houses. CISP through partners and child protection committees will continue to identify and train foster families on child protection and family tracing processes. Activity 1.3: Provide case management, including psychosocial support and medical care, for UAM. (Training of all staff working with children and/or GBV survivors on case management will be conducted, and development of referral networks to ensure coordinated child protection support. CISP through its capacity building team will provide capacity building of health staff including doctors, nurses, and community health workers on clinical management of rape at MCHs, and case managers, community caseworkers, to be trained on survivor-centered approaches and psychosocial first aid. CISP ensures continued provision of psychosocial support services and clinical care services for girls, boys and women who have experienced violence, exploitation, and abuse at MCHs, and referrals to hospitals, through outreach in communities. Cluster Objective 2: Outcome 2: Violence, exploitation and abuse of girls, boys, and women are prevented and addressed, and existing systems responding to the needs of GBV survivors are improved. Activity 2.1: Develop and implement multisectoral response corridors for women, girls, and boys, through linking services and referral pathways. These will align with the most used movement routes for displaced populations and populations in flight with special attention to separated or unaccompanied children. The routes will include the Afgoye corridor to Mogadishu, Galmudug route, Bulo Burto to Belet Weyne. Activity 2.2: Provision of psychosocial support, clinical care, access to safe houses and referrals for girls, boys and women who have experienced violence, exploitation, and abuse, ensuring appropriate quality care is provided.

Mitigation Measures

Regularly monitor and discuss with beneficiaries, especially the most vulnerable, if they feel pressured, directly or indirectly to give something in return for humanitarian. Establish hotlines and complaint and feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries to reach out to different agencies. Include local authorities and host communities in decisions making processes around the selection of beneficiaries and selection criteria. Consult beneficiaries in identifying assistance needed and consider providing the host community with access to services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of basic services and facilities. Keep registration lists of camp residents and their personal data confidential and safe from unintended use; apply secure data protection measures. Install adequate lighting, especially in public or communal areas, or provide alternatives such as solar lamps or torches for each household. Avoid establishing IDP camps near borders, military installations, and conflict frontlines. Secure the camp premises with fencing. Install partitions or solid barriers between families in communal shelters and introduce door locks to better protect women and girls, particularly single women and female headed-households.
