Project Summary

in line with the South Sudan 2025 HRP and the FSL Cluster priorities and cluster objectives, in 2025, the Food Security and Livelihood Cluster aims (1) to provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations facing IPC phases 3, 4 and 5; (2) to enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing, and livestock support; and (3) to reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability both to absorb shocks and adapt to a changing context, especially in relation to climatic, economic and conflict related risks. Agency for Child Relief Aid (ACRA’s) project is aimed at providing food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations in IPC 5, 4 and some in 3 target Counties of Akobo of Jonglei State, Maiwut, Ulang, Panyikang and Baliet Counties of Upper Nile State. The project aims to improve the food security and livelihoods of IDPs, returnees and host communities through provision of timely and appropriate main season agricultural inputs/kits (crop and vegetable seeds, and fishing kits), (linked to cluster objective 2), capacity building & training, asset building and market support and training in improved agronomic and fishing practices, (linked to cluster objective 3). ACRA will also provide emergency assistance and livelihoods support to save lives and avert total collapse of livelihoods in the target Counties. The provision of agricultural and fishing inputs/kits, training in agricultural production and fishing is expected to improve the food production and in turn the food security of the target households (HHs) thus supporting them to recover from crisis and placing them on an accelerated path to early recovery and resilience to acute climate shocks.
ACRA seeks to support 21,500 HHs (129,000 ) in the targeted Counties with agricultural and livelihood kits. The kits will be procured and distributed directly by ACRA and some will be secured from FAO. Training on improved agronomic and fishing practices, marketing and group dynamics will be done through model farmers and fisher folk who will undergo a Training of Trainers (ToT) and subsequently train the farmers and fisher folk in their communities. The knowledge gained from these trainings will improve the agricultural and fishing practices of the target HHs thus improving food production, addressing their immediate food security needs and building their resilience to future shocks. The provision of agricultural inputs and fishing kits and subsequent enhancement of agricultural production and fishing is expected to contribute to improvement of the Household Diversity Score (HDDS) and Food Consumption Score (FCS) of the target HHs
ACRA will employ a community-based beneficiary selection approach which will entail establishing Project Management Committees (PMC’s) to lead the selection of eligible beneficiaries based on the beneficiary selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs with severe food insecurity including female, child, disabled/chronically ill headed HHs; persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS and survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) will be targeted in this project.
ACRA will hold regular meetings with the PMC’s and community members and conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to get their feedback on the project implementation. ACRA will ensure that their feedback is incorporated in the implementation of this project and design of future projects. Further, to promote accountability to the affected populations, ACRA will use the community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns. ACRA has ongoing WASH and FSL programs in Upper Nile and in Jonglei which will be compliment this projects to employ a multi sectoral approach.

1. Needs

The situation regarding acute food insecurity and malnutrition in South Sudan is worsening due to a combination of factors including an ongoing economic crisis, climatic shocks—most notably extensive flooding—conflict, and insecurity. The influx of returnees and refugees escaping the conflict in Sudan further complicates this already precarious scenario, placing additional strain on a nation that is struggling to cope.Recent IPC assessment indicates that between September and November 2024, approximately 6.3 million individuals, which represents 47 percent of the population analyzed, are categorized within IPC Phase 3 or higher (indicating Crisis conditions or worse). Among these individuals, around 1.71 million are experiencing critical levels of acute food insecurity, classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). Additionally, there are about 41,000 people facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity, designated as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). South Sudan continues to face a heavy burden of displacement, including about 271,000 people who have been displaced by flooding this rainy season alone. In addition to the 2 million people internally displaced by prior conflict and flood events, a total of over 840,000 refugees and returnees have fled the war in Sudan.The economic landscape in Maiwut, Baliet, panyikang and Ulang counties of Upper Nile and Akobo county of Jonglei states is characterized by hyperinflation that affects purchasing power significantly. Prices for essential goods have surged due to supply chain disruptions caused by the recent flooding. This inflationary pressure makes it increasingly difficult for families to afford basic necessities like food, clean water, and shelter.This alarming trend highlights the urgent need for humanitarian assistance and intervention to address the escalating crisis affecting millions in South Sudan.In the harvest and post-harvest projection period from December 2024 to March 2025, it is estimated that approximately 6.1 million people, representing 45 percent of the analyzed population, will likely experience food insecurity at Phase 3 or above on the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) scale. Within this group, around 1.71 million individuals are projected to be classified in IPC Phase 4, while an additional 31,000 South Sudanese returnees are expected to face IPC Phase 5.The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states and Upper Nile and Jonglei are among them. Looking ahead to the second projection period from April -July 2025, the food security situation is anticipated to worsen due to the onset of the lean season. During this time, it is likely that about 7.69 million people, which accounts for 57 percent of the analyzed population, will find themselves in IPC Phase 3 or higher. This includes an estimated 2.53 million individuals who may enter IPC Phase 4 and approximately 63,000 people who are expected to be in IPC Phase 5. According to the OCHA (October 18, 2024) flood snapshot, the flooding is estimated to have affected approximately 1 million people across 42 counties, displacing over 271,000 people. It has damaged crop fields, pastures, and essential infrastructure, disrupted livelihoods, trade flows, and humanitarian aid deliveries, and increased the risk of disease outbreaks, including cholera.Therefore, immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and avert total collapse is required especially in the ACRA target Counties in IPC4 in Jonglei & Upper Nile State

2. Project activities

1.1. Community-Based Participation and Planning (CBPP): The CBPP exercise will focus on the full participation of selected community planning teams, who in participatory ways, will identify community needs, suggest some recommendations and draw the final community’s plan. A range of participatory methods will be used to guide the planning process. This exercise will be conducted at the Boma level. ACRA will then establish beneficiary’s selection committees (BSC) and project steering committees (PSC) 1.2. Formation and training of BSCs and PMCs/PSCs: ACRA will facilitate the formation of the Boma-level PMC’s. Each PMC will comprise of 10 members (5 women, 5 men) who will be trained in beneficiary selection criteria and monitoring of the project implementation. The PMCs will also be trained in protection and mainstreaming cross-cutting issues across all activities with a particular focus on gender mainstreaming, GBV, putting additional effort to ensure accountability to the affected population. 1.3. Beneficiary selection: ACRA adopts participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches in identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. ACRA will furthermore strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria, and how to provide feedback or complains to ACRA. The PSCs with guidance form ACRA PMC’s will identify and register the beneficiaries according to ACRA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Survivors of GBV. 1.4. Distribution of complementary cropping and livelihoods inputs: ACRA will Protect and stabilize livelihoods for 8,370 vulnerable HHs (female/male 51/49%) (46,800 individuals) through provision of Livelihoods inputs (seeds and tools plus fishing gears and distribute Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum and Cowpea), vegetables (Amaranth, Collard, Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda) and Fishing kits (hooks, monofilament and spools of twine). This will also improve food availability and income from sale of surplus vegetables 1.5. Formation and training of farmers and fisher folk groups: ACRA will facilitate the formation of farmer and fisher folk groups. ToT training on improved agronomic practices, fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics will be provided. The trained farmers and fisher folk will then sensitize and build the capacity of the other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. Training on crop disease control including training of farmers and surveillance on Fall army worm (FAW) and Desert Locusts control through Community based surveillance and Early Warning. Training farmers on grain/seed harvesting, storage and support through traditional granaries and hermitic bags. Training on post-harvest management and market linkages. Fisher folk training in good fishing techniques, preservation, processing and marketing and on the use of fishing kits received. 1.6. Support provision of veterinary services through community based initiatives to 20,000 livestock. 1.7. Provision of livelihoods skills training: Female-headed HHs and fisher folk supported through training on IGAs. IGA’s will be linked to the other two activities of fishing and vegetable production on market linkages. Some markets in Jonglei, Upper Nile and EES are functional and ACRA will target this market-based activity in these areas 1.8. Facilitate formation of VSLA groups and supporting with start-up kits and supporting small scale farmers on group formation, value addition to their produce and linking them to markets and agro-input dealers and other value chain actors 1.9. Monitoring and reporting: ACRA will also conduct post distribution monitoring after every distribution to ascertain the appropriateness of the response.
