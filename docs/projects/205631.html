Project Summary

United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) report released in Juba in March 2023 specifies that South Sudan accounts for the second highest prevalence case of GBV/CP in East Africa. A study conducted by WHO in 2018 indicated that Uganda reported the highest rates of child violence followed by South Sudan.
UNFPA survey results rated Ruweng Administrative Area with the highest with cases of child violence & GBV, followed by Warrap, Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei and then Central Equatoria. The report ranks Ruweng Administrative Area at 43.1%, Warrap at 39.7%, Eastern Equatoria at 39.1%, Jonglei at 39.1% and Central Equatoria at 38.6%. Similarly, the study documented a high prevalence of child marriage (34.6%, nationally) with Lakes and Jonglei states accounting for the high rates of child marriage in the country; 60.7% and 40.9%, respectively.
The Abyei, Abiemnom and Pariang have experienced violent incidents over the past years, resulting in significant displacement of people in and out the counties, which significantly disrupted existing family structures. People’s coping mechanisms has weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopting negative practices and children continue to be expose to psycho-social distress, family separation, abuses, neglect, exploitation, sexual exploitation, forced labor, child abduction, child re/recruitment, malnutrition, communicable diseases and physical violence.
In responding to needs Abyei region, Abiemnom and Pariang, Integrated Humanitarian Aid (IHA) will identify vulnerable 232 girls and 187 boys, including adolescents and children with disabilities who are at risk will receive individual and specialized case management services that meet their contextual or specific needs. IHA will also identify unaccompanied and separated children (girls 71 and boys 86), document them and carry out family tracing and reunification services (UASC). Among the activities to be implemented, 12,121 girls and 12,432 boys including children with disabilities will be provided with focused and non-focused MHPSS and other related services. Adult beneficiaries, 6,455 women and 3,712 men will receive psychosocial support and knowledge through parenting sessions. Communities will be reached age and gender specific messages through awareness raising activities in child protection benefitting females 25,721 and males 12,877 and finally, 140 women and 70 men will be trained on child protection in emergencies (CPiE) including case management, PSS, etc).
Integrated Humanitarian Aid has active presence in the 3 counties, currently implementing CP & GBV projects funded by UNICEF and Save the children international. Past projects in the 3 counties funded by UNICEF focused on education in emergency targeting flood and conflict affected communities.

1. Needs

The HNO 2022 estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's populations, about 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 people compared to 2021. It added that Due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. At least 90,000 returnees from abroad remained displaced within the country, unable to reach their homes. In 2021, there were 2 million IDPs in the country (55% of whom are women and girls), as compared to 1.7 million in 2020. An additional 2.3 million South Sudanese remain refugees hosted in neighboring countries.Abyei and Ruweng administrative Areas have experienced a number of violent incidents over the past years, resulting in significant displacement of people in and out the counties, which significantly disrupted existing family structures. These unstable and difficult living conditions have resulted in trauma for children; the counties also have a high a caseload of unaccompanied and separated children, which are in need to be reunited with their caregivers. The children are under constant risk of being separated from their care givers, particularly with the recent outbreaks of violence and displacements, which is expected to continue. For example, in Abyei there is an ongoing conflict between Dinkas and the Arab Misseriya Baggara over land for grazing cattle and the situation is even made worse by land conflict by Dinka Ngok from Abyei and Dinka Twic from Warrap of county boundary. With Abyei being a contested area, there is limited government services reaching this hard-to-reach region leaving the people at the mercy of humanitarian actors like UNISFA, Integrated Humanitarian Aid, Hold the child, Save the children, among other few partners working in Abyei.A recent DRC-Rapid Protection Assessment march 2022 has furthermore shown that child abduction is currently the biggest fear in displaced communities, due to lack of shelter and disruption of previous community protection structures. IHA need assessment in the 3 counties shows that there are several unaccompanied and separated children in Abyei and Ruweng who come from Sudan unaccompanied and are in need of support. From the CPSC estimations for People in Need (PIN) of humanitarian assistance in Abyei, Abiemnom and Pariang including the limited targets for assistance in 2022 are as follows. The PIN for children with specialized needs especially IDP & Returnees is 8328 IDP children (7,071 girls and boys 281) of these IHA will target 2,227 children (1,946 girls & 281 boys). While returnees number of children in need is 1,203 (1,021 girls and 182 boys) of this IHA will target 572 children (500 girls & 72 boys) as per the target set by CPSC. In Abiemnom county, CPSC estimates that 1,542 children (1,309 girls & 233 boys) of IDP are in need of assistance and IHA will target 491 children (girls 429 & boys 62) While Returnees estimated to be in need of assistance are 7 (6 girls & 1 boy), IHA will target all the 7. In Pariang, CPSC estimates that of the IDP, 4,579 children (3,980 girls & 599 boys) are in need of humanitarian assistance and IHA will target 1,391 of these children (1,210 girls & 181 boys) while returnees of need of humanitarian need is 1,791 children (1,521 girls & 270 boys), IHA will target 643 children (562 girls and 81 boys) as per the CPSC recommendation.

2. Project activities

The main activities to be implemented by IHA in Abyei, Pariang and Abiemnom counties will be the identification of vulnerable girls and boys, including adolescents and children with disabilities who are at risk will receive individual and specialized case management services that meet their unique needs. IHA will also identify unaccompanied and separated girls and boys, document them and carry out family tracing and reunification services (UASC-FTR). Among the activities to be implemented are, girls and boys including children with disabilities will be provided with focused and non-focused MHPSS and other related services. Adult beneficiaries, women and men will receive psychosocial support and knowledge through parenting sessions. Communities will be reached age and gender specific messages through awareness raising activities in child protection and women and men will be trained on child protection in emergencies (CPiE) including case management, PSS, etc).
