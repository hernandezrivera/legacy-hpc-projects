Project Summary

Access and distribution of sufficient and clean water for drinking, sanitation and production purposes in Tigrai remains in critical condition for many years and unfortunately the ongoing conflict has been largely affecting the limited resources the services in various ways. The role of clean water supply in realization of healthy communities is quite critical. Good health is a key factor to socio-economic development of any community, because healthy people are more productive and have greater cognitive ability than unhealthy people. The limited access to clean water in Tigrai is found to be very dire in big towns and cities where hosting large number of IDPs and other war affected groups.
In the last 30 years the government of Tigrai has been trying their best to improve supply of potable water and coverage on access to clean water had reached above 50% despite the variation among different areas. Similarly, services on hygiene and sanitation facilities were among the major focus areas of the government, communities and NGOs in rural areas that have been registered substantial results in the sector. The ongoing war, however, has reversed these all achievements by destroying water points, dismantling hand pumps and motorized generators, damaging and looting of water distribution pipes and systems, dispersing of WASH committees. These all interferences have seriously affected access to clean water as well as hygiene and sanitation services throughout Tigrai and the supply of potable water coverage is expected to highly decreased although it requires thorough assessment.
Access to clean water is measured with different parameters like type of water sources (protected and unprotected), travelling distance to fetch water from main sources, knowledge and experiences in hygiene and sanitation, household water consumption, geographical locations in relation with availability of various options for water supply, etc. Before the war the average household drinking water utilization per day in the Tigrai was below the average rate of WHO recommendation and similarly water utilization per person was below the Growth and Transformation Plan (GTP II) which was standard 20 liters/person. Unfortunately, the war has caused further deteriorations of the limited resources and expanded the gap in water supply, hygiene and sanitation services.
To improve access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation in Tigrai in general and assist war affected groups in particular remains as an assignment to the people and government of Tigrai as well as other development partners. In relation to this, ERDA has taken a leading role and designed a project proposal to improve access to water, hygiene and sanitation in war affected communities in Tigrai in the coming 12 months.

1. - Projects activities

ERDA is planning to implement a comprehensive project known as rehabilitation of war affected people in Tigrai with major focus on unaccompanied children, women and elders. In connection with this, ERDA is exercising to establish nine clusters throughout Tigrai in order to accommodate the 70,300 beneficiaries and the nine centres will serve as a cluster for adjacent weredas will be established based on their geographical locations, livelihood zonation and level of damages by the war. The nine clusters include Wurko, Mekelle, Shire, Aksum, Chilla, Abiyi Adi, Mehoni and Della. The project comprises temporary institutional carrying for the affected children until they reunified with their families; and home based support for women and elders through Income Generation Activities (IGA) to possess different livelihood components. This project will, therefore, seek to improve the supply of clean water and improve hygiene and sanitation in the nine clusters and local communities around the nine clusters with major focus on the conflict affected households. Components of the project will mainly emphasize on two major interventions include hardware and software parts. The hardware part will emphasis on the improvement of clean water supply through establishment of water points and related systems to distribute water to the community. The software part includes installation of sanitation facilities, establishments of local institutions like WASH committees, awareness creations of beneficiary groups and local communities, improve knowledge and practices of the community, establishments of sound systems for sustainability of effective hygiene and sanitations. From these project activities more than 100,000 war affected households will be benefited throughout Tigrai with major focus on the highly affected areas. ERDA in collaboration with government sector lines, local administrations, institutions and communities will undertake screening of eligible beneficiaries based on transparent and concrete selection criteria then short term technical training will be given according to the types of activity to be involved. With the support of this project nine deep wells will be constructed in the nine clusters to ensure year round clean water supply in the temporary carrying camps to serve the unaccompanied and separated children as well as to the surrounding communities. The construction of nine deep wells will include installations of systems like reservoirs, delivery pipes, distribution fountains, etc. for efficient utilizations of the available water. Besides, to improve hygiene and sanitation of the beneficiary children ERDA is planning to construct communal toilets, bathing facilities, washing basins, etc. in the nine camps. To benefit the local communities within the cluster, the project will construct water distribution points and community serving dry pit latrines. The project will also focus on awareness creation and capacity building activities within the camp and local community levels through provisions of technical trainings, exposure visits, modelling practices, etc. to improve knowledge and management of beneficiaries. The project will give due attention for the sustainability of the proposed activities through installation of working systems like direct involvements of beneficiary communities in planning, implementation and monitoring and evaluation processes, cost sharing approach from local communities in labour as well as local materials, establishments of local institutions like WASH committee, installations of fair payments from the beneficiaries for the services to be used for further maintenance and managements.

2.1.1 - Is it better to use cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project is mainly focused on the installations of hardware like water supply and sanitation infrastructures at the nine camps and surrounding communities that will largely implement through private construction companies. On the other hand, the software part which includes the improvements of knowledge, information and attitude of beneficiary households towards hygiene and sanitation through continuous trainings and awareness creation activities. The envisaged project will apply both cash and voucher assistance approach during implementation processes depending on the nature of expenses and sometimes beneficiary households may receive in kind supports like concrete slabs, ventilation PVC pipes and related materials to promote hygiene and sanitation at household levels. Basic hardware expenses of the project include construction of deep wells and system installation as well as construction of dry latrines for communal uses will be implemented in cash payments through private companies. Similarly, beneficiary training on hygiene and sanitation will dominantly be covered through cash payments to the trainers and training materials. Nevertheless, some interventions like introducing of concrete and plastic slabs (SatoPan) and ventilation pipes to beneficiary households will be implemented in kind supplies. Once the beneficiary households have received technical trainings on hygiene and sanitations each household will gain sanitary devices to be installed in each house. The construction of slabs will be linked with income generation activities of the project areas that a group of people from the communities will be responsible to produce the required inputs aimed at the promotions of hygiene and sanitation with the objective to introduce sanitation market at community level. The role of the project and relevant government sectors will be limited to capacity building as well as monitoring and evaluation of the project. The project will encourage and facilitate self-initiated group formation of trained groups to promote sanitation market for effective and efficient hygiene and sanitation practices. Groups will be responsible for the production of required inputs and install at household levels to make hygiene and sanitation as part of livelihood of the beneficiary communities that would ensure sustainability of the project.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

It is known that community health is cumulative effects of individual households who give due attention to personal as well as family health cares. The day-to-day hygiene and sanitation practices both at household and community level is also affected by factors like knowledge, information and attitude of individuals within a given community. In relation to this, beneficiary households will be actively participated in project designing, planning, implementation and monitoring proposed activities. The participation of stakeholders mainly beneficiary is important to adequately ensure ownership, sustainability of the schemes and explore all options and come up with feasible development option or recommendation.To ensure the participation of the beneficiaries in planning and development of hygiene and sanitation facilities the project will actively work to make the beneficiary to be part of the planning on the construction of water supplies and sanitation facilities. Awareness of the beneficiaries on their rights and responsibilities greatly determines the users to claim or ask when they feel there is a problem and properly fulfill what is expected from them. The project will give due attention on improvement of awareness of beneficiary communities on their rights and responsibilities include getting safe and adequate in nearby water point, voicing on tariff setting and WASH committee election etc. Providing feedback and establishing proper complaint mechanism are essential to ensure transparency and accountability in any system. Similarly, in the promotion of hygiene and sanitation the establishment of local institutions like WASH committee to be responsible for the overall management of the services as they will be elected by the water users need to report overall performance including financial report. Similarly, there should be a system to entertain complaints from beneficiaries on the supply of adequate safe water, equity distribution, management and maintenance of infrastructures, rate and frequency of water users’ fee, etc. The project will establish system for complains on hygiene and sanitation services and enable beneficiaries to clearly understand where to report complaints regarding the services. One of the major focuses of the project in terms of effectively managing of their hygiene and sanitation services is that the creation of transparent system and common understanding among beneficiary households through updating of water services in regular bases and mediating with other service providers like the government water office. Accordingly, the project will create regular meeting forums within the beneficiary communities as meetings are often considered an indicator of participation. Meetings remain one of the important forums for sharing information, decision-making and towards effective management of water scheme. Ultimate objective of the envisaged project is to provide hygiene and sanitation services of the war affected households through the installations of water supply and sanitation, provision of technical trainings and sanitary materials aimed at enhancing of household hygiene and sanitation services. Promoters of this project understand that the involvement of conflict affected households in hygiene and sanitation activities is a vital integral of empowerments of the war victims, they know from experience that better healthcare would enable the beneficiaries to involve in productive sphere and generate more incomes and feed their families. In connection with this, the envisaged project will have clear vision on participatory approach to work with government and non-governmental organization starting from beneficiary targeting up to graduation. In connection with this, ERDA has been organized consultative workshops with government sectors and donor agencies to enrich the proposed five years rehabilitation plan and signed cooperative agreement with six government bureaus. Elshadai with its partners will work strongly on capacity building of the beneficiary communities to improve knowledge, attitude and practices of beneficiary households and family members on hygiene and sanitation. The local communities are expected to actively participate in planning, implementation, as well as monitoring and evaluation of the input supplies with beneficiary households.
