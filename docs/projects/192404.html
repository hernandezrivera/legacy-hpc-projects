Project Summary

In line with the Emergency Response Plan (ERP), World Vision in Lebanon will address the unmet immediate basic needs, including food needs, of the most vulnerable Lebanese and Migrant populations in the areas of Akkar and Baalbeck-El Hermel. The current situation in Lebanon including the multi-faceted socio-economic crisis, increase in cost of living, and breakdown of essential basic services in the country has taken a toll on the most vulnerable individuals, especially persons with specific needs including children and people with disabilities. The amount of available funding in relation to the growing needs on the ground is becoming increasingly divergent. This has been the case with WVL’s WFP funded cash and in-kind assistance program in Akkar and Bekaa whereby the number of self-referrals through WVL’s hotline is far exceeding the budgeted targets for assistance. As such, WVL will leverage its presence in the areas, its long-standing partnerships with municipalities and communities, and its internal existing structure to ensure the provision of relief food and basic needs assistance to the most vulnerable individuals not covered by on-going and planned interventions until December 2022. The aforementioned will improve cost-efficiency and increase the readiness to start the distribution with a lead time of one month followed by 5 months of distributions.
To assess potential beneficiaries, WVL will apply its vulnerability assessment and scoring process and tool, while applying a validation process and quality control on both data and process. The fact that WVL is the sole implementing partner for WFP under the same type of intervention is of added value as it will significantly reduce any risks of de-duplication. To further mitigate this risk, WVL will make sure to conduct an inter-agency de-duplication exercise to ensure that the eligible families are not receiving similar assistance from other actors.
WVL's proposed intervention consists of using a blended approach of in-kind food parcels that contain staple food items (flour, sugar, vegetable oil) along with cash assistance to secure a regular supply of fresh foods. It will target a total of 1,600 beneficiaries (800 in Akkar, 800 in Baalbeck-El Hermel).
In Lebanon’s context, staple food commodities are subdued to wholesalers’ monopolies and retailers withhold food items for price speculations. In such a context, in-kind food assistance of these staple food items offers vulnerable households certainty of regular supply of staples such flour, sugar, vegetable oil, etc. On the other hand, it is not viable to include perishable food items in the in-kind food parcels. Vulnerable households however need a regular supply of fresh food items including bread, tubers such as potatoes and onions, dairy products and fresh vegetables which they consume on daily basis and adds to their required food diversity. In this case, cash assistance is a more appropriate mechanism as it enables vulnerable households to secure a regular supply of fresh foods that are locally produced.
The blended approach provides a buffering element that rounds the edges of any increase of SMEB, and reduces the impact on vulnerable households.
WVL proposes giving the assistance in USD to minimize losses related to the different accessible exchange rates (LBP, Sayrafa, Black Market rate...).

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

WVL will provide food assistance to individuals not covered by ongoing and planned interventions until December 2022 through a blended assistance of in-kind and cash to 1600 individuals (800 in Akkar, 800 in Baalbeck - El Hermel). Through this modality, the household would monthly receive an In-kind staple food parcel coupled with an amount in cash ensure all food and dietary needs are covered. WVL develop outreach lists from households who reached out to the WVL hotline and weren’t targeted in WFP projects. The outreach list will then be assessed using WVL’s vulnerability assessment, while applying a validation process and quality control on both data and process. Once households are confirmed, distribution plans will be developed. For in-kind assistance, a message will be shared with each household describing the type of assistance as well as the place, date and time for distribution and documents needed for identification. For cash assistance, households will present their identification cards to the service provider and receive an awareness and information session on how to cash the assistance. WVL has an ongoing agreement with a cash service provider (BoB Finance) that allows transfers in USD. All distribution sites will then be set to ensure accountability, transparency, and dignity for all beneficiaries. By the end of the project, WVL will implement post-implementation monitoring while collecting necessary information for the evaluation of the intervention.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Women and girls among others are subjected to greater protection risks and discrimination based on gender, nationality, age and capacities. They lose opportunities to access life-saving services, are excluded or not prioritized to receive assistance and bear risks of being exposed to various types of exploitation. WVL will ensure that distribution sites are gender sensitive and will prioritize gender considerations like the weight of the parcel, gender training of staff, ensuring the presence of male and female staff to support men and women. Gender-responsive questions will be incorporated into the routine project monitoring and evaluation disaggregated and analyzed by sex and age. WVL supports the inclusion of people with disabilities in its programs. The inclusion process focuses on lessening the barriers to disability by identifying PWDs using WGQ, raising awareness on the community level and tailoring activities whenever needed.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

WVL will guarantee the presence of a functional and accessible Accountability system to enhance beneficiary and community participation as well as ownership of project design and implementation. Targeted beneficiaries will have access to information packages including information about WVL, the project and the community response mechanism via SMS, flyers, helplines, Service map platform and information sessions held at field level. The Accountability team will ensure that a help desk is available during major activities and distributions. A hotline is available for raising complaints, and providing tailored information. Complaints relating to the implemented project or to WVL’s area of intervention will be handled internally and closed within the timeframe after proper actions are taken to solve it. Complaints that are not related to WV (health or legal sectors, etc.) will be referred to the relevant agency (through RIMS).Monthly reports will be drafted to share project findings.
