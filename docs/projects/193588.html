Project Summary

In 2022, UNRWA will seek to provide comprehensive WASH assistance for Palestine refugees through: (1) solid waste management (target beneficiaries: 276,332 residents of 19 UNRWA camps), (2) improved access to sanitation and hygiene (target beneficiaries: 276,332 residents of 19 UNRWA camps), (3) floods mitigation and prevention (target beneficiaries: 276,332 residents of 19 UNRWA camps), and (4) improved access to WASH services in institutions (target beneficiaries: 45,692 students of 96 UNRWA schools and 694,006 persons served at UNRWA health care facilities). Through these activities, UNRWA aims to ensure that the vulnerable population in the West Bank improve their access to equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed WASH services. UNRWA strives to improve the safety and accessibility of complaints, including complaints of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The project has multiple channels to receive and respond to complaints which are adapted to the target population (including a local phone line for troubleshooting and complaints, face-to-face reporting via social workers and camp service officers, and hotlines for SEA and serious misconduct complaints) and UNRWA SOPs abide to CBCM SOPs from different target populations, including vulnerable groups. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor centered approach in how the complaint is handled and support is offered to the survivor. UNRWA WBFO works closely with the PSEA Network and will undertake compulsory reporting and external referrals.

Implementing Partners

The United Nations Relief and Work Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) has fundamental humanitarian operational presence in the West Bank, both geographically and in terms of sectors of intervention. Next to the Palestinian Authority, UNRWA is the biggest service provider in the West Bank. In total, the Agency serves 1,111,064 Palestine refugees and other registered persons (UNRWA, Registered Population Dashboard, June 2022), which amounts to one-third of the population in the West Bank, of whom more than half a million are women and girls. UNRWA was established by the General Assembly in 1949 and is mandated to assist and protect some 5.7 million registered Palestine refugees in the region. As the main service provider for Palestine refugees in the region, UNRWA delivers primary education, health care, relief and social services, infrastructure and camp improvement, microfinance, and emergency assistance. The Agency has extensive experience providing emergency assistance to Palestine refugees during humanitarian crises to save lives, preserve human dignity, and prevent and respond to the impacts of such crises. In the West Bank, the Agency's programmes seek to reduce the impact of the protection crisis stemming from over 50 years of the Israeli occupation by addressing the critical needs of the most affected individuals. In terms of response, UNRWA provides critical services to those exposed to protection threats, including psycho-social counselling, medical assistance, cash-based interventions, and the provision of dedicated programming to specific vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian context of the WB, including EJ, remains directly tied to the impact of the Israeli occupation. Representing one-third of the WB population, many of the 891,384 Palestine refugees continue to be one of the most vulnerable segments of Palestine society. As of 2022, approx. 25% of Palestine refugees were recorded to be living in the 19 camps established across the WB, and the number is only increasing every year. The volume of solid waste generated in camps is also increasing. According to the results obtained during a waste generation study conducted by the international NGO Cesvi, on average residents of the camp generate per capita 0.52 kg/day. In 2021, 54,133 tons of waste were collected from 19 camps in the WB. From January to September 2022, 42,889.18 tons of solid waste have already been collected. Such amount is beyond UNRWA’s operational capacities. UNRWA employs over 180 environmental health staff including sanitation labourers who perform solid waste collection with manual carts and face occupational health and safety risks. Waste piles, in fact, may contain sharp or infectious materials, as for example syringes, pieces of glasses, small dead animals, while the movement of waste may cause the fall of objects. Other challenges include lack of sufficient number of solid waste containers and deteriorating SWM fleet. Due to the lack of resources, UNRWA’s solid waste management service is often disrupted, creating additional public health risks through overflowed containers and piled up containers. According to MSNA 2022 result, 15.7% of in-camp refugees reported waste accumulation for more than three days out of the seven days prior to data collection, as opposed to 13.6% for non-refugees. UNRWA has been also working hand-in-hand and with partners to address the absence of sewer networks in refugee camps across the WB. So far, only 11 out of 19 refugee camps in the WB have adequate sewer networks. Without sewerage networks, many camp residents resort to cesspits for the disposal of wastewater, and/or discharge wastewater into streets, especially in winter, as residents cannot afford the high cost of sewage tankers at this time. Most cesspits have been constructed without engineering oversight, increasing the risk of leakage and contamination of groundwater. Additionally, because the discharging of the cesspits occurs irregularly and does not consistently meet quality standards, sewage often pollutes the living area around the cesspit. These practices have been contributing to environmental degradation and also a public health concern. Furthermore, sewerage and storm water system blockage is a problem experienced by all camps across the WB, with serious implications for the residents. During winter, some houses get flooded by sewerage water due to the setting down of leaves, mud, rubbles and other materials into the pipes. Recently due to climate change, flooding became more frequent and have caused significant losses of assets, displacement, disrupted access to livelihoods and services, and exacerbated the risk of waterborne diseases. Women bear the brunt of the hardship in flooded homes, as they are expected to continue fulfilling their domestic duties under these adverse conditions. Due to the traditional gender division of labor in Palestinian society, women and girls bear the bulk of the burden related to the shortage of WASH services. In order to minimize the damage of flooding, the sewerage network therefore needs constant maintenance by both sanitation labourers and engineers. In the WB, UNRWA operates 96 schools that host 45,692 students and 1,640 teachers every day as well as 43 health clinics where 886,475 medical consultations take place annually. Accommodating a large number of populations, maintenance and rehabilitation of WASH facilities are constantly needed in these UNRWA operated institutions. Furthermore, UNRWA must accelerate the maintenance and rehabilitation of WASH facilities, especially toilets, in school from a protection standpoint. Today, 10 out of 96 UNRWA operated schools conform to the WASH Standards for Schools in Low-Cost Settings issued by WHO. The impact of UNRWA failing to meet the standards was identified in the internal consultation process in 2018 that “toilets were repeatedly perceived as unsafe, with some students and staff seeing them as major hotspots for violence and bullying.” In addition, 4% of refugee households reported to have children with disability, and 27.2% of them reported that the school infrastructure (non-classroom, including WASH) is the major challenge for children with disabilities to access education services (MSNA 2022). At UNRWA, consultations with beneficiaries on complaint channels (including specific consultations on SEA complaints) are planned through several health and relief interventions implemented in 2021/2022. Through its relief and social services programme, UNRWA has developed a survey for Q4 2021 to gather feedback and preferences from beneficiaries on complaints mechanism. Specifically on SEA complaint channels, consultations will take place with women and girls, including women with disabilities, single headed households and elderly women, in partnership with CBO partners supporting GBV survivors. The MSNA 2022 data found that 8.5% of refugee HHs reporting awareness of how to access and use complaint or reporting mechanisms (compared to only 1.7% of non-refugee HH). While this is a significant improvement and an indication that UNRWA’s efforts to raise awareness on how to access and use complaint or reporting mechanisms are producing results, there is still a need to strengthen these mechanisms.

2 - Activities

In 2022, UNRWA will seek to provide comprehensive WASH assistance for Palestine refugees through following activities. (Activity 1: Solid Waste Management) (1.1-The provision of domestic solid waste containers/bins) UNRWA will distribute household bins among selected households in the camp, aiming to improve the hygiene condition of the camp. The target households will be selected among the camp residents, sanitation labors, Infrastructure and Camp Improvement Programme (ICIP) staff. In order to improve waste storage inside camps, UNRWA will procure and install household bins to mitigate the overflow of garbage in the public space.(1.2-The provision of community cleaning materials and tools) The procurement of personal protective equipment (gloves, shirts, trousers, boots, high-visibility vest, hats, masks, front lights, etc.) is essential for sanitation labourers as they are required to manually collect the waste bags from the streets, throw them in containers and sweep. In the early morning, the phosphorescent vest helps the labourers to be seen in the darkness and to avoid possible accidents with motorised vehicles. (Activity 2: Improved Access to Sanitation and Hygiene - The extension/rehabilitation of sewer collection network and HH connections)(2.1) UNRWA will establish household connection to the sewer collection network for 200 households in Aqbat Jabr refugee camp. With the completion of this project, all households in the Aqbat Jabr camp will be connected to the sewer collection system which safely transport the waste water to Jericho waste water plant. (2.2) UNRWA will install new sewage line of 200 meters in the contaminated area of Far’a refugee camp. (2.3) In all 19 UNRWA camps, UNRWA’s engineers and sanitation labours will maintain and conduct small scale rehabilitation of the existing sewer lines in order to prevent the clogging of sewer lines which leads to the overflow of sewerage water. (Activity 3: Floods Mitigation and Prevention)(3.1-The construction/rehabilitation of storm water network) UNRWA will install new stormwater line of 200 meters in the contaminated area of Far’a refugee camp so as to separate sewage from storm water.(3.2 -The provision of storm water systems critical operation and maintenance materials and tools) In all 19 UNRWA camps, UNRWA’s engineers and sanitation labours will clean and maintain the existing stormwater system in order to prevent floodings expected in the winter. (Activity 4: The construction/rehabilitation of WASH facilities in HCFs and in schools) Lastly, UNRWA will conduct maintenance and rehabilitation of WASH facilities, targeting 96 schools and 43 health clinics in the WB. Engineers of ICIP respond to the reporting of problems from each facility, conduct technical and situational analysis, and develop an appropriate maintenance/rehabilitation plan. All engineers have received trainings in August 2022 to appropriately consider the need of persons with disabilities in their work, and other needs will also be taken into account through consultations with the beneficiaries. (Common Activities) All UNRWA project-related documents contain a standard clause on PSEA in accordance with IASC principles and guidance, including all documents related to this project. To ensure the execution of PSEA, WB Field Office has developed an internal system to receive and respond to SEA complaints in accordance with the oPt PSEA SOPs on Inter Agency Referrals, UNRWA guidance and internal SOPs on Case Management for the WBFO that support frontline staff to safely identify and refer protection cases within the agency. Additionally, project staff will complete at least one PSEA training during the project period, building on the previous safe identification and referral training that 80% of WBFO staff had completed in 2020/21. This training has been developed for all UNRWA fields of operation and will be adapted to meet the needs of the West Bank staff from all departments and programmes. All frontline staff who interact with beneficiaries from vulnerable groups, including sanitation labours, are considered priorities for this initiative. UNRWA strives to ensure that all project related contractors will take PSEA training and will continue to advocate this with Palestinian Contractor Union. Complaints of SEA will be handled by PSEA focal points who will ensure a survivor centered approach in how the complaint is handled and support is offered to the survivor. UNRWA WBFO works closely with the PSEA Network and will raise issues requiring technical support and/or external referral through this forum. Within this project, UNRWA will conduct a minimum of 3 focus group discussions to discuss the establishment of community-based complaint mechanisms (CBCMs), awareness activities and community mobilisation interventions on PSEA including how to report SEA-related complaints. Randomly chosen beneficiaries of different gender, age, and vulnerability type will be selected to gather voices of all target beneficiaries, and the result of this discussions will be reflected to improve the existing complaint mechanism. This project will disseminate PSEA awareness raising materials including information on rights and safe complaint channels at all 19 camps including Camp Service Officer’s Office, where UNRWA currently receive most of general complaints from the camp residents, as well as schools and health clinics. Age and gender-sensitive materials have been developed by UNRWA targeting both staff and beneficiaries and will be disseminated on general rights of the beneficiaries, available complaint channels (including specific consultations on SEA complaints) and how to access survivor-centered assistance.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The project adopts a do no harm and conflict sensitivity approach, including specific measures to ensure that identified risks are addressed within the action and unintended negative consequences are minimised. UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. UNRWA’s Case Management SOPs ensure that protection cases are identified and referred to specialised support services. SOPs cover compulsory reporting on SEA and support to survivors. A dedicated psychological consultation hotline is available to all refugees. UNRWA has existing guidelines for disability inclusion, gender equality, PSEA and child protection which are all fully applied in this project. In UNRWA's Protection Strategy (2012), the prioritisation of safety and dignity is explicitly address as a key protection standard where UNRWA programmes deliver services in a manner that ensures the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and staff. UNRWA ensures that its actions do not cause unintended harm or result in reduced protection of beneficiaries, communities or staff, including risk mitigation for SEA. On a biannual basis all of UNRWA's activities are subject to a protection mainstreaming audit, which assesses the integration of protection principles in UNRWA's work. This audit included extensive interviews and focus group discussions with beneficiaries, and sought to assess UNRWA's application of the four main protection principles, including the principle of Do No Harm. In 2019 UNRWA developed a new information management system for the collection of sensitive protection monitoring and documentation data; specifically the system now separates out biographical data from incident related data, with individual cases being only referred to through generated case code. For the purposes of ensuring that briefings involving affected persons, are carried out in a way that prioritises the safety and dignity of these persons, UNRWA has developed an internal guideline for ethical practices in the context of briefings, field visits, communications and others. This includes a checklist to guide all staff working in this capacity. UNRWA utilizes proxy means testing formula to determine the poverty level of the household, which considers not only the consumption levels but also other aspects of vulnerability, such as the presence of chronic diseases, housing characteristics and accessibility to utilities, as well as household composition (e.g. the number of elderly and persons with disabilities, and female-headed households). UNRWA ensures that vulnerable individuals can access assistance by external referrals. UNRWA has developed dedicated case management SOPs and internal/external referral pathways, to ensure that the comprehensive needs are met. In various areas of operations, UNRWA engages with a range of community representatives, village councils, camp resident committees – as well as holding focus group discussions including for women, children, youth and the elderly in target communities. In the context of the COVID-19 response, additional steps taken to ensure UNRWA's activities prioritise safety and dignity. UNRWA has adapted its case management in response to difficulty of physical access to concerned individuals/households.
