Project Summary

In response to the immediate need for support to the increasing numbers of IDPs in northwest Syria, including the negative impact of COVID-19 infection to the far-flung communities in Idleb, IHH proposed to extend the work of two mobile clinics to provide medical services during this COVID-19 crisis inside NW Syria.
Two mobile clinics provided by IHH provided services in the underserved communities ( IDPs in the camps ) who currently face challenges to access to health services due to distance and transportation difficulties.
The mobile clinics provide medical services according to Essential Health Services Package (EHSP) which was developed by WHO and are the following:
1.Integrated management of childhood illness (IMCI)
2.Reproductive health (RH) through the gynecology clinic.
3.Management of non-communicable diseases (NCD) through the GP clinic where all referred chronic cases (elderly, women, and children) can be treated.
4.Management of communicable diseases (CD) through both clinics that provide preventable and curative treatment as well as services that have great roles in the life-saving of children.
5. Nutrition and community health services:
like life-saving preventive and curative nutrition services among children under 5 (boys and girls) and PLWs.
Where 2 CHWs conduct community outreach activities promoting on IYCF, Maternal nutrition, health, and WASH-COVID-19 -19 and related topics and massages based on Health and nutrition cluster recommendations in the context of COVID-19.
Each mobile clinic contains medical team consists of: GP, midwife, lab technician, nurse and 2 CHWs in addition to 1 Data Entry for both clinics.
Each mobile clinic has lab room includes the following equipment (Hematology analyzer, CBC (Complete blood counter), Hematology Centrifuge Table top centrifuge, Microscope, Auto clave and Refrigerator &Freezer) which will provide blood and urine analyses.
The mobile clinics will operate 8 hours during 5 days per week serving about 25000 beneficiaries during 6 months.
IHH ensure the proper application of CRM mechanism as it will be informed with the targeted community along with hotline, WhatsApp number, Facebook page, complaints box and field monitor to keep track of the services provided and response to the complaints effectively. The project will be monitored throughout the project through field visits, checklists of the beneficiaries, staff, work methodology, and tools and equipment of the mobile clinics.
IHH will apply IPC protocol to avoid any spread of COVID-19 which includes.
-provide PPE equipment and IPC supplies.
- follow up triage protocol at the entry of the facility.
- refer any suspected cases according to the health cluster guidelines.
IHHwill coordinate regularly with the IHD, Health cluster, submitting 4Ws and coordinate with the HFs and NGOs operating in the area.
INSAN Charity will lead the project for 6 months.

4 - Needs

Compared to 2020, the health sector PiN has increased 4% with the largest absolute change seen in Idleb governorate where the PiN increased more than 220,000. A larger health sector PiN reflects the concurrent stressors of the protracted crisis, the COVID-19 pandemic and the economic downturn in Syria. As the COVID-19 pandemic has revealed, a weakened health system poses a risk to the entire population and therefore efforts must be undertaken to increase the responsiveness and resilience of the health system as well as its capacityto protect vulnerable groups. Emergency, stop-gap measures such as task-shifting and training must be complemented by longer-term efforts to address retention and shortages of health care workers such as training and production of clinical staff. There is also a need to leverage existing interventions in parts of Syria, such essential health services packages, to guarantee immediate availability of humanitarian health services while also laying the foundation for health system recovery. Based on the meeting with Idlib health directorate, there was a recommendation to address the basic health needs of the Syrian community affected by the conflict especially IDPs in the camps through mobile clinics project.

5 - Implementation of activities

1- The project provided 8898 consultations to IDPS in the camps during two months November and December 2020.2- The project staff is well qualified where it was trained and followed by medical doctors on the ground through daily visits to work sites.3- The 2 mobile clinics are well equipped with modern devices that include (lab equipment, echo devices, nebulizers ………and many other equipment and devices.4- The mobile clinics moved weekly between the camps to make access to medical services easier for IDPs.5- The clinics are provided with medicine and children supplies by IHH in addition to medicine provided by WHO.6- The clinics apply SOP for triage of suspected COVID-19 patients where it referred about 20 cases to isolation centers and PCR test centers.7- The clinics provided nutrition and community health awareness sessions about COVID-19, washing, vaccinations, and other topics to CU5 and PLW.8- The project achieve complementarity between health and FSL needs where INSAN&IHH targets the following camps with food assistance distribution.9- The project applied the referral system where 15 SAM complicated cases of CU5 referred to the specialized hospital to receive treatment to save their lives during November and December 2020.10- The project provided treatment for 56 cases of MAM CU5 and 20 cases of MAM of PLW.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Thefragilehealth system in Syria continues to face concurrent emergencies1and chronic challenges2which affect the availability3and quality of health services across Syria,as well as the physical and mental wellbeing of the population.With more than 164,000 confirmed cases and close to 5,800 deaths4,COVID-19 continues to disrupt the already-fragile health services and systemsdue tolow levels of COVID-19 vaccination, lack of adherence to preventivepublic healthmeasures,and emerging variantswhich, together,strain attempts to stabilize and restart services affected by the pandemic, including routine childhood immunization services which are reporting reduced coverage rates5. Security incidents in northeast Syria persist alongside renewed hostilities in Dar’awhich displacedmore than 140,000 persons6in need of inclusive emergency health services. These trendshavealso been accompanied byan uptick in attacks on healthcare which are on track to reach or exceed the total number of reported attacks in 2020.
