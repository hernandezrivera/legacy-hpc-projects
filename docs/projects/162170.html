Project Summary

The Gender in Humanitarian Action Working Group (GiHA WG) for the Rohingya Refugee crisis response in Cox’s Bazar provides cross-sectoral support to ensure the integration of gender aspects in humanitarian action with the following key focus areas: i)Coordination ii)Technical advice, guidance and capacity development iii) advocacy iv) assessment, analysis and monitoring v) information sharing and management. The GiHA WG is co-chaired by UN Women and UNHCR and its members gender focal points representing sectors, WGs and humanitarian organisations. The GiHA WG coordinates and works closely with the ISCG Gender Hub. The Gender Hub aims to strengthen the accountability of humanitarian actors for gender equality and empowerment of women and girls from Rohingya refugees and host communities. To do so, the Gender Hub provides support to all humanitarian actors in terms of 1) technical guidance at a strategic level, including hands-on support for mainstreaming gneder in the response, 2) capacity building on gender mainstreaming and gender transformative programming, 3) knowledge management and sharing for good practices on gender-mainstreaming in and gender transformative humanitarian interventions.

Needs

Pre-existing gender inequalities, gender-based violence (GBV) and discrimination are often reinforced, perpetuated and exacerbated during crisis and this is disproportionately affecting women, girls and the most vulnerable and marginalized Rohingya refugee population groups. Gendered isolation and restricted mobility of women and girls according to traditional norms of Rohingya are limiting their access to life-saving assistance, services and information as well as their participation in decision making. Rohingya women and girls are not merely vulnerable victims. They play a key role in increasing the resilience of families and communities. There are Rohingya women leaders, including survivors of conflict related sexual and gender-based violence, that are self-organizing and forming their own groups in the camps to advocate for their rights and call for justice. Leveraging women’s participation and leadership capacities is not only a way to ensure humanitarian efforts respond to the specific needs and vulnerabilities of affected communities – whether women, men, girls or boys- but also a strategic investment in whole community resilience. For these reasons, integrating gender equality measures as well as age and diversity dimensions into the cross-sectoral crisis response efforts is critical to ensuring that women, girls, boys and men — particularly the most vulnerable and marginalized — have equitable access to (and benefit from) relief, services and information. This will improve humanitarian programming and make it rights-based and more effective in meeting the needs of Rohingya Refugees. Sectors should make gender equality a central priority in Sector Response Plans, which will guide project partners in designing projects that meet the distinct needs of women, girls, boys and men equally/equitably. While the humanitarian response is based on the IASC Policy and Accountability Framework on Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women and Girls in Humanitarian Action, and the Protection Framework, gaps such as lack of sex and age-disaggregated data and understanding of the gendered impacts of crisis, lack of substantive women’s participation and leadership in humanitarian response, lack of targeted livelihood opportunities for women and insufficient protection mechanisms for women continue to impede the realization of effective gender-responsive policies and programmes in humanitarian and crisis response. During the midterm review of the JRP 2019 conducted by the GiHA Working Group and Gender Hub reported the achievements by various sectors, it also highlighted a lack of understanding among humanitarian staff on the importance and need to mainstream gender across the response; lack of prioritization of the issue; and persisting gaps in inclusion and access to services across camps and sectors for persons with specific needs. These gender gaps in the response were further elaborated in the Gender Hub’s Gender Reflection Report (September 2019). The Gender Capacity Assessment conducted in May 2019 by the Gender Hub and GiHA WG also found gaps in humanitarian actors’ understanding of gender issues, ability to conduct gender assessment and analysis and capacity to translate these into effective gender responsive and gender transformative programmes.

Activities or outputs

Through the GiHA WG, UN Women will continue to support gender responsive humanitarian action across humanitarian sectors and humanitarian stakeholders in the following key areas:Coordination:- Convening regular meetings of gender focal points, advisers and advocates from each sector and key organisations to exchange information and address gender equality issues (needs, gaps, response efforts) in sectors’ response- Identifying opportunities for learning of good practices across sectors, undertake capacity development on gender-responsive humanitarian action, preparedness, resilience and recovery- Including local women’s rights organisations and networks and local gender equality actors in the GiHA WG to promote their influence and engagement in all aspects of response, including through engagement in ISCG coordination structures, camp management and oversight committees- Convening sub-WG on women’s leadership and implement key joint activities to promote women's leadershipAdvocacy- Promote ISCG sectors and organizations’ commitment to gender equality and women’s empowerment in humanitarian action by all humanitarian actors in the response- Organise joint GiHA WG advocacy campaigns and events e.g. 16 days of activism against GBV, World Refugee Day, etcAssessment, Analysis and Monitoring- Provide inputs to Gender Hub led gender analysis and gender-responsive assessments and research, and feedback mechanisms- Conduct joint GiHA WG field monitoring in camps and host communities to measure sectors’ progress on gender mainstreaming, and feed findings and recommendations back to Gender Hub, Sectors and ISCG- Promote gender-equal community led monitoring and feedback mechanisms.Information Sharing and Management- Identify, analyse and share needs, gaps, good practices and success stories in gender mainstreaming - Routinely meet and share information on gender - these issues can then be raised with the ISCG, SEG, and the UNCT Cox’s Bazar Task Force in conjunction with the Gender Hub The Gender Hub supports gender equality and the empowerment of women and girls in the humanitarian response through providing support to all humanitarian actors in the following ways:Technical assistance- Provide hands on support to sectors and partners to mainstream gender in design, implementation and M&E of their projects- Support sectors to develop gender action plans to mainstream and prioritize gender equality and the empowerment of women and girls- Support sectors and their partners to use the Gender with Age Marker to assess gender and age mainstreaming in their projectsCapacity building- Provide gender induction training for all humanitarian staff - Provide sector specific gender mainstreaming training for each of the sectors - Provide Gender Leadership in Humanitarian and Development Nexus for senior and middle management- Support capacity development of GiHA Gender Focal Points Knowledge management- Organize learning events and other workshops to strengthen evidence base for gender responsive interventions- Develop, strengthen and/or adapt gender-related guidance notes or tools to meet knowledge gaps- Develop/synthesize and disseminate gender-related research, studies and other assessment to provide greater context-specific information on the needs of women, girls, men, boys and other vulnerable groups in Rohingya and Host communities Modes of Operation- GiHA WG is co-Chaired by UN Women and UNHCR. Based on self-nomination, open to national NGOs and CSOs strongly advocating gender mainstreaming/equality, a third Co-Chair will be voted in by the GiHA WG’s standing members based on consensus. UN Women will maintain the secretariat role of GiHA WG. - Gender Hub is seconded to the ISCG by UN Women. The Gender Hub collaborates with the Protection Sector, GBV and Child Protection Sub-Sector, PSEA Network and the GiHA WG and other gender experts to build on the existing evidence, interventions, analysis and guidance.
