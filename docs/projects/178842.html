Project Summary

This program is designed to contribute to the reduction of malnutrition and mortality, improve overall health conditions for mothers & children's conflict-affected Syrians, and provide equitable access to nutrition services in order to support the overall well-being condition of mothers & children in the targeted areas, as well as to provide life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian assistance.
The intervention will focus mainly on the provision and improvement of IYCF, CMAM services.
The program will provide essential Nutrition services of many sub-districts's targeted beneficiaries through the already supported health facilities as an integration of services for reaching more comprehensive services providing in the north of Syria.
this program consists of these projects:
1- Activate the nutritional screening (MUAC screening) for pregnant and lactating women, in Darbasiyeh PHC, Afrin PHC, Bab Elhawa PHC, Muhamble PHC, Atareb PHC, SDH`s mobile clinics.
2- Implementing awareness campaigns on proper infant feeding, in Darbasiyeh PHC, Afrin PHC, Bab Elhawa PHC, Muhamble PHC, Atareb PHC, and through the SDH`s mobile clinics.
3-Refer infants who need formula (BMS) to the specialized centers in coordination with partners ( Darbasiyeh PHC, Afrin PHC, Bab Elhawa PHC, Muhamble PHC, Atareb PHC, and through the SDH`s mobile clinics).
4- Training and capacity building: two nurses in each center on the use of tools (by using the weight/ length indicator & MUAC screening) to identify cases of malnutrition among children under 5 years of age and women (Pregnant and Lactating), also on correct feeding methods for children under the age of 5 years.
5- Provide of treating malnutrition for children and women (Pregnant and Lactating) with nutritional supplements,
6- Activate the referral system for cases to:
-Cases of malnutrition associated with other diseases that require hospital care (for children under 5 years old),
-Cases of infants (under the age of two years) who breastfeed are not available, to centers specialized in distributing the formula.
the program within its all projects will provide nutrition services for targeted areas' populations including the host community and the IDPs.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 Needs and Response Summary report, the Nutrition needs & gaps figures are as follows: PEOPLE IN NEED 4.9M INDICATIVE TARGET 4.1M INDICATIVE REQUIREMENTS $60M.• Urgent and life-saving nutrition needs for 4.9 million mothers and children in Syria are anticipated in 2021, Where most of the nutrition needs lie in high severity areas.• Chronic malnutrition is anticipated to increase, with nearly 600,000 children losing their learning and developmental potentials every year in Syria.• Prevalence of maternal anemia and maternal malnutrition is expected to continue rising due to deteriorating socioeconomic conditions. In 2021, an estimated 1.2 million mothers and women of child-bearing age will continue to suffer the consequences of maternal anemia and/or malnutrition if not treated appropriately.• An estimated 141,540 children aged 6-59 months face an elevated risk of morbidity and mortality attributed to acute malnutrition, out of which 37,863 children are under the direct risk of death because of severe acute malnutrition and additional 103,677 moderately malnourished children are at risk of co-morbidities and development of severe acute malnutrition if they are not treated appropriately.• Poor dietary diversity is observed among women and children in Syria and will impact mothers’ capability tooptimally breastfeed their children. Therefore, the already low levels of poor infant feeding and caring practices will continue to be evident. Poor infant feeding and caring practices will adversely deteriorate the levels of acute and chronic malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months in Syria. A similar effect will be potentiated by maternal anemia and malnutrition.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Violence against Health Facilities and nutrition service Providers: possibility: high effect: high mitigation measures: - Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities. - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), - Providing preparedness training for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents. 2- Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding and shared toilets as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery: possibility: high, effect: high mitigation measures: Establishing separated toilets for men and women in the health facility - Ensuring that the waiting areas are sufficient for all patients – Allocating someone from the guards to regulate the flow of patients – Establishing an easy-to-reach complaints mechanism( complaints box, WhatsApp number, exit interviews) - Training all employees on the policy of preventing harassment in the workplace and including this policy in their contracts. 3- neglection of nutrition for Vulnerable Groups and Equitable Access to nutrition Services: possibility: moderate, effect: high mitigation measures: - Analyze and disaggregate all data by sex, age, and disability. Analyze the needs of vulnerable and minority groups (such as women & children with disabilities), throughout the program cycle (assessment, design, implementation, and monitoring) with identification of risk factors and rights violations impacting service provision for beneficiaries. 4- Discrimination in service delivery: possibility: moderate, effect: high mitigation measures: - Selecting accessible and safe locations of health facilities.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Assumptions: SDH will coordinate closely with the Nutrition cluster, as well as local authorities (including the health directorate) and local NGOs operating inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, sending feedback to the cluster when interventions are needed to be addressed. Risk: an emergency status occur (increase the #COVID-19 cases "the second wave"). Mitigation: SDH will use its coordination channels for developing a coordinated response plan with the partners. Risk: The close of borders which prevent the shipments of supplies and medications to access to facilities. mitigation: Develop a strong sustainable strategy for accessing and deliver goods through local actors. •Assumptions: health facilities remains accessible for the SDH team and for the beneficiaries. Risk: The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities. mitigation: SDH through the S&S team has developed a strong risk management strategy for emergency situations. •Assumptions: currency exchange rates remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned activities. Risk: high volatility of exchange currency. mitigation: fixing the operational cost inside Syria in USD.
