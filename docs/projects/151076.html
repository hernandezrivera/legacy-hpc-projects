Project Summary

The proposed interventions are centrally located within the 2019 Protection Cluster Objectives 2 and 3. Responses to address the recent conflict, displacement, floods, and violence in Lower Shabelle districts are current priorities within the cluster. New Ways will respond to the needs of vulnerable populations including boys and girls from affected communities, IDPs and host communities in hard-to-reach areas in the region.
The project will conduct regular analysis of the situation of children about causes and consequences of child rights abuse and respond accordingly through prevention and establishment of proper protection system and services. The project is designed to empower the target communities to meet the challenges they face in the prevention and protection of children from violence and all forms of abuse. This project will map the existing services for appropriate referrals and coordination to ensure immediate response to the children with disabilities, neglected children, unaccompanied, and separated children at risk.
This project reinforces the protection element of providing assistance based on need and without discrimination and ensures children from vulnerable communities are not neglected or deprived from humanitarian assistance.
Close cooperation with other IP, local authorities, elders, and all relevant stakeholders will be also established and maintained along the project implementation. The intervention will target both urban and rural population to sensitise protection issues and negative cultural beliefs.
The project will focus on activities that meet the growing identified and perceived needs of children as outlined in the need, mainly focusing on improving the quality of response and prevention services as well as establishment of community-based child protection mechanism with meaningful child participation through capacity building and coaching of protection service providers, community members; women, youth, children, elders, social workers, caregivers and school teachers.
In order to reach more children with better protection from all kinds of violence and abuse, the project aims to build and strengthen child protection structures at community and district level, with the meaningful participation of children as a key component. On monthly basis, the project will bring together authorities, parents and children in a workshop to discuss violence against children and the other protection issues and their effects to their children and their communities, and encourage them to work together to stop all forms of child rights abuse and to protect their children to the best they can. Children are themselves actively engaged in identifying the risks and protection issues as well as proposing protection measures.
The project main objective is to establish and support community-based child protection method in the region and to improve quality of child protection response and access of services for children at risk of or experiencing abuse, abandonment, separation, neglect or exploitation.
The intervention starts with an extensive protection TOT training for 8 child protection advocates 4 male and 4 female. The CPAs will organize and facilitate all planned training events for the protection teams, community volunteers, children, duty bearers, elders and other stakeholders. Two protection teams will be deployed in each of the districts that will mobilise, support and form community protection committees. Organising and forming 16 effective child protection committees at IDP settlements in the target districts, which will be provided with capacity building opportunities and will be supported to design and implement an initiative that addresses their needs within their community. Organising 48 awareness sessions for the target communities. Empowering stakeholders including children and equipping them with the necessary skills of preventing, detecting, documenting, managing and responding to the protection issues. Organising 4 Group-based psychosocial support and life skills sessions for children. Training 80 community members, duty bearers and volunteers on child protection issues, empowering them identifying and supporting children with special psychosocial and child protection needs. Training 40 health-service providers on child protection and referral, enabling them to detect, document and refer protection cases and report protection-related cases. Conducting psychosocial support intervention training for parents, on how to support children in high stress and emergency situations, including displacement due to natural disaster and protracted conflicts in the region. Organising four Positive discipline training to school counsellors and community people to reduce harsh disciplining practices and identification and referral of distressed or maltreated children who need extra care to appropriate services through comprehensive case management service.
Conducting 4-stress management support and ‘positive parenting’ workshops to caregivers, community leaders and influencers to reduce harsh parenting practices and family conflict, and increase knowledge of social norms and harmful practices. Meanwhile 880 boys and girls including children with disabilities are planned to participate in the structured community-based psychosocial support activities. More 6800 individuals of will be targeted with rights-based public outreach and awareness raising activities to raise their levels of awareness. In addition to that 20 children with physical disability will be reached with protection-oriented direct assistance and construction of disabilities access in New Ways’ managing health centres in the four target districts.
The project strategy prioritizes 4 areas of work:
- Engaging communities carefully particularly traditional elders, women, youth, boys and girls by giving considerations of how different community groups and interests can be included and addressed while recognising of that communities are not homogenous, and neither are the interests of their subgroups.
- Contributing enhancement of the quality and access of child protection services through strong coordination with other on-going humanitarian/emergency projects in the region including New Ways’ projects, realising that protection is placed as mainstreaming in all projects.
- Increasing protection knowledge and information to the decision makers, women, youth and children.
- Understanding how and why violence occurs and to equip communities with tools to stop the violent behaviour before it starts, emphasis will be also placed on traditional custom law ‘XEER’ to respond to the children’s rights.
The project targets vulnerable children according to the humanitarian operations in Somalia defined vulnerability categories through direct support, empowerment, referral and advocacy. Among them are:
• Children from minority groups and IDPs.
• Unaccompanied boys and girls living alone (no foster family).
• Children from large family and Child headed household.
• Children with severe medical condition.
• Children from single female heads of households, especially those with multiple dependants.
• Children with a physical disability from birth, or resulting from illness, infection, injury, or trauma.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The prolonged conflict, and the above average GU’ rains in this year and floods are already worsening conditions in the IDP settlements and displacing more people. According to our field offices’ reports; all forms of child protection issues predominantly exist in the IDP settlements in the region. Women, children, persons with disabilities, and marginalized communities are impacted differently by the crisis and displacement, having distinct humanitarian needs and facing protection concerns.Sexual violence, early marriage, child labour, neglect and separated child, drug sniffing, children with disability and other protection issues are affecting the children from IDP communities. The project mitigation plan is to identify and map and document all the above mentioned protection issues. Then empower the displaced community through awareness and mobilising them to form protection committees that boys and girls play an active role in term of identifying the best protection strategies based on their context and in the decision making. Our protection committee will regularly support them with technical skills; documentation, advocacy, referral and case management.they will also help them to meet once a week to review and discuss their protection concerns.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

According the Somali context, men always dominate the interest of the communities. Children and young people have less chance to the decision making.This project will make sure that boys, girls, men, women, elderly people and children with disabilities actively participate all the planned project activities. Over all the project target beneficiaries are 12000 where 26% will be from the host communities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Among children and youth in the region are vulnerable to conflict, mainly in Marka, Qoryoley and Sablalele (Partially controlled by AS), there are indications that they are being radicalised and recruited into armed groups. Similar issues have arisen in response to inter-clan conflict, whereby many children directly or indirectly involve the armed conflicts. As there still going on sporadic clashes in some areas of the region, the project leaders will work hard to bring together local authorities, police, community protection committees, the CPAs, elders, religion leaders, school teachers and parents at the early stage of the project life for community-based awareness against violence.Stakeholders will come with prevention strategy. We work close with the traditional elders and the local authority for appropriate prevention.PCAs will document and report for any serious incident. New Ways will advocate and coordinate to the right part/responders including SWS army, AMISOM, the police and district authorities to minimise the risk of the armed conflict.armed conflict
