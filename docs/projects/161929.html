Project Summary

A lots of progress have been made in addressing malnutrition related issues caused by the Boko Haram insurgency as reported by recent surveys. But as the lean season gets to its peak, household foods stores are completely depleted, and purchasing power significantly stretched with consistent increase in food prices and lingering recessive national economic condition, vulnerable population are more at risk of becoming malnourished. There is high need for a nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive intervention in other to preserve and prevent the nutritional status of these vulnerable groups from deteriorating, otherwise all efforts made through the humanitarian response will be lost to obvious aggravating factors.
Therefore, CDHI main goal is to provide a live-saving intervention that will prevent the death of children due to malnutrition and contribute to reduction and prevention of malnutrition in communities affected by Boko Haram insurgencies.
Specific Objective:
• To improve access and utilization of nutrition and basic health services in primary health care facilities in accessible communities
• To improve the knowledge and practice of members of communities on good nutrition and key household practices
• Improve coordination and integration of nutrition specific intervention with other multi-sector nutrition sensitive interventions at the community level.

1 - Needs

About 1.8 million people are displaced and as a result many have lost their source of livelihood which has impacted household (HH) food insecurity putting women and children at increased risk of malnutrition. Productive assets are fast depleting among displaced HHs and members of the host community because of the needs to respond to shocks. In February 2017, the UN Secretary-General stressed the urgency of the food security situation in Nigeria – one of four countries in the world facing the threat of famine and calling on the international community to provide immediate assistance. Recent household economic approach HEA studies shows that IDPs are only able to meet up 30% of their food needs and depend on humanitarian actors for complementary food assistance for survival. Result from recent nutrition and food security surveillance in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa using SMART methodology shows that prevalence of global acute malnutrition GAM is between 5 – 10% which is at poor level according to WHO classification. And only less than 15% and 6% of HHs in Borno and Yobe are enrolled in food assistance program. Poor nutrition behaviour is presumed among both IDPs and members of the host communities as exclusive breastfeeding rate was at 20% and 37% in Bono and Yobe respectively. Similarly, the proportion of children achieving minimum dietary diversity and minimum meal frequency are both below 10% for Borno and Yobe. The conflict in NE Nigeria has caused displacement and human suffering on a massive scale, leaving 5.2 million foods insecure, 2.5 million malnourished, and 450,000 children under five with severe acute malnutrition.

2 - Activities

This is a community directed project and all stakeholders in the community will be involved throughout the program cycle from planning, to implementation and evaluation. Community directed projects requires that good community entry is achieved to gain maximum support from the community, which is a determining factor for the success of the project. All the details of the project shall be communicated to UNIICEF, IOM (CCCM) and community members for them to understand the objective, scope and benefit of the project, so as to be able to take informed decision about the project. Position mapping of all relevant stakeholders will be done to identify community focal point and ensure continual feedback or progress report from the community which should improve program outcome. Community mobilization and sensitization: Chabash Development and Health Initiative (CDHI) will conduct meetings with the local authorities, key stakeholders, other humanitarian actors (ACF, IMC, IRC, MSF, Save the Children) and communities in Borno states. CDHI will utilize the existing structure of community volunteers (CVs) (referred to as community leads in this project) provided by the existing humanitarian actors in the various communities to access and reach out to these vulnerable populations. In situations where CDHI confirms the existing structures of CVs is faulty, CDHI will work closely with the community leaders to select members of the community based on established criteria who are living in the community to be engaged in direct implementation of the program. Because community mobilization is a continuous process, CDHI will ensure that CVs continuously engage and sensitize members of the community on key household practices including hygiene promotion and good nutrition practice. Women in the community including grandmother, mothers of SAM/MAM children and women of child bearing age will be encouraged to form joint action-oriented Infant and Young Child Feeding IYCF support groups. The activities of this support groups will be facility by community leads supervised and supported by IYCF-trained health workers. Mothers with difficulties in IYCF practices will be properly counselled by the community leads and where necessary shall be referred to nearest health facility for skilled attention and counselling by health workers.Active Case Finding: Selected members of the community referred to as community leads will be trained by CDHI with support from UNICEF and state primary health care development agency SPHCDA on active case finding using MUAC tapes. Trained community leads will be subjected to a standardization test to ensure that those with precision and accuracy in MUAC data collection are utilized. CL with poor accuracy and precision from standardization test will be dropped out from the program implementation. Group of community leads will be guided and supervised by CDHI staff trained on CMAM and IYCF with experience in nutrition programming and who understand the dynamic context of NE intervention. Children who are identified to be severely acute malnourish SAM will be referred to the nearest out-patient therapeutic program OTP within the communities for treatment. Community leads shall monitor the progress of SAM treatment of children they referred, to ensure that default rate is mitigated. Home visits shall be provided to households of SAM children as a follow up to ensure adherence to treatment protocol at household level so as to achieve cure rate above SPHERE standard. Mothers of children identified with moderately acute malnutrition will be counsel or referred to HF IYCF corners where such services exist for counselling. Such mothers will also be referred to join an action oriented support group to improve her knowledge and practice of good nutrition.
