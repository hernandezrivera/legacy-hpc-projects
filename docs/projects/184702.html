Project Summary

Due to socio-economic inequalities exacerbated by multiple conflicts, famine, and widespread insecurity in Somalia, constant displacement and instability forces more than three million vulnerable boys and girls are out of school due to lack of an enabling learning environment. With one of the lowest primary school enrolment rates in the world, girls are at a greater risk of being deprived of their right to education due to lack of a positive environment for women, cultural biases towards boys, and the prevalence of GBV in form of psychological, economic and physical abuse for adolescent girls who have a higher school dropout rate. In lieu of proper water and sanitation facilities, adequate teachers, books, and classrooms the quality of education is prone to be poor. These hardships are worse for children with disabilities who also have to maneuver physical challenges in their pursuit of education. The right to education can be realized by improving these children’s access to quality learning and education-related services and facilities to allow both boys and girls between 5 to 18 years old, with special emphasis on the inclusion of children with disabilities and girls, to ensure increased gender-equal school completion.
Additionally, the impacts of COVID-19, almost 18 months since the first case was officially announced in Somalia, still expose children to higher risks of abuse and increased psychosocial distress as a result of confinements. The COVID-19 pandemic has indirect impacts on human capital formation through deteriorated educational outcomes as it affects enrolment, age-appropriate attendance and educational grade achievements. This impact will have long term effects on the delivery of educational services. The prolonged closure of schools was not only a loss of education but also many children’s main source of sustainable access to food, clean drinking water, social care and a safe haven for play and interaction.
A survey conducted by UNICEF indicated an increase in child labor with 12% of children engaged in forced labour without pay, while 35% of children experiencing violence at home. 20% of boys and girls faced sexual abuse and harassment and many vulnerable children still face challenges that work against their re-entry to school, including financial struggles. The secondary impact of COVID-19 on already impoverished families has been severe placing them at greater risk of deepening poverty and long-term negative impacts on their social and health situations.
This proposed intervention is designed based on critical education needs in Somalia. The program seeks to build on existing knowledge of the targeted communities and the capacity of ministry officials with the aim of achieving increased access and retention of out of school and children from vulnerable households. The proposed activities will focus on strengthening education in Banaadir, Galmudug and Hiraan Region in an attempt to help out of school children and those who are economically disadvantaged.
The project targets 5000 boys and girls comprising of 2500 girls and 2500 boys including 500 persons with disability. The project aims to ensure a safe and protective learning environment with good quality teaching and increase access and retention of crisis-affected and IDP children through an integrated package of school-based emergency assistance.
The project sought to enhance access and retention to education and will include establishment or rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces with gender-friendly and inclusive WASH facilities ((gender-sensitive latrines, water points, girl-friendly spaces), provision of school furniture, teacher incentives, and teacher trainings in pedagogical skills, handling special education needs children and psychosocial support, inclusive, protective and socio-emotional teaching and learning, as well as training of Community Education Committees in school management and school safety and preventing risks of exclusion

1. - Projects activities

1.1: Provision of teaching, learning and recreational materials to enhance the quality learning. 1.2: Teachers trained on basic pedagogical skills, psychosocial skills and psychosocial support First Aid, as well as knowledge of referral pathways that are tailored to the varying needs of crisis affected children, to address quality education, including safety and protection 1.3: Teachers supported with emergency incentives to improve participation and retention (include Female Teachers)1.4: Community Education Committees (CECs) trained on school governance, disaster management, child protection services, resource mobilization and management, and community mobilization to sensitize duty bearers, including community representatives and local authorities, on their roles and responsibilities to protect and support all children and ensure safe access to, from and at the schools 1.5: Provision of dignity kits for adolescent girls 1.6: Provision of safe drinking water, water storage facilities and hygiene promotion services in learning centers, to promote retention and reduce dropouts and absenteeism targeting 5,000 children 1.7: Provision of emergency supplementary school meals, to promote retention and reduce dropouts and absenteeism 1.8: Rehabilitation and/or construction of safe and protective temporary learning spaces (TLS) with gender responsive WASH facilities targeting 5,000 children1.9: Support the Ministry of Education (MoE) on system strengthening, including governance, management, coordination and monitoring of Education in Emergencies, as well as training on emergencies preparedness and response 1.10: Support Child to Child Clubs (CTCs) to acquire life skills, to ensure they engage in child-led advocacy and community outreach on hygiene promotion and child protection 1.11: Support children with disabilities access education services.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

HIWA will ensure community consultations and discussions take place during the assessment, planning, implementation and monitoring phases of the project. This will ensure the views and needs of the communities are taken into consideration, as the stakeholders themselves are best placed to find the most suitable solutions to address their own problems. Key stakeholders, such as Community Education Committee (CECs), community leaders, parents, teachers and all learners will be engaged, to raise awareness on child rights and influence behaviors and practices that enhance overall enabling learning environments for children. At the school level, CECs will support peer to peer support, rights awareness and will take part in schools' decision making, to ensure children's voices are heard. The project will be designed to address the specific needs of girls, in recognition of the specific challenges they face, including the provision of sanitary kits to adolescent girls to ensure regular school attendance. This will enhance girls’ access to learning opportunities. The community will be engaged, to ensure improved safety at and on the way to and from schools. In addition, the communities will be involved in the establishment of safe and violence-free environments with improved infrastructure, such as gender responsive learning and sanitation facilities. Teachers will be trained in psychosocial support and provide referral pathways for children in need of specialized child protection services, such as counselling and case management. Schools will identify a male and female teacher to be the child protection focal point of the school, who will support learners in need of protection support
