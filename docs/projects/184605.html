Project Summary

Decades of conflict, recurrent climate shocks, disease outbreaks and increasing poverty are devastating the people of Somalia. Despite progress in recent years, the compounding impacts of these shocks continue to erode coping strategies and undermine resilience against future crises
In 2021, the country faced heightened political tensions, at times associated violence, in the context of a delayed electoral process and power struggles at the leadership level. In southern and central Somalia, conflict and insecurity spiked, driving cycles of displacement, disruptions to livelihood activities, and constraints on trade and humanitarian access. Increased competition for natural resources and economic rents generated conflict at the local and sub-clan level.A total of 7.7 million Somali women, men and children are estimated to require humanitarian assistance in 2022. With 2.9 million people estimated to be internally displaced throughout the country, Somalia has one of the highest numbers of IDPs in the world. Of these, 2.2 million require urgent humanitarian assistance. IDPs are chronically more food insecure and vulnerable than host communities. Displacements are typically rural-urban in nature, where displaced people moving into urban centres often lack the skills required for urban livelihoods and IDPs and rural migrants are frequently confined to poor-paying unskilled jobs, if any at all. As with IDPs, their needs are driven by pre-existing vulnerabilities, as well as recurring multiple shocks. Poverty is pervasive, particularly in rural areas and areas where access remains a challenge, and the current estimates of 71 per cent of the population living below the poverty line are expected to remain at similar levels for 2022-23.
Bay ,Bakool and Hiran region in which HIDIG intends to provide life-saving food security to the affected population are among the severely affected regions where Bakool have a records of 41000 IDPs, 462,000 in Bay and 89000 in Hiran respectively additionally, Baidoa in Bay region have a total of 103,472 and 732 of people in needs and whose livelihood are in severe and stress respectively while on other hand Beletweyne in Hiran region records 52,448 and 166,084 people in stress and severe and likewise Elbarde district 1ith 15,265 and 33,916 in stress and severe livelihood. This couples with continuous drought displacement as well as restriction of movement specially among the rural livelihood in the three region have increased vulnerabilities among the already poor population in the regions. However, the three region have also suffered the impacts of COVID-19 and Desert Locust which continues to pose a serious risk of damage to both pasture and crops at least until the end of 2021. The 2021 Deyr (October-December) rainfall season is likely to be below average to average across the country, which could lead to drought and trigger a worsening of the humanitarian situation if the 2021 Gu (April-June) season rainfall is also delayed or performs poorly.
HIDIG project will therefore, intends to provide immediate access to unconditional and conditional cash assistance to 7,200 individuals most vulnerable women, girls, boys and men in IDPs and host communities, it will also provide capacity enhancement, farming tools as well as farm inputs to these target farmers in Beletweyne as well as agricultural seed inputs and tools to increase their farming production thus improving the nutrition of the target population in Bakol , Bay and Hiran region. The project will provide cash for work to enable provide immediate access to 720 household income thus improving the overall well-being of the target communities. The project will ensure inclusion of poor women, girls including disable population thus ensuring protection of the vulnerable household in the target location.HIDIG will ensure to coordinates the foods security cluster and regional administration in the target district

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Due to the triple affected of locust infestation, flooding and global COVID-19 pandemic many vulnerable households in Bay, Bakool and Hiran region have been widely affected and could not afford the basic needs. This patterns, and the power balance within the families. Elderly people have also been affected due to lack of essential food and medicines and reduced availability of care. Therefore, the project will particularly target these vulnerable groups, which includes women, children, elderly persons and ethnic minority groups in the target regions in South central Somalia, people with disability will be considered in the inclusion of the project beneficiaries to enable benefits from lifesaving assistance to protect them from further harm and refraining them from adopting negative coping mechanisms. Direct beneficiary’s selection will be ‘inclusive’ and based on the consultation with communities by the respective local administration following the humanitarian principles. A proposed list of beneficiaries (from community consultations) will be validated by the project in a door-to-door beneficiary verification exercise. Beneficiary selection shall not be restricted by documentation provided available with the beneficiaries and solely the selection criteria shall be followed. Local authorities and relevant government departments will be consulted for final selection of beneficiaries at the targeted areas. Additionally, affected communities and local government authorities will be engaged in identification of the beneficiaries most in need. The selected beneficiaries will be registered and provided with unique cards for future verification and monitoring purposes. The cash will be transferred through mobile company/financial service provider which will be selected based on availability of network and/or services, readiness and ability to provide a prompt delivery, and reasonable service charge, thus HIDIG organization propose an inclusive assistance through provision of unconditional cash assistance and conditional cash grant is the most appropriate intervention at this immediate response stage and will allow beneficiaries to priorities their most pressing need

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

This project targets areas with high concentration of IDPs including Baidoa and Beletweyne and pastoralist in Ceelbarde and Rabdhure therefore possible community tensions between IDPs and host communities is a risk for displaced populations who may face animosity from host communities who already feel overburdened and with limited services. With the IDPs living in camps, many of them not legalized, constructing permanent structure or own a land for food security and livelihood center may be a challenge exposing them to lack of comprehensive and timely care. To overcome these challenges, HIDIG will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive access to services within the IDP camps by establishing cordial relationship between them and with the host communities and local authorities. Based on the food security and livelihood Cluster Guideline on inclusion, both displaced and host communities will be consulted about needs and assess whether education interventions could cause tension, harassment or conﬂict between the IDPs and host communities. Lack of desegregated data impedes well planned interventions based on needs. HIDIG intends to overcome this by having a need based assessment and disaggregated data so that the special needs of IDPs beneﬁciaries and those with special gender needs are taken care of. Due to poor health and food security and livelihood infrastructure, many centers do not have proper planning so that those with disabilities can easily seek services. However, HIDIG will ensure services provided reach the most vulnerable IDPs and pastoralist in the far areas through identiﬁcation and prioritization so that discrimination and exclusion is avoided. Location, distance and access routes to the cash collection sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement on better livelihood. Building community structures including local temporary spaces will have provided in consultation with community/member who owns the land to ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conﬂict and violence. Communities ﬂeeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate food security and livelihood services without any discrimination. Where IDPs are involved, sites that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence
