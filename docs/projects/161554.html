Project Summary

The proposed intervention offers a quick response tailored to the returnees, IDPs, and host communities’ safety in raising their awareness of the threat from Explosive Ordnance (EO) and to promote safe behaviour among the large number of civilians at risk particularly the children in the shortest possible time. Further, the action will undertake Non-Technical Survey (NTS) to identify and report confirmed or suspected hazardous areas to enable safe freedom of movement, early recovery activities and expansion of humanitarian operations. In addition, the project will support in building the capacity of the Police EOD personnel in Northeast Nigeria to enable them dispose explosive ordnance (EO) to prevent harm to civilians and to remove obstructions to securing livelihoods needed for recovery from conflict.
The proposed project will have three integrated team, namely: Explosive Ordnance Risk Education, Non-Technical Survey, and Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD). All the team will closely work with the relevant stakeholders to (a) promote safe behavior; (b) identify and report confirmed or suspected hazardous areas; and (c)build the capacity of the Nigerian security force to dispose dangerous items. As such, the proposed intervention will provide multiplier effect as it ensures returnees, IDPs and host communities’ are provided with the knowledge to act safely in the event of encountering explosive hazards in their environment, determine the prioritization or future intervention and clearance operations, and build the capacity of the government to dispose Explosive Ordnance (EO). Hence, the action will create a secure environment for vulnerable people for development, releasing contaminated land for productive use, and by removing blockages for humanitarian and development assistance to take place. Hence, mine action is key not only for saving lives but as a precursor for peacebuilding, stabilization and sustainable development. As such, the proposed intervention a key contributing element of the nexus between peace and security and development.

1 - Needs

Nigeria’s North East has been experiencing a significant humanitarian crisis since 2009, which has resulted from the on-going armed conflict between Armed Opposition Groups (AOG), Nigerian Security Forces and the Multi-National Joint Task Force (MNJTF). A decade long conflict has left north-east Nigeria littered with dangerous contamination from a wide range of Explosive Ordnance such as : rocket-propelled grenades, artillery shells, mortar Bombs , air-dropped weapons, and grenades that did not explode or have been abandoned. Although the full scope and scale of contamination is unknown, it is assessed to be considerable. Reports indicate that as much as 20 percent of munitions that were fired did not explode on impact, resulting in high volumes of unexploded ordnance contaminating communities. The increasing hostilities related to the Chad Basin Campaign since last year, have contribute to the rise of the direct risk of accidents with Explosive Ordnance, especially to IDP population in BAY (Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe) states. An undetermined scale of contamination from a wide range of ammunition that have been fired, used, launched and propelled but not exploded plus others abandoned during this campaign remain in the ground as a risk for host communities, IDPs and returnees. Only in 2019, at least 03 accidents related to EO have been report in BAY States. In addition to the direct risk to the population and the impact on humanitarian, early recovery, and livelihood activities, the presence of Explosive Ordinance is providing a readily available source of explosives to construct improvised explosive devices (IEDs), which compounds the threat to communities. There have been numerous reports on the presence of different types of Explosive Ordnance (EO), which have been verified and unverified. DRC/DDG conducted a mine action assessment in 2015 on which different types of ERW, seen by IDPs in various areas of Borno state, have been mentioned. Subsequently, in 2016 the United Nations Mine Action Service (UNMAS) conducted a scoping mission in northeast Nigeria, which further confirmed the presence of EO in populated areas. While the exact nature of the items was not confirmed, various reports of pressure-plate, victim-activated Improvised Explosive Devices (IED) which function like landmines have been collected by UNMAS, INSO, and DRC/DDG teams on the ground through their interaction with IDPs, returnees, and host community members. There are also numerous reports of unexploded ordnance (UXO) found in populated areas, on farming land and/or on roads, namely in Konduga, Mafa, Ngala, Bama, Dikwa and Gwoza (Borno). The presence of EO represents a serious protection issue, as IDPs, returnees and government workers involved in early recovery and rehabilitation work (e.g. roads, schools, agricultural support, water provision, etc.) are at risk of being injured or killed by explosive hazards.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention will have three broad activities as described below.1. Risk Education: Based on the delivery of risk education in all three north-eastern states, DRC/DDG has learnt the following key lessons which will continue to inform the design and delivery of its future mine action programming in the region:• Continue to collaborate and share information with the military, and national and state authorities, e.g. NEMA and SEMA, as keeping them informed of DDG activities helps to facilitate access and acceptance.• Before initiating the implementation of activities, it is essential that key stakeholders, for example camp managers, are identified and their authorization is obtained in order to facilitate access and acceptance.• When delivering RE in host communities, target three homes at a time in order to keep numbers manageable, i.e. from 20-30 people, and avoid drawing larger crowds, which could pose security risks to the DRC/DDG team members.The design and delivery of DDG’s risk education programme is also based on data collected through Knowledge, Attitude, Practice (KAP) surveys conducted in Yobe (August 2016), Borno and Adamawa states (March 2017), on adults and children’s knowledge on mines/ERW, risk-taking behaviours and beliefs as well as the most at-risk groups. The level of effectiveness of RE activities will be measured against these baseline surveys. More importantly, target will be divided a 50:50 Of between adults (aged 18+) and children (aged 6-17) as adults make up the majority of the ERW related casualties. Further, more females will be targeted than men given knowledge gap is almost always slightly higher for males than females according to KAP surveys. Under the intervention, the following key activities will be implemented: • Risk Education Sessions (IDPs/Returnees/Host Communities)• Risk Education Sessions (INGOs/NGOs/Local Government Authorities)• Emergency Response Risk Education Team• Public Service Announcements• Risk Education Messages through Drama 2. Non-Technical SurveyDRC/DDG Nigeria will utilize its current structure, specifically its GIS specialist to identify areas of intervention that need to be targeted. This will be done using DDG Nigeria’s current database of EO reports, Casualty Reports and will continue to be updated by the EO Risk Education Teams deployed daily. It will also incorporate an open source reporting, knowledge gained from local security providers and other INGOs and UN organizations. DDG Nigeria will keep the engagement with Federal and state level key stakeholder’s during the execution of the strategy. The surveys will then be conducted, and information mapped and recorded in the IMSMA database. The proposed intervention will have the following key activities.• Desk Assessment • Planning the intervention• Deployment of Teams and CL Strategy• Identification of Threats• Marking Areas • Sharing findings with Communities/Authorities• Reporting and Prioritization• Monitoring of NTSCurrently there are no permissions in place for EOD activity except by Nigerian Security Forces, this is likely to change as the dynamics of the conflict changes and the knowledge gained through Non-Technical Surveys will assist in the safe return of IDPs to their host communities. 3. Explosive Ordnance Disposal TrainingDDG will enhance the Police EOD capacity through provision of training as per the existing international standards to reduce the protection risks associated with the Explosive Ordnance (EO) in North East of Nigeria. To this effect, DDG will make use of some special equipment such as the 3D printed Inert Explosive Ordnance (EO) and explosive also known as Advanced Ordnance Teaching Materials (AOTM).This activity also includes the mentoring phase after each EOD training where an EOD specialist will reinforce the attitudes, skills and knowledge of the Nigerian Police EOD personnel during the live demolition of the UXOs reported by the NTS Teams.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC/DDG will use an inclusive method during the need assessment where at risk communities are involved. A participatory approach shall be employed to generate interest and ownership at the community level from the beginning of the project. Further, the needs assessment shall address the different needs, vulnerabilities and expectations of various groups and shall be sensitive to culture, gender, and age. A review of existing community social networks, key community opinion leaders (both male and female) and local development committees shall be included in the needs assessment. The information collected through participatory methods shall be validated by the different segment of the community members. In addition, regular meetings will be held with different segment of the community to get feedback throughout the life of the project. These meetings sometime require to hold separate meetings for women and girls due to cultural sensitivities.
