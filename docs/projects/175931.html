Project Summary

It is undoubtedly clear that conflict, which has existed in the cultures of most South Sudanese ethnic groups, coupled with natural disaster such as floods and famine have continued to undermine the socio-economic development of the country. Additionally, the global crisis on COVID-19 has put immense burden on the needs of South Sudanese across the country. Thus, Nile Hope is responding to the flood intervention in Akobo, Bor South, Canal/Pigi, Duk, Fangak, Nyirol, Pibor and Pochalla Counties of Jonglei State, Nasir and Maiwut Counties of Upper Nile State.
Nile Hope aims at improving the living conditions of the affected populations by providing safe and appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items and cash assistance to 28,000 individuals (5,600 men, 7,840 women, 7,000 boys and 7,560 girls) of IDPs, returnees and the most vulnerable members of the host communities (14,000 IDPs, 5,600 returnees and 8,400 host communities). This number translates into 4,666 HH (373HH in Akobo, 467HH in Bor South, 373HH in Canal/Pigi, 700 HH in Duk, 420 HH in Fangak, 420HH in Nyirol, 933HH in Pibor, 233HH in Pochalla, 467HH in Nasir and 280HH in Maiwut).
The project will identify, register, verify and distribute both in-kind and cash assistance to the beneficiaries of the targeted locations as well as monitor the movements of both the IDPs and returnees at the project locations. In addition to this, the project will conduct need analysis in all targeted locations as well as conduct PSEA/GBV awareness campaigns in collaboration with our GBV/Protection department, to ensure that any issues of sexual exploitation are eliminated. Furthermore, the project will conduct periodic stakeholders’/community representatives’ workshops in all the targeted locations for enhanced community engagements, transparency, and accountability to the affected populations. Likewise, the intervention will train 6 enumerators from each county on the distribution processes in accordance with the cluster’s guidelines and requirements. The project will also conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) in all the ten counties. Moreover, when PDM is not possible, a rapid monitoring right after the distribution is completed will be conducted.
Additionally, the intervention will support the construction of 100 emergency shelters (10 in each county), specifically for displaced families, which are headed by the disabled, the elderly and children under 18 years. Nile Hope will not only raise awareness campaigns on and adherence to due diligence guidelines
and the do no harm principle, but will also provide widespread education and training to ensure all parties are aware of their land rights as guaranteed by the national law. Additionally, Nile Hope will enhance capacities of national and sub-national land authorities on land records management, document verification and issuance, the application of relevant laws and statutes. Moreover, there will be comprehensive training in collaborative/alternative dispute resolution (CDR/ADR) methodologies, to provide community members with tools to settle disputes in a transparent, equitable and accessible manner, without reliance on overburdened and inaccessible court systems.
The beneficiaries will be responsible for the maintenance, renovation and/or repair of the houses once constructed and handed over to them. Lastly, Nile Hope provide cash assistance to families with business ideas and skills as a settlement startup kit, adopting to existing cluster CBI modalities in the locations listed due to their existing market access.
The project equally hopes to use its existing M&E tools to monitor, report, feedback, document, and share evident lessons. Similarly, the project will develop a feedback/ complain mechanism system, which will include suggestion boxes, quarterly briefs forums with the stakeholders and reporting desks in specific public locations.

1 - Needs

As per UN-OCHA, report (2020) more than 1.6 million people are internally displaced and 2.2 million are refugees within the country. This number was caused in the rise of sub-national violence, flooding and COVID-19 pandemic. Concerning flooding, Jonglei, Upper Nile and some parts of Lakes States were the most affected. Over 600,000 people have been affected by the flooding in areas along the White Nile since July 2020 (OCHA, 2020). Jonglei State and Greater Pibor Administrative Area were the worst affected by the floods, with over 495,000 people affected since July 2020 (OCHA, 6 Nov 2020). For instance, in Bor South, Twic East, Duk and Ayod counties, more than 200,000 people have been displaced by flooding and conflict (OCHA, 3 Sept 2020). Approximately 1,800 households have been displaced in Bor South, and crops have been submerged in Duk. In addition to this, a number of families were reported displaced throughout Pochalla County of Pibor state (OCHA, 3 Sep 2020). Consequently, a consecutive year of flood-induced crop and livestock losses in low-lying areas along the Nile River and Pibor-Akobo-Sobat basin would exacerbate existing food insecurity into the post-harvest period (FEWSNET, 4 Aug 2020).The continued long history of conflict and underdevelopment dating back to 2011 have not only resulted in mass displacement and destroyed livelihoods but have also hindered humanitarian assistance. Many areas of the country have continued to witness national, sub-national, localized and grassroots violence mostly driven by resource scarcity in areas that have continuously experienced years of severe food insecurity. Severe flooding has caused temporary displacement and disruption to more than 1 million individuals in 2020 (OCHA). In the first half of 2020, there was 232,000 new displacements associated with conflict and violence and 23,000 because of flooding (DTM, 2020). Consequently, of these shocks, 8.3 million people were classified as “in need” as January 2021, an increment from the 7.5 million people in need in 2020 (DTM). 442,682 IDPs were reported to have moved across nine states between May and December 2020 due to flooding. Jonglei state witnessed 37% of its population being displaced by the floods, whilst, Upper Nile state experienced 3% population displacement (DTM, 2020). On one hand, 56% of the IDPs were displaced to locations within the same payam, while 5% moved to another payam within the same county. On the other hand, 29% of the IDPs moved to another county and 9% to a different state. With 2.1 million being displaced (inclusive of IDPs, returnees and host communities), social cohesion is under threat thus making peace co-existence between the displaced, host and returning populations. These populations have also continued to experience challenges disease burden mainly from malaria, diarrhea and acute respiratory infections. Therefore, through this project, Nile Hope seeks to respond to the provision to access safe and appropriate emergency shelter, life-saving NFIs and cash assistance to the affected populations of returnees IDPs and vulnerable members of the host communities, who live in areas razed by flooding and conflict, where they often resort to living under trees. Therefore, the overall objective of this project is to provide access to life-saving assistance and cash assistance to 28,000 IDPs, the returnees and the most vulnerable members of the host communities. The targeted locations include Akobo, Bor South, Canal/Pigi, Duk, Fangak, Nyirol, Pibor (under severity 5 and catastrophic situation) and Pochalla Counties of Jonglei State, Nasir and Maiwut Counties of Upper Nile State.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Nile Hope staff will be required to sign a code of conduct that will guide them in the project implementation and. This code of conduct will also stipulate on the guidelines and measures of how to deter and handle sexual exploitation cases and abuse. The staff will also be trained on PSEA principles and through the staff and community workers; the beneficiaries will be sensitized on their rights and the existence of the policy to protect them from sexual exploitation and abuse

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The ES/NFI staff will be encouraged to ask the vulnerable members of the community to feel free and discuss the issues affecting them with the people they trust the most. The vulnerable members will be urged to seek assistance to write and drop their complaints anonymously in the suggestion boxes provided. This project will ensure that the vulnerable members play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will develop a feedback/ complain mechanism system, which will include suggestion boxes, quarterly briefs forums with the stakeholders and reporting desks in specific locations. Through stakeholders and community representatives, feedback from the community will be received and acted upon. Details of the project will continuously be disseminated during community meetings so that all people are in the know about what the projects seek to achieve

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The project will actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with any breaches of guidelines and stakeholders’ dissatisfaction. In situation where the feedback does not result in significant change of the project design, internal deliberations including with the respective donor(s) will take place and agreed actions implemented. Feedback that require support beyond the project scope will be referred to appropriate entities for action.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project will work closely with the authorities to monitor the security situation and where applicable, suspend services if it cannot guarantee the safety of staff and beneficiaries. Additionally, Nile Hope staff, stakeholders and community representatives will be expected to sign commitments that they will never try to use their positions of influence to take advantage of the women and children served in the project. Furthermore, Nile Hope will prioritize the safety, health, security, privacy and dignity of its beneficiaries through careful assessment and analysis that incorporates conflict sensitive approaches, and also ensure not to exacerbate existing tensions that might lead to negative outcomes such as increased exposure of women to GBV risks, deprivation or coercion. Nile Hope will also work with national and sub-national coordination teams to ensure an effective and coordinated response. Lastly, Nile Hope will use effective monitoring and feedback of the response using the national cluster Rapid Monitoring Tool and PDM Toolkits to evaluate the cycle of the response.
