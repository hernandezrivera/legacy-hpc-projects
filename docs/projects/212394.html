Project Summary

Nearly 60% of children in the camp do not receive education, and gaps in nutrition and healthcare services, including mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), remain pervasive. In response to these needs, in 2023 the NGO began implementing this project to support Girls and boys aged 3-18 to safe access to quality, inclusive and safe education services including non-formal education for displaced and marginalized children, with a focus on an integrated protection and education response. The proposed program will provide access to quality education in the annex area for 1,260 children (630 girls and 630 boys) who are out-of-school by providing them with safe, quality and inclusive non-formal education services, targeting both pre-school children and those in basic education, including children with disabilities, the project education objective will reached out through receiving children aged (3-5 Y) in its Early Childhood Care and Development (ECCD) center and a Temporary Learning Space (TLS) for non-formal education (NFE) (for ages 6-14) in adherence to SPHERE standards. the children displaying low competency levels will will provided with Basic Literacy and Numeracy (BLN) classes in the TLSs, this project will also provide basic education support for children using UNICEF’s Self Learning Program (SLP). Ready to Learn curriculum and Emergent Literacy and Math (ELM) will be provided for pre-school children aged 3-5 and will also include targeted support for parents/caregivers through ELM at home for caregivers, the NGO’s education support also integrates academic skills with social emotional learning (SEL), recognizing the interconnectedness of cognitive and social-emotional development. Facilitators will be provided with the required materials, and children will receive regular SEL sessions.
In addition, this project will enhance the safety, well-being, and resilience of conflict-affected girls, boys, and their families, the Child Protection (CP) component of the project will provide integrated protection programming. Child Friendly Spaces (CFSs) will be established to provide safe, accessible, and inclusive places for 800 children aged 6 to 17 to socialize, learn, develop, and be protected, in response to the gap in such spaces. The project will focus on providing age, gender and context-adapted life skills and psychosocial wellbeing support through structured community and family support, MHPSS programs and culture-sensitive sports activities that are tailored to the participants' age and gender, additionally this project will provide comprehensive case management for the most marginalized children in Alhol camp, through this responses the cases will provided with emergency case funds: in-kind emergency support for the most serious cases and/or to remedy an urgent need arising from exposure to a protection incident.
Combined, the education and protection intervention will fill critical gaps in Al Hol camp and ensure that children have access to safe spaces where they can learn.
Protection procedures:
The NGO prioritizes the Centrality of Protection and is implementing a conflict sensitivity study and developing subsequent action plan. all staff members trained on relevant protection topics and is expanding capacity building of staff in conflict sensitivity, protection analysis, and participatory approaches. The project conducts a child safeguarding risk assessment, gender analysis, safety audit, and conflict sensitivity analysis, identifying gaps, informing project design, and mitigating protection risks for children and their families while accessing project services. Contingency and emergency preparedness planning is undertaken to prepare for potential changes in context, with a particular focus on the impacts on CM and alternative care. This planning is conducted in accordance with robust child safeguarding policy and adherence to ‘do no harm’ principles.
