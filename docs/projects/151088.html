Project Summary

This project aims at providing access to education for marginalized children from IDP communities, children with disability and vulnerable host community children who face challenges in accessing school. Through sustained education campaigns that will also advocate for girls and children with disability , out of school children will be identified and enrolled in school. With the proposed infrastructure support that includes rehabilitation and equipment of classrooms, construction of gender segregated WASH facilities and hygiene campaigns, supply of learning kits and school materials, provision of school feeding and clean water to IDP learners, the project purposes to remove key costly barriers to education for these marginalized children,to provide a conducive learning environment that enables them to attend and progress in school. Girls will be specifically targeted with menstrual hygiene education as part of lifeskills and provided with dignity kits to mitigate any absenteeism due to their periods.As part of durable solutions and resilience building, the IDP schools will linked to public schools for transition of learners,and to promote integration of IDP learners into the national education system. Using an integrated child sensitive and gender responsive approach that puts child protection and psycho social support at the center of activities, the project aims to build the resilience of the learners by providing them with key lifeskills information for their personnel well being and social interaction. Further, the project goes beyond access to also incorporate quality components that ensure meaningful and positive learning outcomes out of the learning process. Teachers will be trained on basic pedagogy skills, learner testing and participatory classroom instruction for effective learning,and the teachers code of conduct enforced in all target schools. The teachers will also be closely supervised and mentored to improve their practice, and also assisted to establish structured student testing, based on the national curriculum timelines to inform students learning. The CEC's will be trained on their roles and responsibilities, including child protection principles and standards in emergencies, because of changes in the membership that could have arisen in the last year (2017)while follow up support will be given to the CEC's in the target schools in Wajid, as they have already been trained. The MoE and respective district education stakeholders will also be trained on disaster risk management and response and education coordination during emergency aimed at ensuring effective and coordinated education response that minimizes disruption to learning during crises. Schools will also establish children support centers and clear referral pathways for learners facing protection issues. INTERSOS and the Ministry of Education in the respective district will conduct joint school monitoring to determine the quality of the teaching and learning process and provide feedback to the teachers and CEC's on areas of improvement. The project will also use its internal monitoring structures to track the quality and progress of activity implementation, ,as well as using the cluster based monthly 4W matrix. School structures and the ministry of education will also be mobilized to sustain the schools upon project phase out.

Needs

Somalia has faced protracted conflict and natural disasters, negatively impacting education and raising critical protection challenges for children and families. The government has limited capacity and weak governance systems to mitigate education and protection in crisis situations. The Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP 2018) indicates an estimated 3.4 million school age children are out of school, with only 30% attending school (HNO 2018). Inequalities in children’s education access and progressions, gender disparities, untrained teachers, lack of teaching and learning materials, an estimated 1 million IDP school age children and a fragile school system mark the education sector. Further, the proposed Regions -Bay and Bakool, in South Central Somalia, continue to register poor education indicators (Somalia Education Cluster indicators 2018),due to lack of external/humanitarian support. These indicators are similar to the rapid internal (INTERSOS 2018) survey, whereby Wajid district, with an estimated population of 69,694 (17,424 M, 52,270 F), (District Commissioner 2018),has only 1,378 (806 boys,572 girls) enrolled in 5 schools. With an estimated 12.9% of this population being aged 10-14 years ,(www.populationpyramid.net/somalia-2017),this translates to an estimated 8,990 children out of school. This poor trend is replicated in Dinsor district which has only 1689 (572girls, 1117b) learners enrolled in 2 Public schools which are the only functional schools in the entire district- which also has no secondary school. Both Baidoa and Wajid continue to receive new IDP's periodically with new IDP’s sites in Baidoa currently standing at 377 (CCCM 2018). The need therefore to expand the infrastructure in the public schools in both Wajid and Baidoa to absorb more IDP learners is critical for integration. Further advocacy on safe hygiene and sanitation practices, Child rights and child protection is also needed coupled with teacher and CEC mentorship, and supervision.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

This project proposes to support IDP's children within the Inter Agency Standing Committee (IASC) framework and principles on durable solutions for internally displaced populations, which recommends that IDP's should enjoy without discrimination, a minimum access to basic education among other rights, also as part of building their resilience in the face of the challenges they face. In this regard,this project aims at supporting IDP children not only access school,but also transit to public schools that are in the host communities to benefit from quality education within the national education system Therefore each IDP school will not only be equipped with the necessary learning resources based on the national curriculum,but also linked to a public school in all the target districts, where the children will transit to upper grades to complete their education. However where IDP camps are near public school,learners will be enrolled directly in the public schools. With these collaboration (of IDP and public schools),learners will transit and integrate with host communities with ease,teachers in the IDP schools will improve their capacity through exposure and interactions with their more experienced public school teachers on various academic and non-academic issues to improve learning. Additionally, all the schools will use the national curriculum for uniformity. This will ensure that the IDP children are not marginalized from the mainstream education system and are able to easily transit and fit in the public school system. Incorporation of WASH, gender, child rights and protection, safe hygiene and sanitation practices like washing hands after toilet use, among other lifesaving information and life skills education will enable the learners and communities to build their resilience during disease outbreak and/or crises. Teachers and volunteers in the IDP schools will be mentored by the more experienced teachers in the public schools under the teacher mentorship initiative, besides pedagogical training's that ensure quality learning is sustained after project phase out. Additionally, the training of CEC’s, education stakeholders and district disaster committees on emergency preparedness and response will ensure that that schools are resilient and have the knowledge and skills to mitigate crises and ensure coordinated and minimal learning disruption in schools

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The 2018 Somalia Humanitarian Response Plan estimates the total IDP population at 2.6 million (February 2018) ,with an estimated 1.5 IDP's displaced due to conflict . Most of these families move from rural areas to urban areas in search of safety,livelihoods and humanitarian assistance,where they settle in IDP camps with limited local acceptance ,primarily due to social background .These families face the trauma of rejection and exclusion in accessing their basic needs and rights, and therefore are unable to resume a normal life (IASC-2017). Here the risk of exclusion from community engagement, participation and empowerment towards self-determination is high. For instance,IDP's children risk exclusion from mainstream public schools where education affordable or free for the host communities. In situations where schools charge levies,the IDP families are unable to pay these levies as they do not have any sustainable income. They are left to provide cheap labour like working on the farms of the host community families or running petty errands for them for meager income. Their children more often,are forced into child labour to supplement household income to meet basic needs. IDP families also risk exclusion from participation in key decisions that affect them. and are unable to access basic amenities like health facilities,schools and markets. Other risks include exclusion from justice,decision making,and governance. INTERSOS will mitigate exclusion from education for IDP children,by supporting schools within the IDP camps. IDP children will be given resilience lifeskills for their well being and IDP schools linked to Public schools to ensure they progress to upper grades and complete school. In the public schools they will intergrate with host communities. INTERSOS will work closely with the CCCM cluster -Somalia by providing information and data that will improve the response of various agencies to the living conditions and access to services of the IDP families

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

IDP families face multiple risks of exclusion and marginalization based on gender and age. This especially affects women,children and the elderly. Women face exclusion in key decision making that affects them and their children's due to the patriarchal society that characterizes,where men are the key /primary decision makers at household and community level. For example,its the men who make decisions on where to migrate to, children's' education and household income. In situations where the women contribute to household income,its the men who decide how to use the income. In schools,women are excluded in the CEC composition. Most CEC's according to INTERSOS internal monitoring findings,reflect 30% and less women representation.Women also shy away from taking up these roles due to socialization. The elderly on the other hand face exclusion as they are seen as burdensome since they do not contribute to income, are left unattended for long periods .Girls also face the risk of exclusion in accessing education as families preference goes into investing in boys rather than girls (INTERSOS 2018). Therefore when it comes to sending children to school,boys are given preference to girls, who remain at home to provide labour in preparation for their marriage. In mitigating these risks ,INTERSOS through this project will coordinate with the CCCM cluster initiatives to incorporating women in the camp management. Additionally,INTERSOS will collaborate with CEC's, and camp leaders to ensure each CEC's has atleast 50% women representation. Through enrollment drives and education campaigns,INTERSOS will advocate for the equal rights of the girl-child ,including access to education and information. Priority for registration of out of school children will be for the girls. All school clubs will have equal representation of both boys and girls to empower girls. Further,through lifeskills,girls will gain knowledge and skills on how to be more resilient,confident and focused.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Communities in Somalia continue to grapple with impact of armed conflict and violence that has caused displacement of over 1.5 million persons.The education sector totally collapsed in Somalia due to the protracted conflict, and efforts to revive it ,by various actors including humanitarian,development and the government continues to face multiple challenges . First, the governments efforts to harmonize and nationalize the education system to protect learners from materials and information that may cause them to be violent,especially the A.S curriculum has faced barrier by A.S that is totally opposed to this system. Further,the government has weak education systems and structures to enforce the national system,and protect schools and learners/teachers in conflict areas like Middle Juba has faced challenges too . Schools,have been closed, taken over by the army,or A.S as their residence. Land mines or bombs have been planted on the way to and/or from school. On the other hand,supporting schools in the IDP camps continues to face challenges of sustainability. Teachers and education personnel lack skills to mitigate the conflict . In school,violence among children from various clans though rare has also been reported. To mitigate the above,INTERSOS ,using internal security mechanisms,will work closely with the district and UN security personnel to establish the security situation of potential response areas to inform any mitigation plans.. In the communities by supporting IDP schools,INTERSOS will have reduced the distance to public schools and the risk of children being abducted or harmed,or stepping on land mines/explosives en route to school. Additionally CEC's and teachers will be trained on Child protection and Psycho social support to be able to identify children at risk or facing trauma as a result of the conflict or violence.District and MoE will be trained on disaster risk reduction and education coordination in crises to reduce the interruption of learning
