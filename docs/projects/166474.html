Project Summary

COVID-19 has become a major pandemic. In a span of just 13 weeks from January to end of March 2020, the virus has progressed from a discrete outbreak in Wuhan, China, to clusters of cases in many countries, and then to a pandemic with most countries reporting cases, and many experiencing significant outbreaks. Thus far, overall fatality rates have been higher than 4.8 per cent, and considerably higher for older people and those with underlying health conditions. The virus is also affecting the health of younger adults.
On 11th March 2020, WHO declared the COVID-19 PHEIC event as a pandemic due to the number of cases of COVID-19 outside China increasing in 13-fold, and the number of affected countries tripling. There are now more than 750,000 cases in 202 countries, areas and territories, with 36,571 deaths. Many more patients are still fighting for their lives in hospitals. The Africa continent has had the effects of the outbreak. All the index cases in the Africa region were imported from the affected countries in Europe and Asia. As at March 30, 2020, there have been 3,486 confirmed cases in Africa with 60 deaths. Nigeria has also recorded a total of 139 confirmed cases with 2 fatalities.
The crisis in North-east Nigeria has evolved over 10 years leading to unpredictable continuous displacements. Since November 2018, there has been an upsurge of civilian displacements in Nigeria’s North-east. The impact of the displacement for communities and persons displaced is huge and has remained key to increase in disease situation with consequences. Borno recently experienced an outbreak of measles with over 21,052 cases as at the end of December 2019. This was due to non-immunization amongst displaced children from locations that are security-compromised, which further puts at high risk children under 5 years already facing dangerously high levels of malnutrition, endemic malaria and other epidemic prone diseases. In 2018, there were extended cholera outbreaks across the BAY states (6,230 in Borno; 2,498 in Adamawa and 2,108 in Yobe). In 2019, only Adamawa experienced Cholera outbreak with 839 cases and CFR of 0.48% as of 6th December 2019. Borno and Adamawa have also recently experienced Lassa fever outbreaks recording 3 and 4 positive cases respectively with more than 300 people on regular monitoring in quarantine.
The deterioration of the security situation in the past months has exacerbated the situation especially for displaced women, children and the elderly being the most vulnerable. Overall, in 2020, around 7.1 million people need humanitarian support including health services. Borno is the worst affected by the insurgency among the three North-east states and contribute 83% of the internally displaced persons as at 2019. The humanitarian community continues to struggle to respond to urgent humanitarian needs with the already overstretched available capacity. The response capacity to a crisis of this scale is not commensurate to the needs of the affected population especially with the new influx and forced displacement of communities.
The pandemic is adding to the burden of endemic infectious diseases prevalent in North-east Nigeria, such as cholera, measles, and malaria. Pre-existing poor hygiene practices, poor coverage in water and sanitation services and overcrowded living conditions especially in IDP camps also augment the incidence and spread of contamination by the virus.
This project is aimed at promoting risk awareness and healthy practices among the vulnerable population of the BAY states

1 - Needs

In the North-east of Nigeria, more than two-thirds of the health facilities are dysfunctional due to the crisis that engulfed the region in over a decade. Hence, vulnerable populations urgently need real-time health information to enable them take informed decisions to protect themselves and family from disease infection including COVID-19. At the community levels, health education officers and community health champions are reliable resources for useful health information messaging. This critical group of the behaviour change continuum have not been trained on current trends, rapidly changing health emergencies communications and new communication tactics in the last decade. As such, they need capacity building on current strategies for risk communication, health information generation, message development and dissemination, rumour management among others during disease outbreaks. This is to enable them to adequately empower communities for protective behaviours in emergencies. Risk communication is a core public health intervention in any disease outbreak and health emergency especially as it provides real-time exchange of information, advice and opinions between experts, officials and people who face a threat to disease infection to enable informed decision-making and to adopt protective behaviors. In this era of incessant/indiscriminate information sharing from numerous sources including reliable and unreliable sources, it has become urgent and essential to establish reliable and evidence-based scientific health information sharing regularly and from trusted sources, especially for vulnerable populations affected by the ongoing insurgency in north-eastern Nigeria. Inadequate information and lack of access to reliable health information predisposes vulnerable populations to the risk of unhealthy behaviours. Rumours and false information thrive extensively among populations affected by crisis due to inadequate access to reliable sources of correct health information.

2 - Activities

The existing structures of community mobilization teams and social mobilization staff and staff working with different sectors like CCCM, DTM, WASH, Food security will be engaged for mass awareness and community mobilization through close community interactions and dissemination of IEC materials. Health and other sector partners will identify and work with local influencers in the site community (such as community leaders, religious leaders, youth and women leaders, health workers, community volunteers) and local networks (women’s groups, youth groups, traditional healers, etc.). Where and when possible, community staff will work with camp management teams, camp/site committees and/or community leaders to carry out consultations on risk assessment, identification of high-risk population group, existing trusted communication channels (formal and informal), and setting up of surveillance focal points per blocks and sections, as well as community task teams, etc. Teams will provide clear and unequivocal messages focusing on what people can do to reduce risk or which actions to take if they think they may have COVID-19. Perceptions, rumors and feedback from camp residents and host communities should be monitored and responded to through trusted communication channels, especially to address negative behaviors and social stigma associated with the outbreak. Partners across the sectors will use the existing large-scale community engagement for social and behavior change approaches to ensure preventive community and individual health and hygiene practices, in line with the national public health containment recommendations. Awareness raising activities may also represent an opportunity to include joint messaging and an occasion for MHPSS actors to provide psychological first aid (PFA) to alleviate the stress and anxiety resulting from the situation. If the response to disease outbreaks such as COVID-19 is to be effective, it is important to ensure that gender norms, roles, and relations that influence women's and men's differential vulnerabilities and status in the societies are considered and addressed. Other activities will include: - Branding of procured health and medical supplies with WHO and donor logos- Short documentary productions on supported activities including hard-to-reach- Media briefing/ visits to supported activities- Production of general visibility products - Capacity Building for LGA Health Education Officers
