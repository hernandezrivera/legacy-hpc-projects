Project Summary

Women and children in NW Syria experience complex and multi-layered issues concerning Protection, MHPSS, and Nutrition-related issues. As it relates to Protection, the recent Voices from Syria report, indicated that residents encounter physical and psychological violence, early marriages, and high rates of sexual assault, rape, and harassment. Additional insights from the Protection Analysis Update (2023), indicate that 99.6% of the reported incidents were female survivors of GBV while the most common forms of GBV experienced by survivors are physical assault (41.5%), followed by psychological/emotional abuse (27.4%), and denial of resources, opportunities, or services (22.4%).
In addition, the recent large-scale MHPSS needs assessment, conducted by Mihad in NW Syria, highlighted the dire need for MHPSS support amongst adult populations. Specifically speaking, adult women exhibit higher rates of presenting symptoms, including a concerning 21.56% experiencing suicidal ideation. As it relates to children in conflict settings, a recent study conducted by Raslan et al. (2021) revealed alarming findings regarding children's mental health and protection issues. Within the sample of children surveyed, a staggering 73.7% were found to have a probable mental health problem. Regardless of the significant need for MHPSS services amongst children, only 23% of MHPSS services in NW Syria are dedicated to children, as reported in the latest 4WS reported by the MHPSS TWG in 2023. These findings underscore the urgent need for comprehensive mental health interventions targeting adult women and children affected by conflict.
In addition, The 2023 HNO highlighted a surge in nutrition assistance demand in NW Syria, with 400,000 more individuals needing support compared to 2022. Across all populations, children under five years, adolescent girls and PLW have relatively high nutritional needs and are the most vulnerable groups. Gender-based Violence (GBV) is common in Syria, exacerbated by poverty and protracted conflict. Girls and women are already disproportionately affected by the impact of emergencies on nutrition, and nutritional insecurity can greatly increase the risk of GBV. GBV, similar to mental health, is a key barrier for uptake of optimal Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices and one of the key drivers in the worsening nutrition situation in Syria.
The main objective is to reduce the prevalence and impact of GBV in NW Syria through comprehensive and integrated prevention, response, and recovery strategies, ensuring the safety, dignity, and well-being of at-risk women and children in the context of the ongoing humanitarian crisis. We aim to increase community awareness and understanding of GBV, its consequences, and prevention strategies, promoting a culture of non-violence and respect for women and children and ensure accessible, quality, and integrated services for at-risk women and children and GBV survivors, including nutrition care, MHPSS support, and other vital basic services.
In addressing the intricate challenges confronting women and children in Northwest Syria, our response to gender-based violence (GBV) services adopts a holistic framework. Emphasising confidentiality, safety, both internal referrals, our approach mitigates stigma to address the multifaceted nature of the situation. Ultimately, we prioritise our PHC activities through Mother Baby Areas (MBAs) with a comprehensive support, integrating Protection, MHPSS, and Nutrition services to address the pressing needs of the population.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Introduction: The following guide outlines the implementation strategy for the holistic approach to GBV services. This initiative aims to enhance the well-being of adult women, pregnant women, new mothers, and their children in communities within NW Syria through an integrated approach. Approach: In our approach, Mihad’s field facilitators play a crucial role in sensitising internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities about GBV and protection risks. These facilitators, are composed of GBV case workers for GBV related issues and PSS workers for MHPSS related issues, and Nutritionists for Nutrition related issues. They are all trained in targeted activities related to their technical expertise, providing gradual awareness-raising sessions, adapting to the unique dynamics of each community. This initiative not only enhances understanding of GBV but also fosters a supportive environment for discussing culturally sensitive topics and ensuring safe identification and referral for cases in need of cross sectoral services. Simultaneously, Mihad's comprehensive service centres and mobile teams are pivotal. These service centres, staffed by professionals trained in gender-sensitive and trauma-informed care, offer a holistic range of services from nutrition and medical care to mental health and psychosocial support, tailored friendly for at-risk women and girls and GBV survivors and based on an in-depth assessment of the context's needs and concerns. In addition, the deployment of mobile and outreach teams ensure that these essential services reach even the most remote or underserved areas, demonstrating Mihad's commitment to accessible and integrated care for affected populations, vulnerable groups, and those at risk in Northwest Syria. Service Cycle: detailed explanation to the approach The project's service cycle is designed strategically, in line with project objectives, to alleviate stigma and maintain cost-effectiveness. To illustrate, the outreach mobile teams are chosen as an entry point to conduct Nutrition and Protection Assessments in designated outreach areas. Here, assessments related to malnutrition risks are conducted while Protection assessments identify GBV risks, leveraging the nutrition teams to engage communities and mitigate stigma. Identified individuals are then seamlessly referred to the MBA centers, where the respective technical facilitators categorise beneficiaries into Nutrition, Protection, and MHPSS services. This structured approach ensures tailored support for acute malnutrition, infant and young child feeding, Protection-related issues, along MHPSS-related issues. Continuous monitoring and evaluation, feedback mechanisms, and adaptive strategies will aim to enhance the project's efficacy and comprehensive documentation aids in reporting to stakeholders. The project concludes by focusing on sustainability, promoting local ownership, for a lasting impact beyond the initial implementation phase.
