Project Summary

The project intends to expand its provision of Inclusive Non-Formal Education to IDP children in 15 camps in northwest Syria.
IAC has access to targeted sites through its team on ground due to the implementation of a FSL activities in same targeted camps, IAC team in Turkey has good experience in the education sector through the implementation of many educational activities within Turkey over the past four years, and is currently planning to carry out educational activities targeting displaced people inside Syria.
IAC will provide Accelerated Learning Program (ALP) for IDP children who have been displaced to Dana (severity 4) and Maaret Tamsrin (severity 4) according WOS PiN Severity 2023, the targeted children are 2,734 (1,504g/1,230b-3% Children with Disability (CwD) aged between (8-16) (grades 1-9). IAC will establish 15 TLS (accessible to CwD) – 6 classrooms and an admin room in each – in 15 camps in Dana and Maaret Tamsrin subdistricts.
A preparation period of 1 - 2 months to establish the TLSs, hire the staff and lightly train them. Trained staff and members of EAs in Harim will apply ASER to decide the levels of students. Two cycles of ALP to be conducted (one-month set up followed by 5 sessions /day, 5 days/week to cover 1 cycle in 5 months.).
ALP is the best fit for children who have been out of school for 2 years or more, and are older than their classmates who are in formal education. One academic semester is accelerated and covers two semesters, and final goal is to transition of students to formal education, as defined by NFE Task Force of Education Cluster.
146 teachers and education personnel 45%m, 55%f, 60%IDPs, 40%HC) will be provided with incentives and trained on ASER, TICC and ALP. All of them will get orientation about prevention of COVID-19 and cholera, CP, child safeguarding policies, and PSEA, and let them sign IAC`s Code of Conduct.
2 blocks of 2 gender-sensitive and disability-friendly toilets will be constructed in each TLS.
Classrooms in TLS will be lightly decorated to make TLS safer and more welcoming Adapted Curriculum B will be printed and distributed.
TLS will be provided with school supplies, teaching & learning materials and furniture.
Safety and security plans will be established and drilled in TLS which will be provided with safety equipment.
TLS will be supported with hygiene supplies to support prevention of COVID-19 and cholera infection.
TLS will be provided with operational running costs.
15 Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) will be established.
Project will target IDP students and their parents through mobile outreach teams covering 17 communities surrounding the TLS in followıng activities:
- Conduct BTL campaigns.
- Run recreational, psychosocial and resilience activities in TLS.
- Awareness raising activities for children and parents focusing on Education related issues, Child Labour, Family tracing and reunification, Basic CP, and Child Rights.
- Preparedness training such as fire drills - self-care - first aid.
- Providing psychosocial support and referral mechanisms based on needs.
- Conducting awareness campaigns about prevention of COVID-19 and cholera.
The project will be prepared by equipping TLS and recruiting and training the educational staff within one or two months and then starting education activities.
The beneficiaries of project are the students (2734), teachers and education personnel (146), Parents in 15 PTAs (1000), children and parents benefiting of BTL campaigns and various awareness raising campaigns (7568).

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2022-2023, provision of education services remain highly prioritized by communities.6.9M children (3-17 years) in need of education support (with an estimated over 2M out of school children), the response will focus on accessibility and availability of relevant education opportunities for learners.Transitioning to secondary school remains a challenge, only 6 out of 14governorates had <10% of children attending secondary school.As in past years boys dropping out at a higher rate than girls, IDP children living in camps were much less likely to attend than resident children.At the beginning of 2022 IAC has conducted a baseline assessment in 17 camps in Dana&Maaret Tamsrin, hosting 19,172 IDPs(4960m,5123w,4517b,4572g) - (at the start of the academic year 2022-2023 the assessment data was rechecked)There are 2,817 (1540g 1277b) school-aged children, 77 (30g 47b) with disability.2,511(1390g 1121b) grades 1-6, 223 (114g 109b) grades 7-9, 83 (36g, 47b) grades 10-12). There are 92 (50f 42m) teachers as well.16 of 17 assessed camps have no schools, a minority of students are attending a school which is far away.Camps managers confirmed presence of spaces to establish TLS and WASH facilities. also sensitization campaigns and recreation activities are not available.There is various anxiety levels among families due to presence of some cases resulted of absence of education mainly girls and low level of awareness of parents-cases like early marriage, child labor

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1- Learning facilities could be attacked (airstrikes, fire..)Mitigation1:Preparedness training such as fire drills - self-care - first aid.Providing TECs with safety and security equipment.Reporting Attacks on Education Reporting Mechanism.Risk2- Teachers or education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students, students participate in bullying of students.Mitigation2:Activating complaints mechanism and clarifying the mechanism used for students Teachers sign code of conductTraining teachers and other staff on professional development on preventing and dealing with bullying, positive discipline, classroom management, and participatory approaches with children. Risk3- Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protectionMitigation3:The construction of the learning centers and thier facilities should be under supervising of specialist engineers to ensure that all TECs'facilities are safe and protected and supplied with necessary equipment, and also will be gender-sensitive and disability-friendly.Risk4- Humanitarian staff, volunteers or associated personnel are not able to make informed decisions.Mitigation4:All teachers and educational personnel will get orientation about CP, child safeguarding policies, and PSEA, and will sign IAC`s Code of Conduct, and also the organization has clear Duty of Care policies that clarify organization’s responsibilities and obligations to its staff.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

To prevent any PSEA issue from occurring during the implementation period of the project activities, especially the activities in which the project staff are in direct contact with the beneficiaries. All field activities have been reviewed and assessed for risk factors for PSEA with the use of the PSEA Network Field and Organizational Mitigation and Prevention Checklists and the following prevention and mitigation measures will be taken: 1- IAC will make sure that All staff, contractor, and volunteers understand and sign the Code of Conduct.2- Field staff with direct contact with beneficiaries will receive PSEA Training.3- IAC will review internal reporting mechanisms and ensure all staff know about reporting mechanisms.4- Activating complaints mechanisms to protect PSEA in particular, placing them in easily accessible places, designing awareness-raising banners, placing boxes for complaints and suggestions, and activating the hotline number (WhatsApp).5 - provide awareness-raising sessions in the targeted community on PSEA and methods for reporting them and gaining the trust of society and spreading humanitarian principles and stick to them.6- Contractors and their workers will not have direct contact with beneficiaries


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption A:- Parents support children's enrollment.- Out of school children show interest to back to school.Risk1: Children aged 8-16 are not interested in program because they are affected with child labor, early marriage and other exploitation form.Mitigation1:-Outreach mobile team will conduct awareness sessions about the value of education.-Back to learning campaign will be launched to explain project to communities.Assumption B: Qualified teachers (females/males) will be hired to launch the education process.Risk2: Lack of qualified teachers (females/males) for non-formal education.Mitigation2: Training teachers (females/males) on non-formal education modality and curricula.Risk 3: Lack of qualified trainers.Mitigation 3: Providing training online and sending trainers from Turkey to Syria.Assumption C: School supplies are delivered to beneficiaries (girls/boys) on timely manner.Risk4: Closing the border-gates between Syria and Turkey hindered the education supplies to be delivered on timely manner.Mitigation4: Procure the supplies and print textbooks and brochures in Syria.Assumption D: Project will not cause increasing of COVID-19 and cholera cases.Risk 5: Increased number of cases infected with COVID-19 and cholera in NW.Mitigation 5: Providing sterilization (sterilization + masks), instructions brochures, and awareness sessions.
