Project Summary

This project aims to address critical humanitarian needs across the child protection interventions to reach 3,000 child including IDPs, returnees and host communities in Roseries, Damazine, Wad-Almahi, Baw , Altdamon and Gissan localities of the Blue Nile Region. The project will respond to needs and gaps created by the recent inter communal conflict in Blue Nile erupted in July 2022 and escalated this October. This conflict resulted in the displacement of about 70,000 people from Wad-Almahi and Gissan localities to different areas within and outside the region including to Damazine and Roseries localities. The project aims to save, increase protection prevention and response capacities through implementing the following activities:
Child Protection: support community-based protection networks to facilitate referral of GBV and child protection cases to healthcare, awareness raising on child protection to 2000 people, support foster care to unaccompanied and separated children

1 - Needs

The security situation is fragile in Blue Nile following the inter-communal conflict that erupted in mid-July 2022. At least 69,550 people have reportedly been displaced within the state and into neighboring localities and states (IOM DTM; Ar Rusayris, Blue Nile; 23 October, 2022). Inter-communal violence reignited in Blue Nile's Wad-Almahi locality on 13 October. Different reports indicate that many people were killed and injured, while many homes were burnt down in the Dam towns. Internal displacement, food insecurity and natural disasters are the key drivers of humanitarian needs in the region, all of which are compounded by the economic crisis and inadequate basic services such as water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), health, Protection and education services. In addition, there is also a significant shortage of PSS and case management services. Community-based protection networks (CBPNs) are not functional, and there is a critical need for experienced GBV social workers and psychologists to provide PSS and counseling services. The total number of children reported on the ground by the social worker was 1,774.In the proposed localities, among conflict affected communities VSF Germany intends to address critical child protection , support community-based protection network and PSS, as well as referral services is intended to target 3,000 people.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

VSF-G will carry out intensive community mobilization and sensitization campaigns for the target beneficiaries along with the local partners, to effectively inform the target communities on the project and ensure participation and adoption of the project activities. The working methodology will orient the target people on accountability/CFMs and basic protection concepts. As well as the project beneficiaries will be sensitized on gender inclusion (at least 50% women participation in project activities will be ensured). Participation of girls/women and boys/men of all age groups with and without disabilities will be considered through: (a) beneficiary targeting for the different activities (i.e. beneficiary selection criteria which will be developed employing community participation approaches, to reach those who are most vulnerable among affected people including IDPs, returnees, pastoralists and farmers. With focus on families with malnourished children, female-headed households and families with disabled member(s)); (b) selection of water points (sites) to be rehabilitated or upgraded taking into account the safety of women and girls and other vulnerable members of the communities; (c) ensuring women representation in all community committees (at least 30%); (d) CFMs will assist in early detection of problems such as targeting (exclusion/inclusion) and misconduct; (e) the safety and dignity of beneficiaries will be prioritized through assessing and identifying risks associated with different locations/activities, to ensure implementation/distribution sites and roads leading to these sites are safe for all people involved (particularly girls, women and disabled persons), and that beneficiaries will not face problems with theft, intimidation and extortion; (f) and special arrangements will be made for people with special needs, as appropriate. Moreover, VSF-G always tries to create equal access and opportunities for all community members to benefit from humanitarian interventions through adequate community sensitization; inclusive participatory beneficiary selection and verification processes; and implementing Accountability to the affected Population (AAP) and Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFMs). Through the abovementioned approaches we expected to be able to reach girls/women and boys/men of all age groups with and without disabilities equally with the planned project activities. VSF-G fully considers “do no harm” in its activities through application of conflict-sensitivity, inclusive targeting of people from different ethnic groups, pastoralists and farmers, selection of distribution sites.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Planning of this project interventions is majorly based on the feedback from the target communities and other key stakeholders in the target locations. VSF-G adopts Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) guidelines and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in setting standards, building capacity, communication, participation, monitoring and reporting on compliance, addressing complaints and development of strategy to improve the quality of partnership on principles of accountability. VSF-G approach focuses on assessing and addressing the needs of all within the communities, including men, women, boys, girls, elders, persons with disabilities among other vulnerable and marginalized groups. Community participation will be promoted through all the stages of the project (planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation), community consultation meetings and orientation sessions will be held at the beginning of the project to verify needs, gauge acceptance, ensure the project is well understood by target communities, raise the communities’ awareness with regard to their participatory roles and responsibilities, and the responsibilities of the program partners towards them, as well as to identify the most appropriate channels for communication. Community-based committees including representatives from a cross-section (with respect to age, gender and diversity) will be formed to participate in developing an effective communication strategy including appropriate complaint and feedback mechanisms (e.g. hotline, complaints/suggestions boxes), taking into consideration literacy rates, availability of communication devices and accessibility to VSF-G’s ad partners’ offices; to ensure accessibility of all community members (men, women, children, elders and disabled people) and provision of timely response. Community committees will also be involved in developing beneficiary selection process, follow-up of activity implementation and project evaluation
