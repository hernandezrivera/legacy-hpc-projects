Project Summary

The main goal of this Action is to ensure targeted crisis-affected households, including those in underserved communities have their essential non-food item and winterization needs met before the winter of 2022-23. The Action will ensure immediate access to non-food items and winterization items to the population in need to maintain health, privacy, and dignity.
WWGVC plans to distribute core NFIs and minimum winterization package (excluding fuel) to 6,000 vulnerable and underserved adults and children (1,200 HHs) in 2022 and 850 additional HHs in 2023, including IDPs and returnees in rural Aleppo and Deir Ez Zor. The kits will be distributed for HHs in rural areas with high severity and with non-suitable shelter conditions. The distribution of items will comply with the NFI cluster specifications, including the selection criteria of beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will be selected also according to vulnerability criteria set in close consultation with the target population. Different stakeholders, targeted population, community leaders and SARC will be consulted in setting the criteria, to ensure transparency, acceptance and accountability. Selected HHs will include female-headed HHs, HH including elderly people, and/or people with disabilities, and/or infants and families with low or no income.
The project will be initiated with 1)Needs assessment, in coordination with SARC, taking into consideration the existing databases of WWGVC's education program. The assessment will also study the different needs of the gender and age groups of the targeted population. 2) Market assessment to study the availability of core NFI and winterization kits in the country. 3) Procurement and storage of kits in SARC warehouses in each governorate. 4) identification of community distribution points taking into consideration C-18 precautionary measures following the guidelines of the S/NFI cluster. This will be followed by the in-kind distribution of core NFI kits and winterization kits to 1,200 HHs between Aleppo and Deir Ez Zor.
GVC will coordinate with SARC, MoE, NFI and WASH Sectors as well as other present humanitarian actor (including UN Agencies, i.e. UNICEF and UNHCR) to ensure information sharing, and avoid any duplication and overlapping during implementation.

4 - Needs

Needs have been assessed through UNOCHA’s HNO and other reports/updates, along with triangulating the data with meetings with local stakeholders including SARC in Aleppo and Deir Ez Zor. In 2022, and with the COVID-19 pandemic and the further deteriorating economic conditions, more people are in need of humanitarian assistance related to NFIs. According to the 2022 HNO, there's a 4% increase in the PiN of NFI support increasing to reach 4.91 M. 70% of the households in need are facing decreased affordability to meet basic needs including clothing and other NFIs. Residents of rural Aleppo and rural Deir Ez Zor are chronically underserved, living in sub-standard shelter conditions, with no electricity, and with no access to heating or protection from the weather elements. The population at risk includes the female HoHs, youth, and people with functional disabilities who’s access to income is reduced and the same goes for their affordability to basic non-food items. A high number of out-of-school children is also identified in the same areas through WWGVC's education activities with a high percentage caused by child labor and inability to afford the costs of education even. The needs will also be confirmed through an ongoing assessment by WWGVC targeting access to WASH services and NFIs that is set to be completed in March 2022.

5 - Implementation of activities

The activities planned to be implemented are the following: A1) Assessment of family needs in the prioritized areas of Aleppo and Deir Ez Zor. A2) Market assessment to study the availability of the supplies in the local market including the intended quality. The national-level market assessment will ensure the identification of suppliers able to supply and preposition stock in Aleppo and Deir Ez Zor. A3) Procurement of kits including quality inspection at bidding and reception phase in addition to the storage of kits in SARC warehouses in each governorate. A4) Identification of community distribution points taking into consideration C-19 precautionary measures following the guidelines of the S/NFI cluster and distribution of core NFIs, and core winter items to 1200 HHs. The NFI clusters' safe distribution guidelines will be followed. A5) Monitoring, evaluation, and reporting: standard monitoring tools will be used to collect qualitative and quantitative data from the field. The data collection tools will include distribution reports, key informant interviews (KIIs) and observation checklists. PDM will also be conducted after the distribution following the unified template by the NFI sector.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

RISK: Tension between host communities, IDPs, and returnees, and between households receiving and not receiving the assistance. Likelihood: High, Impact: High, Mitigation Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. Selection criteria are transparent and communicated with the community in advance. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. RISK: Abuse of Authority by distribution actors (GBV, discrimination, monetary coercion). Likelihood: Hight, Impact: Hight. Mitigation: Ensure the team is aware of the code of conduct. The internal procedure is developed and applied to identify perpetrators. Coordinate with local protection actors and involve them in the planning and conducting of the distribution. RISK: Risk of physical harm at distribution points. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Well-organized and planned distribution in schools with enough safe space for the distribution. Consult with authorities and communities and agree on the location of distribution that is close and accessible to persons with disabilities. RISK: Lack of accepted documentation may unfairly exclude certain populations. Likelihood: low. Impact: low. Mitigation measures: Assessment is done irrespective of the availability of documentation. Households are asked for available documents during the confirmation of the eligible phase on the phone and informed and agreed on the methodology of verification.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

WWGVC has a child safeguarding policy aimed at ensuring that children’s rights are recognized and respected and that children are protected from any form of violence. The policy contains a set of standards that all WWGVC staff must take into consideration, including • placing children’s safety well-being above any other considerations • reporting any concerns cases of suspected child abuse or concerns about the welfare of a minor • planning and organizing project activities whilst minimizing risks for children • avoiding culturally inappropriate physical contact with children WWGVC is committed to create and maintain an environment where children swell-being is promoted in every aspect of its operations. Preventions measures include:• AWARENESS RAISING: All new staff is introduced to the Child Safeguarding Policy. •TRAINING: WW-GVC commits itself to conduct periodical training in relation to thisPolicy to adequately inform the staff, raise their awareness and enhance their capacity in preventing, and dealing with possible events. • RISK ASSESSMENT: WWGVCendeavors to reduce these risks to a bare minimum. Regular risk assessments are carried out in order to identify, evaluate and mitigate risks throughout the organization's operations. • SAFE PROGRAMMING: WWGVC takes proactive measures at every stage of project implementation to avoid causing harm inadvertently whilst mainstreaming best practices.
