Project Summary

PUI throughout the planned project will provide vulnerable, conflict affected populations with access to improved, safe, healthy living accommodations as follows :
- Rehabilitation of emergency shelter spaces in collective centres, unfinished buildings, transitional outdoor spaces, spontaneous settlements, and other emergency spaces reaching 200 Household .
- Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing and related community/public infrastructure and facilities to owners/tenants/host families (can include: materials, cash, voucher, cash-for-work, labour, etc.) reaching 300 Households .
PUI will ensue the full cooperation with relevant stakeholders, SARC, and Shelter sector, to facilitate needed approvals, avoid overlapping between INGOs and share previous experiences and knowledge

Needs

As the crisis and armed conflict continues within the Syrian Arab Republic, an economic recession has emerged along with international economic sanctions all further exacerbated by the level of destruction across the country , ­ The Syrian context remains fluid, fragile and is characterized by both high needs and high vulnerabilities. And, although 2018 saw much change on the ground and the on-going political negotiations, the solid political solutions are yet to materialize and as such Humanitarian Actors must adapt and tailor their programming to include both life-saving needs and early recovery efforts. In more details: The shelter situation in Syria continues to worsen with 4.7 million in need of urgent shelter support, and no adequate housing is available & low standards of currently available shelters. This will lead to the deterioration of the health status of the population, reduction of community cohesion, dignity and self-respect. As the security situation is getting more stabilized in some areas, an accelerating return trend is witnessed. Those returnees are seeking more durable shelter solution, however they lack the capacity to repair or rehabilitate their own damaged shelters.  For those who had to leave their houses and can barely afford renting a house because of the continuous increase in rent prices, they are forced to live with friends/family, or within collective shelters or in unfinished buildings. This leads to increasing pressure on host communities which can result negative coping strategies on both populations. Increasing demand for segregation of the living places and WASH facilities in collective shelters taking into account the special needs of women and girls as they live among extended families and over-crowded shelters. As conducting deep needs assessment, remain restricted for INGOs, PUI field staff , through field visits and meeting with key informants in targeted locations identified huge needs for shelter support as the selected locations are: • Newly accessible for INGOS, stable, with a low risk of conflict seen as recurring. • The location is more than 6km from an active frontline; • The location has suffered direct impact from conflict; • High return trend, • High severity scale(3-6), however, for northern rural Lattakia, PUI saved no efforts to support the population there in a multi sector integrated approach, mainly in education and shelter, and will continue to support them as the needs remains high, in addition to its multi ethnic and religious complexity that is already making Lattkaia a breeding ground for instability. • As the final political solutions are yet to come to fruition, humanitarian actors need to adapt their interventions to reduce the impact of these eight years of conflict has had on the population. And though emergency humanitarian response is still a requirement to cover the immediate lifesaving needs of the population, the scope needs to be expanded. A focus on recovery is required in suitable areas, to mitigate the severe impact of this protracted crisis has had on the population, and to reduce the risk of further deterioration in the coming years

Implementation of activities

PUI is planning to support conflict affected population in targtted areas with better access to shelter services through : - Rehabilitation of emergency shelter spaces in collective centres, unfinished buildings, transitional outdoor spaces, spontaneous settlements, and other emergency spaces targeting IDPs - Rehabilitation of partially damage departments targeting returnees PUI will coordinate with the municipalities, Governorates and Ministry of Local Administration and Environment (MoLAE) to identify the shelter needs of each area. The response could include rehabilitation of collective shelters, light shelter rehabilitation, and repair of damaged houses. PUI will give priority to the vulnerable families including women headed household, elderly and people with disabilities during the selection of the beneficiaries.For both objectives.The needs of males and females will be considered ensuring gender segregated latrines with lockable doors and sufficient lightning are rehabilitated. Additionally, ramps to access shelter and bidet toilets for disabled, elderly and mothers with infants will be provided.HLP issues, if identified, will be referred to the municipalities and MoLAE.Once the prioritization is completed, PUI engineers will identify the needs of the shelters in consultation with the owners and beneficiaries in order to bring the living spaces as per the guideline and minimum standards agreed by the shelter sector.After selecting qualified contractor through the PUI procurement process, the monitoring team of PUI will visit the shelter on regular basis to ensure that the work is implemented as per the agree specification and standards, report any issues at the contractor and beneficiaries level to PUI office to be addressed.PUI will explain the scope of the rehabilitation to the beneficiaries and local stakeholders; however, the rehabilitated apartment will be handed over to the beneficiaries in the presence of PUI monitoring staff.

Protection Risk Analysis

PUI is working in accordance with “Do No Harm” principle, seeking no further harm to the people in need. Therefore the proposed activities taking in consideration the potential risk that might unintendedly happen and, the measures that should be followed to mitigate them.More specifically for this project, according to our previous experience and Shelter sector-PRA PUI proposed the followingFor collective shelter rehabilitation,- it is likely that the rehabilitation/upgrading collective shelters for IDPs might create tension within the community, leading to a feeling of discrimination , as a mitigation measure during the implementation PUI staff will engage with the community leaders, to raise awareness about the selection criteria- Additionally , Crowded living conditions and lack of lightening and gender segregated sanitation might lead to increase in GBV among the IDPs, To mitigate this PUI will built Gender segregated toilettes with locks and lights, to ensure safe access to women and girls.For partially damaged houses. :- Risk of increasing tension among Returnees, due to different assistance packages, that received from other humanitarian actors. To mitigate this PUI fully coordinating with the shelter sector to harmonize the standers, and currently is applying the sector-standardized assistance for the rehabilitation of partially damaged apartments- Lack of required legal document proving the property/ occupancy, to mitigate this PU will refer beneficiaries to Humanitarian partners carrying out legal support. - The risk of increased tension among Returnees due to unclear list of beneficiaries, as mitigation to this risk PUI works in close coordination with MOLA to select the beneficiaries based on clear sets of standards includes vulnerability level and possession of property/ownership legal documents.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The following are seen as key assumptions: - Security situation remains stable, and humanitarian access consistent; accordingly PUI ensure through the intervention that all security procedures are in place and followed . - No major fluctuation of Syrian Pound; - No mass movements of populations into/out of intervention areas;- Financial transactions are still possible to Syria . Below are the main risks to the action and the measures PUI will take in response: - Local population does not accepts PUI´s intervention; to mitigate this PUI will ensure the full engagement of the community leaders and local stakeholders in project design and well introduce the benefits to the targeted community that will occur . - Lagging Return movements; it is not fully under PUI control, however PUI follows a multi sector integrated approach in programming to support the community resilience in a multi dimensions which might encourage more people to return to their original houses, if the lack of access to basic services is their main hinder to return . - Delay in receiving authorizations and clearances to quickly intervene. As mitigation to this PUI is fully coordinating with the relevant line ministries and updating MoLAE with them to facilitate the process of getting needed approvals.
