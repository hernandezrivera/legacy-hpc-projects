Project Summary

The protection needs in Somalia changed and evolved over time, prompting the current crisis to affect individuals of all ages and genders, as well as from diverse social backgrounds, in different ways and scale. The prediction of conflict, floods and others natural disaster is high in 2021 due to no visible change can be seen in the ground, where country is going to host new government whose political performance is yet to predict, protection needs will be the priority one in a negative political effects hosted by the country. The total figure of those in need of protection services in 2021 include an estimated 28,000 refugees and asylum seekers in Somalia and 109,989 refugee returnees. People who are lacking or unable to access services are susceptible to various human rights violations, cyclical displacement and erosion of coping mechanisms and social cohesion.
Generally, in this year, Somalia has witnessed various shocks fronted by conflict, desert locust and serious flooding since June affected, driving over 191,800 people in Hirshabelle, South West and Jubaland states as well as Banadir region. Lower Shabelle region which is part of South West State territories was one of the badly affected by these shocks, particularly in the river banks parts in Afgooye, Wanla Weyn, Marka, Qoryooley and Kurtunwaarey.
The driven population estimated of 2.6 million IDPs in Somalia was forced to live in informal and unplanned settlements where living conditions are poor, exposing in concerns of safety and security. Among the most vulnerable groups were comprising of Persons with specific needs: people with physical, sensorial, intellectual and mental disabilities, adolescent girls and boys, pregnant and lactating women and girls, female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children, persons from minority clans, older persons without caregivers, persons missing documentation, persons facing eviction, persons living in areas with armed conflict and violence.
The HNO report 2020, The lack of protection from violence, abuse and neglect remains a key consequence of the current humanitarian crisis in Somalia for up to 3.2 million people out of which1.9 million are living in displacement and 1.2 million people belong to host communities. The number of children in need of protection has increased significantly due to recurrence of conflicts, droughts and increased military operations led by the SFG against AS. From January to June 2019, the Country Task Force on Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) verified and documented 1,473 cases (1,272 boys and 201 girls) of violence against children, with abduction and recruitment/use of children accounting for 41% and 55 % of the overall number of children affected. A multi-sector joint assessment on IDP Influx in South West State September 2020 noted Major protection issues in the community as FGM with 70%, restriction of movement 69%, recruitment of children by armed forces 60% and gender and sexual violence 51%. 67% of the respondents reported witnessing or hearing of children being recruited or used as soldiers/fighters.
This project aims to address the aforementioned needs and ensure that protection concerns among children living in crisis, women, girls, boys and men including those with special needs such as the disabled are addressed through provision of prevention and response services by conducting constant monitoring, reporting all protection/child protection and GBV risks and responds to any victim/survival in need for GBV and Child Protection responses including referral and response mechanisms to the services facilities, provision of PSS, case management and advocacy through training the humanitarian actors, government representatives, community leaders and survivors on Protection and GBVs risks and measurement to take for risk prevention and mitigations.

Project Activities

The project has the following objective through the implementation of this project;Objectives 1: Provide well-coordinated and disability, gender-sensitive quality child protection services to 1200 Boys and girls including adolescents, children with disabilities, facing life-threatening risks of abuse, neglect, violence, exploitation, and severe distress by 2021 in Marka, Afgooye, Qoryooley and Kurtunwaarey districts.Activity 1.1: Establish Child friendly spaces and psychosocial support in Lower Shabelle.Activity 1.2: Assessment, monitoring, awareness raising and outreach (Child Protection) and reporting of displacement and general protection violations Objective 2: By 2021, AYUUB will support 1200 vulnerable and at risk girls and boys in IDP camps and in host communities have access to quality child protection prevention and response services 2021 in Marka, Afgooye, Qoryooley and Kurtunwaarey districts.Activity 2.1: Protection oriented assistance for households with specific needs or heightened vulnerability targeting the disabled and victims of sexual violence including rape. Objective3: To enable 500 Women and girls (including those with disabilities) in IDP camps and host communities to access to safe, timely, confidential, quality coordinated GBV specialized services through integrated referrals.Activity 3.1: Access to Basic General Protection Services including Case management, family tracing and reunification for minors lost during conflict including reintegration for ex-child soldiers and referral services to vulnerable and IDPs. The project will conduct constant monitoring, reporting all protection/child protection and GBV risks and responds to any victim/survival in need for GBV and Child Protection responses including referral and response mechanisms to the services facilities, provision of PSS, case management and advocacy through training the humanitarian actors, government representatives, community leaders and survivors on Protection and GBVs risks and measurement to take for risk prevention and mitigations.

Mitigation Measures
