Project Summary

This project is a response to critical and lifesaving, Nutrition, and health needs of the IDPs and Host communities in Ulang County Upper Nile state targeting a total of 7923 beneficiaries (MAM under 5=3712 and MAM PLW=4211 with the main objective of reducing malnutrition prevalence rates amongst children 6-59 months and pregnant women (PLWs)
This project will be implemented by GOAL in conjunction with local NGO (UNKEA). GOAL will implement TSFP while UNKEA will implement OTP services. Food security activities will be integrated as part of the main nutrition program. The proposed response aligns with the HRP 2022 priorities for food security and livelihoods, nutrition, and health cluster strategies and the interventions will meet both immediate and lifesaving needs among affected populations while strengthening local capacity to deliver high quality service and contributing to reducing dependencies on food assistance as well as fostering longer term behavior change outcome.
This project will be implemented in all Payams in Ulang County targeting hard to reach areas and key activities will include; providing integrated preventive and curative nutrition services targeting the most vulnerable groups including responding to severe and moderate acute malnutrition and improve maternal infant and young child feeding practices comprehensive

1 - Needs

According to the humanitarian needs overview report February 2022, 8.9 million people 24% children and 53% women are in need of humanitarian assistance in 2022 in South Sudan, with food assistance ranked as the number one priority need ( at 36% average).About 2 million people are in need of nutrition services alone,67% children under 5 and 33 % Pregnant and lactating women with over 14,000 in extreme need in Ulang County.The latest IPC data for South Sudan indicates serious levels of food insecurity. This is the result of the ongoing change in weather patterns leading for example to prolonged flooding affecting agricultural activity as well as the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and related restrictions in movement which affected access to and functionality of markets. The escalation in localized conflict during 2021 which claimed many lives and caused significant damage to infrastructure has also had a profound impact in Upper Nile State. The geographic area targeted by this project, Ulang County ,is one of the most affected counties in Upper Nile State. The population is affected by a multitude of complex challenges, shocks, and stresses, because of the prolonged impacts of conflict, inter- and intra-ethnic disputes, climate-related hazards (such as flooding, and unpredictable rainfall), and economic shocks. The impact of these shocks and stresses continuously undermines development, frequently resulting in disrupted agricultural production and loss of harvests; loss of livelihoods, property, and assets; mass displacement; inter community tensions; destruction of infrastructure, dysfunctional markets, and disrupted trade routes. Cyclical conflicts and climate-induced disasters are becoming more frequent and more intense. The populations suffer from chronic lack of access to food and sustainable livelihood options, forcing households to resort to negative coping mechanisms detrimental to health, well-being, and long-term development. Ulang county’s population 54090 and 13401 population are displaced by flood facing ‘Crisis’ levels or worse acute food insecurity in 2021 (IPC, 2021), and the situation has been further impacted bythe current economic downturn and high inflation rates, in addition to food price hikes exacerbated by the impact of COVID-19. High malnutrition level persists with deteriorating food insecurity, and limited access to health and nutrition services severely exacerbated by the 2021 flooding which affected access to much needed nutrition and health services in both counties and surrounding nutrition sites resulting in an average of 8 hours' walk to access nutrition services. With the already dire conditions facing the population, malnutrition cases increased in 2021 as evidenced in the FSNMS reports 55.0% with GAM rate of 16.1% and SAM rate of 2.6%,MAM 13.5%. This, coupled with high morbidity, limited access to basic services, as well as poor childcare and feeding practices contribute negatively to the prevalence of acute malnutrition. Limited access to health services coupled with high levels of acute malnutrition, frequent outbreaks of preventable diseases such as malaria, acute watery diarrhea, and RTI.COVID-19 adversely affect basic health service delivery. Particularly in Ulang, the flooding and inter communal conflicts in 2021 led to displacement of 13401 people (IRNA 2021, Ulang). As a result, the county is faced with increased morbidity, inaccessible health services, increasing waterborne and hygiene preventable diseases.GOAL and UNKEA are operational in Ulang county and have identified high levels of need through engagement with local government and communities. The proposed activities respond to the needs identified during ongoing projects, rapid assessments and in consultation with communities and relevant stakeholders.

The project activities have been aligned with the nutrition cluster objectives and they include:Conduct training for 40 nutrition staffs on MIYCN Counselling.Conduct MIYCN counselling seasons in all the 20 nutrition sites using IYCF charts provided by MOH/UNICEF/WHO/WFP to caregivers.Community screening for malnutrition and referral.Conduct community or household level/Family MUAC screening of children 6-59mnth for acute malnutrition and refer to nutrition sites..Admit and treat all children with SAM in CMAM programTreatment of Moderate acute malnutrition in children 6-59month.Admit and treat all children with MAM in CMAM program.Conduct training to all the nutrition staffs on CMAM program.Treat Moderate acute malnutrition in Pregnant and Lactating women.Conduct GBV safety audits in all the nutrition sites in UlangConduct training on protection/GBV to all 40 nutrition staffs in Ulang...Conduct malaria testing using RDT for children with SAM and MAM in all the 20 nutrition sites.Integration with Food Security and livelihood Cluster Establish kitchen garden demonstration in all the 20 nutrition sites.Conduct cooking demonstrations in all targeted sitesConduct sensitization to make raise community awareness of systems in place for representation of their different interests and needs.Establish QAAP committee in which Mother to Mother support groups are part of it.Establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing a suggestion boxes in the OTP/TSFP sites, initiating community focus group discussions with members of the communityProvide training to the nutrition programme staff to understand how to serve best the beneficiaries using the QAAP approach.Raise awareness and provide information to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement in the nutrition sites county (ies) Involve the communities in design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation on the planed or on-going nutrition project
