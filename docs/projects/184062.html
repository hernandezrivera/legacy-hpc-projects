Project Summary

This Project intends to support the people in needs through livelihood and food security projects and to mitigate the imminent deterioration of food security and nutrition conditions among IDPs and destitute vulnerable returnees and host communities and to protect livelihood assets of direct beneficiaries in Kabkabyia, Kornoe, Um baru, , ELserief , Saraf Omera , Katila and Umdafug localities in South and North Darfur States. The project will target the displaced persons, returnees and extremely vulnerable host communities through the provision of agriculture support services and supplies, livestock assets protection services including beneficiaries’ capacity building. Particular attention will be given to women-headed households and households with multiple children under the age of five, as well as orphans. People with special needs (PWSNs) , disabilities, elderly people and pregnant and lactating women(PLW) will be targeted as well. The main objective of the project is to reduce food and nutrition insecurity and save lives of the targeted population. However, the project also aims to improve the food and nutrition security of the returnees in South and North Darfur. The project will provide livestock assets protection and equipment for the CAHWs , train in basic livestock production and management and some CAHW’s will be identified, trained and provided with kits. The project will provide livelihood restoration packages to host communities and returnees in the targeted areas , provision of agriculture seeds and tools to 7250 House Holds of vulnerable small holder farmers; in the seven targeted localities and provision of Veterinary Vaccination and treatment , provision of livelihood and income generation activities to 490 beneficiaries including 245 women and 245 men and provision of donkey carts to 490 beneficiaries in the targeted localities
The project as well will establish community agricultural committees and develop roles and responsibilities of committees: e.g., supporting agricultural-related income-generating activities. Besides seeds distribution such as watermelon, okra, tomato, cucumber, carrot, rocket etc..

1 - Needs

Food insecurity and livelihood remain alarmingly high in the Sudan due to a number of factors. Firstly, as a result of the protracted conflict and displacement since 2003 that is still lingering. The situation is further impacted by the existing economic downturn and higher inflation rates, in addition to food price hikes exacerbated by the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic lockdown. The prevailing humanitarian situation deteriorated rapidly in the first six months of 2020, increasing the numbers of rising hunger across the country. In 2020, the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) estimated that more than 9.3 million people, almost a quarter of the entire population of the Sudan, are in need of humanitarian assistance; an increase of 75 percent compared with the 5.2 million in need in 2015. North and South Darfur states are among the states with the highest acute food insecurity. These localities are currently classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). The most vulnerable groups in the Sudan include internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, vulnerable residents who are engaged in farming and pastoral livelihoods. Women and girls are more likely to experience a worsening of existing inequalities and disproportionate secondary impacts of restrictions compared with men and boys. Even more at risk are other marginalized groups, including persons with disabilities and those in extreme poverty.Stability and security in the two targeted states under this remain precarious due to different contexts current Sudan is passing, such as peace agreement and expected return in North & South Darfur localities. As per the FSL sector in Sudan, 7,139,718 people identified as in need of food security and need action, for this specific project HDPO targeting South Darfur (Umdafug, Katila) and North Darfur (Kernoi, Um baru, ELserief, Kabkabyia, and Saraf Omra), these localities have been identified as high priorities and in IPC phase 3 and HNO 2020/2021. The above-mentioned situation leading to urgent actions is required to save lives and livelihoods of populations by providing emergency agricultural and livestock support to farmers, with special focus on localities prone to natural disasters including floods and drought and affected by the new arrival returns. Lack of basic seeds (sorghum & groundnuts) of 7 localities in North & South Darfur and the main gabs to secure food are low inputs (through vouchers & fair), and ignorance of new basic agricultural techniques (farmers to be trained on agricultural extension, restocking and IGAs). Seed shortages, worsened by insecurity and constant increase in agricultural inputs prices, result in lower cultivation. This project aims to improve access to agricultural assets, inputs, and services to increase and diversify their food and seed production and hence restore and strengthen their capacity to sustain their food and nutrition security; improve access by female and male pastoral and agro-pastoral groups to livestock protection and production inputs and services to restore and sustain their food and nutrition security; especially women-headed households, malnourished and elders to diversify their income sources. The awareness, knowledge, and skills for better environmental protection and sustainable management of natural resources are improved in and around villages hosting IDPs, returnees, and drought-affected households in the affected areas. Livestock looting and losses due to lack of veterinary services affected the complementary feeding for children. Scale-up diversified livelihoods program for improved self-reliance, resilience building, and social protection to all vulnerable communities and implement water harvesting programs in water- deficit areas and to improve the existing water sources for more water access for the livestock. Scale-up and improve access to basic services (health & WASH) throughout the year.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

This project will work with both beneficiaries and the authorities. The food vendors across the intervention areas avoid the provision of unsuitable food packing material (against local regulations; not recyclable) and related improper disposal of food packaging waste. Work together to eliminate or minimize the activities related to environmental degradation and possible security and conflict risks through the increased need for firewood/water collection for cooking caused by food selection that may require long cooking time or large quantities of water. Increase awareness that reduces rate Procurement goods that may lead to unsustainable cropping and livestock production systems, agrochemical use, land conversion (e.g., from forest to pasture) if not properly sourced, and the lack of treatment of waste in food processing. Technical support and awareness sessions at different levels to reduce environmentally unfriendly activities such as charcoal making, woodcutting, illegal hunting, distribution of assets, machinery, and cash in an area of insecurity: insecurity can lead to conflict and the displacement of people, the abandonment of business properties and assets, creating a problem of waste management. Eliminating the use of un-recyclable packaging and labels to boost commercialization creates waste if it does not absorb the product.
