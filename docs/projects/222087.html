Project Summary

The project is designed to address the pervasive issue of Gender Based Violence in Upper Nile and Unity States of South Sudan, specifically in Rubkona, Leer, Malakal and Renk Counties. The interventions will support adolescent girls (10-19 years) and young women and vulnerable communities- those displaced by conflict and those at heightened risk of GBV due to economic factors, who are at risk of GBV and survivors of GBV including child marriage to mitigate, prevent and respond to GBV effectively. Through provision of LifeSkills to build their resilience capacities, provision of comprehensive GBV case management services and multi sectoral referrals. The project will benefit 7800 vulnerable communities directly who are adolescent girls, young women, young mothers and persons with disabilities through access to comprehensive GBV response services, including GBV case management, psychosocial support, and referral to legal and health services, life skills and livelihood support and consequently improve their physiological wellbeing.
Plan Intenational with its implementing partner HACO will regularly report implementation of GBV interventions on 5W`s and resources mobilized on FTS.

1. Needs

Gender Based Violence (GBV) is a critical protection concern in South Sudan putting women and children at high risk. Sudan crisis, intercommunal conflict situations and climate change vulnerabilities drive a growing number of refugees and returnees and internally displaced Populations and increase the demand for protection services. The displaced populations, particularly women, children, and marginalized groups, are at heightened risk of violence, exploitation, and abuse. According to the national prevalence study on violence against women and men conducted in 2022, 58% of women and girls experienced at least one form of GBV over the last 12 months. 2.75 million people need GBV prevention, risk Mitigation and Response Services in 2025. 27% are vulnerable communities in IDPs, 14% are returnees and 59% of the population is in host communities (HNRP, 2025). Gender-based and conflict-related sexual violence, which includes child marriage and early pregnancies was among the most pressing concerns that require urgent attention in Rubkona, Leer and Mayardit counties of Unity State (MSRNA Report December 2024). The 2023/2024 Multidimensional Poverty Analysis-South Sudan reports 52% of all girls under 18 are married and 1 in 3 girls are pregnant before turning 15. The GBV risks situation is aggravated by crises, inter-communal conflicts, floods, and the Sudan Crisis, where returnees, IDPs and host communities in affected areas experienced high protection risks. Women and girls are more vulnerable to GBV risks due to displacement, climate change vulnerabilities separation of families, cultural beliefs, and changing roles within the families. Customary systems, running parallel to the formal legal system, often perpetuate gender bias and discrimination. Additionally, economic hardships, harmful cultural norms, food insecurity, flooding, and limited livelihood opportunities force communities especially women and girls to be subjected to negative coping mechanisms including sexual exploitation and child marriage to girls and women.Some of the locations including Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei States are highly affected due to crises, climate change vulnerabilities and Sudan crisis vulnerabilities, for instance, Child sexual violence (25%) and forced marriage(21%) were reported to be high forms of GBV affecting girls and women returnees, IDPs and host communities in Unity and Jonglei States also in Yei County, Central equatorial 45% of surveyed households faced threats against women and girls, including rape (32%), sexual assault (26%), physical assault (23%), forced marriages (20%), and domestic violence (17%) . There is limited GBV prevention mechanisms and inadequate response services to respond to the needs of vulnerable populations, especially limited access to specialized services for survivors, including GBV case management, psychosocial support, safety and security, and legal aid. Safe houses, Women and Girls' spaces. The GBV incidents are underreported, particularly GBV is hindered by risks of stigmatization, retaliation, and shame as well as lack of survivor-centred service provision, when the services are in short supply, the returnees, IDPs and host community members resort to negative coping mechanisms including child marriage. Adolescents are disproportionally impacted by GBV due to their young age and inexperience with relationships which can heighten their risk of GBV. The proposed intervention will empower vulnerable communities with training, awareness and skills in the prevention of GBV, awareness on access to services, and provision of comprehensive GBV case management services to survivors, establish/strengthen referral pathways, with cash and Dignity kits assistance included in GBV case management to respond to the needs of the survivors. The intervention will also empower local communities with livelihood skills to enhance their capacities in preventing and mitigating GBV risks.

2. Project activities

The overall aim of the project is to enhance access to life-saving comprehensive GBV prevention, response services and mitigation actions for internally displaced, returnee’s girls, women, boys, men, and their communities in Unity and Upper Nile States. This will be achieved through two outcomes: Outcome 1: Increased access for adolescent girls and young women to comprehensive GBV case management services, PSS and livelihood support• Conduct 5 days GBV case management training to 30 GBV case workers Rubkona, Leer, Renk and Malakal: Plan International in collaboration with HACO will conduct 5 days of training on GBV case management for newly recruited case workers to ensure quality GBV service provision. The trained caseworkers will provide survivor-centered case management to adolescent girls and young women who are at risk of GBV/GBV survivors.• Distribution of dignity kits to 1500 Adolescent Girls and Young women in need, based on assessed need and vulnerability. The content of the kits will be based on the input and preferences of adolescent girls (through consultations) and in line with UNFPA’s Standard Kits SOPs in the country.• Establish women and adolescent girls' safe spaces for psychosocial support, life skills and enterprise skills sessions to 600 vulnerable adolescent girls and young women. The women and adolescent girls in safe spaces will be supported with life skills and enterprise skills including energy-saving stove making, soap baking, beads making, and tailoring. They will be supported with start-up kits as part of livelihood support to enable them to generate income. Pregnant and lactating adolescents will be prioritized for livelihood opportunities. This activity aims to prevent further experience of violence or the need to resort to negative coping mechanisms, such as sexual exploitation, to make a living.• Provide cash assistance to support 150 GBV survivors and their families, particularly adolescent girls including persons with disability and other vulnerable groups. • Facilitate life skills training for young people (600 girls,600 boys) using the Plan International Champion of Change Gender Transformative Modules for young people, session will cover protection and, GBV risks and prevention measures, Menstrual Hygiene Management, and preventing forced marriage.Outcome 2: Community-based safety plans strengthened to address GBV concerns and create protective environment in Rubkona, Leer, Malakal and Renk Counties•Conduct awareness session/sensitization in the community on GBV: Awareness-raising sessions on GBV risks, mitigation measures and response will be conducted with specific community members including key stakeholders: community leaders, religious leaders, gender desk police officers and health personnel to strengthen their knowledge on prevention and response to GBV, including harmful practices and understand their role in mitigating GBV.• Conduct training on GBV basics and referral pathways for community structures (women groups, police, local security: GBV training will be held for existing community structures to increase understanding on GBV and support survivors to access safe and confidential services in the community.• Conduct community dialogues and engagement with men and boys to promote positive masculinity and support prevention of GBV to girls and women• Identification and capacity building to 9 women groups, (180 females) 9 youth groups (80 females, 80 male) to increase understanding of GBV risks, prevention, and support survivors to access safe and comprehensive services through referrals to Organizations providing case management in the area. • Printing and disseminating updated GBV referral pathways in the targeted locations• Conduct safeguarding and PSHEA training for 180 staff, community volunteers and partners including Government representatives in the targeted locations• Support commemorations of 16 days of activism against GBV in targeted communities
