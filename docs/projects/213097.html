Project Summary

Support conflict affected individuals with lifesaving food and livelihood assistance across the governorates of Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor, Idleb, Al-Hasakeh. Response will be prioritized based on the severity of the needs in the target location. Mainly, Mercy Corps will provide:
Multi-purpose cash Assistance (MPCA): whenever cash is feasible, Mercy Corps will prioritize provision of one-off MPCA to newly IDPs and severely food insecure population. MPCA will be based on the survival minimum expenditure basket (SMEB) rates of each location, which includes food assistance. In-kind distribution will be considered only when cash and voucher deemed to be infeasible.
Multi-round food assistance: Eight rounds of food basket assistance will be provided to IDPs and food insecure population covering 70% of the individual monthly dietary needs.. Electronic transfer will be utilized whenever possible. In-kind food will only be used when CVA deemed to be infeasible.
Inputs for food production: Mercy Corps will support vulnerable farmers to purchase quality summer and winter crops inputs from local markets. Each farmers will receive USD500 or equivalent amount in the form of voucher. In-kind distribution of inputs will only be considered when CVA proved inappropriate.
Livestock: Mercy Corps will provide subsidies through vouchers for fodder and animal health consultation services to vulnerable livestock herders in the targeted locations.
Kitchen Garden: Mercy Corps will provide vulnerable households with in-kind kitchen garden kits for improved vegetable and summer crop production.
Target households will be selected based on vulnerability criteria and food security indicators. Additional eligibility criteria will be considered to target farmers and livestock herders.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection risk analysis: Protection risks continue to increase in Syria due to the difficulties households face in their communities daily. Mercy Corps (MC) prioritizes the following in order to mitigate such protection risks: (1) Regularly monitors conflict dynamics in order to identify when conflict may result in population movement and program impact. (2) Where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, MC will support partners in intervention methods & engagement with local actors to increase opportunities to operate. (3) MC will agree on a vulnerability criteria in order to ensure relations between IDPs and host communities are maintained. This will result in assistance directed towards vulnerable community members and households regardless of returnee, IDP, or host community status. Participants are identified in partnership with local communities including IDPs & host communities. Communities & local stakeholders are sensitized on selection criteria. (4) MC has codes of conduct and safeguarding practices for partners and staff. Partners and staff are engaged in PSS and case management exercise in areas where there are limited/no services for onward referral. MC will ensure that both program teams and partners are engaged in the appropriate cluster service mapping exercises. PSEA strategy: Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where MC and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programs. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures: (1) Increased inflation. MC will conduct market assessments in order to monitor for price increases. MC will coordinate with UN clusters/peer agencies and follow modalities agreed upon by clusters. (2) Key local stakeholders do not have the interest and/or capacity to fully engage in the project. MC will conduct a stakeholder mapping at the beginning to identify local stakeholders, who have the capacity and willingness to support interventions designed to promote resilience.
