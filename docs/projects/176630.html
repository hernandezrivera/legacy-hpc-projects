Project Summary

The deterioration of the humanitarian situation in northern Mozambique has led to an estimated 1.3 million people in need of emergency assistance (HRP 2021). The security context deteriorated in early 2020 and worsened by June 2020 as several non-state armed groups (NSAGs) continued to attack both civilian population and government forces. The NSAGs expanded their areas interventions, which resulted in the capture of several district capital cities and intensified the level of violence over the past few months (ACLED data: more than 500 security incidents with around 1,800 fatalities). Of note is that the presence of the Islamic State Central Africa Province (ISCAP) has been acknowledged by the Mozambican authorities on 24 April 2020.
In light of the situation in Cabo Delgado, more than half a million Mozambicans are considered to be internally displaced according the latest DTM Round 7 report from October 2020 (560,000 people according to Government estimates). Amongst them, 51 percent are children under the age of 18 and 27 per cent are women. The majority of them are located in Cabo Delgado and neighboring Niassa and Nampula provinces where nearly 90 percent of IDPs live in host communities amongst family members or acquaintances. The remaining 10 percent are in overcrowded camp-like settings without appropriate living-conditions. Nearly 800,000 host communities have also been directed or indirectly impacted by the crisis, including the displacements.
As a result, key protection risks and incidents, including against children and women, have been reported as displacement further exacerbates the vulnerability level of the affected populations.
Heavy rains and subsequent flooding experienced in early 2020 caused widespread infrastructure damage in Cabo Delgado including the destruction of the main bridge over Montepuez river that connects the northern districts to Pemba. Although some bridges have been repaired, the current, 2021, rainy season has exacerbated access to these locations via routes not yet repaired.
Insecurity has hampered access by road to most of the areas from Nacala or Pemba hubs, between the districts or within the districts themselves. Consequently, the following districts are largely isolated either wholly or partially: Mocimboa da Praia; Palma; Macomia; Muidumbe; eastern part of Meluco; Mueda (periphery villages); Ancuabe (periphery villages) and Matemo most recently. The combination of insecurity, displacement, COVID-19, cholera, and extreme weather presents a significant challenge that requires an immediate review of the logistics arrangements which includes the use of sea and air operations to be able to transport personnel and humanitarian commodities to the affected areas. It is expected that access will be limited to the districts of, Nangade, Palma, and Mocimboa da Praia due to high levels of insecurity. The districts of Mueda and Macomia can potentially be accessed during the rainy season (provided that security conditions allow).
The most northern and coastal districts of Cabo Delgado have been considered hard-to-reach areas and/or partially accessible due to the sharp decline of the security context. This concerns districts such as Palma, Ibo, Nangade, Mocimboa da Praia, Muidumbe, Macomia and Quissanga.
WFP Logistics remains operational across borders and ports for transportation however, logistical challenges continue to arise, particularly as the humanitarian operation expands to reach more people people fleeing the conflict. The project aims to address these challenges by providing logistics information sharing, storage and transport of humanitarian items and passenger movement facilitation.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

In light of the growing insecurity in north Gabo Delgado province, internal displacement has resulted and Nampula and Niassa provinces have also become the hosts of displaced persons fleeing the north. Support to all these provinces will continue to be required until resettlement. Many of the areas where humanitarian needs are most acute are also volatile and this is compounded by infrastructure damage from cyclones and flooding. Consequently, access to transportation is becoming limited and costly. The project will combine different modes of transport to reach the areas where the vulnerable populations are. Humanitarian partners already face major challenges to store critical relief items in Pemba and potentially other areas. The project will establish common spaces which will be shared by the humanitarian actors to ensure safe and cost effective storage of relief items before transport. The project will target over 40 organisations both humanitarian, development as well as diplomatic missions and government departments requiring logistics related support.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The project targets over 40 humanitarian, development organisations as well as government institutions and the diplomatic missions in Mozambique. WFP Mozambique will use its experience and expertise in humanitarian logistics to offer common services to the the organisations, where no other reliable solutions exist. Although these services are not intended to replace the logistics capacities of other organizations nor compete with the commercial market, they will fill crucial identified gaps and provide a last resort option. The Logistics Cluster will facilitate road transport based on humanitarian partners identified needs, from Pemba Logistics hub to final destinations. Many of the areas where humanitarian needs are most acute are also volatile and this is compounded by infrastructure damage from previous cyclones and current rainy season effects. The project will combine different modes of transport to reach the areas where the vulnerable populations with most of the heavy and voluminous transport anticipated to done mainly via road or by sea. The Logistics Cluster will also facilitate the transport of partners’ cargo from Pemba airport and from Pemba port to their warehouse facilities and if requested to Logistics Cluster additional warehouse facilities, where set-up in the affected areas. In addition, the Logistics Cluster will facilitate temporary storage in Pemba airport and in other strategic locations according to ongoing logistics assessments. This will include the implementation of Mobile Storage Units (MSUs) to facilitate temporary storage for National Authorities and partners. A Logistics Cluster coordination and information sharing platform which is already established will be reinforced. The forum encourages information sharing between the logistics sector (cluster, once activated) stakeholders including the government, humanitarian community and diplomatic missions on various areas related to logistics. This mechanism will be enhanced during the lifetime of this project to ensure relevant logistics information is collected and disseminated. This is expected to increase collaboration and efficiency in the delivery of both supplies and humanitarian support to targeted vulnerable populations. Tacit information includes amongst other, international transport options, procedures for donation of goods and services, customs clearance process (exemption of tax), national supply chain options, bottlenecks and potential solutions as well as mapping of the response. The project will also play a key role in enabling humanitarian actors to access certain parts of Cabo Delgado province and deliver life-saving humanitarian items. UNHAS will provide air support to hard-to-reach locations such as Palma, Mueda, Macomia and Ibo districts to transport life-saving cargos, passengers and ICT equipment’s. Additionally, UNHAS will be provide medical and security evacuations as needed. Furthermore, through this intervention, WFP aims at supporting the government and humanitarian actors to provide life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian assistance and protection, prioritizing those most at-risk due to the prevailing insecurity in Cabo Delgado province, the effects of the rainy season and the COVID-19 pandemic. Capacity Strengthening activities of the partners especially the national actors to respond to emergencies will also be conducted.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

WFP constantly evaluates risks and establishes mitigation measures depending on the context. Common logistics services such as transport, warehousing and handling of cargo, will only be provided to humanitarian partners recognized and registered by the host Government and the humanitarian community in Cabo Delgado through the Clusters system once activated. As a standard procedure, WFP uses the Consolidated United Nations Security Council Sanctions List list to check if a humanitarian partner is not rated as or associated with a terrorist organization. Only cleared humanitarian entities will benefit from common logistics services. Furthermore, the requesting organizations’ mandate/operations and activities will also be reviewed. Organizations whose needs are incongruent with the provision of humanitarian assistance, will not benefit from WFP logistics services and UNHAS. UNHAS develops a Risk assessment tool for each airport used and WFP security also works with the UN security cell to develop an SRM (Security Risk Management plan) which is endorsed by the UN Security Management Team which includes risks and mitigation measures. For procurement of goods and services WFP has a rigorous vetting process for identifying vendors and service providers through a vendor review and approval process. As a standard procedure WFP uses the Consolidated United Nations Security Council Sanctions List to check/clear suppliers and service providers and engages in business cleared suppliers and service providers. WFP will continue to work with the partners to ensure prior assessment of areas of operations before supplies and equipment are expedited. Such assessments include the interagency needs assessments led by OCHA and Security assessments undertaken by UNDSS.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

WFP works in tandem with the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) system in the country. Any decisions for response are guided by the system and only areas where the partners have decided for intervention will be considered. The project will capitalize on the common risk communication and community engagement strategy developed by WFP, ICCG and other on-going work on community engagement in the field by UN agencies, international and national NGOs, faith-based groups, the Red Cross and Red Crescent and its National Societies.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Since this project supports 3rd tier beneficiaries (humanitarian organisations, governmental), direct beneficiaries identification will be determined through inter-agency assessments carried out prior.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Logistics performance is inherently linked with environmental degradation through carbon dioxide emissions. The project will attempt to reduce negative emissions by working with transporters using trucks with lower amounts of carbon dioxide emissions as well as use shorter travel routes where applicable.
