Project Summary

Since 2016, the North West and South West regions of Cameroon have witnessed a socio-political crisis which has deteriorated overtime with violent clashes between armed forces and Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG) which have led to loss of lives and property, internal displacement, attacks on education and a growing climate of fear and incertitude. Much of this violence is targeted at local communities and, in particular, at schools, with teachers frequently facing attacks in the form of kidnapping, harassment, decapitation and killing, while children have been exposed to a number of protection risks such as harassment, exploitation, abuse and recruitment and some have been killed due to the crisis. The NSAG have adopted school boycott/disruption strategy since 2017 to apply pressure on government in the form of Monday ‘ghost-towns’ and other spontaneous calls for ‘lockdowns’, especially on commemorated national days. The school boycott strategy has had a severe impact on children’s ability to attend school, learn and retain previously learnt content. Calls for a return to normal functioning of schools from different individual, national and humanitarian actors have been challenged by other separatist groups. In addition to the threats and direct acts of violence leading to school disruptions, other factors such as social media misinformation, false alarms and panic have led to the further disruption of school activities. The ongoing violence has affected some 1.3 million children and left more than 955,700 out of school in the Northwest and Southwest regions. As of October 2022, an estimated 54% of schools are not operational in both NWSW regions (Government Statistics, October 2022). On its part, the reaction by the international community has not been robust enough in terms of condemnation and action to ensure that the right to education is upheld. The Norwegian Refugee Council has referred to the crisis in Cameroon as one of the most neglected in the world. This research seeks to identify and provide an overview of all the converging factors leading to disruption of school activities, outline a timeline of school attacks, and provide an educational needs assessment for children of school going age in the Anglophone regions. It provides a brief background to what is now known as the Anglophone crisis, a timeline on attacks in schools and other factors including, social media misinformation, false alarms and panic, leading to further disruption of school activities. Around 7 million learners are currently affected by schools’ closure, of these an estimated 15% of children live in the NWSW region. In order to ensure education continuity and mitigate the impact of school closing, the Education Cluster has developed Education response plan in line with National response strategy that includes measures to mitigate the impact of school closure. These measures include adapting provision of e-learning, radio education and other alternative learning and well-being interventions. Plan International aligns the HPD strategies with the National response strategy, Education Cluster, the NWSW Education Cluster Response Annex and Plan Global Education COVID 19 Response considerations. Plan on the ground will coordinate and work closely with the WASH and Protection partners including Plan’s WASH, NFI and CPiE thematic areas, to ensure vulnerable girls and boys affected by the conflict and closure of schools and non-formal learning spaces, access education and minimize on the lost time for learning.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Access to education:An estimated 1,033,000 school-aged children have been affected by the crisis and denied the opportunity to learn and develop themselves, failing in achieving Sustainable Development Goal (SDG), specifically, the goal 4: ensure inclusive and equitable quality education and promote lifelong learning opportunities for all. The current system is leaving more than 955,700 children behind. In the 2022-2023 academic year, 884 new schools have opened to make a total of 3,013 operational schools (46%) out of the planned 6,515 in the NWSW regions. A total of 3,502 (54%) schools are still not operational. Secondary data (Education Cluster) revealed that the majority (75%) of schools that were open in 2021, remained operational in 2022, with some exceptions (Ndonga Matung, Menchum, Mezam of NW) where the insecurity is still high. During the same academic year, out of the expected 771,186 students, a total of 418,381 children (54%) responded and actively participated in educational activities in primary and secondary schools in the NWSW regions. In the Northwest region, 248,535 or 58% of the expected 177,186 students have been enrolled. The influx of children was most noticeable in basic education with 80% of students present and only 32% in secondary school. As for the Southwest region, only 49% of the expected 345,039 students, or 169,846, showed up. Contrary to the situation in the Northwest, the secondary school was the one that registered more students with 56,408 (52%) compared to the number of students expected (109,286 students). Of the 235,753 students expected to attend elementary school, 48% participated in educational activities.The situation of teachers is reportedly only slightly improved since 2020/21 school year when in NW out of 7,548 expected teachers in the primary section, 41% of teachers were present in functional schools while out of 12,054 expected teachers in the secondary 53% of teachers were reported. In the SW, the situation was more encouraging as 77% out of the expected number was reported in primary school and 56% of teachers were reported for the secondary level. At the onset of the 2022/23 school year in the NW out of 9,846 expected primary school teachers, only 41% have resumed work while out of 9,230 teachers, 44% resumed work in secondary. In the SW, there is a great improvement regarding the primary school teachers as out of 8,035 expected teachers, 84% have resumed. However, in secondary schools 73% teachers (of 9,625 expected) did not resume work.Lifesaving Activities.Ongoing Conflict. The main driver of lifesaving needs is the ongoing conflict, in the NWSW, since late 2016. It has entered its sixth year (2016-2022). It has triggered humanitarian needs across the two regions, linked to significant internal displacement.Sustained Displacement. Coupled with the conflict, the displacement continues. 368,742 displaced population of which 147,984 are estimated displaced school aged children in the NWSW regions, as of Aug 2022. The sustained displacement continues creating urgent and immediate needs including life-saving education in emergencies needs for those children.Returnees. Another urgent and immediate need is returnee school-aged children. In the NWSW, during the period of Jan-Aug 2021, an estimated 142, 544 returnee school aged children have been recorded.Protection NeedsUnhealed Trauma. Of the issues associated with the ongoing conflict, psychological trauma occupies 18.3% among the assessed villages by MSNA 2019 in the NWSW regions of the country. Summary of priority educational needs: Access to education, Identification documents, Lack of educational infrastructures and learning material, educational actors working with little/no pay, child protection and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) risks, lack of educational and life skills related to learning and wellbeing (MHPSS), DRR and MHM, poverty, no school feeding, health education and safe access in learning spaces etc.
