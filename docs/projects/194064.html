Project Summary

As highlighted in the country’s National Strategic Frameworks (NSFs) and Multi-Year Work Plans (MYWPs) for Victim Assistance (2020 – 2025), in the communities including Darfur (DAR), existing assistance of prostheses provision is extremely limited in size and opportunities for economic reintegration are far out of reach for majority of Explosive Ordnance (EO) victims. In addition to the number of EO victims, their living conditions, physical and psychological wellbeing, or needs for particular assistance are hardly known. Against this backdrop, Victim Assistance (VA) component of this project will aim to expand the coverage and improve the sustainability of physical rehabilitation services. Likewise, it will aim to strengthen economic security and independence of Explosive Ordnance (EO) victims to re-establish themselves in their communities. Provision of victim assistance in Central North ,West and South Darfur state and its remote areas will be done through the activities: The EO victims will be supported through strengthening and expanding the existing local capacities to coordinate physical, psycho-social, and economic rehabilitation and reintegration of mine/ERW survivors. Victim assistance will be enhanced in other MA activities. A swift referral pathway will be promoted and equal access to government institutions including medical care, rehabilitation, psychosocial support, education, social protection, and livelihood support. VA activities will include 1) Need assessment and medical screening 2) Provision of prostheses, orthoses and assistive devices, 3) Psychological and psychosocial support, 4) Microeconomic initiatives (ME), 5) advocacy for the rights of persons with disabilities (PWDs) and awareness raising, and 6) Data collection and 7) Establishment and Capacity enhancement and involvement of the Explosive Ordnance Victim Association (EOVA) To conduct these activities and considering the International and National Mine Action Standards (IMAS) and (SNMAS) and NADA’s approved Standards Operating Procedures (SOPs) and it previous experiences.

1 - Needs

Sudan undertaken radical political change which resulted in signing Juba Peace Agreement (JPA), other developments, adoption of the national plan for protection of civilians, establishment of national and state level committees for the implementation of the national plan, recent military coup etc. Long lasting internal conflicts in Darfur have resulted in people displacement and death. 1,229,971 are affected by contamination of Explosive Remnants of War (ERW) including Explosive Ordnance (EO it led to severe restrictions on emergency and developmental aid. It as well as deaths and injury of community members. Majority of livelihood activities in Darfur, are either farmers or nomads, especially in targeted remote areas, are either farmers or nomads. The fear of present of EO and unexploded ordnance (UXOs) has also impacted agricultural communities and farmers who are afraid to use their land; nomads and cattle headers cannot use pastures to graze their cattle in some areas. In Darfur women and children have additional social roles such as brining water and fire wood from far distances, this explore them to higher risks of being injured or killed due to the present of EO and/ or UXOs. Women and girls some time reach to 5- 9 Km away from their residence searching for fire wood. In Darfur, according to the Information Management System of Mine Action (IMSMA) which is managed by NMAC, EO contamination resulted in the casualty of 655 individuals in Darfur . Limited or restricted movement due to the present of EO will significantly impacted the overall national economy and hinder the improve of developmental programs and as such affected Sudan from achieving sustainable development goals.
