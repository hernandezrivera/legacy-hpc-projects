Project Summary

Evidence of Shelter, NFI and WASH needs highlighted through rapid, multi-sectorial and technical assessments conducted by DRC in all of these areas indicate that a lack of shelter and poor settlement is creating overcrowding and extremely poor shelter conditions leading to health and protection risks. This includes skin disease, respiratory infections, increased risk of eviction, increased risks from climatic and environmental impacts, and negative coping mechanisms. Coping mechanisms when there is a lack of NFIs include sharing items with other families, which impacts a broader number but may also open-up specific groups, families or individuals to exploitation or other protection risks. On average, a family of 4-6 persons has around 40 litres of water storage, equating to at worst 6 litres per person per day, compared to the Sphere minimum daily standard of 15 litres/person/day. 67% of households assessed spend between 15- and 60-minutes queuing for water, although in areas of recent, large influxes throughout 2017 households have spent several hours queuing due to over-stretching of scant resources. 30% of households still rely on unprotected water sources such as traditional wells or bodies of water, while 70% of persons interviewed state they practice open defecation. With cholera being endemic in Nigeria during rainy season, the risks of high morbidity and mortality associated with unsanitary and overcrowded living conditions cannot be over-emphasised.
