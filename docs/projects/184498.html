Project Summary

The Somali population continues to face combined impact of recurring stress factors including drought, desert locusts, political uncertainty, COVID-19 restrictions, and an upsurge in conflict which have deepened and widened poverty conditions in Somalia, compounded pre-existing vulnerabilities. The secondary economic and humanitarian consequences of COVID-19 in 2020-2021 are estimated to have been more significant than the health consequences of COVID-19. The vulnerability exposure of the population has increased due to these multiple stress factors with seven in ten Somalis currently living in poverty. The number of people in need of humanitarian assistance has increased to 7.7million and 2.9million persons are internally displaced across the country.
Somalia’s health system is inadequately equipped to provide a minimum amount of coverage for equitable access to health care, resulting in increased morbidity and mortality. Somalia is among the highest rankings in infant and child mortality rates with respectively 76.6 and 121.5 per 1000 live births. The maternal mortality rate (692 deaths per 100,000 live births) and the fertility rate (average of 6.9 children per woman) are also among the highest in the world, while 79% of all births are home delivered without skilled assistance. Access to birth spacing services remains limited, and up to 99 percent of women experience Female Genital Mutilation/Cutting (FGM/C) (SHDS, 2020). Displaced persons and persons with disability reported particularly high access barriers to food, nutrition, health, water, protection, sanitation, and hygiene services within IDP sites. Reports have shown that the low level and difficult access to health services in the IDP sites in Baidoa and Kismayo tends to particularly disadvantage women and their ability to access maternal healthcare and personal hygiene Also the COVID-19 epidemic is also exacerbating gender-based violence (GBV). COVID-19-related restrictions led to the termination of several GBV services. Women, adolescents, girls, and children account for 95 per cent of survivors who reported GBV occurrences in 2020, with 75 per cent coming from displaced populations.
In this regards, UNFPA interventions will ensure access to life-saving maternal and reproductive health information and services for the affected women and girls as well as men and boys in IDPs and in hard-reach locations including persons with disabilities. The project will contribute in the prevention and management of consequences of sexual and gender based violence such as unintended pregnancies, increased acquisition of sexually transmitted infections (STIs) and increased transmission of HIV through compassionate, confidential and competent care to the survivors among displaced and host communities in the crisis affected population. The following are the specific objectives of the intervention:
• Ensure access and utilization of quality and age appropriate emergency obstetric and newborn care services and information to IDPs and host communities including persons with disabilities in the affected locations.
• improve availability of a range of long-acting reversible and short-acting contraceptive methods in the EmONC facilities of the affected areas
• Support the capacity of service providers on mental health and psychosocial support (MPHSS) for pregnant and lactating mothers and adolescent girls affected by the humanitarian crises in Somalia.
• Enhance the capacity of managers and service providers for sexual and reproductive health in Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) and clinical management of rape (CMR)
• Support the procurement and the distribution of Emergency Reproductive Health Kits (ERH Kits) to deliver quality health services.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project will endeavor to ensure maximum consultation with marginalized communities in all phases of the program with a particular focus on including vulnerable women and girls from minority clans and those living with disabilities. The project will ensure inclusion of marginalized individuals in community-based structures to ensure their priorities are tailored in to the program. UNFPA partners will closely work with community committees including GBV survivor advocates, youth organizations, organizations of persons living with disabilities to select beneficiaries for the project. Also UNFPA and its implementing partners will ensure consistent sex disaggregated programming to ensure men, women, girls, and boys are targeted adequately.
