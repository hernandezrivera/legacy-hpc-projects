Project Summary

This Shelter and NFI project will target most vulnerable households from IDPS, returnees and host communities in the targeted project location. The project will be implemented and integrated where possible with the relevant clusters such as WASH, CCM and protection cluster in order to achieve maximum benefit to people affected by conflict, natural disasters and derive of basic services. Utilising approved standard tools and policies, all activities will conducted to minimize or reduce exposure of affected communities to no HARM.
By providing training to CCM team, WASH and other cluster partners in implementing this project, CH will leverage on its experience and others to deliver wholistic response to people in need across the targeted locations in statics and mobile response as coordinated with the cluster. Most of the project activities are implemented in remote and hard to reach areas where there is proposal for establishment of humanitarian hub, in that connection CH will utilize the available resources to efficiently deliver the project activities.
In order to improve delivering of the cash based modality in this project, Coalition for Humanity will conduct Rapid market assessments and monthly price monitoring to determine appropriate and specific CVA modality. CH will undertake due diligence and make referrals to mitigate cases, and support either as standalone or integrated training on HLP access rights for returnees, IDPs and host community mostly women in collaboration with relevant authorities. Beneficiaries will be support and pointed toward direction and mechanisms around dispute resolution, legal assistance, land right among other issues.
With involvement of other cooperating partner such as IOM,NRC,UNHCR and relevant government authorities at state and national level, beneficiaries will be provided with general information about land rights, ownership and provide clarity on tenure arrangements.
A total case load of 150,000 beneficiaries will be reached through this intervention. The figures are broken down to 45,000 women, men 37,500, girls 37,500 and 30,000 boys across the targeted locations.
The project will provide IDPS, host communities and returnees with shelters and NFI both for new displacement as well as old to refurbish existing structures to meet basic dignified shelter needs.
The project will enhance market functionality by using mixed intervention of cash and in-kind support to people most in needs where there are basic market structures and functionality.
This shelter and NFI project is designed in line with the HRP 2021, humanitarian need overview (HNO) that shows serious gaps. Additionally, the IPC findings contained in the first projection period of December 2020 to March 2021, put an estimated total of 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. During this period, an estimated 1.79 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity.
In order to fight COVID19,CH will integrate awareness messages on prevent and emergency to help protect beneficiaries and staff implementing activities.
The counties targeted for this project are among the 21 and 46 counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and Crisis (IPC Phase 3). The finding further explained that the food insecurity levels will remain elevated due to insecurity, the effects of COVID-19, persistent poor macroeconomic conditions, and the impact of flooding on livelihoods. These finds have direct effects on huge population in need of shelters and non-food items.

1 - Needs

''According to the findings of an assessment on people’s priority needs conducted in 2020, shelter and NFI was identified as the priority need by 14 per cent of women assessed settlements in the country, after food assistance, livelihoods and health. For men in assessed settlements, shelter and NFI was identified as the priority need by 12 per cent and for children, by only 4 per cent''. From this background and need analysis South Sudan generally remains fragile and faced with myriads of natural disasters, unprecedented levels of communal violence, political conflicts and diseases. The last quarter of 2020 witnessed some of the most devastating levels of flooding across many parts of the country and particularly the counties targeted by this project. According to Humanitarian needs overview analyses conducted by UNOCHA for South Sudan, it is estimated that the need of emergency shelter and non-food items will affect the living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 2.1 million people in 2021.This is an increased from 2020 where an estimated 2 million were in need of shelter and non-food items. The IPC findings contained in the first projection period of December 2020 to March 2021, put an estimated total of 5.82 million people (48.3% of the population) in crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. During this period, an estimated 1.79 million people are likely to face Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The counties targeted for this project are among the 21 and 46 counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and Crisis (IPC Phase 3). The findings further explained that the food insecurity levels will remain elevated due to insecurity, the effects of COVID-19, persistent poor macroeconomic conditions, and the impact of flooding on livelihoods. These findings have direct effects on huge population in need of shelter and non-food items across different locations in South Sudan. The locations targeted by this project have limited or no access to local materials, markets connectivity and the poor economic status of most households mostly, women and children is projected to worsen the situation across the country and in particular areas targeted for this project. The protection of civilians sites (POCs) have transitioned to IDPs and it is expected most people will likely wish to return to their homesteads if the current peace implementation continue. This will put huge shelter and NFI need and burden to these groups of returnees, IDPS and host communities which is estimated at more than 50% of the shelter needs. There has been new displacement caused by internal conflicts, cycle of communal violence, cattle raiding and natural disasters such as flood that have caused wave of displacement with high need for shelter and NFIs to many households in the targeted locations for this project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Coalition for Humanity has Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policy in place and all staff members , consultants and volunteers sign it as first step in engaging them. Our protection focal persons in the projects mostly female staff organize as standalone sessions on PSEA and where possible incorporate it in meetings and trainings before and during the project activities. This means the dissemination of PSEA is not one time off but an ongoing process and constitute an important of aspect of our project implementation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Local staff are leading the process of collecting the feedback from the beneficiaries especially individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are illiterate, women and girls. Most of the meetings and consultations are delivered in local languages hence helping members to effectively contribute. We also encourage beneficiaries who have skills in different languages(Nuer, Dinka, Arabic) to provide it as they know it and if need be get our translators to interpret for us the content they shared. This help not to leave anyone out.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the start of the project activities, a number of inception meetings will be carried out. These consultative and engagement meetings with beneficiaries will help further explain the nature of support to the beneficiaries and obtain their feedback on how such support will be delivered to them. The inceptions and other consultative meetings will build on earlier engagements (pre project design engagements) done with members of the community while this proposal was being prepared. Number of important tasks to do will include setting up complaint mechanisms and referral channels during and after the project delivery. Effective community mobilization meetings and Focus group discussions, safety audits with different groups mostly women and girls will be done to seek their views on safety of distribution points.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback gathered from the beneficiaries during inceptions meetings, monthly complaints feedback boxes place in strategic locations are collected, analyzed and the feedback used to improve and adapt the project implementation. An action on feedback from the beneficiaries is incorporated into the project implementation matrix.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

1. Project prioritize PSEA dissemination and sharing of information to ensure that women and girls are not exposed to social violence during the distribution or registration. This risk is mitigated through series of inception meetings, community engagement with leaders and safety audits to map out areas of potential threats during distribution and to enlist the support of leaders during registration and distribution. All registration/distributions will be done during day allowing women and girls time to reach home before dark.2.During registrations and distributions, marginalized groups, PWD, women, girls and other special groups are provided with special attention by giving specific percentage to such groups. A different queues or door to door registration and delivery of items is done. Where there is caretaker, such person is entrusted with such responsibility.3. All registration/distributions points are provided with feedback and complaints boxes that all members can see and provide their feedback anonymously. Beneficiaries are informed of the importance of feedback during all Focus group discussions, Key informant interviews and inception meetings.4. Community leaders and other members of the communities are engaged in good time to avoid any dispute over land tenure and especially IDPS and returnees access with focus on women headed households and girls.5. In every step of registration and distribution of SNFI, mitigation measures will be taken to ensure minimization of the risk and that the construction of shelter do not leads to or escalates land related disputes and/or even outright conflicts

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Access to dignified shelter and non food items in most the project locations will help households have access to safe food and water storage. These will help in nutritional uptake mostly for vulnerable children, PWD, Pregnant and lactating members and other vulnerable members of the households.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will support households most affected within IDPs, host and returnees with basic SNFI that will complement the equitable access of cross sectoral basic services to women, children, elderly people and persons with disability.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Lack of cooking sets, sleeping mats and water storage facilities have devastating effects on children under 5 and other vulnerable members of households. Food preparation and how it is served play an important part in nutrient content preservation. By providing this SNFI support children and other members of households will benefit improving their nutrition uptake and hence reduce global acute malnutrition.
