Project Summary

Across Syria, over 6.2 million persons are expected to be in need of NFI assistance in 2024, thus a 8 per cent increase since 2023, as per the (unreleased) 2024 HNO. Due to limited access to livelihoods, camp residents, IDPs and conflict-affected people are highly dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs, particularly with regards to non-food items (NFI) and winterization, as limited purchasing powers means households must prioritize food and water at the expenses of NFI. With the security environment remaining somewhat volatile, deterioration in economic conditions, compounded by a depreciating Syrian pound, has led to further intensification of the severity of need for NFI support. As formal camps face critical capacity issues, newly displaced households turn toward informal settlements, where access to NFI items is the most limited. This is compounded by continued displacement trends, particularly across NES. This highlights needs for in-camp NFI support. The proposed project aims at addressing emergency and more sustainable NFI needs of vulnerable households with acute needs (both IDPs and host community members) in Northeast Syria. Acted’s intervention will focus on providing seasonal NFI items as well as heaters to 59,558 conflict-affected IDPs and camp residents across Northeast Syria to better equip them for winter. In NES, Acted plans to cover the fuel needs of all the households residing in Areesheh and Serekaniye camps, in Al-Hasake governorate through the provision of heating fuel and diesel heaters. In addition, Acted will preposition itself to cover the winterization needs of the sites targeted with the provision of mobile CCCM services in the region, should emergency needs arise throughout the project. All winterization support will be complemented with fire safety and awareness sessions. Acted will take into account the different risks and concerns affecting women, men, girls and boys and people with disabilities when designing this intervention. With regards to the latter, Acted will ensure that the distributions are conducted respectfully of the needs of the most vulnerable, with specific distribution lines dedicated to women and elderlies. Acted will also conduct the distributions directly at the HH level when needed. All Acted staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Information on the Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) will be communicated to camp manager, community leaders, camp residents. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints (WhatsApp group, letterbox, desks). Acted staff are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by Acted SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of Acted responsibility as member of the network.
