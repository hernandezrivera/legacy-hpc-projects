Project Summary

This project is informed by the IRC 2020 Cameroon strategy that seeks to achieve evidence driven, gender transformative outcomes across safety and economic wellbeing outcomes. This project will contribute to IRC’s safety outcome are a defined as ‘people are safe in their homes and communities and receive support when they experience harm’. It is guided by the Women Protection and Empowerment (WPE)Theory of Change (ToC) and evidence based, field tested program approaches. Women and girls protection and empowerment programming aims to ensure “Women and girls pursue their potential, free from violence and inequality” across all outcomes and sectors. Women and girls have inherent strength and power that they use to transform the world around them in spite of being born with unequal life chances and the constant threat of GBV. IRC’s WPE programming is informed by evidence that violence against women and girls (VAWG)is systemic and is used to maintain the subordinate status of women and girls. VAWGs is a primary obstacle to women and girls pursuing their potential as equals. The proposed intervention will contribute to expanding the space for women and girls to transform their lives while engaging men through accountable practices. EMAP is not intended to be a standalone intervention. To ensure that survivors have access to care, this project will be complemented byGBV response services provided by the IRC.Information on how to access GBV response services will be shared with participants in EMAP sessions of women's and men's groups

Besoins / Needs

The following project contributes to the reduction of gender-based violence (GBV) among women and girls from displaced and host communities affected by the crisis in the Department of Mayo Sava (Mora and Meme localities) of the Far North region of Cameroon, through the Preventing Violence Against Women and Men: Engaging Men through Accountable Practices(EMAP) approach. EMAP is a 10-month primary prevention intervention informed by women’s voices and priorities, for engaging men in transformative individual behaviour change and contains three programming components: a three-week training of trainers, an eight-session women’s curriculum, and a 16-session men’s curriculum. Within this project, 350 persons (175 women and 175men) will be directly targeted through different activities. At least 1,000 girls, boys, women and men will benefit in directly from this project through changes at the household level and social change created in the wider community by women and men graduates of EMAP who will role model and champion gender equality and nonviolence at the community level. The project will be implemented in Mora and Meme-Igawa in the Department of Mayo Sava and will have an implementation period of 10 months.
