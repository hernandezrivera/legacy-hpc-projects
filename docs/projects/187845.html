Project Summary

Syria is one of the world’s most complex humanitarian emergencies laded with ongoing hostilities, triggering one of the worst displacement crises in modern history, resulting in widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure, including health and agricultural facilities leading to food, nutrition and related insecurity.
According to HNO there are 12.4 Million people that are food insecure, out of which there are 1.3 Million which are severely food insecure.
The proposed action seeks to protect and enhance food security and livelihoods of conflict‑affected highly vulnerable IDPs and host communities in North West Syria (NWS) (Aleppo). This will be achieved through an integrated programme of assistance including repeated mixed‑modality life‑sustaining food assistance (monthly assistance to the most vulnerable households) and emergency lifesaving food security assistance in addition to bakery and livelihoods support. The complementary package of livelihoods support will increase household resilience through the promotion of entrepreneurship and the development of small businesses along with the improvement of agricultural production and increasing the ability of targeted households to meet food needs. The bakery intervention will assist bakeries in filling the gaps in supply chain for flour and yeast. Further the bakeries intervention will also assist un-employed youth is setting up small bakery outlets to support income and will contribute to developing entrepreneurial skills.
Agriculture Response: (1750 HHs.) – We have already proposed an action in consultation with BHA to addressed the needs of 100 Farmers to be provided assistance.
Food Response: (4500 HHs.) – We have already proposed an action in consultation with BHA to addressed the needs of 2500 Farmers to be provided assistance. Funding has already been secured through SCHF for 922 HHs.
Bakery Response: (2 Bakeries) – We have already proposed an action in consultation with BHA to addressed the needs of 2 Bakeries to be provided assistance. Funding has already been secured through private donations to support 1 bakery.
Livelihood Response: (100 Youth) – We have already proposed an action in consultation with BHA to addressed the needs of 100 youth to involve in FSL related livelihood by establishing out lets for 2 supported bakeries.

Modality – The planned activities will have a repeated support for a period 10 months through our food kits, food vouchers, micro grants and direct contracting.
Data Collection and Monitoring - The monitoring will be through our well defined M&E systems. Monitoring will be diligently executed starting from Verification, Distribution Monitoring, Post Distribution Monitoring and Satisfaction Surveys. The data will be collected online, primarily using tablets and Kobo tools. We deploy several tools to include Excel and SPSS to analyze and report. A robust system is in place for complaints receipt and redressal, with dedicated teams available for handling and resolving complaints. A robust system of lodging complaints is already in place through communications tools such as WhatsApp, SMS and email. Further the complainant can also lodge a complaint via hard copy and drop it in the drop box placed at our office locations.
Reporting – We will ensure prompt reporting on all of our projects through regular filing of donor as well as 4W reports and ad hoc reporting requirements.
CVA Support Systems – A fully automated and online Cash Voucher System developed internally and utilized for last 6 years will be deployed. The system is flexible to use paper vouchers or cards and can be of single or multiple use. To complement our voucher programs, we have framework agreements with Fund Service Providers that provide cash distributions through formal arrangements. Due to this system, our response time is short.

4 - Needs

The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for Syria has been finalized. The HNO was informed by several assessments, including the Food Security and Livelihood assessments in Northwest Syria. As the Syrian crisis marks over 10 years, the situation in Syria continues to deteriorate drastically with an estimated 14.2 million people in need of food and agriculture assistance, compared to 9.8 million as reported in the previous years (2020). In Northwest Syria, the HNO indicates: • The number of people in need (PIN) in NW Syria (Aleppo) is up to 2.7 million people, of which 1.595 M people are in acute food insecurity and in need of food assistance. • The agriculture sector has been severely impacted, with the loss of livelihood and income for many households. Currently, about 0.18K people are at risk of food insecurity in NW Syria.• The bakery sector is severely impacted increasing bread prices due to depreciating Syrian Pound as well as shortage of wheat. As part of the FSL priorities, further support to life-saving food assistance is required to meet the food gaps of the most food insecure people in NW Syria particularly IDPs, through in-kind food and cash-based transfer, along with technical assistance to micro gardens and small farm holdings chain to restore crops production by local farmers and bakeries’ production capacity.

5 - Implementation of activities

Direct funding from BHA is sought to respond to critical FS gaps in Suran, Al-Bab and Mare sub-districts through the provision of 10 rounds of repeat, in-kind FS assistance targeting 3,500 food insecure IDP and host HHs. Subject to funding, proposed action is anticipated to start in October 2022 with 2 of total 10 rounds of repeat assistance during 2022 and 8 rounds delivered in 2023. The proposed action will compliment with in-kind RTERs, a 1-month food ration to meet emergency needs of a further 1,000 HHs newly affected by conflict. In addition, and to maintain current levels of assistance, funding will be sought to provide 6 rounds of repeat assistance to 2,500 food insecure host and IDP HHs in Suran, Al-Bab, Mare and Ar-Rae sub-district from June through to December 2022, with total 8 rounds repeat assistance anticipated to be delivered during 2022. A mixed-modality approach is proposed, with an estimated 500 HHs receiving cash-based food voucher assistance (average $60 per HH per month) and the remaining 3,500 HHs receiving in-kind family food rationsFunding is sought to restore and protect agricultural livelihoods and build household resilience for 350 HHs for Micro Gardens.Funding is sought to support 2 bakeries servicing 46,000 HHs. 136 of flour and yeast plus minor renovation.Microgrants will be provided to 100 youth to set up bakery outlets. This will enhance the outreach of bakeries.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. Moreover, by applying lessons learned from previous interventions, we will engage with local councils, relief committees and other key actors to mitigate risks faced by women and children, in particular, and to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights. To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable populations in the face of conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries in Syria will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cycle wherever possible. Accessibility measures will be built into assistance delivery, including the option for home delivery to beneficiaries unable to safely access distribution points due to mobility or other restrictions. To mitigate against protection risks, we work closely with LCs and other administrative bodies to ensure open and accountable feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries served. Refresher training on NGO PSEA and Child Protection policies were delivered during 2020 to ensure all staff understand how to identify and correctly report any SEA or CP concerns


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Syria faces the most complex humanitarian emergencies laded with ongoing hostilities which resulted in massive numbers requiring assistance. With increased deteriorating situation, the coping mechanisms are failing leading to serious protection issues. This is further complexed by change of roles where females are heading the households, a role traditionally held by the male population. These insecurities comes with protection issues, particularly GBV and child labor. Coupled with increased hostilities and COVID, resulted in negative coping mechanism such as early marriages and child labor. A safe estimates informs that 42% of the affected population faces one or more protection issues. No significant risks were found during implementation of current programming. To counter any risks, we have designed and put in place a strong Protection mechanism, where protection issues can be reported and complaints handled speedily both at field and HQ level. Our staff is continuously trained in PSEA. Variety of mediums have been deployed for complaints including telephone, email and physical receipts. To counter risks, a strong awareness campaigns are designed along with a robust Complaint Redressal Mechanism to handle complaints. A strong team is in place with appropriate trainings and refreshers to handle complaints. All complaints will be duly reported to the funding agencies.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

We monitor risks real‑time and will continue to follow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary. The following potential risks exists and appropriate measures are applied to program design: Risk 1 Authorities: LCs and local authorities (including AFAD) remain supportive of the NGOs activities and continue to cooperate with established Standard Operating Procedures designed to ensure targeted and transparent delivery of assistance on the basis of need alone. We continually meet the local authorities to ensure optimal cooperation. Risk 2 Verifications: 100% house-to-house beneficiary verification reduces the risk of inclusion or exclusion criteria while close coordination with other FSL actors reduces the risk of duplicationRisk 3 COVID 19: Established guidelines for COVID-19 prevention measures for all field operations continue to mitigate against the risk of transmission Risk 4 Security: Security is continuously monitored and context in targeted areas allows ongoing access by NGO staff. Risk 5 Protection: Protection risk will be identified earlier and mitigated through established systems. We have an established Risk Advisory Department managed by expatriate staff. We are continually update our contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in Syria, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Risk Mitigation is updated on a monthly basis.
