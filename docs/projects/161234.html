Project Summary

Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) are likely to continue living in with host communities, facing the direst living conditions without an alternative coping mechanism. Also, given the long rainy season, the limited scope to earn a livelihood, the lack of access to markets and the often-protracted nature of displacement, need for provision of basic and domestic items remain a priority. Families are often displaced without substantial household items. Therefore, stock needs to be replenished on a regular basis so that UNHCR can respond quickly to the identified vulnerable persons as part of the protection responses. Through the provision of core relief items, UNHCR’s material assistance will continue to be targeted and protection focused, aiming that the most vulnerable displaced households are not left without basic material support and specific needs of woman and girl are catered for.
In 2020, the UNHCR will continue to identify the most vulnerable IDPs (up to 30% of the priority target) through outreach and implement targeted interventions with selected Core Relief Items (CRIs), aimed at reducing the burden to communities and improve the protection situation of the most destitute. Priority will be given to stronger advocacy and field-based outreach, timely life-saving activities, streamlining prevention-oriented initiatives, evidence-based planning, and strengthening community-based protection for capacity development of affected communities to cope, recover, contribute to and achieve solutions. In efforts to minimize the risk of SGBV, prevention/response activities will continue with a focus on community engagement, strengthening referrals and prevention with awareness raising.
There is an increasing trend and wiliness of IDPs to return to their homes or habitual residence. UNHCR projects that there will be up to 1.3 million IDPs in the country by 2020, a decrease as compared to 1.87 million IDPs in 2019 with fluctuations throughout the year. It is anticipated that more development-oriented programming will be implemented in South Sudan considering R-ARCSS and 2019-2020 UN Cooperation Framework, as well as 2018-2021 National Development Strategy for South Sudan. In order to increase the absorption capacity of receiving communities due to anticipated increased number of IDP returnees, UNHCR will focus more on the support at the community level, maintaining the focus on targeted assistance at the individual level to persons with specific needs (PSNs). In 2020, UNHCR aims to reach 50% of the projected IDP population equal to 732,771 persons with targeted assistance to 219,831 persons. The returning IDPs and refugee returnees often face the additional challenge of finding their former housing damaged, destroyed and/or occupied by other households. The need for decent shelter in areas of potential return are critical to realizing UNHCR’s solutions oriented IDP strategy. UNHCR will assess the HLP and conflict sensitive issues with focus on protection response to support the most vulnerable IDP and returnee families to reduce the protection risks and associated vulnerabilities.

9.15 - If you have not selected any of the items 9.1 - 9.13, please explain why here.

UNHCR and its implementing partners ensures that the beneficiaries are well informed of the project activities through mainstream AAP. Community participation is ensured through regular consultations, meetings interagency participatory assessments and meeting with one on one meeting with PSNs.IDPs are consulted through interagency participatory assessment (PA) exercises conducted by UNHCR, its implementing and operational partners countrywide. The PA process aims to gather information on specific protection risks faced by IDPs, analyse risks jointly with them, understand their capacities, and to hear their proposed solutions through structured dialogue. The principles of age, gender and diversity, a rights-based, and community-based approach guided the process. PA ensured that IDPs are put at the centre of the decision-making concerning their protection and welfare as well as ensured accountability to affected populations. Qualitative data collection was done through FGDs and feedback sessions were held with communities to present findings and recommendations.
