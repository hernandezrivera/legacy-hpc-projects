Project Summary

Through access to quality health and nutrition care, ALIMA aims to reduce morbidity and mortality among vulnerable populations (displaced and host populations) in crisis-affected communities in Borno (Jere and Maiduguri LGAs) and Yobe (Karasuwa, Yusufari, Nguru, and Bade LGAs) States. ALIMA will collaborate with the minister of health and community structures to ensure sustainability and ownership through the community engagement approach. Our activities will center on primary and secondary health care with a focus on CU5, nutrition, SRH, MHPSS, and emergency response.

1 - Needs

Poor food consumption patterns in terms of diversity, high prevalence of child morbidities (fever/malaria, diarrhea), poor health seeking behavior, and poor hygiene practices are all contributing to the acute malnutrition of nearly 4.4 million children aged 0 - 59 months in northwest and northeast Nigeria between May 2023 and April 2024, according to the 2023 IPC analysis. 1.04 million instances of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 3.37 million cases of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) are included. GAM prevalence grew from 15.5% in 2022 to 17.2% in 2023 in MMC / Jere LGAs (Borno State), whereas SAM prevalence declined from 3.4% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2023. However, due to population growth, the SAM and MAM caseloads have also increased (from 34,342 children to 36,678 children for SAM cases and from 61,108 children to 113,089 children for MAM cases). As a result, Maiduguri/Jere is a hotspot for acute malnutrition, and financing for the treatment program remains a top need in 2024. Furthermore, the management of SAM cases with difficulties remains difficult due to limited infrastructure and a lack of beds to meet the needs of children suffering from SAM with complications during the Peak months. ALIMA opened 120 emergency beds with two temporary ITFCs during the peak time in 2022. In 2023, ALIMA formed a partnership with Maryam Abacha Hospital to move the two temporary ITFCs into a permanent MoH facility with a 100 bed capacity. Maintaining this inpatient capacity during peak periods will be important in 2024 but challenging because of the lack of resources. Yobe state, on the other hand, is still afflicted by the conflict as well as floods, food insecurity and high prevalence of malnutrition. According to the October 2023 DTM report, Yobe State has 151,366 IDPs, with 88% residing in host community settings. Yobe State has been subjected to recurring outbreaks such as cholera and diphtheria. According to the IPC analysis, Bade and Karasuwa were in IPC AMN Phase 2 (Alert) whereas Nguru and Yusfusari were in IPC AMN Phase 3 (Serious). Furthermore, according to the nutrition sector report for October-November 2023, the number of SAM children admitted into treatment programs between January and September 2023 increased by 47% compared to the same periods in 2022. Clearly defining the gravity of the problem that requires immediate response.

2 - Activities

Under the health sector; the intervention aims to respond to the identified needs with the result of ensuring access to free and quality health care to displaced populations and host communities in Borno and Yobe states (Jere, Maiduguri, Bade , Yusufari and Karasuwa LGAs.) As part of this project, ALIMA proposes to act in these 6 activities under the health sector:- 1. Emergency primary health care including OPD and references 2. Support to emergency secondary healthcare for paediatric cases for Children under 5 (CU5) 3. SAM management including screening and treatment 4. SRH care including Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMoNC) and management of sexual assault survivor (GBV) 5. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) 6. Rehabilitation and maintenance of WaSH infrastructure in health facilities And under the nutrition sector, ALIMA will 4 major activities that cut across all five objectives but mainly aiming at improving access to quality curative nutrition services through the early identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished cases in collaboration with the health sector to enhance sustainability. These activities will include: 1. Management and referral of complicated cases to ITFC are ensured in Borno, and Yobe States. 2. Ambulatory management of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) is ensured in health facilities in Borno, Yobe, and Katsina States. 3. Mothers and caretakers are trained on screened malnutrition by the MUAC and edema in Borno and Yobe States. 4.Mothers and caretakers receive IYCF training sessions in Borno and Yobe States. ALIMA will strive to reduce malnutrition in children aged 6-12 months in Nguru and Karaswa by supplementing them with SQ-LNS for twelve months. Along with the previously listed activities. ALIMA will integrate WASH and environmental conservation, which includes waste management, sanitation, and hygiene promotion, while implementing the above actions.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The projects have a feedback and complaints handling mechanism that allows the local population to provide feedback. In addition, an e-mail address is made available and communicated to all project staff. These feedback and complaint mechanisms are inclusive. Feedback and complaints are stored in a secure database. In terms of Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse ALIMA is committed to prevent any sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers. ALIMA's staff recognizes the problem related to the imbalance of power in the humanitarian field and the potential exploitation that this could cause. Sexual exploitation and abuse are condemned by law and international standards and ALIMA condemns such behavior. They are absolutely contrary to the humanitarian principles and ethics to which ALIMA and its employees adhere. Measures are in place to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse: Any employee involved in a case of sexual exploitation or abuse will be subject to immediate termination and ALIMA may refer the matter to the national authorities or the authorities of the country of origin of the employee being prosecuted. Sexual activity with persons under the age of 18 is strictly prohibited, even in the event of differing local laws regarding age of consent or age of majority. Mistaken belief in a child's age will not be taken as a defense. Any exchange of goods or services, including assistance due to recipients, for sexual services or other sexual or exploitative behavior will not be tolerated. All ALIMA representatives and managers are responsible for creating and maintaining a safe environment that protects against sexual exploitation and abuse and are encouraged to pay attention to promoting the safety of women and girls and any other vulnerable population. ALIMA staff are urged to report any inappropriate behavior they may witness. Several complaint mechanisms have been defined. One of them is to send an alert to this newly created email address: alert@alima.ngo. All of ALIMA's employees are key elements in the enforcement of this policy. We are a humanitarian organization and therefore have a great responsibility, individually and collectively, to prevent abuses or violence from being committed in our name or through the positions that are assigned to us by our organization At the field level, the HR/Admin Manager is in charge of enforcing the policy, while at the country level the HR coordination, the Deputy Head of Mission as well as the Head of mission have the task to enforce the policy.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members, ALIMA will engage communities in enhanced service delivery and the establishment of a community-structured feedback mechanism. The conversations will take place during project meetings, which will include sensitizing the community to the benefits of providing feedback on services received as well as walking them through the current communication and feedback technique ALIMA is examining. The objective is to get feedback on the current system and tweak it to fit the community structure, allowing clients to respond with ease and confidence. Throughout the year, ALIMA will continue to strengthen and improve community capacity and confidence to have a say not only on the method in which services are delivered but also to freely discuss any concerns they encounter in reporting problems they encounter through such sites. Moreover, every year, surveys are done at the various ALIMA-supported sites. However, in 2024, ALIMA will conduct two surveys to not only assess the quality of services offered but also to solicit feedback on the community participation strategy outlined in our implementation models.
