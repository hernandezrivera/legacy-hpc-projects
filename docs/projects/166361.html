Project Summary

In the midst of deliberate attacks on the civilian population by Non-state Actors, Borno state currently accommodates one of the world’s largest humanitarian crisis. With a reactivated force of attacks by splinter groups of insurgents, there has been a tremendous increase in family separation, including an astronomic rise in the number of separated and unaccompanied children who may also be out of school thereby increasing the vulnerability of the child; heightened risks of sexual and gender-based violence, particularly affecting women and children, arbitrary deprivation of land, homes and other property and displacement into inhospitable environments, where PoCs suffer stigmatization (especially during this Corona Virus era) and other protection concerns such as marginalization, discrimination, harassment, plundering, forced displacement, arbitrary detention etc.
With the application of SHO's mammoth protection finesse, the project aims at improving the delivery of quality protection services, enhancing the protection environment and supporting IDPs, IDP returnees and vulnerable host community members in Borno State to have access to services. Salient Humanitarian Organisation interventions will strengthen the protection environment by identifying and minimizing protection risks to which the affected populations are continually exposed and by improving the quality of protection response in Borno State
Throughout the implementation, the proposed intervention is designed to ensure strong community involvement which gives a primary place to the partnership with effectiveness and accountability to the affected population, it is articulated to provide both individual and community level protection assistance by responding to the most endemic protection concerns and risks of affected individuals and enhancing the overall protection environment of targeted communities.

1 - Needs

The on-going humanitarian crisis affecting North-East Nigeria has brought about massive civilian displacement across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe State as PoCs also struggle to flee from life-threatening incidences posed by armed groups which could include killings, kidnapping/abduction, forced recruitment, rape, sexual assault or harassment, and arbitrary detention by the military etc. According to the 2020 BAY State level consultations on HNO, 6.7 million people are currently in need of humanitarian assistance in North-East Nigeria, of which 4.84 million are in need of protection services, this figure has shown how essential protection is craved in this era of the crisis that is also being overwhelmed with a recent worldwide plague. In the first quarter of 2020, SHO Protection team conducted regular community, household, and individual level protection assessments and protection monitoring in MMC, and Jere Local Governments of Borno State and key ﬁndings revealed that in addition to Human Rights violation/abuse, incidences suffered prior and during displacements such as serious threats to life, sexual violence, forced recruitment into armed forces, and kidnappings, signiﬁcant protection concerns continue to be faced in areas of displacement. These include exposure to abuse and exploitation, sexual and Gender-Based Violence, forced and early marriages, child labour, begging, and odd survival practices. In IDP settlements, we discovered inter-community tensions, insecurity and restrictions to freedom of movements which would also amount to protection risks as rights are violated. Following 2019 HNO, women and children make up 80% of the displacement population in North-East Nigeria, as most adult men are unable to ﬂee during attacks, forcefully recruited into armed groups, abducted or killed. SHO protection assessments and monitoring ﬁndings also identiﬁed astronomic rise in female and child-headed households, as well as a high number of unaccompanied and separated children as a result of the conﬂict and massive displacement caused by the nefarious activities of insurgents. Of these IDP groups, women and girls are particularly at risk as conﬂict and displacement crisis have made worse pre-existing gender inequalities and increased their exposure to threat, vulnerability levels, and continuously limited their capacities. Risks for women and girls particularly include but not limited to Gender-Based Violence, of which intimate partner violence, forced/early marriage, and sexual exploitation and abuse are the most widespread forms. While women and girls are most at-risk, men and boys are also exposed to violence; forced recruitment into armed groups, kidnapping, killing, and torture. When they escape captivity or they are released, former combatants and captives face serious discrimination, stigmatization, social exclusion and distrust from community members and authorities, resulting in extreme poverty and further exposure to abuse and exploitation. Despite these signiﬁcant concerns over the overall protection situation in Borno State, and the continuous threats to life, fundamental human rights, and access to basic services for displaced populations, signiﬁcant gaps remain in the provision of humanitarian assistance, and particularly of specialised protection services.

2 - Activities

A gamut of Protection acumen will be deployed to attain favourable protection environment to afford; fair protection processes and documentation, security from violence and exploitation, protection from effects of armed conﬂict through protection monitoring and protection by presence, awareness creation and sensitisation sessions to bring to the barest minimum the effect of stigmatisation on those affected by COVID-19. SHO assuredly strengthens its protection monitoring system and employ enhanced presence in the ﬁeld to support affected populations as issues arise by involving community members with the identiﬁcation of protection risks and incidents as well as human rights violations faced by the PoCs through community-based protection monitoring systems to capacitate them (community volunteers) to be able to refer/respond to issues and provide comprehensive protection response including through referrals to other stakeholders. Analyze information on protection risks and incident trends as well as develop and disseminate high-quality protection products as a basis for evidence-based advocacy and programming in order to ensure appropriate response by humanitarian actors, as well as to ensure protection mainstreaming within other sectors. Salient HO will follow up vulnerable IDPs and IDP returnees through the vulnerability screening process to allow for perfect delivery of assistance tailored to speciﬁc needs, including referrals for specialized services such as child protection, MHPSS, legal protection and medical response, especially at this time that medical attention is needed in every part of the Borno State. SHO will focus on massive awareness creation, sensitizations and distribution of IEC materials in English and all Borno State languages. To ensure referral to quality legal aid and services, provision of pro bono lawyers, including legal advice; this includes legal referral on Housing, Land and Property, referrals of Human Rights violation and abuse including the provision of mobility or transportation fare for those who are referred by us to access services. SHO will conduct advocacy interventions for IDPs, and IDP returnees on access to services, we will also build the capacity of relevant community representatives to support the resolution of minor issues at the camp level even in our absence. Capacity-building activities of relevant national institutions will be conducted to support advocacy as well as extended to humanitarian actors to ensure that assistance is provided in line with international standards and that core protection principles are strictly adhered to by service providers. Engage in advocacy and efforts to establish the conditions for the pursuit of durable solutions both for IDPs and returnees and provide reception/safe space and transit facilities for returnees.
