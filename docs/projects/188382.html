Project Summary

The project consists of two main components:
**Component 1 for 24 months - Standard facility-based primary healthcare services through 4 PHC centers according to the Gaziantep Health Cluster's EHSP, including the integration of the following services:
-Mental health and psychosocial support
-Screening of acute malnutrition and treatment of moderate acute malnutrition and severe acute malnutrition without complications, referral to care for severe acute malnutrition with complications, and counseling for Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF),
-GBV first-line support with referral to specialized GBV services,
-NCD care,
-Provision of facilitated referral to diagnostic services
The PHC locations and codes are:
Sarmada PHC Center!G0703011
Tarmanin PHC Center!G0703028
Zarzour PHC!G0704018
Daret Azza CPHC!B0200006
This component is planned to serve 244,800 beneficiaries.
**Component 2 for 24 months - Outreach community health services through 150 CHWs linked with health facilities to provide the CHWG standard package of services includingCOVID-19 related social mobilization, active detection and community referrals to standard health facilities for nutrition, CD, NCD, MH, and GBV cases. CHWs will provide additional services to PWDs and vulnerable people including assisted referral to healthcare services using a community-based referral SoP, provision of assistive devise following medical consultation, and active HH follow-up for NCD patients. The 300 CHWs will be linked with UDER and non-UDER supported health facilities.
This component is planned to serve 235,200 beneficiaries.
UDER is sharing the information about this project with members of the coordination clusters in Gaziantep. At the moment, UDER coordinates with a number of actors to provide complementary services at the mentioned facilities including Shafaq and PAC to provide nutrition services, Mentor to provide leishmaniases treatment, SIG to provide immunization services, Alameen and SRD to provide COVID-19 vaccines. UDER will continue engaging with the PHC network in Harim to ensure that the facilities are part of the coordinated referral mechanism and applying the same standards and information system (DHIS2) for streamlined data collection.

4 - Needs

The 4 health facilities provided 56,525 primary health consultations between December2020 and February 2021. These consultations were provided by a qualified health workers, who were trained on infection control, communicable and non-communicable diseases, reproductive health, mhGAP, nutritional screening, triage and referral mechanisms, and health information systems.During the previous phases of the project, UDER provided the 4 health facilities with the necessary equipment and furniture, improved their buildings through maintenance and rehabilitation, put systems and protocols in place, and enhanced their linkages with the referral networks.After this investment in infrastructure and capacity building of the staff, UDER is now able to provide more efficient and cost-effective health services, without the need to procure major equipment or rehabilitation, and with a focus in the next phase of improving the quality and enhancing systems and integration with other services.An investment was made to develop the skills of CHWs by the CH TWG in Gaziantep within the COVID-19 response in NWS. Over 750 CHWs managed by UDER were able to reach over 2,676,336people across NWS with COVID-19 and standard educational messages including 761,116boys, 676,312 girls, 594,328 men and 644,580 women. Vaccination hesitancy and poor healthcare seeking behavior remain a challenge requiring advance community health approaches

5 - Implementation of activities

The project is implemented through 4 static PHC facilities and 150 mobile CHWs. UDER has already trained the CHWs and they are ready to be dispatched after confirming their referral pathways and linkage to existing PHC facilities. 80 CHW will be linked to the 4PHCs which are already functional. An additional 70 CHWs are already trained in north Aleppo and will be linked to health facilities in their area of operation. CH teams will be provided with equipment to support EWARN teams in the active detection and referral of COVID-19 cases and other infectious diseases.CHWs will work according to the CHG SOP. They will apply the standard CH package, screen for CD and NCD and refer vulnerable people to health facilities using community referral cars. They will identify PWDs who do not have assistive devices and provide them with the devices after a consultation with health facilities. PHCs will provide an average of 5100 EHSP medical consultations and 12 MH specific consultation per facility per month.BeMONC facility will provide an average of 20-40 deliveries per month. All facilities will provide ANC,PNC and FP services, provide patients with essential medicines. Facilities will follow IPC measures.Monitoring is conducted through field team visits, electronic data collection tools, and beneficiary CFM. Data will be collected monthly and a M&E reports generated quarterly. Checklists are used by UDER to ensure meeting the standards of the technical WGs in Gaziantep.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

-Discrimination in health treatment: Probability: low. Risk severity: high.Mitigation:UDER MEAL officer will conduct spot-check visits to the PHCs, conduct exit interview with beneficiaries and promote a confidential complaint mechanism including UDER's WhatsApp hotline, complaint email account, and promotion of UDER website for maintaining anonymous complaints. -Health staff are infected with COVID-19.Probability: low. Risk severity: moderate.Mitigation: Facilities are provided with PPE, and trained on IPC. UDER will monitor the adherence to IPC measures and document infected staff members to ensure that they commit to isolation measures.-Household data is exposed to leakage or destruction. Probability: low. Risk severity: high.Mitigation: - Record all beneficiary data through Kobo surveys using smart phones on an online data base stored on a server that can only be accessed by Turkey staff. This prevents data storage on hard or soft copies in Syria which are exposed to be stolen or destroyed;-To enhance confidentiality, beneficiaries’ names are only accessed by the information team. Family records are coded anonymously before sharing with other parties. -CHWs are harassed by local entities. Probability: moderate. Risk severity: high. Mitigation measures: CH team members are recruited from the local community to make sure trust is exchanged between them and communities. The project is backed by local health authorities (Independent Health Directorates) and community.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Abuse or harassment by CHWs or medical staff towards patients and HH members. Probability: low. Risk severity: high. Mitigation measures: -Recruitment of female CHWs and limited the recruitment of male outreach workers. Organizing teams in two CHWs per household visit and conducting random supervision visits by supervisors; -Training staff on PSEA, code of conduct and ethical data collection;-Ensuring the commitment of staff to UDER’s PSEA, Child Safeguarding and Code of Conduct policies;-Contracts including a strict term of dismissal towards any misconduct of SEA;-Maintenance and enhancement of Complaint and Feedback mechanism including hot lines for receiving reports and allegations of SEA misconducts;-Regular surveys conducted by MEAL team to monitor the conducted visits. Surveys include question about team members behaviors,misconducts and their respect of privacy;-Allegations are seriously investigated, where aid workers are subjected to contract termination and further disciplinary actions if allegations were proven.Awareness of GBV and PSEA issues will be incorporated into project activities and beneficiary satisfaction will be verified during household and facility visits which will include female employees to facilitate collecting suggestions/complaints from female beneficiaries especially in relation to GBV and PSEA. All of the CHWs under this project are trained on the PSEA policy during their initial induction.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: -Permission for UDER to operate in Idleb and Aleppo continues.-Staff infected with COVID-19 do not exceed 2% at all times.-Community acceptance for this project continues.-Current conflict doesn't affect program activities.-Drugs and medical supplies continue to be received through WHO medical supply-line.-Complementary projects continue to be in place, including referrals and Harim PHC network.Risks:-Potential increase in violence in Idleb and Aleppo through a military offensive or fights among Organized Armed Groups. Probability: moderate. Risk severity: high.Mitigation: UDER continues monitoring security conditions and receiving security briefing through INSO and UNOCHA. Staff continue to receive orientation on safety and security procedures. Safety and security policies and procedures are regularly revisited to adapt to context changes.-Most of the staff is infected withCOVID-19 and activities are suspended or terminated. Probability: low. Risk severity: high. Mitigation: Staff are trained on COVID-19 preventive measures, provided with PPE, monitored to strictly commit to IPC. A record is maintained on infected and isolated staff to prevent a major infection spread within the project team;-Duplication of services: UDER will commit to sharing information about activities, consult HC before dispatching team, consult communities and health directorates before reactivating CHWs.
