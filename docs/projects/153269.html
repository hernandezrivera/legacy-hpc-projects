Project Summary

Hunger and malnutrition have increased persistently in South Sudan, with nearly 1.1 million children under age 5 estimated to be acutely malnourished and in need of life-saving services (HNO, Nov. 2017). Admissions to Tearfund Feeding Centres continue to rise even in the post-harvest season when typically malnutrition rates dip as the dry season brings improved access for nutrition and health services as well as humanitarian assistance, and a reduction in morbidity and outbreaks of disease. With the rapid depletion of food stocks and subsequent early onset of the lean season the nutrition situation is expected to deteriorate with persistent food insecurity, outbreaks of diarrhea and other illnesses, poor Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices and high morbidity, exacerbated by limited access to services during the seasonal rains. The GAM rate in Uror is currently 16.0 while the GAM rate in Twic East is 24.3 and in Pochalla it is 15.8- all above the WHO Emergency Threshold.
The high levels of food insecurity seen among children under 5 in the targeted areas can be partially attributed to the scarcity of food and the consequent lack of variety in diet. This is further compounded by the lack of knowledge of minimum dietary diversity required. Tearfund’s MIYCN component aims to address this issue through mothers groups both within the community and at the feeding centres. Furthermore, Tearfund’s wider nutrition programme contains a kitchen gardening component, in which the IYCF information in complemented by increasing access to more diverse food items.
Tearfund will continue implementation of a nutrition program in Uror, Twic East and Pochalla counties. Tearfund is currently responding to the crisis in Uror, Twic East and Pochalla, supporting 22 nutrition centers in total. This project will continue to address high rates of GAM through the provision of Outpatient Therapeutic Programme (OTP) and Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP) services, thus alleviating malnutrition and reducing susceptibility to other health issues, including water-borne diseases. As children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women and girls are the most vulnerable to MAM and SAM, these individuals will be the primary target group for this intervention. A total of 3580 children under 5 will be targeted for treatment of SAM (100 Pochalla, 808 Twic East, 2671 Uror). A further 14963 children will be targeted for treatment of MAM (6817 Pochalla, 1406 Twic East, 6740 Uror). As part of the MAM and IYCF component, a total of 9266 PLWs will be targeted (226 Pochalla, 3262 Twic East, 5777 Uror).
Tearfund is the sole organization supporting nutrition interventions in Uror, Twic East and Pochalla, therefore without this response, GAM rates would inevitably rise and morbidity and mortality would increase.

Needs

Current malnutrition rates remain at critical levels, with the GAM rate between 16% and 19% across Jonglei state, as a result of the collapse of the local economy and lack of access to markets, insufficient crop production limiting access to food, and restricted access for humanitarian agencies to provide an adequate response due to ongoing insecurity. The post-harvest decrease in GAM rates is short-lived and attributable to humanitarian assistance, food stocks from harvests, and the reduction in morbidity and disease outbreaks during the dry season. There has been a steady improvement in Infant and Young Child Feeding Practices (IYCF) since 2016, which can be attributed to continued IYCF programming across all villages in the counties. Conversely, early initiation of complementary foods and dietary diversity were identified as common issues in the surveyed population. This messaging will be reinforced and monitored in the proposed program. Malnutrition rates are expected to deteriorate further in 2019, caused in part by continued lack of access to basic services to treat and prevent malnutrition, the inaccessibility of safe water, poor sanitation and hygiene, and severe food insecurity due to poor harvests and dysfunctional markets. Moreover, the continued conflict and the resultant displacement of people has meant that WASH and other existing infrastructure is not only insufficient to meet the needs of the people but completely overwhelmed. Inadequate water supply and poor sanitation and hygiene sustain the cycle of malnutrition prevalent in the target counties, leading to high rates of diarrhea and water borne diseases and affecting nutrient uptake for infants and young children. The undernourished are more likely to suffer from higher incidences of and increased mortality rates from diarrhea. This, coupled with poor IYCF practices and vastly inadequate food supplies, results in sustained high levels of malnutrition and infant mortality rates. Only 33.3% and 26% of all caretakers knew how to treat diarrhea in Uror and Twic East respectively, with poor knowledge of basic hygiene practices in both locations and 63.8% and 64.8% of children in Uror and Twic East respectively reportedly being sick in the two weeks prior to the surveys (Tearfund Post-Harvest SMART surveys, 2017/2018). In addition to the extensive health and hygiene education provided through existing IYCF counselors and community volunteers, the project will actively encourage mothers to seek prompt medical treatment from ICCM volunteers recently trained by a local NGO in the operation area to reduce the number of SAM cases deteriorating and requiring stabilization center admission.

Activities

Treatment of severe acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months Admission and Treatment of SAM cases (22 FCs): All children meeting the criteria (Z-score or MUAC) will be refereed to OTP for treatment of acute malnutrition with Ready to Use Therapeutic Food RUTF. Malaria Testing: All children admitted into the OTP programme will be treated for malaria and referred accordingly. Child Friendly Spaces: Tearfund will integrate child friendly spaces into the 22 Feeding Centres, ensuring that Early Childhood Development activities are integrated and involving mothers enrolled in the IYCF programme. Referral of SAM with Complications to SCs: OTP/TSFP children with medical complication/failed appetite test will be admitted in Stabilization center. All discharges will be through OTP where they will continue with treatment and follow-up. Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months Admission and Treatment of MAM U5: Tearfund will continue an emergency targeted TSFP program through 22 FCs located in village centres. Admission and discharge criteria will be in line with Ministry of Health and WHO guidelines Provision of quality MIYCN and micronutrient supplementation services in nutrition sites and community levelAdmission and Treatment MAM PLW:. Mothers of children less than 6 months or less than 4kgs who are not able to produce enough breast milk will be admitted to the TSFP for PLW.Hygiene Promotion: Nutrition staff will engage those attending coming to health facilities in order to deliver targeted health, nutrition and hygiene promotion messages. MIYCN: PLWs attending the feeding centres and within the community will be targeted for individual and group counselling on IYCF practices, following the UNICEF recommended approach. Tearfund will disseminate hygiene promotion messages, while providing complementary hygiene kits.Train health and nutrition care providers on management of acute malnutrition : Training will be conducted for at least 75 CHD staff on management of SAM and MAMMonitor availability of core pipeline supplies at nutrition sites: Tearfund has an existing agreements with UNICEF and WFP for supply of RUTF and RUSF in three month lots. Conduct nutrition SMART surveys in prioritized counties: SMART surveys will be conducted in the pre and post harvest period for Pochalla and Twic East counties.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

At the beginning of each intervention, Tearfund provides the whole community with information about the project, including targeted beneficiaries, locations, feedback mechanisms and expectations of Tearfund staff and volunteers. This is frequently re-iterated through community discussions and focus group discussions. Targeted communities are also made aware of the timing of key project activities and the locations. Feedback on these decisions is welcomed at the project design stage and throughout. The information is further disseminated by Tearfund's network of trained community volunteers, who are embedded within each village.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Tearfund will promote the use of culturally appropriate learning materials including IEC materials, particularly targeting women and girls.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

All Tearfund staff adhere to internal PSEA policies throughout implementation. Protection risks are identified and assessed before the start of the project, and will be continually monitored throughout implementation. Beneficiaries will also be sensitized on confidential feedback mechanisms and Tearfund staff are trained on how to make referrals to appropriate protection actors when possible. Tearfund will coordinate with other protection actors in the intervention areas to ensure that there are always active referral pathways to the appropriate service providers.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Tearfund is committed to ensuring the affected populations actively participate in project activities; the IYCF counsellors and volunteers that have been recruited are from the community and trained in IYCF protocol. Their role is to train the team leaders of the Mother to Mother Support Groups, also from the community, who lead weekly meetings of the support groups. Each member of the support group in turn shares their knowledge with 3-5 others. Therefore, the engagement of community members and their ownership of project activities is essential to successful implementation.All monitoring and evaluation activities conducted by Tearfund aim to have a representative sample of all groups within the community (IDPs, host communities, disabled etc), as well as representatives from the local authorities. Separate, confidential discussions will be held with different community groups to ensure that each group’s views are heard. Feedback will be collated and used to inform project reviews, resulting in amendments to future projects and implementation where appropriate.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Feedback mechanisms are already established in all project locations, and include reporting to community volunteers, Tearfund staff, quarterly focus group discussions, meetings with local leaders, and feedback boxes at feeding centres and distributions. The feedback mechanisms have been shown to be accessible, appropriate to the context and preferred by the community during other Tearfund projects. Tearfund will ensure that targeted communities are aware of all feedback and complaints mechanisms and the locations of feedback boxes during community sensitisation meetings, and at the beginning of feeding sessions. Tearfund staff are trained on how to handle feedback ensuring anonymity and confidentiality where appropriate, and pass on any concerns to relevant staff members. All feedback is recorded and responded to, after which it is incorporated into the current and future project designs. Any feedback regarding critical or sensitive protection issues will be referred directly to Tearfund Executive Team in Juba, investigated appropriately and responded to according to Tearfund’s Safeguarding Policy.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As outlined above, Tearfund aims to be representative in all monitoring and evaluation activities. Project designs actively take into account feedback received from the community during needs assessments and previous interventions including on programme quality and effectiveness, timing of project activities and safety of beneficiaries (including cultural issues that may arise). Furthermore, Tearfund frequently conducts community consultations to assess the effectiveness of the wider nutrition programme.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Tearfund is committed to ensuring protection is mainstreamed across all nutrition sites. In terms of the physical protection of nutrition facilities, trained security guards are constantly on duty and ensure that only beneficiaries (primarily children under 5 years and Pregnant and Lactating Women) are allowed into the nutrition sites. All consultations and assessments within the sites are held in private rooms where only the mother, child and trained clinician are present. Tearfund has also opened two stabilisation centres in Uror to reduce the 60+ km that women and children used to have to walk to the nearest site when referred. Due to the strategic location of these sites, this has also ensured that beneficiaries do not have to risk their lives crossing over between IO and Government held areas. In order to further enhance accessibility, Tearfund staff have been trained on how to make admissions using special adapted criteria (other than MUAC), in order to ensure the facilities are accessible for persons with disabilities. Tearfund has embedded 2 community volunteers in each village who conduct house to house visits and are capable of assessing and referring cases to the nutrition sites. As the stigma associated with having a child with a disability often prevents mothers from having these children screened at mass screening campaigns, this approach is more effective at ensuring that every child is assessed. All assessments and surveys aim to have a representative sample of all groups within the community (IDPs, Host communities, women, elderly etc). This ensures that programming is responsive to the needs of all members of the community. In order to maintain this responsiveness, Tearfund ensures that targeted communities are aware of key points of contact for complaints and feedback (ie community volunteers, Tearfund staff) and locations of complaints boxes at community briefs that are held at the beginning of the feeding sessions.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project has identified the cultural, social and physical barriers that targeted communities can face in relation to the project. As outlined above, this included social stigmas related to bringing malnourished children out of homes (addressed through house to house screening), as well as dangers faced when trying to cross between government and IO lines in the community (addressed through parallel programming across lines). Previous experiences have also shown that marginalized members of the community (such as IDPs) may fear involvement in community discussions. Therefore Tearfund strongly advocates and encourages for the involvement of all groups in community discussions and decisions.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The proposed project will aim to empower women and girls and reduce some of the burden that is culturally placed upon them as primary caregivers within the household. The project will also actively assess protection risks, including physical and social risks and address them appropriately. This included through continuing to provide services on both government and IO sides, to ensure that beneficiaries are not forced to cross conflict lines, and continuing to provide community outreach services

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Access is a key issue which in built into all Tearfund monitoring activities, particularly as Tearfund aims for full coverage of the operational counties. This includes identifying specific cultural, social and economic barriers that communities face in relation to the project. This is done through frequent focus group discussions. In consideration of the sensitivity of the subject, Tearfund undertakes separate discussions for groups such as IDPs as well as women, and ensures that all groups are aware of individual feedback mechanisms.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety (both physical and otherwise) is measured through individual and group community discussions as well as frequent field visits and field reports detailing the safety and security situation in the operational areas. Beneficiaries will frequently be asked to provide individual feedback, which will remain confidential. Reports will be generated which separate feedback related to safety and protection, and all incidents will be logged and reported. Appropriate actions will be taken and this will inform future interventions.
