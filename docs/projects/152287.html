Project Summary

This project will focus on strengthening the capacity of management of severe acute malnutrition across the BAY states. It will strengthen the capacity of existing government health workers and partner organizations in provision of quality treatment to over 18,000 SAM children with medical complications. WHO will also preposition essential medicines for the smooth running of the Stabilization Centres to ensure uninterrupted supply of commodities to the implementing partners. This will be achieved via procurement and distribution of 396 SAM kits to the various SCs and support the establishment of new ones. Also over 300 health care staff will be trained on inpatient management of SAM with complications. The limited capacity of health care workers which is worsened by high staff turnovers in especially the security compromised areas is an additional justification to sustain these training sessions. It will also facilitate access to life-saving nutrition services through early detection and referral of malnourished children especially from Hard-To-Reach areas to nutrition treatment centres for timely intervention. It will complement the humanitarian nutrition response of the sector in general and WHO’s efforts in particular by addressing unmet nutrition and health needs. In addition, this project will build on the current achievements of addressing unmet nutrition needs for complicated SAM cases e.g the planned expansion of SC bed spaces and setting up of new ones. Partners will be supported with the sustained supply of SAM kits throughout the project lifespan. It will also support the State MoHs by providing integrated life-saving nutrition services to the under-5 children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in the priority areas. The services will be provided through 113 mobile health teams positioned in hard-to-Reach communities in the states by enhancing their capacity to strengthen community mobilization activities for timely detection of SAM children through routine screening for malnutrition and subsequent referral to the nearest treatment centres at the same time promoting IYCF practices. In summary, project is to support the following aspects: • Identification and referral of children with SAM children in HTR areas plus Protection, promotion and support of optimal IYCF practices in these location. • Procurement of life saving drugs nutrition supplies namely SAM Kits. • Training of Health workers on inpatient management of SAM. • Establishment of nutrition surveillance system on inpatient management of SAM

Needs

Malnutrition is still a public health concern in the North-East Nigeria and a key priority for emergency response. Acute malnutrition is among the leading causes of death weather directly or indirectly for children under the age of five. It is estimated that 1,094,467 children aged 6-59 across the BAY states are undernourished with 367,526 severely malnourished (SAM) while 726,941 with moderate acute malnutrition (MAM). There has been a reduction in the prevalence of SAM cases from 439,520 in 2018 to 367,076 in 2019 following sustained scale up of services the same is not the case for MAM where cases increased from 502,536 in 2018 to 723,326 in 2019 . The nutrition situation has further been exacerbated by protection concerns related to new displacement following on going military operations as well as outbreak of diseases such as cholera where 18 LGAS were affected by cholera outbreak in 2018 with 4,250 cases reported .The food security situation also continues to impact on undernutrition, access to food and maintenance of adequate nutrition status are critical determinants of people’s survivals in emergencies, addressing the nutrition needs of infants and young children must be a priority, given their vulnerability. Prevention of undernutrition is just as important as treating acute malnutrition . The first 1000 days, age from conception until two years is particularly important in preventing undernutrition, emphasis must be placed on ensuring appropriate nutritional support to pregnant women and children under 2 years. With 745,271 number estimated to benefit from intervention promoting optimal infant feeding in emergencies. These interventions are critical to mitigate the long-term impact that nutrition can have on health, cognitive development and physical growth and its long-term impacts such as reduced capacity to learn, increased risk of diseases and irreversible damage to brain. Micronutrient deficiencies are worsened by the disaster when they have already been prevalent in the population, prevalence of micro nutrient deficiencies are highest in the North- East stunting is at 52.4 meaning one in very two children below 5 years are shorter for their age. This reflects chronic malnutrition culminating from failure to receive proper nutrition over a prolonged period, these deficiencies must be tackled using population-wide interventions and individual treatment. In the long term, extended micronutrient deficiencies impact on child development, brain development, learning capacities . Vitamin A deficiency can mean a child will be growing up with lower immunity, which can trigger frequent health problems and poor growth. The health system in these locations have also been greatly compromised hence the need to use mobile outreach teams to reach the majority who are not able to access health and nutrition service especially in the Hard to reach areas. WHO HTR mobile teams will be equipped with the necessary skill to screen and promptly refer SAM cases to the nearest treatment centre, will also increase and improve nutrition interventions through the scale up of preventative strategy namely Infant and Young Child feeding (IYCF) counselling, multiple micro-nutrient powder (MNP) supplementation thus greatly increasing access of nutrition services and reducing the risk of mortality as well as exposure to other protection related threats as they try to access the existing static sites. Within the BAY states there exists no adequate services to treat SAM cases with medical complication while incidences of malnutrition are expected to peaks during the lean period and raining season without adequate resources to treat these cases, there is increased risk of mortality. New SCs need to be established to widen the coverage and the existing ones need to be strengthened. It is estimated that about 36,753 SAM children will have medical complications in 2019 and 25,727 have been targeted for treatment.

Activities

The main thrust of WHO in emergencies is to strengthen, through information dissemination and training; the national, sub-national and international capacities of partners to meet the nutritional needs of populations in emergencies. This includes mainly supporting in-patient management of SAM with medical complications with supplies and training, provision of effective technical support through the production and dissemination of scientifically validated and up-to-date guidelines, norms, criteria and methodologies relating to nutritional standards. WHO will: • Provide integrated nutrition services (MUAC screening, IYCF ) to children in Borno ,Yobe and Adamawa State during 12 months through routine screening and prompt referral for malnutrition by mobile teams especially in Hard-To-Reach areas, provide IYCF counseling to caregivers of children under 2 years. • Provide lifesaving drugs for treatment of SAM children with medical complications: More than 25,000 severely acute malnourished children with medical complications will be provided treatment with lifesaving drugs through the provision of 396 inpatient kits to the stabilization centres across the 3 states. The kits consist of basic, supplementary and malaria drugs as well as include some equipment and supplies for the SC. • WHO will conduct 12 training each six days on inpatient management of SAM according to the national protocol. About 25 SC staff from MoH and partner I/NGOs will participate and benefit from each of the training. • WHO will conduct regular supportive supervision field visits to at least bi weekly to nutrition stabilization centres. SC staff will be provided on the job training on gaps identified in their knowledge and skills. WHO will also conduct coaching sessions with SC staff to sit with them in detail and support and demonstrate addressing the gaps identified. WHO will also develop job aids/IEC material for the SCs, some of them will be pasted on the walls as important reference material for the staff. • Nutrition surveillance system will be established strengthen These interventions will improve nutrition status and infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices among most vulnerable population of target areas. It will also reduce morbidity and mortality related to malnutrition in the BAY states. Close collaboration with sector partners including SMoH, Hospital management board, nutrition sector, health sector, and partner organizations will be maintained to review needs, assess progress and devise a strategy for strengthening inpatient management of SAM.
