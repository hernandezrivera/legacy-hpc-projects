Project Summary

The infrastructure in Syria requires substantial repair and operational support, including training for O&M (Operations and Maintenance) of rehabilitation sites and on-grid electricity supply in areas of high need. The proposed intervention will provide life-saving WASH interventions at both the community and HH levels to increase water supply that is in most cases below the Sphere Standard. Furthermore, Medair’s activities will continue to address community sanitation through sanitation system rehabilitation at the Assad hospital in DEZ. Medair will also address the ongoing cholera epidemic through the distribution of cholera kits, which will tie in to the Medair's health response. Through the proposed projects, Medair estimates to reach 205,000 beneficiaries.

1. Water Network Rehabilitation
WASH systems suffered additional damages due to years of functioning at high capacity, lack of maintenance, displacement of technical staff, climate change and poor water resource management. Medair aims to rehabilitate up to 11 community level water systems. Activities will include: upgrade/installation of water pumping equipment; (re)equipment of wells; generator replacement; repair of water distribution lines; and development of water purification stations. Alongside these activities, Medair will promote water conservation awareness, and teach proper operation and maintenance (O&M) techniques to Water Economic Unit (WEU), the water system operators responsible for systems after they are rehabilitated. Medair will further promote environmental sustainability by identifying water stations that have the capacity and/or need to have solar panels installed.
2. Sewage Network Rehabilitation
A large portion of sewage is presently discharged untreated and many of the necessary sewage systems are completely non-functional, in disrepair, or nonexistent. This is even a bigger risk in a hospital setting, especially when Syria is dealing with disease outbreaks. Medair has identified the sewage system in al Assad Hospital in DEZ as one with a great need to be rehabilitated. The intervention will ensure that waste can be safely transported away from the hospital public spaces and that the systems will continue to be monitored sustainably.
3. Cholera response
With the current Cholera outbreak, Medair is aiming to respond under WASH through the distribution of 10,000 Cholera kits (alongside Medair's health intervention that includes health messages on cholera). These kits include soap, a jerry can, aquatabs when available, and an awareness poster.

Medair’s proposed intervention will target vulnerable households and communities in Government Controlled areas within Homs, Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor, Idlib, Quneitra or Rural Damascus, Hama, and Ar-Raqqa. Currently, Medair has a presence within these areas, subject to approvals and identified needs.
In terms of resilience, Medair will insure the sustainability of all interventions. Also, Medair conducts Operations and Maintenance trainings for operators at the project locations and conducts community awareness for safe water and water conservation. Medair‘s staff meet with the community and local authorities, asking about their highest perceived needs and involving them in the design. Additionally, to enhance resilience, sustainability and to reduce the impact on the environment, each rehabilitated water system will be fitted with solar power as a renewable energy operating modality. Water systems are generally connected to a city power source; however, for situations where this is not the case, a generator will be provided in addition. This combination of power sources should power the water point at all hours.

3 - Needs

An estimated 13.55 million people are in need of life-saving WASH interventions. The majority of communities assessed by Medair last year did not meet Sphere standards for water and at least 47% of the population are relying on alternatives to piped water (often unsafe modalities) to reach their water needs. At least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and half of the sewage systems are non-functioning, causing and exacerbating life-threatening health risks such as COVID-19 and AWD/Cholera. (draft HNO, 2023). Currently Cholera was found in all 14 governorates, with the highest numbers in DEZ, RAQ, and ALP. (Cholera outbreak situation report #7, 2022) The water crisis also continues to affect NES. With low water levels in the Euphrates since January 2021, and severe and long-term droughts. (OCHA, august 2022) The water crises and drought-like conditions have reduced access to water in an estimated 70% of all communities in NES. According to the DEZ water establishment, in DEZ 89% of people receive their water from the Euphrates, in which in RAQ and DEZ almost all untreated raw sewage is discharged into. Governorates with lower sewer connection rate (RAQ, DEZ, HAS, ALP) are the most affected by the cholera outbreak, with 98% of all cases by 08-10-2022. A general deterioration in water quality has been registered with 21% of households not drinking chlorinated water, and only 61% of water from water trucking resulted chlorinated. (Draft HNO, 2023)

4 - Implementation of activities

Medair implements an integrated response, targeting areas where they can provide a package of both health and WASH programming in the same community. Medair aims to reach around 205,000 beneficiaries with the following activities. Medair will rehabilitate 11 water systems to restore adequate and clean water supply to communities. Installation and repairs include various water pumps, air compressors, PLC systems, electrical service panels, ventilation devices, pipelines, filter basins and other purification components. These activities will bring each individual above 35 L per day. After rehabilitations, projects are handed back to the Water Establishment Units with Operation and Maintenance training as a sustainability measure. Wherever possible, Community Awareness-Raising workshops on water conservation and safety will be organized for key local leaders. Medair will rehabilitate one sanitation network at al Assad hospital in DEZ, which is even more essential with the current cholera outbreak. Medair will also support communities with the distribution of 10,000 Cholera kits. These kits consist of 10 bars of soap, one jerry can, aquatabs (number depending on availability with WHO/UNICEF), and a leaflet for instruction of aquatab use. This activity is a joint activity with Medair's health response in which community health workers will do health and hygiene messaging on Cholera.
