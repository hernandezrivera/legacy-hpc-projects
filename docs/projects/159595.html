Project Summary

Lack of sustainable access to basic Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) facilities remains a prominent issue within the humanitarian landscape in Somalia. Recent displacements, repeated disasters and a chronic lack of investment in durable WASH solutions have resulted in exposing the Somali communities to all categories of WASH-related public health threats. According to Somalia WASH cluster 2019 needs overview, 2.7 million people in Somalia are in need of emergency WASH assistance. Among these, the most vulnerable groups include the IDP communities (0.8M), with people exposed to high risks of disease outbreaks. Furthermore, lack of adequate sanitation facilities at the camps is a major leading factor for open defecation; exposing IDPs to contracting diseases and contributing to the increase in reported incidents of sexual harassment towards the IDPs - particularly women, children and PWDs. Lack of basic WASH facilities also adversely affects school attendance in the region (Somalia WASH cluster HPC strategic guidance note 2020)
Poor hygiene and sanitation practices combined with the use of unsafe drinking water has resulted in Acute Diarrheal Diseases (ADD) outbreaks to the IDPs camps in Bardere district. According to Human Appeal's own survey, a number of considerable cases of diarrhea and AWD/cholera have been reported this year at the Habal Adey IDP camp, with children under the age of 5 years being most affected.
This project aims to improve the health and living conditions of the IDPs living in the Habal Adey Camp, in Bardere District by providing them with sustainable access to clean drinking water through the construction of safe drinking water sources in the camp. The project will also support in reducing/controlling the spread of diseases (AWD/diarrhea, cholera, etc.) caused by poor hygiene practices with the construction of Ventilated Improved Pit (VIP) latrines to improve the access to basic sanitation facilities of the IDPs in that camp and for school-going children in Al-Hikma Primary and Secondary School in Bardere district.
Furthermore, the project will promote good hygiene behavior by providing 2 months of training to these IDPs on promoting good hygiene practices.
In total, this project will support a total of 12,899 individuals (6,013 male and 6,886 female) with different WASH interventions which will be the construction of 110 VIP Latrines (10 in a school and 100 in an IDP camp) to benefit 5,399 people; the construction of 15 shallow wells to benefit 7,500 people; and hygiene promotion. Human Appeal will work in close collaboration with the Somalia WASH cluster (national and regional levels), Humanitarian WASH partners working in Gedo region, relevant Line Ministries of the State and the Federal Government Institutions in Somalia.

Project Activities

Specific project activities 1. In compliance with the global WASH guidelines, support 7,500 IDPs (2,500 Men, 2,900 Women, and 2,100 children) in the Habal Adey IDP camp, with improved access to clean drinking water and for domestic use; through the construction of 15 shallow wells.2. Support 5,000 beneficiaries (including 2,250 men, 2,750 women – including 500 PWD) with the construction of 100 Ventilated Improved Pit (VIP) latrines in the Habal Adey IDP camp in Bardere. These latrines will be gender-segregated and lockable for privacy purposes. Furthermore, 25 of these latrines will be constructed to ensure accessibility by PWD. The project will ensure that the locations of these latrines will not cause any harm or discomfort (i.e. smell etc.) for the camp ID3. Support 390 school going children (206 boys and 184 girls – including 10 children with disabilities) and 9 teachers (7 men and 2 women) with the construction of 10 VIP latrines in Al-Hikma primary and secondary school in Bardere district. The school has 60 Students in Secondary School (Boys: 35 Girls: 25) and 330 Students in Primary School (Boys: 171 Girls: 159). Gender consideration will be observed in the construction of these latrines and 2 special latrines will be constructed to support access of students with disabilities.4. Support the total targeted 12,500 IDPs by training 500 selected IDPs as hygiene promoters (60% men and 40% women) on Good Hygiene Practices and providing 5,000 hygiene kits This will support in enhancing WASH Knowledge, influencing behavior and common hygiene practices amongst IDPs in Habal Adey camp. The project will secure commitments from the IDPs receiving the training to spread the knowledge among the rest of the target beneficiaries within the camp. Operational activities:1. Recruitment of project staff.2. Identification, selection, and registration of beneficiaries3. Community mobilization and sensitization through meetings with stakeholders - HA staff, Local Authorities, line ministries representatives in the project locations. 4. Conducting a project learning assessment

Mitigation Measures

2a. Exclusion and marginalization: Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistanceMitigation measuresTo ensure people with disabilities are not marginalized, the project has included the provision of basic WASH to PWD with the construction of 20 VIP latrines for PWD, ensuring the location and construction enable easy access for PWD. Also, the 10 latrines for schools will also include 2 latrines specifically designed to meet the needs of children with disabilities. Furthermore, the project will ensure that the beneficiaries include disability segregation, and a % of PWD benefitting from the services is stated in both planned targets and reported outcomes2b. Exclusion and marginalization: (Women, girls, and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes) Mitigation measures- The project will ensure the provision of WASH facilities in a gender-sensitive manner i.e. ensure the provision of separate latrines for men and women in both the camp and the school. - Ensure provision of door locks to enable privacy. - HA MEAL team will review and update the Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) commitments, their implementation and reporting against the set indicators. Specific commitments include ensuring staff and local stakeholders are trained on AGD (Age, Gender and Diversity) approaches, assessments and responses to take into consideration the diverse needs of men, women, boys, and girls. - HA will ensure gender balance in the recruitment of project staff. - A gender and age analysis will be included in the project’s needs assessments and reflected in one or more of the project’s activities and outcomes. - 4d. Protection mainstreaming: (Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms)Mitigation measures1. The project will ensure engagement and participation of the target beneficiaries in the project's implementation/monitoring activities by having Focus Group Discussion with beneficiaries and interviews to access satisfaction with shallow wells, latrines constructed and training provided2. Mainstream the protection principles in all the project activities (proper beneficiary feedback mechanism, key messages that adopt the local context, community approaches) 3. Neutrality and impartiality are part of HA’s approach, and HA asserts its commitment to the humanitarian principles. HA has a communication strategy with beneficiaries that are designed to increase acceptance and understanding of the purpose of our activities with affected populations.
