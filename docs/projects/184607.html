Project Summary

According to HNO 2021 an estimated 1.4 million school aged children (45% girls) will be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021 to retain children in schools or enroll in schools, of these, 1,139,632 are children from non-displaced families and 300,398 are children from displaced families. 45% of which are girls need sustained equitable access to quality education in a protective learning environment. According to the Joint Multi-Cluster Needs Assessment (JMCNA 2020), Bakool and Hiran region are among the district with low enrolment rates. Many children in Bay and Bakool region are out schools due to the regions experiencing high levels of instability with continued conflict leading to displacement as well as access constraints exacerbating existing vulnerabilities couple with continuous drought that lead number of pastoral dropout due to unsustain livelihood. In addition to that the Education Cluster Children’s Voices survey across the country showed that 46% of the girls do not receive sanitary materials, 15% of the children reported that their schools do not have gender segregated latrines and 67% of the children reported that their schools do not have a girl friendly space. Baidoa, Ceelbarde, Rabdhure and Beletwein in Bay, Bakool and Hiran region respectively have all high number of children out of school leading to high number of school going children engaged in force labour as well as other child unfriendly activities within the communities, the poor an vulnerable school children specially boys have been lured in joining the militia group hence further increasing the risk of many children in these regions, accordingly, HIDIG managed schools in Ceelbarde and Yeed in Bakool region recorded a dropout rate of 10% due to severe drought that affected the community livelihood with no alternative feeding within the school since there were funding gap in this area, equally, Baidoa HIDIG managed schools recorded high enrollment rates estimated at 30% in the first quarter of 2021 when there school ration and provision of water as well as sanitary kits and learning material distribution however, a decline was noted in last quarter of 2021, this coupled with increased risk of COVID-19 that has destabilize learning in 2020 and 2021 as well as lack of school support both in terms of teaching learning materials as well as teachers incentives lead to poor learning in most of the schools in Bay, Bakool and Hiran region where HIDIG is currently having active programs. The project will ensure protection mechanism are in place for children including disable 10% of the target gender sensitive hygiene facilities, psychosocial support, teachers training and communities in identifying and preventing gender based violence against girls in school. The training and support of teacher and community education committee(CEC) will ensure to provide equal opportunity for men and women and will provide critical skills for duty bearers to identify how gender roles create risk for girls and challenge to their enrollment and attendance in schools, inequalities in classrooms and ensure that both boys, girls including disable children are able to access safe learning space in which they are protected from violence (both in schools and in the community).The project will employ several strategies to promote girls and boys education among the pastoral communities in Bakool, Bay and Hiran where HIDIG have active presence through social mobilization campaign, gender dis-aggregated WASH facilities in schools. The project will also focus on pedagogical component related to mobilization, establishments of 24 an/or rehabilitation temporary learning space, training and mentoring of 207 teachers and 90 community education committee, 24 children to child club and delivery of teacher’s incentives including teaching and learning materials with regular monitoring and supervision.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

HIDIG will ensure IDPs have access to a comprehensive access to services within the IDP camps by establishing temporary learning spaces in consultation with the host communities an education authority. Where not possible in areas like Ceelbarde and Rabdhure pastoral communities will be provided with mobile school where HIDIG will procure donkey and carts to ensure schools are integrated within the already existing dugsi to enable provide needed services. Based on the education Cluster Guideline on inclusion, both displaced and host communities will be consulted about needs and assess whether education interventions could cause tension, harassment or conﬂict between the IDPs and host communities. The following strict measures will be adhering to: 1. To support the inclusion of women teachers HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. To ensure equity, mobile schools will be used to reach rural communities 2. Location, distance and access routes to the education sites and services will be accessible to all members of the boys and girls, including the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women teachers HIDIG will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. 3. HIDIG will ensure learning space are constructed in far from military base to avoid the cross ﬁre between the militia group and the military thus reducing the risk of explosion and gender based violence against vulnerable girls.4.Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violence
