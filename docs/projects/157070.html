Project Summary

In 2019, continuous return dynamics from Chad and IDPs camp to villages in Darfur are likely to be observed. In parallel to this “normalization” process, localized and violent clashes are likely to occur particularly in Jebel Marra, causing massive displacements. The action aims at responding to both dynamics by providing emergency responses and durable solutions to be implemented in protracted contexts to develop access to basic services and build capacities of communities on facilities management while including a timely and appropriate response to new emergencies.
Based on this current situation, an integrated approach is required in order to address the most basic humanitarian needs to ensure an access to WASH, Food security and livelihoods, NFI/Shelters and Education services that will allow the beneficiaries to maintain or regain minimal dignity and mitigate the health, environmental and the other displacement/return related risks. Chronic deficiency of basic services is aggravated by the increased pressure on natural resources induced by new arrivals and the increase in prices of items of first necessity due to the current economic crisis. Return dynamic is expected to continue and to potentially increase in Central and West Darfur while new population displacements are expected considering the formal restart of the tripartite agreement for Sudanese refugees repatriation and also the persistence of a relatively stable security context .
The combination of protracted and urgent needs in Darfur calls for the humanitarian-development nexus, a core element of the project is therefore the development of sustainable approaches to support the process of returns started in 2016. As the security context has significantly improved in 2018 in most of the areas of CD and WD, longer-term solutions are needed to find viable exit strategies. Involvement of communities in the design of the action as well as in the management of facilities is a precondition for durability and a progressive phase out of humanitarian actors.
TGH therefore proposes an integrated project in line with following sectors:
- WASH: through the proposed project TGH will ensure both a timely emergency and sustainable WASH response to reduce mortality and morbidity and prevent disease to vulnerable, conflict or natural disaster affected communities of CD&WD.
- FSL: The project will aim at building livelihood opportunities, improving economic self-reliance, reducing food insecurity and vulnerability. The ongoing economic crisis is leading to the deterioration of livelihood opportunities for vulnerable HH in a WD&CD. The project will therefore aim at mitigating the economic issues related to the reduction of the HHs’ sources of income.
- NFI/IES: By enhancing the access to NFIs and shelters for vulnerable people, TGH will improve living conditions of new returnees, IDPs and conflict/natural disaster affected population in WD&CD.
- Education: In response to the lack of educational infrastructures and services TGH will support schools with hard (rehabilitation, furniture etc.) and soft (trainings; enrolment campaign, school gardening, school clubs, etc.) components. The empowerment of educations actors to mainstream conflict sensitivity and peacebuilding into education services will be increased as well (WD&CD).
- RRR sector: TGH will align its project with the durable solutions approach developed in 4 villages namely Aboujaradil, Marari, Salaley, Sorey (around 34,115 individuals) in order to support the self-sustainability and self-resilience of IDPs, returnees and host communities, in coordination with DSWG&RRR strategy. The intervention will be in line with TGH current RRR project implemented in Aboujaradil, Magan, Beltebei, Garaia since 2017 and up to 2019, and will be integrated in the action plan of each villages as developed last 2 years.
TGH will coordinate with other stakeholders on the ground in order to ensure the quality and efficiency of the reponse.

-WASH: The continuous presence of TGH in West and Central Darfur enabled the organization to evaluate the needs in term of WASH. TGH assessed the WASH situation in Kreinik locality (Nurei, Kony, Umtajook, Umseneina, Shawaya); Beida locality (field observations); Um Dukhun (Umsory; Malo; DarElsalam; Zontora; Seref Albukhus and field observations in Al Garaia, Karkar, Abougaradil, Seleyle, Sorey, Magan); Bindisi locality (Multi-sectorial assessment Samukidik, Erbee; Klombusana; Geger; Mujeli and Bindisi IDPs camp); Golo locality (field observations in Golo Town, Jokosty; Bari Ari; Dar El Salam and assessments in Jambow, Kawa, Foro Dogo, and Turtura and Yara). Main findings are : lack of sustainable water points providing safe drinking water; lack of latrines or sanitarian facilities leading to poor hygiene conditions; lack of good hygiene behavior; uncovered urgent needs related to natural disaster or conflict.-FSL: In Sudan, in July 2018 there were an estimated 6.2 million people acutely food insecure. This number doubled compared to last year. The FEWS forecasted that if in 2019 the economic situation does not improve, food prices are expected to rise further to 200-250%. Between March and May 2019, food security is likely to deteriorate further as the most vulnerable households begin to deplete their food stocks and become even more dependent on markets; the range of livelihood opportunities is likely to reduce particularly those related to agriculture ( due to fuel shortages, rising labor costs, etc.). The coping strategy of rural Sudanese will be then to move to non-agricultural employment, i.e. traditional mining, low-skilled labour, etc. but not providing them with increased income (source: FEWS- NET 12/2018_- ACAPS 02/2019). - NFI/IES: In the 2019 HNO, Um Dukhun, Kreinik and Golo localities are ranked 5th, the highest level on the localities vulnerability scale with 31 629 returnees (UMD); 25 358 IDPS (Kreinik) and 14 360 IDPs and 52 675 returnees (Golo) in need of NFI/ IES assistance. The instability of the context of Golo might led to urgent needs in NFI and IES. Through its assessments TGH identified 961 HH in need of NFI and that will be targeted through fairs; 200 indv. with special needs – reduced mobility targeted by special items adapted to their needs; 340 HH in need of IES.- EDUC: Main needs In Kreinik camp (WD): trainings for teachers, support for PTA to help reducing the school fees, support to students for stationaries and uniforms, support in school feeding, support in organizing recreational activities for students, sensitization of communities on education. In Golo locality, (early 2019 assessments): classrooms and latrines construction/rehabilitation, stationaries and uniforms, sensitization of the community on education. RRR: Continuous presence of TGH in Selelyle, Sorey and Abourajadil since 2017: shortages in seeds and agricultural tools; lack of WASH facilities and services and good Education services.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Darfur states are facing major environmental problems i.e. deforestation/ desertification, water scarcity, and regular natural disasters such as floods; heavy rains and droughts. Climate change and desertification have contributed to a reduction of trees and shrubs. Communities and their livelihoods are particularly vulnerable to these environmental shocks when the resources are scarce and/or unevenly distributed. The natural disasters also increase the vulnerability of the HH as they destroy houses; belongings, crops etc. The proposed project integrates environmental issues into its design and implementation in order to achieve positive outcomes and create environmental benefits. In general, reducing the pressure on natural resources is a mitigation measure as it can support livelihoods through income creation, nutrition and protection (by reducing lengthy walks for firewood collection) as well as safeguarding the drinking water quality and quantity. Main risks related to WASH/ NFI-Shelter/FSL/EDUC/ RRR activities: - Depletion of groundwater resources in some areas if recharging capacities of shallow aquifers are compromised by drought;- Running taps causing overuse of water- Environmental damage e.g. deforestation may be caused by not environmental friendly material e.g. not renewable wood - Important and not treated wastes that can impact children’s health- Environmental damage caused by non-ecofriendly construction material e.g. non local resources- Risk of soil and water contamination due to use/overuse of fertilizers and insecticides.- Planting of crops on unsuitable soil type causing loss of nutrients and overuse of water Main environmental mitigation measures implemented by TGH: - Before a borehole drilling, systematic geophysical survey assessing the potential yield in the area and its sustainability + coordination with the Water and Environmental Sanitation Department (WES)- Regular monitoring of groundwater table level, rehabilitation of existing water points to avoid drilling of new boreholes and location of emergency water points at appropriate distance to limit the risk of surface groundwater depletion in certain areas- Provision of environmentally friendly emergency shelter material (bamboo is a renewable resource in Central African Republic, origin of the procured items) to mitigate the risk of deforestation and land degradation in new settlement areas- Cleaning campaigns in and out of school - Construction of school with material provided locally- Support and train on environmentally friendly farming techniques- Dissemination of varieties of sorghum and millet adapted to the constraints of the environment in rural areas
