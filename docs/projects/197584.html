Project Summary

NRC’s project will contribute to the HRP Education Sector’s Objectives 1, 2, and 3. NRC will deliver this project through a combination of direct, partner-led, and hybrid modalities.
The Education intervention forms part of NRC’s safe, integrated, and inclusive response in Syria. NRC’s Education team will continue working in close collaboration with NRC’s ICLA/Capacity-building, Shelter, WASH, and Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS) programmes, to enable conflict- and displacement-affected populations to meet their basic needs, enjoy their rights, and benefit from pathways to durable solutions. NRC will deliver pathways back to learning, integration into formal education, acknowledgement of previous learning through supportive policies (accreditation, certification, examinations, and documentation), and expansion of skills for employment. NRC will respond to the diverse problems and needs faced by vulnerable children and youths across Syria through the delivery of its Child Education Pathways (CEP) and Youth Education Pathways (YEP) programmes. Each response modality (Emergency, Transition, and Recovery) is designed to ensure that conflict- and displacement-affected children and youths are provided with age and context-specific educational pathways. All three modalities have been designed to provide equal access and to welcome all out-of-school children and youths, prioritizing those that have been displaced by the conflict and are returning home from both inside and outside Syria. All three modalities include capacity development opportunities for teachers, trainers, facilitators and coaches, and will be implemented in cooperation with NRC’s ICLA/Capacity-building, Shelter, WASH, and LFS programmes and partners. The teacher training package has been adapted from the INEE Teachers in Crisis Context, and includes NRC’s Better Learning Programme (an evidence-based psychosocial support (PSS) intervention developed by NRC and the University of Tromso). Particular focus is paid to safe return to school after periods of COVID-related lockdowns: teacher circles will integrate the guidance included in UNICEF’s “Ready to go back: Teacher preparedness Training Package”.
The target group of NRC’s Education Programme in Syria is defined as conflict and displacement-affected children and youths, including Syrian IDPs, IDPs and refugee returnees, host community members, third country nationals, and Iraqi refugees. Exact target group definitions will vary for the different response options:
- Emergency response activities will target newly displaced and returnee children and youths;
- Transitional response will target out-of-school children and youths in contexts of long-term displacement and prolonged absence from school, with activities tailored on their specific context (see more below);
- Recovery activities will target vulnerable populations living in post-conflict and/or stabilized camp- and non-camp settings (see more below).
Teachers, facilitators, and trainers working in NRC programmes in formal and non-formal education settings, as well as vocational trainers, will also be targeted across the three response options. Whilst equal access will be provided to both male and female educators, considering the current scarcity of male educators, those present will be actively encouraged to participate, in an effort to provide positive male role models in all activities.
For all NRC Education activities, civil documentation will not be a pre-requisite to receive assistance. Parents’ engagement is fostered through awareness-raising campaigns, and the set-up of Parents and Teachers Associations.

3 - Needs

12 years into the Syrian crisis, there has been a 4% increase in the sector PIN since 2022, with 6.9M children and education personnel needing emergency education services (draft HNO 2023). Rates of non-attendance were highest in Al-Hasakeh (24%), Ar-Raqqa (22%), Deir-Ez-Zor (19%), Aleppo (16%), and Idleb (15%). Data also shows that if a child is displaced more than three times, there is a higher chance of them not attending education at all (20%), and being 42% less likely to attend in the first three years of displacement. Dropping out due to marriage is more of a barrier for IDPs in camps (5%) than in other population groups. Additional data sources estimate that there are over two million children (6-17) that are currently out-of-school in Syria. Children with disabilities do not attend school due to inaccessibility, an unconducive learning environment, and the lack of pedagogical skills to address their needs. The Education needs of Syrian children and adolescents are also worsened by the protracted crisis, economic duress, displacement, water shortage, and the ongoing cholera outbreak. Improving school conditions remains the highest priority for households (54%), followed by improving teachers’ ability to teach (44%), and providing cash to support Education expenses (38%). Teachers’ capacity and effectiveness are also constrained, with teachers not being paid regularly and/or an adequate amount, in addition to the lack of continuous professional development.

4 - Implementation of activities

CEP- & YEP-Emergency: designed to provide safe/welcoming spaces for children/youths when newly displaced. Includes life skills, psychosocial & recreational skills. CEP-Transition: non-formal learning opportunity for out-of-school children, preparing them to access education in formal settings. CEP-SLP: A non-formal programme for children who have been out of school for over a year to catch up on learning. CEP-in Camps/Communities: A non-formal programme targeting children without access to formal schools.YEP-Mobile Vocational Skills: 5–10-day competency-based training modules providing opportunities for unemployed male & female youths to develop new skills. Creating Welcoming Schools: Rehabilitation of learning spaces, teacher professional development, in-kind materials, Student Councils & Parent Teacher Associations, NFI kits. YEP 3+3: 3-month competency-based training, followed by 3 months of coaching, targeting unemployed male & female youths for safe & sustainable livelihood. CEP-Remedial: A 3-6-month programme for children recently returned to school at risk of dropout. YEP-Transition: A 3–4-month programme for youths who are preparing for Grade 9 & 12 exams. PSS activities are integrated in all programs. NRC pathways are integrated and available for distance learning.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Exclusion of Children/Youth with disabilities who cannot access education centers. MM: Learning facilities improved to ensure ease of access to PwD; Staff and teachers trained; Coordination with specialized actors and training to teams on inclusion; Complaints/Feedback mechanisms for students to voice their recommendations or concerns. 2) Children and Female Youth might be exposed to the risk of, or experience, physical harm, bullying or harassment. MM: PSEA policies in place and staff and teacher awareness on such policies; Children/Youth have access to reporting systems; Field monitoring systems in place; Children/Youth learning sessions divided based on different age groups; Youth learning sessions divided as culturally appropriate; Complaints/Feedback and Feedback mechanisms for students to voice their recommendations or concerns. 3) Learning facilities and surroundings, including sanitation facilities, might be structurally unsafe or do not provide sufficient protection. MM: Facilities are improved by specialized technical staff (e.g. Shelter); Sufficient WASH facilities provided and maintained, gender and age segregated; Complaints/Feedback and Feedback mechanisms for students.4) General insecurity, surrounding damaged buildings and water/energy scarcities around/in schools rehabilitated by NRC, disrupt a safe and dignified learning environment. MM: Technical assessment/selection tools consider such risks; relevant WASH action is integrated; regular risk analysis.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Potential SEA risks are identified during beneficiary selection process, the distribution of assistance and while accessing services. NRC communicates clearly with communities on the expected assistance modalities and confidential complaint and feedback channels available to them. During post-delivery monitoring (e.g. PDMs), NRC inquires if people were asked to exchange anything for aid or service. In order to reduce perceptions of power, NRC asks in PDMs who, according to the recipients of aid, provided the assistance they received, enabling identification of possible areas of concern. All NRC staff and non-staff are bound by NRC's Code of Conduct, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and child safeguarding (CSG) policies. Several sessions take place with staff to raise awareness on PSEA and CSG, in addition to sessions for contractors. All NRC teams have to complete mandatory online PSEA and safeguarding essentials e-courses as part of their induction. NRC's Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is open and sensitive to SEA and CSG reports, which are managed by a dedicated team in Head Office with support from regional and country office teams who regularly updates guidance to emerging needs and trends to tailor management and support to cases. Child Friendly CFM is now fully integrated in both North West and North East Syria. Child Friendly PSEA materials are also made available to the project teams.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. NRC staff are injured, killed, or suffer psychological trauma due conflict: Mitigation: Security briefings, HEAT and HEIST trainings, briefings, more support from UNMAS.2. Fraud/corruption/bribery. Mitigation: enforcing NRC's policy of zero tolerance through strengthening of procurement and operating procedures, promote the "whistle-blowing" policy, corruption risk mapping, ensure partners implement anti-corruption measures. 3. NRC is unable to timely operate due to the impact of internationals sanctions. Mitigation: request for derogation, looking for different banking channels, advocacy.4. NRC is unable to timely implement due to delays in approvals from Government/local authorities. Mitigation: Advocacy plan in place, set the lead time for approval submission to be 2 weeks with a close follow.5. NRC is unable to timely deliver the planned activities due to rapid inflation and change of prices. Mitigation: review budget and targets on a quarterly basis, communication with the donors, find more convenient payment terms to suppliers.6. Donor policies, including overly restrictive redlines and perceptions, affect the ability to implement and scale-up programs. Mitigation: Engaging with donor, Joint advocacy efforts with DINGOs, Intensify the negotiations with local partner and MOUs, Long terms programs and reconstructions, media coverage in private and public advocacy.7. COVID-19 Restrictions: SOPs for safe implementation during COVID-19; adapted implementation.
