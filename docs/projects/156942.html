Project Summary

The main objective of the proposed project is to improve the living conditions of extremely vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host community members within Deir-ez-Zor and Daraa governorates, by providing life-saving and life-sustaining shelter support. To create adequate shelter solutions and increase the safety and protection of those families, whose shelter has been severely damaged by the conflict. Areas with high rates of displacement and large-scale destruction within Deir-ez-Zor and Daraa governorates have been targeted for the rehabilitation of 1000 shelters and for the distribution of essential relief items (shelter kits).
The consequences of the conflict have been particularly severe in both targeted governorates and have converted many parts of Deir-ez-Zor and Daraa into extremely hard-to-reach areas, where people suffer from extreme needs, due to the enormous destruction and inability to access the local markets. To support the conflict affected people in those areas, the intervention will include the rehabilitation of existing shelters that are below minimum standards, and the provision of shelter kits to allow beneficiaries to perform repairs on their own shelters.
Based on the cluster approved vulnerability criteria, the extremely vulnerable, newly and long-term displaced IDP families, returnees and host-community-members have been targeted for this response. A specific focus will be on female and adolescence headed households, households with high levels of dependent minors, disabled and elderly. To ensure participation and equal access RSRP will apply a gender sensitive approach and consider specific needs and protection concerns of different vulnerable groups and categories. Detailed needs assessments, continuous communication and involvement of community members and beneficiaries into the planning and implementation process, regular monitoring and periodic evaluations will ensure the provision of the best quality of works possible.

Needs

The Syrian crisis has become the worst humanitarian crisis in modern history. Eight years of conflict and violence have caused extreme suffering, devastated many cities and regions in the country and destroyed large parts of the basic infrastructure. Almost 70% of the population lives in extreme poverty. 13.1Million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance. Of these, 4.7 million people are in need of shelter support due to a convergence of vulnerabilities resulting from displacement, exposure to hostilities, and limited access to basic goods and services The hardship in the northeast region of the country, especially in Deir-ez-Zor is overwhelming and more severe than in all other Syrian governorates. The very recent liberation of the Governorate from ISIS occupation has shown the extreme devastation caused by numerous battles, inhabitants being cut off from national markets, limited services and infrastructure and the ongoing threat of armed confrontations and attacks have led to 710.000 inhabitants of Deir-ez-Zor being in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. Similar conditions have caused approximately 600.000 people to depend on humanitarian assistance in Daraa. An estimated 4.7 million people remain in need of shelter support in Syria, representing an increase of 14 per cent from 2018. This includes those in need of emergency assistance due to a lack of any basic shelter, millions currently in temporary hosting arrangements, and people living in damaged, crowded, unfinished or otherwise inadequate shelters who urgently need more durable shelter solutions. The increase has been driven primarily by an increase of severity of need in high-density urban population centers.Even though displacement continues in some areas of Deir-ez-Zor, many IDPs have further expressed the wish of returning to their place of origin. By creating dignified and durable shelter solutions the proposed project will also facilitate safe and sustainable returns of displaced families and contribute to the relief of overburdened host communities.

Implementation of activities

RSRP will rehabilitate 1000 damaged housing units (apartments and houses) and distribute 1000 shelter kits with basic items to support those IDPs and returnee families who have lost their homes and belongings. The works will comprise of restoration of the roof, walls, floors, windows and doors. Furthermore, civil works will be implemented to fix pipes, wires, gas lines, etc. to create access to electricity, water and sanitation and sufficient heating.The implementation of such activities will include the following:Rehabilitation of damaged houses:1- Send/receive approvals from local authorities.2- Determine the exact location of neighborhoods in need of rehabilitation.3- Conduct a detailed needs and technical assessment, to assess the damages in every apartment and the general information about the people in need of rehabilitation.4- Prepare all technical documents (BOQs, Technical specifications for rehabilitation…)5- Conduct procurement process and select contractor/s to start the rehabilitation.6- Rehabilitate damaged apartments according to the BOQs and technical specifications through tendering and contracting.7- Handover apartments to owners and residents.8- Conduct post rehabilitation survey to measure results against outputs, outcomes and overall objective and ensure the affected people’s satisfaction.Distribution of Shelter kits:1- Send/receive approvals from local authorities.2- Determine the location of houses with slight damages and in need of shelter kits to make small repairs.3- Prepare technical specifications for shelter kits.4- Conduct procurement process and select supplier/s to provide shelter kits.5- Install shelter kits in the damaged houses by RSRP field staff.6- Completion and close out of the project.7- Conduct post project survey to measure results against outputs, outcomes and overall objective and ensure the affected people’s satisfaction in the shelter kits and the installation process.All the above-mentioned measures and processes will be translated in assessments, surveys, monitoring and evaluation reports and photos that will be shared with donors, partners and relevant clusters to participate in the humanitarian coordination process.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Vulnerable gender and age groups (women, children, disabled and elderly) may be less visible and have less capacity or power to manage their self-recovery and access to the assistance. – HIGH Mitigation Measure 1: Identification and inclusion of those vulnerable groups and their specific needs into the project planning and implementation. Prioritization of women and youth headed households during the beneficiary-targeting process.Monitoring and Evaluation: Include gender and age disaggregation within the collected data, determine if all age and gender groups are receiving the support in monitoring visits and evaluation reports.Required resources: Monitoring and evaluation team, program and planning team. Protection Risk 2: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers. – LOW Mitigating measures: Inform and train humanitarian workers on all aspects related to protection.Obligate humanitarian workers to adhere to a code of conduct.Establish an efficient and effective complaints mechanism.Monitoring and Evaluation: Conduct satisfaction surveys to ensure beneficiaries are content with the distribution process. Conduct regular door to door visitsRequired Resources: Monitoring and Evaluation team, distribution coordinator. Protection Risk 3: Shelter Kits can lack essential items, which the beneficiary might need for shelter repairs. LOWMitigation Measure 3: Consultation with community to assess which items are most needed by the households. Check availability of the items on local markets and include items that might be useful for future interventions.Monitoring and Evaluation: Conduct satisfaction surveys to ensure beneficiaries are content with the delivered support.Required resources: Monitoring and Evaluation team, program team. Protection Risk 4:Tension within the community, due to some members not being targeted within the intervention HIGHMitigation measure 3: Clear and comprehensible communication of the selection criteria to the community, community leaders and beneficiaries.Monitoring and Evaluation: A solid Monitoring and Evaluation process to oversee the procedures related to technical assessment and selection of damaged houses.Required resources: Monitoring and Evaluation team, experienced and qualified field staff. Protection Risk 5:Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/ to lack of HLP 8 housing, land and property) documentation. – Medium Mitigation Measure 9: Shelter rehabilitation will be based on the explicit consensus of the owner, the proof of registered HLP ownership and/or verified ownership claims.Monitoring and Evaluation: A solid monitoring and evaluation process to conduct assessments and analyze the data prior rehabilitation.Required resources: Experienced and qualified monitoring and evaluation team.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Access limitations could prevent the humanitarian actors to provide assistance to the affected population in a timely manner. – LOWMitigation Measure 1: Constant monitoring of the security situation on the ground and capacity to mobilize all resources into another project area if required. Risk 2: Threat to the safety of RSRP field-staff through confrontations between armed groups in the project region. – MED Mitigation Measure 2: Application of RSRP-internal Standard Operation Procedures (SOP) and daily evaluation of the security situation. Risk 3: Significant population growth in the target areas leading to insufficient assistance. – HIGH Mitigation measure 4: Consider feedback from monitoring reports and situational overviews of the area and coordinate regular coordination meetings with local authorities for further and more detailed up-dates. In case of a change in the situation in DZ and Daraa, an open and direct dialogue with donors and other humanitarian actors should be initiated to increase capacities in the region. Risk 4: Delayed, denied or revoked GOS approvals which prevent delivering the assistance in a timely manner or prevent implementation of the project.Likelihood: HighMitigating Measures: - Transparency with local authorities in the areas of intervention, in addition to strong communication and collaboration prior to project implementation with local partner. Risk 5: Unavailability of materials on the local markets due to closure of the border for commercial shipments or not renewal of SC resolution. - MEDIUMMitigation Measure 6: Detailed market analysis in the area of intervention and development of contingency plan. Risk 6: Depletion of the market and inflation of the prices. - MEDIUMMitigation Measure 7: Price monitoring and market assessments done by the RSRP logistics department. Risk 7: Rehabilitated houses can turn into overcrowded living spaces, due to absence of adequate services or increased return movements and/or new displacements. – MEDIUMMitigation Measure 8: Provision of adequate space for each targeted family and post-rehabilitation monitoring.
