Increase access to and utilization of integrated emergency lifesaving primary health care services to affected population of Aweil East, Aweil North, (Northern Bhar El Ghazal state), Renk and Melut Counties (Upper Nile state) and Abyei Administrative Area

Basic Info

Project Name

Increase access to and utilization of integrated emergency lifesaving primary health care services to affected population of Aweil East, Aweil North, (Northern Bhar El Ghazal state), Renk and Melut Counties (Upper Nile state) and Abyei Administrative Area

Start Date

01/01/2024

End Date

31/12/2025

Project Summary

ARDF proposed intervention will support two primary health care facilities including Abayok PHCU in Renk, Agordit PHCU in Melut, and 10 mobile medical units (MMUS), two per each targeted counties to reach vulnerable population in hard-to-reach locations, and each supported fix facilities and MMUs will be equipped with eight qualified health workers including a clinical officer, two registered midwives, two nurse, community health workers (CHW), and two EPI vaccinators to provide lifesaving health care services. Alongside MMU, ARDF will increase health care coverage at grassroot level by establishing Boma Health Initiative (BHI) structures comprises of Boma health Workers (BHWs) to undertake treatment of under 5 years top morbidities including malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhea, and community screen of children 6-59 months for malnutrition using MUAC tape and children who defaulted from immunization and refer them for services.
ARDF will equip Health facilities, MMU and BHI with essential drugs, equipment, and medical supplies. ARDF will procure and distribute essential drugs, medical equipment, and medical supplies to the fix facilities, MMUs and BHI supported. MMU teams and Boma health workers will also be equipped with protective gear including gumboots, raincoats, back bags enabling them access villages during rain or floods and provided with metallic trunks for storage of supplies. MMUs will be provided with portable furniture, mats for patients to sit on and locally made screens to provide privacy.
ARDF will provide a range of lifesaving primary health care packages comprises of, Curative consultation, testing of malaria and syphilis using rapid diagnostic test kits(RDT), integrated management of childhood illnesses(IMCI), screening of children for malnutrition and refer malnourished children to outpatient therapeutic sites, routine immunization services, antenatal care services, skilled delivery, Postnatal care, PMTCT, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) services, Clinical management of rape, health education, integrated community case management (iCCM/BHI), mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, integrated Disease surveillance and prompt response to disease outbreak, distribution of LLINs, strengthen infection prevention and control(IPC) and integrate WASH services in the facilities.
ARDF will build the capacity of health care providers to improve on their skills and quality of health care services; topics to be trained include; integrated disease surveillance and response (IDSR), integrated management of neonatal and childhood illnesses (IMNCI), immunization in practice (IIP), Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC), Mental health Gap action program (mhGAP), SGBV/CMR, and use of malaria rapid diagnostic test (mRDT), Boma health initiative (BHI) training for BHWs and infection prevention and control.
The ARDF project team will regularly conduct monitoring of activities through monthly supportive supervision and monthly beneficiary satisfaction survey at the exit points in the facilities and MMUs. ARDF will establish accountability mechanism for the affected population through installation of suggestion boxes, and monthly community feedback meetings to document beneficiaries’ complaints and feedback, where actions points are documented and implemented to improve quality of services. Quarterly program review meeting will be conducted to review the progress of the project, where documenting bottlenecks and actions are drawn for improvement. ARDF will equip all facilities, MMUs and BHI with Ministry of health registers for data collection and reporting. Health workers will be trained on how to use the tools. ARDF MEAL team will be trained on how to report the data through activity info and DHIS2. The MEAL team will be reporting IDSR reports weekly through EWARs.

1. Needs

About 8.9 million people in the country need one or more forms of humanitarian assistance, especially in the Counties most affected by floods, conflicts, returnees, and disease outbreaks (SSD HNO 2023). Upper Nile, Abyei and Northern Bahar El Gazal states continue to face severe humanitarian crisis due to multiple shock including intercommunal conflicts, flooding, drought, and access impediment to services, placing the population in a dire need of health care services. The current influx of returnees and refugees to South Sudan from Sudan crises have further stressed on the existing health services which call for lifesaving emergency health intervention. The health system in Renk and Melut Counties of Upper Nile, Aweil North, Aweil East Counties of Northern Bahar El Gazal, and Abyei is unable to cope with the emergencies, with health services provision relying heavily on humanitarian partners. According to the IRNA reports conducted on 20th September 2023 and 5th July 2023 in Renk and Melut Counties respectively, access to health services remain limited due to long distance to reach the facilities, flooding, and lack of essential drugs and medical supplies in functional facilities. Further, Medair Assessment report of May 2023, confirm that, the population of Aweil East and Aweil North counties are facing challenges of accessing health care services due to long distances to reach some health facilities, lack of essential drugs and medical supplies, lack of outreach strategies to expand health care coverage, poor referral support for severe cases in hard-to-reach locations. In Abyei, continuous intercommunal conflicts between the Ngok Dinka and Misseriya Community, and Ngok Dinka with Community of Twic County of Warrap State have led to the continues interruption of services provided to the population (UN Secretary Report 1st May 2023, Page 8, Point 39). This calls for lifesaving health intervention.Immunization coverage in Upper Nile and Northern Bahar El Bahar remains significantly low evidenced by the recurrent outbreak of measles in Renk, Melut Maban and Aweil North. This has put children under 5-year risk of vaccines preventable diseases. In addition, access to maternal health care services is a challenge with low coverage of antenatal care services during 4th visit, low coverage of Skilled deliveries, and postnatal care (DHIS2 Data). There remain significant protection concerns in Renk and Melut counties of upper Nile, Abyei and greater Aweil Counties due to the recurrent intercommunal conflict, and the influx of Returnees and Refugee from Sudan crises placing Women, girls and children in extremely multiple protection risks including psychological distress, sexual gander-based violence (SGBV), and other abuses. Lack of CMR services due to limited access to health care services. To address these needs, the proposed intervention intends to increase access to and utilization of essential health care services by population hard to reach communities and displaced population by conflicts or floods in Renk and Melut Counties of upper Nile, Aweil East, and Aweil North of Norther Bahar El Gazal, and Abyei to reduce excess morbidity and mortality. The intervention will response through primary health care facilities including PHCCs and PHCUs, and Mobile medical unit (MMUs) to reach the most vulnerable communities with no access to health services. Boma health Workers will be established to provide health care services at grassroot area. The Services provided encompasses, child health, maternal health, treatment of injuries, early outbreak detection and timely response to, adequate surveillance systems through Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) and Early Warning Alert System (EWARS), EVD prevention, surveillance, and response readiness to mitigate Ebola outbreak. Improve Referral for mental health and psychosocial support, sexual and gender-based violence survivors, and for maternal and child health emergencies.
