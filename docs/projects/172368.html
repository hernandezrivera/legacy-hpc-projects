Project Summary

UNHAS will aim to maintain an air-transport service to all humanitarian organizations operating across Libya, with focus on main hubs such as Tripoli, Benghazi and Sabha, based on operational bottlenecks, humanitarian access and presence, in order to facilitate movements and projects implementation within the country.

1 - Needs

The COVID-19 crisis is predicted to have enormous consequences – it concerns the health sector as well as all other sectors due to the wide socio-economic consequences. The aviation sector is alsoimpacted. WFP is constantly reviewing its air operation procedures to make them as safe and effectiveas possible, in line with best practice, in a dynamic situation where the spread of the virus andmeasures taken by countries to combat it are changing by the day. WFP is keenly aware about the need for humanitarians to reach Libya, to continue their work and help limit the impact of the pandemic on lives and livelihoods, however, this must be weighed against any additional risks. UNHAS is ready to enable movement of vital equipment and staff that can rapidly reach the areas where they are most needed, as without these essential logistics services, the response to COVID-19 will be at risk.Due to the unpredictable security situation on the ground, travel by air has become an essential need. Implementation of the 2021 Libya Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) depends on reliable air services into Libya. With the lifting of the evacuation status, humanitarian actors have the opportunity to be consistently present in Libya, which is crucial. As a humanitarian aid enabler, UNHAS aligns its services to the needs of user organizations to support them in implementing their respective mandates through the operation of regular flights or even ad-hoc flights when required by user organizations. This enhances a more effective delivery of all sector’s life-saving humanitarian assistance but also reinforce the capacities of local staff and local partners to implement humanitarian actions through face-to-face interaction with international staff in the country. Increased access will also boost user organizations’ assessment capacities. In order to support all sectors of needed humanitarian assistance, and enable access for all UN agencies, NGOs and diplomatic missions to the populations need and implementation sites, UNHAS provides a safe, reliable and efficient passenger air service to the humanitarian organizations operating across Libya. Established in 2018, UNHAS supported humanitarian response in Libya and enabled organization to reach more people in need. In 2021, given the COVID-19 and the ongoing instability, humanitarian needs are expected to increase and UNHAS intends to enhance access to beneficiaries and project implementation areas in Libya.

2 - Activities

UNHAS contributes to improve access for all humanitarian and development actors in Libya, which represents one of the four pillars of Protection Mainstreaming. Through this action, UNHAS indirectly contributes to the three other pillars (Prioritization of safety and dignity while doing no harm, Accountability and Participation and Empowerment of Beneficiaries) by supporting all organisations in accomplishing their mandate. UNHAS, as a neutral entity, continues to communicate with all the parties involved in the region, in order to continue guaranteeing safe and secure access into the country, which is crucial for humanitarian organizations, including UN agencies, NGOs and donor entities that will support vulnerable people within Libya.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

Indeed, security-driven access limitations, rapidly changing situations and shifts in areas of control, hard to reach locations are impacting the capacity of the humanitarian actors to meet the increasing needs of the most vulnerable people. Commercial air travel options into Libya exist, but are prone to disruption and do not reliably provide the required reach. Moreover, travel via commercial airlines is avoided by NGO workers, International UN staff and diplomatic missions for security reasons. Security all over Libya is particularly volatile. The country is fragmented between several antagonist entities and roads are controlled by armed groups. This exacerbated security context means humanitarian workers must reinforce their security procedures for their interventions: UNHAS remains the primary reliable way to reach the most affected areas.The service, as a neutral entity, continues to communicate with all the parties involved in the region, including the Civil Aviation offices in Tunis, Tripoli and Benghazi, the actors involved in this conflict and the humanitarian and development community in Libya, in order to continue guaranteeing safe and secure access into the country. UNHAS developed a deconfliction mechanism in order to ensure the security conditions are met for flying, taking off and landing in Libya. This deconfliction mechanism is done with both GNA and LNA counterparts. The service proved to be reliable for the community working in both sides of the conflict as, even with the difficulties connecting the east and west, UNHAS remained functioning and connecting both operational areas.
