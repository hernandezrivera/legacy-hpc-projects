Project Summary

Somalia remains one of the most complex and long-standing humanitarian crises in the world. The year 2021 the protracted crises in Somalia, now compounded by the effects of Covid-19, Floods and droughts has resulted in massive challenges pertaining protection. According to Somalia protection cluster, Conflict and insecurity have forced hundreds of thousands of people to flee their homes in 2021 and are expected to remain key drivers of displacement in 2022. Conflict-induced shocks exacerbate the humanitarian situation of both IDP and host communities, with increased numbers of children married earlier as a coping strategy.
The proposed strategy seeks to address the growing humanitarian crisis in southern Somalia , Dialog Forening is proposing the following interventions to expand lifesaving child protection services to vulnerable populations in Gedo and Lower Juba regions
the project seeks to strengthen case management through direct service provision to children at risk in lower Juba and Gedo region , specifically the project will target vulnerable under age groups affected by the recurring humanitarian situation .these vulnerable groups will comprise of unaccompanied and separated children, children with disabilities, those exhibiting severe signs of distress, and children associated with armed forces and groups (CAAFAG). The interventions will offer direct services like Identification, Documentation, Tracing and Reunification (IDTR) of the primary duty-bearers if in the best interest of each individual child, reintegration into the community for children formerly associated with armed groups (including psychosocial first aid- PFA) and Psychosocial support to both children, caregivers in addition to victim support and referral services for where a holistic approach is necessary. Project beneficiaries will receive comprehensive case management and psycho-social support (PSS) – including emergency care, referral to other actors for services not offered by Dialog Forening, access tailored material assistance, then follow up on all services (both directly provided and those received during/after referrals). Capacity building of duty bearers (including formal and informal authorities) and child protection committees to strengthen and improve identification and provision of child protection services. Considering current approaches, the emphasis is on scaling up prevention and response services to protection-related risk for children (including vulnerable caregivers to improve the protective environment and the quality of care).

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

DF has a good standing with communities where we work. On the basis of this, DF has a fair understanding of the current sensitivities surrounding ‘conflict prevention’ in Somalia. Recognizing these complexities and sensitivities, DF has developed risk mitigation strategies that have been utilized to enable project implementation. First, DF employs national staff who understand the local context and are well known and respected by local authorities. DF national staff can access locations that non-Somali nationals cannot access, hence ensuring continuous project implementation and monitoring even in challenging contexts. Further, DF also employs remote monitoring systems to follow up ad monitor projects where physical access is a challenge. Second is a deliberate approach to project branding: selecting language that will explain the project fairly and accurately, in a way that will be acceptable to stakeholders. Third, is an approach of open, ongoing stakeholder engagement involving DF, local communities and government. Regular security updates will be sought from International NGO safety organization security teams to ensure that staff are well informed on security situation for necessary precautions and arrangements.The emergency response will (as far as local conditions allow) meet minimum SPHERE standards, work within the framework of Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) and Do No Harm (DNH) to ensure beneficiaries wholesomely participate in project planning and implementation. The program will promote empowerment of women by creating greater opportunity for their involvement in key institutions and decision-making processes for instance in the child protection committees
