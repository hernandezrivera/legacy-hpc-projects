Project Summary

The project will take place in Harim, Al-Dana, Jisr-Alshugur districts – Idleb and Atareb district- Aleppo

(1050 formal education – 900 nonformal education - 93 schools’ staff)
This project will support the educational activities through establishing 5 TLSs inline with cluster guidance and DP action plan for dignified living condition while considering access of CwDs, cultural and context appropriateness, and safe and equitable access for girls to provide children with the chance to continue their studies and ensure access to quality formal and non-formal education by providing incentives and capacity building for teachers and education personnel.
The project will focus on out-of-school children getting them back to education and will provide unconditional cash aid (monthly $5) in exchange for commitment to education in schools (attendance rate of more than 80%) while adhering to CWG and EC CVA guidance.
The project contributes to the Education Sector’s HRP objectives through:
1- Scale up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education in Harim, Al-Dana, Jisr-Alsahogour districts – Idleb governate and Al-Atareb districts - Aleppo governate age (6- 17) by establishing and operating 5 temporary Education Centers.
2- Enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and adolescents (aged 6-17 years) within a protective environment and raise the capacity of teachers and education personnel through several needed training and coordination with the education directorate.
The project targeted the camps area within Harim, Jisr-Alshugur, and Dana districts in Idleb and Atareb districts - Aleppo governate through the following activities:
1- 5 TLSs will be established and supported by providing supplies and equipment
2- Provide staff salaries and running costs, and heating fuel.
3- Ensure minimum package in schools is provided, including WASH & Cholera (enough safe water and soup...etc., hygiene promotion and awareness, child-friendly gender-friendly WASH facilities), and CP (MHPSS and referral)
4- 1050 students will receive formal education as well as receiving monthly cash aid based on their attendance.
5- 900 students will receive nonformal education: remedial classes using SiG curriculum; and catch-up and literacy and numeracy programs using curriculum B to ensure achieving learning outcomes according to cluster guidance.
6- 93 school staff will be provided with incentives and capacity building.
7- Transition from nonformal to formal will be ensured by making assessments for children who complete the classes and moving them to formal school if they are eligible.
The project will increase the boys’ and girls’ access to formal and non-formal education and both will access these activities for children and adolescents (6 – 17)
The project will ensure the inclusion of boys and girls with disabilities by ensuring TLSs and their facilities are accessible by CWD according to the cluster guidance (e.g. adapted WASH facilities, height and color of boards, adding ramps, in addition to training teachers and PSS team on inclusion topics...etc.)
MEAL activities: conducting daily field visits to ensure the process is being implemented accurately. Photos and videos will be taken for each of the monitored activities. MEAL team will work on corrective action tracing tables updated on monthly basis to enhance the quality of activities. Interviews with beneficiaries, and stakeholders will be analyzed to assess the achievement of the project and measure the impact of the intervention. FGDs will be conducted with the children, parents, and staff to get in-depth information and feedback about the project activities. IPTT will be monthly updated.
- The project fund is not secured yet.

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, 6.9M children and education personnel needing emergency education services. Over two million children (6-17) are out of school. The transition to secondary school remains a challenge with five governorates having less than 10 per cent of children aged 6-17 years attending secondary school. Non-attendance rates were highest in Al-Hasakeh (24 per cent), Ar-Raqqa (22 per cent), Deir-ez-Zor (19 per cent), Aleppo (16 per cent) and Idleb (15 per cent). Data show that if a child is displaced more than three times, there is a higher chance of them not attending education (20 per cent), and 42 per cent are less likely to attend in the first three years of displacement. Dropout due to marriage is more of barrier for IDPs in camps (5 per cent) than other population groups. The main reason for children not wanting to go to school is that they do not perceive it as useful or insufficient learning is taking place (66 per cent). The dominant form of education is in-person formal learning (98 per cent), with only two per cent ofchildren attending non-formal education. Schools have limited absorption and retention capacity, and lack flexibility to accommodatelearnersm, and suffer from overcrowding, lack of school furniture/school supplies, and inadequate heating, lighting and operational WASH facilities and teachers are not being paid regularly and/or an adequate amount.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Beneficiaries are at risk on being exposed to any sexual misconduct by the humanitarian workers and they might not report the misconduct due to the following reason: 1. lack of knowledge of reporting mechanisms, especially for people with disabilities and women living in far away areas. 2. feeling afraid of retaliation. 3. fear of stigma. 4. fear of deprivation of assistance. 5. being unable to understand written banners for being illiterate and/or for having a visual disability. 6. for children, language might be complicated To mitigate these risks, SDI will : 1. aim at clearly announcing the hotline number and email specialized for PSEA of SDI and UN numbers. through many means, like, banners, social media, and messages to beneficiaries phones through telegram channel to ensure reaching to the biggest number of beneficiaries including women living in far away areas and people with disabilities. 2. Conduct PSEA training for all staff members and contractors/suppliers dealing with beneficiaries and make them read and sign CoC that state Zero Tolerance towards SEA violations. 3. Conduct awareness raising sessions on PSEA for beneficiaries to encourage them to report any violations and stress on the confidentiality and on eliminating any other fears that they might have. 4. Conduct awareness raising sessions for children in an appropriate language. 5. complaint boxes 6. Using PSEA field and organizational checklists tools.
