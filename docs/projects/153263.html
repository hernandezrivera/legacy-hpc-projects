Project Summary

The project will contribute at increasing timely and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene services to 15,000 (women, men, girls and boys) in emergency affected populations in Magwi, Torit and Lafon counties of Eastern Equatoria State. HCAWFO has been responding in Eastern Equatoria State to the ongoing humanitarian crisis since the onset of the conflict in the country. The following are the main objectives of the planned project;
• To ensure that the affected populations are provided with safe, equitable, and sustainable access to sufficient quantity of water for drinking, cooking, and personal and domestic hygiene (15 L/p/day).
• To ensured that affected populations have access to safe, sanitary, and hygienic living environment through provision of sanitation services that are secure, sanitary, user friendly and gender appropriate.
• To ensure that affected populations have reduced risk of WASH related diseases, or negative impacts on nutritional status, through access to improved hygienic practices, hygiene promotion and delivery of hygiene products and services on a sustainable and equitable basis.
• To ensure WASH related GBV is mitigated through this intervention.
HCAWFO's response strategies:
• HCAWFO will support WASH Cluster response in payams of critical malnutrition in Magwi, Torit and Lafon counties by ensuring provision of a minimum standard of improved basic WASH services in the areas. The activities include; comprehensive rehabilitation of hand pumps, distribution of WASH NFIs, improving latrines, and hygiene promotion campaigns both at institutional and community levels,
• HCAWFO will ensure that WASH services, specifically in regards to latrine construction, incorporate the minimum safety and privacy considerations with use of the WASH Cluster Latrine Checklist, developed with the GBV Sub-Cluster.
• HCAWFO will work with WASH Cluster and NFI, Logs and other clusters to ensure adequate supplies are strategically prepositioned in the field to respond to new displacement and potential disease outbreaks through the 2019 rainy season.
• HCAWFO will maintain its active participation in the WASH Cluster meetings both at the state and National levels where ideas and project updates are shared and monthly WASH reports are submitted.
• HCAWFO will encourage active participation of the local community including women and children in the implementation of the proposed project. through Consultation of local authorities at all levels, women, men, boys and girls in the selection of the project payams within the county based on the identified needs. Hygiene promoters including hand pump technicians will include both host community and IDPs men, women, boys and girls in the targeted project sites in the county. The local authorities, host community and IDPs men, women, girls and boys will be involved in the design of the community and institutional sanitation facilities putting into consideration the need for privacy for females for the case of latrines and bath rooms construction.
• HCAWFO has been also delivering emergency WASH services in Hai Miaro one of the hot spots for cholera outbreak in Torit since 2016 to date. The key activities being delivered and HCAWFO would like to continue providing in Torit include; hygiene promotion, solid waste management, improving emergency latrines and bathrooms to semi-permanent structures, and improving drainage at the water and sanitation facilities.

Needs

The conflict that erupted in 2016 affected communities in Eastern Equatoria State. Magwi, Torit and Lafon counties have remained consistently below minimum sphere standards in WASH infrastructure and services due to the conflicts, leaving vulnerable communities with little resilience to withstand the chronic and acute crises that has affected the country since December 2013. The conflicts have forced thousands to flee their homes and seek relative safety in various locations including UNMISS premises in South Sudan and as conflict becomes protracted, host communities remain vulnerable with limited coping abilities to withstand the shocks. Among the states directly and badly affected by the crisis included Eastern Equatoria State. Different groups left their places at different times since the beginning of the crisis, to present. With the recent signing of revitalized peace agreement between the government forces and the SPLM-IO groups, it is likely that there will be a continued influx of IDPs/returnee in targeted locations. The displaced persons who are mainly women and children in these counties remain vulnerable to high level of contracting water and sanitation related sicknesses. Magwi and Lafon is one the counties in Eastern Equatoria State that had inadequate WASH services even before the outbreak of the conflict in the State, and the situations has worsened with the ongoing conflict in the country as most WASH infrastructures have been impaired especially the community hand pumps and sanitation facilities both at the household and public places like health facilities, schools and the local authorities in the area have estimated that only 20% to 25% of the community hand pumps are functional . Poor and bad hygiene practices among the IDPs and the host community in these counties is one of the biggest challenges as nothing is being done to equip the IDPs and the host community with basic knowledge of good hygiene practices. Magwi, Torit and Lafon are some of the counties that are hosting a number of displaced people from other neighbouring counties due to the conflict, majority of which have been absorbed by the host community. The situation has made the few functional community hand pumps and sanitation facilities being extremely over stressed and most of them are broken down or damaged. This has posed a big threat to health of the both IDPs and the host community members within Magwi, Torit and Lafon counties. According to the SS 2019 HNO, in 2018, women, men and children were pushed into much deeper crisis due to their inability to receive lifesaving support. Due to the crisis, GBV cases including sexual violence is not reported because of stigma, shame and lack of confidence in the rule of law ensuring justice to the victims, about 2,300 cases of all forms of GBV reported by mid-2018 and yet this represent a small percentage of issues of GBV. Very low per cent of the population have access to a borehole or water yard within 30 minutes’ walking distance and mostly face with protection problems. Majority of the population access water from unprotected sources. More than 90 percent of the population practice open defecation due to lack of sanitation facilities and they lack NFIs. The conflict has continued to drive people’s WASH needs, from blocking or destruction of WASH infrastructure to limiting WASH commodities. Insecurity at times prevents humanitarian actors from accessing areas that have high WASH needs.

Activities

WASH activities for IDPs/host communities and returnees • Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations • Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. • Enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate. sanitation services WASH activities to Mitigate WASH-related GBV • Gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers • Provide safe water and access in secure location to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis • Provide safe sanitation and hygiene facilities to women and girls with after appropriate risk analysis
