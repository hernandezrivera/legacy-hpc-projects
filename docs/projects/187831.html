Project Summary

IOM will contribute to community revitalization and resilience through supporting local economic recovery, improving access to basic/social services, and promoting community engagement and social cohesion. The project will strengthen access to livelihoods by creating income-generating opportunities and improving access to production and market infrastructure to foster local economic recovery. This comes in line with both Early Recovery and Livelihood Sector 2021 objectives.
IOM will improve access to basic services by rehabilitating essential infrastructures identified by the communities, through a process of community engagement and consultation, as being most needed. IOM will also provide vocational training and business development support (BDS), including training and financial or in-kind support, to vulnerable IDPs for them to establish or expand their own business. Prior to implementation, IOM will identify the local market gaps in coordination with the communities.
Furthermore, IOM will provide income-generating activity through Cash for Work (CfW) programmes to enable vulnerable beneficiaries to cover their households’ most urgent needs. CfW wages will be based on local average wages so as not to distort the local labour market, and activities will be identified in consultation with local community representatives and business owners. CfW activities will be implemented in line with the CWG’s minimum recommended actions. For all cash assistance, IOM will coordinate with the ER Cluster and the Cash Working Group to harmonize transfer values and avoid overlap.
Lastly, IOM will improve the livelihood opportunities and community engagement for IDPs and vulnerable communities through the continuation of the Community Enhancement Center (CEC) and establishment of Community Engagement Teams (CETs) in Afrin, Aleppo, A’zaz, Harim, and Idleb sub-districts.

4 - Needs

As of October 2021, 2.8 million IDPs reside in NWS, and more than 25,000 individuals were displaced during that month alone. The economic situation in NWS is dire, with 99 per cent of the population of several districts in Idleb and Northern Aleppo Governorates in extreme poverty (defined as living on less than 1.90 USD per person per day (HNAP/UNDP Livelihood Situation Analysis, Sept 2021). The top priorities for newly displaced households in NWS as of October 2021 were livelihoods (for 63% of households), basic services (58%), and food (56%) (HNAP NSAG and TBAF MNM, Oct 2021). As of May 2021, 86% of men and 98% of women had no stable livelihood (HNAP/UNDP, see above). In August and September 2021, IOM conducted community assessments via Household Surveys (HHS) and Key Informant Interviews (KII) in IOM’s 11 IDP camps and 12 host communities in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates. Food and livelihood were the top priorities, identified by 85 per cent and 62 per cent of IDPs respectively. The main barriers to starting a business were a lack of start-up capital and a lack of training/skills. Respondents in the camps mentioned health facilities, functioning markets, and electricity as the most needed public infrastructure.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. IOM activities unintentionally lead to intra-community tensions due to beneﬁciary selection for livelihood activities or limited resources and response capacities. 2. Conflicts and tensions about the communities can arise during community consultation sessions where community members debate their most urgent needs and priorities. 3. Pressure on the implementing partners from the local authorities on who should or should not receive the assistance through livelihood activities, or on which contractor to select for the rehabilitation projects. Disagreements between the IP or TPM teams and the contractor for rehabilitation projects can lead to risks for the safety of the teams.4. Intra-household tensions on the use of cash. Mitigation measures: 1. Clearly communicate the selection and vulnerability criteria to the communities prior to selection, as well as the project’s limited resources.2. Train IP staff and CETs on conducting effective and respectful discussions with the communities and how to manage any tensions.3. Raise awareness with IPs and CETs on principled humanitarian assistance, work with CETs to mitigate undue influence from local actors, and train IPs on reporting any incidents or interference to IOM’s Programme and Access teams as early as possible to ensure the safety of the field teams and the integrity of the project.4. Communication and messaging designed by protection/GBV and CVA partners addressing potential household tensions.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks:- Abuse or exploitation of beneficiaries by IP staff during beneficiary selection and distribution processes.- Lack of staff awareness and code of conduct on PSEA.- Lack of availability of information on reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries.- Lack of female IP staff in the field. Mitigation/prevention measures:- Code of conduct includes PSEA language.- All IP staff are trained on PSEA.- Internal SEA reporting mechanism for staff and investigative capacity exist and are known within the organization. - PSEA awareness-raising activities and materials for beneficiaries and local communities are conducted and shared during the activities and reporting mechanisms for beneficiaries are available. - All field activities are supervised by humanitarian staff, including female staff members to mitigate PSEA and other protection risks.- Beneficiaries regularly receive information regarding the free and unconditional nature of humanitarian aid and know about and have access to complaint mechanisms and hotlines.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1. Security situation in NWS allows IOM to implement response activities.2. Activities can be implemented in coordination with local councils.3. Beneficiaries start their business after receiving cash or in-kind support.4. Vendors in targeted areas have sufficient capacity.5. Active community participation and engagement. Risks:1. Security situation in NWS does not allow IOM response activities to be undertaken.2. Local authorities are not in favor of IOM intervention or influence on beneficiary selection/aid diversion 3. Support packages are sold by beneficiaries instead of being used as planned.4. Vendors lack the capacity requested to provide required items.5. Disengagement of communities during the assessment, planning and implementation stages.6. Social tension between the different groups resulted from the partial inclusion of community members. Mitigation measures:1. Establish a security and accessibility plan for the targeted areas, including a contingency plan to target alternative locations.2. IPs ensure local authorities are engaged from the initial stage.3. Support packages are designed and cash assistance is provided based on beneficiaries’ business requirements.4. Well-designed market assessment to make sure of items' availability for local procurement5. IPs work closely with the communities to prioritize the main sectors to be targeted.
