4 - Needs

Food Consumption Score (FCS) category data confirm that the surveyed populations have faced high food insecurity with nearly 80% of the households classified as poor (37.61%) or borderline (41.96%). The average FCSs of host community households was 33.05 and camp households was 35.37. Further examination of the host community and camp households revealed that, while both groups had approximately 20% in the acceptable category, there was a disparity in the households classified as poor. Nearly 4 in 10 host community households (39.96%) are classified as poor compared to a quarter of camp households (25.29%). Of the three governorates where the surveyed host community population was located, Al Hassakah had the greatest percentage of poor (42.68%) and borderline (41.22%) households and the least of acceptable households (16.10%). Ar-Raqqa host community households fared the best with 29.44% classified as acceptable, 37.66% as borderline, and 32.90% as poor. At the sub-district level, the challenges faced by those in Al Hassakah governorate come into focus with a poor FCS exceeding 40% of households in six of the nine sampled sub-districts. This includes Ya’robiyah sub-district with 74.29% of households classified as poor and Tal Hmis and Qahtaniyyeh both exceeding 50% of its households. Given that all camp households received emergency food rations, this likely contributed to less severe FCSs as compared to the host communities. The one exception is Tal Abiad Camp, which classified all sampled households (100%) as poor.

5 - Implementation of activities

1) Bakery Support; Blumont will support bakeries to increase the availability and affordability of commercially baked bread in NES by providing local and/or regionally procured wheat flour and yeast to local bakeries.2) Bread Distribution to Host Communities and Camp Populations. Bakeries will produce bread for local non-camp populations. Blumont will distribute it free of charge to IDPs in four formal camps and the collective centers too. 3) Supplemental Food Vouchers. To increase dietary diversity among acutely food insecure households living outside the camps, while simultaneously strengthening local markets Blumont will provide monthly supplementary food vouchers targeting at least 3,000 food-insecure HHs in host communities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Blumont’s approach is to integrate Do-No-Harm principles in the activities design, selection, and implementation strategy through comprehensive consultations with the beneficiaries, FSWG, and LAs. To ensure the prevention of any tension between the IDP and the host community through supporting bakeries that will produce a subsidized bread, as well as food vouchers targeting food-insecure people with the host community. Vulnerable people, especially women, children, pregnant and lactating women, and elderly-headed households, will be prioritized during the selection process and the bread distribution points by ensuring having especial priority distribution lines for these groups.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Blumont staff and volunteers are trained on PSEA, CFM, gender, and protection mainstreaming, and will receive regular sanitization and refresher training during the lifetime of the project. Blumont is sensitizing vendors on its code of conduct and its intolerance for gender discrimination and sexual harassment or abuse. Vendors are obliged to sign a commitment as part of their contract to adherence to Blumont code of conduct and values that prevent sexual abuse and gender dissemination. Blumont is updating is a complaint feedback mechanism to ensure that beneficiaries, especially women, girls, and elderlies, have better and easy access to raise concerns, feedback, or complaint.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk Identification and Analysis, Pre-Treatment, and mitigation; 1) Infectious Disease - Covid-19: COVID-19 threatens all staff and program beneficiaries, Blumont will continue to monitor developments in relation to COVID-19 through advice being published by WHO and the U.S. Government. Blumont staff wear gloves and masks, use hand sanitizer frequently and practice physical distancing by maintaining 2 meter distance between themselves and camp residents. 2) Civil unrest; Program staff or objectives impacted by groups of people protesting in the areas of program activity to the actions of Govts/State/Non-State actors. Constantly, Blumont will monitor national news, media and social media, and maintain low profile and avoid areas of demonstration or protest. 3) Physical Security & Crime : Program staff or objectives may be impacted through vehicle hi-jack. Blumont Security Team to monitor, advise and direct. Staff will adherence to Blumont Global Safety & Security Policy and Security SOPs.
