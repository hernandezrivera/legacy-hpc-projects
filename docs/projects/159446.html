Project Summary

PCO3. Improve social cohesion and resilience of conflict-affected people, including IDPs, through community-based protection and identification of durable solutions
Community mobilization project aims to support grassroots initiatives of vulnerable communities to increase resilience for future development. All project activities are based on the analysis of issues, and prioritization of protection risks and solutions in every particular community. Community mobilizing component seeks to empower communities to develop their own community-based coping mechanisms, to exercise their rights, and to find solutions for the most pressing issues in their communities. Communities will mobilize themselves when they have trust and confidence in the process and when they see benefits emerging from their own efforts.
Once communities are selected based on assessments conducted by the project team, PIN will select key community volunteers/activists to participate in a training on project development. The training will be conducted in each of the communities and includes a three-hour session conducted by the project team, during which topics such as Protection, Safeguarding, CRM are presented as well.
The communities will be selected based on the following process: At the start of the project, PIN will disseminate the information in community meetings, cluster meetings as well as among local administration. Applications will be invited, with the project team also offering support to any community that might require assistance with the applications. Afterwards, the internal selection committee will create a shortlist based on the applications received. Only applications tackling Protection-related issues will be considered.
The key community volunteers/activists will be selected through a dialogue with local authorities from the selected communities. Prior to the training, the community volunteers/activists’ representatives should identify key needs within their community that can be addressed with an external financial assistance. During the training, participants will acquire an overall understanding and basic skills in project cycle management for community-based projects, and will get acquainted with the application procedure for PIN funding. Each community will develop their own proposal with assistance from the project team as needed. Once PIN has approved the proposal, a contract will be signed, and materials/funds will be provided/disbursed for the project to be implemented.
PIN is responsible in front of the beneficiaries in terms of the main following accountability commitments and prioritizes:
- Accountability to affected population: PIN involves people affected by crisis in key decisions and processes that affect them, and have effective communication and feedback channels that engage all sectors in a community.
- Leadership/Governance: compliance response mechanisms that are integrated into project proposals, strategies, monitoring and evaluations, trainings and performance tracking management, partnership agreements etc.
- Information Provision: accessible and timely information to beneficiaries on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organisation and its beneficiaries over information provision.
- Beneficiaries’ Feedback: actively seek the views of affected populations to improve policy and practice in programming, ensuring that feedback and complaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction.
- Non-discrimination: PIN ensures that all vulnerable groups of women and girls, men and boys, have access to the assistance.

1 - Needs

As the protracted conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues, millions of people require prolonged relief and assistance across the entire spectrum of emergency and humanitarian aid. The humanitarian needs remain significant in the Protection areas. Basic social, economic, and administrative infrastructures are either difficult or impossible to access. If they are accessible, many have been damaged as a result of the conflict or have fallen into disrepair due to lack of maintenance. It is unknown how long this crisis will continue and the local governmental and municipal systems are not capable of responding to the protracted emergency and humanitarian needs of conflict affected populations in both GCA and NGCA. They not only lack resources, but also operational and technical capacities to take the lead in mobilizing, coordinating and carrying out the response. Although some social assistance in GCA is operational through municipal structures or local non-governmental service providers, still they are not accessible for many vulnerable people (especially elderly; people of different age with disabilities, single-headed households led by females with minor children etc.), mainly in the areas near the LoC. The Community Mobilization component of PIN focuses on the needs of people living in close distance to the contact line who still suffer from the consequences of a military conflict. The needs of the most vulnerable communities remain the same e.g. damaged infrastructure system: ruined roads, absence of transport connection between villages and regional cities, unstable water and electricity supply, absence of socially vital services such as medical treatment and cultural life in addition. Therefore, residents of these areas rarely travel outside of the village and live under constant potential threat to their lives and health. The limited transportation and the lower mobility increase the vulnerability of elderly residents, especially those with disabilities, chronic diseases and other serious health concerns (including but not limited to mental health issues. The noted people are often left behind by the community and respective local authorities. The children (both boys and girls) are facing the lack of spaces for socialization and out-of-school activities focused on development of art and sport skills within their peers. Girls and women from single-headed households are often not involved in the social life and elaboration of initiatives focused on deployment of the community. According to the communities` assessment needs (managed by PIN during the first phase of the community mobilization component), in majority of cases, the communities require a creation or restoration of public/unity spaces such as halls of cultural clubs for leisure activities and stress relief, central parks and avenues, as well as health care centres, children’s indoor/outdoor spaces etc. According to PIN PDM report and SWOT-analysis, it was identified that Community Mobilization Projects allow to mobilize the local people’s resources and tackle/solve social needs in their community, to encourage its further development as well as civil society in general, and ensure community mobilization advocacy (May, 2019). The results of this survey indicated that the needs of communities can be divided into several main groups: reconstruction of the social infrastructure, premises, purchase of type equipment (media, office, child, etc.). Engagement of girls and boys, women and men of different ages into the discussion, design and implementation of the projects contributes to the gender equality, including across age groups within the communities. All the community members without distinctions on the basis of gender and age can benefit from the implemented project initiated and designed by themselves.

Improve social cohesion and resilience of conflict-affected people, including IDPs, through community-based protection and identification of durable solutions Activities:1. Community-based protection, Support to Peaceful Coexistence & Durable Solutions Rationale: This intervention seeks to empower communities to develop their own community-based coping mechanisms, to exercise their rights, and to find solutions for the most pressing needs/issues in their communities. Communities will mobilize themselves when they have trust and confidence in the process and when they see benefits emerging from their own efforts. PIN will provide grants to 15 communities in the Donetsk and Lugansk Oblasts GCA for community-based projects which will be designed at the community level and meet needs identified by community representatives. An obligatory condition for receiving the grant was the community's own contribution to the project, including all direct activities for the implementation of the project.Examples of successfully implemented mini-projects in a previous projects such as centres for retirement age and people with disabilities; leisure activities and personal development; parks of the inhabitants etc. Some of the successful examples of implemented mini-project is Sviatogorsk Centre for people with disabilities and IDPs, refurbished training hall in the House of Culture & village Library in the House of Culture (Novoselivka Persha), refurbished a physiotherapic office in out-patient clinic (Pivnichne) from a previous DFID phase. 2. Capacity building for activists of communityRationale: PIN will strive for building capacity of local communities as a means to increase access aid and ensure meaningful transition and exit strategies, which are part of a process of moving from emergency to rehabilitation and development. Under this activity, identified community activists in the Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts, especially along the line of contact in GCA, will receive trainings on Community Mobilization. Community Support Initiative (CSI) includes a range of activities designed to mobilize and empower conflict-affected and displaced communities, building on the education, skills, and capacities of affected populations in order to strengthen their abilities to organize emergency response, protect themselves and identify solutions.Community Mobilization involves a training on skills and gaining experience on a variety of topics: training on community mobilization and empowerment; on (self) management, or support for the establishment of an inclusive leadership structure; a complaint mechanism, a platform for ideas exchange and a dialogue with the authorities or other relevant actors; a project design, from an idea till implementation and finalization process; a mechanism enabling community participation in government decision-making on measures that affect them, etc. Finally, PIN proposes to conduct workshops (including all active participants from the communities under the project component) at the end of the projects to share experiences among participants, analyze successes/challenges, and draw lessons.This will allow local activists to gain adequate knowledge and expertise to continue this activity independently after the intervention. In addition, their improved professional skills will have long-term effect for their further work and well-being of beneficiaries.
