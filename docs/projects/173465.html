Project Summary

In line with IOM’s GBV in Crises Operational Framework and the 2020 JRP’s three strategic objectives and two of the GBV sector’s objectives, IOM will develop its multi-sectoral GBV programming working with Rohingya refugees and host communities focused on three pillars: 1.) survivor-centered support, 2.) risk mitigation and 3.) addressing root causes that perpetuate GBV. IOM will provide specialized services and multi-sectoral support via its case management entry points embedded throughout 11 Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) across 10 sites working alongside its local partner PULSE. The WGSS ensure that women and girls can safely access multi-sectoral services, including safety via referral to IOM´s emergency safe shelter.
To mitigate risks, prevent GBV and empower women and girls, the WGSS provides age-appropriate and structured psychosocial and educational activities, life-skills and vocational learning, forums for consultation, information-sharing and learning, including targeted awareness on GBV, sexual and reproductive health, menstrual hygiene management, life skills and vocational training, psychosocial support, risk and resilience building, and awareness-raising working with an extensive network of community volunteers, male advocates and other community champions, and ensures community feedback using tested tools and satisfaction surveys. IOM and PULSE provide a wide-range of static interventions and community-level interventions focused on GBV prevention and risk mitigation, and community sensitization working with men, boys, women, girls and specifically targeting groups with diversity of need including male survivors and persons with diverse sexual orientation, gender identity, and expression. Such approaches also ensure safe referrals for male and gender-diverse survivors with jointly mapped counselling spaces in collaboration with other IOM Protection teams in community facilities where appropriate to ensure safe and non-stigmatizing case management services. IOM will also ensure capacity building and training in GBV working with local authorities and as part of community-based disaster risk reduction efforts.

1 - Identified Needs

The COVID-19 crisis has exacerbated GBV trends disproportionately impacting on the most vulnerable households & individuals. GBVIMS reports since Mar. 2020 indicate an increase in incidents of intimate partner and domestic violence since the onset of COVID-19 across both Rohingya and host communities. According to the latest MSNA findings & ACAPS analysis, lack of income-generating opportunities have impacted on the most vulnerable households and individuals including single female-headed households & other groups at heightened risk & diversity of need, like persons with disabilities or persons with diverse gender and sexual orientation. There are several well-documented & acutely gendered risks related to COVID-19 that disproportionately affect women & girls, related to the interruption of SRH and GBV services. According to the Rapid Gender Analysis published by ISCG Gender Hub, UN Women, OXFAM, Care & ACAPS/NPM, the increased restrictions in movement of women & girls in public spaces and negative household coping mechanisms including early marriage, create new risks and have further impacted on service access in a deteriorating protection environment overall. GBV prevention, risk mitigation & response activities will require tailored and harmonized approaches at the interagency level which consider differential social norms and holistic behavioral change models at community-level tailored specifically for Rohingya and host communities and that focus on male engagement. IOM will continue its proactive role in the GBV SS, Male Engagement TT & related thematic working groups to ensure harmonized & targeted engagement strategies in line with a do-no-harm approach. Within both Bangladeshi & Rohingya populations, women & girls are among the most vulnerable & are exposed to high levels of violence, sexual harassment & exploitation & harmful social & cultural practices and norms. There is a need for multisectoral services to address the ongoing impacts of conflict-related sexual violence in the Rohingya community, including support for male survivors & the secondary and tertiary risks of exposure to GBV that are extremely prevalent in Cox´s Bazar within the camps located around Ukhiya, Teknaf & peripheral areas of mixed communities. In addition, Rapid gender analysis highlighted, there are key GBV risks that intersect with certain groups with diversity of need including persons with disabilities and Hijra communities who often face additional barriers in accessing services and usually have different health-seeking behaviours than women and girls. There is a need for targeted strategies of engagement to ensure their inclusion in services as well as evidence based & holistic programming around risk GBV risk mitigation and prevention which carefully consider shifting social norms. The MPES & the recent Aarar Dilor Hota UN Women-IOM social norms mapping study on changing gender norms have highlighted that there are highly nuanced and complex trade-offs with their need to engage in “less acceptable” practices such as non-traditional or masculine forms of work or non-gender segregated activities to meet their needs in the camp setting. A focus on safe & meaningful women´s participation and empowerment is crucial to wider GBV prevention efforts & access to services. FGDs and reports have indicated that pressure & fear of parents & community leaders as well as the low participation of women & girls in making decisions at household level constitute barriers for survivors. Risks of forced marriage with the perpetrator & not being protected against reprisals are commonly cited concerns. Social stigma and perceptions about rape survivors, the community leaders’ interference in handling rape cases, lack of protection of survivors, insufficient awareness of consequences of rape, benefits of services and service accessibility could considerably delay survivors’ decision making in reporting and accessing services on a timely manner.

2 - Activities or outputs

IOM will ensure expertise and quality survivor-centred care working closely with Health and Protection teams to ensure diversity of need. Integrated capacity building and coordination as well as a focus on supervision/training for local organizations & national authorities on GBV will be emphasized. Technical training for caseworkers/health workers on key groups such as male survivors, LGBTI groups and PwD will cascade beyond IOM teams into field training in line with interagency guidelines. IOM and PULSE will strengthen referral pathways working with humanitarian stakeholders, government authorities and community groups in the camps and host communities (HC) and will orient health service providers in handling different types of GBV cases and work with communities to better understand health-seeking behaviors.To ensure sustainability and localization, IOM GBV will scale up work with its community groups, Community Advocates & local partners to transition day-to-day management of WGSS activities including: group psychosocial support activities, life skills training, structured GBV prevention activities, & radio listening group facilitation to its community groups. Child friendly corners will be integrated in the WGSS to improve women’s access, participation & leadership in WGSS management. IOM & PULSE will play a supportive role in mobilizing resources to support activities and capacity building to community groups/advocates in the camps and HC & scale up its work with community volunteers to ensure awareness on GBV, referrals & dialogue on GBV prevention in line with appropriate social change curriculum. PULSE & IOM will focus on capacity development of Rohingya from 100 sub-blocks to broadcast the information related to GBV services, contribute to sensitization & structured social change. This approach focuses on supporting community members to inspire & influence positive change in their own lives & the life of others by challenging the social norms that perpetuate violence in communities. This will allow IOM to better work with communities to address root causes of gender inequality & affect holistic social change of harmful behaviors/practices related to GBV. In addition, IOM will reduce the use of diesel generators & start using off-grid solar panel system in WGSS. IOM will produce, distribute & encourage use of re-usable cloth masks to community membersSkills development/empowerment of women/girls will be become more focused & structured. Participants who have graduated from skills-building classes/groups will be engaged as peer trainers & supported with coaching & capacity building in facilitation, communication & management skills. IOM will identify opportunities to affect power imbalances identifying opportunities for women’s participation through multi-stakeholder approaches & a do-no-harm approach. IOM will scale up several self-reliance pilot projects (Women’s Participation Project & Women’s Income Generation Support) with IOM & partners across other areas of operation. IOM will replicate these pilot interventions, sharing lessons learned & working with partners beyond IOM’s areas of operation. IOM will work closely with relevant sectors, share key lessons learned & facilitate workshops for other humanitarian stakeholders to ensure coordinated programming & long-term strategies for participation and empowerment throughout the response, particularly around inclusive EPR planning.IOM and PULSE will collect feedback from the community by conducting consultations, using beneficiary satisfaction survey tools, baseline, Mid-term, endline surveys & promoting the use of CFP (Common Feedback Platform) desks in each IOM WGSS operating area. The result & analysis of the consultations/surveys will allow IOM to regularly improve service delivery & adapt to the changing environment & support AAP. IOM will continue to conduct training for staff including IPs & community volunteers on PSEA, & serve as the PSEA Network co-chair
