Project Summary

The conflicts in Darfur between 2003 and 2010 caused hundreds of thousands of Sudanese citizens to seek refuge in Chad where they were recognized as refugees on a prima-facie basis. They also found refuge elsewhere including South Sudan, Ethiopia, Egypt and other countries.
With improvements in the security situation in most of the former conflict areas of Darfur, the Jebel Mara area and its ongoing armed clashes, being the exception, UNHCR anticipates as many as 20,000 refugees would register to return in 2020. The main return areas are Central, West and North Darfur.
Therefore, UNHCR is expecting to facilitate their return from Chad. They will be going back to Central, North and West Darfur. It is also anticipated that majority of the Chadian refugees in Darfur will return to Chad, leaving only a small remaining residual group for which a local integration solution will be attempted.
UNHCR and COR will continue to advocate the Local and Federal level authorities to facilitate access to land and documentation in Sudan, and families will be provided with cash grants for provision of shelter materials to re-establish their homes in Darfur. Individuals will be further provided with multi-purpose cash grants to facilitate the reintegration process and initiate livelihood opportunities. After the provision of reintegration packages and protection services in the transit center, UNHCR and COR will facilitate safe and dignified movement of returnees from reception center to final return areas. The AGDM participatory assessment in return villages in West Darfur, has revealed the physical Security, SGBV and livelihoods as the major problem, followed by Education and community empowerment and self – reliance.
Return areas in Darfur are generally underdeveloped and require reconstruction to ensure sustainable reintegration and peaceful sharing of resources and services among returnees and local communities. As such, UNHCR will act as a catalyst for reintegration projects through UNHCR funded projects and existing coordination structures, including the Durable Solutions Working Group and Return, Recovery and Reintegration Sector, to improve access to basic services and enhance rule of law in return areas. In particular, returnees have indicated that water, education, protection and health are amongst their highest priorities. There is further need to enhance the capacity of communities to manage resources and resolve conflict peacefully, through maintenance of community-based protection networks, awareness raising sessions, and tailored community-based protection projects that benefit both return and host communities.

1 - Needs

With an indicative budget allocation, Darfur operation would be able to repatriate 8,000 individuals in an organized manner, while the rest may return spontaneously. While spontaneous returnees will not be prioritized for full return package, as a contingency planning response, the operation will only put in its plan to address 500 HHs with shelter assistance. UNHCR will work with the COR, implementing partners, VRRC and RRR sector as well as relevant local authorities to monitor the return and reintegration process of refugee returnees. Six reception centers will be maintained at key border crossings, where refugee’s bio data will be verified and screened. Protection services including counselling, family tracing and reunification, medical screening, awareness raising, as well as referral pathways will continue to be provided to returnees. In collaboration with COR and local authorities, three-way stations in ND will be maintained. UNHCR and COR will continue to advocate with Local and Federal level authorities to facilitate access to land and documentation in Sudan, and families will be provided with cash grants for provision of shelter materials to re-establish their homes in Darfur. Individuals will be further provided with multi-purpose cash grants to facilitate the reintegration process and initiate livelihood opportunities. After the provision of reintegration packages and protection services in the transit center, UNHCR and COR will facilitate safe and dignified movement of returnees from reception centers to their final areas of return. AGDM participatory assessment in return villages in WD, has revealed the physical Security, SGBV and livelihoods as the major problems, followed by Education and community empowerment and self – reliance. In order to mitigate the risks, most vulnerable individuals, including gwomen and children will be assisted through multipurpose CBI program. Return areas in Darfur are generally underdeveloped and require reconstruction to ensure sustainable reintegration and peaceful sharing of resources and services among returnees and local communities. As such, UNHCR will act as a catalyst for reintegration projects through UNHCR funded projects and existing coordination structures, including the Durable Solutions Working Group and Return, Recovery and Reintegration Sector, to improve access to basic services and enhance rule of law in return areas. Returnees have indicated that water, education, protection and health are amongst their highest priorities. Furthermore, there is need to enhance the capacity of communities to manage resources and resolve conflict peacefully, through maintenance of community-based protection networks, awareness raising sessions, and tailored community-based protection projects that benefit both return and host communities. UNHCR will continue enhancing the capacity of the Government of Sudan, the Commissioner of Refugees, local authorities, and line ministries to uphold the rights of returnees. This aims to increase their capacity to manage basic services, including the deployment of staff, such as police personnel, teachers, as well as health and nutrition workers. UNHCR will further ensure that the Darfur Development Strategy and other development funding are targeting return areas for reintegration projects. UNHCR will prioritize in making sure that information is swiftly communicated to relevant service providers and authorities. As the repatriation operation expands, it is vital that UNHCR identifies additional partners to implement repatriation exercise and engage in reintegration projects.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Rights and community-based approaches will be at the core of the project activities. Returnee population will be involved during the entire humanitarian program cycle through participatory assessment. In this process, primary consideration will be given to the principle of accountability to affected populations, to identify, understand and support their own protection measures. Different segments of affected people will be meaningfully engaged in all decisions and actions that have a direct impact on their well-being.

7 - Accountability to Affected People and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse
7.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback and use it to improve your activities? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with individuals (in other venues/locations) through aid workers

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The Office will build on its previous years work, enhancing the robust PSEA response across the operation. This includes strengthening the culture of accountability across the operation through the integration of PSEA actions within protection functions and broader Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) strategy. This will be achieved through regular PSEA training for UNHCR staff, partners and contractors; regular SEA risk assessment and development and implementation of annual PSEA action plans for field offices; and collaboration with operational partners to establish inter-agency and community-based complaint mechanisms accessible to refugees in all locations with an active refugee response. The Office will continue to lead the Protection Sector activities and will maintain its active participation in- and contributions to other inter-agency PSEA fora, including the RC/HC’s PSEA Network.The project will ensure that activities will not expose most vulnerable groups including women and children to protection risks. Community feedback and complaint mechanism will be set up. Project will ensure inclusion of most vulnerable groups within the target communities in close coordination and collaboration with key stakeholders. The project will support humanitarian actors to mainstream protection throughout all activities and to coordinate specialized protection services for affected populations.
