Basic Info

Project Name

Provision of integrated and equitable lifesaving nutrition service for vulnerable children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women in Pariang of Unity and Fangak (Jonglei), South Sudan.

Start Date

01/01/2022

End Date

31/12/2022

Project Summary

The provision of integrated emergency lifesaving Nutrition services will target a total of
19,827 vulnerable individuals; Children U5s; 5,533 Boys, 5,993 Girls, and 8,301 Women, pregnant and Lactating mothers; (Fangak County U5s 6,879, 5,283 PLWs; Pariang County U5s 4,647 and PLWs 3,018)
Through fixed sites and mobile approaches in Fangak (Jonglei), and Pariang of Unity respectively.
During 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan, Hold the Child Organization (HCO) proposes to contribute to the 4 Sectoral objectives; 1- To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women; 2- To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition through Active and passive screen for malnutrition at community level through the community nutrition volunteers and Maternal Infant Young Child Nutrition Promoter focusing on the prioritized locations of Pariang (Unity) and Fangak (Jonglei) by the end of 2022; 3- To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information; 4- To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education (through Early Childhood Development activities) through conducting malaria screening and treating, Strengthening livelihood skills with kitchen gardening, and demonstration gardens to support on complementary feeding for children under five years both girls and boys. Pregnant and Lactating mothers integrated with Maternal Infant Young Child Nutrition activities in 2022 and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2022.
During 2022, Hold the Child Organization is anticipated to utilize 290,338 $ targeting Children below the age of U5s, Pregnant and Lactating children, Internally Displaced people living within communities and returnees seeking refugee provided they meet the admission criteria indicated in the South Sudan CMAM guideline adopted in 2017, Community Mobilization through the involvement of Community Nutrition Volunteers, Mother to Mother Support Groups who will be used to support Vitamin A and Deworming supplementation campaigns during National Immunization Days, (NIDS), timely identification of nutrition status through active and passive surveillance to monitor Proxy GAMs through Community Nutrition workers.

1 - Needs

According to IRNA assessment conducted August 2021, The Humanitarian situation in Fangak continues to deteriorate due to flooding. The flooding is as a result of excessive rainfall and overflows from Phow river which has caused both pluvial and fluvial floods, the people of fangak are living in dire situation due to effects of flooding. The most priority needs of the flood affected people which needs an immediate provision are comprising of food security and livelihoods, Shelter NFIs, WASH NFIs and health to mitigate the effects of the flooding. In the projection period of December 2020 to March 2021, the food security situation deteriorates and the number of people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity is likely to rise to 3.02 million (66.5% of the population in Greater Upper Nile region). At the peak of the lean season in April to July 2021, the number of people facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in the Greater Upper Nile region will likely rise to 3.47 million people (76.4% of the population in Greater Upper Nile region). The deteriorations in the first and second projection periods is driven by low crop production, seasonality, the economic crisis, and the impacts of COVID-19 that are expected to persist into next year. Crop Production in Jonglei and Unity national level production is yet to be determined through the CFSAM analysis scheduled to be released in April 2022. However, the overall production in the current year is anticipated to be equivalent or slightly below last year’s. According to IPC analysis of January 2020, 5.29 million people (45.2% of the population) are estimated to have faced Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 1.11 million people faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. About 40,000 people were classi ed in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) in the counties of Akobo, Duk and Ayod in Jonglei State. The threat of COVID-19 has also worsened the already bad situation. The floods have submerged and swept away people’s homes, schools, water points, health centres, markets, food warehouses, humanitarian agencies compounds, airstrips, food drop zones and food distribution points. The floods have rendered some of the villages completely inaccessible making it difficult to provide the much-needed assistance. Lack of equipped health care facilities with crippled referrals, WASH facilities, localized insecurity, the economic crisis, and prolonged years of asset depletion continue to contribute morbidities and Nutrition related mortalities and high levels of acute food insecurity in the country. Isolated insecurity incidents displace populations, disrupt livelihoods and impede households’ access to other food sources, the common reported morbidities were tuberculosis, malaria, watery diarrhoea and upper respiratory infections and the deaths were commonly among children and elderly people. Lack of health facilities, treatments and delays in seeking health care were cited as the major causes of deaths. Diseases and outbreaks especially Hepatitis E, acute Watery Diarrhea based on ISDR weekly report on Week 48. In January 2020, nutrition report indicate the proxy prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) among under- ve children ranges from 1.3 per cent to 33.4 above the emergency threshold of 19.59% Fangak and 20.27% Pariang County respectively. Poor access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition conditions across South Sudan and contributes to one of the highest child mortality rates in the world. Hold the Child’s progressive evidence based programing projects 2020 and 2021 for Paraing and Fangak county respectively indicate there is dire need of humanitarian intervention to avert declining situation in communities (Pariang and Fangak) characterized with GAM rates above the WHO threshold of 15% standard recommendations.

2 - Project activities

Output 1: Children under 5 with severe and moderate acute malnutrition, and pregnant & lactating women with moderate acute malnutrition are treated through the CMAM program Activities: 1. HCO will Provide therapeutic and medical treatment to 3,632 Pregnant and lactating Mothers for Fangak, (1,987 Pregnant and 1,645 Lactating); 6,879 Children U5s (3,577 girls and 3,302 boys); For Paring 4,647 U5s and 3,018 pregnant and lactating mothers for moderate acute malnourished and Pregnant. 2. Screening of children in OPD/at entry point (tri-age area) in health facilities and referral to OTP/TSFP services within the health facilities or outside depending the availability of OTP/TSFP services. 3. Screen all new children aged 6-59 months at the OTP for Malaria, using RDT and treated timely, U5s admitted with severe acute malnutrition receiving WASH kits and number of nutrition sites in priority counties with access to WASH services/ facilities. Output 2: Increased nutrition prevention and service awareness and inter-linkages/collaborations with complementary sectors at operational levels. Activities: 1. Training to staff and CNVs on CMAM, MYICN and the use of WASH/Health/GBV on identification and referral of GBV cases at nutrition sites cases identified. 55 Fangak, 25 Pariang. 2. Establish hand washing facilities, latrines and clean drinking water for appetite test at Nutrition sites. 3. Sensitize communities in the priority counties with hygiene and sanitation messages to mothers/caregivers of children under 5 and PLWs at SC/OTP/TSFP sites, as well as, at community level on Nutrition, MIYCN, WASH inNutrition and promotion of basic health services, as well as existing protection referrals, 4. Organize Mother to Mother Support Groups with a focus on training of Lead Mothers on MIYCN facilitating techniques within the catchment areas. 5. Provide Vit A and deworming to children aged 6-59 months in the nutrition program that were missed during the routine National Immunization Days.Output 3: Improved quality and ow of nutrition information, and utilization of nutrition surveillance date at field levels. Activities: 1) Conduct quarterly (and otherwise scheduled) MUAC screening of children (6-59m) and PLWs at community level for early case detection and timely referrals 2) Ensure availability of cluster indicated reporting tools, and adequately skilled personnel to monitor project implementation 3) Support and coordinate biannual FSNMS assessments in the areas of operation to determine the access and coverage and strengthen routine program monitoring 4) Conduct regular CHDs, supply pipeline managers and stakeholders joint monitoring and supportive supervision visits to project sites 5) Support early pre-positioning of specialized nutrition products for the treatment of SAM and MAM to project sites, 6) Liaise with FSL partners to engage nutrition beneficiaries for home gardening activities, distribution of farm tools and seed to enhance the community diversity and Improve their feeding habits. 7) Enhance psychosocial stimulation of children with severe acute malnutrition by providing Early Childhood Development kits in Nutrition locally made, 8) Form and Strengthen Quality and accountability to affected persons committees including mother support groups in all project areas, 9) Establish a community complaint feedback mechanism with 12 nutrition facilities equipped with suggestions boxes placed nutrition sites; OTP/TSFP sites.

Joint and Regular supportive visits shall be documented on Quarterly basis, key lessons learnt and challenges will be highlighted for improvement and future Intervention planned appropriately.
