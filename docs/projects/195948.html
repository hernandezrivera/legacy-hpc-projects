Project Summary

In alignment with the Humanitarian Response Plan and the United Nations Strategy on Mine Action 2019-2023, UNMAS will conduct the following activities: survey and clearance of confirmed hazardous areas (calculated in square meters (sqm)), safely dispose of explosive ordnance (EO), as well as conduct explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) by deploying one multi-tasking team (MTT) with embedded Community Liaison Officers (CLOs). UNMAS intends to survey and clear 150,000 sqm of land consisting of confrontational areas and cluster munition strike sites. The clearance team within the MTT will be mobile in six counties assessed as having catastrophic needs, to respond to urgent and priority tasks. Concurrently, CLOs within the MTT will provide EORE sessions to 7,500 beneficiaries including Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees, refugees, and persons with disabilities, disaggregated as follows 1,910 boys, 1,910 girls, 1,910 women, and 1,770 men. During the EORE sessions, further information will be gathered on suspected hazards, and this in turn will be shared with the clearance teams for surveying, marking and clearance.

1 - Needs

Over 16 million sqm of land is suspected to be contaminated with landmines or explosive remnants of war (ERW) in South Sudan. The highest levels of contamination are mainly located within the Greater Equatoria region, along major supply routes and areas for returnees from Uganda. Specifically, Juba, Terekaka, Yei, Magwi, Torit and Canal/Pigi are the six counties with the highest level of recorded contamination. More than 530,000 people are estimated to be at the risk of injury or death from landmines or ERW. The risk of EO not only inhibits civilians from accessing essential social services and the resumption of their livelihoods, but also prevents humanitarian assistance from reaching vulnerable communities. Furthermore, returnees are expected to increase in 2023, particularly to the Equatorias if peace and stability continues. The provision of EORE is also required to ensure that communities and individuals are able to develop strategies and coping mechanisms to reduce the threat of landmines and ERW. Furthermore, beneficiaries of EORE are expected to play a supplemental role in assessing and re-surveying the extent of landmines and ERW contamination, particularly in the hard-to-reach areas, as reports from communities on suspicious hazards help facilitate the process.

2 - Project activities

For this project, UNMAS will survey and clear confirmed hazardous areas; safely dispose of identified items of EO and deliver EORE. All of the activities will be carried out in accordance with the International Mine Action Standards (IMAS), the International Ammunition Technical Guidelines and Standards (IATG), the Strategy of the United Nations on Mine Action 2019-2023, and the National Technical Standards and Guidelines (NTSG). To achieve this, UNMAS will train and accredit the survey, clearance and EORE personnel, as well as conduct operational field visits for the necessary quality assurance (QA) throughout the project period. For the first two activities, the prioritization of tasks will be determined in close coordination with stakeholders including relevant cluster partners in order to reach the most vulnerable populations in need of assistance. To identify the presence of explosive hazards, non-technical surveys, and technical surveys where necessary, will be carried out. Confirmed explosive hazards will be recorded, marked and cleared/demolished. Once the hazardous area has been cleared, the land will be handed over to the community in liaison with the local authorities and the National Mine Action Authority (NMAA). Throughout the process, the surrounding communities shall be informed of the status of the hazardous area. The clearance progress will be recorded in the Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) database in a timely manner. Maps that indicate hazardous areas and roads will be populated on a monthly basis and shared with relevant stakeholders for their awareness. For the provision of EORE, CLOs will tailor the contents of EORE to the needs of targeted beneficiaries. EORE sessions will be conducted either through a door-to-door modality or for a limited number of beneficiaries in order to observe COVID-19 as well as Ebola preventative and mitigation measures.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

In order to ensure that the voices of the most vulnerable are heard, this UNMAS project will ensure to have gender-mixed CLOs taking into account their ethnicity and the socio-economic background of the beneficiary groups. The CLOs will be trained to sensitively deliver messages to and obtain feedback from diverse beneficiaries including persons with disabilities and the elderly.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

This UNMAS project will ensure that the CLOs are composed of both men and women, taking into account the ethnicity of beneficiaries and stakeholders, in order to effectively reach out to the people targeted and ensure that the people are able to share their feedback and information in a safe and free manner. During the EORE sessions, contact numbers for the feedback and complaint mechanism as well as to report suspected explosive hazards will be shared.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

This UNMAS project will have both male and female CLOs within a team in order to create a safe and free space for information sharing and reporting. During the EORE sessions, the CLOs will share their contact number, through which beneficiaries can anonymously report a case if needed.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

This UNMAS project will ensure that the beneficiaries’ feedback is collected in a safe and free manner during Non-Technical Surveys (NTS) and EORE by deploying gender-mixed CLOs, and that all feedback is incorporated into the project. The feedback on suspected explosive hazards will be utilized to prioritize tasks, deploy teams for survey and clearance, and to update the IMSMA database.
