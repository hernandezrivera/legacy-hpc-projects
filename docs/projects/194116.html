Project Summary

The objective of the project is to address survival needs of the most vulnerable crisis- affected households through the provision of emergency Multi- Purpose Cash Assistance through one off or three rounds. The project will target 3,000 HHs representing 18,000 beneficiaries (based on the calculation 6 members per HHs) in Ar-Raqqa, Menbij, Kobane (and Deir-ez Zor if access allows). This will provide to newly displaced HHs and communities facing emergency gaps in humanitarian service provision and ability to meet their survival needs.

3 - Needs

Throughout 2022, the macro-economic situation across Syria deteriorated sharply, and this trend is accelerating into 2023. Currency depreciation, soaring prices (especially on food), reduced fiscal revenue, and increasing domestic debt have plunged additional segments of the population into humanitarian need. According to REACH HSOS data from Sep 2022, since late 2020 NES has been experiencing drought conditions due to reduced flow in the Euphrates River and a reduction in groundwater levels. The drought has not only affected access to water but also to electricity, agricultural livelihoods, food security, and health in general. Many households continue to live in fear of conflict, displacement, destruction of property, and injury or death. By the end of 2021, 6.9 million people across Syria were living in internal displacement because of conflict and violence, the highest figure worldwide. Around 80% of displaced households have been uprooted for four or more years. Disasters including floods, storms and drought also trigger displacement and force many people already displaced by conflict to flee again. While fighting has somewhat ebbed across the country, hostilities have intensified in frontlines in the northwest, northeast and south, driving displacement. And due to the existence of markets and access possibilities, the emergency cash support does not constitute a negative on this.

4 - Implementation of activities

DCA will utilize MPC as a mechanism for newly displaced HHs with a significant gap in assistance. At community level, this will be defined in collaboration with coordination mapping, needs analysis, and severity score. If it is not possible to cover the entire community in needs, HHs with the highest vulnerability will be selected. In addition to the provision of MPC support (3 rounds), where possible, DCA will try facilitate linkages to other humanitarian support to ensure needs can be addressed longer-term. Besides that, DCA will collect primary data and undertake rapid market analysis to confirm the availability of core goods and services. Assessment and PDM will be conducted prior to and after cash distributions and include HH level surveys as well as trader and consumer surveys, and KIIs to inform the relevance, Cash assistance has been deemes the best modality, due to its flexibility, as people can better prioritise their needs, as well as efficiency, both in terms of cost and timeline for implementation, and effectives, when thinking about economies of scale and long-time multiplier effects on the local economy. DCA will seek to mitigate possible tension between IDPs, returnees, and host communities through community engagement. DCA will ensure that all community stakeholders have access to DCAs FCS which includes dedicated hotline numbers, complaints boxes and Email as well as the ab ility to submit feedback and complaints in person.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Tension due to jealousies between IDPs, Returnees and host communities. Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigation Measures: 1. Targeting, where possible, at community level where emergent needs are widespread and impacting the majority HHs; 2. Where required, targeting will be defined in collaboration with local community leaders who will support also support in defining initial assessment lists; 3. Include consideration of conflict dynamics in defining of targeting and where possible ensure complementary support is provided to nearby communities not receiving MPC support. Protection Risk: Older women with disabilities are seen as “easy targets” for theft after cash or vouchers are delivered. Mitigation Measures: Identify safe delivery mechanism; ensure safety after delivery; monitor, feedback and response mechanisms in place. Protection Risk: Individuals do not wish to register with the agency for fear that gender identity or sexual orientation could be determined and lead to discrimination as a direct result of participating in a cash assistance intervention. Mitigation Measures: Explore options to ensure confidentiality; alternative / discreet delivery mechanisms; broad targeting criteria to avoid the stigma.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA staff members or partner staff members.Likelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation measures: DCA Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about the provision of available services and who services are for. DCA PSEA focal point in coordination with HR will conduct training and refreshers to project staff and partner staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DCA will establish a complaint box that will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentiality. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by the member of complaints handling team or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. DCA follows principles of Do No Harm across its project activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project location impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas and threated the safety and security of DCA project staff and beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOP), and conducts regular security- related training for staffs. Project Management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation, and evacuation plans remain in place. Risk: Local authority- imposed COVID-19 curfews in NES prevent DCA project staff from moving and/ or working, and/ or local authority-imposed curfews in NES/ KRI prevent hawala agents from transferring money for distribution and salaries. Assumptions: Project locations will remain accessible and DCA will continue to have permission to operate (through modified activities to protect beneficiaries and staff) and Hawalas will be able to continue transferring money. Mitigation measures: DCA staff will follow local authority- imposed curfews and restrictions related to COVID-19. DCA will regularly communicate with hawala agents to ensure supply chains are operational. DCA will communicate with other NGOs and NES Forum should selected hawala agent is not able to transfer funds.
