Project Summary

This intervention (approved 2019 HRP HPSE19-FSC-150621-1) is Action Against Hunger (AAH)’s immediate response to protect women, children and men from external shocks affecting their food security status and livelihoods. The targeted beneficiaries are vulnerable small-scale farmers who base their income on agricultural activities and the viability of these livelihoods are currently threatened by the rapidly degrading economic context.
This project has been developed to contribute to the achievement of the three FSS objectives (O1) Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced, (O2) Households suffering from limited of access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs, and (O3) FSS activities, including advocacy and awareness, are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, and partnership with key national ministries is strengthened.
The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability to affected population (AAP) as well as participation and empowerment.

1 - Needs

Despite the relative absence of armed conflict since 2014, the situation in GS continues to deteriorate. Poverty rates reached 60%, deep poverty more than 42%. Unemployment rates increased up to 54% (HNO, 2019) disproportionately impacting women and youth. The level of food insecurity remains high with 62% of households severely or moderately food insecure. Female HHH, PLW are particularly vulnerable to food insecurity and malnutrition (HNO 2019).Historically, the agriculture sector was the backbone of Gaza economy: agriculture played a large role in providing employment opportunities, especially in times of crisis when it was difficult to work in other sectors. These characteristics enhance the role of agriculture in strengthening the resilience of Palestinians and increasing their capacities to adapt. Since 2006, the agricultural sector has witnessed a significant decline. Although agricultural production has grown in absolute terms, its contribution to GDP has declined to about 4.5% in the first Quarter of 2019 (PCBS 2019) and its contribution to job creation has declined to 5.7% in the same period (PCBS 2018). Women in agriculture are particularly disadvantaged, despite being a significant unregistered portion of the workers (22.5%). This is reflected in limited economic opportunities, restricted mobility and limited technical skills, hence marginalizing their role in the agricultural production process. The decline in the sector is caused by different factors; the blockade imposed on Gaza restricting access to inputs of agricultural production (seeds, seedlings, fertilizers and pesticides) and material to build/rehabilitate productive assets; fragmentation of land ownership due to passing ownerships from one generation to another, that increases production cost, reduces farmers bargaining power hence increases the chance of land sale thus agricultural land loss in favour of urbanization.In July 2019, AAH team launched a rapid assessment on agricultural needs of farmers in the GS. Below the main recommendations:-Financial Assistance and Support: Package of financial assistance and support to help farmers across GS in restoring production assets and improving their lives. This covers a wide range of needs and required support: rehabilitation of agricultural land; provision of agricultural inputs (pesticides, fertilizers, seedlings); maintenance of greenhouses; procurement of needed greenhouse materials, water storage and drip irrigation system; repair poultry and livestock farms as well as procure livestock fodders and veterinary medicines.-Improved market system: Improving the market system and market access to address weak marketing capacities that negatively influence the farmer’s income and hinder the achievement of self-sufficiency.-Capacity building for farmers to improve the farmer's capabilities in good agricultural planning and practices; se of Integrated Pest Management system; safe agricultural practices; farm management; new farming techniques and introduction of new products.-Improved Productivity and Profitability: continuous access to quality and affordable agricultural inputs and supplies; provision of temporary employment (CfW); supervision/quality control over imported pesticides and fertilizers; improve agriculture infrastructures facilities (agricultural roads); improve quality of local products; introduce economic stimulation packages, improve crop pattern and diversity (type and quantity) based on consumers’ needs.-Enhance Access to Water: Rehabilitation of agricultural water wells and irrigation networks; introducing new irrigation technologies; water harvesting; provision of suitable and non-traditional water sources such as refined sewage water and sea water desalination.-Use of alternative power sources: Investing in the application and use of renewable energy as a sensible and strategic direction to support economic development and overcome implication of energy crisis on the sector.

2 - Activities

The project activities were developed to be delivered to the most vulnerable HHs in three packages of support: (i) Immediate relief responses to alleviate immediate needs by supporting vulnerable HHs (prioritizing HHs including member(s) with disabilities) to react and cope with shocks that are affecting their food security status; and therefore enable HHs to protect their livelihoods, increase productivity and improve their food and nutrition security status (immediate relief responsive actions=70%). (ii) Activities that enhance people’s medium-term livelihood protection (high impact remedial actions=20%), while ensuring adequate food security and living conditions, restoring people’s dignity, preventing secondary shocks and risks, with a particular focus on preventing the adoption of negative coping mechanisms. (iii) Create and improve a consolidation of structural changes in the described context of vulnerability (long-term actions for environment building=10%). Actions under this part will conduct a change in HH attitudes while addressing the underlying causes of humanitarian vulnerabilities. A1: Immediate/urgent relief actions to rehabilitate existing and not fully functioning key productive assets. This activity will target 210 vulnerable HHs (145 male-headed and 65 female-headed) in Rafah, Khan Yunis, Deir Albalah and Gaza Governorates. Small-scale farmers that are facing the consequences of the current lack of economic resources in Gaza Strip which has squeezed their capacity to repair or conduct good maintenance for their key agricultural infrastructures such as productive lands, water ponds, irrigation systems, etc. The electricity shortage is affecting their productivity and their capacity to create agricultural jobs. Because of that, under this activity, the selected families will be supported to rehabilitate 300 dunums of agricultural open lands (150 farmers: 120 males and 30 female), 30 water ponds (30 farmers: 25 males and 5 females), and 60 solar units (30 of those will be installed in the rehabilitated water ponds) will be installed to reduce expenses and mitigate livelihoods impact from the electricity shortage affecting poultry farmers and improve the irrigation capacity of the beneficiaries. Farmers will be also provided with an emergency package of agricultural inputs such as fertilizers, pesticides, seeds and tools. A2: Providing support to enhance people’s medium-term livelihood protection while ensuring adequate food security and living conditions. Under this activity, a range of cash transfer options (accessible to people with disabilities) will be established to support economically vulnerable HHs in the targeted area to help them to prevent secondary shocks and risks, with a particular focus on preventing the adoption of negative copings mechanisms. Thus, this activity will target 275 economically poor HHs (225 male and 50 female HH, with specific focus on HHs who care for member(s) with disabilities) particularly encouraging the involvement and ownership of women to be supported to restore livelihood actions that are affected by the current dire economic situation. Based on the context analysis conducted to prepare this project and the consulted families’ suggestions the actions are Cash for Work opportunities (with support of UWAC) and cash to expand the type of sources of HHs'income and production level. A3: Enhancing the nutritional practices of the families in the targeted areas through ongoing community awareness campaigns addressing the specific needs of women (primarily responsible for the nutritional care of the family) and children (approximately 1500 HH).Under this activity, selected HHs (prioritizing HHs with member(s) with disabilities) will benefit from technical training sessions on agroecology and organic farming. Community awareness campaigns will be conducted, based on community suggestions on most critical thematic areas, to improve practices and increase knowledge of nutrition.

3 - Indicators

The proposed intervention will provide immediate relief responses to support vulnerable HHs (women, men and children) so they can react and cope with shocks that are affecting their food security status; enable to protect their livelihoods, increase productivity and improve their food and nutrition security status. This includes HHs who base their income on agriculture activities and cannot maintain or rehabilitate their key productive assets; and farmers who are at risk of food insecurity, whose livelihoods were affected by the current situation, with family members unable to work (with physical or mental disabilities, medical cases), with a high number of dependents and do not have a stable source of income. The expected results are the following: Improved production and productivity capacity for vulnerable farmers: rehabilitation of agricultural open lands, water ponds, installation of alternative source of electricity, provision of an emergency package of agricultural inputs. Increased capacity of families, with particular focus on women to prevent the adoption of negative coping mechanisms: Cash transfers options in order to support economically vulnerable HHs in order to develop CfW opportunities cash to expand the type of sources of HHs'income and production level. Increased awareness, knowledge and practices, improved appropriate nutritional practices, agroecology and organic farming. Core promotion messages will be tailored to community needs based on focus group discussions conducted at the beginning of project implementation, and focused on technical training on organic farming, management of sustainable agroecosystems, reduction of using chemicals in the crop production, nutrition to reduce the need for responsive activities in the future. Technical trainings will follow the priorities set by the MOA in Gaza. Through FSS and the local partner, the project will establish a communication channel to ensure full coordination and with the relevant contact in MoA. AAH will contribute to the exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community to be used for advocacy purposes, which will be fed back to the target communities.Expected indicators and targets: •300 dunums made productive or with improved production capacity (full and partial land rehabilitation, piping and irrigation system, seeds and seedlings distribution, etc.) •1200 m3 of water storage rehabilitated or provided •180kW of renewable power supply systems installed. •485 HHs (headed by farmers, herders, breeders, receiving inputs and services •20 localities improved their capacity to react and cope with shocks affecting their food security status through a timely access to immediate relief responses. This includes measuring the improvement in the number of women who did not resort to negative coping mechanisms as a result of the initiative. •Living conditions for 11,315 women, men, girls and boys are improved. To measure the improvement of the living conditions of the BNFs, AAH will conduct a baseline and endline survey. Both exercises will track and compare the following proxy indicators: (i) Coping Strategy Index (CSI), (ii) HH Food Insecurity Access Scale (HFIAS), (iii) HH Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS), Food Consumption Score (FCS) and Minimum Dietary Diversity for Women (24-MDD-W). To ensure accountability, AAH has Beneficiary Feedback and PSEA mechanisms, allowing beneficiaries to provide their suggestions and complaints on the activities and are designed to increase their protection, in compliance with sensitive data management and adhering to “Do No Harm”. A phone number will be distributed to target communities enabling BNFs to report feedback or complaints relating to the project. To further ensure protection is mainstreamed in project implementation, the indicator “% of project beneficiaries (male/female) reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” will be measured.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

As part of AAH accountability to affected populations, a feedback mechanism emphasizing fluid and transparent communication will be in place. It serves as a mechanism for project staff, beneficiaries, host communities, local authorities, and other key stakeholders to share and respond to project performance, data and information. Beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries alike will be able to reach out to the project with complaints, suggestions, and any other feedback. The information collected will be regularly fed into our programming to ensure no harm.The mechanism will consist of a mixture of channels, SMS/phone numbers, email addresses, and feedback and complaint desks when possible, accessible to people with disability. AAH will reach out to beneficiaries through regular consultation processes (assessments, surveys, FGDs, community meetings, calls on a randomly selected sample etc.).During the assessment at the inception phase, beneficiaries from different ages/genders/disabilities will be consulted to identify their preferences in terms of providing feedback. The mechanism will take into account the perception or possible reservations that the target population might have in relation to their use, especially for complaints mechanisms. Vulnerable groups groups (including people with disabilities) will receive additional sensitization to make sure they feel comfortable in using the mechanism. Beneficiary feedback will be analyzed and presented in a systematic way – also to project partners - and the MEAL Officer will be in continuous contact with the Coordination team. Data will be sex-and-age disaggregated to show possible trends and understand barriers that might need to be addressed, specifically in relation to gender dynamics. Emphasis will be placed on closing the feedback loop, meaning that the person who raised a complaint will always receive feedback on their case and that as far as possible, beneficiary data will be used to inform programmatic decision-making. AAH is an active member of the Disability Working Group, AAH food security team participates and uses other member organisations’ advice and expertise to inform disability inclusion throughout the project cycle.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In 2019, AAH has conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent the month of March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. Also, AAH started engaging with Humanity & Inclusion as inclusion experts to sensitise and inform AAH’s programming. For instance, instead of asking directly if people have a disability, AAH is increasingly using ‘The Washington Group Short Set of Questions’ to detect people with disabilities. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, AAH started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs.
