Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates that the number of crisis-affected people in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria is 14.2 million (48.7 % male and 51.3% female), of which 6 million are in acute need. The above figure of people in need includes 6.7 million protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs); estimated; 320,000 spontaneous IDPs return; 75,000 refugees return; 438,000 Palestine refugees and the rest overburdened host communities. Of the population in need of humanitarian assistance, 70% are accessible from within Syria according to the Syria Response Access Dynamic report by OCHA (December 2018).
Overall, 12.4 million people are estimated to be food insecure (including 1.3 million severely food insecure and 9.5 million moderately food insecure) and 1.9 million at risk of food insecurity. In this context, FAO has designed a project to support the coordination of the Food Security and Agriculture Sector to ensure the food security needs of 7.5 million people in need are adequately addressed by all partners responding to the Syria crisis from different hubs.
The objective of the proposed action is to strengthen the effectiveness and quality of the food security responses (including all hubs) based on evidence, capacity building and strong coordination within the Food and Agriculture Sector and across sectors. Project outputs include:
• Output 1: Accessibility of food security and agriculture information for stakeholders response planning improved.
• Output 2: Capacity for national and subnational Food Security and Agriculture sector strengthened.
• Output 3: Capacity for subnational coordination of Food Security and Agriculture sector built.
This project is expected to contribute to improve planning (evidence-based decision–making) and supporting timely, efficient and effective responses to the needs of affected population and avoiding duplication to ensure efficient use of limited available resources. And to ensure that affected population have equal access to assistance and services in proportion to need and without any barriers or discrimination, paying special attention to vulnerable groups (women – Children and People with disabilities..), who may have difficulty accessing assistance and services.

4 - Needs

he Food Security and Agriculture Cluster/Sector, co led by FAO and the World Food Programme (WFP), was established to coordinate responses to the sector. Operating from Syria and southern Turkey, the Sector coordinates activities implemented by more than 100 partners across all hubs, and links with inter-sector coordination. FAO and WFP, as cluster lead agencies, jointly contribute to the sector secretariat, which includes coordinators, information management officers and technical experts. Annually, the sector/cluster develops key components of strategies including the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) based a number of assessments and technical studies undertaken by the sector and the lead agencies to guide responses. The current strategy of the Sector aims to ensure food security for all crisis-affected Syrians through food assistance, support to agriculture-based livelihoods to increase productive capacity of households complemented with light rehabilitation of critical agriculture-related infrastructures that support production to ensure adequate food consumption and dietary diversity for the most affected people. This is to ultimately reduce the number of people adopting negative coping mechanisms while supporting early and medium term recovery of critical agricultural/livelihood assets and essential services related to food security as well as linkages with value chains. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: repercussions of the COVID-19 crisis and the restrictions on movement at times between and within the regions and governorates, which leads to difficult personal communication and meetings.This risk will be countered by enhancing work, communication and meetings through communication platforms (Skype, Zoom, WebEx, etc.)Risk 2 : The weakness of the communications infrastructure (telephone and mobile communication networks, and the Internet) in the Syrian governorates and regions,Enhancing communication by providing employees with the necessary means to connect to the InternetRisk 3 : lack of coordination, cooperation and information exchange between the national authorities concerned with the food security and agriculture sector,Exerting more efforts and increasing the awareness of representatives of national authorities of the importance of coordination and exchange of information necessary for planning and sound decisions in the field of food security,Risk 4 : Monitoring systems disrupt and the information chains for monitoring agricultural and food security indicators for long periods of time in several regions and governorates, this affects the quality of the data and the access to a complete perception of the environmental and natural factors affecting food security.Promote reliance on remote sensing methods on electronic platforms (especially the FAO website “GWIS) that provide digital information and the required maps and their compatibility with field information.
