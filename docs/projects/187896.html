Project Summary

Building on the rehabilitation and re-equipment of PHC facilities in Medair’s health programming, integrated preventative and curative nutrition services will be provided at each facility and wherever possible in the community through community health workers (CHWs). Medair will contribute to the following strategic outcomes set by the nutrition sector:
- Provide facility and community-based one-to-one counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding and maternal nutrition practices
- Provide Micronutrient supplementation to pregnant women and children
- Building partners capacity in Nutrition program, coordination and management
- Early identification and referral of Acute Malnutrition cases
- Ensuring adequate coverage of treatment of Acute Malnutrition for children, pregnant and lactating mothers
- Provision of Quality services in CMAM centers
- Strengthen/Establish Nutrition surveillance system
Medair will contribute to these objectives through the activities outlined below;
Facility based activities will include malnutrition screening for CU5 and PLW, treatment of severe and moderate acute malnutrition, provision of micronutrient supplements for CU5 and PLW and iron supplementation for pregnant and postnatal women. IYCF counselling including breastfeeding support and promotion of appropriate complementary feeding will also be provided, both in health facilities and in communities. Relevant clinic staff will receive both formal and on the job training. Therapeutic food and supplements will be provided through a well-established partnership with UNICEF and WFP. In total, Medair expects to support around 2,000 beneficiaries with SAM or MAM.

CHWs will work in pairs in the clinic catchment area, delivering targeted relevant nutrition messages to households and providing malnutrition screening to CU5 and PLWs. They will also deliver health, hygiene and disability messages and act as a link to the PHC clinic, referring any malnutrition cases identified. In line with MoH guidance the CHWs are currently given a performance related quarterly gift in kind incentive worth up to approximately 60 USD.

Medair will target locations which are newly accessibly, hard to reach or have high severity needs. Planned locations include Rural Damascus, Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor, rural Aleppo, Hama, rural Homs, southern Idleb and Dar'a and Quneitra. Medair has already established access and initiated multisectoral programming in these areas.

4 - Needs

It is estimated that some 5.5 million (up from 4.9 million) women and children in Syria are considered vulnerable and in need of nutrition services (HNO 2022). These include malnourished children and pregnant and lactating women. Areas with catastrophic nutrition needs are in Dara'a, Deir-ez-Zor and al Raqqa. In 58 sub-districts, 25% of children under 5 suffered from stunting, and in 44 subdistricts 46% (up from 27.4% in 2019) of children under 5 suffered from anaemia. In 2019, there was poor IYCF practices such as low exclusive breastfeeding rates at 28.4%. Households have decreased purchasing power and therefore access to breast-milk substitutes. Nutrition sector partners report that the breastfeeding practices increased, and this trend was observed by Medair LQAS 2021. In 2021, Medair observed in select locations (DEZ/ALP/IDL/QUN/DAR) children under 5 at a 14% General Acute Malnutrition rate according to PHC level. Through RNAs, Medair has noted that communities in Deir-ez-Zor rely on food assistance at irregular intervals, mainly from SARC and WFP. Deir ez Zor and surrounding areas remain at critical risk in terms of nutrition needs, this is reflected by the high number of malnourished children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women benefiting from Medair’s support to the malnutrition treatment programme in the PHCs. Out of 2146 CU5 screened, 158 were malnourished (GAM rate =8,6%).

5 - Implementation of activities

Integrated nutrition activities will be implemented in PHCCs already rehabilitated and reequipped in Medair’s health programme. Activities include screening and treatment of SAM and MAM in all CU5 and PLW, IYCF counselling of PLWs and provision of appropriate micronutrients to up to 2000 CU5 and PLWs. Cases of severe malnutrition with complications will be referred appropriately. Clinic staff receive an initial IYCF/CMAM training and on the job training. Provision of key messages and counselling in clinics will promote appropriate IYCF behaviours. Therapeutic supplements are expected to be supplied as GIK from UNICEF/WFP. Monthly supervision visits will monitor quality. Through the CHP, CHWs will provide house to house malnutrition screening and IYCF promotion. About 20 CHWs (mostly women) are recruited working in pairs, conducting 15-20 HH visits/week. CHWs will receive a training, followed by bimonthly refreshers. Topics include IYCF, malnutrition screening, communication skills, first aid and disability. All activities will be closely coordinated with MoH/UNICEF/WFP. Medair/DoH are planning monthly joint supervision visits to the supported PHCCs, dependent on DoH availability and security clearance. Wherever permitted, community members will be consulted during assessments. Beneficiary feedback will be sought through exit interviews if permitted. Monitoring visits by UNICEF/WFP will be supported.
