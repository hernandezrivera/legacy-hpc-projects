Project Summary

This proposal aligns with needs response of conflict-affected populations in Sinjar and Salah Al-Din areas. RNVDO offers a distinctive contribution to expand immediate access to resident or returnee communities in need, and mobilize the investment in physical rehabilitation of schools, protective environments, access to learning, and psychosocial care necessary to help children affected by this crisis overcome the consequences of a conflict, and capacity building of teaching faculty. Facilitating teachers' training to add quality and enhance motivation and prepare them to host special summer school vacation recreation classes have been factored in. Necessary educational supplies and recreation kits/material will be incorporated in the project implementation phases. It is also envisaged that this strategic approach will provide the motivation for displaced families to return. Accelerated rehabilitation of schools focusing on inclusiveness (disabilities and access) and other key infrastructure will motivate community to return RNVDO will offer incentives towards livelihoods opportunities for vulnerable sections of communities ensuring at least 30% women beneficiaries. Inclusiveness will be factored in through sensitization of families and school authorities/teachers for school enrollment of able and disabled children through special events and campaigns, as also peace education, positive discipline

Needs

RNVDO as an active Education cluster partner and a member of the SAG, contributed to several education assessments on schools that require rehabilitation.Our assessments in both Sinjar and Salah Al Din have identified priority areas that need to be targeted to improve access to schools for returnees and the host community.The current situation in both areas indicates that Iraqi children and youth have faced and continue facing, an increased intensity and number of risk factors, such as non-existent or weakened and overwhelmed school systems.The five major barriers to school access identified were the financial burden on parents to attend schools (74.09%) (RNVDO barriers to education assessment, 2018) , teachers not being able to give sufficient time to students (56.48%) primarily due to overcrowding and the extreme shortage of qualified teaching staff. Other barriers identified were security concerns, lack of qualified teaching staff, and poor infrastructure.In terms of school infrastructure, 50% of the assessed locations had partially rehabilitated schools, which allowed students to return to their daily school lives, but prevented safe and inclusive spaces for them.School assets were also assessed, and it was reported that the children lacked a conducive school environment in terms of climate control, other unavailable assets were the provision of meals, access ramps for disabled students and playgrounds.In Salah Al Din, four schools have been selected (Shirqat and Baiji) based on the level of damage and students accommodated. The total number of children targeted are 1184. For the total number of students, currently only 16 teachers are present, according to the Directorate of Education another 16 qualified teachers are required.The schools that have been selected in Salah Al Din have been assessed to be partially damaged, requiring minimal physical rehabilitation.In Sinjar, four schools have been selected, in Khanasor and Gulhba. The total number of children targeted are 1280, with 20 teachers who are currently employed in the four schools.In Sinjar, specifically, the major reasons for limited access to school was reported to be the direct costs, lack of qualified teaching staff, security concerns and lack of learning materials.RNVDO with its access and strong community engagement in these areas has used its unique advantage to work in restricted areas to map out needs that will be used to accelerate the proposed project implementation to close the education gap.

Activities

The proposed project activities include:1. Rehabilitation of classrooms, classified as partially damaged, including functional WASH facilities.2. Asset replacement where required 3. Incentives for school rehabilitation to increase household income promoting community ownership4. Community sensitization on importance of education including Back to school campaigns5. Evening shift classes- Non formal education to target dropouts, children above the legal school age6. Remedial classes- dropout children7. Training on positive and inclusive teaching methods, and risk education.8. Promotion of life skills in schools9. Liaise and monitor distribution of school educational supplies through MoE/DoE and UNICEF10. Capacity Building for teachers on pedagogy, PSS- life skills, positive discipline, GBV, teaching methodology, communication and hygiene promotion11. Linking with Directorate of Education and UNICEF for a quality teaching program activity12. Facilitating the creation of PTA13. Social cohesion and peace building through Sports, and recreational activities

Mitigation Measures

All RNVDO programs are developed and implemented to be conflict sensitive and work to prevent negative reactions (Do No Harm) but equally to maximize positive impacts of communication delivery. It strives to understand the context in which it is operating, particularly intergroup/secretarian and ethnic relations. RNVDO has fully functional offices in Erbil and Salah Al Din, with security personnel monitoring the security situation.RNVDO is informing and coordinating with security actors in Sinjar, and works closely with NOC, HOC, and other actors to keep abreast of security threats in the area.In terms of child safety within the schools, strategies will be developed to embed a culture of child safety, with clear child safety reporting mechanisms along with performance management procedures. Adequate monitoring will be done in all the construction sites, and child safe information and awareness for visitors, staff, volunteers and contractors will be disseminated.

How is your project accountable to affected population

RNVDO is dedicated to fulfilling the five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP). The organization strives towards this principle by promoting community leadership, maintaining transparency in its work, opening feedback channels, promoting participation, and continuously monitoring and evaluating its work.RNVDO will involve the affected population throughout program implementation and monitoring and evaluation activities according to IASC Commitments to Affected Populations by facilitating and engaging communities in dialogues to empower and enhance ownership of the project.RNVDO will empower local communities through the CFW activities for school’s rehabilitation. This will increase immediate household income for selected households participating in activities and in particular empower women through their engagement in economic activities.To reiterate, it is envisioned by RNVDO, this intervention with all its proposed activities and the specific approach used is expected to expand community mobilization and involvement in a socially inclusive support network that will raise awareness on social issues (engagement of out of school, drop out children and youth in anti-social activities like drug abuse, radicalization and joining militant groups) and foster cohesion. RNVDO's approach is participative throughout the project period with a built-in feedback mechanism. Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism (BFM) will include a beneficiary helpdesk as well as UNOPS hotline and will be set up in close coordination with Protection cluster. A pathway for referrals of sensitive protection cases will be established, possibly making use of RNVDO Protection Services Map to identify adequate social services entities and structures in proximity to project sites. The project will be phased out by the end of the project cycle in a sustainable manner. The program will be designed to inculcate knowledge, skills and tangible assets within the fixed time taking into consideration the funding cycles.RNVDO will ensure that we achieve program impacts, which will guide the time line for successful phasing out without jeopardizing the project outcomes. The monitoring of the exit strategy benchmarks will be integrated into the overall program’s monitoring and evaluation plan. M&E department will ensure to use more one tool for accountability. Other mechanisms will include: Liaising with cluster - Hotlines - Complaint boxes - FGDs - Personal Interviews.
