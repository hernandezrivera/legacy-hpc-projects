Project Summary

The intervention is part of a project aiming at securing the basic needs and providing integrated community-based protection activities in Karm Jazmati, Seken Shebebi, Karm al Tarrab, and Dahret Awwad. The project is held by Caritas Germany (CS), back-funded by the German Federal Foreign Ministry (GFO) and implemented from 01.04.2019 -31.12.2022 by Caritas Syria (CS). It was initially designed based on the results of a needs assessment conducted in Karm Jazmati in January 2019, and has been, upon extension and top-up in 2021, adjusted to changing needs as per M/E results. In course of the project phase two, which started on 01.04.2021, the project locations have shifted: While Jazmati is still the main project location where the Community Center is based, Jabal Badro has been taken out of the intervention since there have been other actors providing basic assistance and CS has, under another project, started implementing Livelihood and Early Recovery activities. Coordination meetings with other (I)NGOs as well as the sub-sectors FSL, and protection, three new neighborhoods bordering Jazmati, have been included in the project. In direct and continuous contact with the FSL and protection sector, CS has started to implement BA and protection activities in the new areas as of April 2021 and is considered the main actor for these sectors in Jazmati, Seken Shebebi, Karm al Tarrab, and Dahret Awwad.
Following a participatory approach, the needs have been regularly assessed, i.e., via PDMs applying a proportional piling method, and FDGs. Furthermore, a robust FCM has contributed to ensure the engagement and thus self-identification of the needs in the community. Hence, throughout the project lifetime, the engagement of all community members has been promoted, establishing a two-way communication with the community. This has ensured a timely incorporation of community feedback.
The regular coordination with the Aleppo-based sub-sectors Protection, CP, GBV, and FSL as well as the application of a protection scoring system,CS ensures the inclusion of high at-risk groups in protection activities while avoiding duplication of efforts. The community center in Jazmati has been well received by the residents of Jazmati since its opening in 2019 and continues to be the central point for the provision of integrated community-based protection services, to women, adolescent girls, children and elderly.
The center follows an open-door policy, ensuring the active engagement of the community, allowing real-time evaluations and timely adjustments of activities as per beneficiaries’ feedback. In response to the feedback collected from beneficiaries, i.e. via PDMs, satisfaction surveys, and FGDs, and following the strategy of the protection sector, the activities are adjusted in 2021-22. Following an inclusive approach, non-specialized PSS, and awareness sessions for women, children, elderly, and men are planned. Contributing to the reduction of GBV risks, literacy courses for women will be provided. Applying interactive learning approaches, informal education activities (Maths, Arabic, and English) for school-aged children will contribute to reducing child protection risk factors. Further, capacity strengthening activities for the CS team will be offered to further increase the responding capacity of CS.

4 - Needs

To inform the new project, a mixed method approach is applied. Secondary Data, such as the HNO Syria 2021, are considered. Primary data was collected via structured KII with UN-agencies in Aleppo (UNHCR, UNICEF, UNFPA and WFP), the moukhtar of Jazmati, Caritas Sister Organisations, and other relevant (I)NGOs such as Al-Ishan. The analysis of this data suggested a gap in the provision of protection services in Seken Shebebi, Karm al Tarrab, and Dahret Awwad. Thus, groups of women, men, children, and elderly living in these areas, will be included in the protection activities in the CC in Jazmati. The needs assessment further includes the review of the most recent M/E data, such as the internal mid-term review conducted in January 2020, results of satisfaction surveys, post-tests, and FGDs with participants of the protection activities. At the time of writing, CS is in the process of gathering more detailed information about the demographics and needs of the residents in the project locations.According to the HNO 2021 and other secondary data sources, humanitarian needs in Syria are deepening, with a wide array of specific protection risks and needs: The population in Syria is facing increasingly complex and interlinked protection needs that have the potential to create long lasting or irreversible consequences if they remain unmet. Additionally, the deterioration of the economy, and the COVID 19 pandemic have further fueled marginalization and the neglect of groups with specific needs such as the elderly and people with disabilities. The HNO 2021 indicates that Jebel Samaan, the district where all four targeted project locations are located, is ranked "3", reflecting major protection needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

All activities are planned with C19 prevention measures. MEAL is an integral part of the project cycle, based on participatory planning methods and continuous integration of lessons learned through PDMs, satisfaction surveysand FGDs.Objective 1: Protection risks of vulnerable groups (women, men, children, and elderly) in 4 neighborhoods are reduced through integrated community-based approaches, including awareness raising, and psychosocial support. Activity 1.1: Provide protection-related awareness sessions to high at-risk groups of women, children, and men. Target: 244 persons. Activity 1.2 Provide non-specialized PSS for women, children, and elderly prone to protection risks, aiming to strengthen resilience of individuals and support healthy, and functioning community structures. Target: 340 persons. Objective 2: CS staff has increased capacity to respond to protection needs and provide high quality of services. Activity 2.1 Conduct trainings and supervision on outreach activities, referrals, protection assessment and analysis to improve quality provision of protection services. Target: 28 staff members. Objective 3: Risks of GBV for high at-risk women in the project areas are mitigated. Alphabetization courses will be provided in the CC by trained CS staff enhancing the empowerment of women in the community. Target: 80 women. Objective 4: Protection risks for school-aged girls and boys are decreased. Activity 4.1 Structured and sustained informal education courses (tutoring) will be provided to school aged children 7-14 years in the community center. With the support of a MHPSS specialist, the children are internally or externally referred to other activities or specialized services if needed. Target: 120 children.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Target groups of the project such as children, women, and elderly, are at particular risk of being affected by PSEA. The COVID19 pandemic as well as the economic hardship have increased the risks for these groups. During the design phase of the “new phase” of the project, these risks have been identified. Caritas follows a zero-tolerance strategy of sexual abuse and exploitation. Supported by an external consultant, CS has reviewed and updated its Safeguarding policies in the first quarter of 2021. On an organisational level, all staff members in Caritas have been trained on the safeguarding policy as well as the Code of Conduct which is binding for all staff and volunteers working in this project. In each CS regional office – as in Aleppo - there is a Safeguarding Focal Point appointed to ensure that the policies are followed by each staff member and FCM for beneficiaries and staff are understood and followed-up on. Verbally as well as with sensation material visible in the center or during distributions, beneficiaries are informed about their rights and about safe feedback channels.
