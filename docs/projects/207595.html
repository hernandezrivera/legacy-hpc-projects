Project Summary

The project aims to assist the protracted Rohingya crisis by supporting the vulnerable Rohingya community and host communities through long term impact and the promotion of sustainable livelihoods, particularly emphasizing skill development. The project will focus on developing livelihood options for women, and youths of marginalized host communities and Rohingya camps to enhance their access to sustainable income sources in Myanmar and camp contexts in Bangladesh.
While this initiative enables refugees to maintain hope for the future and have dignified and productive lives in the camps, it will also support sustainable return and integration when conditions permit. It includes engagements in multi-sectoral camp-based activities, community works and livelihood options with using acquired competencies for livelihood options. Accordingly, strategies will be geared towards enhancing skills development, economic and social empowerment, and self-reliance of the Rohingya refugees for durable solutions.
MUKTI will provide support through targeted skill enhancement interventions that are to continue organizing 120 hours long non-formal skill development training support to the vulnerable youths and adults, including men and women (18-24 years) of the selected camp and host communities. This non-formal intervention includes on different technical issues, for 130 beneficiaries handicraft related training like Gas stove repairing, Blok batik, embroidery, jut craft etc). Based on the identified needs of skill trades, the project would provide center-based vocational training in different trades, for 20 beneficiaries are solar instalment and 50 beneficiaries are agricultural crop production. Upon completing the vocational training on solar panel maintenance, these 20 beneficiaries will be able to support as volunteers in other sectors which will open a window of opportunity for them to generate income. The training module on the above trades is developed aligning with Myanmar National Qualification Framework (MNQF) and ASEAN Qualifications Reference Framework (AQRF) standards. Each of the vocational training courses will be 360 hours in duration. The project will arrange a formal assessment of the training participants, where successful candidates will receive a certification to help them pursue their future education/training. It will also assist them in uplifting to the upper skills category in Rohingya camps and Myanmar as a skilled professional.
MUKTI plans to provide support for skills and livelihood activities in only one refugee camp (Camp 17), and including the host community of Ukhiya (Rajapalong and Palongkhali Unions)and Teknaf upazilla (Whykong and Hnilla unions).
The project will target 600 individuals (HC-400 & RC-200) . The participants of this intervention will be youths and adults, including men and women, others, and persons with disabilities (This will be addressed the questions by using Washinton Group short set) from segregated age groups.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

As the Rohingya crisis evolves and becomes more protracted, needs are increasingly becoming diverse and complex. 18% of households in Rohingya refugee camps were unable to access to employment, where 42% of households in host communities were unable to access income-generating activities. Besides, 3% of the households in refugee camps and 6% of households in host communities could not afford access to conditional cash (cash for work) compared to 2022. (Source: J-MSNA-2023)On the other hand, 71% of households in refugee camps and 36% in host communities reported barriers to income opportunities. 53% of households in refugee camps and 29% of households in host communities faced obstacles, and 27% of households in refugee camps had to wait a long time for volunteer engagement and cash for work in camps. 6% of households in refugee camps and 1% of households in host communities faced obstacles due to lack of opportunity for women (e.g. no other female staff). A need assessment conducted by UNHCR and UCEP in 2022 showed that 76% of the Rohingya Youth have not received any skills training since they arrived in the camps while 30.9% of Rohingya youth had received education while living in the camps. The baseline camp needs assessment conducted in 2022 highlights that 6.07% of Rohingya youth had participated in vocational training prior to their arrival in Bangladesh. Moreover, the results of the assessment also highlighted that, among female refugees, the major skills training requested included industrial sewing machine operation (26.5%), nursing (8.6%), community health worker (6.4%), midwifery (4.3%), agricultural production (3.8%), banking and nutrition (3.8%), etc. In contrast, the interests of the male refugee population were related to the occupations of community health worker (17.4%), masonry (14.7%), machine mechanics (13.6%), solar electrician and system maintenance (10.6%), electrician-house wiring (10%), plumber (8.1%), etc. The assessment found barriers to female participation in productive engagement due to stereotypical ideas about women's roles, low self-esteem due to low educational attainment, polygamy, unintended pregnancy, lack of opportunities and information, and a lack of family decision-making provisions. Entrepreneurship development and context-specific skills training were recommended as the two most important aspects of skill training. The trade for skills training under this project will be selected considering and assessing the UN/GoB framework, the demand by the Rohingya youths, the portability of skills to Myanmar, the needs as well as the possibility for volunteer engagement and market linkage within the camps. Moreover, as the experience of ongoing projects shows, there is not only a huge need and demand to offer vocational training but also to offer non-formal technical training to project participants of host and Rohingya communities on different issues.Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar generally have no regular income opportunities and lack the skills that would allow them to adapt quickly to bring change to their situation. Besides, half of the refugee workforce was not engaged in any type of income-generating activity. According to REVA-6 The highest deprivation in income was faced by female-headed households in the Rohingya camps followed by Rohingya male-headed households and male and female-headed households in the Bangladeshi community. The long-term humanitarian responses need to call for a review of the response and market linkage modalities currently in place and a rethink of humanitarian strategies for sustainable job market for Rohingya and host community. Greater emphasis should be placed on market linkage and strengthening linkages between sectors with a drive towards an integrated approach to enable meeting the essential needs of affected populations.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This proposed project is aligned with the rationalization principles of the LSDS Sector. The project will strongly adhere to all Rationalization Principles, emphasizing the adoption of an integrated approach, predictability and continuity. Mukti will implement this initiative by closely coordinating with its Protection, Gender, WASH, FSS, and other relevant sectors, promoting a comprehensive response to the protracted Rohingya crises. By supporting LSDS sector and aligning all activities within the same camps and regions, Mukti will ensure an integrated approach between various activities and cover refugee camps (4Ext and 17) including the Ukhiya and Teknaf host community. MUKTI will Coordinate with key partners and stakeholders including Sector, NGOs, Government of Bangladesh, line departments, Chamber of commerce, Disaster Management and Relief, NGO Affairs Bureau, RRRC (Rohingya Refugee Relief and Repatriation Commissioner), Deputy Commissioner, and UNO (Upazila Nirbahi Officer). Mukti Cox’s Bazar will actively participate in regular meetings and engage in 5W response, Sector meeting, CiC meetings, RRRC meetings, and DC office coordination meetings etc. All activities are aligned with the JRP following current Sectoral/Working Group objectives and strategy. We also actively participate in the relevant Sector and/or Working Group and coordination meetings. Notably, MUKTI has a strong government Liaison team to obtain the relevant approvals from the government promptly.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Mukti Cox’s Bazar will ensure mainstreaming of protection throughout the implementation of the activities. “Do no harm” is the guiding principle for implementing the activities for the vulnerable people. Privacy and confidentiality of the beneficiaries will be considered while designing and implementing the project. Beneficiaries will also receive awareness raising session to protect them from any unwanted incidents.Mukti Cox’s Bazar will deliver services and assistance in ways that preserve the physical integrity of individuals and communities, that are culturally appropriate and that avoid any unintended negative consequences. MUKTI will provide assistance and services according to needs and not on the basis of age, sex, gender, nationality, race, ethnic allegiance and so on. Services and assistance are provided in an adequate scale, within safe and easy reach, are known by the affected individuals and accessible by all groups including medical cases, disabled individuals, discriminated groups and the like. Mukti beneficiaries and volunteers provide feedback through different channels, which allow us to improve the project quality and prevent or mitigate situations.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Mukti Cox’s Bazar mainstreams an age, gender, and diversity (AGD) approach throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes of the proposed project. We collect data disaggregated by age, sex and disability for both analysis and programming purposes. As a result, operations are able to account for the differing needs of persons of concern with respect to their age, sex and disability. The proposed project will engage a variety of different groups, notably women, PwD, faith leaders, caregivers, parents, youth and adolescent from both Rohingya and Host Community, encouraging widespread participation. Awareness and mutual understanding will be raised through joint activities, the improvement of the shared environment and soft skills development. Mukti Cox’s Bazar will ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders receive information on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), including child protection, along with complaints and feedback mechanisms to ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders can provide feedback or raise concerns or complaints on project implementation. Mukti organisational Code of Conduct, PSEA policy, Regulations on Child Protection are binding for all employees and all collaborators need to commit to the key principles of the CoC. Moreover, Mukti has standardized recruitment process. Reference checks are carried out as a mandatory part of this process. During the design and implementation of the project, MUKTI participatory process and complaints and feedback mechanism contribute to age and child safeguarding as well.Among the targeted participants, at least 60% will comprise women, while 1% will comprise persons with disabilities.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to affected populations (AAP) is an active commitment to use power responsibly by taking into account giving account to and being held to account by the people humanitarian organisations seek to assist. MUKTI will provide beneficiaries with a meaningful influence over decision-making in inclusive, non-discriminatory arrangements for diverse communities. Besides this, MUKTI will also put individual feedback mechanisms and innovative approaches to joint feedback mechanisms that can reinforce transparency and mutual accountability and have a positive impact. Project information will be shared with the beneficiaries effectively and transparently. MUKTI will ensure the inclusion of persons with disabilities and special needs through the project activities, which include the participation of the persons with disabilities, and taking the opinion during the designing & planning of any activities in the project and ensuring the transparency of the project.
