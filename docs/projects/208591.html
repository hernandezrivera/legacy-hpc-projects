Project Summary

LABDI is a women led organization, having a strong presence in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states with over 8 years’ experience working with at risk groups, vulnerable persons, community structures, community groups, internally displaced persons, returnees and host communities and government through the implementation of child protection, WASH, Nutrition, Education, GBV programs. LABDI have successfully implemented humanitarian and developmental projects which activities include Explosive ordnance risk education, establishing and strengthening community structures, case management, Advocacy, Campaign and awareness, Nutrition programs, WASH, working with women and caregivers, programs for in school and out of school children, youth sessions, psychosocial support activities for children and parents as well as other community impacted activities. LABDI is a key partner of GBVAOR, Mine Action WG, Education In Emergency WG, WASH, Nutrition (SAG NNGO forum co-chair), and is a leading member with the Borno Women led civil society organizations (Chair), BAY network of women led CSO and CPAOR SAG (coordinator). LABDI implementation works through community-based approach which ensures community active participation in project and enhances ownership and sustainability of project.
Through this project, in Damboa and Dikwa LGAs of Borno State, LABDI proposes to provide holistic care for malnourished pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls, and their children in targeted supplementary feeding programs through community-based platforms and using macro and micronutrient-rich foods that will be entirely sourced from the community. LABDI will establish a robust network of Community Care Groups (CCG) which will serve as the primary platform for nutrition service delivery, provision of quality messaging on optimum maternal and child nutrition, in addition to enhance linkage to other sectoral interventions. LABDI will engage and train community health workers (CHW) to provide technical and operational support to these groups. LABDI will also prioritize mothers and caregivers of underweight (low weight-for-age) or wasted (low weight-for-height) 1-5 months old infants as well as mothers and infants with breastfeeding difficulties for intensified, needs based MIYCN support; as thee groups of children, if not catered for, would translate to become part of the caseload of acute malnutrition once they turn 6 months. To achieve significant impact from this MIYCN intervention, LABDI will engage and train ‘’MIYCN Promoters’’ (IYCF-P) whose goal is to ensure needs based MIYCN support is provided to the above stated groups, based on their needs. Another crucial role of the IYCF-Ps would be consistent growth monitoring of these children to closely watch the progress of the children and their caregivers. LABDI will engage and train a team of Community Nutrition Mobilizers (CNMS) to conduct house-to-house screening of the mid-upper arm circumference of pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls (PBWG), and children 6-59months to identify and enroll those who are malnourished in care groups. The CNMs and health workers will also carry out anthropometric assessment (WFA and WFH) of children 0-5 months to identify undernourished (underweight or wasted) children to enroll their caregivers for intensified and needs-based MIYCN support.
Thrice during the life cycle of the project, LABDI will conduct mass MUAC screening for PBWG and children 6-59 months old to identify and enroll them into TSFP. In the same vein, LABDI will carry out mass WFH and WFA screening of under-6 children to identify undernourished infants and refer them and their caregiver for intensified feeding support either in stabilization centers or in community care groups.
It is intended that LABDI will engage beneficiaries in backyard gardening activities and train them in various livelihood-enhancing skills, including gardening.

1 - Needs

A significant humanitarian and protection crisis has resulted from the protracted conflict in the northeastern region of Nigeria; with an estimated 7.8 million people expected to need humanitarian assistance in 2024, according to OCHA. Children (especially girls) along with women continue to be the most affected; together, they account for 83% of the population in need. There is an urgent need for protection, especially for women and girls for which access to services, justice, and proper protection are still lacking. A major result of these protection concerns is the increasing burden of malnutrition among women and children over the past 5 years. According to the Integrated Food security Phase Classification for Acute Malnutrition (2023), at least 206,000 pregnant and lactating women are likely malnourished in the BAY states, indicating a 16% increase from the previous year. The IPC reported that 7,696 and 5,265 PBWG are malnourished in Damboa and Dikwa, respectively, two of the LGAs with the highest number of malnourished PBWGs in Borno State. These two LGAs alone also host about 58,000 under-5 children who are moderately malnourished and another 18,700 children who are severely malnourished.
