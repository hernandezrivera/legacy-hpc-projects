Project Summary

The conflict has widely disrupted markets and livelihoods, with negative consequences worsening year after year as the conflict ensues and exaggerated by the 2023 earthquakes. Typical livelihood strategies, in particular agricultural systems, have been eroded or completely destroyed. Many families have been left without traditionally male breadwinners, leaving female heads of household to enter the labor market, in many cases for the first time, with lack of access to education, training and experience, and conservative social norms limiting opportunities. The main changes at HH level identified by participants were an increase in HH members contributing to HH income, including negative coping strategies engaged in by women and children. PIN will support communities in Northern Syria to enhance market systems and empower vulnerable, conflict-affected households to launch viable income-generating activities to meet their basic needs through activities including Cash for Work (CfW),business training, business grants and self-help groups (community-based saving groups). Initiatives supported will include public infrastructure rehabilitation and activities explicitly intended to support women, including daycare, knitting and sewing activities. Furthermore, PIN will implement Small Business Grants (SBGs) for start-ups, earthquake rehabilitation and expansion, which are accompanied by business trainings and further support in form of networking and incubation events or lectures. Target populations will include vulnerable male and female heads of household within host, IDP and returnee communities. PIN aims to provide the most needed assistance to such groups by employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet distinct needs, establish beneficiary selection criteria to prioritize the inclusion of those most vulnerable and adapt its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people.
Lastly, PIN facilitates and supports the creation of community-based saving groups and social cohesion working groups, to enhance the self-reliance and resilience of the communities, as well as increasing social cohesion and the decision-making capacities. PINs programmes are carried out in coordination with local authorities at various levels – from cross-border to hub, to district and village level. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages.
PIN is committed to ensuring inclusivity, ‘’do no harm’’, and accessibility throughout the project. The selection of women, children, people with disabilities, and the elderly as primary target groups is grounded in a deep understanding of the distinct vulnerabilities these populations face in conflict-affected settings. Women, often disproportionately impacted by conflict as well as social barriers, confront elevated risks of gender-based violence, encounter obstacles to accessing essential services and have much less opportunities to sustain income. Prioritizing their needs is not only a matter of addressing immediate challenges but is also critical for advancing gender equality, community resilience, and long-term sustainable development.
Tailoring our interventions to address their specific needs aligns with the principles of inclusivity, ensuring that humanitarian assistance is accessible to all members of the community, regardless of ability. The approach is embedded in all PIN policies and technical manuals that govern the implementation of all the activities under this action. The intervention will be carried out in consultations with community members using a participatory and inclusive approach.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2024 indicates that majority of HHs reported that their income was insufficient to cover the cost of their basic needs. In addition, 51% of all HHs across the country only have one member earning an income. As per the latest Humanitarian Needs Assessment Programme (HNAP) Livelihood Situation Analysis of NWS (2022), only 43% of all individuals aged 18-64 had paid job over the past three months. While nearly 82% males have worked in the past 3 months, only 6% of females reported being in some form of employment. Additionally, 86% of in-work individuals are members of HHs that are still in extreme poverty. Moreover, young females (18-34) face cultural barriers to find employment, leading to higher rates of females seeking employment but being out of work. Furthermore, 50% of females who are unemployed but actively seeking work are living critically below the Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB) threshold Female-headed HHs face particular challenges in meeting their basic needs with 7% of them relying on child labour to help generate income, compared to only 2% for male-headed HHs. During the process of area selection, PIN is conducting sessions with the community including PwDs, where the project is introduced, including the process of implementation, eligibility and vulnerability criteria etc. All members of the community can ask any questions or raise any concerns, which will be taken into account. Similarly, during the implementation of the project, the CFRM mechanism is available to all members of the communities, and all feedback as well as complaints are considered in the implementation of the project.
