Project Summary

UNHCR is the leading agency coordinating NFI responses in Syria, NES, and NWS. UNHCR will provide lifesaving and life-sustaining CRI support in cases of large-scale population movements, including refugee flows, IDPs, returnees, and affected host communities. The following situations may trigger such a response:
Natural Disasters: such as earthquakes, floods, hurricanes, tsunamis, droughts, or wildfires
Armed Conflict: Civil unrest, or violence leading to displacement and humanitarian crises
Public Health Crises: Outbreaks of infectious diseases, pandemics, or health emergencies resulting in population movements and significant humanitarian needs.
Sudden-Onset Crises: Sudden displacement due to eviction or building collapses.
Protection Concerns: Human rights abuses, persecution, discrimination, or threats to the safety and security of individuals or communities leading to displacement.
The support provided will encompass the distribution of essential relief alongside seasonal items during winter or summer periods.
The CRI assistance will be administered through inter-agency convoys to remote locations, in addition to the provision of seasonal items.
The project's objective is to assist approximately 475,000 individuals with CRIs and seasonal support when necessary. The assistance will also be extended to 25,000 individuals residing in hard-to-reach areas through Crossline interventions. Assistance will be directed towards vulnerable individuals based on established protection criteria and will be dispensed as a one-off allocation.
Needs
As outlined in the 2024 HNO, the overall NFI PiN has seen a 7 percent increase to 6.1 million. Given the current funding shortages, the limited NFI sector response in 2023 is anticipated to further compound needs as 2024 progresses. Essential core relief items are crucial for individuals, but unfortunately, they remain inaccessible to most due to affordability issues.
The data from 2023 supports this assumption, seeing a rise and expected to continue increasing in 2024, particularly following the destructive earthquake that struck the country in 2023.
UNHCR's CRI response will prioritize addressing significant population movements, in cases of large-scale population movements, including refugee flows, IDPs, returnees, and affected host communities, in addition to beneficiaries residing in camps and informal settlements. Furthermore, UNHCR plans to provide support to the NWS IA crossline, offering nearly 5,000 CRI kits and seasonal or supplementary items in line with identified needs.
UNHCR's initial cash feasibility assessment’s findings show that MPCA would be feasible, and the major market would be able to respond to increased demand as it has a good capacity to absorb the increase in demand. It is planned to be aligned with the targeted preference who already reported using markets as their main source for purchasing basic needs.
Implementation of activities
With available resources, UNHCR is strategically planning to supply CRIs and seasonal to more than 95,000 HHs within Syria. Furthermore, UNHCR intends to deliver CRI packages to serve 5,000 households through a crossline modality. These CRI kits consist of essential items such as blankets, mattresses, plastic sheets, jerry cans, and solar lamps, among others. The distribution plan prioritizes vulnerable groups, including unaccompanied minors, women, elderly individuals, and female-headed households, as well as those with physical or mental challenges.
Beneficiary identification is meticulously carried out by UNHCR Field Offices in collaboration with relevant authorities and sector partners to ensure a comprehensive approach and prevent duplication.
