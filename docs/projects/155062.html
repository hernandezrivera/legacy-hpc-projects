Needs

The child protection needs overview is based on an integrated analysis, recognizing that a number of key child protection issues are linked to sectors such as food security, education and access to livelihoods, which play a determining role in risks. The 2018 JRP midterm review indicates Rohingya refugees continued to arrive in Bangladesh of whom many witnessed family members killed or endured separation and deep psychosocial distress. Additional risks and needs are placed onto them in displacement with one-third of the refugee families having at least one specific protection vulnerability that requires specialized protection attention and 55% of all refugees being children, the majority of whom do not have access to education. Children are also exposed to significant threats within their own families and communities and are therefore in need of critical lifesaving support. Child protection actors have managed to reach only 48 % of identified children with immediate child protection concerns. Psychological distress continues to be a pressing issue, and global health sector estimates are that 17% of children are suffering severe mental health impacts. For girls’ issues are related to memories of violence before movement and exposure to sexual violence before the flight, and for boys, memories of violence before movement and missing family members. Observed changes in behavior and psychosocial wellbeing of refugee children include not going to school, emotional disturbance, and social withdrawal. Beyond delivering stabilizing support to significant numbers of children, there is an urgent need to scale up more structured and targeted psychosocial support The child protection subsector reports 6,115 unaccompanied and separated children registered, who are at further risk of child trafficking, abuse and exploitation in the camps. Only 19 % of these identified unaccompanied and separated girls and boys have been reunified with their primary caregivers or placed in a long-term family-based care arrangement. There is therefore a need for case management services for these children including alternative care arrangements, positive parenting sessions for foster families, regular monitoring and followup to ensure children safety and linking up these children and families to existing services. The monsoon and cyclones in the camps at particular seasons are a threat to children getting lost and family separation hence the need to strength families and reunification during such DRR periods. Adolescents especially girls represent a larger proportion of this vulnerable group at risk of child marriage, sexual exploitation, abuse and neglect. Only 29 % of the target adolescent girls and boys have been reached with life skills and resilience support. Adolescents face specific risks exacerbated by the absence of education, training and safe livelihood opportunities. Early or forced marriage is used as a negative coping mechanism with some girls marrying as young as 12. Boys are at heightened risk of child labor, exploitation and human trafficking. The Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (Sep 2018) indicates only 3% of 15-17 year adolescents of which girls are only 1% were attending Child Friendly Spaces (CFS). With 14% of the population being adolescent age between 12-17, adolescent led interventions need to be prioritized coupled with strengthening community based child protection systems, community access to information and knowledge through awareness campaigns on child protection as a community approach to respond and participate in addressing child protection concerns such as child labor, exploitation and human trafficking, neglect and other forms of violence against children The 2018 JRP midterm review further indicates Additional risks identified in relation to deterioration of the relations between the host communities and the Rohingya refugees. Activities or outputs

Summary description of project outputs The project will contribute (including reporting) to the Child protection Subsector objective by providing specialized child protection services to children most at risk including adolescents most. 15183 beneficiaries out of which 13707(7216f:6491m) are children and 1476 (679f:797m) are adults as direct beneficiaries. Pathways to project contribution will include: strengthened community-based child protection systems, adolescent led initiatives(including peace building) to enhance their participation and contribution to community development and own protection, case management including alternative care and reunification, PSS services leveraging on existing CFSs and other service points like learning centres and adolescent social hubs among others while working with other sectors like education, access to information and awareness raising. Output 1.1 Access to psychosocial support for children and families through integrated CFSs and community based pathways.World Vision will leverage on its existing 12 CFSs to transit them into service points for children and children and adolescents. Among the services provided therein will be PSS sessions and education while working closely with the education and child protection sub sector. The CFSs will transition from being full time CFSs to service points for education and PSS sessions. This will come with modification of the CFS structures, implementing a shift based approach for other services and a rotation approach for PSS sessions. The PSS schedule will be integrated within the general centre time table for 6 days to ensure that at least each child coming to the service point receives a PSS session once or twice within the six days of a week. This approach will be be followed for all other WV supported service points for children and adolescents. Mobile community based PSS sessions will be conducted for families including men and women to enhance their resilience and participation in supporting children to cope up with distress. Output 1.2 Strengthening community based child protection systems and mechanismsThe project will map out community based child protection groups with a focus on men, women, boys and girls representation. Adults in active years between 18 to 45 will be targeted so as to provide quality and timely services to their community including being able to participate in situation monitoring, identification of children at risk, children education and GBV related concerns among others. Activities will include provision of equipment and materials, facilitating review meetings with community based child protection groups, community based situation monitoring and disaster risk reduction monitoring, trainings and mentorship on child protection including their roles and responsibility, referral pathways, disaster risk reduction, social cohesion, identification and reporting of children concerns, community participation, DRR, among others. Other activities will include awareness raising to ensure the community is able to demonstrate basic knowledge on Child protection, DRR, GBV and education concerns and equally participate in awareness rising targeting boys, girls, men and women of all ages within the community. Output 1.3 Case management addressing violence against children and family separationThe project will coordinate case management services for unaccompanied and separated children and other vulnerable children including children with disability to manage cases related to family separation, alternative care, child marriage, child neglect, physical violence, sexual and gender based violence, child labour. Men and women of all ages will be targeted to support family strengthening and providing family based care for boys and girls in alternative care.
