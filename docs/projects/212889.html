Project Summary

The Qatar Red Crescent Society (QRCS) aims to enhance the continuity and coverage of life-saving nutrition services for children under 5 and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs) in northwestern Syria (NWS). The project targets 24 communities, including IDP camps and host communities, through the deployment of Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTPs), and Mother-Baby Areas (MBAs). It expects to reach over 175,000 direct beneficiaries, prioritizing females, children, and people with disabilities. Key aspects include uninterrupted provision of nutrition services, screening and treatment, maternal nutrition support, community outreach, micronutrient distribution, capacity building, and precautions against cholera, meningitis, and COVID-19. The project emphasizes inclusivity, community feedback mechanisms, and coordination with relevant clusters and local authorities, reflecting a comprehensive humanitarian approach.
Need:
Analysis: According to recent surveys conducted by humanitarian organizations, over 1.5% of children under five and PLW in Northwest Syria are suffering from acute malnutrition, with a significant portion facing SAM. This demonstrates a critical need for immediate intervention to prevent further deterioration of health outcomes.
Challenges: The ongoing conflict has led to widespread displacement, disrupted food supply chains, and destroyed infrastructure, exacerbating food insecurity and malnutrition among vulnerable populations.
PRA:
Root Causes: Through participatory discussions with community members and key informants, it's evident that limited access to nutritious food due to economic constraints and disrupted agricultural activities is a primary driver of malnutrition. Additionally, inadequate knowledge about proper nutrition practices and hygiene further perpetuates the problem.
Cultural Factors: Certain cultural beliefs and practices, such as early introduction of complementary foods or preferences for specific foods that may lack essential nutrients, contribute to poor nutritional outcomes.
PSEA:
Environmental Factors: The region experiences seasonal variations, including droughts and limited agricultural productivity.
Opportunities: there's an existing network of local NGOs and that can facilitate project implementation and ensure community ownership.
Risk:
Funding Shortages: There is a risk of insufficient funding to sustain the project over the long term, given the competing priorities in humanitarian assistance.
Security Concerns: Ongoing conflict and insecurity in the region pose risks to the safety of project staff and beneficiaries, potentially disrupting operations.
Logistical Challenges: Limited access to certain areas due to damaged roads and checkpoints may hinder the timely delivery of supplies and services.
Assumption:
Assumption: It is assumed that local authorities will provide necessary permissions and support for project implementation, including access to target populations in conflict-affected areas.
Validation: Consultations with local government officials and community leaders confirm their willingness to collaborate and support the project, based on previous successful partnerships with humanitarian agencies.
Risk Mitigation:
Diversifying Funding Sources: In addition to seeking funding from traditional donors, exploring partnerships with private sector entities or seeking alternative funding mechanisms, such as crowdfunding, can mitigate the risk of funding shortages.
Security Protocols: Implementing strict security protocols, including staff training on risk awareness and evacuation procedures, and coordinating closely with security actors can mitigate risks associated with insecurity.
Community Engagement Strategies: Establishing community feedback mechanisms, conducting regular community meetings, and involving local leaders in project planning and decision-making can enhance community ownership and mitigate risks related to mistrust or resistance.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

QRCS aims with this project to ensure continuity and increase the coverage of life-saving comprehensive nutrition services among children under 5 (boys and girls) and PLWs. QRCS usually supported 8 RRTs for the last three years and reported monthly on 4Ws in coordination with nutrition clusters, UN agencies, health directorates, and local stakeholders in which targeted areas are ranked as high severity according to the published HNO report as well as nutrition cluster reports in NWS. In this project 24 communities (including IDPs camps and host communities in Idleb governorate and rural Aleppo) will be served with quality comprehensive nutrition services through the resumed deployment of 8 Rapid Response Teams (RRTs), the establishment of 8 Outpatient Therapeutic Programs (OTPs), and 3 Mother-Baby Areas (MBAs) with community outreach activities, effective referrals mechanisms and adopt procedures to facilitate the access of people with disabilities to the project services reaching 175,384 expected direct beneficiaries including (68 % Female, 32 % Male, 45 % Child), with 6,800 disables people, 119,253 displaced population and 56,131 overburdened host communities in the following districts in NWS Salqin, Zuf, Jabal AlZawia, and Idleb.The main aspects of the project:Continuity of Nutrition Services: The project prioritizes the uninterrupted provision of life-saving nutrition services, including Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM), micronutrient supplementation, and Infant and Young Child Feeding in Emergencies (IYCF-E). These services are guided by approved protocols and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).Screening and Treatment: The project involves the screening of children aged 6-59 months and malnourished Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs) using Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) measurements. Complicated (SAM) cases are referred, and therapeutic interventions are provided for SAM and (MAM) cases.Maternal Nutrition and IYCF-E Support: Pregnant women and mothers receive counselling and information on maternal nutrition and appropriate (IYCF-E). An effective referral mechanism is in place for children and PLWs with medical issues, including reproductive health services, provided by another health project supported by QRC.Community Outreach: A team of 37 (CHWs), 37 (Nutritionists), and 2 (Area coordinators) conduct community outreach activities. They promote IYCF-E, maternal nutrition, health, and Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) topics using recommended materials and hygiene promotion items.Micronutrient Distribution: The project involves the distribution of micronutrient supplements to PLWs and children under 5 years old, following the SOPs of the nutrition cluster.Training and Capacity Building: Training will be conducted for the respective staff on the approved topics. Cholera, meningitis and COVID-19 Precautions: Protective measures are implemented during project activities. Staff receive training on IPC.
