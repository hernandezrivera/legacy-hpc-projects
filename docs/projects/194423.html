Project Summary

According to the 2022 HNO, more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. An estimated 2.6 million people risk facing gender-based violence in 2022, which represents a 25 per cent increase compared to 2021 estimates.
UNFPA aims to reach 156,342 Beneficiaries ( women 96,106, Men 7817, Boys 15,634, Girls 34,395, PWD 2.390) with GBV services and information particularly through implementing community engagement and awareness-raising activities focusing on risk factors to GBV, available GBV response services, and timely seeking of GBV response services immediately after the GBV incident ( with in 72 hours). UNFPA will also support primary GBV prevention programs such as EMAP and SASA. The GBV risk mitigation measures include advocating and supporting integration of GBV and survivor support across different clusters, dignity kit programming, conducting safety audits and providing livelihood and economic support.
The GBV response interventions include providing psychosocial support, GBV case management services, in UNFPA established One-Stop Centers (OSC), other GBV service provision centres and Health facilities in the project areas.
As the Core Pipe Line Manager for Dignity Kits, UNFPA will procure and distribute Dignity Kits (DK) to women and girls of reproductive age to enhance their Dignity and hygiene as well as use DK programming as an entry point to provide women and girls with information on GBV prevention, and GBV service availability including information and services on Sexual Reproductive Health.
The livelihood and economic support in the Women and Girls Friendly Space will also act as an entry point for women and girls to receive more psychosocial support and other empowerment activities. Cram activities, such as knitting, beadwork, crocheting and embroidering bed sheets, and tea discussions, will be introduced as the women often enjoy these activities, so they are a good way to bring them together encourage conversation and relationship- building with each other. On the other hand, some of these activities will be introduced as income-generating activities to allow women and girls to earn some income to meet their basic needs and referrals for livelihood support with other programmes supporting women and girls.
It is important to note that as a result of the degradation of community protection systems and the inadequate GBV lifesaving services, women and girls have increased exposure to various forms of violence, including rape, sexual assault, psychological abuse, physical violence, child and forced marriage, denial of opportunities, resources and services where some basic services exist. Also, livelihood options are increasingly limited due to the ongoing conflict, exacerbated with COVID 19, disproportionately affecting adolescent girls and young women, resorting to harmful coping mechanisms that include child marriage, survival sex and sexual exploitation and abuse.
Survivors urgently need to access specialized services, including case management and psychosocial support services. A clear referral pathway needs to be established to facilitate the survivors' access to critical life-saving services.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview, (HNO) more than two thirds of South Sudan's population, 9.4 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. An increase of 1.1 million since 2021. An estimated 2.6 million people women and girls, men and boys risk facing gender based violence in 2022Based on FSMNS+ assessment, 48% percent of the HH expressed fear/ concern on GBV. Among the population groups, IDPs expressed greater concern (57%) to GBV including rape and forced marriage. Women and girls face risks to different forms of GBV in their routine activities. The findings from FSNMS+ indicate that 38% of HHs expressed that women and girls avoid more than one area such as water point, latrines, distribution areas, markets and fire wood among others because they feel unsafe. 32% percent of HHs also face increased risk of GBV as they travel more than 30 minutes to fetch water.The FSMNS+ assessment revealed that only 6 % percent of the community members are aware of the available GBV response services in their neighborhoods, which represents a 25 per cent increase compared to 2021 estimates. The country is characterized by political fragmentation, intercommunal violence, civil conflict, frequent disease outbreaks and a weakening economy among others. These are complicated further by perennial flooding and droughts worsened by climate change. Between July to September 2022, UNMISS HRD recorded at least 142 incidents of violence affecting 745 civilians (285 killed, 308 injured, 60 abductions and 92 subjected to conflict-related sexual violence). As of 28 October 2022, over 1 million people were verified as affected by torrential rain and flooding in 36 counties across South Sudan and over 20,000 people in the southern part of the Abyei Administrative Area (AAA). According to the 2017 Verisk Maplecroft climate change vulnerability index, South Sudan is among the five most vulnerable countries in the world in terms of the risk of exposure to climate change and the resources of the country to cope with extreme weather events. These interlocked shocks have resulted in frequent and forced displacements, destroyed livelihoods, disrupted and fragmented social services delivery. The COVID 19 pandemic is still a significant health emergency in the country with a current total of 18,339 COVID-19 cases confirmed and 138 deaths, revealing a Case Fatality Rate of 1.1 per cent. Unfortunately, COVID-19 testing is low, control measures to prevent the spread of the disease generally remain unobserved. Difficult access to large parts of the country, due to lack of infrastructure, insecurity, and flooding has slowed down the implementation of regular and intensified COVID-19 vaccination activities.Primary GBV prevention programs will be implemented to addresses harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality and power imbalances. Essential GBV actions would be integrated and undertaken across multiple clusters to save lives and mitigate GBV risks. GBV response interventions will also be implemented in a coordinated manner to ensure the provision life-saving specialized GBV response services.

2 - Project activities

UNFPA will implement community engagement and awareness-raising activities focusing on risk factors to GBV, available GBV response services, and timely seeking of GBV response services immediately after the GBV incident ( with in 72 hours). It will also support evidence based primary GBV prevention programs such as EMAP and SASA. The GBV risk mitigation measures include advocating and supporting integration of GBV and survivor centered support across different clusters, dignity kit programming, conducting safety audits and providing livelihood and economic support. The GBV response interventions include providing psychosocial support, GBV case management services, establishing and strengthening GBV referral systems, and implementing Women and Girls Friendly Space programming. This service can be accessed by women and girls at particular risk of sexual violence, exploitation, abuse, denial of resources or forced and early marriage. As a Core Line Manager for Dignity Kits, UNFPA will procure and conduct Dignity Kit Programming to enhance dignity and hygiene of women and girls as well as use it as an entry point to enable women and girls access information on GBV prevention and risk mitigation including information on GBV service delivery points. Similarly women and girls will receive information on SRH services Women and girls with disabilities are an easy target of all kinds of gender-based violence. In order to protect people with disability from different forms of GBV, UNFPA will increase its efforts to identify barriers, risks and enablers for people with disabilities and take concrete steps to strengthen the inclusion of people with different types of disability, and to build and strengthen partnerships and capacity building with organizations of persons with disabilities as well as improve monitoring, reporting and learning in order to strengthen inclusion of people with disabilities especially women and girls with disabilitiesUNFPA will challenge any deliberate attempt to exclude parts of the affected population and will provide assistance based on need and guard against any form of direct or indirect discrimination. Failing to address GBV in the sectoral responses endangers safety of beneficiaries and denies access to humanitarian response. UNFPA will work and advocate to other non-GBV actors and clusters to integrate GBV in their sectoral responses including conducting of joint safety audits with targeted recommendations as per the 2013 call to action to integrate GBV in other sectoral responses and to contribute to centrality of protection.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

As in previous projects, UNFPA will work with Community Leaders to identify and address the heightened risks faced by people with disabilities and older persons in humanitarian contexts with considerations for intersectionality of age gender, socio-economic factors and other diversities. UNFPA conducted an accessibility Audit of Health Facilities where GBV services are offered to monitor and document the barriers faced by the people with disability in accessing services, particularly sexual and reproductive health and GBV services. The project will use the results this year to inform further actions together with Disability People Organisations. UNFPA will support activities meant to create awareness on the human rights of the Deaf people to proper inclusion in respect to the promotion of sign language as well as conduct GBV and SRHR/GBV training for people with disabilities using the guidelines.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

For effective and efficient implementation of the project, bottom-up approach on project implementation will be applied. During the Implementation Phase, feedback will be solicited from the beneficiaries to determine the possible gaps and how these can be addressed to ensure that the project is responsive to the needs of the target beneficiaries. For example, through the GBV safety audits, the community /beneficiaries will be able to raise GBV concerns and how the GBV prevention, response, and services should be packaged. Similarly, UNFPA will conduct FGDs during dignity kit distribution with some of the communities in the project. We will conduct additional GBV Safety Audits to ascertain GBV risks that will be emerging from time to time and use them to update our response strategies on community Based Complaint Mechanisms and GBV concerns and UNFPA will work with partners to address these concerns and feedback will be provided to communities after that.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

UNFPA will enhance awareness raising about SEA as a violation and information on victim assistance through existing GBV and Health Referral Pathway through partners who provide GBV case management. The project will work in coordination with the State PSEA Task Force and the Community Based Complain mechanisms (CBCM) to support the CBCM Watch Groups to create awareness rasing on PSEA within their communities in addition to providing training for the CBCM members on basic PSEA concepts, prevention and Victims assistance. In some project locations, CBCM boxes do exist and information about the location/placement of the complaints boxes will be shared with the beneficiaries in most of these locations. However, many of the beneficiaries indicated that many of them did not know how to read and write making them to shy away from using the complaint boxes. Recognizing the limitations regarding reading and writing with established Community Based Complaint mechanisms, especially the use of complaint boxes for those who cannot read and write. UNFPA in consultation with the beneficiaries will discuss and introduce modifications, which will be agreed upon so that everyone has the opportunity to express their feelings, needs and ideas in form of visual illustrations/ pictures /photos.The beneficiaries will also be reminded of the designated Complaint and Feedback Hotline numbers that they could use and specific staff who speak the local language and who are well versed with the project will be selected to receive the calls and act or refer the complaints for appropriate action. Piece of laminated paper indicating the numbers will be distributed and also displayed in strategic places with clear messages to further inform the beneficiaries.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary Feedback will be discussed during the Bi-weekly GBV Sub-Cluster meetings for partners to address these concerns and feedback provided to communities after that. Similarly, meetings will be held with communities to agree on their suggestions and explore available possibilities to adapt some of their immediate feedback into ongoing and subsequent programming.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will be implemented within a context where the risk of GBV and SEA is already high when accessing humanitarian assistance. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence and exploitation during access to humanitarian assistance would have a devastating impact on the project. The women and girls would be gravely affected physically and psychologically, among others, while the image of the humanitarian system would be tainted. The consequences of sexual violence and exploitation are life long and can severely influence the community. With that in mind, the project will scale up awareness raising sessions on prevention, mitigation and response for both community members and survivors of violence. Similarly, the project will coordinate with protection partners/GBV actors to facilitate training for other partners’ staff. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities presents the likelihood of exclusion to be High and similarly with High impact. Project consultations with communities will be broad and representative, beyond community leaders and usual constituencies. Women, men, boys and girls across all social setups will be engaged in the consultation. So far, UNFPA consulted women, men, boys and girls through focus group discussions and a key informant interview. The team will also engage community leaders and religious leaders including special groups in the community like women group, Youth group and people living with a disability so that the project will be designed to meet their specific needs. Therefore, not consulting women and girls would pause a significant risk to the project. Women and girls input into the project design would enhance ownership and improve service uptake. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. The likelihood that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms is high with a corresponding high impact if they do not access/use the complaint and feedback mechanism. UNFPA from experience often works together with beneficiaries to explore a range of options to cater for the varying needs. For example, some beneficiaries who can read and write may prefer complaint boxes, others will prefer telephone calls and others would just wish to confidentially report directly to a focal point who can receive their feedback. Either way the project will ensure all these mechanisms are in place to enhance access and use of the established complaint mechanism. Data management is an important part of project management, and all those involved in the implementation of the project must commit to managing data safely and ethically. Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries has (Likelihood - Medium and impact - High). Unsafe and unethical data management, especially in the provision of health services, can result in severe consequences. In some situations, health workers have been attacked and injured in the past due to it, while beneficiaries have shunned services due to such practices.
