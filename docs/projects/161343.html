Project Summary

ACTED concurs with CCCM cluster objectives to continue core camp management activities in PoCs and camp settings, expand mobile CCCM capacity, engage with coordination mechanisms, and to engage deeper with the capacity building of NNGOs. In line with these objectives, ACTED plans to directly respond to the needs of 81,367 IDPs through the following interventions:
1. Response to the needs of 31,367 IDPs located in Juba PoCs (29,429) and Bor PoC (1,938) through static CCCM interventions.
2. Response to the needs of some (50,000) of the approximately 1.2m IDPs (OCHA) residing outside of formally recognized camp-like settings through 10 mobile CCCM-led missions.
3. Support to a coordinated response and advocacy for the needs of IDPs through hosting the Camp Management and Camp Coordination (CCCM) cluster co-lead.
4. Capacity-building to NNGOs.
1. Static Responses:
As of September 2019, the total of number of civilians seeking safety in Juba UN House was 29,429, and in Bor PoC 1,938 (CCCM cluster).As camp manager, ACTED will provide residents and partners with CCCM support aimed at improving living conditions and effective delivery of services. Activities include:
• Strengthening community governance mechanisms
• Ensuring accountability through communication and community engagement (CCE)
• Site maintenance improvements
• Service provision coordination
• Information management
Under RSRTF funding, ACTED intends to respond to the recommendations of the Secretary General’s 180 day report by expanding capacity in protection (particularly HLP) and by working with the PoC service providers to expand provision in the peri-PoC and urban areas, equalizing historic service imbalances.
2. Mobile Responses:
Of the approximately 1,465,542 IDPs in South Sudan (OCHA, September 2019), only around 18% or 278,206 resided in PoCs and collective sites (CCCM cluster, September 2019). ACTED’s mobile responses will target hard-to-reach displacement-affected populations outside of camp or camp-like settlements.
ACTED intends to conduct at least 10 mobile missions in 2020, which will be CCCM-led quality, information-driven, multi-sectoral interventions that help people adapt to disaster and prepare to transition towards self-reliance.
Activities will include:
• Multi-sectoral assessment and CCCM site profile assessment
• Establishing and strengthening community governance mechanisms
• Ensuring accountability through CCE
• Site maintenance improvements
• Service provision coordination
• Information management
• Contingency planning and emergency preparedness
As many of targeted locations demonstrate severe emergency needs across sectors, advocacy to other partners and clusters is prioritized. To streamline this process, ACTED also intends to leverage multi-sectoral funds (S/NFI, FSL and WASH) to deliver a more comprehensive and holistic response.
Interventions target a whole ‘displacement-affected community’ (IDPs, host community, returnees and local polity) with CCCM activities. Protection staff accompany mobile missions to support coordination with protection partners. Where protection gaps exist, ACTED will collaborate with mobile protection partners for joint missions.
3. Coordination:
ACTED will host the CCCM cluster co-coordinator, supporting improvement of participation in coordination mechanisms. ACTED will continue to work closely with the CCCM cluster and other parnters to coordinate all activities, including strengthened collaboration with SFPs.
4. Capacity-building:
ACTED is in discussion with the NGO Forum to deliver a comprehensive package of training to NNGOs in South Sudan, in Juba and nationwide. This will focus on supporting capacity for all humanitarian responses, including specific training on CCCM. ACTED trains local authorities and local NGO partners on CCCM in all responses. ACTED will soon support Health Link, an NNGO, with their new mobile CCCM activities, and we will expand this focus on mobile CCCM capacity building.

1 - Needs

Years of conflict have left South Sudanese people facing one of the world’s most devastating, complex and deep-rooted humanitarian crises in recent history. The compounding effect of repeated waves of violence and an increasingly fast economic decline have uprooted millions from their homes, both internally and across international borders. An estimated 1,465,542 civilians (OCHA) are estimated to be internally displaced, of whom 13% (194,954) are seeking safety in six Protection of Civilians (PoC) sites (UN).PoCsThe populations in the PoCs are largely dependent on the delivery of humanitarian services, which ACTED coordinates as camp manager in Juba and Bor PoCs. Without CCCM response there is a risk of service gaps or duplication, failed opportunities for creating synergies, and lack of accountability toward beneficiaries. Out-of-camp settingsThe needs of the wider population largely exceed the ability of humanitarian actors to respond as there are now chronic cycles of emergency and instability. Humanitarian actors are also experiencing impediments in reaching conflict-affected populations, both related to poor infrastructure and also insecurity. ACTED’s CCCM Mobile Team, after deployments across South Sudan during 2018/9, continues to see new and existing displacement sites that require enhanced coordination of humanitarian and civilian actors, better information management system, enhanced community governance and integration at the area level and clear service mapping to de-duplicate service provision or conversely advocate for service provision in the absence of humanitarian actors. Through continued assessments, ACTED continues to identify pockets of displacement-affected populations that are both hard to reach and disconnected from the humanitarian response. Furthermore, existing formal and informal governance structure capacity remains low, impacting the ability of national civilian actors in accessing available resources. It is these populations ACTED intends to target through mobile responses.Under this funding, ACTED plans to conduct assessments in Bor South, Luakpiny/Nasir, Canal/ Pigi, Twic East, Uror, Tambura, Duk and Tonj South, all of which have been identified as highly vulnerable priority areas for response. Locations for further assessments will be based on displacement affected locations that are identified and prioritized by NAWG and ICWG through consultation and coordination with the CCCM Cluster. Due to the unpredictable nature of displacement new locations may be prioritized along the way. Coordination and capacity buildingCoordination from the CCCM cluster is essential to ensure the continuation of quality and sustainable camp management activities across South Sudan. ACTED intends to support this through hosting the cluster co-coordinator. There will be particular focus will be on improving engagement with coordination mechanisms and with SFPs.In tandem with the objectives of the CCCM cluster, ACTED also concurs with the need to support capacity-building of national NGO partners to increase the scope and sustainability of mobile camp management responses in particular. As mobile CCCM is a relatively new activity, which is unfamiliar to many NNGOs in CCCM, capacity-building from both the cluster and more experienced INGO partners in terms of project design and implementation is necessary to support this objective.

2 - Activities

Static CCCM support for 31,367 IDPs in Juba and Bor PoCs. As camp manager ACTED will provide camp residents and partners with CCCM support aimed at improving living conditions and effective delivery of services.Activities include:• Establishing and strengthening community governance mechanisms• Ensuring accountability through communication and community engagement• Site maintenance improvements• Service provision coordination• Information managementA small cash-based response will be included for CCCM activities related to community-led initiatives and suitable micro-business ideas.Furthermore, under RSRTF funding, ACTED will respond to the recommendations of the SG report on the PoC sites, by expanding capacity in protection and by working with the PoC service providers to expand provision in the peri-PoC and urban areas, equalizing historic service imbalances. ACTED will also support durable solutions for the residents of Bor PoC. Mobile CCCM support for 50,000 displacement-affected populations in hard-to-reach locationsOf the 1,465,542 IDPs in South Sudan (OCHA, Sept 19), only around 18% or 278,206 resided in PoCs and collective sites (CCCM cluster, Sept 19). ACTED’s mobile responses will target hard-to-reach displacement-affected populations outside of camp or camp-like settlements. ACTED will conduct at 10+ mobile missions in 2020, which will be CCCM-led quality, information-driven, multi-sectorial interventions that help people adapt to disaster and prepare to transition towards self-reliance. Following a trigger, ACTED conducts a rapid multi-sectoral assessment and CCCM site profile assessment. If needs are identified, ACTED will launch a full, tailored mobile mission for 8+ weeks. Proposed CCCM activities include enhancing/creating local governance structures and reinforcing their camp management capacity, improvement or implementation of NGO/local authority engagement and coordination, improved access to information for populations, construction of community spaces or basic repairs to communal infrastructure. Further to this, ACTED can provide basic site mapping, service mapping, flow monitoring and contingency and evacuation planning. As many of the locations targeted demonstrate severe emergency needs across sectors, advocacy to other partners and clusters for multi-sectoral emergency response is prioritized. To streamline this process, ACTED also intends to leverage multi-sectoral funds where possible to deliver a more comprehensive and holistic response. Currently, ACTED is including emergency S/NFI distributions into mobile missions; in 2020 we hope to expand this approach to include emergency FSL distributions and WASH activities, where appropriate. Building on previous experiences, ACTED targets a whole ‘displacement-affected community’ (IDPs, host community, returnees and local polity) with CCCM activities. A small element of cash-based response will be included for mobile activities. ACTED will support durable solutions during mobile responses where the conditions are appropriate, in coordination with other partners. Facilitation of CCCM Cluster coordinationACTED will continue to host the CCCM cluster co-coordinator through a grant under OFDA. There will be particular focus will be on improving engagement with coordination mechanisms and with SFPs. ACTED teams will work closely with the CCCM cluster, ICWG, NAWG and other partners, especially SFPs.Capacity-building of NNGOsACTED will deliver a comprehensive package of training to NNGOs in Juba and nationwide through the NGO Forum. This will focus on supporting capacity for all humanitarian responses, including specific training on CCCM. ACTED will deliver training on CCCM to local authorities and local NGO partners during responses to ensure continuity & sustainability. We will also work with NNGOs who are new to mobile CCCM activities to offer direct support and capacity-building.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

This will be supported by broader local authority engagement initiatives. ACTED will engage with HLP partners in all interventions.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project is targeted at IDPs currently residing in the PoC. Working with existing community networks within and outside of the POC sites and in Mangateen, ACTED and partners will hold discussion fora and trainings to support individuals to identify, unwrap and take steps to resolve safety issues and risks faced by those in the communities, focusing specifically on children and women. Key target groups will be teachers, religious leaders, influential community figures, youth and new parents. Activities will involve risk mapping and risk mitigation planning and this represents a “soft” component of understanding better specific issues faced and specific solutions identified at a local level. ACTED will also support with these activities in Bor PoC and mobile responses where appropriate.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

As discussed above, the RSRTF project will work closely with local stakeholders including community leadership, state leadership and the RRC. The project is in line with the Secretary General report on the PoCs and aligns with the longer-term planning on PoCs.In Bor PoC and in mobile interventions, ACTED will collaborate closely with other partners to support returns and durable solutions only when the conditions are deemed to be appropriate. ACTED’s mobile CCCM approach prioritizes working closely and directly with local leadership and the community and will ensure that all activities are linked to government and community systems.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with the goals to ensure durable solutions for returning IDPs, as well as addressing historic service imbalances with populations outside of PoCs through increasing service provision.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

As part of role of camp manager in the PoCs in Bor and Juba, ACTED is working with UNMISS and the RSRTF fund in particular to work towards a future beyond the PoCs. However, these are building blocks towards a longer term strategy and will prioritise the protection needs of the residents of the PoCs.Implementation and success of the R-ARCSS remains a key assumption as it links directly with the perception of safety and security of PoC site residents as well as those residing in urban Juba. This move to a transitional government symbolizes cooperation and political will from higher levels of government to strive for the peace and development of South Sudan. Building the trust of communities within and outside of the POC sites is a fundamental component of the success of the RSRTF project and will run throughout its entire course. This element of the project will use the experience and expertise of UNMISS CAD given its lengthy engagements in the peri-POC site and Mangateen areas, and rich understanding of the local and broader political dynamics. During the initial phases of the project, ACTED and partners will hold “sensing” meetings with various levels of leadership, including women and youth, inside and outside the POC sites as well as in and around Mangateen. Humanitarian partners will also be encouraged to conduct an updated protection risk assessment in the peri-POC site and Mangateen areas. Engagement with Jubek state leadership and RRC is a critical component of this activity.In Bor PoC and in mobile interventions, ACTED will collaborate closely with other partners to support returns and durable solutions only when the conditions are deemed to be appropriate.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project integrates service provision elements intended to ensure investment in longer-term, sustainable service provision outside of PoCs by integrating them with national systems (e.g. integration of new health facilities with the Health Pooled Fund).

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Within the PoCs and in Mangateen, ACTED establishes and supports inclusive community governance mechanisms. These groups will be consulted throughout with a focus on ensuring all minority groups, as well as specific interest groups such as women and youth, are included in consultations and project design.ACTED’s mobile interventions all include a protection officer to ensure training of leadership, identification of referral mechanisms and identification of PSNs. However, in mobile interventions which are often remote, it has previously proved challenging to identify protection partners able to take referrals. Therefore, ACTED is increasingly collaborating with protection specialists to run joint missions where possible.The RSRTF project looks to address the risks related to dispute on land ownership and intercommunity conflict over access to resources. The project design will be very closely coordinated with UNMISS and dependent on the prevailing security situation, to ensure that the project will support HLP rights of affected populations and do no harm.Living in insecure settlements exposes displaced women and girls in particular to GBV. Site maintenance activities will therefore be designed with a GBV angle in mind. Furthermore, women and girls will be prioritized by vulnerability criteria for shelter/ NFI interventions (e.g. torches). ACTED’s interventions will target not only IDPs, but also the host community, returnees and local polity (known collectively as a displacement -affected community) with mobile CCCM activities. Where multi-sectoral assistance is targeted, vulnerable host communities will also be considered under vulnerability criteria. ACTED has previously experienced low utilization of CRM in mobile responses. This will be mitigated through clear and consistent communication with the community, as well as establishment of a community based complaints mechanism (CBCM).

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with a dedicated Accountability Officer based in Juba that will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) in WBeG, and oversee reporting for the project to ensure beneficiaries can objectively report (verbally) complaints and feedback. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels, as well as the training of project staff on collecting and responding to complaints, and sensitization of the community in accountability practice will be utilized. All complaints will be compiled and reported on each month and the Accountability Officer will ensure that follow up is conducted by the relevant people. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and the community, and improve the quality of our programmes. For mobile responses, ACTED will initiate bi-monthly feedback sessions with the community, in which they will be able to provide feedback. In here ACTED will ensure that most vulnerable individuals will have access to these sessions. Furthermore, feedback sessions will be arranged in coordination with leadership groups including women and youth groups to ensure broad participation. Overall, ACTED will ensure to collaborate with Protection partners, to ensure that the most vulnerable community members will have access to feedback. Following all mobile interventions, ACTED will conduct follow-up monitoring to ascertain sustainability and effectiveness of responses gather more direct beneficiary feedback. These monitorings will include specifically targeting vulnerable community members such as those living with disabilities to ensure that their feedback is received.
