Project Summary

REACH proposes to conduct a child protection assessment on the situation of Libyan and refugee and migrant children. The aim will be to supplement the evidence base on the needs of Libyan and refugee and migrant children in Libya in particular, with a focus on child protection and education.

Child protection

Needs

The situation and the protection risks for children in Libya are severly underexplored. While refugees and migrants are among the most vulnerable population groups in Libya and 9% of them are estimated to be children according to IOM DTM there is almost no informaiton on the needs of this vulnerablte group. At the same time, while some infomration on the needs of Libyan children exist, no information in particular on challenges in access to education is available - neither is this the case for refugee and migrant children. The aim of this study will be to shed light on Libyan and refugee and migrant children’s access to protection, education and other key issues pertaining to the well being of children in Libya.

Activities

A qualitative assessment through primary data field collection with Libyan children as well refugees and migrant children across Libya. Gender considerations will be made central during any interview and survey conducted by REACH staff and its direct network present in the field, which represent a key source to gather relevant information on children in Libya. To ensure that all children's perceptions and needs in the geographic areas are captured and that their vulnerabilities and protection concerns are reflected in REACH’s information management, REACH will prioritize data sets disaggregated in terms of gender, age, and vulnerability at all levels of collection and reporting. Furthermore, interviews with key informants (KI) aim at targeting both male and female representatives within the geographic areas to ensure that results are coherent and representative to a given scale following the development of the research design and sampling frame. Nevertheless to ensure full compliance to best practices in interviewing children, in case of arising ethical concerns female respondents shall be excluded from data collection activities. Furthermore, REACH will follow best practices to ensure the best interests of the child and international best practices in the ethics of research with children can be maintained, including the training of REACH staff and its partners in Libya on the ethics and practicalities of data collection with children ensuring that they abide to international child protection standards. Thanks to the recently completed study on Children on the Move, administered in close collaboration with UNICEF, REACH has developed a sound expertise in data collection with children, which impact the entire design of a study, ranging design of the methodology, training prior to data collection activities, daily data collection procedures, follow up mechanisms to data collection, subsequent analysis, anonymisation and storage of data. This includes the areas of Child Rights and Child Protection; Child Protection terminology, definitions, principles and their application to data collection activities; training of data collectors on communication with children (incl. active listening, age-appropriate communication, identification of signs of distress); the establishment of referral pathways and urgent action for daily data collection activities. Data analysis and storage mechanisms implemented by REACH follow the same strict Ethics procedures. The REACH-UNICEF study on Children on the Move underwent a rigorous external ethical review process by an Ethical Committee appointed by UNICEF, which reviewed the study’s set-up at the beginning of the study, as well as a second time prior to publication to ensure that procedures were in line with international Best Standards on data collection with children. This procedure will also be followed in the proposed study. The assessment will further be designed and conducted in close collaboration with the child protection WG to ensure that information collected are relevant and useable for actors working in child protection in the country. Data collection will employ a qualitative methodology with semi structured individual interviews with children in a range of locations, selected on the basis of presence of (migrant) children. All data collection procedures will be in line with international best practices in data collection with children.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Indicate any study/assessment that has been used, if any

A number of protection risks have been identified when conduction interviews with children or migrant children: 1)No child below the age of 15 will be interviewed in the study. 2)In Libya, children will be informed and asked to provide their oral consent to participate in the interview. The consent of the legal guardian will be also requested whenever available. 3)Should data collectors assess that the child is under an imminent life-threatening risk, they will fill an urgent action form and send it to the Assessment Officer, who will discuss with child protection actors (UNICEF) the need to refer the case to the relevant humanitarian actors present in the field.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

REACH ensures that safety and dignity of beneficiaries is maintained. In Libya, data collectors will seek informed assent of the child and informed consent by the caretaker. Whenever the caretaker is not available, the informed consent of the child, will be sought by explaining in simple, age-appropriate language why information is being sought and what it will be used for, including how it will be shared in line with best practices in data collection activities with unaccompanied and separated children. In the latter case, data collectors will take into account all elements affecting the vulnerability of the child to decide whether she/ he is a position to provide her/his consent, including her/his age / level of maturity. No children under the age of 15 will be interviewed for this assessment, and in all cases, it will be ensured that participation is in the best interest of the child. Interviews will be anonymous, and no information will be asked on respondents’ migratory status on the Libyan territory.
