Project Summary

The communities in South Omo Zone SNNPR have been faced with multiple hazards for the past 3 consecutive years. These include recurrent drought, flooding, Desert Locust infestation, inter-communal conflicts, and cholera epidemics. With the poor performance of the 2021 Belg rains (March-May) and Maher rains (September – November), water shortages are affecting three woredas over XXX people. Water sources especially ponds, spring & wells have dried up.
South Omo zone Water, Irrigation and Mines Department report provides that zonal potable water coverage is less than 35%. The report further affirms that, 36% of the existing water supply systems in Bena Tsemay and 74% of the systems in Dasenech are nonfunctional and need urgent repairs. The report further pronounces the need of rehabilitation of damaged water schemes as crucial. Communities practice and behavior of using protected latrine system is justified to be far below the standard. With unsafe water sources and open defecation, the risk of contamination
of water is high. As pastoralist way of life community culture to use open defecation largely contributed towards escalated water borne diseases.
The seasonal and flash flood from overflow of Omo River has caused flooding in Dasenech woreda. About 94,800-hectares of land covered by the water. The primary data collected from the field and then cross-checked with Zonal DRM office and Displacement Tracking Matrix Round 27 indicated that more than 10,716 households or 62,862 people are displaced in the Dasenech woreda from 27 kebeles along Omo River and lake Turkana. The displaced population have lost their houses, household items, clothing, and productive assets. Community institutions such as schools, health centers, irrigation schemes and water points were also damaged. Authorities estimate that they need 15 water trucks, but there is no capacity to cover these needs.
The overall situation in South Omo suggests that more focus has to be given to improve water and hygiene facilities. In view of this, the proposed project plans to increase the coverage of clean water, hand washing facilities, sanitation and hygiene services in Dasenech, Malie and Bena Tsemay woredas of the zone. The three target woredas have been identified based on the case load as per 2022 HRP and WASH Cluster strategic priorities and based on the recommendation of Zonal DRM.
The proposed project aims to undertake the following key activities in Bena Tsemay, Male, Dasenech Woredas. Detail activities include:
1) Rehabilitation of water points - This is aimed to address lack/shortages of safe drinking water to 72,544 communities in three woredas. This will be done by rehabilitating 28 non-functional water schemes and 2 pipeline extensions from existing water schemes with construction of additional water points as well as water trucking for displaced people.
2) Improving access to hygiene and sanitation facilities - Construction of 22 trench and 8 semi-permanent latrines on IDP centers in Dasenech woreda. Provision of handwashing facilities at health centers, schools, IDP centers and other public places.
3) Enhancing awareness of communities to hygiene and sanitation including provision of IEC material, community and HH awareness sessions through audio vans, posters and HEWs/ community health volunteers.
4) Provision of water purification and sanitary items - Soap distribution consisting of 250gms of soap for laundry and 200gms for bathing, jerry cans will be provided to 24,493 households as per Minimum Sphere standards. Water treatment chemicals will be distributed to 24,493 households for 90 days in locations where river/pond water is being used in three woredas.
Mechanism will be put in place to awareness raising to minimize the risks of COVID 19 at WASH NFI distribution sites during each distribution. At the end of the distribution sites will be disinfected and cleaned to prevent risk of COVID 19.
5) Post distribution monitoring

1. - Projects activities

• Rehabilitation of 27 non-functional water wells in Dasenech, Malie and Bena Tsemay woredas. Comprehensive rehabilitation of hand pumps will include dismantling all the parts of nonfunctional hand pumps, install new Indian Mark II handpumps, flashing the boreholes, pump testing, treating wells with chlorine and aluminum sulfate, reconstruction of headworks and drainage. Water quality test will also be conducted.• Pipeline extension from existing spring, rehabilitation of collection chamber and construction of four water points and reservoir to benefit 5098 people. • Rehabilitation of two motorized water schemes to benefit 5300(2703F) people• Emergency water trucking that will benefit 55,396(28,252F) in Dasenech & Benatsemay. As per the Sphere standard. Existing damping points will used as distribution point.• Refresher training for 210 WASH committee members on operation, management and maintenance of rehabilitated water schemes pipelines - To ensure community-based management of the water sources, potential women, men and youth community representatives have to be selected, get trained on sanitation and hygiene of the water sources and foresee orderly utilization by the entitled community members.• Construction of 22 trench latrines which benefits 2100(1071 people) & semi-permanent latrine which benefits 1600(816 people) in Dasenech woreda • Construction of 5 wash basins at rehabilitated water points - The masonry wash basins will be constructed near to the rehabilitated water schemes which the communities easily fetch water from the scheme and use the basin wash their cloths and household items.• Hygiene promotion and awareness-raising on safe hygiene and sanitation practices and COVID-19 through trained HEWs, hygiene promoters/volunteers and distribution of IEC materials• Distribute water treatment chemicals (Aqua taps), sufficient for 90 days, to 24,493 households in 3 woredas (90 sachets for 3 months);• Distribution of 122,465 bathing soaps and 122,465 laundry soaps for 24,493 households sufficient for a month (5 bathing and 5 laundry soaps per household);• Distribution of Jerrycan 20 litters capacity for 24493 HHs 1 jerry can per household• Post distribution monitoring

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

CA and AFD have strong operational presence in South Omo Zone and currently implementing WASH, livelihoods and desert locust responses to which this project will potential complementarity and integration. CA is active member of the WASH, DRM-ATF cluster, ES/NFI Clusters and HINGO and AFD actively participate in regional and zonal level cluster coordination platforms. CA has a collaborative relationship with the Government, national and International NGOs. All work will be carried out in close liaison with Zonal Disaster Risk Management which will be coordinating body of this emergency response process. CA and its partner will actively take part in bi-weekly joint coordination meetings including the Humanitarian INGOs such as IRC, World Vision, Amref Health Africa, Doctors with Africa and Government ministries which help to jointly coordinate its humanitarian responses in a way that avoids the risks of duplication.In South Omo, AFD has a field office at Jinka with its own program coordinator and field officers as well as community facilitators based at community levels as well as logistics. For the past 3 years, CA has an emergency program officer based in Jinka and working closely with the partner, ensuring coordination and maintaining relationships with Government and other humanitarian agencies. This will ensure continued delivery of services given the current movement restrictions due to COVID-19.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Christian Aid is a certified member of the CHS, safeguarding guides and accountability is key to our programmes regardless of the nature of the project. Therefore this project will cater to establish a safe, effective and accessible information sharing and complaint handling mechanism in suitable manner to the affected persons and will be duly monitored and ensured that affected persons have goodrepresentation in decision making process and be able to respond to the different complaints subject to our failure to deliver in our commitment.Target HHs will be identified verified jointly with community committees established from among the affected communities and these committees will be actively engaged in all decision-making processes. WASH activities involve the Department of Water and the target communities closely and supports will be provided to ministry of water, health and community health extension workers for health activities.Location of distribution sites, safety appropriateness and convenience of items will be decided in consultation with the target communities, particularly women, adolescent girls and any people with disabilities. Communities will also be involved in joint monitoring of responses. As the response continues, communities will continually be consulted to assess the appropriateness and adjustments made accordingly.Beneficiaries will be informed of the type and forms of assistance, criteria, amount and duration of assistance to be expected and the start and end dates as well as the source of the assistance. The different options for providing feedback including confidential information will be designed with target communities. This will include discussion with CA teams, individual discussions, phone numbers and any other methods the communities see appropriate. The safety of the complaints will be ensured.
