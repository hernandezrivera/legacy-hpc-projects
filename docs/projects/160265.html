Project Summary

HACO through the Project, intends to provide integrated GBV assistance and specialized services to the vulnerable host community, IDPs and returnees in Western Equatoria (Maridi ) and in Unity State (Panyijiar and Rubkona Counties). The activities will be linked with services provided by other clusters/sub clusters in an integrated approach (WASH, Health, nutrition, GBV, Education, FSL and S/NFIs) with focus on addressing critical gaps in providing information and knowledge on services provided by various actors and services available for GBV response and referral to be accessed by all vulnerable people (women, men, girls and boys and people with disabilities).
The project targets 12,000 vulnerable individuals: (5,174 women, 1,546 men, 4,066 girls, 1,214 boys) including people with disabilities.
The Project will establish and improve response to cases of Gender Based Violence (GBV) which includes rape, sexual assault, domestic violence, forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation, abuse, exploitation and segregation; look into discriminatory practices within the legal system and harmful traditional practices. The project will enhance the management and referrals of GBV cases comprehensively for the survivors to recover from GBV. The project will establish and maintain the women and girls friendly spaces where the GBV survivors and other vulnerable women can freely express their situation to the service providers and get psycho-social counselling and legal support if needed; Cash Based Intervention (CBI ) will be another way to support the vulnerable women including GBV survivors by providing a little money as a startup kit for small income generating activities to enhance economic survival and support healing, recovery and resilience process of the survivor. HACO will work with the targeted beneficiaries, the community and strengthen the existing community based protection monitoring committees in locations of operation. HACO will conduct protection awareness raising sessions on GBV prevention with campaigns and messages to make progress towards changing the social norms that make it difficult to eradicate GBV. Information on available services and access for survivors will be shared with the community through mass awareness campaigns and using the traditional and religious media to reach people in areas difficult to reach.
Assistance and specialized services to the beneficiaries will include provision of comprehensive case management at the minimum standard, provision of psycho-social support to GBV survivors, individuals and groups, through counselling. Extremely vulnerable individual women, girls and the disabled through direct dialogues with them individually and in groups will be assessed on their capacities to be assisted to access life saving basic services to mitigate exposure to more risks. The community will also be consulted through focused group discussions and with key informants in the community to assess and understand the existing capacity of the community and the vulnerable people themselves, gaps and challenges in supporting their people. HACO will provide legal assistance to individuals to access justice with the intent of discouraging similar GBV cases and others happening in future and fully mitigating the effects of GBV. The project will work with other clusters (wash, Education, health, and FSL) to identify and mitigate GBV risks that are linked to services provided by the sectors to have an integrated approach in identifying and addressing GBV related needs while planning, implementing and monitoring the projects. The project will also make legal awareness on human rights through community meeting and awareness sessions also sensitize the community on peace building and peaceful co-existence to create a good ground to end GBV in the community.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian Protection GBV needs assessment estimates people in need are 1.85 million. 77% female and 23% are male. While on the same target 56% are adults 44% are children, 15% are people with disability (HNO 2020). This project will target 12,000 individuals (5,174 women, 1,546 men, 4,066 girls, 1,214 boys) in the two locations of the target population in the area. HACO has been working with the communities in Rubkona and Panyijiar counties in Unity State implementing WASH project integrated with GBV and Nutrition with funding from South Sudan Humanitarian Fund (SSHF) while in the Western Equatoria State HACO has operated in Yambio up to Maridi implementing the UNDP project under Access to Justice September 2018- March 2019. Since 2018 march HACO has built the base with the community to support GBV activities as well as WASH and Nutrition activities. HACO continues in the area of Rubkona and Panyjiar supporting other agencies in community mobilization and awareness on WASH and GBV prevention and response, while in Yambio and Maridi awareness activities and advocacy for justice and legal redress to the vulnerable is on going . HACO maintained active presence in the area and has continued working with the community and other partners in the area to continue supporting the people in need. There has been active engagement of the affected community in the project planning process to build foundation for sustainable ownership of the project when it will pick up. Through interaction, by formal and informal dialogues and consultations, the communities (women, men, girls, boys and people with disabilities) without considering their age, gender and disability though their active engagement in the the humanitarian planning process have shown that there is a need to provide accessible support services in the area to the vulnerable and survivors of GBV violence. The community in Rubkona and Panyijiar has shown that lack of information and awareness on GBV is what is perpetuating the exploitation of the vulnerable that they can not voice out their situation. Early marriage/child marriage has been pointed that has extended GBV challenges in Unity State and Western Equatoria States, families because of poverty decide to give out their daughters early for marriage to get cows (wealth) before also Domestic violence perpetuated the violation of women rights. Because of conflict Rape has been rampant in the areas because of the broken apart families and community protection systems. There is a need to revive and strengthen the community protection systems to be held accountable for the protection of the vulnerable in the communities and protect female from sexual exploitation. The community lacks information and knowledge on human rights to be able to bring forward their complaints to the legal instrument for redress on their violated rights.. The community needs to be aware that the challenges of GBV they are facing have ways of prevention and response, and The community also showed that people who suffer from GBV violence need special psychological counselling and social care to recover and be re-embraced by the community. This calls for integrating mental health care services at the specialized referral services delivery points. As GBV programming is considered as an intervention there is also a need to consider prevention activities. To prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of the targeted people, an appropriate, safe, and inclusive community based feedback and complaints handling mechanism will be established and implemented in collaboration with other stakeholders to provide platforms for beneficiary’s active involvement in project and avenues to voice any case of sexual exploitation and abuse or concerns about the project. The community needs to strengthen its systems to protect the people and more especially the vulnerable groups. The community needs to know where the established services are and how people can access them.

2 - Activities

GBV programming activities will include all vulnerable women, men, girls, boys and people with disabilities taking into account the gender marker and inclusion of disabled individuals. Activity 1: Conduct GBV assessment in the community with other clusters to identify the protection risks and concerns raised by women, men, girls, boys and people with disability as a result of services provided and share the findings and recommendations with other actors and sub-clusters to mitigate the identified risk and in an integrated approach address the challenges. With a very strong component of community based interventions on raised challenges all sectors will plan to work together to reduce GBV among affected populations ensuring availability and access to health/nutrition services, safe water sources within safe and reachable distance, improved sanitation and hygiene practices including provision of NFIs to the vulnerable, physical safety and protection of the people, support life skills and livelihood activities for economic survival of the vulnerable. Activity 2: Mapping specialized delivery points for referral services, establish and maintain the existing. Activity 3: Capacity building of service providers by training and coaching case managers, social workers, and community outreach workers on the guiding principles of GBV, PFA, and referral pathways to be able to offer comprehensive case management and effectively to respond to cases of GBV survivors including management of child survivors Activity 4: Train service providers, case managers, police, community leaders, community support teams on GBV referral pathways, survivor centered approaches and psychosocial first aid. Activity 5: Offer quality, comprehensive and timely services to survivors. Activity 6: Provide individual psychosocial support (IPSS) and group psychosocial support (GPSS) using appropriate case management tools to all vulnerable and GBV survivors (women, men, girls , boys and people with physical disability). Activity 7: Integrate Mental Health Care Services at the services centers to be provided to GBV survivors to get counselling and life skills to help do income generating activities that will help survivors in healing, recovery and resilience process. Activity 8: Conduct awareness on GBV prevention and response, availability, and accessibility to GBV services and importance of accessing the services within 72 hours after the GBV incident by the survivor. Activity 9: Conduct regular community outreach, dialogue, mobilization and community awareness raising and sensitization on human rights and legal rights and provision of legal aid to those in need. Activity 10: In consultation with the community design, produce, print and distribute IEC and visibility materials that show the referral path way, where to access GBV services and role of the community in the healing, recovery and resilience of the survivor. The community will be sensitized on their commitment and accountability for the project to have benefit to the community and the targeted beneficiaries. Activity 11: In consultation with the community and the vulnerable through focused group discussion, dialogue and home visits where necessary decide to procure and distribute dignity kits to all vulnerable women and GBV survivors. Activity 12: Provide little cash to vulnerable women and GBV survivor for small income generating activities to help the healing, recovery and resilience process to reduce economic dependence. Activity 13: Conduct peace building and peaceful co-existence meeting sessions with the community to have sustainable peace that could bring to an end GBV in the community.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

HACO will ensure all the GBV survivors access psycho social support and counseling as the case may be and ensure redress of their human rights that have been violated in the process. Individual survivors will be supported with legal assistance to reclaim in the court of law their rights and their dignity that have been violated. Awareness and mass campaigns will be conducted to educated the community to know that their human rights are not to be violated. This will bring order and reduce the protection risks.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project will be set in the community for the protection of the community. It is to help everyone in need in an inclusive way. Every body has liberty to access the available GBV services in the area. Awareness campaigns will be conducted to ensure inclusion and that no GBV survivor is left. The project does not segregate the displaced people.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is linked to both the government and community systems. The project has been initiated after joint dialogue between the community, the government and the partner a need was identified. HACO has been in this area of Unity in Rubkona and Panyijiar working with the community. When the SSHF funding for the Wash project ended in September 2018 HACO being a national organisation did not leave the places continued working with the people to support the Government's initiative to improve the deliver of quality Wash and Nutrition services to the citizens. The project is linked to the needs of the community in the locations. The organisation will work with other cluster members in an integrated approach to ensure projects support each other to deliver services without duplicating their work to the same community. Through integrated efforts the organisation will be able to address the challenges faced by all the vulnerable gender and age groups including individuals with physical disability in the area.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned with National objectives of ensuring the vulnerable communities in very difficult to reach areas are well protected and getting all the basic services in an integrated way. This will make the Agencies operating in specific areas work together so as to identify and address the identified protection risks and challenges together.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The over all situation in Rubkona and Panyijiar is not yet very stable though with the on going political development at the national level it is expected the situation will improve in 2020 leading to reduced risks of GBV which have been precipitated by conflict. The community will be able to recover and continue with their normal life and access other humanitarian services to be provided in the area. The project looks forward to take services closer to the people so that distance and security do not hinder access to the critical and life saving services. In places where communities will still seem hostile awareness will be raised through the community structures on peace building, safe and secure environment to enhance community development, however services will be offered separately in both locations. With the improved safe environment, HACO will be able to implement the project by reaching the targeted population and address their priority needs together with other actors in the areas of Unity State.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. The project includes sustainability. The community will be made aware from the beginning that the project belongs to the affected people and the project team has accountability to ensure the project is implemented successfully while the community structures have accountability to follow up and ensure feedback from the community for corrections during implementation for the project to succeed and benefit the community. From the beginning to the end of the project will work in consultation with the community and other actors in a multi-sectoral approach to ensure everyone knows what is going on. At the end of the project all GBV cases not fully managed will be well documented and handed over. This is part of the exit strategy. The incoming agency will take over from there and continue working together with the community and the survivors looking for solutions to the pending issues. The project includes sustainability because it is designed in consultation with the community, assessment and monitoring during the implementation will be done jointly with the community to ensure they know the project and own it.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Inclusion will ensure all the targeted beneficiaries are equally accessing any service provided. Staff implementing the project together with community based protection committees should always make proper verification and monitoring. HACO staff have signed the code of conduct guiding on GBV and sexual exploitation and abuse as service providers. Beneficiaries should be part of the project monitoring committee as such they will be accountable to themselves. The project will set up a mechanism of collecting complaint from the community either by setting complaint desk or suggestion/complaint box which will be easily accessed by all members in the community or through face to face meetings and community dialogue. This will include timely reporting on all malpractices that have the nature of SEA through the complaint and feedback mechanism to mitigate GBV risks. The feedback mechanism will help to share where the good and bad experience has been noticed during the project implementation. The community should timely get a feedback on the grievances raised while community awareness and sensitization goes on to ensure the project implementation goes on for the benefit of the intended beneficiaries and the community is not left out or exposed to the risks. The project will raise awareness in the community and the beneficiaries on how the protection threats can be minimized and addressed. The project team having signed the code of conduct have accountability to the affected people (AAP) to offer free services. Affected people whom the project team are accountable to understand the behavior they should expect from project staff that they have protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). In case of any signs of SEA by aid actors the community should know how and where to report. The Affected people have also to understand how the assistance packages and services of the sexually exploited and abused survivor are and can be accessed in the area.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

The project will ensure accountability to the affected people by involving everyone by all possible ways: through meetings, individual consultation, organised focused group discussions, home visits for informed decision making to have successful implementation of the project. Those who can write will be given chance to express their view in writing secretly and placing their view in the complaint and feedback box.
