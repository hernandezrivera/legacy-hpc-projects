Project Summary

The project objective is to improve access to inclusive quality basic education for 37,226 girls and 42,941 boys, including children with disabilities, who are affected by conflict and natural disasters in 9 localities in North Darfur, Kassala, and White Nile states. This will be accomplished through improving facilities and furnishing schools affected by heavy rains and floods. The school facilities will be designed, constructed, and maintained to be resilient to flooding. The learning will be supported by the provision of teaching and learning supplies, recreational materials, as well as menstrual health and hygiene kits for adolescent girls. Safe water and sanitation facilities will be constructed that are gender sensitive and friendly for children with disabilities and teachers/PTAs will be trained on COIVD-19 School Protocol. Teachers will be trained on Package for Teachers in Crisis Contexts and provided teaching supplies. PTAs will be strengthened to participate in school governance, develop School Improvement Plans, and carry out awareness raising activities on the rights of all children to education and identifying and addressing oppressive norms that hold girls and boys back. The target schools will be linked to child protection, psychosocial services, and child protection referral pathways will be established.

1 - Needs

In North Darfur, White Nile, and Kassala states 671,059 conflict and disaster-affected basic education (primary school) and secondary school-aged children (134,508 internally displaced, 84,249 refugee, 66,186 returnee, and 470,367 non-displaced vulnerable children) have been identified by Sudan Education Sector. These children will require humanitarian assistance to fulfil their right to education. 225,744 school age children have been identified in need of education assistance in the 9 targeted localities of these states. Plan International Sudan will target 13,585 internally displaced children (7,463 girls and 6,122 boys) in 17 basic schools in Abu Shook camp of Al Fasher locality who were affected by heavy rains and flooding. In the 17 schools, a total of 122 classrooms were affected with 87 classrooms completely damaged and 35 partially affected. Moreover, 4 stores, 6 latrines and 5 teacher offices were partially damaged. In Kassala State, Plan International will target 7,564 non-displaced vulnerable children (3,677 girls and 3,887 boys) in basic schools in 6 localities of Reifi Hamashkureib, Rei Aroma, Rei Khashm Elgirba, Rei Nahr Atbara, Rei Shamal Adelta and Rei Kassla that were affected with heavy rains and floods that damaged 208 classrooms and 36 teachers offices and very low enrollment rates in particular among girls. In the 2 localities of White Nile state; Tendalti and Um Rimta; 55 basic schools affected by heavy rains and floods, where 129 classrooms, 36 teachers offices, and 72 latrines were damaged—affecting education of 60,829 non-displaced vulnerable children (27,897 girls and 32,932). The heavy rains led to further deterioration of the already poor schools’ environment that lack proper seating, qualified teachers, teaching means, etc. Children’s education in the target localities are challenged by poor access, poor retention and poor learning outcomes resulting from complex and mutually reinforcing patterns of disadvantage including: poverty, social norms that lead to gender inequities in education, disability, conflict, and displacement. The majority of target schools are in rural areas and already suffer from a shortage of clean water and lack gender-sensitive sanitation facilities, which affects both attendance and learning. The quality of education is generally poor with many children in grade 3 struggling to read simple familiar words and less than half comprehending what they read. (Ministry of Education, 2018 National Learning Assessment report). School environments are often unsafe, with cases of bullying, gender-based violence, abuse, and corporal punishment happening in schools. Additionally, due to COVID-19 all schools in Sudan closed March 2020 and remained closed for an extended period with school reopening postponed until at least December 2020. It is expected that the most vulnerable children, particularly children living in areas affected by conflict, will not be able to return to school or to progress in school unless significant support is provided to mitigate against this long absence from school.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Plan International maintains high standards of Safeguarding and PSHEA (preventing sexual harassment, exploitation and abuse), more-so in emergency situations. Plan’s Safeguarding Policy and procedures are implemented during the design and implementation of project interventions with additional vigilance and we adapt and strengthen existing interventions to identify and reduce risks to girls, boys, children, young people and beneficiaries. Safeguarding and PSHEA risk analysis and assessment of project interventions will be conducted with mitigation measures/contingency plans identified for each risk. Plan will work to ensure staff, partners, beneficiaries and communities are aware of the safeguarding policy, its minimum codes of behavior, and requirements to uphold high standards of professional behavior. The implementing partners were assessed to ensure the adequacy of the partner’s safeguarding and PSHEA policies and guidelines. Mitigation strategies take account of particular risks or vulnerabilities associated with girls, boys, children and young people. Children and young people are respected and their safety and protection is not compromised when obtaining and using information on and images of them.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Plan International will ensure accountability to affected people, as well as the donor, local partner, relevant government stakeholders. The project will involve the target community members, where appropriate working through existing community structures whilst ensuring representation of women, youth, people with disabilities, elderly and the related CBOs, in the project cycle management. Target groups and other related project stakeholders will be fully oriented on the project objectives and deliverables using safe methods and forums such messages to ensure that, a sense of ownership is created among target communities and increased accountability towards the beneficiaries targeted by the project. Plan International Sudan has a child friendly Community Feedback & Response Mechanism (CFRM) system rolled out at state level in all its locations.This CFRM are in line with the Core Humanitarian Standards and will enable community members to provide their feedback, concerns and reflect their opinions on the project performance in a safe and confidential manner. The accountability systems have multiple entry points, designed to be accessible for young people, women, PWDs, and other vulnerable groups through introduction of different approaches in collaboration with the communities. Entry points will include hotline/Sim numbers, WhatsApp and boxes as safe entry points to allow target beneficiaries to share their feedback/complaints. Beneficiaries are oriented and encouraged to use the system, which established in each target community. The received feedbacks/complaints are analyzed and addressed following the internal procedures that are aligned to the Common Humanitarian standards. Feedback/complaints concerning other project stakeholders (government staff, partners) will be referred to their senior management as appropriate where concerned organization can take the necessary actions. Plan will also seek through safe methods through community volunteers and CBOs members to allow children to reflect their opinions and feedback about the project as well as using logbooks at schools that target beneficiaries can register feedbacks. Externally Plan is a member of the AAP/PSEA network and mechanisms and interacts with UNCT/UNOPS call centers and AAP focal points in other organizations that is set up by the humanitarian actors in Sudan. Plan will contribute to enhancing technical standards and improving AAP and PSEA mechanisms and approaches based on lessons learned and experience from Plan globally, has strong internal Safeguarding policies, standards, procedures as well as robust training and capacity building manuals that can enhance AAP and PSEA roll out, reporting and responding. Plan will also ensure to participate in needs, information and assessments on AAP and PSEA as initiated by the AAP network as appropriate. Plan will commit to adhere to implement the AAP and PSEA in its design and delivery of projects and reporting requirements.
