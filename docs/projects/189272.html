Project Summary

WASH needs continue to grow amongst the most vulnerable communities of Tonj East County, Warrap State. The most recent WASH severity mapping puts the county at five (5) largely characterized by insufficient access to safe water supply, sanitation and hygiene services (WES, 2021; HNO 2021; HNO 2022). According to rapid needs assessment recently conducted by Help Restore Youth (HeRY) in the county in January 2022, more than 65% of the households do not have access to clean water with over vulnerable households struggling to access water for over 1 hour travel of water collection. In the same vein, over 84% of the sampled households in Romic(h) in Paliang, Makuach, Maparah, Pagor2, Pannhial, and Paweng Payams of Tonj East County do not have safe human excreta disposal facilities. According to the health data provided in the assessment, children below ages of ten (10) years in the fourth quarter of 2021, 70% admissions were related to watery diarrhea, dysentery and typhoid illnesses. In some months of 2020/2021, the county was flagged by WHO and on ground health partner-CCM having growing cases of cholera. Equally vulnerable lactating mothers and elderly records indicated an estimated 51% similar treatments administered to the patients on watery diarrhea, dysentery and typhoid illnesses. The health authorities reported that common infections amongst under children had increased, the frequency and severity of such infections could be linked to very low immunity levels of the children partly attributable to poor nutrition and dieting for the children during the 1,000 days and saw delayed recoveries of these children from the preventable illnesses. HeRY key informants interview with health facility management further revealed that the community lacked improved WASH facilities (inadequate clean water, poor sanitation and inadequate hygiene) arguably justifying the humanitarian need in provision of emergency integrated WASH services in Tonj East County.
In the last three years in row (2019-2021) Tonj East (csrf south sudan 2020) and its neighboring counties like Tonj North have seen exponential deterioration in human life caused by a number of factors such as bloody communal conflicts amongst cattle keepers and farmers, clashes between armed groups continued to occur over water sources and grazing lands, cyclical floods and covid 19 effects among key vulnerabilities. This led to sizeable displacements, loss of livelihoods and the Tonj communities being cut off to essential integrated humanitarian services due to insecurities and access impediments. The IPC /FSNMS data for 2021 revealed the county was in IPC5 reflecting high food insecurity and loss of livelihoods to a number of households.
It’s against this background, that in line with WASH cluster WES priorities, HNO and WASH Cluster objectives, HeRY proposes an integrated lifesaving WASH services targeting the most vulnerable 20,065 individual (under 5year SAM/MAM children, lactating women, youth, elderly and persons with disabilities) in top six (6) severely affected payams: Romic(h) in Paliang, Makuach, Maparah, Pagor2, Pannhial, and Paweng Payams of Tonj East County.

1 - Needs

Inter-communal clashes continued in 2020/2021 communal clashes between the Thiik, Jalwau and Konggor communities in Tonj area and resulted in over 30,000 people being displaced (IOM DTM, 2020; csrf South Sudan 2020). The conflict drivers concerned competition over access and control of resources. In the dry spell that runs from January to July every year, Tonj East county and its neighboring areas experiences severe WASH needs. Protected water sources such as boreholes and spring wells, usually dry up or dysfunction due to operation and maintenance challenges among others no spare parts, limited WASH partner presence to mobilize resources to maintain the water points and limited capacity of the line ministry county directorate of water and sanitation. The Tonj East community largely believes there is ho harm in open defecation as evidenced in the sanitation and hygiene mapping through a transact walk held by HeRY WASH focal persons most recently in the area. Whereas this assertion could not hold true according to the community’s belief systems with a pastoralism lifestyle, recent needs assessment conducted by HeRY revealed worrying trends in preventable diseases such as watery diarrhea, dysentery, and typhoid among the most vulnerable population (children, lactating mothers, elderly persons and elders). According to rapid needs assessment, more than 65% of the households do not have access to clean water who access it with over 1 hour travel of water collection in the dry season. In the same vein, over 84% of the sampled households do not have safe human excreta disposal facilities at household and key public gathering places. According to the health data from Comitato Collaborazione Medica in Paliang PHCC, children below ages of ten (10) years in the fourth quarter of 2021, 70% of the admissions were related to water diarrhea, dysentery and typhoid illnesses. Equally vulnerable lactating mothers and elderly admissions record estimated 51% similar treatments administered to the patients. The county health authorities reported that common infections amongst under children had increased, the frequency and severity of such infections could be attributable to low immunity levels amongst the most vulnerable partly attributed to poor nutrition and dietary intake in the meals, especially for the children during the 1,000 days and these patients have recorded delayed recoveries than usual. Further, Key informants sessions with PHCC facility management revealed that the community lacked improved WASH facilities, arguably justifying the humanitarian need in provision of Integrated emergency WASH services. In the last three years in row (2019-2021) according to conflict sensitivity resource facility South Sudan, 2020, Tonj area has seen exponential deterioration in human life caused by a number of factors such as bloody communal conflicts amongst cattle keepers and farmers, clashes between armed groups continued to occur over water sources and grazing lands, cyclical floods further increased the vulnerabilities leading to sizeable displacement of the population, loss of livelihoods and the most often, the community being cut off due to insecurity to critical access to essential integrated humanitarian services. For instance, In the violent clashes from October/November 2021, water tanks which supplies water to residents in Romich market, Gokloch, Turagot, Malek and Piokjiak villages were destroyed. The County WASH situation continues to be dire.In line with WASH cluster and HNO 2022 priorities, HeRY proposes an integrated emergency WASH services through repair and rehabilitation of water points, sanitation facilities at critical health Centre and provide household led hygiene awareness to the targeted to 20,065 vulnerable individuals (malnourished children, lactating mothers, households with a disabled and elderly) in 6 payams of the Tonj East county Romic(h) in Paliang, Makuach, Maparah, Pagor2, Pannhial, and Paweng Payams.

2 - Project activities

1.Provision of clean wateri. Conduct WASH safety audit around 20 dysfunctional water pointsii. Repair and rehabilitate 20 dysfunctional water points ( 3 solarized pumps , 14 Borehole and 3 protected wells) at Nutrition site, and in the community with high GAM and flood vulnerabilities iii. Form 100 community led water user committees and pump mechanics and equip them with scalable critical WASH operation and maintenance skills.iv. Undertake 2 WASH needs market assessment and Link community led water user committees and pump mechanics to market for sustainable access to WASH spare parts.v. Undertake risk communication awareness on Covid 19 prevention. 2. Sanitation i. Repair or construct 10 gender disaggregated pit latrine at Nutrition site (patients- F/M; Staff)ii. Equip 10 repaired or constructed disaggregated pit latrine at Nutrition site with hand washing stationiii. Form and train 50 community sanitation committee for operation and maintenance of sanitation facilities.iv. Undertake risk communication awareness on Covid 19 prevention. 3. Hygiene activities i. Couples of 200 SAM children provided with standard WASH Kitii. Training of 200 SAM children mothers/care givers on the use of standard WASH Kit and conduct hygiene promotion awareness on WASH and Nutritioniii. Conduct standard WASH Kit and conduct hygiene promotion awareness to vulnerable 20,065 individuals on WASH and Nutrition at community with high GAM and WASH severity needsiv. Undertake risk communication awareness on Covid 19 prevention.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

A number of affordable and confidential complaints reporting mechanisms will be embedded in this project not limited to : dedicated phone line, help desk, and adequate information sharing on the dangers while accessing WASH services. In line with the project schedule, scope, their feedbacks obtained through WASH safety audits will be used to deliver no or less harm interventions especially to the most vulnerable girls, women and PWDs

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

At the onset of the emergency WASH project, PWDs representatives (women, girls, boys etc) will be part of the WASH operation and maintenance structures e.g. Water user committee, community hygiene promoters and sanitation committees. Their leadership structure will be targeted to obtain confidential feedback and HeRY will also focus on front staff together with local authorities to target involvement, and incorporation of complaints and suggestions of these groups using the affordable means of outreach (speech, sign language channels of communication)

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

HeRY upholds safeguarding principles in all our WASH interventions. Our staff, volunteers and WASH service providers sign a zero tolerance PSEA compliance/code of conduct. At our beneficiary engagements/activities implementation, the community will be encouraged to confidentially in accessible way share PSEA concerns to HeRYs focal point for support (inclusive referral) to protection specialists within the working groups

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

At the design of the project, needs of all gender groups and ages will be consulted on the priority need. At the inception/roll out of the project, all most vulnerable men, women, boys, girls and persons with disabilities (PWDs) will be provided information on the project, seek out to engage them in checking whether assistance reached to the beneficiaries using M/E tools, participating project beneficiaries will be sought information of how best they can offer feedback and the most channels in their community. HeRY will provide additional guidance on how the organization supports zero tolerance on PSEA and encourage the community members to report on any incidences. In the WASH project, HeRY will target assistance after having conducted safety audits at the WASH facilities/infrastructure to be able to adopt project assistance and have context specific interventions supporting all the most vulnerable grounds and deliver integrated emergency WASH services (addressing nutrition and health outcomes).
