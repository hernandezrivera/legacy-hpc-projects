Project Summary

Increased likelihood of below-average rainfall during the 2021 Deyr (October-December) season across most of the country, which would adversely affect food security and nutrition outcomes(FSNAU, Post Gu Report 2021) for 2022. Supporting Rural Livelihoods through Improved Food Security in Sanaag Region is designed to address food shortages among the poor households in Sanaag Region. The project will improve the household’s food and economic status through climate-smart agriculture for 250 smallholder farmers and economic development through Savings and Loans for transformation for 100 women in villages of Bixin, Ceelcade, Gowsawaye, Garadag and Karasharka IDP. The project aims to improve food production and resilient dependable agri-food system from farm to market to table and strengthen the pillars of food security and spur the availability of sufficient food, access to nutritious food and meet the emergency needs of the target communities.
Food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced populations due to the impacts of anticipated, below-average 2021 Deyr (October-December) season rainfall, rising food prices and cost of living, and a likely reduction in agricultural employment opportunities during the forthcoming Deyr season(FSNAU Post Gu Report, 2021) . To bridge this, the project will mainstream innovative, sustainable, affordable and locally owned approaches to improve food security in Sanaag/Sool. The project will enhance the capacity of households in low-income brackets of 5 villages in Sanaag to produce a wide variety of vegetables using sustainable and environmentally-friendly technologies. Priority in the selection of target groups and beneficiaries will be given to resource-poor and vulnerable HH without access to secure livelihood among the 117,000 beneficiaries in IPC3&4 in Sanaag, according to FSNAU Report
A total of 10 Farmer organizations and 5 women savings groups(Savings and Loans for Transformation-SALT) will be formed as the management units at the village level. The women SALT groups will be linked with the farmer groups and ensure women purchase the farm produce for sale to improve market chains from farm gate to the market. To ensure immediate access to food, the 350HH will be supported with an emergency conditional cash transfer of 70 USD to ensure they can access food and save in their SALT/farmers groups. The cash transfer will be conditional on attendance of training sessions and formation of the SALT/farmer groups in the first three months. Household purchasing power is rapidly declining due to the reduction of key income sources coupled with sharp increases in domestic and imported cereal prices(FEWSNET, Oct 2021). If the HH access food and can save in the SALT groups, they will be able to initiate small-scale businesses that will support their HH in the period to acces food and other needs.
Vegetable production using hydroponic system, with less water, which makes it ideal for the target region will deliver a higher yield per m2 of land with a faster maturity period compared to conventional farming methods. Ten hydroponics greenhouses will be installed in 5 villages inclusive of an IDP site in Garadag. Farmers will be trained on the farming technique for ownership and sustainability of the techniques. Lead farmers as village agricultural advisors in the 5 villages will be trained to ensure farmers in the five villages continue to receive support and assistance during and after the project implementation period. Seasonal inputs will be provided to ensure the households are producing food.
The project will target 350 vulnerable men, women in IDPs& host, people with disabilities and minority clans. The identification, selection and approval of beneficiaries will use a community-managed targeting approach with WCDO staff and village committees. The community-led approach will be complemented with a robust verification process to validate the BNFs

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Using cash or vouchers recognizes that beneficiaries are best placed to decide what their households need, and gives them the flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities. Thus improving the dignity of the affected population. There are positive effects to support local economic development mostly in the rural where the business will be providing the needed commodities by the beneficiaries. This Supports or revitalizes local trade through the Injections of cash with local traders and spurs recovery of trade and the local economy. The multiplier effects that come by stimulating local markets through cash-based interventions may benefit more than the direct recipients of cash. Local traders and service providers, transport workers and suppliers and transporters of goods may all benefit from households having money to spend. The process is time and cost-efficient for agencies and beneficiaries. When goods are available and accessible locally, cash or voucher-based interventions have the potential to be more rapid than bringing supply from out of country or region

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The feasibility of CVA rests on the market's functionality and seasonal factors to provide adequate goods to the affected population. The project has put into consideration of the functional markets in the target villages, access to those markets and linkage to other major markets and price variability. Owing to the fluctuation of the shilling against the dollar, the three months will be monitored through Post distribution monitoring and assess the price change(if any) against the major household commoditiesThe project is assessing risks that should be mitigated in a cash project including ensuring digital transfer to minimize delays of providing assistance and delivery mechanisms capacity through mobile money. The program will ensure fundamental CVA training for the staff so that appropriate engagement with the market-based programming is internalized as we implement the project

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project will ensure a comprehensive community-based targeting to select the most vulnerable households with an inclusion lens. Elaborate selection criteria will be developed together with the community that will include minority clan members, female-headed households, IDPs among others. The targeting criteria will be discussed and agreed with the field teams, who will, in turn, work closely with the targeting committees to ensure the agreed groups are included in the projectWorld Concern has a solid PSEA policy and all staff are inducted on and signed to its agreement in spirit and letter. Raising awareness on how one can report such a case if it happens. During the project, we will ensure proper information sharing about the project scope and the entitlements therein and the duration of the project, sensitize the community on issues of PSEA and the available reporting mechanism for WCDO; retrain staff on PSEA and Core Humanitarian Standards and ensure the Code of Conduct and Child Protection Policies are made aware to the staff. during targeting, WCDO will ass the likely groups to be marginalized and why are they marginalized( Is it because of sex, age, disability, ethnic) and include that as a criteria The program will ensure community engagement every quarter is carried out to share issues affecting the project and the communities. Practicing accountability through complaint response mechanism, staff will consult a cross-section of BNFs through community meetings and ensure views of marginalized groups especially women and minority clans are captured and addressed. A toll-free number is always provided to the communities to share feedback and any complaint that is recorded and followed up for resolution Accountability to beneficiaries will involve designing appropriate, clear messages, explaining the targeting and validation process and training beneficiaries to understand their entitlements, including cash transfers. Communities will also be trained on the WCDO existing community feedback and whistle-blower mechanisms (which enables raised concerns to be communicated in good faith and honestly with the individual(s) causing the concern associated with WC Code of Conduct or sharing anonymous emails or calling a toll free number to provides a wide space for communicating, managing complaint and feedback).To enhance participation, WCDO staff will therefore encourage both men and women to participate through community meetings and individual sessions. Information on awareness of the accountability system as well as utilization will be captured in post distribution and community meetings and disaggregated according to gender.To enhance accountability, the project will ensure key staff are retrained on Core Humanitarian Standards to ensure transparency by sharing information on project activities, budgets and timings, using posters in the local language and posting them at accessible places. Staff will be trained on how to capture stories, case studies and pictures to share with beneficiaries, governments and donors. Many IDPs, live on land with high tenure insecurity. Low-income groups and IDPs lack the networks, resources and ability to navigate the systems that facilitate access to land, shelter, finance, and services. Entering into informal rental arrangements leaves them at risk of sudden and forced evictions. World Concern will engage with clan elders in areas of dispute mostly among the IDPs and ensure if any land issue is amicably resolved
