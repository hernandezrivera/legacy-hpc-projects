Project Summary

The response is primarily focused on supporting children who have lost learning and on those who have no access to safe, quality education due to their displacement. This will be achieved by providing boys and girls with early childhood care and education (ECCE), and basic non-formal education (NFE) services with other integrated MHPSS, CP, WASH and cash components to mitigate the effects of the multiple barriers and vulnerabilities that boys and girls face as they attempt to access education.
To ensure access to early learning and child development opportunities, non-formal early childhood development (ECCE) services will be provided.
Wellbeing needs of children is therefore a key part of our response where social emotional learning support is provided for children, along with wellbeing support for teachers. The data indicates children who do not attend school seem to be more likely to come from a household with reported psychological distress with under 18-year olds than children who attend school.
Supporting children’s safe access to quality education does imply the importance of working with teachers and improving their capacity to provide education that is child centered and responsive to the different needs of boys and girls. The project will be supporting the teachers knowledge and skills, through different approaches, so that they are better positioned to showcase positive teaching practices inside the classroom and outside the classroom, specifically when they interact with parents and communities. Learning spaces infrastructure will be upgraded to support the children’s right to safe and quality education. WASH facilities will be upgraded so children have access to clean drinking water, latrines are segregated and functional and connected to wastewater collection systems that are safe.

The project will be targeting such locations that has no or limited education services in the camp/community, high number of out of school children Internal Displaced children in school age, children in risk for child labor, early marriage and other CP risks. Clear vulnerability criteria will be established to select the most vulnerable beneficiaries, and especially for the CVA support.
The project will be supporting objective number 1: This will be through the ECE, NFE support, BTL campaigns, upgrading classrooms, provision of education materials, winterization and heating support for children and for facilities, winter support/clothes, cash for protection.
As for objective 2: The project will be supporting Teacher and education personnel with professional development opportunities in accordance to INEE standards, PSS support for children and school staff, education materials and supplies and life skills support.
As for objective 3: PTAs will be either established or strengthened and on supporting partners inside Syria to develop strategic thinking on advocacy uses for data on attacks on education: monitoring, reporting.
Children will transition from one non formal education level to another, and same for ECCE. Teachers capacity will be enhance by various Teachers professional development opportunities and the teachers learning centers,. The community structures will be strengthened in the second year based on the learnings from the first years.
The project will work on building the capacity of the trusted and responsible adults in children’s lives – their caregivers, teachers, other community members and service providers. It will strengthen their ability to communicate with children, and better support their learning and development needs. This will be achieved through the establishment of PTA’s. At the wider community level, positive parenting messages and gender equality will continue through the constant dialogue with community structures, Parent/Teacher Associations (PTAs). Out of the 37930 targeted children, 21% are assessed to be in Severity 4, and 79% in severity 3.

4 - Needs

According to the HNO 2022, a self-reported non-attendance rate of 36% IDP children living in. SC’s integrated education programming is centered on the provision of basic Non-Formal Education (NFE) via learning spaces with a concerted focus on developing basic literacy and numeracy skills, while also providing integrated CP, MHPSS and other basic servicesECCE boosts children’s school readiness by improving early literacy and numeracy skills. ECCE has also been shown to effectively improve equity and mitigate the impact of children’s socio-economic background on their developmental trajectory. Economic related issues were the most prominent top three reasons why children were not in school. 33% of households reported that they could not afford for children to go. CVA support becomes important to mitigate the effect on school access.Improving teacher’s abilities is also a key priority for households’ with school aged children (60%).All new education personnel will undergo comprehensive capacity building TiCC inclusive for PSEA. To contribute to state, non-state actors and the humanitarian system better understanding and taking actions to reduce rights violations against children affected by conflict and crisis, Save the Children will work on monitoring attacks on education. This work will be achieved by supporting partners inside Syria to develop strategic thinking on advocacy uses for data on attacks on education: monitoring, reporting, training, speaking out.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will utilize BTL campaigns which are an important step to outreach the wider community and inform them about the project’s objectives and activities. SC utilizes these evens to dialogue with the community on important message related to children’s education, and the available options that can create pathways for their future formal education enrollment. SC also establishes and strengthens important community structures like the PTA so that challenges are raised during implementation related to children’s education, and to ensure a constant interaction and engagement between parents, teachers and communities. Issues related to attendance, achievements, parents support is discussed. Constant engagement is also made during the project period with the other important structures like the child protection committee to support protection actions for children in the learning spaces with the protection team. Coordination is also made with any actors to ensure the project reaches the most vulnerable and that no duplication in action occurs.Education will link with WASH by ensuring children have access to clean drinking water, latrines are segregated and functional and connected to wastewater collection systems that are safe. The hygiene promotion team will work with the TLS staff to encourage hand washing practices. A monitoring plan is developed during the inception phase of the project.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Learning facilities can be attacked by parties to the conflict or by communities. Attacks may include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of teachers or students, direct attacks on teachers or students, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school, arson, theft or looting. Likelihood: Medium, impact: high. Mitigation: TLC should have safety protocols in place. Risk 2: Teachers, or other education personnel participate in physical punishment. Students participate in bullying or act violently towards students and/or to teachers. Likelihood: High, impact: high. Mitigation: TLC should have an enforced code of conduct against all forms of violence .School personnel should be trained on prevention and reporting of. Risk 3: Learning facilities are unsafe, does not provide protection form adverse weather condition, inadequate WASH facilities or lack of sex disaggregated WASH facilities. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High. Mitigation: Improve safety of learning facilities through rehabilitation, repairs and provision of additional WASH facilities.Risk 4:Risk of diversion of cash by the Money Transfer Agency (MTA). Mitigation: the Framework Agreement with the MTA, is vetted by an ATC check in UK and US platforms. Programme/MEAL staff are present during distributions to oversee that this is done in accordance with established procedures, collect feedback from beneficiaries exit surveys.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks: Women and girls not able to access services or participate in income generating activities due to sexual harassment; exploitation and abuse by aid workers; female heads of HHs and adolescent girls in particular risk of sexual exploitation in exchange to services (Voices from Syria, 2022); risk of not reporting SEA for fear of retaliation; lack of awareness on reporting channels; stigma and shame and fear of losing on the services making it difficult for SC to hold staff/partners involved accountable. Mitigations: SC has a Child Safe Guarding policy which includes PSEA. To mitigate the risk of PSEA during the assistance delivery, the following will be included: mandatory training and regular refreshers on Child Safeguarding, Safe programming and PSEA policy to SC and partner staff; working closely with the WoS PSEA TF to strengthen feedback, reporting and response mechanisms for SEA; conducting community awareness and sensitization sessions on PSEA with emphasis on reporting and ensuring safety of survivors through referrals; regular participatory safety audit to identify protection and safety concerns arising as a result of project implementation and developing action plans to mitigate identified risks collaboratively with the community; including PSEA messages and topics in all IEC materials and training sessions conducted under the project; ensuring that centrality of protection is embedded in all projects to mitigate risks and prevent potential harm.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Key risks and assumptions identified and mitigation measures include: A volatile security environment and escalation in conflict, such as recent incidents in Idleb and Al-Hasakeh, which may prohibit movement of staff and limit access by children and families in need to services. SC regularly monitors contextual developments and employs security focal points to advise on and guide staff movements. SC staff complete mandatory security trainings and context-specific safety and security management plans are regular reviewed and update. SC actively engages in advocacy efforts at multiple levels to highlight risk to civilians, particularly children, and advocate for sustained humanitarian access. Risk of continued COVID-19 restrictions may delay or hamper project delivery. Mitigation includes adapting programming modalities (reduced group size, remote, etc.), use of PPE by all SC and partner staff; incorporating COVID-19 awareness and health safety messages in all activities with children and families. The deepening economic crisis in Syria is leading to decreases in purchasing power for communities and complicating budgeting process for humanitarian actors. Mitigation measures include diverse money transfer channels and continuing to deliver our CVA in foreign currencies (USD) so beneficiaries do not lose part of their assistance due to currency devaluations. Engagement with donors on budgeting flexibility as well as reviewing currency fluctuations and tracking market trends.
