Project Summary

The project addresses main problem "rise in GBV, discrimination and harmful practices against vulnerable women 18-59 and>59years/ girls 14-17years including with disabilities affected by escalations & protracted humanitarian crisis in Gaza and limited access to multi-sectoral GBV services." It addresses protection/GBV risks, access barriers and needs for women/girls identified in needs assessment & gender analysis conducted on sheltering services MHPSS services like , group/individual PSS, couple therapy, specialized mental health; legal counseling/representation; dignity kits; economic enablement; protection cash assistance; case management including awareness & information materials on PSEA; self-care
The project targets directly 4590 beneficiaries women, men, girls, boys, identified based on vulnerability criteria: with disabilities, elderly, IDPs, refugees, GBV survivors & poverty according to their location, status, protection risks & needs severity.
The project links to MSNA/HNO 2022, PC objective 2/ HRP SO2, HRP2023 eligible actions (activities, indicators).
CWLRC consolidate his experience in geographical& thematic areas and coordinate with CBOs to target women/girls GBV survivors and address their needs.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in the oPt is often classified as a protracted protection crisis (HNO 2022) and protection concerns are interwoven to some extent throughout all other sectoral aspects of the MSNA. Particularly telling of the impact of protection related incidents on the well-being of Gaza households is the observation that 40.0% of households reported at least one member of their household experiencing signs of psychosocial distress of trauma in the year prior to data collection. Specific population groups,including women, children, and persons with disability are considered particularly at risk for experiencing protection threats, with 25.3% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns for children, 38.4% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns for children with a disability, and 15.7% of households identifying specific safety and security concerns forwomen. HNO (2022) indicates that 37.5% of women 18-64 years experienced violence. Of those, 56.6% experienced psychological violence, 17.8% physical violence, 8.8% sexual violence, 32.5% social violence, 41.1% economic violence, 8% cyber violence & 24% early marriage. 19% and 17% of households are concerned about safety/ security of girls & women respectively. 8,250 persons were internally displaced need legal aid for GBV survivors and widowed women relating to denial of resources, custody of children and loss of identity documents; girls<18 yeas who are still displaced need PSS; 58% of WWDs lack information on GBV services; socio-cultural patriarchal norms; and limited access to multi-sectoral GBV services. UN WOMEN (2022) states that most common types of violence against women observed in Gaza include domestic violence, sexual harassment, early marriage and femicide. The outdated and discriminatory Palestinian laws hinder GBV survivors from accessing gender-responsive services and obtaining justice. In addition, survivors of violence often face social stigma, and are blamed as responsible for the violence occurred to them.according to SDR findings that ,Women and girls in oPt are subjected to GBV in both public and private spheres. Most prevalent forms of GBV: psychological abuse, child/early/forced marriage, intimate partner/family violence, and sexual exploitation and abuse;Women and girls highlight that GBV negatively impacts their movement and community participation and ,The legal framework in oPt does not provide full protection for women and girls subjected to GBV. Certain legislation does not fully meet international human rights standards and secure women’s access to justice and protection from violence.The data of the case management services of CWLRCP indicate that there has been a drastic increase in GBV cases. CWRCP has measured the increase of requests for assistance in the period from 23. August 2022 to 29th of September 2022 they have received 40% increase in phone calls from GBV survivors, 13% more legal service consultations, 25% more requests for psychosocial support services and an increase in 39% with regards to GBV survivors requesting sheltering in the safe house. The number of women that seek sheltering in the Hayat Centre has increased dramatically for two reasons: the first one is related to the general increase in cases due to the increase the violence average against women and girls . The second increase in cases is related to the temporary closure of the state-run shelter in conflict emergency . the Hayat Centre as the only institution who provides continuous and high-quality services to GBV survivors. In December 2021, a project-based gender analysis was conducted by an external consultant. According to this gender analysis, the main findings are summarized as follows: GBV prevention and response is crucial in the Gaza Strip, gendered interventions should not stop there. This gender analysis study shows the structural causes of GBV in Gaza and its impacts on the situation of women and girls amid masculine community and lack of protective laws from GBV, poverty, conflict and war. As illustrated in the findings above women and girls survivors of violence face many obstacles that limit their opportunities t emo be empowered. There are various challenges that heighten a woman risk of GBV which include poverty, male dominate and family interventions. The gender analysis also demonstrates that the project to certain extent has succeeded in empowering women and girls in some aspects such as psychological resilience, social relations, legal knowledge and protection. Based on desk review, FGDs and interviews conducted for this gender analysis, below are main recommendations to support women and girls survivors of violence:A) Recommendations to Address Structural Causes of GBVo To conduct awareness raising sessions for families including men about early marriage. o To conduct awareness raising workshops for women and men on GBV, sexual harassment and the risks of suicide. o To network with media institution for raising the awareness of Gaza community in GBV.o To network with social media influencers to promote women rights through social media platforms.2) Lack of Effective Laws Related to GBVo To continue coordination meetings with the main actors of Justice Sector (Police, Public Prosecution, and Sharia Court) to influence them to support GBV cases and apply legal procedures that protect GBV cases and encourage them to report violence against them.o To continue capacity-building activities for the members of Justice Sector on women rights, CEDAW convention and ways of protection women and girls from GBV. 3) Poverty and Unemploymento To refer GBV cases to other organizations that implement job creation projects and economic support for women and girls.CWLRCP will cover the above gaps and counties in providing the protection services as ( legal, social, psychological, sheltering, economic support) as top priorities needs for women.

2 - Activities

Activities are developed in line with 2023 GBVSC HRP eligible actions, GBV Minimum Standards, referrals made according to GBVSC Referral Pathway and Service Directory, Case management are linked to GBVIMS+ Case Management Guidelines, and seek to align their procedures with Case Management Guidelines. Activities are: -Provide sheltering services to 30 women -Provide structured individual sessions to 310 women (310 WWDs) / and refer those with common problems to group PSS. -Conduct structured group PSS sessions for 100 women 15 participant/group, 5 sessions/group). -Conduct group couple therapy sessions for 100 couples. -Organize 5recreational open days for 150 women. Provide individual legal counselling to 500 women and men on inheritance, property rights, marriage, divorce, alimony & child custody. -Provide legal representation in courts to 100 women - Provide dignity kits containing essential hygiene items to 300 women -Provide protection cash assistance of one-off payment tailored for each case at an average of 100$/women to 20 women -Provide economic enablement & support for 10 women to start own business. Conduct 100 awareness sessions (20 participants/ session) on PSEA including information on rights and safe complaint channels available to beneficiaries, protection risk mitigation and response for 2500 women/ girls and men - Print and disseminate 1 GBV service directory, number of printed is 3000. Provide child visitation services for at least 70 children for families (divorced couples) which entails biweekly visits though hayat social worker coordination between families. - Provide case management of specialized individual psychosocial support for 100 children( ( both gender)each year through individual sessions - Provide 15 children protection who affected by grave violations or ill treatment who require legal assistance
