1 - Needs

According to the HNO draft for 2020 year 570 houses were damaged in 2019. It confirms that civilian property and infrastructure remain a target for both parties of the conflict. In GCA, the Shelter/NFI Cluster found a gap of 2,000 households that require humanitarian repairs with approximately 1,260 houses and 740 houses in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, respectively. The extent of damage in NGCA remains unknown due to the inability of partners to conduct a detailed damage assessment in view of the lack of security and humanitarian access.Winterization remains a life-saving need for vulnerable residents on both sides of the ‘contact line’, especially in settlements with limited security and difficult physical access, including to basic services and NFIs. During the cold winter months, many such households depend on coal, wood or other fuels for heating, which are often unavailable or costly, especially in isolated settlements. Over 1.4 million people are registered as IDPs by the Government of Ukraine. Over 7,600 IDPs live in collective centers across Ukraine, many of them in dire conditions. Due to accumulated debts for rent and utilities, many of them are at risk of eviction. Since last year, reports on cases of IDP evictions have increased dramatically and continue to grow. The Shelter/NFI Cluster is aware of at least four collective centres facing the risk of imminent eviction, while seven modular settlements will have their right of use expire soon. This will leave over 2,000 IDPs at risk of homelessness. Many IDPs who reside in collective centers are elderly pensioners, people with disabilities and children whose lives will be at risk without a roof, especially considering the approaching winter and the lack of winterization assistance. Coal to survive the winter as a priority need, the remaining 6,600 people living in other oblasts report to local authorities and Cluster partners the urgent need to find durable housing solutions. Around 50,000 vulnerable IDPs, returnees and non-displaced persons living in social institutions and non-specialized collective centres in NGCA are in need of critical winterization assistance to be able to survive the upcoming winter.

2 - Activities & outcomes

With the above needs/strategies in mind, PIN proposes the following shelter/NFI activities for 2019. Outcome 1. Satisfied essential shelter and NFI needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and conflict affected population through monetised/in-kind assistance and contingency1. Providing solid fuel & heater distribution (GCA and NGCA)• PIN Shelter team plans to cover needs for winterization with solid fuel (coal or briquettes) to the residents of the frontline zone. The fuel will be delivered to the houses of the most vulnerable categories of the population. Delivery of 4 tons of coal or briquettes for each HH will be carried out exclusively within the 5 km LoC, on both sides of the conflict. Average cost coal per HH is $6102. Providing winterization cash grant transfers (only GCA)• PIN will provide conditional winterization cash grant. This intervention will enable beneficiaries to meet their basic winterization needs by purchasing solid fuel. Average grant size is $300• PIN will provide support with house insulation, prioritizing insulation of the ceiling and walls for homes in order to ensure winterization for conflict-affected households; the surface of houses qualified for the programme should range between 40 and 80 m2.Outcome 2. Provided adequate transitional solutions [monetised or in-kind] related to shelter and NFI needs meeting minimal international and national shelter standards1. Implementation light and medium repairs (GCA and NGCA)• Materials and labor for light and medium scale repairs on war damaged private homes and multistory buildings, mainly consisting of roof patching, installation of high quality plastic/PVC windows with frames, full rehabilitation of roofs, light wall repairs, doors, and/or some light interior work. Average value of light repairs set at $500, and $1500 for medium repairs. Outcome 3. Provided/upgraded permanent shelter solutions for the most vulnerable conflict affected population1. Implementing structural repairs ("heavy repairs") (GCA and NGCA)• Materials and labor for heavy scale repairs and reconstructions (using existing foundations) of private homes consisting of rehabilitation of exterior/interior walls, roofs, windows, flooring, electricity, plumbing, etc. Average value of repairs set at $4000.2. Implementing full reconstruction repairs (GCA and NGCA)• Partial reconstruction of one or several walls. Full concrete ring beam and appropriated retrofitting for the structure. Reconstruction on existing foundations of a NEW full structurally sound small house. Average 10 to 12 m2 per person (gross surface). Insulated. Basic furniture (bed). Heating system. Sanitation. Average value of repairs set at $10000.
