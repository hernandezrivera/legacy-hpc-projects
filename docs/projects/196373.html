Project Summary

This project presents an innovative approach aimed at increasing the resilience of host communities and refugees, which 1) increases the adaptive capacity of host communities and refugees by supporting the adoption of climate-resilient livelihood solutions and the by strengthening local market systems, 2) enhances the absorptive capacity of host communities and refuges by providing cash support and in-creasing awareness of the target groups and 3) fosters the transformative capacity of the target groups through community restoration and awareness raising activities. The objective of the project is to increase resilience of the host communities and Rohingya refugees, in Cox’s Bazar, by improving food security, reducing disaster vulnerability and increas-ing social cohesion.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Helvetas and Concern (Alliance 2015 partner organisation) surveyed over 600 households of refugees and host communities in the district. The decreasing trend of poverty incidence before the pandemic is expected to be temporarily reversed because the pandemic threatens the liveli-hood of many people. 43% of those interviewed said they were eating less now, when compared to the period before COVID-19. 51% stated that COVID had negatively impacted the financial situation of the household, with virtually all respondents (94.5%) saying their ability to earn an income had been affected by COVID-19. When data is disaggregated by gender it is possible to verify a gender imbalanced impact. Female respondents were more likely to say their household was eating less than male respondents were (48.1% compared to 38.2%). There was an even more striking difference between men and women in terms of their response on quality of the food ingested with 60.6% of women saying it had gotten worse compared to 41.5% of men. Those living in the camps were also more likely to give a negative response on quantity and quality than those living in the rural (host) communities. The emotional strain of COVID-19 is affecting everybody. 2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project has been designed coordinating with the relevant sectors and working groups and other key stakeholders to ensure as little gaps and overlaps as possible. This coordination will be constant to identify gaps and adaption strategies throughout the implementation of the project, especially during the selection of the beneficiaries. The project specifically aligns with principle #3 of rationalisation as it integrates skills development with the creation of livelihood opportunities that increase food security and nutrition, gender considerations and DRR and environmental protection – offering a package of activities that mutually reinforce each other. Cost-effectiveness has been considered as much as possible by building on existing initiatives, capacity and existing infrastructure as well as building a system of proper planning and monitoring of the project implementation. Moreover, the project aligns with principle #4 as it is designed and already partially funded for 3 years. The past experience has shown that increasing self-reliance and ownership requires a continued project over multiple years. The activities in this project are designed to built on each other to ensure sustainability of the intervention.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

HELVETAS will ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders receive information on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) along with complaints and feedback mechanisms to ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders can provide feedback or raise concerns or complaints on project implementation. All complaints will be treated confidentially by an independent M&E team and addressed in a prompt and appropriate manner, in order to close the feedback loop, and mitigate further damage related to potential risks. All staff members, contractors, volunteers, and any other associated individual will sign an organisational PSEA policy and will be held accountable to their obligations.

3.2 - Gender

A gender-sensitive approach will be applied during all phases of the project. HELVETAS mainstreams an age, gender, and diversity (AGD) approach throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes of the proposed project. To strengthen the assistance provided, HELVETAS will use the Inter-Agency Standing Committee’s (IASC) Gender with Age Marker (GAM). The project will engage a variety of different groups, notably women, PwD, youth and adolescents, to encourage widespread participation by the refugee community. To ensure an equal participation of women and girls, the project mainstreams awareness raising throughout all its trainings, offers trainings in safe spaces for women, encouraging women-led Sales and Collection centers. Furthermore, the LSP model has proven to strengthen the inclusion and empowerment of PwD and women as the LSPs are able to offer need-based mentoring support at the doorstep.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

HELVETAS mainstreams an age, gender, and diversity (AGD) approach throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes of the proposed project. HELVETAS will ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders receive information on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), including child protection, along with complaints and feedback mechanisms to ensure that persons of concern and stakeholders can provide feedback or raise concerns or complaints on project implementation. HELVETAS organisational Code of Conduct, PSEA policy, Regulations on Child Protection are binding for all employees worldwide and all collaborators need to commit to the key principles of the CoC. Moreover, HELVETAS has standardized recruitment process. Reference checks are carried out as a mandatory part of this process. During the design and implementation of the project, HELVETAS participatory process (see AAP) and complaints and feedback mechanism contribute to age and child safeguarding.

3.4 - Diversity

HELVETAS mainstreams an age, gender, and diversity (AGD) approach throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes of the proposed project. We collect data disaggregated by age, sex and disability for both analysis and programming purposes. As a result, operations are able to account for the differing needs of persons of concern with respect to their age, sex and disability. The proposed project will engage a variety of different groups, notably women, PwD, faith leaders, caregivers, parents, youth and adolescent from both Rohingya and Host Community, encouraging widespread participation. Awareness and mutual understanding will be raised through joint activities, the improvement of the shared environment and soft skills development.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

To ensure AAP, HELVETAS will put in place a robust system of participation and complaints and feedback mechanism throughout the planning, implementation, and reporting processes of the proposed project. The selection of beneficiaries will be done transparently in consultation with the community, with the relevant local authorities and CiCs and Site Management agencies in the camps. For the implementation of the project, HELVETAS will put in place a complaints and feedback mechanism for all stakeholders. All complaints will be treated confidentially by an independent M&E team and addressed in a prompt and appropriate manner, in order to close the feedback loop, and mitigate further damage related to potential risks. HELVETAS will use tools for assessment, baseline and end-line survey, post distribution monitoring, and monitoring visits to track progress against the activities and indicators, constantly incorporating beneficiary feedback in the monitoring and implementation process.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project will also seek to raise public awareness of Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) among host communities and refugees. The project will do so through local Disaster Management Committees (DMCs) on DRR. These committees will demonstrate and facilitate mock drills and fire drills for host communities and refugees. The DMCs will also raise awareness of DRR by developing contingency plans for host communities. The project will promote bamboo cultivation/plantation in unutilized land areas especially adjacent to disaster-prone areas. The bamboo plantation aims to tap the social and ecological potential of the area through a program involving 1) the Establishment of a bamboo nursery with a diverse collection of species 2) Community awareness and 3) Development of a community-based business.
