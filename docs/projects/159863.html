Project Summary

The situation of people affected by displacements and disasters remains precarious with respect to physical safety and dignity. As a result of destitution, women, men, girls and boys, are exposed to particular and various forms of
Violence, exploitation and discrimination.
Frequent protection risks reported on the ground include (i) GBV, (ii) forced evictions, (iii) exposure to explosive hazards and (iv) child rights violations, such as child recruitment by armed groups and early marriage. Displacement increases the risk of family separation, and currently, there is an estimated caseload of 8,000 unaccompanied or separated children in Somalia. Furthermore, due to the loss of men’s livelihoods, displaced women are increasingly taking on the role of breadwinners, which exposes them to the increased risk of domestic violence, exploitation and abuse.(See HRP 2019)
This project will focus on GBV response in humanitarian contexts. The main focus will be on enhancing service provision in line with SOPs and GBV guiding principles.
IRC will provide Clinical management of
Rape (CMR), case management (CM); psychosocial support services (PSS) including psychosocial
counselling, psychosocial first aid (PFA), social networking and access to information on available GBV
services, through women and girls friendly spaces/safe spaces, and establishment and strengthening of
GBV referral pathways (to enable access to comprehensive GBV services including legal, medical and
Livelihood services) in target communities, including IDP communities in the target locations. The IRC
will also strengthen GBV risk reduction through strengthening and engaging community protection
groups, distribution of dignity kits and solar lanterns to vulnerable women and girls, provide
capacity building and trainings to community volunteers/workers and GBV service providers to enhance
Service provision and risk reduction for women and girls, and conduct safety audits for GBV risk assessments to inform the project implementation and decisions, among other interventions.

Project Activities

The following activities will be carried out • Trained PFA will carry out GBV awareness-raising and outreach in the community on consequences and causes of GBV throughout the project period in all the target locations. • IRC GBV nurses will provide clinical care for sexual assault survivors in IRC supported facilities, and trained IRC Case workers will provide Case management to GBV survivors at the IRC supported safe spaces in the target locations, throughtout the project period.• IRC wil provide material assistance (dignity kits; solar lanterns) to vulnerable women and girls in the target locations in the second quarter of the project• Safe space activities including skills-building (tailoring, heena application, weaving, cooking and tie &dye) as well as literacy and numeracy will conducted by designated facilitators and IRC staff for women and girls at the safe spaces, and IRC response officers will conduct quarterly listening sessions with beneficiaries (W&G) in safe spaces in all target locations throughout the project.• Psychosocial support for GBV survivors including counseling and social networking will be provided at the safe spaces by case workers, and referral pathway development/ updating and dissemination conducted semi annually by IRC in coordination with UNFPA/UNHCR in all the target locations. • Safety audits to be conducted by IRC staff on a quarterly basis in all the project locations .The IRC will continue to provide life-saving GBV response services including clinical care for sexual assault survivors, case management, GBV screening, and PSS services through the safe spaces for women and girls. The IRC will also support GBV awareness-raising and outreach in the community on consequences and causes of GBV through the community and religious leaders’ engagement as well as carry out GBV messaging and awareness on available services through the psychosocial first aid volunteers (PFAs) who conduct community outreach and door to door campaign activities. This will be done in both host and IDP communities in the target locations. The IRC will provide regular, structured PSS and skill-building activities, based on participants’ interests. Beneficiaries for skills-building activities like tailoring, henna application, weaving, cooking and tie &dye as well as literacy and numeracy all conducted by designated facilitators and IRC staff will be conducting listening sessions with beneficiaries (W&G). The safe space works to increase protection for survivors, ensures confidentiality and builds strong social networks for beneficiaries, which increases protection. All activities will be conducted in all our project locations.

Mitigation Measures

• Members of marginalized community’s/ minority groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance-Establish a selecting criteria targeting beneficiaries from all different groups including minority groups and marginalized groups-Establish a feedback mechanism that will allow everyone’s voice to be heard. • Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processes- Mobilize and sensitize community members including women, girls and boys on the agreed selection criteria to raise the level of information awareness about the project-Consult with women, girls and boys when conducting FGDs on services available at target locations • Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance.-Ensure that women and girls are aware of services provided by humanitarian actors are free of charge -Ensure the Do No Harm approach that requires humanitarian actors to endeavor not to cause further damage and suffering as a result of their actions is applied while providing services.-Ensure that services provided are in line to SOPs and GBV Guiding principles - Ensure women and girls are well informed on risk of sexual exploitation when humanitarian assistance is provided.-Ensure all the staff working on this project ae aware of and trained on SEA and they sign the code.• Barriers for beneficiaries to report concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms- Establish complaint feedback mechanism and train beneficiaries on the reporting mechanisms-Ensure staff has gender representation when engaging with different groups of beneficiaries and also trained on protection mainstreaming -Ensure to exploit other feedback mechanisms like sex disaggregated listening sessions where beneficiaries can give feedback without fear.
