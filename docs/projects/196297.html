Project Summary

With an estimated over 2 million out of school children (OOSC), the response will focus on accessibility and availability of relevant education opportunities for learners. Transitioning to secondary school remains a challenge, only six out of 14 governorates had <10% of children attending secondary school. Expansion of relevant non- formal education (NFE) and support to the formal system to retain current students and absorb additional learners is needed across all learning levels. This includes expanding/rehabilitating/establishing classrooms and/or temporary learning spaces that benefit from gender and disability sensitive WASH facilities, adequate heating/cooling environments for learners.
The intervention aims to fill the gaps in education in northwest Syria. School-age children dropping out of schools, as 28% of school-age children have never entered schools due to lack of schools, educational equipment, and the inability of displaced people and vulnerable families to bear Education costs, girls, women and children are at risk due to the lack of protection means in school buildings, as well as children with disabilities suffer from access to education services, as most schools are not equipped with tools and aids for people with disabilities. The formal system is unable to absorb and retain all school-age children. Many students continue to learn in unfavorable and/or unsafe learning environments. The project will target 50,000 students and teachers (49,000 students ages 5-17 years (24,990 girls and 24,010 boys & 1,000 teachers) The purpose of the project is to increase school enrolment rates, maintain the retention of children in schools, provide them with the means to ensure the continuity of their education, improve the quality of education, and strengthen the educational system through the implementation of the following activities:
1. provision incentives to teachers and education personnel (male/female)
2. provision of school supplies to children (5-17 years, girls/ boys), School/ teachers (female/male)
3. provision personal protective equipment to (5-17 years, girls/boys, female/male)
4. provision teaching materials to teachers/facilitators (male/female)
5. provision school maintenance and repair, building gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH facilities.
6. provision solar power systems.
7. provide school feeding materials for students (5-17 years, girls/boys).
8. provision of heating materials to targeted schools
9. Hygiene awareness
10. GBV training for school administration teams and teachers
The activities will target areas where there are large gaps, according to the cluster reports.
To facilitate access to education services for people with disabilities, the following activities will be implemented:
- Building latrines for people with disabilities, and rehabilitating the demolished ones
- Building ramps at school entrances.
- Installing armrests on stairs to help people with disabilities
- Providing Assistance tools for people with disabilities (glasses, wheelchairs, crutches, hearing aids, etc.)
The monitoring and evaluation team will participate in the design of the project and develop a comprehensive monitoring and evaluation plan for all activities, and ensure that the goals and indicators are achieved, through the use of monitoring and evaluation tools (questionnaires, interviews, KII, Feedback, field visits, reports.)

3 - Needs

According to HNO 2023, There has been a four percent increase in the sector PIN, with 6.9M122 children and education personnel needing emergency education services. At the national level, the average of self-reported attendance123 is 88 percent and an estimated 12 percent of children do not attend school.124 Additional data sources estimate that there are over two million children (6-17) are out of school.125 International assistance is on a declining trend126, while at the same time local resources, where available, have significantly reduced. The lack of non-formal services will continue to limit the number of OOSCs who can return to learning and the formal system will continue to be unable to absorb and retain all the children who should be in school, undermining viable pathways to formal education. The education system is unable to absorb and retain all school-age children. Many students continue to learn in unfavorable and/or unsafe learning environments. Most schools have classrooms designed to accommodate 30 students, 20% of schools are overcrowded (more than 50 students in a class) and 42% of schools have medium overcrowding (30-40 students per class). As for feeding programs, education equipment, and stationery, 98% of the schools did not have meals available for students, and 64% of the schools did not distribute stationery to students. 23% of schools suffer from the lack of heaters and their accessories, and 90% of schools are not covered with fuel for heating

4 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented according to the following methodology:- Conducting a comprehensive needs assessment for the educational sector in northwestern Syria to identify the gaps and work areas in coordination with the Education Cluster.- Coordination with local authorities and organizations operating in the area to avoid repeating beneficiaries.- Determining the needs and launching the purchase operations in accordance with the procurement policy of Islamic Relief and in accordance with the policy of the donor.- Choosing the target schools in coordination with the education directorates and local authorities.- Carrying out recruitment operations in coordination and partnership with the directorates of education- Monitoring and evaluating all activities since project design to close the project. - Submitting periodic reports to donors of the education cluster and documenting all activities in accordance with the documentation policy approved by Islamic Relief and agreed upon with the donor.

Sign the Code of Conduct for Protection by School and International Relations Officers Resources Required: Qualified trainer, Communications, Awareness posters, Referral aids, Code of Conduct, Acceptable.2- The inability of boys and girls to reach schools because of customs and traditions. Probability: MediumMitigating measure: awareness sessions for the local community will be provided. Required resources: Qualified trainer, awareness posters.3- Inability of people with disabilities to access education.Probability: Medium Mitigating measure: Building ramps at school entrances, providing aids (glasses, headphones, crutches, etc.) Psychological support for integration with healthy students. Required resources: Budget, specialist awareness, case management, and referral. 4- Lack of privacy in schools by Boys and girlsProbability: MediumMitigating measure: building separate toilets according to gender, segregation between males and females, and awareness sessions. Resources required: budget, qualified trainer, awareness posters, case management, and referral.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IR guarantees protection against PSEA risks in the education sector through:Beneficiaries (boys, girls, males, females) are at risk of harassment, exploitation, or abuse from project staff, teachers, school management, local authorities, or students among themselves. Mitigating Measures:- Staff sign the Code of Conduct, conduct PSEA training for staff, educate beneficiaries, and share means of communication and reporting.- School management and teachers sign the Code of Conduct, and conduct PSEA risk training.- Emphasis on protection issues from PSEA in all MoU signed with the Directorate of Education.- Conducting awareness-raising for the beneficiaries, sharing awareness posters in the places where the project activities are implemented.- Sharing the means of communication and reporting PSEA cases (phone number, WhatsApp, Skype, complaint box, etc.), while ensuring confidentiality and efficient case management. An employee who is a focal point in each school is responsible for following up on PSEA and GBV cases and referring them to the case management centers.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risks, assumptions and mitigation measures are listed below: 1. Spread of COVID-19 among students, schools staff, and field teams. IR staff will take the following measures, organize an awareness session, fix awareness posters at schools, use hand sanitizer, and face masks.2. No access to the project location. In coordination with all relevant stakeholders, IR can change the project location if access to the planned location becomes impossible. 3. Closure of the Turkish border will hinder the access of some material and staff, IR will look for another alternative to get the material from the Syrian market or coordinate with the Turkish authority to deliver the items through the humanitarian border. 4. Interference of armed groups in the project areas ( In order to ensure that armed groups do not get involved in programming, IR will engage with relevant stakeholders and make sure that the local councils and local authorities are aware of the projects and have provided adequate access and space to conduct activities.) 5. Risk of children dropping out of formal education ( Identify specific criteria to all implementing partners for accepting children into the program; meeting with parents to encourage them to send their children to school.)6. Spread of Cholera: IR team will conduct awareness sessions and share WHO guidelines. In case of any positive case, IR will coordinate within and refer the cases to health centres that deal with the cholera infections.
