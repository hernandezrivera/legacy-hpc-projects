Project Summary

Charity Mission Corps (CMC) aims at providing life-saving emergency static Food Security and Livelihood to the most vulnerable populations, targeting the internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees in Pibor, Likuangole, Gumuruk, and Budi counties in the former Jonglei through improvement of access to timely, equitable livelihood to the population affected by armed conﬂict, insecurities, climate change and displacement. This project strives to support the existing coping mechanisms of the local community and building resilience towards other shocks due to disasters related vulnerability. CMC’s intervention will be part of an integrated response with other actors, where CMC will provide FSL, and other partners will provide health, WASH and nutrition to support and rebuild digniﬁed lives in the face of displacement and need for safety. CMC will provide FSL support to the most vulnerable groups in order to alleviate human suffering in a safe and digniﬁed manner, targeting 20,000 beneﬁciaries (Men, Women, Boys and Girls) displaced due to the ongoing conﬂict and for returnees to the areas focusing on female headed households, unaccompanied elderly, people with no links to the community, the separated children, women at risk (pregnant and lactating mothers), child-headed households, people living with disability and returnees whose properties were damaged and looted during the conﬂict. CMC will be focusing on; 1. Providing vegetable kits in areas where communities can access suﬃcient water for vegetable growing, beneﬁciaries will be provided with vegetable kits followed by extension trainings on good agronomical practices including; season planting, seedbed preparation, seed planting and spacing, weed management, irrigation, integrated pest and production management, harvesting and post-harvesting management. Each kit will contain a variety of vegetable seeds that grow well in the area 2. Providing ﬁshing kits to the beneﬁciaries: Intervening at the household level to meet the emergency food security needs through provision of ﬁshing NFIs. Fishing has a potential to immediately address the food security needs of the targeted vulnerable households through provision of productive assets (ﬁshing equipment) to enable the people to immediately harvest ﬁsh from the rivers and swamps in the areas. The ﬁsh so harvested will immediately provide food (protein) and income to the household for use in accessing other basic commodities. 3. Providing cereal seed kits: This is to ensure the target communities recover from hunger that occurred partially because of conﬂict, insecurities and displacement, cereal seed kits and tools accompanied by agricultural extension activities, focusing on good agronomical practices including; season of planting, seedbed preparation, seed planting and spacing, weed management, irrigation, integrated pest and production management, harvesting and post-harvest management will be provided to vulnerable households. This will enable households to produce their own food and enhance household resilience to food security shocks. This intervention will be delivered using lead farmers and farmer ﬁeld school approaches with demonstration plots used for community trainings on the above topics 4. Training of Farmer Field Schools (LFFS): In order to improve sustainability of the project, lead farmers and extension workers will be trained as trainers for community farmers. The lead farmers and extension workers will then facilitate discussions and demonstration on good agronomic practices for the vegetable and cereal crops producer groups. This will strengthen semi-autonomous farmer-to-farmer advisory systems allowing independence and sustainability and resulting in a local food market that is selfsuﬃcient and community orientated. 5. Setting up irrigation system; To ensure the success of vegetable production, irrigation systems and adequate training on their use will be provided.

1 - Needs

The Protracted conflict in South Sudan has caused erosion of physical and social infrastructure, death and displacement of hundreds of thousands of people in former Eastern Equatoria State. The revitalized peace agreement signed did not hold hope in 2016 as conflicts then echoed to tribal conflicts, cattle raiding, and several clashing with the armed groups including SPLA-IO, NAS and other unorganized forces in Eastern Equatoria at least 30,000 to 45,000 people displaced. Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria State are among the most affected with recurrent attacks often causing more displacements, death and destruction of infrastructure. The newly signed peace in 2019 created little hopes as population begun to return in some of the areas Pibor and Budin search of resiliencies and rebuilding livelihood through agriculture. The national financial crises have worsened the situation. According to the World Bank, over half (51%) of the 12.3 million South Sudanese live below the national consumption poverty line attributing to the situation continuing to worsen resulting from more people displaced, livelihoods destroyed and more fighting being reported. Renewed inter-tribal fighting, cattle raiding, and political frictions as a results of the boundaries conflicts in the mentioned counties early June 2019 displaced an estimated at least 15,723 people in Magwi and population returns in Torit according to IOM-DTM report. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) ‘Key IPC Findings: January-July 2019’ and September-October 2019’ report highlighted Pibor, Jonglei and Budi counties in Eastern Equatoria state as one of the most food insecure crises affected counties in South Sudan with IPC3 and possible increment to IPC4 by March 2020. Additionally, the October 2018 Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) survey indicated the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) was 9.9% and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) at 9%.23, Cattle raids and tribal conflicts, which previous REACH studies have linked to food insecurity, have become progressively more frequent, far ranging, and violent letting to luck of proper sheltering and NFIs among the displaced population in the counties. In order to further understand the worsening food insecurity, cattle raiding, tribal conflicts and the linkages between cattle migrations and deteriorating humanitarian conditions including the luck of FSL, IRNA assessments in Pibor, and Budi Payam between May and August 2019 indicates population returning in hope of the revitalized peace agreement between the warren parties in South Sudan. The report indicates displaced population totally with no access or less access to Food security and livelihood services. The report indicated that, nearly 16,000 people were staying with host communities in Pibor and Budi; while more than 3,500 people returning home in hope of the Signed peace agreement. Inter-agencies assessments indicates IDPs and returnees are in dare needs for , FSL, S/NFIs, Health,Nutrition, WASH and few to mention. Lack of these services are likely to increase the mortality and morbidity of the disease burden and human sufferings among the displaced population (IDPs). The displaced population are reported to have loss most of their belongings including food and non-food items, further increasing hunger and the risk of malnutrition among children under five years, pregnant and lactating women. According to survey conducted by CMC in early 2019 to the recent peak displacement, the GAM rate in counties is at 21.6% with a very low immunization coverage of below 50%. The purpose of this project is to increase access to FSL services to the internally displaced persons and the Host Communities in Eastern Equatoria state

2 - Activities

1. Conducting detailed FSL needs assessments for the in-kind Fishing and Digging kit, and Market assessment for the CBI with focus on risk level, vulnerability and protection threats using mobile data collection method. Every intervention will be preceded with a detailed needs assessment conducted by the FSL response team. Protection Officer will be part of the assessment team and will capture information linked to safety and dignity of the beneficiaries, and prioritize the assessed needs. Mobile data collection tool or paper tools will be used, depending on the context. All data collection tools will be used such as household interviews, focus group discussions, and key informant interviews will be used to collect data. 2. Conducting verification and registration of target beneficiaries for the FSL kits and CBI with support from CMC’s protection team and focus on community copping mechanisms. Verification of needs and numbers of beneficiaries will be conducted with support of CMC’s protection team, local community and local enumerators will support the process. This activity is only recommended where needs have been identified and clear targeting criteria have been agreed on with the community. This activity proceeds a needs assessment. Verification report is shared with SFPs and cluster coordination team and approval to proceed is given. CMC’s protection team will conduct community awareness and sensitization sessions on reported protection risks, verifying vulnerable persons with specific needs (people with disability, elderly, unaccompanied minors). The protection staff do facilitation of referrals with during FSL intervention cycle. 3. Preparing for distribution through submitting the core pipeline request and pre-positioning of the items to intervention location by pipeline and logs cluster. This activity is only approved once a verification and registration report is approved by SFP and cluster coordination team. A pipeline request is prepared and sent to the SFP and cluster coordination team with an attachment of the assessment and registration reports. This is done for every intervention. Pre-positioning is done by delivery of the supplies to the location before mobilization of registered beneficiaries. 4. Conducting trainings and capacity building of the beneficiaries; CMC project shall carryout the training of different groups within the beneficiaries including; Reliefs Distribution Committees (RDCs) the Local Shelter Experts (LSEs), the Volunteers, the AAP focal points, the community participatory monitoring and evaluation team (CPM&ET) to be part of resilience and sustainability since the group remains within the beneficiaries. CMC will ensure Protection and hygiene promotion packages are mainstreamed in the training with coordination from the other actors. 5. Conducting distribution of the Fishing and digging materials to only targeted most vulnerable affected beneficiaries with support of protection team Distribution is organized in a safe and accessible locations accepted by all beneficiaries. Distribution duration is determined by the caseload of the response. This is done in all interventions under this project. Distribution will have designate start and closing times, which is agreed upon by the beneficiaries. This will be conducted across all interventions. The protection staff will do facilitation of referrals during FSL intervention cycle. The FSL ERT will conduct demonstration on the proper and sustainable use of the FSL kits; they will also conduct sensitization environmental issues related to shelter materials collection and energy 5. Conducting rapid monitoring during interventions and Post Distribution Monitoring 30 days after distribution when applicable. Rapid monitoring will be conducted during all distributions by a MEAL officer this is to monitor the quality of the interventions and programming. PIM will be conducted three months after the intervention
