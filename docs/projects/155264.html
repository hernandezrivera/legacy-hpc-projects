Needs

Since 2014, Haiti has been regularly hit by the "El Niño" phenomenon causing repeated episodes of drought accompanied by a poor distribution of rain. According to the latest IPC Analysis (CNSA, October 2018) the situation does not seem to be improving as a prolonged drought hit again several communes of Haiti impacting drastically on crops and pastures during the spring 2018 agricultural season. As a result, 852,149 people in the departments of Grande Anse (197,358 people), South (170,705), West (14,415), North (319,256) and Northeast (150,415) are in acute food insecurity. In addition, the weather outlook indicates a strong likelihood of strengthening the "El Niño" phenomenon for the next year.Domestic animals have been greatly affected by the lack of fodder and water, causing a decrease in the sales (especially of small livestock), which is the most important safety net for the vulnerable households in the affected communes. The objective of this project is to assist the households to to re-establish their small livestock (especially poultry and goats), improve care for sick animals, and to initiate forage production and conservation.The approach that will be used is: (1) distribute goats or chickens to the affected households; (2) organize mobile clinics for veterinary care of sick animals and (3) provide planting material and technical support to beneficiary to improve forage production and conservation.

Activities or outputs

1. Repopulation of small livestock• Installation of henhouses for 900 vulnerable households.• Distribution of four poultry (3 chickens and one coq) for 900 vulnerable households.• Distribution of two goats for 1,200 vulnerable households. • Distribution of male goats for breeding stations managed by farmer’s organizations.2. Re-establishing fodder plants• Distribution of planting material for 3,000 vulnerable households.• Training beneficiaries on integrated fodder and crop production.3. Mobile clinics• Organization of 5 mobile veterinary clinics• Animal care treatment for 6,000 animals belonging to at least 2,000 breeder’s households.
