Project Summary

The socio-political crisis in the Northwest and Southwest regions has led to a massive population displacement. According to Need Assessment (MSNA) February 2023, round 8 the conflict has forced approximatively 1 million persons to be displaced in the Northwest and southwest regions. The NWSW also count 569,311 IDPs 442,580 returnees and Cameroonians refugee returnees. HNO reports for May 2023 some 607,000 people in need of protection in the NWSW. Civilian population continues to face severe human rights violations, unlawful arrests and detention, destruction and extortion of property, threats to life, physical assaults, sexual assaults, rape and child abuse. From January to October 2023 some 5,277, priority protection incidents have been reported by Protection Monitoring actors INTERSOS, UNHCR, DRC and IRC. This number is an estimate given the geographical limitations of Protection Monitoring projects. 6 out of 13 divisions are covered by IRC and DRC. The North-West and South-West regions continued to be marked by high levels of insecurity in 2023 with armed conflict, criminal offences against civilian populations, beside the lockdowns imposed by NSAG, and frequent roadblocks. UNHCR identified 4 key priorities in 2023 and 2024 for intervention and to contribute to the protection response and play its role as last resort provider and the Protection Cluster lead: 1) strengthen the centrality of protection, 2) coordination of lead clusters, 3) emergency preparedness and response and 4) securing solutions. UNHCR will cover the Northwest, Southwest Regions, . Through a community-based approach, UNHCR intends to secure protection and provide solutions through reinforcing protection monitoring and empowering communities to contribute to their own protection, respond to protection incidents as much as possible and search for solutions, and ensure peaceful coexistence among their members. The intervention will respond to critical child protection and GBV cases including communities and women empowerment, legal assistance, support for civil documentation and psychosocial services. Cash-based interventions as a modality, and referrals to possible available services will be an integral part of the response. In kind support will be done where cash intervention is not feasible. Adopt an area-based approachwhich will consist of selecting specific areas especially those with limited or no humanitarian response in the regions affected by the displacement and work with all stakeholders in the priority locations to respond to the needs of the affected populations including the displaced, host community, IDPs and returnees (refugees’ returnees and IDPs returnees). UNHCR will work with local authorities, civil society, agencies present in the designated areas to increase protection outreach and support to the most vulnerable; mitigate tensions, empower local authorities; increase local coordination. This approach will contribute to build sustainability of key services and prevent displacement as well as guarantee a greater accountability to affected populations (AAP). UNHCR will ensure the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), and accountability to affected populations through the implementation of zero tolerance policy. UNHCR will conduct sensitization sessions in the targeted communities on their rights and reporting mechanisms, capacitate implementing partners whose PSEA assessment report proved to be not fully capacitated, and setting up various confidential reporting channels: a toll-free helpline in collaboration with WFP and protection desks during all protection activities will be ensured. UNHCR will targets 28 communities in 6 divisions 3 (Fako, Meme and Manyu) in the South West Region and 3 (Mezam, Momo and Menchum) in the North West Region, to reach an estimated target of reach of 607,000 persons with the following specific targets:
