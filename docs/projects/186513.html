Project Summary

GBVS will provide access to, and improve quality, resilience and sustainability of basic education and vocational skills for 5,500 conflict-affected school-aged girls and boys (including children with disabilities and special needs) aged 3 to 17 years (800 Returnees, 4,100 IDPs, and 600 host community), and enhance the capacity of 145 adults (58 female and 87 male) in Adamawa state.
Specifically:
1. 5,500 conflict-affected children and adolescents (including children with disabilities and special needs) in Adamawa State have access to inclusive quality basic education and vocational skills opportunities within a safe learning environment.
2. 5,500 conflict-affected children and adolescents (including children with disabilities and special needs) in Adamawa State receive quality and conflict sensitive educational services to enhance their learning achievements with resilience.
3. Government Ministries, Departments and Agencies (MDAs) and Communities in GOMBI AND MAIHA LGA have increased capacity to participate in school development and risk reduction planning and demand equitable access to conflict sensitive quality education for target children and adolescents.
Through this project, GBVS will provide educational access to 5,500 conflict affected children of age 3-17 years in Gombi and Maiha who are unable to access the formal school system, or are not sufficiently supported to maintain the desired level in the formal school system due to school closure. Under specific community education needs, GBVS will increase access by creating and/or strengthening safe learning environments to support sustainable and resilient basic education. GBVS will prioritize the rehabilitation of classrooms, establishment of temporary learning spaces (TLSs) and alternative education / bridging classes and improved pathways to return to formal education as a durable solution to transition from emergency to recovery. This way, the project targets children both in and out of the regular school system.
Project will also support School Return Campaigns using TfD towards boosting enrolment. GBVS will thus provide non-formal basic literacy and numeracy, vocational education, and psycho-social support (PSS) for IDP, host community and returnee children in the GOMBI AND MAIHA LGA of Borno State. Project will deliver lifesaving basic education services to target these distressed children who have either stopped schooling due to displacement or never attended school in their young lives. Ongoing professional development/mentorship will be provided towards boosting the capacity of145 teachers and school-based management committee (SBMC) members with requisite pedagogical, managerial, psycho-social (PSS) and protection-sensitive skills as a means to enhance the quality of education/skills and support receivable by the target children.
PSS will relieve distress and contribute to restoring normalcy for the children. Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) training will enhance the safety of all beneficiaries. The project promotes gender equality through ensuring that the majority of children benefitting from the project are girls (58%) and dignity kits for girls. Also, WaSH in school component shall be incorporated into the project, with the elements of sanitation (waste management/construction and maintenance of latrines), handwashing stations/hygiene promotion having key messages on personal hygiene and covid-19, and water facilities provided for school use. This will contribute to reduction in hygiene-related and water-borne diseases among children and curtail the spread of Covid-19. Life skills education will reduce the likelihoods of recruitment into armed groups, and vocational training will reduce idleness among other associated tendencies. Thus, the project overall, creates pathways for affected children to join the mainstream formal school system and become more informed/skilled participants in society.

1 - Needs

According to the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), 7.7 million people in Northeast Nigeria (2.5 million girls, 2.2 million boys, 1.7 million women and 1.5 million men) need humanitarian assistance due to the complex violent Boko Haram insurgency entering its eleventh year. Specifically, 1.9 million people are internally displaced, mostly in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) States, and new displacement continues. About 57 per cent of those affected are children, including many unaccompanied minors or children orphaned by the crisis. According to 2020 EiEWG HNO, more than 1.2 million children need urgent education services, a decrease from 2.2 million from 2019. Still, institutional and human capacities are overstretched to respond to the educational needs of children and youth in Northeast Nigeria, forcing the Sector to target only 1,000,000 people in need. More than 19,000 teachers are displaced, and more than 645 teachers have been killed since 2009. Approximately 1,200 schools have been damaged or destroyed or are used for military purposes. In the Northeast, education facilities are often explicitly targeted by the non-state armed group Boko Haram. The UN Secretary General’s S/2017/304 report pursuant to Security Council Resolution 1612 indicates that educationally disadvantaged children in Nigeria’s northeast have become ready recruits for insurgents. According to Human Rights Watch, in some cases, students recruited by non-state armed groups attacked their own school and killed their own classmates and teachers. This worsens the out-of-school children catastrophe that troubles Nigeria. According to UNICEF, in Nigeria, about 10.5 million children of school age are out-of-school. Of these, more than 5.2 million are in the Northeast of whom more than 1.4 million children are in Adamawa state (71% of these are girls, the highest rate in the country). The 2015 USAID-funded Nigeria Education Data Survey (NEDS) classifies more than 75% of parents in the north-east region as illiterate. Children of such parents are at risk since these parents are less able to support their learning, hence the strong need for school-based management committees (SBMCs) to provide support. GBVS rapid assessment conducted in January 2021 identified gaps across informal camps in Gombi and Maiha This found a huge gap in Gombi and Maiha , part of the 32% of children that have access to no kind of education, hence require urgent intervention. There have been reports of an increase in violent crime and drug abuse, all these serving as coping strategies for survival with increase in out of school and children without opportunity to attend school. Teachers need to be supported with psychosocial services in order to cope with the crisis’ impact and deliver quality, learner-centred education. There is need therefore to address these conditions to promote quality, safe inclusive education for boys and girls in IDP Camps and members of host communities. As GBVS implements the present project, the localization priorities of the Sector are furthered as more learning takes place under the guidance of the Sector, and Adamawa EiEWG sub-sector.

2 - Activities

The project shall provide 5,500 conflict-affected boys and girls (3-17 years) accessing basic education and vocational skills, with psychosocial and protection components. This is important towards ensuring that these children, who have been out of school or never attended school, are able to access, integrate, maintain or reintegrate into the formal school system within their State or Local Government Area (LGA) together with government recognition of alternative education studies. Given considerations of context, girls and boys shall receive alternative basic education models (Integrated Qur’anic Education and accelerated learning programmes etc.), while other conflict-affected out-of-school boys and girls (3-17 years) are provided access to education through the learning center/school. Alternative basic education fits in with the culture of the majority of the population of Gombi, many of whom are reluctant to enroll in the main school system. The project shall further construct/rehabilitate or equip temporary learning spaces/classrooms, depending on the specific realities of each given community. Part of the devastation that has been suffered in the region from the Boko Haram insurgency is the widespread destruction of schools, hence the need to rehabilitate facilities in some places. In other places, particularly in camps and Quránic schools, facilities are typically either non-existent or of extremely poor standards, hence the plan to construct new classrooms. All 5,500 girls and boys shall receive early childhood development for emergencies (ECD) kits and other learning supplies throughout the project period. This activity shall involve the procurement and distribution of these materials that shall include toys, games, books, writing materials, crayons and other art-making materials, et cetera. Activity 1.1.1: Community mobilization and sensitization of IDPs as well as host communities to discuss project and have consensus on beneficiary targeting including locations for provision ofWASH facilities and classrooms/ TLS’ to be constructed/rehabilitated etc. Activity 1.1.2: Back to school campaigns conducted across target community Activity 1.1.3: Identification and registration of children to be enrolled Activity 1.1.4: Enrollment of registered children in schools in Gombi and Maiha Activity 2.1.1: Identification of 35 SBMC members and 110 Teachers to be trained on school management, PSS and Life skills, including crosscutting issues Activity 2.1.2: Training of 110 Teachers on emergency accelerated teaching and learning, PSS and Life skills, including crosscutting issues such as Covid-19 (basics on mode of transmission, prevention and control), school safety, etc Activity 2.1.3: Training of 35 SBMC members on school management, PSS and Life skills, including crosscuttingissues such as Covid-19 (basics on mode of transmission, prevention and control), child protection, school safety, etc Activity 2.1.4: Child-to-child hygiene promotion campaign Activity 3.1.1: Provide vocational training sessions to selected and organized cohorts of males and females on environmental friendly local stoves, Brick making and brick laying; sack-farming, tiling, tailoring, and crafts and knitting soap making Activity 1.3.1: Procurement and transportation of assorted school supplies (books, pens/ pencils and erasers; white boards; packs of chalk/markers; and sporting equipment eg., balls, volley ball nets etc) to project locations Activity 1.3.2: Distribution of assorted school supplies (books, pens/ pencils and erasers; white boards; packs of chalk/markers; and sporting equipment eg., balls, volley ball nets etc) to project locations Activity 1.2.1: Rehabilitation of 10 classrooms in Gombi and Maiha and construction of 10 TLS in camps to increase access to education for affected children Activity 1.2.2: Provide WaSH facilities including latrines, handwashing stations, and solar borehole in Gombi and Maiha

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GBVS PSEA manual will be fully implemented, following UN and inter-agency monitoring group advisories. The project activities are designed such that nothing is to the detriment of girls', boys', or other beneficiaries' physical or mental health, education, moral or social-emotional development. Gibran Society shall ensure that mechanisms are put in place to prevent situations of manipulation, misuse, abuse, victimization, oppression or ill-treatment. The project recognizes that sexual exploitation and abuse is the abuse of a position of vulnerability, differential power, or trust for sexual purposes; this includes profiting monetarily, socially or politically from the exploitation of another as well as personal sexual gratification. Gibran Society PSEA manual clearly provides measures for guarding against these occurrences and actions to adopt in the event of occurrence, following UN guidelines. Beneficiaries shall be involved in key decisions pertaining the project, from needs within their immediate context and throughout implementation and monitoring. A Women's Beneficiaries Committee shall be constituted to work closely with the Gibran Society Gender Focal Officer on the project. Project personnel including volunteers, teachers, and SBMC shall receive training on protection with emphasis on GBV, PSEA, and UXO. Proper feedback mechanisms shall be established to ensure that beneficiaries are able to make complaints in a proper fashion and such that speedy action can be taken to address any arising protection issues. Beneficiaries shall be properly sensitized on their rights under the project during project inception.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

The project is anchored upon the needs expressed by the EiEWG in the 2021 HNO, which set the total needs as well as targets for the BAY states, each of the target states, as well as LGAs. For instance, the EiWG HRP dashboard as at October 2020 (Education | HumanitarianResponse) highlights extent of reach of educational interventions during the yearand existing gaps. In addition, Gibran Society has conducted rapid assessment of the situation in Gombi, Maiha and Mubi North LGAs in November 2021 and has identified critical needs in educational intervention. Community leaders spoken to during the assessment have expressed serious concerns about the growing number of out of school children, especially girls. The final report on this survey is forthcoming in Gibran Society's at the end of December. Overall, Gombi and Maiha LGAs of Adamawa State need urgent intervention in basic education and alternative basic education.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Project feedback system will be designed with participation of beneficiaries. Likewise, feedback systems shall be incorporated into project monitoring to ensure compliance and effectiveness. Gibran Society shall ensure that that all relevant teams are involved and are clear about their roles in managing the feedback system. Community engagement and accountability or monitoring and evaluation focal points will help provide technical advice and quality assurance for the feedback mechanism. Gibran Society brings to bear on the project the awareness that not everyone will be able to read a poster on a wall or will have access to a mobile phone to receive a SMS message. Hence, community meetings shall be incorporated into the feedback collection strategy, and beneficiaries encouraged to utilize the most preferable channels to them in giving feedback (such as in person to Gibran Society monitoring team, through parents or teachers, through women leaders, through religious leaders, through traditional leaders, through student members of SBMCs, et cetera). Confidentiality shall be ensured in matters of feedback.
