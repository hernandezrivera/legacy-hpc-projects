Project Summary

GC implements integrated protection services to prevent, mitigate, and respond to harm, exploitation, and abuse for the most vulnerable boys, girls, men and women in northern Syria in Idleb, Al Hasakeh, Ar-Raqqa, and Deir ez-Zor governorates. Under this program, we will manage 19 safe spaces and 23 mobile teams. The objective of the project is to enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond to protection needs through supporting the protective environment in Syria, by providing prevention and response services to vulnerable individuals and communities. We will prioritize newly displaced IDPs and support people within and outside camps.
GC will operate four types of safe spaces: Child Friendly Spaces (CFS) for children ages 4-14, Youth Empowerment Program (YEP) centers for youth ages 14-18, Women and Girl Safe Spaces (WGSS) for adolescent girls and adult women, and Community Centers (CC). Safe Spaces serve as the hub for GC prevention, empowerment, and response services. Activities include recreational and structured PSS, case management, individual or group counseling, non-formal education, life skills training, parenting courses, awareness sessions, and referrals as needed through referral pathways.
Mobile teams will support the static facilities by providing integrated protection services to those not able to access the safe spaces. Services will differ from static facilities only in that mobile teams will respond more in emergencies and not provide formal case management. Mobile teams will further support the centers by conducting needs assessments and protection audits in the communities, organizing household visits for children with disabilities, and conducting community outreach and awareness. Mobile teams are able to quickly respond to emergencies by providing Psychosocial First Aid (PFA) to newly displaced HHs and conducting rapid registration of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC).
GC will provide case management and individual PSS services to individuals that are particularly high risk and in need of specialized support, as identified by GC’s protection teams or referred by another organization. A case worker will be assigned to each identified case and will support the beneficiary through a process of identification, assessment, planning, implementation, monitoring and case closure (under ideal circumstances). While case workers may provide some aspects of services related to the case plan, their primary responsibility will be to ensure that the individual is able to access the agreed services and to support them to mitigate a specific protection risk(s). GC will provide case management for both CP and GBV cases. GC has previously supported cases of identification, documentation, tracing and referral for UASC; Family Tracing and Reunification (FTR); reintegration of children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG); parenting skills for caregivers where there are concerns of abuse or neglect; prevention of early/forced marriage; and safety planning for GBV survivors. Trained GC staff will provide cash for protection in cases of urgent need, where other services are not available and a specific protection risk has been identified.
GC will provide recreational activities such as art classes and informal educational sessions as well as more structured curriculum that combine games and group discussions that build trust among the children, provide coping mechanisms for trauma, and restore children’s sense of control and empowerment. Through these activities, GC will identify children, women, and other vulnerable individuals (e.g. elderly, people with disabilities) in need of specialized services or case management support. Participation in structured PSS may also be included as part of an individual’s case management plan.
For MEAL GC uses daily attendance, registration, satisfaction surveys, well-being pre/post tests, case management files, training pre/post tests.

4 - Needs

With the Syrian conflict in its ninth year, over 13 million people remain in need of humanitarian assistance. Child labor, including recruitment to armed groups, is an ever-growing concern, with 88% of communities reporting child labor as a concern. Child marriage is reported in 69% of assessed communities, with adolescent girls the most affect group. Psychosocial distress was reported in 42% of surveyed households (HNO 2020).Our largest concentration of protection services are in Dana sub-district of Idleb, which is severity level 6 and as of Sept 2019 over 700,000 people in need and 1 million across Harem District, not including the hundreds of thousands that have arrived since and continue to arrive with the ongoing changes in control in Idleb. The narrowing space for those fleeing violence is closing on Harem District and those arriving are largely vulnerable, women-headed households and those who were not able to leave before. With a lack of shelter available to host these new IDPs, protection risks will increase, such as for women lacking privacy and safety within shelters hosting multiple families, to increase tensions within and between families leading to increase child protection risks, and inadequate services such as for those with disabilities and the elderly. Youth face a risk of recruitment and lack of opportunities, with girls in particular facing high risk of early marriage. There is a lack of services targeting adolescents with any protection, education of livelihood services. Other hazardous child labor is rampant and getting worse. In NES, there are gaps in GBV case management and support for youth, especially in Deir-ez-Zor where we are expanding activities. New and informal camps in Ar-Raqqa and Al Hasakeh have a lack of protection services for previously displaced families and more recently those displaced during the Turkish operation in October 2019.

5 - Implementation of activities

GC/CHF has been implementing specialized and non-specialized protection services in northern Syria since early 2016. We are currently operational in Idleb, Ar-Raqqa, Al-Hasakeh, and Deir Ez-Zor governorates and will continue working in these locations throughout 2020. During 2020 our priority is to add services geographically in NES, include additional services for youth, and expand our case management capacity to support more beneficiaries.Child-Friendly Spaces, Youth Empowerment Centers, and Women & Girls Safe Spaces will implement prevention (i.e. awareness sessions, recreational activities, non-formal education (NFE), life skills) and response (cash for protection, structured PSS, referrals of survivors, individual/group counselling, case management) services. In NWS: 4 WGSS, 6 CFS, 2 YEP, 1 CC, 17 MT. In NES: 4 CFS, 4 WGSS, 6 MT. While our staff is experienced, we will provide refresher training as needed and for new staff. Mobile teams comprise Team Leader & 4 staff. Caseworkers are active in all static facilities and Team leaders are trained to provide light case management and referrals of sensitive cases to centers as needed. Focal points within our mobile teams are trained to implement cash for protection, using case management principles. We will expand services for youth, PwD and the elderly, through center-based and mobile activities, through added activities and increased access. Our protection mobile teams will be primarily responsible for emergency response, including supporting our role as Emergency Focal Point for Harem District. They will provide PFA, information about services, referrals, and distribute emergency items (i.e. sanitary pads, school kits, children's clothing). Adolescent girls are included through YEC and WGSS activities, targeting them through peer support groups, referrals to mentorship program, NFE courses, and targeted awareness sessions.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risks: For awareness raising: expectations of response/services in locations where services may be limited/non-existent Measures: Develop clear communication to manage expectations and receive feedback on the quality and usefulness of the information being provided. Member agencies to use referral pathways where possible.Risks: GBV service providers safety and security because of their role and PTS dealing with violence. Privacy concerns on confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services. Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging. Persons with disability have difficult accessing GBV services. Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girls mobility. Measures: Self care strategies as part of programmes and organization HR policy. GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. Targeting adolescent girls with socially accepted activities like life skills as safe entry point. CP Risks: Risk that existing traditional practices and community structures conflict with child protection principles. Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and caregivers) through the provision of specialised child protection services if not adequately trained/ services are of low quality. Risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys and their families if staff are not appropriately trained or if client data is not managed appropriately. Measures: Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community. Ensure consultation and mobilisation of key persons in the community. Provide culturally adapted information on key child protection issues and responsibilities of different people in the growth and development of children

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The border crossings remain open and accessible for humanitarian goods and staff. If the border closes for humanitarian goods or commercial traffic and our ability to receive essential items or funds, we will consider other, less accessible means of delivery. GC/CHF staff continue to have consistent access to target locations while maintaining acceptable levels of independence, impartiality, and neutrality. We will continue to utilize our community engagement team to liaise with the relevant camp stakeholders, and continue to reinforce that we are a humanitarian agency that abides by the humanitarian principles of independence, neutrality, and impartiality. Should our level of access change significantly or impact our ability to work within our humanitarian principles, we will look to identify alternative locations or solutions. Active violence does not directly impact our areas of operation for extended period of time; Violence in the targeted areas remains at a level which allows GC to implement the program, with both armed and unarmed parties to the conflict allowing access to vulnerable HHs for provision of services. We will regularly assess the risk to our beneficiaries and staff of violence in our project locations. If the risk becomes unacceptable we will suspend activities temporarily and close permanently if needed, according to ethical closeout procedures and in communication with the Cluster. Camp managers, Local Councils, relief committees, and community stakeholders will collaborate with GC, and facilitate access for delivery of humanitarian assistance. We regularly engage with local actors to coordinate activities where appropriate. We are also expanding our use of community networks to both implement some activities and encourage local acceptance of our activities. Risk of SEA: staff are trained on PSEA, sign Code of Conduct, active Feedback Mechanism is available and BNF are aware of the FM, GC has staff to receive and investigate allegations.
