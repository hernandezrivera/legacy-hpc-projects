Project Summary

Under Sudan’s MYHS 2017-2019 and HRP 2019, UNICEF will endeavour to support the most vulnerable and marginalized particularly, women and children. UNICEF positions itself to work in the deprived communities with the highest level of vulnerability aiming to address the underlying immediate and long-term causes. Based on the comparative advantage of UNICEF’s programming and hands-on experience of 60 years in Sudan, it is evident that a meaningful impact and results only be attained by adopting inter-sectoral approaches given they are fully integrated.
UNICEF aims to deliver an integrated package for children by adopting a “whole child” approach wherein, the UNICEF led sectors would work together by identifying the geographic convergence, improving programmatic complementarity under a common result framework thus, realizing rights of the most vulnerable children in the areas of health, education, WASH and protection. This will allow UNICEF to enhance sustainability within the projects and build resilience of the affected population and thus, bridging the humanitarian and development.
UNICEF in partnership with UNHCR has developed a modus operandi for refugee response both for in-camps and out-of-camp population. UNICEF is ensuring the delivery of an integrated maternal child health and nutrition package along with WASH, education and child protection in camps. Provision of out-of-camp services has been mainstreamed with the service delivery for host communities. In the spirit of partnership and the new way of working, UNICEF submits an intersectoral proposal – a progressive step in the direction of achieving integration.

Needs

Conflict that broke out in South Sudan in December 2013 displaced thousands of civilians. As of 31 December 2018, over 384,478 (registered) South Sudanese refugees had arrived in Sudan. UNHCR estimate an additional 70,700 new arrivals to Sudan in 2019, with an anticipated total of 833,994 South Sudanese refugees in need of protection and assistance by the end of 2019, over 64 per cent of SSR continue to live in out-of-camp settlements alongside host communities, and in urban centres. Results of the 2 SENS surveys conducted in West Kordofan and White Nile record critical levels of Global Acute malnutrition amongst SSR both within and out of camps while anemia prevalence was found to be high. Coverage of services was found to be low, consumption of nutrition rich foods low as well and key Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) practices poor. Recent assessments revealed that during their escape, children heard terrifying stories and witnessed violence, fighting, burning of villages, deaths of parents, siblings and relatives. Children were exhausted when they reached their destinations. Many children are unaccompanied or separated and the risk of being exposed to violence, rape and exploitation is very high. Survivors of sexual violence are at risk of suffering severe and long-lasting health and psychosocial problems. Adolescent boys have reportedly disappeared and been recruited by armed groups. children are significantly deprived and distressed, impacting their psychosocial wellbeing.Of the 3 million children, out of school, refugee children are the majority exposing them to various vulnerabilities. In the 1,271 public schools in communities hosting refugees, the enrolment rate is less than 5%. The draft 2019 Refugee Response Plan (RRRP) 110,000 refugee children in Sudan need Education in Emergencies (EiE). There is high prevalence of poor hygiene practices with only 40 per cent of households having a specific place for hand washing, widespread open defecation inadequate treatment and storage of drinking water, low access to improved water supply and sanitation are considerably contributing to the high incidence of diarrhoeal diseases and malnutrition, mainly stunting among children in Sudan. Low WASH service increases the risk of SSR to different type of WASH related diseases. Most of the diseases reported are skin infections, trachoma, and diarrheas which clearly correlated to a shortage of water and soapThe South Sudanese refugees are extremely vulnerable with high risks of outbreaks of diseases due to low access to the basic health services, poor health seeking behaviour and inappropriate hygienic practices. Childhood illnesses among this population continues to be high due to malaria, respiratory infection, malnutrition and neo-natal deficiencies.

o Support basic education for 70,655 (55% girls) children (56,132) SSR and 14,523 children from host community) through safe learning spaces, gender sensitive WASH facilities, and distribution of learning material o Train 1,178 teachers on teaching methodologies, and Education in Emergencies (EiE). o Train 1,413 education actors, including PTA members on school management skills and EiE.o Provision of integrated psychosocial support (PSS) and referral mechanisms to advanced PSS and mental health services for children.o Family tracing and reunification and provision of alternative care to UASCs.o In kind support and cash assistance for vulnerable children.o Establishment and strengthening of community-based child protection structure o Mine Risk Educationo Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism (MRM) of grave violation against children o Procure and distribute the essential heath supplies (vaccines, ORS, Zinc, IMCI kits, MW kits, LLITNS and AWD kits) and commodities including cold chain and IMCI equipment, o Build the capacity of the front-line health care providers, case management of childhood illnesses, PMTCT, maternal and newborn care o Support provision of health promotion and communication for behavior change through media, campaigns and house to house visits, o Provide treatment for 9,534 children suffering from SAM including procure 11,441 cartons of Ready to Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) and support for operational support including the expansion of the current number of Outpatient Treatment Program (OTP) siteso Support community mobilization to improve awareness about the signs and dangers of severe acute malnutrition and CMAM services.o Support early case finding and referral using multiple strategies and provision of counseling on optimal IYCFo Water trucking and emergency latrines construction for newly arriving SRR o rehabilitation of the non-functional water source and construction of water supply systems. o Hygiene and sanitation promotion
