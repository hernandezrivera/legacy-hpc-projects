Project Summary

The project aims to assist IDPs, returnees and crisis-affected communities by fostering an improved protection environment through the provision of community-based protection services. Protection interventions include 1) legal counselling and assistance 2) protection monitoring, referrals and information sharing on services 3) social protection and support on PSS; GBV prevention and response programmes 4) child protection; 5) case management; 6) provision of in kind assistance covering basic needs and programmes of elderly and persons with disabilities; 7) non-formal education support for children at risk; 8) social protection through small-scale vocational skills training and socio-economic support specifically directed to the most vulnerable individuals, ; 9) campaigns and awareness session including on mine risk education for communities targeting women, men, boys and girls and; 10) capacity building of first responders working in communities, volunteers, partners and government counterparts to provide better protection services. Legal aid interventions will facilitate access to civil/HLP documentation for IDPs and returnees and provide support in legal matters related to GBV, CP and other protection issues. Support to community initiatives will also be implemented engaging communities to address their own protection concerns. Intervention in the context of refugee returnees will focus on, legal aid, coordination with government counterparts and stakeholders, information management and return tracking systems and sub – district profiling to better inform return programming and interventions. In addition, the project will support coordinated protection interventions with UNHCR Syria continuing to lead Protection Sector.
Accountability to affected population in the project will be realized through participatory engagement of communities and outreach programmes conducted through community volunteers’ network. Over 2844 outreach volunteers and 112 CC were mobilized in 2018 and this project will build on this approach, to ensure a continuous link with communities, including to receive feedback on project programmes.
Through the project intervention approximately 3.35 million IDPs, returnees and crises affected individuals in host communities (70.5%, 15.5% and 14% respectively) will be reached in government and non-government controlled areas redressing their protection concerns. In addition 4,140 humanitarian workers and local authority staff will be trained and 624 communities assessed. 30% of the project beneficiaries are girls, 25% are boys, 26% are women, and 15% are men and 2% elderly women and 2% elderly men.

Needs

Humanitarian needs in Syria remain overwhelming in terms of scale, severity and complexity, with significant protection risks persisting in a number of areas. This was verified in the findings of over 800 missions conducted in 2018, some 500 of which had protection focus. As per HNO 2019, out of 11.7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, 5 million are in acute need. An additional 1.1million people live in hard-to-reach locations. In 2018, some 1.2 million displacements were tracked across the country. In addition 468,868 were displaced in 509 communities in Idleb, Aleppo and northern Hama governorates (January- July 2018). Beginning of 2018 experienced humanitarian deterioration with simultaneous crises occurring in different areas of the country, including northwest Syria, Afrin and Eastern Ghouta. The situation in north-west Syria remains a key concern given the complex political and military dynamics. At the same time, 987,216 self-organized returns of IDPs and refugees from neighbouring countries were reported to have returned to areas where stability has been restored. The effect of eight years of crisis has depleted the coping capacities of millions of people, stretching their resources to their absolute limit. In this situation, the large number of women headed households, children, older persons and persons with disabilities, especially those without adequate family or community support, are particularly exposed to heightened risks and are increasingly forced to resort to unsustainable and unsafe means of survival, including forced and/or early marriage (reported in 45% of assessed communities), child labour often in hazardous conditions (reported in 81% of the assessed communities), child recruitment by all parties to the conflict (reported in 30% of assessed communities), survival sex, and temporary marriages. During the eight years, many children have been deprived of family and parental care (family separation reported in 46% of communities surveyed including both adults and children). This situation is compounded by the need to increase specialized services for GBV survivors, the management of complex child protection cases which require durable solutions and other persons with specific needs. The impact of the crisis, destruction of infrastructure, increased poverty and lack of civil documentation have increasingly amplified the protection risks and vulnerability of IDP and refugee returnees. HLP rights issues, including restitution and compensation are particular challenges experienced by returnees jeopardizing their protective environment in Syria and will be increasingly important to address as return numbers increase in 2019. In non-Government controlled areas of north-western Syria, a range of protection risks continue to affect individuals and communities. Grave violations of International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL), including indiscriminate, disproportionate attacks continue in areas of the country still affected by active hostilities. With the lack of official civil registries in non-GoS controlled areas, as well as lack of functioning GoS courts, a void exists in which civilians nonetheless continue to need official documentation of life events and property ownership, as well as dependable and sustainable avenues for dispute resolution.The 2019 HNO reports lack of civil documentation as an issue in 93% of covered communities in non-GoS controlled areas of Aleppo, Hama, and Idleb governorates; likewise the Protection Monitoring Task Force active from the Gaziantep hub in North West Syria reports the issue in 95% of its covered communities. 57% of covered communities in non-GoS controlled areas of Aleppo, Hama, and Idleb governorates reported threats to HLP rights, foremost among them disputed ownership, lack of documents, and rental disputes. Child/forced marriage, child labour, GBV, tensions between communities are overwhelming and need redressal.

Implementation of activities

UNHCR’s project aims to meet the needs of IDPs, returnees and conflict affected communities with focus on legal aid to enhance access to civil/HLP documentation, the protection of children, MHPSS,GBV prevention/response, assistance to PSN, small scale livelihoods and non- formal education. UNHCR will further scale up its outreach for the identification of PSN and diversify available responses, increase efforts to enhance resilience. This will be achieved through an expanding network from 98 community centres(CCs) and 28 satellite centres in 2018 to 144 CC/SC in 2019, related mobile units and outreach volunteers to reach 3 million persons in 13 governorates including in Deir-ez- Zor governorate. CCs will anchor the community based protection approach. Legal aid programme will encompass legal counselling/assistance through an expanding network of lawyers, legal outreach volunteers, legal clinics and CCs to improve access to civil documentation and HLP issues and provide support in legal matters related to SGBV and CP after receipt of referral from actors. UNHCR will support 15 projects for rehabilitation of civil registry offices, support to immigration border points and courts, and in -kind support to increase the capacity of the GoS to respond to the civil/property documentation needs. Mine risk education will be implemented in 39 CCs for 17,160 beneficiaries. Livelihood activities will aim to reinforce resilience, positively affect prevention of CP and GBV issues. Catch up and remedial classes will help children reintegrate into the formal education system. Building the capacity of first responders working in the communities, volunteers and partner personnel will provide better care and support to GBV survivors and CP cases and address MHPSS issues. Prevention campaigns and awareness sessions will reduce harmful behaviours, promote gender equality and disseminate information on available services. The response services to GBV and CP cases include individual case management including FTR interventions for unaccompanied minors – in coordination with UNICEF - and referrals to other services. Prevention of child protection prevalent issues (child marriage, child labour. etc.) will be also carried out through community based structures (child welfare committees and children clubs) to mitigate the exposure of children to multiple protection risks. To maximize community involvement, women committees will be expanded to enhance GBV prevention and response activities. In collaboration with UNICEF and UNFPA, UNHCR will pursue advocacy with key GoS actors with focus on gender discriminatory provisions and CP gaps in the legal framework, joint awareness campaigns as well as more generally aim to engage duty bearers to address protection needs. CP interventions are integrated under generic protection (Protection AoR), i.e., legal assistance/interventions; medical and general assistance or remedial classes for out of school children which are reported under generic protection. UNHCR Syria will continue to lead the Protection Sector/ to ensure coordinated protection interventions. UNHCR cross border activities coordinated from the Turkey hub will include humanitarian response to fresh and protracted displacement in IDP sites in North West Syria, such as : 1) provision of quality and integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness raising, psychosocial assistance, targeted support to persons with specific protection needs and other community initiatives through community centres and outreach mechanisms, 2) provision of legal advice and counselling on civil status documentation /registration as well as on housing/land/property issues in accordance with national legislation and 3) capacity building activities targeting humanitarian actors and national/ local authorities to improve quality provision of protection services.

Protection Risk Analysis

1)Risk: Risk of inter community tension if some communities in the catchment areas of the community centres or reached by mobile units in more remote locations are served while others are not; and risk to increase tensions or damage relationships/trust between communities and humanitarian actors for continuing to assess and ask questions and risk receiving distorted information due to communities' perceptions of humanitarian staff expectations or to communities' expectations of a type of response. Mitigation measures: Conduct an in depth assessment of the envisaged locations for community centres, existing populations groups and their particular needs and provide information to communities on scope and expectation of assessment, triangulate information, train assessors, coordinate with protection actors to see if assessments have already been conducted, provide feedback, ensure that recruitment of staff and outreach volunteers in community centres reflect age, gender and diversity principles; mobilize all communities in the catchment area as part of the establishment of community centres; 2) Risk: High turnover of trained staff who are supposed to provide specialized interventions such as legal assistance, PSS, case management in GBV and child protection, resulting in loss of capacity to provide appropriate services. Mitigation measures: Ensure proper mechanism to recruit qualified staff; provide adequate training opportunities; invest in training of trainers sessions to expand the pool of qualified trainers; provide mentorship options for newly recruited staff; and harmonize awareness and information material to ensure consistency in communication with persons in need. 3) Risk: Lack of involvement and technical capacity of national stakeholders in areas where the response to needs require their involvement, e.g. as regard to access to civil documentation and HLP documents, specific child protection interventions (family reunification, alternative care arrangements and other statutory interventions). Mitigation measures: Advocate with duty bearers on identified needs; support national actors and community based groups to ensure sustainability of protection interventions; support technical capacity of duty bearers; and establish coordination mechanisms with duty bearers. 4) Risk: Exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers when accessing assistance and services Mitigation: Design specific complaint mechanisms and ensure adequate staff to follow up on complaints, ensuring standards of data protection and confidentiality. Provide regular trainings to staff as well as to partners on Code of Conduct and PSEA. 5) Risk: Content of message is not fully adapted to the social and cultural environment and creates misunderstanding or even tensions and has repercussions on the organisation implementing the campaign. Mitigation: Staff members in the field and at hub level, as well as protection network members, are drawn from the local communities (both host community, returnee and IDP) who are aware of the local culture. In addition feedback is collected from participants in awareness sessions as well as the staff themselves on the reception of protection messages and concerns of the community. 6) Risk: Lack of a sustainable intervention due to inadequate service provision or limited involvement of national stakeholders. Mitigation: Coordinate with the Protection Cluster and consult 4Ws to ensure balanced coverage of needs. Build capacity of and relationship with local/national NGO to conduct activities. As well conduct assessment on needs and existing capacities of national stakeholders / grass root NGOs who used to deliver services prior to the crisis, and build on these capacities to respond to protection needs. Base programming decisions on needs identified through protection monitoring. Ensure age, gender, and diversity balance among staff members and protection network members.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1) Risk: Fluid context, shifting front-lines and unstable political situation in NES. Mitigating measure: UNHCR has in place policies, manuals and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for risk assessment and risk management. Its risk management approach is specifically tailored to suit UNHCR's needs in giving visibility to risk considerations in planning and decision-making. The risk management process helps managers to identify, analyse and evaluate risks, and develop and implement appropriate risk treatments relevant to the specific context. 2) Risk: delay or denial of acquiring approval / signing of UNHCR Partnership Programme Agreements by Government to implement the protection activities including on activities of legal interventions, refugee returnee support programmes Mitigating measure: advocacy and putting in place close coordination fora with Government counterparts, MOSAL, MOFA. Liaison and strengthen of coordination at field ministerial level such as DOSAL 3) Risk: Lack of direct monitoring/control mechanisms on partners' activities due to inaccessibility. Mitigating measure; establishing a third party monitoring mechanism for physical verification and adequate monitoring to be done. 4) Risk: Limited number of partners that have the experience and/or capacity to implement the interventions that address the identified needs of persons of concern: Mitigating measure: Ensuring training packages are in line with standards, providing technical support and conducting refresher sessions periodically for partner staff. 5) Risk: Lack of direct access by UNHCR to persons of concern inside non-GoS-controlled areas of North West Syria as well limited access and requirements for approvals to access in Government controlled areas. Mitigating measure: Ensure that partners have staff who have GoT permission to cross from Turkey to Syria to conduct face-to-face trainings and spot checks on activities; In North West Syria, establish a monitoring mechanism through a third-party contractor with researchers inside Syria for physical verification and evaluation of activities and advocacy with GoS to grant UNHCR access and facilitate timely approval of access requests in Government controlled areas. 6) Risk: Due to conflict activity, extended loss of the basic physical security for beneficiaries and staff members. Mitigating measure: In case of escalated physical security risks, including due to increase in conflict activity, checkpoints, and other threats to humanitarian workers and beneficiaries, UNHCR advises partners to follow their security protocols and to notify the organization of suspension of activities. Where such threats are extended, UNHCR can consider, where possible and reasonable, relocation of physical facilities, use of mobile teams, or change in the nature of activities in order to reduce gaps in service to the populations in need.
