Project Summary

The project is designed to reduce shelter vulnerabilities of host communities, returnees, and IDPs inside camps by restoring basic infrastructure to mitigate the risks of floods, replacing old tents and delivering alternative transitional shelters, and then outside of camps by rehabilitation of damaged houses. On the other hand, the project aims to fulfill the winter needs of the targeted population through fuel support, as well as target most in-need HHs with NFI kits according to cluster criteria of targeting. The main achievement of the proposed interventions is to enhance the situation for the current shelter in order to improve the living conditions for those IDPs who are living in the informal settlements, self-settled sites, and managed camps in order to avoid the negative effect of the weather actuations along the different seasons which might lead to food and losing their shelters, in addition, to enhance the targeted population’s resilience against weather conditions by conducting the following activities:
a. Provision of core NFIs.
b. Provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs.
c. Installation of new/ replacement of existing tents.
d. Installation and/ or construction of temporary transitional shelters.
e. Implementation of HH-level improvements (tent graveling)
f. Implementation of site level improvements/ infrastructure
g. Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished housing
h. Supply and installation of HH-level solar energy systems

3 - Needs

In accordance with the MSNA 2022 findings, 34% of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelter, of which 19% reside in finished residential buildings classified as damaged and/ or displaying ‘adequacy issues. Lack of access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, deterioration of shelter integrity (notable increase in damages to windows/doors), in addition to lack of privacy, are among the top shelter adequacy issues identified.Based on 2023 HNO-SNFI, primarily in northwest and northeast Syria, 2.05 million IDPs reside in last resort sites, a marginal increase from 2.02 million in the previous year. Such locations lack crucial infrastructure and rely on emergency shelter solutions and continued humanitarian support.Host communities in 571 communities across Syria are overburdened, hosting more IDPs than resident population. Alongside the displaced, nearly 27 per cent of the host community households are living in sub-standard shelters where repair/rehabilitation is needed alongside a multisectoral response to ensure access and availability to basic services.Overall NFI PiN has increased by 5% to 5.7 million due to both security environment and economic conditions. Fuel, winter clothing, mattresses and blankets have witnessed a significant increase in inaccessibility in 2022, these items are essential, accessible yet unaffordable to most individuals, particularly during harsh winter seasons when access to basic NFIs is considered lifesaving.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

ATAA PSEA policy developed in June 2022 and with official approvals from PSEA network and UNICEF PSEA focal point and delivered to all staff to be signed, on the other hand ATAA became low risk partner in SEA with UNICEF for 5 years after conducting PSEA assessment.ATAA adopted a core concept in all intervention to make PSEA and CSG as mandatory in all projects and activities, in this regard, all ATAA staff, volunteers and contractors will got orientation sessions about PSEA and CSG policies and sign both PSEA and CSG code of conducts.All project locations will include banners and complaint boxes with delivering messages from accountability teams regarding the complaints and feedback channels in addition to stand alone system regarding reporting and response of any PSEA case starting from receiving the report, to investigation process, and provide aid and support to the survivor till closure of the case after achieving the objectives of investigation.ATAA is PSEA network member participate in all regular network meetings, in addition to submit monthly reports to the network, in addition to conduct training for all staff about PSEA and target humanitarian workers in the intervention location to support the role of PSEA network.ATAA is operating PSEA stand-alone project focusing on raising the awareness of targeted communities (78 community) in NW Syria about PSEA and enhance the ability of BNFs to reach reporting channels of ATAA and PSEA network.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks and Mitigations: 1- Overlap, lose some locations due to other NGOs’ intervention, loss of access. - will perform multilevel coordination (Cluster, Field) -coordinated with all the stakeholders during the design phase 2- Risks related to HLP and propriety conflicts - Ensure validation of the documents during the project - communicate clearly with all the stakeholders - involved the camps management committees, LCs, KIs in the process in case of conflict 3- Due of the conditions on the ground that may force some beneficiaries to displace. - ATAA will monitor all updates on the ground if any of the beneficiaries are displaced, we will choose alternative beneficiaries who meet the criteria in the exact location. 4- SEA of affected community members by anyone associated with the aid provision. This risk will be mitigated by strict measures and the awareness of the field staff on this topic and by signing codes of conduct of all staff relating to PSEA, and reporting via established reporting mechanisms of any concerns or suspicions regarding SEA, and investigation if necessary (hotline).
