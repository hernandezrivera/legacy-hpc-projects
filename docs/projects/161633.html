Project Summary

Jireh Doo Foundation (JDF),through funding from CAID/ICCO will be meeting the wash and protection needs in one host (Bulabulin) community in Dikwa, these services will include the construction of emergency latrines and showers,rehabilitation of latrines, hygiene promotion activities, showers and solar Boreholes in camps while establishing women and child friendly spaces through which women and girls will be reached with physio-social support, case management services and economic strengthening activities.

1 - Needs

Dikwa LGA is one of the most affected Local government areas with respect to the ongoing and protracted humanitarian crisis. Dikwa is one of the hard to reach local government areas of Borno state with humanitarian actors getting access only through the UN Air Services helicopters while road networks to Dikwa are heavily manned by Security forces have high risks of attacks by armed opposition groups. Residents of Dikwa LGA are all concentrated in camps and few communities around Dikwa town, but due to the continuous operations of the armed opposition groups, the Nigerian security forces have refused to extend the protective trenches, making it difficult for Beneficiaries to move freely and find alternative sources of livelihood. From recent assessment conducted by REACH, it has been established that;• 82% of assessed settlements reported that some population remains non-targeted by humanitarian actors as they dwell within the host communities • Populations consist largely of vulnerable IDPs and host populations. Most have limited to no information on the availability of humanitarian aid services in Dikwa.• Food insecurity and protection needs especially GBV, and the need to ensure traumatized persons heal, are most severe in Gwoza LGA, followed closely by Dikwa LGAFrom on-going implementation within the LGA it has been observed that there are high WASH needs around the host communities of which little attention has been put to as actors have focused more on the WASH needs around camps. This proposed intervention will therefore address this gap. According to Reliefweb report in August 2019, seasonal rains and windstorms have caused significant damage in northeast Nigeria, especially in camps or camps –like settings that are prone to flooding and have poor drainage facilities. An estimated 21,056 households have been affected in the three BAY states, with the worst damage reported in Borno (MMC/Jere, Monguno, Gwoza and Dikwa LGAs) and in Adamawa (Yola South and Yola North LGAs). The high degree of overcrowding, poor hygiene practises and destruction of WASH infrastructure are leading to increased risks of contracting diseases. About half of those affected have lost their emergency shelter or have experienced partial shelter damage and the majority require household supplies, food items, water purification, latrines repairs, hygiene kits, and other WASH services. Approximately 500 latrines and bathing facilities have been damaged or destroyed by wind and rain to date. UNICEF is coordinating WASH activities in the affected locations and standing by to provide supplies (Especially chlorine and WASH kits) as well as rehabilitation of damaged facilities. This intervention will therefore complement these efforts by filling in the gaps through training and equipping of water user committees to ensure that the beneficiaries own the process of maintaining these facilities that will be either constructed or rehabilitated. According to REACH, WASH infrastructure mapping (January 10th 2019) there are • 91 water points fully/partially working • 63 water points are not functional NJR 2019 Joint Needs Assessment found out that 50% (2% of the entire sample size) of women are at risk of household violence and 62.5% (1.6% of the entire sample size) of women are at risk of sexual violence in Dikwa. These include 79% women and children; IDPs and refugees, civilians in inaccessible areas and individuals from host communities. Female-headed households and child-headed households, unaccompanied and separated children, adolescent boys and girls, face heightened protection risks including GBV, child recruitment and SEA. Return movement of IDPs and refugees, which increased in 2019, have not always met the principles of, safety and dignity. 2 - Activities

As part of efforts to support the people in need, JDF will be distributing hygiene kits to 6000 individuals/ most vulnerable persons within camps and host communities, rehabilitating two solar powered boreholes which aims to 10,000 individuals, 400 women and girls will be reached through the distribution of menstrual hygiene management kits, rehabilitation and construction of showers and latrines to reduce open defecation and poor hygiene practices while training of WASHcoms and hygiene promoters from within these communities to continue to promote these good hygiene practices.
