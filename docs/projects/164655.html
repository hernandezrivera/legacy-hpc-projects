Project Summary

IOM will continue to provide and further expand its provision of integrated protection services to conflict-affected populations across NW Syria in community centres and through mobile protection teams. Activities include:
• Specialised child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) case management;
• Psycho-social support (PSS) and Mental-Health and Psycho-social support (MHPSS);
• Individual Protection Assistance (IPA);
• Protection monitoring (on-site and mobile teams);
• Legal advisory services (civil status documentation and HLP);
• Community mobilization and outreach
• Provision of dignity kits for GBV/emergency response
Overall, IOM will directly support 78,100 beneficiaries (25,800 men, 24,100 women, 14,250 girls, 13,950 boys) across NW Syria through provision of integrated protection services including community based and individually targeted protection interventions and the provision of dignity kits. The purpose of these interventions is to mitigate and reduce protection risks and vulnerabilities for crisis-affected populations. Specifically, activities comprise of:
• Provision of integrated protection activities in community centers to ensure that a greater number of Syrians have access to information and protection services in their community. Services include GBV and CP case management and specialized information/legal support on Civil Status Documentation (CSD)/Housing Land and Property (HLP).
• Provision of integrated protection services at Reception Centres and Planned Camps to provide the full range of integrated protection services; namely PSS, CP case management, legal support and protection monitoring through in-site and mobile teams.
• Legal clinics/mobile outreach teams to provide legal awareness and information sessions on CSD and HLP to conflict-affected populations in Idlib and Aleppo Governorate and to conduct HLP and CSD trainings for humanitarian workers in NW Syria and Gaziantep. Legal mobile clinics will be staffed by experienced lawyers and provide individual legal advisory services on CSD and HLP.
• Protection support in 40 informal IDP sites in Idlib Governorate to mitigate protection risk and build protection capacity of camp management committees. Mobile teams will conduct monthly service mappings and facilitate referrals to specialized/non-specialized services, and provide GBV and Child Protection awareness sessions, and PSS sessions for IDP site residents.
• Procurement of 10,000 dignity kits for distribution in GBV/emergency response among women and girls of reproductive age.
In addition, HLP Due Diligence support across XB Clusters will be continued throughout 2020, including through the formulation of sector-specific guidance on how to minimize the risks of HLP grievances, and appropriate trainings of humanitarian workers. This work will be closely aligned with the Gaziantep HLP Technical Working Group.

4 - Needs

As outlined in the HNO 2020, 44% of assessed communities respondents reported presence of at least one protection issue as very commonly occurring, while in 52% of communities respondents reported presence of ﬁve or more assessed protection issues (HNO 2020). IOM’s protection services are provided in Harim, Jisr As Shigur, and Idlib district of Idleb Governorate and Al Bab, Azaz and Afrin districts in Aleppo Governorate, which are between severity level 5 and 6. These districts have also seen the highest number of new IDP arrivals, which are largely vulnerable, women-headed households and those who were not able to previously leave. Women and girls have been facing particular protection risks, including most severe forms of GBV. 69% of key informants across NW Syria surveyed noted that early/forced marriage is occurring in their communities, with 14% stating that early marriage is very common for adolescent girls. Data also shows an increase in rape, especially perpetrated against girls, widowed and divorced women (Voices 2020). People with disabilities, including children, are also particularly affected by violence, including abuse, neglect or exploitation. Child labor, including child recruitment, is a growing concern with 88% of communities reporting its occurrence (HNO 2020). Other pressing protection concerns include the lack/loss of civil documentation, and HLP issues, reported by KIIs as occurring in 53%; and 11% of assessed communities (Turkey Hub MSNA 2019) and compounding a series of existing protection risks such as freedom of movement. Levels of psychological distress are escalating across all of NW Syria with a cumulative toll on child and adult mental and physical wellbeing, especially for IDPs experiencing multiple/protracted displacement. Protection needs are especially acute in the more than 500 informal IDP sites across North West Syria, which are overcrowded and lack basic services, putting residents at heightened risk of violence including GBV.

5 - Implementation of activities

1.1. Provision of specialized protection services in community centres (Al Bab, Darkoush, Salqeen, Dana, Jarablous and Afrin) Service provision includes PSS, CP and GBV case management, IPA, protection monitoring and HLP/CSD legal advisory services, through on-site and mobile teams. GBV services will include structured awareness raising using the GBV SC Awareness Raising Toolkit. All centres will be equipped with ramps/handles to reduce access barriers for persons with disabilities.1.2 Provision of integrated protection and legal services in IOM’s Reception Centers and Planned Camp (Azaz, Maaret Elkhwan, Al Bab) Community-based and individual protection assistance will be provided by on-site and mobile teams, including CP case management, IPA, HLP/CSD protection advisory services, regular service mapping and referrals. 1.3 Legal clinics/mobile outreach teams in Aleppo/Idlib Governorates Services will include legal awareness sessions, HLP/CSD trainings for humanitarian workers, and individual legal advisory support. 1.4 Building protection capacity of camp management committees (CMCs) in informal IDP sites. Three mobile teams will train CMCs in 40 informal IDP sites in Idleb Governorate on leadership, protection mainstreaming, PFA, GBV and CP basics, and safe referrals. Teams will also conduct service mappings, facilitate referrals, and raise awareness on protection core concepts and prevention of family separation to site residents. 1.5 Provision of 10,000 dignity kits for women and girls to support GBV and emergency response, in coordination with UNFPA. 1.6 HLP support (Due Diligence and research) Continuation of HLP Due Diligence Support across Clusters, including development of operational guidance and ongoing research. Third-party monitoring will be utilized to monitor and evaluate all project activities, including outcome monitoring for GBV/CP case management services. Disaggregated beneﬁciary numbers will be veriﬁed through 4W reporting to the Cluster.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. Expectations of response/services in locations where services may be limited. 2. Risk of discrimination if a service is provided to speciﬁc individuals at the exclusion of others 3. Physical security risk due to conﬂict activity; accessibility barriers 4. Beneﬁciaries not interested in awareness sessions. 5. Limited judicial mechanisms for redress; risk that by providing information about documentation which may not be recognized is perceived as endorsement of said documents/legal system 6. Lack of quality services and referral expose GBV survivors to harm and risk of stigmatization, reprisal and further trauma. 7. Resistance or backlash from sections of the community/family on GBV messaging. 8. Risk that existing community structures conﬂict with CP principles 9. Exploitation or abuse by staff/service providers when accessing assistance and services Mitigation measures: 1. Consult with communities to understand and address local perspectives. Provide communication mechanisms for persons with visual/hearing impairments. 2. Triangulate all information from all sources prior to decision making. 3. Conduct regular monitoring of security situation and centre activities and regular community consultations to identify/address accessibility barriers. 4. Provide awareness raising/information activities providing evidence-based, culturally sensitive, information. 5. Advocacy on non-penalization for alternate documents that follow spirit of recognized legal framework 6. Training on GBV core concepts and dissemination of SOPs for all staff involved in referral use of GBV SC ethical closure tool for all GBV programming; update GBV exit plan as needed. 7. Engage both female and male family members in awareness-raising, adapt GBV messaging to context based on gender analysis, 8. Culturally sensitive parenting awareness sessions and community outreach activities 9. Reinforce that humanitarian services are for free, provide regular staff training for code of conduct and PSEA.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1. GoS retake parts of or all of the implementation areas putting an end to all project activities. 2. Security-related or physical constraints hinder access to target areas. 3. Increased security deterioration in NW Syria (increased airstrikes and proximity to ground battles), severely compromising access 4. Service providers do not fulﬁl their obligations and remain committed to coordination and information sharing, so beneﬁciaries will not enjoy with meaningful access to the referred service providers. 5. Local authorities decide to ban the implementing the project or part of it. 6. Turkish Authorities deny/restrict access to ES and/or Afrin 7. Non-GoS authorities and/or sanctioned groups attempt to inﬂuence programming Mitigating measures: 1. N/A 2. Ongoing situational analysis and adjustment of project activities, including relocation. 3. Continuous monitoring of the situation and provision of services where accessible. 4. Conduct monthly service mapping to ensure the availability of services and make sure beneﬁciaries are aware of such. Coordinating with the Protection Cluster to ensure awareness of referral pathways 4. Ongoing coordination and sensitization between IPs and local authorities 5. Continuous monitoring of the situation and provision of services where accessible. IOM will continue applying its security procedures and policies for program implementation. 6. Strengthen relationship with Turkish authorities, incl. AFAD, TRC, Turkish NGOs, PDMM etc. and observing changes & updates closely. Develop and disseminate targeted key messages on protection to concerned stakeholders. 7. IOM will continue to strictly adhere to neutrality and impartiality and, based on a sound risk analysis, adjust interventions accordingly if the context demands it.
