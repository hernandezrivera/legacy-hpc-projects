Project Summary

The project is designed to respond to the needs of most vulnerable households in A’zaz and Afrin districts through meeting their food and WASH needs, response to the severe needs of winter season and strengthen the self-reliance and resilience of the affected communities through integrated 11 months intervention reaching out to 15000 individuals, supporting their priority needs and enabling them to shift to the early recovery stage.
The intervention targets IDPs camps and host communities in Afrin, Raju, Suran and A’zaz sub-districts as these locations have been classified as one of the highest vulnerability scoring areas.
The project consists of 3 pillars:
Food and WASH cash assistance worth $100 (In the format of MPC as e cards ):
1- Distribution of e cards (cash value 65 USD) for 8 regular rounds and improving of food consumption scores for the targeted HHs, integrated with WASH needs (additional 35 USD) and responding to the needs of water trucking and hygiene promotion of the targeted HHs.
2- Winter response:
Distribution of Cash for winterization (oneoff 150 USD cash) and support the HHs to meet their urgent needs for heating and winter clothes.
3- Livelihoods and Early Recovery:
Improving the access of the targeted HHs to livelihood opportunities and basic services through the integration of ERL, FSL introductory vocational training, mentorship and small enterprises supports (Small business grants). DWHH will target the same markets hosting apprenticeship with market rehabilitation to enhance access and strengthen the market capacities to include higher number of apprenticeship and open the space for new enterprises. Vocational training will be, 240 participants will benefit of vocational training in the sewing, electrical maintenance and vehicles maintenance, beauty care and sewing. Out of 240 trainees, 120 trainees will be selected for mentorship programme which will be 3 months on-job training and 50 will be supported to start their own business. More focus will be given to existing to small business who will be encouraged to join the programme. The Vocational trainees will be selected from the same targeted locations supported by food and WASH cash assistance or locations targeted by winterization.
4- Protection
WHH will aim to enhance the availability, sustainability and accesibility of quality and integrated protection services. For a total of 3000 individuals, the following activities will be conducted: awareness raising, case management services, capacity-building activities, community based protection committees and monitoring, GBV response, etc.
The project will target PWD, Youth and women specifically and will ensure involvement of the community through formation of community committees per each community and activation of CRM system via many channels of communication.

3 - Needs

According to the HNO 2023, the number of people in need is proceeding 14.6 million. The food, livelihood assistance and electricity assistance are the top needs (HNO2023). Number of people reported with food insecurity has been 13.9 million. The continues deterioration in food and nutrition situation and the adoption of negative coping strategies has impacted the resilience of households. As many as 77 per cent of households reported buying less expensive and less preferred foods resulting in poorer diets (63 per cent with low and medium diets). Lack of employment and income opportunities continues, with the burden driven by lack of purchase power which creates an increase in household food insecurity and difficulties to secure minimum food needs. In reference to the needs assessment conducted by DWHH team in Afrin and A’zaz districts in May 2022, the top three priority needs for the households reported were: Food (87%), Employment/livelihood opportunities (53%), and medical care (37%). The findings of this assessment which was based on 107 HH surveys included the livlivelihood – based coping strategies which people use such as buying food on credit (%100 in Afrin, %88 in A’zaz), reducing expenditure on non-food items, health, and education (%33 in Afrin, %67 in A’zaz) and selling HH properties (%33 in Afrin, %28 in A’zaz). 85% of the interviewed HHs reported they faced problems accessing heating fuel in the past winter period.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will start with introduction to the targeted communities including community representatives, key stakeholders, PWDs, youth and women representatives, protocols will be signed with AFAD. Selection criteria and timeline will be discussed and finalized with the community committees who will be formed at the start of the project. Registration for HHs for cash assistance (food&WASH) will be done in February and March through desk registrations and verification. By selection of the bnfs, DWHH will coordinate dissemination sessions to orient the beneficiaries for distribution. Meanwhile market assessment will be done and vendors assessment will be launched to select Money transfer agents. Later, the registration for vocational training will be taking place and two rounds of training with each targeting 120 trainees will be conducted for 20 days starting from fourth month of the project. By attending 20 days, beneficiaries will be selected for apprenticeship or enterprises based on their interest. 50 beneficiaries selected under enterprises will get small business grant of 1000 USD and the activity will be from 5th until 11th (last month) month of the project. Apprenticeship will be 3 months on-job training targeting 120 trainees. Cash for Winter will take place from 8th month until the11th month (Dec. 2023) where DWHH will follow same method of committee's formation and desk registration in September and October. Under protection component, 3000 HHs will be reached.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and Sexual Harassment (SH) are unacceptable breaches of fundamental human rights and a deep betrayal of WHH’s core values. The sexual exploitation and abuse of those who depend on WHH for assistance runs counter to all our personal and organisational values. It is unconscionable, it is intolerable, and it is often criminal. Equally intolerable is the sexual harassment of our fellow aid workers. Working closely with partners and communities, WHH has significantly stepped up its fight against SEA and SH. This work is informed by, and taking place in tandem with, the work of related actors including the broader UN system, donors, and civil society organizations. Strategy covers both SEA and SH, bringing them together in a way that is mutually reinforcing and that creates opportunities to leverage practices between the two. The Strategy also acknowledges the complexity and limitation to WHH’s authority in relation to SEA perpetrated by implementing partner personnel, including Government personnel. However, it does include measures to address and prevent these violations including supporting partners to fully integrate PSEA into programmes in both development and humanitarian contexts, creating a joined-up accountability culture that abhors, sanctions and prevents SEA and protects victims.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

R1)Turkish authorities prevent implementation partners from operating across the border into Syria. M1)WHH ensures good relations with the Government of Turkiye through legal compliance and appropriate coordination with the relevant authorities. R2)Border closed due to acts of war. M2) Measures are partly carried out by vouchers, so that in the event of a border closure, only in-kind measures with procurement outside Syria would be affected. Furthermore, with the existence of PTT and a procurement team in both Syria and Gazinatep, procurement inside SYR is an option in order to be able to continue the project. R3)Strong fluctuations of the SYP and TRY price in relation to EUR/USD. M3)Procurement within the TUR, including lines in the contract to allow price negotiations with the suppliers, and budget realignment when necessary. R4)Project funds are stolen from armed groups in Syria, or armed groups force the IP to hand over parts of the aid to them. M4) As an active member of AWG, coordinating on aid diversion, available robust partner monitoring framework and requesting the support of local authorities. R5) Military actions of the civil war parties force partners to cease their activities. M5) existence of appropriate protocols and shifting to other locations where programming is possible
