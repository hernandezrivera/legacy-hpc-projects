Project Summary

Under this project, IOM will scale-up its provision of shelter assistance targeting internally displaced people, returnees and host communities. IOM will:
(A) Ensure access to different types of emergency shelter solutions for displaced populations through the distribution and prepositioning of emergency shelter kits, construction of emergency shelters, and rehabilitation of damaged public buildings.
(B) Contribute to decongestion of camps/camp-like settings through construction of emergency shelters and rehabilitation of damaged public buildings.
(C) Ensure that displaced populations have access to various transitional shelter solutions.
(D) Ensure that the care, maintenance and upgrading of existing emergency shelters and transitional shelters are conducted for continued safety and comfort of the affected population.
(E) Ensure that returnee households have access to materials and labour for repairing their damaged houses.
(F) Empower the local market/vendors through the use of cash and voucher modalities.
(G) Support protection-informed voluntary return, local integration or settlement of IDPs through durable and sustainable shelter solutions; and
(H) Promote local knowledge, locally adaptable technologies, and locally available materials to provide medium- to long-term shelter solutions.
IOM’s shelter/NFI activities will be implemented in three States of Northeast Nigeria; Borno, Adamawa and Yobe State, targeting 445,000 individuals. IOM will ensure that all shelter activities conducted are accountable to the affected population and that the specific needs of project beneficiaries are considered, including those related to gender. IOM will ensure that kits and other shelter solutions are harmonized at sector level and coordinated with partners, under the aegis of the sector.
The project will also include the continuation and scaling-up of NFI support for people in camp and camp-like settings and in host communities, including for newly arrived households, in line with Government instructions. To ensure the timely response to the emerging needs in case of influx of new arrivals and locations that have the risk of being affected by heavy rains and/or outbreaks, IOM will preposition NFI kits in/close to critical locations, where IOM has a MoU with partners’ warehouses. IOM plans to guarantee the participation of Women, Men, Girls and Boys as equals to ensure that the special needs of each group are taken into consideration. The design of activities and services will address specific needs of each gender and age groups, and address women's, girls' and boys’ specific needs, especially in terms of protection and access. To illustrate, (1) the content of NFI kits will be tailored in accordance to the needs of different age and gender groups and in coordination with the sector (2) all activities related to this project will equally target the different age and gender groups and will be delivered on a sustainable and equitable basis. Regarding assessment of gender specific needs, monitoring and evaluation and to measure the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations and set-up communication channels with them where possible, and all activities will be conducted based on Do No
Harm principles

1 - Needs

Camps and camp-like settings in Northeast Nigeria present a variety of shelter situations, such as emergency shelters and self-made/makeshift shelters. Other types are host family houses, government buildings, schools, individual houses and community shelters. The shelter conditions across the Borno, Adamawa, Yobe (BAY) States for the conflict-affected population show multiple needs such as; emergency shelter construction for newly-displaced populations, reinforcement, repair and upgrading of existing emergency shelters, improvement of emergency shelters in urban/ peri-urban environments, construction of transitional shelters and upgrading of the existing emergency shelters into more durable solutions. The assessments of the BAY States’ IDP camps/sites conducted by the CCCM/Shelter/NFI Sector indicate that one in four of the camps, which hosts 430,000 IDPs, is highly congested with space of less than 15m2.7 per capita due to the high shelter density. Decongestion of highly congested camps and camps like settings is also needed in light of the COVID 19 pandemic in order to reduce the risk of transmission. Widespread displacement, new waves of displacement, or secondary displacement and steadily increasing number of returnees/relocated households have also increased the level of congestion not only in the camps but also in the local communities where high number of the returnees are being hosted, which also increased the need for shelter/NFI assistance in those communities. The latest DTM assessments have accounted for 2,182, 613 displaced population and 1,918,603 returnees. According to the figures from the CCCM/Shelter/NFI sector, a total of 2,360,346 individuals are in need of shelter assistance in the three states in North-East Nigeria with Borno State having the highest caseload of 1,734,459 individuals, followed by 356,868 in Adamawa and 269,019 in Yobe. Out of these, a total of 416,802 returnees/relocated households are living in heavily damaged shelters. An additional 1,206,137 IDPs living in camps and host communities are without adequate shelter and NFI solutions; in host communities 191,601 non-displaced families are living in damaged houses or hosting IDPs in overcrowded circumstances. NFIs remain the second most reported need of affected people in North-East Nigeria, both in host communities and in camps or camplike settings. Blankets/mats remain the most needed items in camps and camp-like settings, as well as host communities (DTM Round 38).

2 - Activities

Shelter Solutions: - Conducting shelter needs assessments, with all groups including the most vulnerable groups in the community (IDPs and Host Communities) - Provision and pre-positioning of emergency shelter kits – Construction of emergency shelters – Rehabilitation and partitioning of damaged public buildings for accommodating IDPs – Reinforcing and repairing damaged emergency shelters – Upgrading emergency shelters– Construction of improved emergency shelters – Construction of transitional shelters Construction of mudbrick shelters– Provision of emergency shelter assistance through cash or voucher programming – Provision of cash for shelter repair for returnee households with damaged buildings – Provision of rental subsidy assistance to IDPs – Cash assistance to small traders of construction materials – Reinforcing and repairing damaged improved emergency shelters - Reinforcing and repairing damaged transitional shelters Provision of shelter repair kits in return areas – Provision of maintenance materials –Shelter improvements for persons with disabilities- Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) for distribution of shelter kits, repair kits and cash assistance, Post Construction Monitoring for constructed emergency and transitional shelters. NFI assistance: - Conducting NFI needs assessments, with all groups including the most vulnerable groups in the community (IDPs and Host Communities) - Procurement of NFI kits that are culturally appropriate, basic and improved kits as approved and endorsed by the sector and / or vendors selection for voucher-based response- Prepositioning and stockpiling of basic NFI kits in / close to areas of expected influx of new arrivals and in locations likely to experience heavy damage from storms in the rainy season - Distribution of basic and improved kits to new arrivals in both displacement sites and host communities - Distribution of improved NFI kits to IDPs living in camps and non-camp settings - Sensitization of communities on the use of NFI kits - Conducting Market assessment in locations with market availability Distribution of improved NFI kits with a cash component to most vulnerable IDPs in camps and host community households - Conducting Focus Group Discussion with the communities, ensuring the involvement of Women, Girls, Youths through all stages of the intervention - PDM / Beneficiary satisfaction survey

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM plans to guarantee the participation of Women, Men, Girls and Boys as equals to ensure that the special needs of each group are taken into consideration. The design of activities and services will address specific needs of each gender and different age groups, and address women's, girls' and boys’ specific needs, especially in terms of protection and access concerns. To illustrate, (1) the content of NFI kits will be tailored in accordance with the needs of different age and gender groups (2) All activities related to this project will equally target the different age and gender groups and will be delivered on a sustainable and equitable basis. Regarding assessment of gender specific needs, monitoring and evaluation and the measurement of the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations during missions and set-up communication channels with them where possible, and all activities will be conducted based on Do No Harm principles. The respective distributions will be conducted by a team of IOM staff. The team will include IOM Shelter staff to verify the kits provided and offer technical advice as to the use of the kits and shelter repair; IOM Psychosocial Support staff responsible to support the identification of vulnerable beneficiaries and ensuring safe, secure and equitable access to the assistance distributed and services accessed. In addition to IOM staff, the Organization will seek to promote participation from community leaders in monitoring the distributions IOM also provides continuous PSEA trainings for all IOM and partner staff hired. Following its longstanding presence in North-East Nigeria, IOM has developed contextualized capacity in the area of protection mainstreaming and supported the establishment of agency as well as inter-agency referral mechanisms to support protection outcomes throughout programming.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population and most vulnerable community throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of the project and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation and feedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, and persons with disabilities, among others. This will ensure, for example, that the physical abilities of the elderly and people with disabilities are factored into any forward planning in terms of accessibility to services provided. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of the project, most notably through the complaint and feed-back mechanism team and tools. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. After the project has been completed, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and promote learning.
