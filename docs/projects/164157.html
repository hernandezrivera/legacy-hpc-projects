Project Summary

The proposed action seeks to increase the access and quality of education and PSS services in target areas by implementing a combination of formal (non-accredited) and non-formal education activities that complement each other where possible. PIN takes an integrated and holistic approach to addressing the Education in Emergencies (EiE) and Child Protection needs. PIN will employ a dual approach to supporting non-formal education, including through: establishing temporary learning centres (TLCs) for provision of PSS and non-formal education using the cluster-approved Self-Learning Programme (SLP) curriculum in areas with acute concentrations of OOSC – including in IDP camps and informal settlements; and assistance to non-accredited schools where education has been disrupted through providing light repairs, equipment, teaching and learning materials, support for running costs, and provision of after-school catch-up and remedial classes to support pathways into education and reduce drop-out. Specific consideration will be given to particularly vulnerable households with Out of School Children or children at risk of education disruption, who will be provided with psychosocial support and pathways for (re)integration into education. PIN takes an evidence-based approach to programming based on rigorous feedback loops to promote accountability and enhance impact.
PIN expects to target in total 9 sub-districts in 4 governorates. 78% (7) are ranked in the severity category 5, 1 (11%) category 4 and 1 (11%) category 6. PIN estimates that 5% of the overall targeted caseload will be people with disabilities. Currently, this number is estimated based on the records that exist on the school level. The estimated cost per beneficiary is 216 USD. In 2019, PIN has received overall funding for Education activities in North West Syria for both non-formal education (UNICEF supported, not included in HRP) and formal education in the value of 4,671,049 USD with overall 21,605 beneficiaries. For 2020, currently, an estimated 45% of the request is funded. In NES no activities are funded yet, in NWS, PIN continues fundraising to ensure the schools it has been supporting for several years are funded throughout all 2020, however, PIN has faced funding shortages.
All activities will be implemented in line with relevant Education cluster tools, materials and approaches, in addition to core INEE and Sphere standards, with a focus upon promoting safe learning spaces and
essential WASH facilities and maximizing accessibility for those at risk of education disruption, including girls and children living with disabilities. Teaching and student kits will be provided to facilitate quality learning opportunities and minimise education barriers for children from low-income HH.
Geographical targeting will take place through further in-depth needs assessments to be conducted in areas pre-identified in cooperation with the Education Cluster. These will determine schools and communities most in need of educational and child protection support. The process will ensure the participation of relevant stakeholders to consider local priorities, whilst also ensuring that transparency is at the forefront and that PIN is accountable to the populations in its areas of intervention. Assessments will be conducted by PIN staff and engineers, in collaboration with local authorities, where relevant. PIN will target communities where education services have been disrupted due to conflict, where there is no, or limited, access to education. Experience highlights that in such areas significant gaps also exist to meeting teachers’ and students’ needs for safe and quality learning.
The organization plays an active role within several clusters and working groups across Northern Syria including the Cash Working Group, the Protection working group, and the Education cluster, of which PIN has played a vital role as a member of the Strategic Advisory Group in Gaziantep.

4 - Needs

In NWS, UNOCHA reports that 50% of the 1,200 schools in NWS are functional, meaning that 325,000 school-aged children are out of formal education (OCHA, Sept 2019). The education cluster anticipates that this will rise to 500,000 in early 2020 if vitally needed funds for education do not materialize. PIN’s 2019 needs assessments in NWS highlighted lack of service provision in education as a principal barrier to education. 100% school representatives in Idlib and 60% in West Aleppo stated that school functioning was only possible through NGO support, citing lack of adequate finances for education, including for teachers’ incentives and heating fuel as major problems. Teachers’ capacities to effectively teach was also highlighted, with ‘improving teachers’ ability to teach’ a top 3 priority for education in the 2020 HNO. In NES, Education is the third most commonly reported priority need, with 71% of REACH KIIs reporting that not all children were accessing education services. Taking Raqqa as an example, where 44% of the operational schools have minor damage and 32% have major or severe damage, there are grave concerns that the structural conditions of the schools are placing students and school personnel at risk. This data is supported by PIN’s recent rapid education needs assessments in November 2019 and January 2020 in NES - respondents estimated up to 40% of children in target areas are out of school (Raqqa: 20%, Al Hasakeh city: 20%, Al Hasakeh countryside: 40%) and that between 15-30% of children have been out of school for a significant amount of time, with this percentage increasing to 80% amongst displaced children in camps in Menbij.

5 - Implementation of activities

PIN plans to implement the activities under the 2 following objectives. Objective 1: Scale-up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth. The activities foreseen include – set up of non-formal learning centers, light rehabilitation of existing schools and classrooms, provision of monthly operational costs as well provision of furniture, school supplies and safety equipment for both formal and non-formal settings. Under Objective 2: Enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth within a protective environment, PIN will focus on building the professional skills of teachers through continuous training. Additionally, PIN will provide relevant teaching and learning materials. The integral part of the education support will be the provision of PSS activities, which aim at enhancing skills and coping mechanisms specific to the conflict context and form the basis for the detection of children in need of personalized PSS or specific services. PIN will also try to integrate PSS activities for teachers through peer to peer support groups and other PSS activities. Overall, active case finding of the most vulnerable OOSC boys and girls will be carried out, with integrated and tailored pathways to non-formal and formal education to facilitate access to essential services. PIN will work with school management to identify children at risk of education disruption and provide remedial classes and tailored protection support. Awareness-raising activities in communities will include a specific focus on marginalized groups. PIN will monitor the outcomes of activities by establishing baselines and endlines for both learning and PSS outcomes. PIN will use Annual Status of Education Report (ASER) testing, recommended by the Education cluster to assess children's literacy and numeracy skills. The Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ) will be used to assess children's psychosocial wellbeing.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

As per PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy for 2019-2021, PIN carries out regular protection risk assessments. These protection risk assessments feed into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks (R) and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified:1/R:Specific vulnerable groups are excluded from accessing education;1/MM: accessibility for all genders and children with disabilities; CFRM mechanism, conducting consultations and FGDs to understand possible barriers related to access and safety (such as distance), organizing community and awareness-raising events, referral of children with mobility and non-mobility related disabilities to specialized actors; Conflict Sensitive teacher trainings (disability sensitive classroom for smooth integration of children with disabilities with their peers and reduction of the negative social effects of their injuries, such as stigma and bullying.2/R: Additional threats (child labor, early marriage, recruitment into armed groups)2/MM: targeting communities with different types of assistance; integrating FSL programming carried out in the same target focusing on the most vulnerable HHs to minimize barriers to education, PIN’s referral system and case management, community awareness sessions.3/R: Behavior of parents and education personnel threatening children’s well-being.3/MM: signature of CoC; Child-friendly CFRM is available in all schools and children know how to access it; PSS for teachers and education personnel, trainings on PSEA policy; any breach of the CoC is investigated and results in disciplinary actions, termination; referral system is in place for child protection related cases, engagement of parents and awareness-raising. 4/R: Overstretched physical education infrastructure posing threats to children. 4/MM: all construction in line with INEE standards; first aid training and provision of safety equipment; Advocacy to donors and partners to increase funding.
