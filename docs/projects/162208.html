Project Summary

The project will provide humanitarian response to returnees and refugees people affected by Sudan and Central African Republic crises through the implementation of emergency nutrition interventions for children under five year suffering from severe acute malnutrition with equity in access, and service delivery

Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

Needs: Chad is a low-income and least developed country that relies primarily on oil revenues. It ranks the lowest (157 out of 157 countries) on the Human Capital Index recently developed by the World Bank. In addition to the food and nutrition crises that affect the well-being of children and their families, Chad faces problems related to population displacement due to instability in neighboring countries mainly Sudan crisis (conflict in Darfur), Central African Republic (Politico-Military Instability) and Nigeria (Bokou Haram). To date, about 90,000 Chadians have fled conflicts in Central African Republic to settle in Moyen Chari, Mandoul, Logone Oriental and Logone Occidental provinces. In addition, 340,000 Sudanese in the East and 105,000 Central Africans in the South who have fled their country due to the violence in their respective countries are settled in 18 refugees camp. These populations face many challenges and require humanitarian assistance, especially children under 5 years and women who are most vulnerable. SAM children (both male and female) will be admitted to for treatment regardless their sex. The only acceptable criteria is the nutrition status of the child. Outputs:1.) Procurement and distribution of 11,401 RUTF boxes and others nutrition supplies, materials and equipment to health facilities; 2.) Ensure training and refresher training for 20 community health workers on the prevention and management of SAM; 3.) Provide training to 10 health workers on IYCF practices; 4.) Setup 15 support groups to promote Exclusive breastfeeding and good IYCF practices; 5.) Conduct mass screening and active case finding of acute malnutrition among children under fives in the targeted areas; 6.) Conduct field monitoring visits and joint supervision to health facilities; 7.) Provide technical assistance to front-line staff Results1.) 11,401 admitted cases are treated 2. 10 Community Health workers trained in the prevention and management of SAM, disaggregated by sex and age3.) 4 joint supervisions organized4.) 5,403 caregivers have improved knowledge on nutrition essential nutrition actions 5. 7,681 children screened for SAM
