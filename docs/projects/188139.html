Project Summary

The IRC, one of the largest health actors in NWS, will provide integrated primary, secondary, and community health care services inclusive of lifesaving medical supplies and pharmaceuticals through over 15 health care facilities both through direct implementation and jointly working with implementing partners. This includes the provision of reproductive health (RH), non-communicable disease (NCD), communicable disease (CD), and trauma care. The IRC’s health program will ensure full integration of MHPSS programming, COVID-19 response and adaptability as well as continues training for health care workers. The IRC will continue to provide support with partners the Idlib Surgical hospital. The IRC will support the improvement of health service access and will continue its provision of pharmaceutical and medical supplies. In addition, the IRC will work with its health partners to provide and ensure technical and clinical training and supportive supervision initiatives to improve the overall quality of care provided and skills of health care workers. Topics included in these trainings to improve the overall quality of care include, reproductive health, nutrition, NCD, MHPSS, waste management and infection prevention and control (IPC). The IRC will support and follow the Essential Health Service Package (EHSP) for NW Syria and work to align protocols and guidance along with best practices and global standards. The IRC will also ensure accurate and timely data management and tracking through a robust MEAL and health information management system. The IRC is and will remain an active member of the health cluster and sub-working groups. These services will continue to be provided to IDPs and host communities in Idlib and Aleppo until the end of 2023 with varied scale based on funding availability.

4 - Needs

There are 12.4 M people in need of health assistance and 4.9 million are in need of nutrition support in Syria. Lack of essential medicine and inadequate health care workers is a key barrier to health access, particularly for non-communicable diseases which account for 45% of all deaths. COVID-19 has put additional stress on the health system and health actors to coordinate, prepare and respond to the outbreak while continuing to provide routine primary health care services. Women of reproductive age and children under five make up more than 35% of the conflict affected population and require routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health. In fact, 1 in 3 pregnant women are anemic. Management of NCDs and Mental health conditions is paramount with services are in high demand with an estimated 45% of all mortality in Syria being linked to early onset of NCDs, integration of these services into the primary health care package is key A localized household survey in NWS found crude and under-five mortality rates were below international standards. With just 58% of hospitals and 53 % of Primary Healthcare Centres (PHC) fully functional, the COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated weaknesses in the health system. According to the directorate of Health in Idlib by the beginning of 2022 there will be a huge gap since 18 hospitals have threatened to stop working due to lack of required funds and support through implementing actors.

5 - Implementation of activities

The IRC will deliver integrated primary and secondary health care through its existing presence across Northwest Syria through static and mobile PHCs, Hospitals, and with local partners. The IRC will ensure all services are in line with the essential health service package and WHO guidelines for Syria. Mainstreamed across its programming is COVID-risk mitigation measures and IPC. The IRC will utilize its experience in emergency preparedness to ensure IPC and quality assessments are conducted at the facility level and routine refresher trainings are provided to health staff. The IRC will aim to support 24/7 BEmONC services and work with partner agencies to create a network for BEmONCs centers linked to a CEmONC facilities. Integrated into this component will be CCSAS services for GBV survivors linked with the IRC’s existing protection programming. Provision of life saving pharmaceuticals, PPE, and medical supplies will be provided to all IRC supported facilities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The IRC’s approach to integrated protection programming and protection mainstreaming and integration at the service delivery level in Syria seeks to provide the most vulnerable clients with access to comprehensive, tailored services that adhere to the principle of Do No Harm. To do so, the IRC depends on regular context analysis through stakeholder analyses, updated security assessments, monthly protection monitoring, regular communication with camp and local authorities, information sharing across partners and humanitarian networks; and meaningful participation of affected populations throughout the project cycle. Service interruption of any kind can have negative impacts on clients, but the interruption of protection services can be particularly harmful and destabilizing. The IRC’s mixed mobile and static approach and timely, safe referrals (including internal IRC services), specially GBV prevention and response activities integrated in most of IRC’s supported health facilities, mitigate the risk of an abrupt end to service provision. Protection monitoring allows the IRC to track whether clients are unable to access services, as well as their degree of satisfaction or any concerns associated with the services received. With data gathered on a monthly basis, insights from respondents can be translated into course corrections and program adaptations, and inform IRC’s understanding of unintended impacts of humanitarian service provision more broadly.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse and sexual harassment of women and girls’ clients by staff and volunteers. Likelihood: Medium/Low Impact: Very High Mitigation: IEC materials for beneficiaries explaining reporting channels for beneficiaries to report PSEA and ASH. IRC adheres to a strong internal code of conduct related to prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and safeguarding beneficiaries, especially children, as well as a safe environment for reporting that allows for anonymity. Each year, all IRC staff attend a full day IRC Way Code of Conduct Training that includes discussions on IRC’s commitments to beneficiaries and IRC’s zero tolerance for sexual misconduct. IRC will work with partners utilizing their existing beneficiary mechanisms to establish common standards for beneficiary feedback and ensure protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and accountability to beneficiaries and other stakeholders. This will include capacity building activities with partners to reach these standards, as necessary. The IRC is a member of PSEA networks inside Syria and provides regular updates on the number of staff receiving PSEA training and number of complaints received through feedback and complaint channels


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Clients' confidentiality is being maintained, and their data security and ownership being respected by providing means and training for data protection protocols. Service provider’s safety and security because of their role and potential impacts of secondary/vicarious trauma (eg. PTSD) by providing self-care and build the capacity of supervisors on staff care and wellbeing and maintaining a duty of care policy in place. Lack of quality services and referral expose clients to harm, enhancing the risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma, which can be mitigated by updated service mapping and liaising with coordination forums on new services. Security situation deterioration which may limit IRC and partners ability to respond, which can mitigated by regular context analysis and program adaptation maintaining flexibility to move activities where access allows and ensure teams are ready to shift from static to mobile service provision where needed.
