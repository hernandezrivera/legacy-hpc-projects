Project Summary


Unreliable access to a temporary water source and/or unprotected water is still an important feature of the WASH humanitarian landscape in Somalia. Nationwide, access to an improved water source remains below 40%, with large variations from one region to another, however, lower Juba and Gedo region remains to below 25% each. In most urban and sub-urban hubs and IDP settlements, access to an adequate quantity of water is often granted but water is unsafe and often microbiologically contaminated due to cross-contamination by poorly design water supply and sanitation systems. More than half of the population lack access to adequate sanitation. The situation is particularly critical in lower Juba region is below 35% and in IDP settlements in Gedo region, Hand washing with soap at critical moment is practiced by only a third of the population as access to soap and availability to a functional hand washing point is limited. This percentage drops to below 25% in Jubaland regional state, despite that these two region were badly affected by AWD/Cholera COVID-19 pandemic as well as severe drought. Because of the ﬂoods and the chronical lack of investment in WASH infrastructure, Somalia continued to suffer from AWD/Cholera in 2021 and risk factors for an outbreak persist in several hotspots Gedo and Lower Juba region included. SORDES will continue to focus activities affecting most vulnerable populations in hard to reach locations; IDPs, people at risk of AWD /cholera, acutely malnourished and people in drought-affected areas in Gedo and Lower Juba region. Water is the greatest need in the community and has remained a serious challenge during this period where the communities are experiencing the triple effect of the COVID-19, Locust infestation, conflicts and drought. IDPs are mostly affected by the water problem and as per the SORDES assessed IDPs it indicated that IDPs cannot also afford to buy water since they don’t have an income to sustain this. The sanitation situation in the area is 1 latrine per 500 persons. Many people are living with disabilities inflicted from conflict and are unable to access any water consumption points. Protection concerns among women and PLWD are also at an alarming rate where GBV cases are reported daily during SORDES rapid assessment.
SORDES therefore proposed intervention will provide immediate access to safe drinking water to 14,000 vulnerable population boys, girls, women and men affected by the drought and other natural disaster, while ensuring 15% PLWD, SORDES intends to rehabilitates 10 shallow wells beneﬁting 5000 men, women and children to enable enhanced the community access to safe drinking water. The project will also provide the target beneﬁciaries with hygiene kits as well as provide hygiene promotion to ensure community is aware of the water born risk as well as sanitation and other vector born disease. Through this SORDES will ensure to establish 80 water management committee at IDPs and rehabilitate 10 water sources to enable ease the water related protection risk among boys, girls, women, men and these with disability

1. - Projects activities

1.1. Provide safe and clean water to 2,333 drought/ﬂoods affected households (13,998 persons) through voucher system. Every household will receive 7.5 liter/day as SPHERE standard for 60 days. The selection of the beneﬁciaries will be done with the consultation of community leader 1.2. Rehabilitate 10 shallow wells (8 with hand pump and 2 with animal trough) beneﬁting around 5000 men, women and children. The community leaders including women and PLWD will consulted the design and the location of the shallow wells. 1.3Rehabilitate 5 water pans beneﬁting 2,500 men, women and children including PLWD while also saving animals from the recurrent drought. The action will include: de-silting, increasing the volume of the water pan and protecting its areas from animals and human, in order to avoid the contamination of the water pan. 1.4 Establish and train: (a) 12 water management committees (WMCs) at community and IDP level from the rehabilitated 20 water sources, composed 120 members shared equally by men and women (60 men and 60 women) on water source management and water source maintenance to keep the sustainability of the water source. (b) 12 well operators shared equally men and women (6 men and 6 women) on operation and maintenance (O&M), chlorination of water sources and collection of revenues. (c) 100 community members from community leaders, community elders, women groups, youth and people living with disability shared equally by men and women (50 men and 50 women) on Disaster and Risk Reduction mechanisms (DRR) and conﬂict resolution mechanisms. 1.5 Rehabilitate/Construct 100 gender-sensitive and ﬂood proof IDP latrines (15% reserved PLWD) benefiting a total of 3,000 vulnerable IDPs and host communities. The action includes: desludging of the ﬁlled septic tank, repairing of broken ﬂoors, replacing the damaged walls and doors. 1.6: construct 150 gender-sensitive, desludging and ﬂood proof IDP latrines with hand washing facilities, SORDES propose 10% (15) the latrines constructed is preserved for people with disability, this will ensure inclusion of vulnerable and disadvantage group in the project services delivery 1.7 Conduct comprehensive community hygiene promotion sessions to promote good hygiene and sanitation practices in targeted communities and IDPs in Badhadde,Afmadow district in Lower Juba and Bardheere,Burdhuubo and Garbaharey district in Gedo region

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Location, distance and access routes to water points and services will be accessible to all members of the communities including boys and girls and the most vulnerable IDPs children. To support the inclusion of women into WASH committee, SORDES will support training of female members through employment and capacity enhancement. Building community structures including local water point that will ensure tension is minimized. Staff will be trained on human rights based approach where all people should be treated equally, especially those who ran from conﬂict and violence. Communities ﬂeeing violence will be immediately supported through access to immediate Wash service without any discrimination. Where IDPs are involved, sites that support them will be built in consultation with them and the host communities. Marginalized groups will be prioritized so that they don’t feel excluded, which can otherwise aggravate their situation if they have run from violenceThere are protection concerns on at risk group specially the women and girls on the issues of gender violances/rape , SORDES will ensure that the project works in closer coordination with protection actors in lower juba and Gedo region target district and hence strengthen the referral pathways while also ensuring that the affected individual received enough access to psychosocial and mental health care services The project target group might have minority group and these might be discriminated by the majority people however, SORDES will work in close consultation with both the minority and majority communities while ensuring to in inclusive manner where all target vulnerable population including the disable own the program and participate in its development stage as well as during implementation
