Project Summary

This proposal outlines an intervention strategy aimed at achieving nutritional self-sufficiency for People with Specific Needs with a particular focus on individuals impacted by disabilities, chronic illnesses, elderly, children and those headed by single parents in the Maratane Refugee Resettlement in Nampula Province, Mozambique. The approach known as Perma garden will empower PSNs to improve their lives through sustainable practices in nutrition, food security and resource management. 1.Objective of the intervention: To enhance food security, nutritional health, and community resilience among PSNs by establishing sustainable gardens that provide fresh produce and promote economic self-reliance. 2. Key Actions: 2.1 Establishing Permagardens: (Develop community gardens that supply a steady source of vegetables and herbs, significantly improving dietary diversity while fostering food self-reliance and reducing dependency on food aid . 2.2. Empowering Participants: (Offer hands-on training on gardening techniques, soil health, crop rotation, and organic farming to improve participants' skills and confidence in managing their gardens). 2.3. Surplus Management (Equip individuals with the knowledge to sell surplus produce, facilitating local food markets or direct sales within the camp).2.4. Sustainability (Ensure that participants understand and practice sustainable gardening methods that can be maintained in the long term. 3. Expected Outcomes. (i) Increased access to fresh, nutritious food for displaced families. (ii) Enhanced economic self-reliance through the sale of surplus produce. (iii) Increased knowledge of sustainable farming practices, improving long-term food security. (iv) Strengthened Community Resilience collaboration and community-building, (v) Improved Health and Wellbeing through access to fresh produce will have direct health benefits, reducing malnutrition and improving the overall physical wellbeing of the displace population . 4. Targets. 250 Refugee/Households (100% with people with disabilities). 450 Women, benefiting from education on gardening techniques and food security. 150 Youth (60% girls and 40% boys), engaged in permagarden initiatives.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

All the activities will take place in the Maratane Refuge camp in Nampula. the priority activities are the following : 1.1. Establishing Permagardens (i) Develop community gardens that supply a steady source of vegetables and herbs, significantly improving dietary diversity and reducing reliance on food aid.(ii) Use low-cost gardening methods and sustainable techniques, suitable for small-scale gardens and for the camp's environmental conditions such as vertical gardening, hydroponics, container gardening, raised bed gardens, especially for those with mobility challenges. (iii) Prioritize high-nutrient, climate-resilient, and easy-to-grow crops that meet both food security and dietary needs of PSNs. 1.2.Training and Empowering Participants (i) Organize training sessions for PSNs, their families, care givers and community members on sustainable gardening practices, soil management, pest control, irrigation, and crop selection. (ii) Offer hands-on training on gardening techniques for PSNs with physical disabilities or other health challenges to ensure they can participate in the gardening activities and to improve participants' skills and confidence in managing their gardens.(iIi) Educate participants about nutrition and the importance of a balanced diet, providing information on how to maximize garden yields for healthier meals (iv).. 1.3.Economic Opportunities and Surplus Management: (i) Establish linkages with local markets, traders, or cooperatives where PSNs can sell their surplus produce. (ii) For those interested in generating income, provide training and equip them with knowledge in marketing, small business management, and the basics of selling garden produce. This can help PSNs become economically independent through selling surplus produce in local markets or direct sales within the camp, contributing to micro-enterprise development and economic self-sufficiency (iii) Encourage value-added products, such as homemade jams, dried vegetables, or processed herbs, which can be sold for higher prices and reduce food waste. Provide training on basic food processing techniques. (iv) Explore collaborations with local markets or organizations to connect small producers with potential buyers, fostering economic inclusion. 1.4. Health Screening and Monitoring (i) Work with health organizations to monitor the health and nutritional status of PSNs before and after the establishment of the gardens. This can include regular visits from health workers to assess the impact of improved nutrition on health. (ii) Dietary Plans and Meal Integration, into regular meals for PSNs and their families. This can include providing specific dietary plans for PSNs with special dietary needs, such as those with chronic illnesses or malnutrition.1.5.Sustainability and Long-Term Impact.(i)Ensure that participants understand and practice sustainable gardening methods that can be maintained in the long term, improving the environmental impact of the project. (ii) Promote the use of locally available resources (e.g., compost, water harvesting) to increase the sustainability of the gardens . (iii) Promote the use of organic waste (e.g., from food scraps) in composting to reduce waste and provide natural fertilizers for the garden. Teach PSNs how to recycle materials for garden use, such as plastic bottles for irrigation or seedling containers.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will use several tools and approaches to identify protection risks for refugees in the camp, especially those who are most vulnerable, including, physical harm, sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), exploitation, discrimination, and psychosocial harm. Protection risks may arise from the implementation of activities if proper safeguards and preventive measures are not in place. The project will identify these risks through: Community consultations and feedback mechanisms: The project will engage with community members, including vulnerable groups (such as women, children, elderly, and people with disabilities), to gather feedback on any risks they perceive in the community. This will be done through focus group discussions, surveys, and community consultations that highlight safety concerns and protection risks. Vulnerability and risk profiling .Profiling individuals and households at risk (e.g., unaccompanied minors, female-headed households, persons with disabilities) will help the project team understand where particular protection risks exist. This profiling will also include identifying individuals with previous protection concerns (such as survivors of SGBV, individuals with trauma histories, and those living in isolation), so that they can be offered targeted support. To ensure that the project will implement HI's "Do No Harm" policy, and take deliberate actions to prevent harm and ensure that protection risks are minimized trough the following : Anti-exploitation safeguards: Based on internal policies , the project will establish strict safeguarding protocols to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). This will include: Clear code of conduct for all project staff and partners, with explicit clauses against sexual exploitation and harassment. Reporting mechanisms that are confidential and accessible to all community members, especially vulnerable groups, to report any incidents of abuse or exploitation.Training for staff and community leaders on protecting survivors of violence and maintaining safe environments.The project will actively work to avoid gender-based harm. This will include (among others the following): Ensuring that both men and women have equal access to training, resources, and opportunities. Creating mechanisms that ensure women’s participation in decision-making processes and in the design of the permagarden activities .Targeting gender-based violence prevention through awareness-raising sessions, discussions, and social support for survivors of GBV.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The project is designed to ensure meaningful access and empowerment for women and elderly persons by providing them with the tools, knowledge, and opportunities to actively participate in economic and social life within the Maratane Refugee Camp. Through inclusive training, accessible resources, leadership opportunities, and the creation of safe and supportive spaces, the project fosters gender equality, social inclusion, and intergenerational collaboration. This approach ensures that these groups are not only beneficiaries of aid but also empowered agents of change in their communities. 1.Women will be actively included in all phases of the project, from planning to implementation and decision-making. In particular, women will be encouraged to lead community gardening groups . 2. The project will ensure that women have equal access to training, resources, and leadership opportunities, breaking down the barriers that typically limit their involvement in socio-economic activities. 3.The project will also create opportunities for women to take on leadership roles, such as managing gardens, coordinating community-based activities, or acting as ambassadors for sustainable agricultural practices. By promoting leadership within these spaces, the project will empower women to make decisions that affect their livelihoods, both within the camp and in broader community contexts. 4.Elderly persons will be included in social networks and support groups within the camp, promoting a sense of belonging and community. They will be encouraged to share their knowledge and wisdom, particularly in areas like traditional farming practices or storytelling, where they can serve as mentors to younger generations. This participation not only contributes to their sense of purpose but also helps preserve cultural practices. 5.The project promotes participatory decision-making, where all stakeholders—including women and elderly persons—are actively involved in decisions about the project’s implementation. By involving these groups in decisions about garden management,, and other key aspects of the project, their voices will be heard, ensuring that their unique needs and perspectives are addressed. 6. also for elderly participants who may be physically frail or otherwise unable to participate in traditional activities, the project will offer customized support, such as access to nutritional food packs, caregiver support, or assistance with selling produce. This ensures that they are still able to benefit from the project, even if they cannot fully engage in its more labor-intensive aspects.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Humanity and Inclusion has been actively working in the Maratane Refugee Camp since 2023, with a core focus on supporting the inclusion and meaningful participation of the most vulnerable groups, including people with disabilities, in all aspects of socio-economic life. This project, Pathway to Nutritional and Economic Sustainability through Permagarden , is designed to ensure that people with disabilities are identified and have equal access to the assistance and resources provided. By removing barriers to participation, the project ensures that people with disabilities are not excluded from benefiting from the interventions and are empowered to take part in improving their own lives and the community as a whole.To ensure that people with disabilities are identified and included in the project, Humanity and Inclusion applies internationally recognized tools for the identification and assessment of disabilities. 1.Washington Group Short Set of Questions: widely used tool for identifying people with disabilities. It asks a series of questions designed to assess a person’s level of difficulty in performing basic activities of daily living (such as seeing, hearing, walking, self-care, etc.). using this set of questions its possible to capture a wide range of disabilities , ensuring that no one with disabilities is overlooked in the community and enables the team to understand the types of disabilities in the camp and identify which individuals require additional assistance or adjustments in the project. 2.Child Functioning Model, which assesses the child’s ability to perform daily activities and their participation in social, educational, and recreational activities. This model helps to ensure that children with disabilities are not left behind, that their needs are considered within the broader scope of the project and that tailored support is provided based on functional abilities and age-specific requirements. Once people with disabilities are identified, the project takes proactive steps to remove barriers to their access to assistance, ensuring their full participation in all activities.The project ensures that all activities, especially those related to the permagardens, are physically accessible to people with disabilities. This includes modifying gardens and workspaces to accommodate wheelchair users, the elderly, and those with mobility impairments. Raised garden beds, adaptive tools, and accessible pathways will be created to ensure that individuals with disabilities can engage in gardening activities. For individuals with hearing or vision impairments, the project will provide sensory-friendly support, including provision of sign language interpreters or audio-based instructions. To ensure meaningful participation, of people with disabilities the project will continue its efforts to raise awareness among the broader community emphasizing the rights and abilities of people with disabilities and the importance of inclusion.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The introduction of new agricultural practices, such as permagardens, may place undue pressure on local resources, such as water, soil, and land. Additionally, improper waste management or overuse of natural resources could degrade the environment. in order to mitigate from this environmental risk the project will ; (i) Train participants on sustainable farming techniques, such as composting, rainwater harvesting, and organic pest control, to reduce the environmental impact. (ii) Participants will also receive training on the importance of environmental conservation, teaching them how to balance food production with the protection of local ecosystems.
