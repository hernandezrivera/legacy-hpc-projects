Project Summary

This project plan is designed to respond to the persistent Health Insecurities in six (6) of the Counties experiencing either natural or man-made disasters; floods, subnational violence and armed conflict. Severe flooding entered the third consecutive year May through December 2021 in South Sudan. The selected Counties under this plan are; Canal Pigi, Akobo, Ayod, Tonj North, Mayendit and Kapoeta East.
Health Action Africa (HAA) works in the above Counties since 2017 and therefore developed this 2022 respond plan to support mobilization of resource for her continuation of Humanitarian Assistance to the Populations described in the Humanitarian needs overview (HNO_Feb_ 2022) face severity level 4&5; acute emergencies and crisis1
This 2022 project plan proposed to support thirty (30) Mobile clinics equitably distributed 5 mobile clinics in each County; Canal Pigi, Akobo, Ayod, Tonj North, Mayendit and Kapoeta East. This will improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services to 52,983 beneficiaries.

Problem statement: The report OCHA Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO_Feb_2022) indicate, the key triggers of the catastrophes are the dual phenomena of extraordinary flooding and localized drought, which have a severe impact on people’s livelihoods, hampers access to social services including Health care, education and water, sanitation and hygiene services. The situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference2
The OCHA report reaffirmed the findings of the Inter-Agency Needs Assessment (IRNA) report 3rd- 5th Dec 2021 Canal Pigi County that flagged the deteriorating Humanitarian crisis triggered by floods resulting in an upsurge WASH induced diseases. Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea, Acute Respiratory Infections, Skin infections, and trauma injuries on account of either snake bites, Accidents, and Gender-Based Violence (GBV).
Equally,The Findings from the Sept_ IRNA _2021 Akobo County reports indicated that most of the schools, Health facilities, and stabilization centers/nutrition centers were submerged in floods. This has affected the routine service delivery capacities of these facilities. An (IRNA Report Aug 2021 Tonj North) indicates, there is wide spread inter-communal violence and most health facilities are dysfunctional. The widespread displacement in Kapoeta East is largely attributed to drought and disease upsurge3. The summation of the above problem statement indicates, the already weak health system is deteriorating due to the collapsing health infrastructure and increasing prevalence of communicable and non-communicable diseases.
These acute needs will be addressed through Mobile clinics. The Key activities are;
provision of essential medical supplies and establishment of mobile clinics, conduct Outpatient consultations (OPDs) and treatments of common diseases such as malaria, acute watery diarrhea (AWD), PENTA 3 /routine vaccination services, mental Health, clinical management of rape (CMR), supply of essential medical products; drugs and Vaccines, Skilled deliveries. Basic emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMONC, Health Promotion/risks communication, awareness campaigns for COVID 19, EVD, Cholera, surveillance , supportive supervision community consultation & feedback
The Project activities are aligned to Health cluster CLHEA/CO1; CLHEA/CO2 & CLHEA/CO3; improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO_Feb_2022) OCHA reaffirmed the deteriorating humanitarian conditions in the Counties; Canal Pigi, Akobo, Ayod, Tonj North, Mayendit and Kapoeta East. Severe flooding entered the third consecutive year and in May through December 2021, the floods caused dire humanitarian needs. The situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints and operational interference, public health challenges such as direct and indirect effects of COVID-19 Pandemic1Literature review of assessment reports indicate, due to compounding shocks, both in areas of displacement and return, populations have been forced to keep displacing time and again. The Inter-Agency Needs Assessment (IRNA) report 3rd- 5th Dec 2021 Canal Pigi County flagged out the deteriorating Humanitarian situation triggered by floods that resulted into an upsurge of WASH induced diseases; Malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea, Acute Respiratory Infections, Skin infections, and trauma injuries on account of either snake bites, Accidents, and Gender-Based Violence (GBV). The semi-permanent health infrastructures collapsed as such the static health facilities are dysfunctional. In the Month of Dec 2021, 5 maternal and 2 neonatal deaths were reported. There is limited access to healthcare services2 Equally, Akobo County was severely devastated by the recurrent floods that lasted for 3 consecutive years; 2019 through Dec 2021. The Findings from the Sept_ IRNA _2021 Akobo County reports indicated that most of the schools, Health facilities, and stabilization centers/nutrition centers were submerged in floods. This has affected the routine service delivery capacities of these facilities. There is frequent stock out of essential medical supplies. Cattle raids and inter-communal violence are the common compounding shocks in Akobo. This resulted to high trauma induced injuries across Akobo3Ayod County is worst devastated by both armed conflict since 2016 and recurrent floods. The limited health care services is only accessible at the County Headquarters. There is widespread communication barrier and most of the communities fled to the remote hard to reach areas in fear of armed insurgencies4. An (IRNA Report Aug 2021 Tonj North) indicate, there is wide spread inter-communal violence and most health facilities are dysfunctional. HAA supports the communities with four (4) mobile health care services/Mobile clinics. The widespread displacement in Kapoeta East is largely attributed to drought and disease upsurge3. This 2022 project plan proposed to support thirty (30) Mobile clinics. HAA intends to work in 5 mobile clinics in each of the six counties; Canal Pigi, Akobo, Ayod, Tonj North, Mayendit and Kapoeta East. The intervention will improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV. There will be effective coordination with the relevant stakeholders and active surveillance, supportive supervision, community consultation and feedback, routine vaccination, Basic emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMONC) services and supply of essential medical commodities across the six counties. Health services to crisis affected populations aligned to Health cluster CLHEA/CO1; CLHEA/CO2 & CLHEA/CO3. This will reduce morbidity and mortality from epidemic prone diseases and improve health outcome.The plan target to reach 52,983 beneficiaries with lifesaving heath care services. 2 - Project activities

Mobilization and coordination of essential medical supplies /Commodities from core pipe managers for the mobile clinics. IEHK Kits from WHO, Reproductive Health Kits from UNFPA, ACTs from NMCP-MoH and Vaccines antigens from UNICEF through the County Health Departments (CHDs) Outpatient consultations (OPD) and treatment for common disease. There will be OPD consultations for both communicable and non-communicable diseases in all the mobile clinics for common diseases; malaria, Acute Watery Diarrhea, Respiratory Tract Infections (RTIs), Skin infestations, congenital birth defects other epidemic prone diseases .This will reduce the case fatality ratesConduct skilled deliveries; prior to assisted deliveries, mothers will be provided with Ante- and postnatal care services /check-ups, Vaccination against Tetanus for pregnant women (if not received). HAA will conduct skilled deliveries in the health care centers; Maternal & newborn health; Clean and safe normal delivery, Essential newborn care: basic newborn resuscitation + warmth + eye, prophylaxis; Basic emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMONC), manual vacuum aspiration (MVA), post abortion care (PAC).Reproductive Health services; provision of prenatal and postnatal education sessions to pregnant and lactating mothers, examine expectant mothers during pregnancy, educate patients on health, nutrition, and provide emotional care for mothers during birth, perform deliveries or make referrals, care for mothers and newborns after birth, and distribute Mama kits to third-trimester mothers (PLW) and above all family planning PENTA 3 Coverage for under one year; Conduct routine vaccination with all the antigens to children according to the formulary guidelines Ministry of Health (MoH)-South Sudan. This will be complemented by Vitamin A supplementation to children (6-59) Months and deworming in areas with high worm load. HAA mobile teams will participate in the planning and implementation of the oral cholera vaccines (OCV). Conduct effective case management incase outbreaks are detected for epidemic-prone diseases (Meningitis, Measles, polio and subsequent referral where necessary. This intervention will improve the expanded program on immunization (EPI) service coverage thereby reducing morbidity and mortality from vaccines preventable diseases. Clinical management of rape (CMR); HAA will dispense services for Expanded Management of sexual an Gender Based violence (SGBV) survivors (PEP, treatment for STI, wound care, emergency contraceptive pills , prevention care for tetanus, Hepatitis B & E, clinical counselling and referrals) and Application of the initial response for HIV/AIDS patients. Mental Health services; provide first line management of mental health services, trauma management, psycho-social support therapy and subsequent referrals Infection Prevention and Control (IP&C). HAA will assess the health facilities for IP&C and ensure compliance during project implementation. Install hand washing stations in health facilities, provide re-usable face masks to project staff and further conduct Risk communication and Community engagement; awareness for COVID 19, EVD, cholera and other diseases of public Health concerns. .Supportive supervision to health facilities. HAA will conduct quarterly supervision to all the health facilities in the project. Surveillance: HAA will establish sentinel sites ; Early Warning, Alert reporting (EWARS) and Integrated Disease Surveillance Reporting (IDSR) for monitoring and reporting the public health information at both community and facility level. Community consultation during project designs, implementation monitoring and reporting. A local community feedback mechanism/ community score cards will be used for effective feedback communications. Conduct effective coordination with the relevant stakeholders; SMoH at state level, CHD at county level, local authorities, health cluster and other humanitarian agencies

3 - Project indicators

Baseline for Project Monitoring: The project log frame and work plan constitute the key baseline tools under which the progress monitoring of the indicators will be examined. Ministry of Health (MoH)-South Sudan requires all health interventions monitored and evaluated using harmonized tools recommended or Adopted from the MoH to ensure data requirement fits into the National Health Management information system (HMIS (Health Management Information System) /DHMIS2-MoH. Tools for the project monitoring: The Health Cluster targets will be monitored using the cluster 5Ws spread sheet for activity, beneficiaries reached and service functionality of the Health facilities. This data is extracted from the patients Registers. The Ante-post natal cards and the MCH registry log will be used for monitoring the percentage of the skilled deliveries conducted or assisted. This data will be keyed the 5ws and into the DHIS2. The Health cluster essential community request forms, stock consumption reports and distribution checklists will be used for monitoring the number of health facilities reached with essential IEHK supplies. The number of outpatient consultations will be monitored with the use of daily patients’ registers, 5Ws and the DHIS2. This tools will also monitor number of patients receiving mental Health and psycho-social support service (MHPSS).Facilities assessed for infection prevention and control (IP&C) will be monitored using the Health cluster 5Ws, public health situation analysis (PHSA) forms, quantitative supervision checklists and the field monitoring visits reports. The PENTA3 in under one year by administrative area will be monitored by the use of immunization cards, EPI monthly reports, 5Ws and the DHIS2. The SGBV survivors and the disability patients will equally be monitored using the line lists in the patients’ registers and the 5ws. Surveillance: weekly Integrated Disease surveillance reporting (IDSR) /Early Warning Alert Reporting (EWARS) forms, Stock cards and Health facility supervision logs for gathering daily data from both the community and the health facilities. Monthly HMIS Tools; The Monthly Report Registers are the main forms. This will be complemented by the 5Ws spread sheet from the Health cluster, stock consumption forms, program supplies and distribution Agreement (PSDA) Spreadsheet from UNFPA for gathering specific data on reproductive health services and consumption of their supplies to fit into their donor requirements. Monthly Drug Consumption Reports. While Medical supplies for this project comes from different core pipeline managers, the tools from WHO through the Health cluster shall also be used for gathering data and reporting on biweekly and Monthly bases as desired by the respective core Pipeline Managers. These tools are the 5Ws spreadsheet, service functionality spreadsheet and the Monthly drug consumption spreadsheets. HAA will also continue to use the program supplies and distribution Agreement (PSDA) Spreadsheet from UNFPA for gathering specific data on reproductive health services and consumption of their supplies to fit into their donor requirements. Quarterly tools for Project monitoring: Quantitative supervision checklists (QSCls) are standard forms used for collecting service functionality and Health Information every three Months period. There will also be complemented by the interim project reports and the UN OCHA finance tracking system (FTS).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Feedback and response: HAA will adopt the tools from the Community engagement and Community working group for effective feedback mechanism. These tested tools are the community score cards. The Community scorecards will moderate reviews, allowing people of concern to provide formal and informal feedback. Information will be received and responded to systematically, taking corrective action as needed. These mechanism allows persons with special needs; disabilities, low literacy or no literacy background; women and girls to inclusively participates in project design, implementation, participatory progress monitoring, Health Action Africa (HAA) will maintain effective feedback systems (including comments, suggestions, and complaints), using a variety of communication channels accessible to all persons of concern and appropriate for both sensitive and non-sensitive feedback. HAA will allocate human and financial resources to ensure that input from persons of concern is systematically collected, acknowledged, assessed, referred, and responded to in a timely, confidential, and effective manner and collaborate with partners in feedback referral and response processes (wherever appropriate).

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA (Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse), Do No Harm Policy: Health Action Africa (HAA) has adopted the PSEA policy from the UN (United Nations) core humanitarian Standards. The HAA management will ensure project staff receive a PSEA refresher training to ensure compliance with the protection of anyone, whether direct or indirect beneficiaries, while protecting project staff from all forms of inappropriate sexual conduct. The HAA Human Resource officer will conduct this refresher training. The summarized PSEA code of conduct will be displayed on stand banners in all offices or project locations and during project implementations, refresher trainings will be complemented by creating awareness every month to ensure all staff, representatives, or third parties connected with HAA are aware of the lofty standards of behavior and conduct expected of them to protect adults or children from any form of sexual abuse and exploitation Dissemination of PSEA Key zero-tolerance messages to project staff: The key messages include: exchanging money, goods, services, or employment for sex, including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading, or exploitative behavior use of child or adult to procure sex for others. Any actual or attempted abuse of a position of vulnerability, differential power, trust, or dependency for sexual or sexualized purposes. These include offering or promising monetary and social benefits as an incentive or form of coercion. HAA will be strict on discouraging the cultivation of emotional relationships with those in positions of vulnerability or inequitable power to manipulate these relationships into sexualized dynamics in the future Prevention: through awareness and good practice, ensuring that all staff minimize the risks of any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. PSEA Reporting: All project staff will be familiarized with the reporting mechanism for PSEA to ensure that they are clear on the steps to take where suspicions or concerns arise regarding allegations of Sexual Exploitation or abuse of adults in vulnerable populations. HAA will take immediate action /response to identify and address reports of sexual exploitation and abuse and ensure the safety and well-being of the sexually exploited or abused.
