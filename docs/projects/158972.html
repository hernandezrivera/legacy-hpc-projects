Project Summary

The project will directly support Food Security Cluster objective 1: Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced through enabling 900 small farmers affected by the conflict in the Gaza strip to restore their effective production. This will be achieved through rehabilitation of small, open-land farms (including leveling, provision of essential agricultural inputs and irrigation system, Installation) as well as equipping of five agricultural wells with solar energy units. The project will provide extension services to the target farmers which will help 240 farmers manage the agricultural resources and thus improve the quality of crops and increase the profitability of farming. This project will help decrease both levels of poverty and food insecurity. The project will also address the environmental degradation through improving farming practices.

1 - Needs

The ongoing Israeli blockade, electricity crisis and the instable political and security situation continue to prevent access to livelihood opportunities and limit the free movement of people, goods, and services. This has also decimated the local economy, public infrastructure and eroded self-reliance and households’ living standards. The high level of unemployment (46.7 % FSS HNO 2020) is generating widespread food insecurity. According to PCBS, youth (19- 29 years) and women are the most affected, as they have fewer opportunities in the labour market with even higher unemployment at 70% and 78% respectively. An estimated 62% of HHs are severely or moderately food insecure. The increased costs and poor productivity pose obstacles to any improvement in food security in Gaza Strip. The resources for repair and improvements are scarce, land quality has deteriorated, and water depletion and water salinity levels are affecting production. Farmers lack sufficient knowledge of modern agricultural techniques and environmental risks connected to excessive use of chemical pesticides and fertilizers. Practices are negatively affecting farmers’ health, soil, and water. As the cost of agriculture inputs has risen, farmers’ income has dropped. Limitations of water for irrigation are a main concern for vulnerable farmers near and within ARAs. The chronic energy deficit has placed additional pressure on farmers who already experience an increase in agricultural input costs. The electricity outage leads to shortage of water for irrigation and irregular irrigation, which negatively affects the agriculture production. The escalation of the public sector salary crisis weakened the purchasing power and increased the recession in the local markets, mainly small-businesses including farmers who have to make additional marketing efforts and explore new markets to sell their products. In September 2019, SCI conducted socio-economic assessments in eastern Khan Younis villages and North Gaza. The results showed continued deterioration of household average income for the surveyed from 1,595 /HH to 495 NIS/month in eastern Khan Younis and 618 NIS/month for farmers in North Gaza. Continued decline in farmers` income will result in stopping cultivation and deepen food insecurity. Through the Do No Harm Assessment which was conducted by SC in 2018 in 6 localities in northern and eastern areas of Gaza strip, the field data collection activities and analysis indicated that female youth, in both target areas in the North and the South, feel as being somehow excluded from benefiting of several Aid and Humanitarian projects in the area. This had created negative feelings, as they share the same vulnerability criteria in terms of poverty and lack of opportunities with the other segments and groups of the society. The results of multi-sectoral assessment conducted by SCI- WFP-UNICEF in 2018 in 9 communities (8 of them in eastern and northern Gaza strip) showed that the poor Food Consumption scoring is worsening (23%) compared to national results in Gaza Strip (SEFSec 2016) where the poor FCS was (7%). The results also showed that 71% of surveyed HHs are reducing the number and quality of meals to cope with the crisis. The assessment revealed the impact of poverty on women and children .The findings indicates that the nutritional status of pregnant and lactating women is poor, with 18% of pregnant women and 14% of lactating mothers malnourished. This calls for urgent attention and possibly some support to some nutritional programming. More than 30% of pregnant and lactating women suffering from anemia as well. Agricultural livelihood needs differ between men and women in the workforce in Gaza. Women face challenges in accessing livelihoods and the labour market is influenced by the fact that women’s economic empowerment is limited by their share of agricultural holdings, and their restricted access to services and resources.

2 - Activities

The project is designed to be in line with FSS HRP 2020 Top Humanitarian Priorities. The project will directly enhance income generation and food security for 5,700 (2,800 F, 2,900 M) people in eight most vulnerable agricultural communities through rehabilitation of agriculture assets, improving access to irrigation, and improving agriculture knowledge and practices for small farmers in the target areas. The proposed project will complement current SCI agriculture interventions which are being implemented with the same partners in eastern Khan Younis and north Gaza. The project partners will coordinate with MOA and FSS to ensure the quality of work and the integration with other actors to increase the project impact and sustainability. Key activities will include: 1) 900 farmers (Attention will be given to include female farmers using gender-sensitive interventions, with the aim of including at least 150 female managed farms) will benefit from the rehabilitation of 1,200 dunums of open agricultural land. The work will include leveling, soil rehabilitation and enrichment (mixing sandy soil with clay to reduce water infiltration and improve the productivity), provision of essential assets and organic fertilizer, seeds and seedlings and installation of new irrigation system. The implementing partners will coordinate with Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), FSS and other stakeholder to provide extension services for the target farmers to ensure good agricultural practices and sustainability. 2) Five agricultural wells will be equipped with solar energy unit to improve access to irrigation for 240 farmers (including 40 female farmers). Implementing partners will hire qualified consultation firm to design and supervise the implementation of solar units. Beneficiaries will be selected based on SCI’s social data and poverty assessment. Household outreach visits will take place to identify beneficiaries for participation. Group consultations will ensure community participation in beneficiary selection and identification of needs.To ensure integration with other livelihood interventions and to avoid overlapping, SC will keep close coordination with FSS including reporting the progress of work, cross –check the potential beneficiaries lists. Although traditionally most farmers are male, but attention will be paid to targeting farms that employ women. SCI has developed this intervention in collaboration with the target intervention to ensure meeting local needs and priorities among most vulnerable communities in the Gaza strip. Acknowledging that humanitarian assistance, particularly actions that target women can increase risks for beneficiaries and staff from non-participation to violence, SCI and partners will regularly track project progress, safety and quality through gender-sensitive feedback mechanisms including focus group discussions. SCI’s partners have been selected based on previous joint project implementation experience with these partners successfully implementing various donor-funded projects of a similar budget value (as proposed here). They are current operational partners of SCI with well-established systems and adherence to SCI’s partnership framework and principles including having passed a comprehensive partner assessment and various donor audits. The intervention will be integrated with SCI’s current livelihood projects. The intervention will be built on the understanding of gender-specific needs and expectations as well as socio-economic factors and cultural sensitivities.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

In December 2017, SCI has conducted a “Do no harm” (DNH) assessment in Eastern Khan Younis and North Gaza. The results showed that there are socio- cultural traditions and dividers in some localities which prevent and undermine women and children’s roles and empowerment. Female youth, and due to social and cultural restrictions on their movement, feel excluded from benefiting from humanitarian assistance and income generation opportunities although they face the same vulnerabilities and poverty as others living nearby. Special efforts will be made to ensure that the interventions are sensitive to the needs of the female youth.• SCI will ensure “Do-no-harm” through our project intervention in terms of mitigation of potential social tensions in communities resulting from community members feeling excluded.• Interventions are based on needs assessments and transparent selection criteria. Appropriate targeting of beneficiaries will be the result of quality assessment of design and planning processes, coordinated through regular meetings with the partners and communities during the course of the implementation.In order to reduce tension or conflict of such socially deprived groups, the program is to provide equal access to all segments, enabling them through community consultation and engagement to increase their control and influence on community public issues and priorities. This is essential for have more peaceful communities, avoiding any undesired impact of the programs.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

• To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to the services provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will be conducted at all project stages with women, men, as well as elders and people with disabilities who will benefit from project through their families. This will enable communities to raise their concerns and together come up with solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create new risks. Additionally, an accountability mechanism will be made available to the public in the form of a hotline as well as a complain box at the CBOs in order for girls, boys, women and men to provide feedback on the applications, selection process or interventions, in the event that someone feels the process is not fair or transparent.
