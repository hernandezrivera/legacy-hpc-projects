Project Summary

The goal of the project is to improve food security, nutrition and resilience of vulnerable people, especially women, youth and persons with disabilities (PWDs) from the refugees and host communities. This is intended to achieve with a landscape perspective, acknowledging that community based natural resource management and long-term socio-ecological system restoration, creates the foundation for socio-economic development of the vulnerable communities.
The project aims to provide the Rohingya refugees with portable skills and knowledge on homestead gardening and accessibility to nutritious diversified food. FAO will provide technical assistance and input support to the 4,000 Rohingya households (i.e. 19,340 individuals) in achieving the desired goal that will capacitate and equip them with relevant transferable technological competencies for sustainable integration into the economy upon their return to Myanmar.
The programme will use conflict sensitive approaches to build capacity with consensus on sustainable use and management of natural resources with communities while safeguarding the ecosystem. This will be achieved through interventions such as forest restoration and watershed management, which are interlinked with agricultural farming practices for sustainable socio-economic improvement. . FAO will also promote disaster risk preparedness and reduction, through scaling-up piloted early warning system for landslides and conducting land stabilization and reforestation activities. Cash for work modality will be applied to implement these reforestation and forest restoration activities.
The host community people will have increased availability, and accessibility to nutritious foods; adopt climate-smart agricultural practices and improved and sustainable household income and resilience to shocks. FAO will provide input support to the host communities in practicing climate-smart homestead vegetable gardening production for year-round households’ consumption and improved nutrition.
FAO will further focus on marketing system approaches to ensure sustainability and adaptation of methodologies for the targeted beneficiaries. To ensure market linkages, income generation and increased productivity, FAO will support 4,000 farming households comprised of 200 farmer groups in practicing collective actions through ten aggregation centers in Cox’s Bazar.
FAO will provide technical support to improve the operational capacities of the aggregation centers to establish vertical integration of the smallholder farmers with the traders and retailers in accessing local market.
To assist smallholder farmers in increasing their farm productivity, a digital financial solution model called A-Card (‘A’ stands for Agriculture) will be piloted to solve the demand-supply gap in the capital market by offering a win-win partnership. FAO will also pilot an area-specific crop insurance programme for smallholder farmers that will include a method for yield measurement by using crop cutting; homogeneous and rain-fed or irrigated cropping system; similar varieties; and adopting improved farming practices through FAO Farmer Field School (FFS) approach. This will include GIS-based mapping tools for area zoning and Agricultural Hazard Vulnerability Analysis Mapping (AHVAM) land use planning for the selection of crop varieties, cropping season, and potential risks of different hazards.
This project will support the school feeding programme implemented by WFP and the Directorate of Primary Education (DPE) with value chain interventions, for ensuring the supply of nutritious vegetables.
FAO will pilot efficient and environment friendly irrigation systems i.e. “Solar charging station with battery operated portable water pump irrigation system”. The proposed innovative technology is new in the region and the solar power charging station will be installed at the FAO-supported Aggregation Center’s premises for charging the batteries.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar, a disaster-prone district in south-eastern Bangladesh, with a population of 2.65 million people, hosts the world’s largest refugee community. Over 900,000 Rohingya refugees reside across 33 congested camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf sub-districts. The geographical context of Cox’s Bazar coupled with human pressure and the exploitation of natural resources makes this district vulnerable to natural hazards. As per the Global Forest Watch report 2021, between 2010 and 2020 Cox’s Bazar lost almost 500 hectares of tree cover, equivalent to over 250 kilotonnes of CO₂ emissions and the total area of humid primary forest decreased by 30 percent. Critical biodiversity areas, such as the Teknaf Wildlife Sanctuary (11 615 ha), Himchari National Park (1 729 ha), and Inani Protected Area (7 700 ha) are also at risk of degradation and over-exploitation.The study of Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) on chronic food insecurity (CFI) analysis of Bangladesh observed that in Cox’s Bazar, a significant portion (70%) of the people are food insecure either mild, moderate, or severe. Among them, 25% are moderately and at least 10% people are severely food insecure (CFI, 2022 ).The FAO/WFP conducted the Resilience Index Measurement Analysis (RIMA) study showed that about half of the Rohingya households were found with acceptable food consumption patterns. Among the remaining half, 44 percent have borderline consumption. The male-headed households are experiencing a better consumption pattern than the female-headed households in both the host and the refugee community (RIMA, 2022 ).The RIMA study also showed that marginal farmers (owning land ≤ 0.5 ha) achieved one-third of the desired resilience capacity. While smallholder farmers (owning land > 0.5 and ≤ 1.0 ha) are in half of the resilience capacity, the large farmers (owning land > 1.0 ha) achieved slightly higher than the smallholder. The underlying factors indicated that the lowest resilience capacity of the marginal farmers was the limited ownership of productive assets, non-productive assets, land ownership and inability to store crops for the lean period.FAO conducted study (FAO, 2021) observed that 38.3% of households’ income in two sub-districts is dependent on agricultural practices. Absence of efficient irrigation system, soil salinity, and inadequate investment capacities on improved farming practices limits achieving the desired farm productivity and household income. Based on the above findings, the proposed intervention for smallholder and large farmers will increase their adaptive capacity and asset ownership. On other hand, the marginal farmers’ resilience capacity will be enhanced through better storing of food grains for the lean period, improved farming technologies, access to agricultural credits, and productive and non-productive assets.The farm productivity and profitability and strengthening the market system by engaging input and output actors with the aggregation centers will create connectivity with the producers, local markets and WFP-led retailers of the Rohingya camps. The project primarily addresses the needs of a total of 14,500 targeted households (i.e. 77,682 unserved beneficiaries) comprising 4,000 HHs from Rohingya refugees (i.e. 19,340 individuals) and 10,500 HHs from host communities (i.e. 58,344 individuals). The interventions will include input support and technical assistance to enhance food security and diversity of Rohingya refugees and host communities.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

FAO’s emergency agricultural assistance is a fundamental part of the humanitarian response to growing food crises, enabling the most vulnerable populations to secure own food production and meet immediate needs. From situation analysis, it becomes evident that the root causes of community vulnerability, and ecosystem degradation, are closely linked to land tenure and power dynamics. Reforestation effort will not be sustainable in a context where forest-dependent communities rely on forest resources and land for food and housing. In this context, FAO recognizes that no intervention is neutral and that any impact and its sustainability needs to acknowledge existing conflicts over resources, trade-offs between social, economic and environmental sustainability, power dynamics and land tenure issues. Investments in agriculture can make substantial contributions to local-level peace and stability by reducing conflict over natural resources and promoting sustainable livelihoods. The project intervention under the food security sector offers an ideal entry point for the nexus approach, as, besides being the main source of livelihood for the majority of those experiencing food insecurity. The key to this approach is building trust with communities and stakeholders, thus enabling sustainable solutions that are accepted by all. The approach takes into account that decision-making requires a clear understanding of trade-offs, and of the implications of various possible options, and synergies between ecosystem services, and on-farm livelihood practices in a sustainable manner. In achieving the significant results, in collaboration with humanitarian actors and development partners FAO is playing co-lead role in the FSS and EETWG. Government stakeholders (DAE and Forest Department) will appoint capable and relevant counterpart staff and will make it possible for staff to actively participate in the project implementation.

3.2 - Gender

Women play a large, vital and growing role in agriculture, nutrition, food security and a wide range of income-generating activities. Production activities largely undertaken by women include post-harvest activities, processing and preservation of crops. The project will strive to provide equal opportunities for women and men to be part of project activities and the 40 percent quota will be introduced for women to ensure gender parity. Women from small farm households and landless families will be prioritized during beneficiary targeting for host communities. The female-headed households for Rohingya people will be prioritized in selecting targeted households. Women-appropriate smart technologies will be employed to increase women’s efficiency and involvement in agricultural farming practices. Field extension services providing agents will be trained and regularly apprised to be more responsive to female farmers’ needs.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Children who come into contact with FAO as a result of the project or programme activities must be safeguarded to the maximum possible extent from deliberate or inadvertent actions and failings that place them at risk of child abuse, sexual exploitation, injury and any other harm. FAO has zero tolerance for such behavior and pursues rigorous policies to prevent and respond to these issues. All representatives of the FAO will demonstrate the highest standards of behavior towards children both in their private and professional lives. They must do all that they can to prevent, report and respond appropriately to any concerns or potential breaches of the child safeguarding rules and regulations. The child safeguarding issue includes mandatory requirements that apply to everyone in all aspects of the FAO’s work regardless of how and where they work including during the response to humanitarian emergencies.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

FAO strictly enforces a policy of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) which ensures that all information relevant to project stakeholders is shared in advance of commencing project participation. A dedicated hotline number will be set up where the beneficiary can directly access any complaints and feedback to FAO. The implementing partner will be sensitized the beneficiary with the hotline number and AAP messages when delivering services at the field level. Moreover, in organizing input distribution events or awareness-raising sessions the AAP message dissemination to the beneficiary will be a mandatory task. If the beneficiary comes up with any complaint through the hotline number then the FAO MEAL team will immediately go for the field investigation to verify the complaint and report to the senior management. The investigation and reporting will be done by maintaining a high level of confidentiality.
