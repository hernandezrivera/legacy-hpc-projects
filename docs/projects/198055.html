Project Summary

PRIDE-Initiative’s proposed intervention will aim to strengthen coordination, inclusive governance structures, improve coordination among humanitarian partners and relevant stakeholders through facilitating local coordination meeting and strengthen community governance structures. It will also strengthen information management to foster coordination and engage the community through community-led initiatives to achieve an empowering approach that allows displacement affected communities to take the lead in activity design, facilitate community-led site improvements and maintenance and ensure the protection of IDPs affected by fire outbreaks, flooding, and forced evictions, through the provision of ESKs and NFIs. Finally, in order to address the prevalent protection risks in informal settlement in MMC, the Action aims to bridge existing protection gaps through CCCM trainings, monitoring and awareness sessions in community centers especially designed for this purpose.

1 - Needs

In NE Nigeria, it is estimated that 60% of the IDPs live in the host community, with some residing in spontaneous makeshift structures in hazardous environments, rented apartments within the urban or peri-urban areas, particularly in informal settlements where state capacity to deliver services and infrastructure is weak, while others live with host families. It is acknowledged that there has been limited capacity and engagement from the humanitarian community to adequately address the need of the affected populations. Apart from suffering the effects of the insurgency, the displaced population and the host community are both exposed to serious risks including natural disaster, communicable diseases, food insecurity and marginalization. The large presence of displaced populations in urban or peri-urban areas has had significant impacts, placing additional pressure on local markets, as well as on the social, economic, cultural and administrative structures that are already weakened by effects of the insurgency. Their vulnerability is expounded as the host community has equally been affected by displacement. The disparity might cause tension and incite violence and scapegoating and especially during tension and changing phase of an emergency or during communicable disease outbreaks. As a result of displacement, they lost assets, housing, have limited social networks, separated from family members, lost documentation and poor access to available services, and are often undergoing trauma. IDPs living in urban areas are often exposed to exploitation, extortion, organized crime and antagonism from host communities. Frequently, IDPs become victims of forced evictions and expulsions. Ensuring security and protection of IDPs in urban areas therefore remains one of the most significant challenges facing the humanitarian community.

2 - Activities

PRIDE will extend urgent need for support to the vulnerable groups among other activities these are Site facilitation (managing out-of-camp sites, site upkeep and repairs, distribution of Dignity kits items, community leadership training, site planning, complaints and feedback mechanisms, focus group discussions, sensitization and awareness raising, camp/community coordination, and information sharing) (out of camp sites). Building capacities helps staff, partners, and authority leaders deliver CCCM activities in a more responsible and efficient manner. Maintenance and rehabilitation of infrastructure, site planning, land advocacy, mapping of currently available suitable lands, relocation, fire response and prevention, flood mitigation for camps, site and host community locations that are most in need of help during the rainy season (including women, girls, boys, and men of all ages and backgrounds, including People with Disabilities), and participation of the affected population (including women, girls, boys, and men of all ages and backgrounds). Community mobilization via leadership and leader selection sensitization and awareness-raising, collaborative community mobilization activities that promote harmony and cohesiveness among leaders in camps and camp-like structures, including leadership in out-of-camp settings. Training for community leaders, volunteers, religious leaders, youth, and women leaders on how to adequately discharge their responsibilities, the understanding of camp management, protection, GBV, trafficking, PSEA, referral system, community participation among other cross cutting issues. . Management of information & Coordination centres in M.M.C and Jere out-of-camp to ensure continuity, This would include providing sporting and recreational equipment to encourage peaceful cooperation and community togetherness,.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The design of this project was informed by the fundamental protection principles that guide the delivery of humanitarian aid, and as such, those principles will be followed during project execution. The entire project will be planned, carried out, and evaluated by PALRI using a rights-based approach. For each of the protection principles, the following steps will be performed to guarantee thorough protection mainstreaming throughout this project: PALRI will continuously monitor the security situation in the area of intervention and mobile teams will use secure and permitted roadways to access persons in need while putting safety and dignity ahead of doing harm during the project's implementation. To ensure meaningful and equal access, in the identification of beneficiaries, men, women, boys and girls will be prioritized based on need alone. Particularly vulnerable groups such as female- and child-headed households, but also elderly and persons with disability will be prioritized. To make communication with women and girls easier, the assessment teams' composition will be balanced by gender. Establish measures aimed at preventing and responding to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, bearing in mind the UN zero tolerance policy. To ensure a comprehensive response, all the findings will be shared with the Protection Sector as well as other relevant Sectors.
