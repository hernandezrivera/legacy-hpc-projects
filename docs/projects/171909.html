Project Summary

The world’s largest congested Rohingya camps and surrounding host communities of Ukhiya and Teknaf of Cox’s Bazar have become food unsecured due to loss of economic opportunities in pandemic COVID-19. Food Consumption Score(FCS) of Rohingya notably dropped down in comparison to 2019 in both host and Rohingya communities The acceptable range of FCS dropped 54% to 15% in 2020 compare to 2019 and miserable to borderline 41% to 50%. The most worried issues are that 35% HH are poor in FCS compare to 5% of 2019, (Ref: J-MSNA, 1st Oct’2020). The same assessment quoted that 54% HH relied on less preferred food, 34% HH reduced amount of food, 26% reduce number of meals, 19% adults restrict of consumption, where men restrict consumption 18% and women 17%. Though, Host are in better position, yet a significant number of Host HH dropped to the borderline almost 50% from acceptable range of FCS (73% of 2019 to 72%). Due to COVID-19, Rising prices of vegetables, eggs, fish, and broiler meat have had a devastating effect on poor households who struggle to afford the basic food basket. Prices have risen to 55% due to a shortage of supply. To manage rising food prices, poor households are eating cheaper and less nutritious foods. Poor households seek the cheapest vegetables and sacrifice on quality. Project focusing on economic crisis recovery due to COVID-19 by adopting on-farm, off-farm and restoration environment by improvement of soil for land capability and erosion prevention by plantation.
The project(funded by FCDO/UNOPS/DFAT) will support 11876(M 5838 F 6038) Rohingya HH and 8678(M 4338 F 4340) host HH for economic recovery from COVID-19.
Under objective 9,376 refugee households will receive self-reliance support and 2500 Rohingya Women headed HH will receive cash grants support for Skill development on homemade marketable ready-made food for targeting local markets, 60 Rohingya men and 10 host men will be supported on Hygienic food corner for diversified food catering business at market.
Under objective 3; 8678( M 4338 F 4340) host HH will be supported for kitchen garden, vertical garden for subsistence by adapt climate smart technology. and will be trained on handicrafts, Hygienic food preparation, vertical gardening for self-reliance’s & additional income for economic recovery.
Under objective 4; 2000 farmers of Host will receive training to gain improved knowledge and techniques on organizational management and quality seedling production to meet local demands and 200 Farmers HH of Host community will be supported to adapt climate smart technology and to produce Horticultural seedlings (Jack fruits, Mango, lemon) those will be transplanted at Palongkhali and Rajapalong union.
The SHRP Project during this time frame have planned to boost up food consumption, increase household income that reduced selling of productive assets by creating option of additional homemade income for women, boost up agricultural productivity and enhance food storage for hard times. These will be done by improving contributing Factors of production and income by supporting on-farm and off-farm, facilitating terms of trade for market actors and increase Food Expenditure. The Project will collect data through KoBo tools. The Compliant response mechanism is inbuild with a software name "COMPASS", beside this complaint response box at every centre. For safety of working centre every centres is equipped with Fire extinguisher, First Aid box, sanitizing devices, hand washing points, Breast feeding corner and Child play corner.

1 - Identified Needs

Cox’s Bazar specially Ukhiya and Teknaf are food deficient zone, almost 70% vegetables and other necessities comes from other districts(FAO Study,2019). Moreover, due to COVID-19, Rising prices of vegetables, eggs, fish, and broiler meat have had a devastating effect on poor households who struggle to afford the basic food basket. Prices have risen to 55%(Scoping Study Potential Livelihood and Enterprise Options for Host and Refugee Communities in Cox’s Bazar) due to a shortage of supply. To manage rising food prices, poor households are choicing cheaper and less nutritious foods. Poor households seek the cheapest vegetables and sacrifice on quality. Bangladeshi people of Ukhia and Teknaf believe that their livelihood opportunities-historically dependent on agriculture, livestock and fisheries have been significantly hampered since the 2017 influx. And historically, Ukhia and Teknaf are climate change impacted areas. Year-round production is not possible due to lack of climate adaptive technologies and inputs. Unemployment is cited by the host community as their most urgent challenge(REVA II). This is linked with reduction in labor opportunities, shrinking availability of agricultural land, environmental destruction, restricted access to Naff River (for fishing), and increasing labor and market competition from the displaced community.Overpopulation and a reduction in local production opportunities (due to diminished agricultural land) are seen by the host community as catalysts to higher prices for essential goods including fish, meat and vegetables, and to services including transportation, education and house rentals. They also report that daily wages for labor have decreased significantly as Rohingya people come out from camp do labor work on one third of wages.Rising prices and falling wages – recognized as the most common challenges for both host and Rohingya communities often hurt the poorest and most vulnerable groups the most. Prices likely to vary with distance to camps. Hosts living closer to camps are more likely to compete with the displaced population in markets for natural resources like land and fuelwood, labour and goods, compared to those living further away from camps.Due to COVID-19 market becomes non-functional and to make the markets normal functioning markets needs favourable conditions for production inputs which can reduce the negative coping strategies reportedly being exercised by both guest and host communities. Cash for Work at host and camp through Portable skill development training can ensure cash interventions in the host community and Rohingya community for local market dynamics.Loss of production contribution factors impacted on Change in crop production, loss of Livestock, distortion of Terms of Trade, loss of Household Income and decreased Percentage of Household Food Expenditure.
