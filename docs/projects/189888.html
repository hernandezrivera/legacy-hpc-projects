1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The proposed intervention aims at preventing and taking into charge 46.452 IDPs and host families at IPC stage 3 and above in Cabo Delgado, in the districts of Ibo, Pemba, Montepuez, Balama, Chiure, Metuge.The project aims at taking into charge undernourished children under 5 years old with specialised nutritional food or micronutrient supplement ; preventing malnutrition under five and pregnant and lacting women; promoting breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices. The approach is based on strengthen the community-based actors’ (APEs, activists, local leaders, primary school teachers, etc.) capacity to single out acute malnutrition cases among U-5 children and PLWs to be addressed at the community, outpatient (without clinical complications) and inpatient care level (with clinical complications), as well as to disseminate IYCF key messages. The reference policies for the implementation of the project will be the PRN (Programa de Reabilitaçáo Nutricional), the PIN (Pacote de Intervenção Nutricional), and the TDC (Tratamento da Desnutrição na Comunidade). The activities will take place both in newly established accommodation centres and communities hosting IDPs. As to key activities, the capacity building aimed at community-based actors to detect and follow up U-5 children and PLWs AM cases will be carried out in close coordination local health authorities and according to MISAU guidelines.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

O.S.1.1.1: Restore and expand capacity for life-saving nutrition interventions through health facilities and outreach activities in communities with high influx of IDPs (including host communities) and accommodation centers/ resettlement site1.2 Procurement and delivery of specialized nutritious foods, nutrition supplies for therapeutic feeding and other supplements as well as nutrition equipment, for treatment of acute malnutrition in children 6-59 months and PLWs, including those with HIV1.3 Support the supply chain logistics for nutrition1.4 Support Human Resource surge to address higher needs at the health system to support the IMB and other outreach interventions1.5 Strengthen the CHWs (APEs and ACS) in treatment of acute malnutrition at community level1.6 Undertake nutrition assessments and SMART surveys for assessing the nutritional status of the populations at riskO.S.2.2.1 Implement active case finding and referral of malnourished children and monitor the nutrition situation through MUAC screenings2.2 Strengthen the CHWs (APEs and ACS) in delivering micronutrient supplementation at community levelO.S.3.3.1 Support in the IYCF corners in both the accommodation centers/ resettlement sites3.2 Promote optimal breastfeeding and complementary feeding practices, and overall Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) best practices messages for caregivers of children under two either though awareness sessions and cooking demonstration in the communities with high influx of IDPs (including host families)3.3 Training of CHWs (APEs and ACS) on topical areas related to IYCF and HIV to provide one-on-one sessions at household level

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project will ensure protection mainstreaming process in order to esure DO NO HARM approach, promoting meaningful access, safety and dignigy in all actions. In order to guarantee this principles all the staff will be trained on the principles and procedures in place and will sign the reference documents such as the Internal Code of Conduct, the Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy (PSEA) and the AVSI Children Safeguarding Policy documents as well as the AVSI lead protection mainstreaming checklists. In order to avoid conflicts between IDPs (new or extended) and host communities, the specific selection criteria (nutritional status) will be well explained and widespread to local communities, institutions, in order to ensure the use of the principles of impartiality and neutrality and at the same time guartantee that specific need of U 5 years old Protection monitoring will be garuatee through routinely monitoring access to services by women, girls, boys and men through spot checks and discussions with communities, and promptly address the obstacles to equal access

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

This project will follow AVSI Foundation SOP described in the AVSI FOUNDATION ACCOUNTABILITY POLICY (DIGE 11/2020) on ‘feedback and accountability mechanism’. All staff are always oriented and re-oriented on the need to seek the views of communities concerning program implementation. Beneficiaries and the population at large is also made aware of the forms available to provide suggestions and complaints. AVSI Foundation will carry out household surveys and ad hoc studies to shed lights on the condition of special and vulnerable groups. The results will be presented and validated with all communities in the camp and host communities, local government, as a means to establish a productive fact-based conversation with affected populations.In AVSI’s experience, this has proven time and again to be particularly productive, as not only people’ concerns and priorities are taken into account, but, more importantly, solutions in line with the specific contexts and people capable to support such initiatives are identified. Example of these practices could be found at: https://assessments.hpc.tools/assessment/c891c32a-7c60-4aa9-bf6d-a8b48a84730ahttps://reliefweb.int/report/south-sudan/multi-sector-household-survey-rumbek-north-county-lakes-state-june-2021The capillary and continuous presence that AVSI has in the communities targeted by the intervention (AVSI has more than 80% of field based staff) ensures that such fruitful cooperation with project beneficiaries continues throughout the project’s implementation.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

In the present project proposal screening, surveillance and participation of people with disabilities have been included as key activities and indicators. During their implementation, the detection of people with disabilities will be reported (disaggregated by gender and age) and given special attention and priority to inclusion as beneficiaires, with a focus on HIV.AVSI Foundation will, when possible, organize specific studies on the inclusion and conditions of persons living with a disability and will utilize such studies to devise inclusive strategies on round table with PwD as well as local communities. Examples of such work in similar context can be found at: https://assessments.hpc.tools/assessment/e3f17126-d1f5-4a78-80a4-99dea7c46e69.AVSI Foundation will ensure the implementation of sensitization sessions throughout its activities to ensure that these people are supported within the community and given special attention when coordinating and planning activities with local SDSMAS and partners.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

AVSI aims to promote a sustainable and healthy environment in order to improve the wellbeing of the community. Given the experience of AVSI Mozambique in energy effciency-related projects, method to save charcoal and wood during cooking demonstration will be also promoted so that to sensitize the target population on preventing deforestation that in turn impact positively on family finances by saving wood and charcoal.In addition of that, it will be prioritazed the use of local materials in order to adopt an environment-friendly approach by trying to increase awareness in the affected communities.In order to guarantee protection Environment, AVSI can rely to an internal Policy on environment on May 11th 2020.
