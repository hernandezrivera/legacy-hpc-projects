Project Summary

2021 Post Gu FSNAU nutrition analysis shows that at the national level the median prevalence of Global
Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has still remained at a serious level of 11.1%. Based on this analysis and the
latest population figures, the cluster estimates that in 2022 around 1.2 million children, under the age of
five years, will be at risk of acute malnutrition and will therefore need emergency nutrition services
urgently. Out of this a total of 295,515 will be severely acute malnourished children (SAM) and around
986,000 children will be moderately acute malnourished (MAM) children. In addition to this, around
245,909 Pregnant & Lactating Women (PLWs) will be at risk of acute malnutrition. The nutrition cluster
will prioritize the districts that have a GAM of 15% or more also with the preventive nutrition services. In
this regard, it is estimated that around 629,567 children between 6 to 23 months and 283,305 PLWs will
at a greater risk as well. Hence, the total people in need (both for curative and preventive services) for
nutrition in 2022 will be 2.37 million including IDPs and Non-IDPs.
The program seeks to improve meaningful access and utilization of lifesaving preventative and curative stand-alone and integrated nutrition services for women and children living in the most affected areas in Bari (Qardho), and Middle Shabelle (Balcad) districts using a combination of static and mobile sites.
It contributes to Cluster Strategic Objective 1 and 2 through both preventative and curative nutrition services targeting 7950 beneficiaries: (3816 boys, 4134 girls), and 1200 pregnant and lactating women through access to providing equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services among boys and girls (06-59) months & PLW through systematic identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished cases, and strengthen lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups focusing on improving nutritional status through the provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate IYCF practices in emergency and micronutrient interventions. Where plausible integrating to WASH and health programs. Cluster objective 1 will target all the three districts.
The program takes into account the nutritional needs of people with disabilities and accordingly in the planning of response will use the 15% global disability estimates.
The project activities; screening of the target population; 6-59 months of SAM treatment (1750). 6-59 months old for MAM treatment ( 6150). MAM Pregnant and Lactating Woman (PLW) treatment (1200).
Cluster objective 2 interventions will target Qardo district. Implement lifesaving preventive nutrition services for vulnerable population groups through the provision of supplementary nutrition products, appropriate IYCF practices in an emergency, and micronutrient interventions. 800 PLW accessing preventative nutrition services will be targeted in Qardho. Boys and girls (06-23 months and 6-59 months) receiving preventive services through supplementary nutrition services, and multiple micronutrient and Vitamin A supplements.
Staff capacity will be enhanced; training of 32 (14 male and 18 female) Nutrition staff on IMAM; 38 (12 male and 26 female) Community Nutritional Workers in basic IYCF/-E and all staff in PSEA orientation.
Data collected and used will be disaggregated by sex, age, and diversity to effectively inform programming at all phases- design, planning, implementation, and monitoring and evaluation. The programming will undertake a continuous assessment of power dynamics to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access and discrimination. Nutrition staff and committees will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) to ensure inclusivity in the consultation process of the program phases.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Mitigation Measures1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. - Identify central locations and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire, or violence. -Ensure the location and access routes to nutrition facilities and services are safe. QRCS will Identify central locations and bring services closer to vulnerable populations in IDPs and host communities. For example, QRCS will use mobile outreach teams to provide services to populations living further away from static facilities to reduce their risk of exposure to protection issues. -Ensure the location, distance and access routes to the nutrition facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. -Ensure services are offered within time to ensure beneficiaries travel back safely. 2. Risk of women, girls, and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance. -Design and clearly mark separate facilities for males and females and account for persons with disabilities and children. -In coordination with protection actors, train nutrition staff to be able to identify and appropriately respond to victims of grave human rights violations, including rape and physical abuse, and set up referral networks and protocols to protect the privacy of victims. -Ensure all staff, implementing partners, and volunteers in direct contact with patients, health staff, and beneficiaries have received training in the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). -In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of community nutrition workers to monitor, report, and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. -In cooperation with protection actors, establish joint reporting and accountability mechanisms to report incidents concerning hospitals/nutrition sites. -Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organizational procedures, and processes that affect them. -Set up accessible, confidential, and well-understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions. -Promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at the nutrition centers, phone lines. 4. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources. -Avoid stakeholders tensions, by providing the local/host community with access to IDP camp nutrition/health facilities. -Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable; identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community, including the host community, and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups. -Adopt clear beneficiary selection criteria communicated to all stakeholders and target communities. -Identify the power dynamics in the area. -Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to their nutrition needs to mitigate external interference. -Ensuring all the stakeholders are well represented to mitigate to buffer any possible interference.
