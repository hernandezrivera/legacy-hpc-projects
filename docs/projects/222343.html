Project Summary

South Sudan faces dire consequences resulting from continuous conflict, disease outbreaks, high returnees, refugees' influx from Sudan crisis and persisted flooding bloating efforts put by South Sudan government and humanitarian in responding to critical health issues.
Medicair plan a year long health response to support reduce mortality and morbidity related to disease outbreaks like cholera, measles and hepatitis, conflicts and natural disasters like floods to most vulnerable communities in Aweil North, Aweil East counties in Northern Bhar el Ghazal state, Akobo and Uror Counties in Jonglei State and Rubkona County in Unity State identified as intersectoral prioritiy1 and 2 locations and aligning to the 2025 health cluster severity levels 3 and 4.
The response will align to 2025 health cluster objectives i.e CO 1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations, CO 2:Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases & CO 3:Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. Where key activities will include provision of essential health services, conduct out-patient consultations, conduct vaccination against vaccine preventable diseases such measles, carry out disease surveillance, conduct capacity building, provide MNCH services, provide MHPSS,CMR, GBV and health promotions services. The plan will target most vulnerable populations in conflict- and flood-affected selected areas/counties, prioritizing women, children, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Medicair's intervention will prioritize collaboration and integration with other sectors like Nutrition, WASH, Protection/ GBV and FSL. Strengthening capacities of Ministry of health and the state and county levels and ensure response to health needs in line with South Sudan National health policy 2016-2026 and 2023-2027 health sector strategic plan.
The intervention targets a population of 45,290 individuals for the next 12 months with a total budget of $ 1,585,150. The intervention will use modalities of static and mobile outreach for service delivery.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to suffer a severe health crisis, complicated by multiple factors such as yearlong flooding, conflicts, displacements, returnees, and disease outbreaks such as cholera, measles and Hepatitis. Service delivery gaps continue to be more noticeable because of the combination of challenges including a lack of funding, inadequate staffing, medicine, and equipment to support quality service provision. Findings from the 2024 ISNA show that only 57% of the population can access the nearest health facility in less than an hour, while 44% cited long distance as a barrier to accessing health care. Health Cluster indicates that about 5.65 million people are in dire need of health services across South Sudan, with 3.08 targeted with services. As of 8 November 2024, Public Health Situation Analysis (PHSA) shows that South Sudan is facing unprecedented floods, impacting more than 1.4 million people nationwide, with Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal, and Unity states being the most affected. More broadly, the humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains concerning with increasing needs and a growing number of people requiring assistance. There is also a continued influx of refugees and returnees due to the Sudan crisis, with 840 000 new arrivals. Generally, half of the arrivals from Sudan crisis are female and about half are children below the age of 18. South Sudanese returnees account for 77% of all arrivals. (South Sudan: Public Health Situation Analysis as of 8 November 2024). Only 34% of the population have access to Universal Healthcare (UHC) 210 and 70% lack basic health care services. 211 Emergency obstetric care services remain limited, with only 40% of health facilities functional and service delivery being largely provided by international organizations. According to IPC Classification Sept 2024 - July 2025, Aweil North, Aweill East, Uror, Rubkona and Akobo will be in IPC 3 (worse) and likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Jonglei state is particularly affected by the same challenges and one of the states with the highest health access gaps, this is evident with Out-patient Consultation slightly above a third (35%) of the population while MNC services particularly ANC standing at 44% (DHIS 2, 2022). Akobo county is engulfed in the already week healthcare system poor access to Maternal and child health services in the county with only 38% (383/1018) of women having access to skilled Birth Attendant in Akobo, 33% of pregnant women attended ANC services. (HMIS/DHIS2, 2022). The health status of the identified counties shows that malaria is standing as the leading cause of death and illness, affecting half of the outpatient consultations where it It accounts for 66% of outpatient consultations, 50% of admissions, and about 30% of deaths followed by Acute Watery Diarrheas' Then Respiratory tract infection (PHS Nov 2024).Poor utilization of health care services due to cultural believes, leading low skilled birth attendance coverage at 7% as resuscitation, trauma, obstetric emergencies, and childhood pneumonia is limited. Lack of WASH facilities, and High GAM prevalence above emergency threshold of 15% in these targeted counties compound the already precarious health situation, and therefore integrated health service delivery will be key in reducing maternal mortality rates. This will ensure, protection needs, nutrition needs, WASH interventions in health facilities, and provision of quality health services to reduce the mortality and morbidity rates in all the target counties.

2. Project activities

Medicair overall response plan will conduct activities in line with cluster objectives as follows, CO1 Medicair will i) conduct disease surveillance both at the community and facility level, as well support CHD to develop EP&RPs and also to set up early warning systems. ii) Support improvement of waste management, through provision of waste segregation supplies, improving WASH in facilities and training staffs on IPC, iii) conduct outpatient consultation for common conditions such as Malaria, Acute Respiratory Infection and Acute Watery Diarrhea, using mobile and static health facilities, iv) carry out vaccination services for measles and Penta 3 and delivery of supplies as last mile, v) support and provide Basic Emergency Obstetrics and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) through Antenatal Care, Postnatal care, skill birth delivery, vi) provide clinical management of rape (CMR) to survivors of SGBV/Rape and also train staff on CMR, Provide support to non-specialized Mental Health and Psychosocial support, counselling and referrals. CO2: Medicair will i) conduct community enagement meetings to ensure community voices are captured and support project implementation, ii) Support RCCE at the County level to conduct community engagement as part of emergency preparedness, during crisis and or disease outbreaks particularly vaccine preventable and water borne diseases(Cholera and hep E) and during the recovery period to prevent relapse. CO3 Medicair will i) Support coordination and capacity building of County Health staff, ii) conduct joint supportive supervision, iii) Procure and distribute emergency drugs to support health service delivery, v) support minor rehabilitation of health facilities
