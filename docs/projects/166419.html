Project Summary

On 11 March 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) has declared coronavirus (COVID-19) outbreak a public health emergency of international concern (PHEIC). The pandemic is spreading fast across countries, with 2,994,960 cases and almost 206,997 deaths globally, including 5,416 confirmed cases and 145 deaths in Bangladesh, with numbers rising at an alarming rate (from three case on 27 April).
Bangladesh has been identified as a country at risk of coronavirus, due to many Bangladeshi nationals living overseas including heavily-infected countries such as Italy and China and its high population density facilitating transmission. Furthermore, the health system faces huge gaps, and there are reports of health professionals, with insufficient health protective equipment, are failing to treat priority cases.
The cramped and unsanitary living conditions and poor basic health facilities could spell disaster for the 859,161 Rohingya refugees living in the Cox’s Bazar refugee camp. The camps have a 40,000 person per km2 density, almost four times that of New York city and ten times that of Wuhan, China, where the virus began. Similar vulnerabilities are faced in the host communities surrounding the camps. The health facilities in the refugee and host communities are unprepared to treat severe cases or a large-scale COVID-19 outbreak.
CARE proposes a multi-sector response to prevent the spread and respond to the coronavirus in the camps 11-16 and Palong Khali host community. The project will focus on the most vulnerable groups, such as the elderly, persons with underlying medical conditions, care-givers and people living with disability.
Approaches will build community resilience to build future capacities at community, households and individual levels to prevent the re-emergence and spread of the disease. CARE will conduct community engagement sessions to sensitize women, girls, men and boys as well as community level actors to create awareness of the prevention and control methods for COVID-19. The health care workers will be trained on IPC, case definition & management, and Covid-19 community engagement & communication.
The community volunteers will be trained IPC, covid-19 prevention measure, symptoms and home management and active referral pathways (covid-19, SRH and GBV).CARE will enhance Covid-19 its community reach by working through different community groups (mahji, adolescents, youths, men and women) who will be trained correct preventive measures on how to stop spread of Covid-19 and to dispel rumors which are based on negative perceptions and norms. To strengthen hand washing practices and general household’s hygiene standards, these groups will carry out door-to-door sensitization. CARE continues to provide essential health services through 4 health posts and 14 outreach team working under the close supervision of the medical doctors adapting and re-aligning activities to observe the recommended service provision practices by the health cluster.

Needs

On 11 March 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) has declared coronavirus (COVID-19) outbreak a public health emergency of international concern. The pandemic is spreading fast across country to country. Bangladesh is already Covid-19 affected, people identified in sixty district including Cox’s Bazar. In just a short time, a localized outbreak of COVID-19 evolved into a global pandemic with three defining characteristics: Speed and scale: the disease has spread quickly to all corners of the world, and its capacity for explosive spread has overwhelmed health systems. The 859,000 Rohingya Refugees currently residing in 34 camps in Cox’s Bazar are highly vulnerable to COVID-19 due to multiple factors including overcrowding in the camps and the limited space to ensure physical distancing measures are adequately implemented. Elderly persons above the age of 60 and those with underlying health conditions are at higher risk of contracting the virus and will be affected disproportionally. The cramped and unsanitary living conditions and poor basic health facilities could spell disaster for the Rohingya refugees living in the Cox’s Bazar refugee camp. With 20% of cases being severe or critical, with a crude clinical case fatality rate currently of over 3%, vigorous efforts must be put in place to minimize morbidity and mortality. Moreover, the camp facilities are not equipped with the needed equipment including oxygen and personal protective equipment (PPE) to treat COVID-19 patients. No facilities exist in the camps to treat those in need of critical care. It is with this that CARE is re-aligning and integrating Covid-19 prevention and response activities along with ensuring continued essential SRH services. CARE’s Community Health workers working under the supervision of the paramedic will provide home cares services to Covid-19 patients with mild to moderate symptoms in accordance to the cluster protocols. The clients on SRH follow-up programs but on quarantine needing urgent service will also be attended by the paramedics in their home or quarantine facilities within CARE’s catchment area. CARE will work at imparting Covid-19 messages at the community level. Risk communication and community engagement activities are aimed at improving the community’s awareness of the disease; preventive measure, management and different service care centers; and clarification of myths and elimination of stigma attached to Covid-19. These level of activities will be adapted to the level of infection cases in the camp. At low transmission rate rates outreach teams will emphasize the available essential services and as they also explain the adaptive modalities of services provision and the updated referral pathways. The health workers will be trained on CMR and care of other GBV cases to meet the anticipated rise of cases. The CARE team will ensure inclusivity by intentionally reaching out to the vulnerable groups and those living with disability during risk communication and community engaged. At the health posts the all the staff will be trained on Covid-19 case definition and management with emphasize on covid-19 IPC protocols. The staff will be trained on appropriate use of PPE and be supplied with the same. The activities and staff at the four Health Posts will be arranged to accommodate Covid-19 response activities. The number of persons in the facilities at a time will be limited. Every client entering the facility will be screened for any SARI / Covid-19 signs and symptoms. Those meeting the case definitions will be temporarily be held at the Covid-19 holding corner as the Rapid Investigation Team is contacted for sample collecting. These patients will be managed for others conditions and be advised on self-quarantine as they wait for the rest results. Out outreach team will be tasked with the daily monitoring of these patients and provide care depending of the test results.
