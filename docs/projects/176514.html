Project Summary

Armed conflict in South Sudan has declined since the parties to the civil war signed a peace agreement in September 2018; and the political situation remains fragile, and millions remain displaced both inside and outside of the country. The humanitarian situation in South Sudan remains fragile due to a multiplicity of vulnerability drivers including inter communal violence, climatic shocks, population displacement, commodity price fluctuations, and pre-existing vulnerabilities such as malnutrition, WASH related diseases, limited access to basic services and high protection risks. The 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) illustrates that about 2.2 million people are in need of WASH services in the country and negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices. National access to safe water is at 35 per and only 10 per cent of the population has access to improved sanitation. This intervention targets underserved and vulnerable population in Pariang County which is security level 3 according to the WASH cluster 2021 severity map and stressed severity stage in humanitarian conditions shown in 2021 HNO. However, Pariang hosts a significant number of refugees whose needs require urgent attention.
Through this proposed intervention, the IRC will support by providing access to WASH facilities in 2 static primary health care centers (PHCC) and 2 primary schools, rehabilitation and maintenance of non-functional community water systems, provide safe access to communal sanitation facilities and hand washing stations at strategic locations, provide hygiene promotion activities and provide trainings to WASH committees.
The intervention will work closely with the County WASH department structure as the custodian of water and sanitation services at the County level to foster coordination and collaboration with key actors in WASH and health in the targeted County. This will also support county led services mapping to ensure complementarity of interventions proposed by this project with other ongoing WASH services delivery partners.
The intervention will mainstream COVID-19 risk reduction measures across all service delivery channels to ensure reduction in transmission.

1 - Needs

The formation of the transitional government of national unity (TGoNU) in Feb 2020 has resulted in a fragile stability. However, increasing humanitarian needs are driven by cumulative effects of years of recurring conflict and violence and aggravated by subsequent shocks including the COVID-19 pandemic, relatively lower oil prices and consecutive years of flooding. The country is ranked as the most dangerous county for people to live in during the COVID-19 pandemic due to its structural deficiencies including a weak and under-resourced health care, high poverty rate and internal displacement. Living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers lack access to sufficient and quality WASH services. As access to water sources is predominately communal in South Sudan, the limited access to WASH services impacts on all the population groups. IDPs, non-displaced and host community members, returnees from internal displacement and refugee returnees are vulnerable with limited access to WASH services. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the county. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people with restrictions to access the already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites and it creates barriers for WASH partners when implementing activities. Pariang is security level 3 in the WASH cluster 2021 severity map and stressed severity stage in humanitarian conditions shown in 2021 HRP. However, Pariang hosts a significant number of refugees whose needs require urgent attention. Through this proposed intervention, the IRC will support by providing access to WASH facilities in two static primary health care centers (PHCC) and two primary schools, rehabilitation and maintenance of non-functional community water systems, provide safe access to communal sanitation facilities and hand washing stations at strategic locations, provide hygiene promotion activities as well as provide trainings to WASH committees.

2 - Project activities

Through this proposed intervention, the IRC will implement emergency WASH activities to improve access to safe and adequate water, sanitation and hygiene practices in Pariang communities. Project activities will be:- rehabilitation and maintenance of water and latrines in two static primary health care centers (PHCC) and two primary schools, - rehabilitation and maintenance of non-functional community water systems. -rehabilitation and maintenance of communal sanitation facilities and hand washing stations at strategic locations. -provide hygiene promotion activities including safe practice messages. -provide trainings to WASH committees and respective government authorities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

-The project will be governed by the IRC guidelines for a survivor centered approach to reporting safeguarding misconduct and the health sector specific safeguarding checklists will be used in setting up the intervention. These measures will ensure that clients receive care in safe environment and protected from safeguarding issues. The IRC's raise concern policy has elaborate and confidential channels to allow for staff and client reporting of any safeguarding concerns which are then independently verified and acted upon. The project will ensure clients are briefed on reporting concerns and awareness will be raised to ensure that clients are aware of rights and understand sexual exploitation and abuse. Client feedback mechanisms will be set up at all communal WASH facilities to allow for self-reporting and provide a confidential channel for clients to raise any safeguarding concerns. Regular monthly meetings with key stakeholders will also provide an opportunity for accountability concerns to be raised and shared with all stakeholders concerning WASH services delivery.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

-Through random sampling of clients accessing services through the project, the most vulnerable in the populations will be accorded a chance to share feedback through the feedback mechanisms. Feedback questionnaires will be made simple and easy to understand and the intervention will employ the use of easy to access helpdesk where persons with specialized needs including clients and caregivers with disability, mental health needs and illiterate community members. Client confidentiality will be maintained throughout the intervention through adequate data protection measures including assigning anonymity to individual client feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

-During the project opening meetings (at the community level) key stakeholders including women and youth leaders as well as leaders of persons with special needs will be engaged. Through this meetings, focused group discussions and key informant interviews, the community will be requested to provide their key needs including decisions on key areas to target institutions with prioritized WASH needs, strategic locations for sanitation and hand washing facilities, and prioritized water sources to rehabilitate and maintain. The community members will be engaged to understand project scope as well as available budgets for service delivery, community complaint mechanisms through the WASH committees and community leaders. Hygiene promotion messages will be tailored to the needs of the community taking into account the cultural sensitivities and barriers to the uptake of health information and services to ensure that culturally appropriate messages are developed and disseminated by the project to increase uptake of WASH services to improve health outcomes.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

-The intervention will strive to engage with communities through channels that the community know and trust to ensure that there is buy in. Efforts will be made towards alerting the community of the mechanisms to share their feedback in an open and candid manner and confidentiality of clients will be protected through strong information management. The project will maintain a database via the Kobo toolbox to ensure that all information is digitalized to allow for more detailed analysis of the feedback information for action and response. The feedback channels and mechanisms will be iterative to ensure that any learning during the project duration is captured and applied to improve on the processes. The client feedback mechanisms will include sessions with key community leaders in dialogue forums to provide feedback on the findings and in engaging the community teams in driving changes to the intervention logic as needed.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

-This intervention will mainstream protection throughout the project duration by ensuring that women, men, girls and boys have equitable access to quality services. The intervention will strengthen linkages with protection partners across the project locations for referral pathways (sexual and GBV). This will include ensuring that the existing pathways are routinely updated and furthermore ensure continued coordination through existing mechanisms and with the support of the County WASH and Health departments and the Ministry of Gender and Social Welfare.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

-The intervention will target children, people with disability, older persons and women headed households through engaging with local community structures. The intervention will coordinate with multi-sectoral state level and sub-state level coordination channels to strengthen collaborations across food security and livelihoods actors and nutrition sector actors to improve dietary diversity and build on the food security gains from previous years. Interventions to reduce morbidity and mortality to improve health outcomes will result in reduction of the negative effects of the seasonal flooding with healthier populations being able to contribute more to livelihoods. COVID-19 risk reduction measures, community based surveillance and risk communication and community engagement activities will likely mitigate the negative impact of COVID-19 on food security.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

-Through focusing on screening of clients seeking WASH services particularly people living with disability be tailored WASH facilities through inclusion of modified access component during rehabilitation and maintenance of WASH facilities. This intervention will ensure latrines are segregated and built with ramps, hand rails and other adaptations that make it easier for people living with disability, and water collection points have appropriate height with ramp.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project will improve access to safe water and sanitation to the people living in Pariang. Safe and adequate water supply, sanitation and hygiene practices, have a direct impact on WASH related infectious disease, especially diarrhea, which are important for preventing malnutrition among children under the age of five.
