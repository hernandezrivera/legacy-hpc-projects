Project Summary

This project will implement targeted Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) interventions focused on slope stabilization and drainage improvement to protect vulnerable populations from landslides and flooding.
DRR: Many camp residents live on risky slopes with insufficient protection. Existing temporary measures offer partial coverage, but only some have proven effective. To provide lasting safety, this project will implement both temporary and sustainable slope stabilization techniques, addressing the slope's risk . Temporary measures, such as bioengineering with vegetation to strengthen soil stability, plantation etc and sustainable solutions will be provided to the most vulnerable areas.
Drainage Cleaning and Improvement: The project will also address critical drainage issues through a regular canal-cleaning program and infrastructure repairs. A structured cleaning schedule will be established to keep the primary drainage canal and main drains free of waste, engaging community participation to ensure sustainability. Additionally, secondary drainage channels will be repaired to support proper water flow and reduce flood risks.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The project addresses critical needs to protect vulnerable populations in risk areas prone to landslides and flooding.DRR/Slope Stabilisation: Many residents live on steep, deforested slopes that are highly susceptible to landslides. Current temporary stabilisation measures cover only portions of these areas and often lack durability, leaving many households exposed to slope failures. There is an urgent need for sustainable solutions to secure unstable hillsides and prevent erosion. Women and children are particularly vulnerable when slope failures occur, as they are often at home during the day and may have limited capacity to evacuate quickly, which increases their risk of injury or displacement.Drainage Improvement and Sanitation:The drainage systems in the camps frequently become clogged with waste, leading to blockages that cause flooding, water stagnation, and unsanitary conditions. Inadequate drainage poses significant health risks by creating stagnant water, which attracts disease-carrying pests. Regular canal cleaning and drainage repairs are essential to prevent flooding and maintain safe, sanitary living conditions. Improved drainage infrastructure will also enhance accessibility for women and children, who are particularly vulnerable to health risks associated with poor sanitation.Gender Considerations: Women and children face unique risks due to inadequate infrastructure. Unstable slopes, limited drainage, and poor sanitation all impact their physical safety, hygiene, and mobility. Women’s responsibilities often keep them at home, placing them at higher risk during slope failures. Infrastructure improvements that provide safe pathways, enhanced drainage, and gender-sensitive sanitation facilities are essential to significantly improve safety, mobility, and well-being for women and children, especially in emergencies.

2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The CREST project integrates protection mainstreaming to ensure that all activities prioritize safety, dignity, and inclusivity for vulnerable populations. Project sites and processes are designed to be accessible to individuals of all ages and abilities, with safe spaces and evacuation routes tailored for those with mobility challenges. Clear guidelines and training sessions for staff and community members promote a culture of respect, non-discrimination, and confidentiality. Additionally, CREST involves community feedback mechanisms to continuously address protection needs, ensuring a safe and inclusive environment for all participants.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project’s focus on creating safe, accessible infrastructure and encouraging participation ensures that age and disability are not barriers to involvement, helping these individuals feel secure and valuable within their community. Safe zones and evacuation routes will be designed to be easily accessible, allowing swift response during emergencies. Additionally, age- and disability-friendly roles will be assigned, enabling them to contribute meaningfully within their capacities—whether through community knowledge-sharing, monitoring environmental risks, or leading awareness initiatives. By promoting inclusivity, including empowers vulnerable groups but also strengthens the entire community's resilience.The CREST project emphasizes age and disability inclusion by integrating tailored strategies to ensure all individuals can participate and benefit equally. Infrastructure, such as evacuation routes and safe zones, is designed to be accessible for people with mobility challenges. Training materials and sessions are adapted to meet the needs of elderly individuals and persons with disabilities, ensuring inclusivity in disaster preparedness and environmental activities. Community roles are assigned based on individual capacities, allowing everyone, regardless of age or ability, to contribute meaningfully. Representatives from these groups are actively involved in planning and decision-making to ensure their perspectives shape project interventions. By embedding these measures, CREST ensures that age and disability inclusion is mainstreamed, fostering resilience and equity for all.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The CREST project demonstrates compliance with Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) commitments by embedding transparency, participation, and responsiveness into its implementation. Refugees are actively engaged in planning and decision-making processes, ensuring their voices shape project priorities. Clear and accessible communication channels, such as community meetings, posters, and broadcasts, keep affected populations informed about project goals, timelines, and progress. Feedback mechanisms, including suggestion boxes, help desks, and community liaisons, enable refugees to share concerns or suggestions, with timely responses provided to foster trust. Inclusive monitoring and evaluation ensure that the project remains adaptive to the community's needs, with refugees participating in assessing its effectiveness. These measures collectively ensure that the CREST project respects, involves, and responds to the needs and rights of the affected populations.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

PSEAH (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation, Abuse, and Harassment) will be essential to improving Rohingya community living conditions by integrating protective measures across disaster preparedness, water treatment, canal repair, and community engagement. Awareness and Training: All staff, volunteers, and leaders will be trained on PSEAH principles, focusing on respectful interactions, safeguarding, and clear reporting mechanisms, especially for vulnerable groups. Safe Reporting Mechanisms: Confidential reporting channels will be established and widely communicated to encourage safe and trusted reporting without fear of reprisal. Inclusive Engagement Spaces: PSEAH policies will ensure safe, harassment-free environments for women, children, and vulnerable individuals in all engagement activities, with women-led groups identifying areas needing enhanced safety. Monitoring and Accountability: Ongoing monitoring will ensure adherence to PSEAH protocols, with community feedback guiding improvements. Regular reviews will maintain accountability and foster continuous PSEAH compliance across project activities.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, the Camp Resilience & Environmental Sustainability Transformation (CREST) project actively incorporates Climate Action by addressing Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), Disaster Risk Management (DRM), and anticipatory actions tailored to the Rohingya camps. Through climate adaptation strategies, CREST aims to mitigate landslide and flood risks by reinforcing slope stability and implementing sustainable drainage systems. Reforestation and soil conservation efforts are also central to the project, helping to restore natural vegetation to reduce erosion. CREST further promotes climate resilience by engaging the community in sustainable resource management practices, reducing environmental and water pollution, and fostering an environment that better withstands climate-related challenges.

4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.
