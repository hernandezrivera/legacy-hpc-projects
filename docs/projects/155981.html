Project Summary

The project is to fill the gaps in the FSL sector through Improving the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance which planned to be achieved by emergency response to crisis affected vulnerable people with short term food assistance through appropriate modalities, and the provision of flour or bread directly to households or to bakeries, also, it aims to support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives in the north of Syria through distribution of agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment; and related training, and support to asset building and asset protection (small livestock and animal feed distribution) including winterization activities; and related training.
We aim to :
Distribute 5,000 food basket monthly for 5,000 HH in need.
Distribute bread for 4,000 HH (200 MT of flour)
Support farmers agricultural Inputs and trainings ( 1850 HH with wheat seeds , and 600 HH with vegetable seeds)
Targeting of 800 HH by distribute livestock and animal feed.

Needs

Drawn from a countrywide household assessment, an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies. Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lack of livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited financial access to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuous depletion of livelihoods assets of the most vulnerable populations. Almost 40 percent of the surveyed households reported to be an IDP or returnee of whom 74.5 percent reported to be displaced for more than 12 months. Among them 478,283 displaced people living in last resort IDP sites/camps are of particular concern as they have limited access to a diversified diet or income opportunities. With an 83 - 89 percent people living below poverty lines, and with cumulative GDP loss of $226 billion between 2011 – 2016 out of which $16 billion in losses and damages attributable to agriculture sector alone, the relationship between poverty and food security is becoming inseparable. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled labourer's monthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary thus demonstrating the existence of “working poor” in Syria. The worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production in 2017/2018 winter season in Syria. This has come as an additional burden on the prolonged crisis with a shortfall of 1.2 million metric tonnes of wheat to meet domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tonnes. Unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation are affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies.The humanitarian needs in Syria are expected to remain high in 2019 and multiple actions are needed to ensure that all four pillars of food security related to access, availability, utilization and stability of food are adequately addressed through lifesaving, life sustaining, appropriate resilience, and early recovery interventions. Emergency response at the early onset of any sudden population movement as well as targeted food assistance and lifesaving emergency agricultural assistance to meet the nutritional and kilocalorie deficits of the most vulnerable population as per food security indicators is crucial across Syria. Agriculture sector is a major contributor to GDP with more than 25/30 percent of Syria’s GDP reliant on this sector, and it is also producing food for more than half of the population. Therefore, saving, restoring and protecting agricultural and related livelihoods and value chains will be critical to increase the self-reliance and strengthen resilience of Syrians.{HNO 2019} As mentioned in HNO_PiN_Severity_2019_FAS:In Aleppo governorate: The total PIN is 1,161,537 in the areas which we target ( A'zaz – Jarablus – AlBab – Jabal Samaan - ِAfrin),and the total population 3,177,599 divided to 794,3995 females > 18 - 635,518 males > 18 - 857,951 Girls 5-17, and 821,686 boys 5-17, in addition the total females headed HH 313,818, and the total IDPs 747,200. Implementation of activities

-Beyaz Eller will provide food baskets 5,000 food basket monthly for 5,000 HH in need(25,000 individuals per month for 12 months), flour, bread monthly for 4,000 HH (20,000 individuals per month for 12 months) , agriculture inputs(wheat seeds and Fertilizers for 1,850 farmer , summer vegetables seeds and Fertilizers for 600 farmer, and legumes), livestock(chickens and its feed for 400 HH, goats, sheep and cows and its feed for 400 HH),to vulnerable beneficiaries(people with disabilities, women and girls caregivers, elders, women headed HHs, IDPs, Poor members of hosting communities)-Beyaz Eller will coordinate this distribution with other humanitarian actors who are also distributing the materials in the same regions. Efforts will be made to ensure there are no duplications or gaps. Cooperation with local councils, community leaders and the FSL cluster will take place via field meetings, e-mails, Skype.-Beyaz Eller (BE) will distribute materials based on a distribution plan from pre-agreed distribution points within communities. Distribution points will be chosen in coordination with the local council and will be walking distance from the homes of beneficiaries, since trucks cannot access houses, and door step delivery to this number of beneficiaries is not feasible. For beneficiaries who are not able to access the distribution points BE will provide door step delivery.-Beneficiaries are able to give feedback at the point of distribution to staff, or via phone, Skype or email. Beneficiaries will also be given a chance for feedback during the post distribution survey (according to the percentage of beneficiaries selected for post distribution monitoring feedback)-Women and men of different ages will participate in awareness sessions and trainings, they will receive needed support monthly, Beyaz Eller will make sure all beneficiaries will have equal chances to access to food and awareness activities.-Based on lessons learned in previous interventions, BE has developed a more detailed distribution checklist that will be used when planning and holding distributions, program staff will fill out the checklist prior to the distribution, and monitoring staff will check it has been complied with during the distribution. The Syria FSL Field Coordinator has responsibility for the process, and to ensure the checklist is fulfilled, a process that will be backstopped by the Turkey team.-The checklist includes more detailed information to guide project staff on distribution times, crowd control, information management, special measures for vulnerable beneficiaries, organization of the distribution site (clear signs, barriers, protection from the weather). The checklist includes ways to explain to beneficiaries how they can complain – both about the assistance itself, and about the behaviour of staff or the distribution management, which are anonymous and free. BE takes seriously feedback from beneficiaries with regards to access for people with disabilities/ elderly/ pregnant, gender considerations in crowd control, access to drinking water and waiting times. It is expected that the introduction of a more detailed distribution checklist, with responsibilities to implement good practices outlined, will result in more dignified distributions. The distribution activity will be fulfilled by BE (self-implementing) without any mediators. 1. Identification and registration via Local Councils, BE leads verification of beneficiaries.2. Distributing (Distribution Coupons) to identify the beneficiaries.3. Identification and planning of distribution – using distribution checklist.4. Monthly monitoring (according to sample size method)5. The implementation will be supervised on the ground by the BE field office.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Large numbers of people in need compared to the provided services, to avoid this risk, Beyaz Eller will select the beneficiaries depending on a criteria, and this selection criteria will be shared with local community, and the leaders of the community will participate in this process. Changes in prices in markets, so to mitigate this risk Beyaz Eller will buy the materials under fixed price condition with traders, delayed delivery of seeds from the preferred date of cultivation, finance issues or delay remittances in these cases the mitigation measures will be replacing the crop with other types of strategic crops that have not yet been completed and thus ensure that farmers receive the appropriate agricultural support for them.
