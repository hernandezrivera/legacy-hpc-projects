Project Summary

A total of 2.9 million IDPs have been displaced by conflict, insecurity, drought, and floods, the majority of whom are currently located in over 2,400 IDP sites across Somalia, often in informal settlements on private land in urban area. The majority of these displaced people are living in precarious conditions with the majority not having their basic needs met due to inconsistent service provision or exclusion from accessing humanitarian support, according to the detailed site assessment findings and site verification assessments conducted by the CCCM cluster, more than 85 percent of sites are informal settlements settled on private land with the majority of sites located in urban areas.
CCCM Cluster monthly service monitoring and complaint feedback mechanism (CFM) trend analysis indicates similar service gaps and issues raised. Most IDP sites recorded needs mostly in Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) support, Shelter, and WASH assistance, but not excluding other sectors such as health and education which also show considerable service and assistance needs. While as the service monitoring cuts across IDP sites and highlights the needs of all IDP populations, CCCM cluster reports indicate that the majority of those most acutely affected are vulnerable groups; women, elderly, refugee returnees, minority groups and people living with disability (PLWD), putting further stress on that demography of displaced families and limiting their ability to cope. In Wanlaweyn district has a population of 39,000 household, the town witnessed series of forced eviction following conflicts between AS and local clan militias affiliate to the communities. There are 6 IDP camps in Wanlaweyn according to CCCM master list with a total population of 6,500 IDPs most of these IDP camps are overcrowded, self-settled makeshift bushes made of locally available materials such as plastics, cartons and worn-out clothes. In Afgoye hosts an estimated population of 21,602 people and a total of 50 IDP sites according to the last verification exercise with a total population of 4,757 households which equals 25,680 individuals due to the Concurrent shocks and operations in the area, displaced families consist of vulnerable group’s women and children. A good number of these population is forced to reside in overcrowded, established IDP sites with limited protection due to nature of shelters. In addition Marka has a total of 5 idp camp hosting 4,570 households (23591 )individuals according to the last verification conducted June 2021 received, majority of these families were displaced from other villages in lower Shabelle due to insecurity and forceful eviction The majority of IDPs settle in informal and unplanned settlements where the conditions are very poor and forced eviction is a common threat, Most IDP settlements are managed by gatekeepers who often act as informal managers: Gatekeepers are diverse group of people ranging from community leaders, to business men, to landlords/owners, some are from the IDP communities while others are from the host community, equitable access to humanitarian aid for IDPs is disproportionately lower than in marka.
The project seeks to address identified frontline humanitarian needs for IDPs by establishing camp coordination structures at the site-level that improve living conditions and protection of IDPs and ensure equitable access to services and assistance for all members of the community. These aims are achieved through regular services mapping, site monitoring, site verification, safety audit, the establishment of feedback and complaint mechanism, and strengthening the governance of the sites

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

The project will make deliberate effort to consult and involve the participation of members of minority clans and or ethnic groups in the design, planning, implementation and participatory monitoring and evaluation. The camp management committees will be constituted to include all gender(female and male) drawn from both majority and minority ethnic groups. Their views and decisions will inform the project cycle. To prevent sexual exploitation and Gender based Violence (GBV) against girls and women, the project will ensure provision of adequate and where possible solar lighting in the camp at night. The project will ensure provision of separate latrines and bathing shelters for men and women. Further, to ensure safety of girls and women as well that of other vulnerable groups including children, the elderly and the disabled and sick, the project will ensure that distances to sanitation facilities and water points strictly adherence of sphere standards and that the number of sanitation facilities and water points are also according to the standards so as to avoid congestion and risks associated with it. While addressing cases of sexual exploitation and GBV, confidentiality, and privacy will be respected and ensured in all forms of consultations, counseling, and information sharing with the affected persons. Only qualified psychosocial workers or trained social/para-legal workers will be entrusted to receive and counsel survivors of violations, especially when dealing with children and/or survivors. Besides, the project will create an effective referral pathway for GBV incidents and ensure that the local authority provide security within and around the camp to avert incidents of violence within the camp. Community consultations at all layers of society will be ensured to identify and respond to Protection needs and promote meaningful access to assistance and services, in proportion to the need and without any barriers such as discrimination or disability of all individuals and groups in particular those who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. With regard to limiting exposure of displaced boys and girls to violence, the project will advocate for education and life skills activities within the camp to prevent the recruitment of young boys and girls to criminal groups. The project's activities will be implemented in line with principles of Do No Harm and conflict sensitivity to the risk of poor community or stakeholder acceptance. The project will ensure that provision of humanitarian assistance is made in a safe and accessible place by all affected populations including the disabled, women children, and elderly people. In addition, the project will establish complaints and feedback mechanisms in all the sites where the vulnerable communities can channel their complaint or feedback regarding humanitarian assistance in their sites. A community-based complaints desk will be established in each location. The project will build the capacity of self-protection within the IDPs and host community and assist them to claim their rights, including the right to shelter, food, adequate water and sanitation, health, and education. To mitigate risks associated with land ownership disputes, the project will work with HLP partner and local authorities to ensure that the IDP site have well-documented land ownership. Further the project will conduct monthly eviction monitoring to ensure that the affected IDP population is not unlawfully evicted, and to ensure and promote durable solutions, the project will work with federal and state governments in an effort to contribute to displaced people being permanently resettled.
