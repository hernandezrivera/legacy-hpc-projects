Project Summary

In the scope of this project, IRC will ensure that affected populations have safe and reliable access to water and sanitation infrastructure, access to basic items and that they can meet their basic needs whilst protecting their livelihoods.
IRC will provide a range of intervention to support access to WASH infrastructure (rehabilitation/construction of water points, work with Water Management Committees, support to construction of emergency or household’s latrines, distribution of NFI wash related items, development of hygiene promotion focusing on prevention of waterborne diseases such as cholera) and food security (distribution of food voucher or cash to vulnerable households, Train clients on financial management and Facilitate gender discussions among client couples in the community).
IRC will continue to use data from protection monitoring activities and safety audits to strengthen its response to life-threatening protection risks and psychosocial issues, with a focus on women and children at risk and victims of human rights violations, providing a tailored individual and community response; this response will be PSS, case management, distribution of IPA, referral to other services… depending on the needs of the person.
IRC will strengthen linkages with community-based organizations (CBOs) and health facilities to ensure an extensive and multi-level capacity response to survivors of GBV, protection risks and violations against children. IRC will train, revitalize these structures and give them monthly support to ensure they can support their community members. IRC will also implement girls shine approach whenever possible.
Finally, IRC will support community-based protection approaches through its paralegal approach in the FN to provide holistic and community-based legal awareness, counselling and assistance to the most vulnerable by strengthening the community paralegal approach. Equally, IRC will support equitable access to justice through justice system strengthening as well as provision of legal awareness, legal counseling and legal assistance to those most at risk, including access to civil status documentation and housing, land and property rights

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon continues to be impacted by three complex, protracted, humanitarian crises resulting in structural development deficits and chronic vulnerabilities that further impede the long-term recovery of affected people. Humanitarian needs continue to be extensive with an estimated 5 million people in need across the country and including those from the Far North (FN) the North-West (NW) and South-West (SW) regions. Humanitarian needs continue to be extensive with an estimated 1.2million people in need in the FN and 1.5 million people in need in the NW and SW regions (HNO 2022). The ongoing security crisis in the FN has led to the displacement of 608,000 people (358,000 IDPs, 115,000 Nigerian refugees, and 135,000 returnees), while the violence in the NW/SW has resulted to the displacement of more than 788 000 people (MSNA nov 2022).In addition to massive displacement, Cameroon continues to face cholera epidemics and floods. The Ministry of Public Health confirmed a cholera outbreak in the SW region in October 2021 that lasted for several months and impacted almost 6,000 people; another one was declared in the Far North region in October 2022 and is still ongoing. Floods in the FN in September 2022 throughout the end of November have affected more than 300,000 people including the death of at least 23 persons and displacement of more than 100,000 people especially in Kousseri. According to the HNO 2023, 1.9 million people require WASH assistance. Displaced populations and vulnerable host communities are particularly at risk of diseases as they do not have access to adequate shelter, households, and hygiene items. Food security and populations’ capacities to meet their basic needs as well as generating income are severely impacted; more than 1 million of people in the NWSW and more than 785,000 in the Far North region are in crisis to urgency phases (IPC phase 3 and 4) while 1.2M are in stress level (IPC 2) in the FN region and 1.6M in the NWSW. The situation will worsen in projected period for the FN region during the lean season with an approximate 959,609 people in phase 3 and 4 and almost 1.5M in phase 2. The ongoing humanitarian crises exacerbates inequalities and increases the vulnerability of women and girls, boys and men. 8,161 cases of GBV (23% of which children) were recorded between January and July 2022, through the GBVIMS. As women and girls are often afraid to report abuse for fear of retaliation, stigma/shame, and social restrictions, the number of cases remains highly underreported. 88% of reported GBV cases were perpetrated by an intimate partner and/or member of the family. Girls are exposed to GBV as their families force them into child marriage as a negative coping mechanism. Furthermore, IRC reported more than 7 781 protection incidents since January 2022 in the FN and NWSW regions. The majority of incidents recorded are related to arbitrary arrest , kidnapping, murder, torture Inhuman treatment, Threat to Life and personal security, extortion of property/looting, property destruction. In the North-West and South-West regions, men and young men remain the primary victims of recorded protection incidents, and are especially exposed to torture or inhuman treatment, theft, extortion, and arbitrary or unlawful arrest and/or detention.
