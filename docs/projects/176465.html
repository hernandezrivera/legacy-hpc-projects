Project Summary

Impact Health Organization (IHO) proposes the Provision of Basic Emergency Integrated Primary Health Services for vulnerable and underserved IDP, Host and Returnee populations, in Aweil North, Aweil South, Terekeka and Magwi County project under the South Sudan Humanitarian plan in the year 2021. The project will meet the cluster response activities with aim to improve access to and utilization of essential health care services by all people in need, in order to reduce excess morbidity and mortality. According to the Interagency assessment, as well as IHO needs assessment in Magwi Eastern Equatoria, Terekeka County, Central Equatoria, Aweil North and Aweil South County, Northern. Bahr el. Ghazal South Sudan, concluded that the health systems in the four locations were not only challenged with limited access to human resource medicine and other supplies but also limited access to blood transfusion services, obstetric care service, mental health services but also limited access to WASH services but the referral system is hampered by poor roads. The community has limited access to health education, Immunization coverage is still low. The leading causes of morbidity include Malaria, Pneumonia, and Watery Diarrhea among children, HIV/AIDS and Tuberculosis.
The IPC analysis December 2020 classified Aweil south under IPC phase 5, Terekeka, Aweil North under IPC phase 4 and For May-August 2020 projection analysis it was reported that the acute malnutrition situation of Magwi County is likely to deteriorate during the lean season. With exception of Magwi County, the 3 counties of Aweil South, Aweil North and Terekeka have been hit by heavy floods that have left scores displaced and the health system and access to health services in dire state. In order to encounter the above risks, the project seeks to decrease excess morbidity and mortality rates from illnesses, such as malaria, diarrhoea, acute respiratory infections, and vaccine preventable illnesses, such as measles, IHO undertaking managment of illiness including malaria RDT screening and treatment and as well as measles vaccination campaign. The project will also provide access to life-saving essential healthcare, including mental health to women, men, girls and boys, including ability challenged persons including deliveries conducted by skilled birth attendants, provide OPD consultations, provide mentally ill persons with MHPSS services and SGBV health. To reduce vulnerability of women, men, girls and boys at risk of mortality and morbidity as well as protection incidents/threats in priority areas the project will undertake protection monitoring, advocacy, awareness raising and risk mitigation, The project will integrate water, sanitation and hygiene, nutrition with health, including sexual and reproductive health to improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services. To reduce the suffering of girls, boys, women, men, older persons, persons with disabilities, and other persons with specific needs at risk of, or who experienced violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect, including gender-based violence, the project will increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly. To strengthen the feedback mechanism, the project will consult the population groups targeted to receive assistance throughout the entire cycle of the response by establishing community feedback mechanism and lastly the project will strengthen coordination and contextual analysis of needs by working closely with the state ministry of health an county health departments to meet the emergency needs of 19483 individuals (Men 3897, Women 4871, Boys 5260 and Girls 5455) 20% Terekeka, 25% Magwi, 30% Aweil South and 25% Aweil North.

1 - Needs

Decades of protracted conflicts, seasonal flooding, economic and political instability continue to negatively impact access to essential health services in South Sudan. Recurrent disease outbreak, poor immunization structures, inadequate qualified health personnel, and high malnutrition rates contribute to high morbidity and mortality rates reported for children under 5 years across the country. In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. According to the December 2020 Integrated Food security phase classification (IPC), an estimated 7.24 million people (60% of the population) will face severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, among whom an estimated 2 467 000 will be facing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and 31000 people in Catastrophe (IPC phase 5). IPC projection (April- July 2021) puts Aweil South (IPC phase 5), Terekeka (IPC phase 4) , Aweil North IPC phase 4) and Magwi (IPC phase 3) among the counties that are most likely to be affectedThese continuous and emergent crises have contributed to South Sudan’s poor health indicators including an immunization coverage rate of less than 50%, under five mortality rate is 90.7 per 1000 live births(CME 2016) and a maternal mortality rate of 789 deaths per 1000 persons(WHO 2015). Majority of the population (about 56%) reside more than a 5km walking distance from the nearest health facility (This is in line with IHO 2019 Aweil South baseline assessment). This is further coupled to inadequate qualified health care practitioners, inadequate access to supplies and medical equipment, poor health workers competencies and morale. Sociocultural barriers have also limited uptake and utilization of primary health care servicesThese targeted counties are inhabited by an extremely vulnerable and traumatized community majority being women, girls, boys, and elderly people, persons with disabilities who are physically and psychologically exposed to multitude of stressors(floods, conflicts, disease outbreaks etc).The Terekeka Inter- Agencies Rapid Need Assessment Report on flooding in Terekeka County/ CES 4th to 6th of November 2020 (RRC, IHO AFOD, Plan international, Plan, SNCDI, SSRC, ACSO, SAADO, HealthNetTPO, TAYA, NPA) and Aweil North inter-agency needs assessment conducted between 12th -13th of octomber 2020 by several organisations (IHO, Health TPO, ARC, SAADO, CONCERN worldwide, world vision and welt hunger hilfe) reported that 2020 floods has affected the two counties severely and left women, children and elderly people more affected and displaced, exposing them to health risk conditions, malnutrition, food insecurity and improper shelter. In addition health facilities were left water looged and cases of mortality and morbidity due to malaria increased. The inter-agency assessment (CINA, RRC, AVSI, IOM, SPEDP, SCI, SSRC, HLSS, ST. MONICA, ICAP, STERWARD WOMEN, FARMSTEW, GIZ, IHO, PLAN international, GLOBAL ARM, LWF, IMA GLOBAL WORLD HEALTH) conducted in Magwi January 2021, indicated that over 598 individuals were displaced from Torit County into Magwi County due inter-community fighting in areas of Magwi, this coupled with the influx of returnees estimated at 11000 in the last 12 months ( AVSI 2020 Magwi-multispectral survey report). This possess a great gap in terms of health services. In order to prevent further more escalation of disease outbreaks and loss of lives, IHO will ensure the provision of lifesaving quality health service to 25977 individuals (Men 5175, Women 6469, Boys 6987 and Girls 7246) 20% Terekeka, 25% Magwi, 30% Aweil South and 25% Aweil North.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IHO has a policy of zero tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IHO staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IHO Contractors. To provide beneficiaries with information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), IHO has PSEA focal person, who will plan and ensure communities are reached with PSEA information, in addition, IHO will conduct PSEA training with beneficiaries. IHO will Conduct Awareness Raising in the community on causes of SEA, how beneficiaries can report SEA, how to handle and direct complaints and receiving and responding to Complaints. This will be communicated during trainings, Health services provision sessions, Community meeting, outreach, radio talks and use of IEC materials.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IHO will facilitate the provision of feedback from the most vulnerable people on the services provided through feedback meeting, consultations, feedback questionnaires conducted on quarterly and monthly basis. In addition the project will undertake a baseline study to establish the needs of vulnerable members and end line study to understand the how the Vulnerable members benefited from the project

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project frontlines beneficiaries as prayers for the project, to facilitate the feedback mechanism, IHO will hold quarterly feedback meeting through will which beneficiaries’ men, women, boys and girls will understand the project operation and achievement and share way to have better outcomes.The project provide will also provide feedback during the monitoring visits by local leaders and key groups in the community.IHO will administer a perception feedback questionnaire to beneficiaries with an aim give feedback on the services received and suggest way to improve where necessary.Finally at the end of the project, IHO will also conduct evaluation survey involving men, women, girls and boys and local leaders. The feedback Information will be available to local communities in local languages were possible.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IHO will use beneficiary feedback mechanisms to ensure greater accountability, hence IHO will be accountable to the beneficiaries, hence will ensure that projects indicators and targets are met, the beneficiary feedback will help to priorities the community needs and align the project centering on people’s needs. Beneficiary feedback will evaluate the project performance in terms of relevant to the context in which you are operating.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

To mitigate the project protection risks IHO will;Ensure that the LOCATION of health facilities and routes to them are away from actual or potential threats such as violence; especially the risk or threat of gender based violence (GBV), and attacks from armed groups;Make INFRASTRUCTURE adaptations such as latrines so that all individuals and groups can access and use them in safety and dignity. Use direct observation and discussion groups with persons with disabilities in the community to identify the type of adaptations that are needed. Health facilities need latrines. DESIGN must preserve the safety and dignity of its users.Ensure that the health services are RESPECTFUL AND INCLUSIVE OF CULTURAL AND RELIGIOUS PRACTICE.Ensure that CONFIDENTIALITY AND PRIVACY is respected in any form of consultation, counseling or personal information sharing.If setting up Health facilities for displaced communities, consult them as well as host communities about health needs so as to AVOID COMMUNITY TENSIONS. Make sure that there is no tension or inequality that could lead to violence and harassment of one group or anotherEnsure that the health facilities are accessible to allEnsure that services can be accessed by PERSONS WITH REDUCED MOBILITY (e.g. persons with physical disabilities, the elderly, bed-ridden individuals)Ensure that services can be accessed by PERSONS WITH NON-MOBILITY-RELATED DISABILITIES (e.g. the blind, hard of hearing, intellectually disabled)MONITOR access, discrimination, and whether any services are being diverted.
