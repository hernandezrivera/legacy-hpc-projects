Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to provide humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable populations affected by the crisis living in unhealthy, unsafe and inadequate conditions in the province of Cabo Delgado and Nampula, through the provision of emergency or transitional shelter solutions and NFI assistance.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Informed by the finding of the presently on-going assessment at Cabo Delgado level, NRC aim to ensure access to a home for vulnerable population affected by the crisis, by providing NFIs and shelter solutions that respond to requirements of People with Special Needs (PSN), and vulnerable women and girls.The NRC integrated building homes & Communities approach includes more than a dignified “roof”, looking also at increasing resilience, improving health and hygiene conditions, connecting people and markets for increased livelihood conditions and fostering integration and lasting communities’ ties. Vulnerable IDPs, population in risk of displacement and Host communities will be targeted, according to Cluster lead guidance’s and in coordination with local authorities plans.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

NRC Shelter response will prioritize transitional upgradable design, locally sourced materials and traditional knowledges and construction know-how, in close coordination with sectorial shelter solutions. Priority will be given to a first-line response shelter model that can be further upgraded with locally sourced material and traditional local techniques (i.e.: from tarpaulin to adobe on a bamboos structure). Nevertheless, considering the geoclimatic characteristics of the area of intervention prone to natural disasters, a mixed materials design will strike the balance between local material/traditional techniques and improvement for resilience to (i.e.) flooding and cyclones. According to needs, the upgradable design could plan for concrete foundations and base, anchoring roofing, shapes to reduce impact of strong blowing winds, etc. Act 1: Provision of transitional shelters to the most vulnerable IDPs households relocated to the identified sites, adapting sectorial shelter designs to the specific needs of PSN, under the “building homes” approach. This may include, among others, shelter access adaptation, privacy screens, kitchen units, to be determined based on community consultation, in particular with women. A mixed-implementation modality will include in-kind provision of construction materials (i.e.: using wood from controlled plantations to avoid deforestation or locally sourced bamboo), technical assistance from NRC team and sourcing of labour from the target community for higher acceptance and to promote livelihood opportunities. Act 2: Provision of retrofitting and up-grading material and technical assistance from NRC for the most vulnerable host families and the hosted IDPs, living in overcrowded and unhealthy conditions. Need-based improvement and retrofitting will primarily target protection, dignity, and public health sub-standards. As such, up-grade may include doors and windows for safety and security, partitions for protection and privacy, mosquito nets at windows and drainage to improve vector control, water harvesting to increase water availability for cleaning and domestic uses. Act 3: Provision of essential NFI kits, complementing the shelter response. Based on needs and protection vulnerabilities and complementing others actor’s intervention, most vulnerable HH may receive complementary NFI materials (including hygiene and household items). Where conditions allow, NRC would promote a market-based intervention to enable target households to address specific needs and requirements and build their home, enhancing the response relevancy Act 4: Need Assessment conducted through KII, focus groups, direct observations, market assessment in the districts of Cabo Delgado and Nampula. More detailed geographical targeting will depend on suggestions by the sector leads as most in lack of data and information.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

In addition to above mentioned details, as part of a Do-No-Harm approach, and for a mainstreamed conflict sensitivity approach, NRC will work with both IDPs and host communities not to exacerbate inter-communal tensions. In particular, the provision of shelters to IDPs will alleviate the burden on local communities and will contribute to regain access to communal infrastructures like schools, community, and agricultural centres. On the other side the direct support in retrofitting and up-grading existing host communities’ dwellings will support a sustainable integration process and improve poor living conditions of most vulnerable host families. The Building-back-safer approach will benefit the host, as much as the hosted targeted population. This could have a potential positive spill-over effect in the overall community, where improved living conditions in targeted HH could lead to increased acceptance of the continuously growing IDPs population. If required, HLP support will be integrated into the response to solve disputes related to access to land for IDPs (the budget for such potential integrated HLP activities is NOT included in the below Budget table).

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

the project will consider opinions and capacities of the target population, involving and creating ownership of the beneficiaries during all phases of the project.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Where locally present, People with Special Needs PSN’s committees will be included or ad-hoc focus groups will be consulted for the revision of shelters design, adapting sectorial shelter designs to their specific needs. This may include, among others, shelter access adaptation, privacy screens, kitchen units dimensioning, wheel-chair drainage friendly design, lightening, ramps, handrails, etc... to be determined based on PSN’s community consultation. To reach out to a representative sample of PSN unable to attend consultation sessions in the community, NRC team will take care to include households visits at their dwellings during assessment, planning, implementation, and M&E activities.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The intervention will strive to limit environmental impact through the selection of appropriate construction materials and local solutions, reducing at the maximum extent plastic in packaging and ensuring non-biodegradable waste will be safely disposed after distributions. NRC will work in consultation with host/IDPs communities to support relocation with consideration for existing community, future shelters/infrastructure, fire/flood mitigation as well as environmental impact, under the overarching NRC “building communities” approach. The environmental aspects of the intervention, looking at sourcing of natural material, as much as overstretched water resources and cooking fuel, have also a crosscutting protection relevance on potential conflicts that may arise in the community due to competition for natural resources. The response will therefore take into consideration the environmental aspect in close connection with the relevant protection considerations.
