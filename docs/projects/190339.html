Project Summary

THE PROJECT ALREADY SECURED FUNDS AND IS CURRENTLY UNDER IMPLEMENTATION.
The humanitarian context in Amhara and Afar regions of Northern Ethiopia under critical situation where remains in dire humanitarian situation. The armed conflict has been severely impacting the living condition of displaced people and creating secondary displacement which has made the situation much worse. According to the Amhara regional DPFSPCC report, over 2.3 million people were displaced from their original place, of which more than 1.4 million IDPs across the region1. In the second half of 2021, armed conflict significantly increased the number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in Amhara region with high displacement figures especially in South Wello, North Shewa and North Gondar zones. Among the total displaced 490,113 HHs2 in City administrations, an estimated 480,401 IDPs3 (based on the DTM’s report) were returned starting in mid-December 2021 onward in South Wollo and North Shewa Zones. The majority of IDPs are women and children4.

Almost all IDPs have already been displaced from their homes during the conflict, and have lost their jobs, and thus their livelihoods. Most conflict affected people home burnt or damaged, household properties and productive livelihood assets either looted or destroyed. The living conditions of recently returned people and host communities remain the most at risk in very critical situation and require an immediate support more specifically on the areas of emergency Food and NFI5.
Thanks to improved security situation, direct intervention in the area is now (as of December 2021) possible. In response to dire humanitarian situation in the regions, Caritas Switzerland (CACH) committed to contribute to meet life-saving assistance to affected people in terms of essential ESNFI with the aim of supporting the pressing needs of the most vulnerable 1504 HHs (602 female) affected by conflict. In consultation with Regional BOFEC, DPFSPCC, ECC, and ESNFI cluster the focus of the intervention lies on the distribution of essential NFIs as food items under distribution by GoE and other UN agencies.
Project intervention area and beneficiaries: The project is implemented in Humera Woreda. After rapid assessment and in consultation with local government stakeholders, direct beneficiaries of the project will be the most vulnerable returnees, host and IDPs in the targeted area who are devoid of assets and income. Beneficiary selection committee will be established; selection and verification of beneficiaries will be carried out based on agreed set criteria. The most vulnerable beneficiaries will be identified to provide essential NFI support in targeted area. The project will be implemented by CACH, however, day to day implementation and ground level operation and coordination mainly carried out by ECCSDCO in close collaboration with concerned sector offices, regional ECC and ESNFI cluster. The most at-risk community group and PWD will be addressed with especial emphasis.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The action was developed in compliance with the Core Humanitarian Standards and its adaptation Humanitarian Inclusion Standards for Older People and People with Disabilities. First, the objective of the project is to find the optimal success path that synergistically enables coevolution to occur between the implementing Organisations and the recipients benefitting from this project’s interventions. The project will implement transparently a gamut of safe and trusted information/communication, and Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) measures and will foster local capacity through committed participatory partnerships. What this means in terms is that clear roles and responsibilities are delineated with representatives, community leaders, and committees for the establishment of selection criteria for distributions, technical distribution procedures, planning and implementing response activities. Furthermore, enabling people to have a voice, including receiving and addressing complaints, is recognized as a key component of accountability to the communities that the project supports. In order to strengthen the project’s accountability towards affected populations, CACH will set up a Complaints Handling Mechanism (an inherent part of CACH Code of Conduct) accessible to all stakeholders of a project. This involves instituting a relevant platform through which affected populations can measure interventions, voice concerns and complaints. Both organisations will closely monitor beneficiaries’ feedback through a myriad of formats.Then, the project closes the feedback loop by incorporating voiced concerns into its decision-making management system and commits to addressing such matters within a specified and quick turnaround. The project’s feedback systems are calibrated to the specific project environment and, in the context of the project at hand, of: 1) A dedicated feedback phone number; 2) A feedback box for written complaints and suggestions installed either at CACH offices or at district/woreda offices; 3) Community meetings (which entails as well, organizing women-specific meetings); 4) Verbal feedback received by partner organizations and staff; and, 5) Close collaboration with local stakeholders to keep them informed about plans, implementation, while helping establishing, and monitoring realistic goals. The use of different feedback mechanisms allows for people with impairments or specific needs to use the channel most appropriate to their needs. For example, it is expected that older people will prefer lodging complaints through the phone; whereas people with hearing or speaking impairments may prefer complaint boxes. CACH and ACORD staff assigned to the proposed intervention will sensitize beneficiaries about the type and scope of feedback mechanisms. In terms of a conflict sensitive Do No Harm approach, as aforementioned, the project will implement a Social Tension Tracking and Mapping system (STTM) to keep abreast of the project's impact within its AO.
