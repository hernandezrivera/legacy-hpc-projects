Project Summary

The objective of the project is to address survival needs of the most vulnerable crisis-affected households through the provision of rapid response, Multi- Purpose Cash grants. The project will target 7,400 HHs representing 37000 beneficiaries (based on the calculation 5 numbers per HHs) multipurpose grant in Raqqa, Menbij, Kobane and Deir-ez Zor. Our target for MPCA is higher than last year because we have shifted our programming from in-kind NFI to cash based intervention. This will provide communities facing emergency gap in humanitarian service provision and ability to meet their survival needs.

4 - Needs

In 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in 2021. Throughout 2021, the macro-economic context continued to deteriorate sharply and is accelerating into 2022. The combined effects of currency depreciation, soaring prices, reduced fiscal revenue and increasing domestic debt, widespread job loss as well as unilateral coercive measures have plunged additional segments of the population into humanitarian need, most notably in areas historically less affected by hostilities and displacement. The conflict in Ar-Raqqa and Deir ez Zor between government forces and SDF continues to displace families fleeing political and tribal conflict, thus reducing humanitarian access to the communities most in need. The proposed intervention will aim to address multisectoral, emergent needs of vulnerable, crisis-affected HHs through 3 rounds of MPCA, providing emergency or seasonal support to most vulnerable crisis-affected HHs for 3 months, until they can be linked to other types of assistance services or sources of income. In line with the Cash WG guidelines for Syria, MPCA will not fully meet the basic needs of targeted HHs. Rather, this will allow crisis-affected people to make choices and prioritize their most urgent needs.

5 - Implementation of activities

DCA will utilize MPC as a mechanism for newly displaced HHs or communities with a significant gap in assistance with immediate life-saving emergency assistance. At the community level this will be defined in collaboration with coordination mapping, needs analysis, and severity score. At HH, if not possible to cover entire community in needs HHs with the highest vulnerability score will be selected. In addition to the provision of 3 months of MPC support, DCA will utilise multi- sector assessment data collected at the HH and community level to support communities with linkages to other humanitarian support to ensure survival needs are addressed past the 3- month period. Support will be provided in line with CWG guidelines. Assessment and PDM will be conducted prior to and after cash distributions and include HH level surveys as well as trader and consumer surveys, and KIIs to inform the relevance, feasibility, and subsequent outcomes of MPCA at all levels.. DCA will seek to mitigate possible tension between IDPs, returnees and host communities through the community engagement in identification of vulnerable HHs and clear information regarding entitlements. DCA will ensure that all community stakeholders have access to DCAs feedback and complaints mechanism, through which beneficiaries will have the opportunity to request inclusion. DCA will closely coordinate with HAOs, city and district level authorities and komins throughout the project implementation.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by DCA staff members or partner staff members.Likelihood: LowImpact: HighMitigation measures: DCA Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about the provision of available services and who services are for. DCA PSEA focal point in coordination with HR will conduct training and refreshers to project staff and partner staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). DCA will establish a complaint box that will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentiality. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by the member of complaints handling team or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. DCA follows principles of Do No Harm across its project activities.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project location impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas and threated the safety and security of DCA project staff and beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigation Measures: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security Standard Operating Procedures (SOP), and conducts regular security- related training for staffs. Project Management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation, and evacuation plans remain in place.Risk: Local authority- imposed COVID-19 curfews in NES prevent DCA project staff from moving and/ or working, and/ or local authority-imposed curfews in NES/ KRI prevent hawala agents from transferring money for distribution and salaries. Assumptions: Project locations will remain accessible and DCA will continue to have permission to operate (through modified activities to protect beneficiaries and staff) and Hawalas will be able to continue transferring money. Mitigation measures: DCA staff will follow local authority- imposed curfews and restrictions related to COVID-19. DCA will regularly communicate with hawala agents to ensure supply chains are operational. DCA will communicate with other NGOs and NES Forum should selected hawala agent is not able to transfer funds.
