1 - Needs

From 15 April 2023, armed clashes erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in multiple cities across Sudan, including Khartoum, Al Fasher, Kebkabiya, Merowe, Nyala, Zalingi, Ag Geneina, and El Obeid. DTM Sudan has been receiving reports of widespread displacement across diﬀerent states in Sudan due to the ongoing security situation. DTM estimates that approximately 1,210,214 individuals (242,666 households) have been displaced internally as a result of the conflict. IDPs have been displaced from six states in Sudan.As a result of continued violence such as this, an estimated 24.7 million people, require urgent humanitarian assistance and protection. The number of PiN of humanitarian assistance has increased from 15.8 M, repre¬senting a 57 % increaseVulnerable population groups are increasingly exposed to protection risks with immediate as well as longer-term harm to their physical and mental well-being. This includes an increased number of women, girls and at-risk groups, who are exposed to risks of gender-based violence GBV and sexual exploitation and abuse SEA.The fighting in Sudan has caused 30 attacks on healthcare facilities, resulting in 8 deaths and 18 injuries. Power shortages, limited medical supplies, and infrastructure damage are hampering the delivery of essential health care. In Khartoum alone, life-saving reproductive health for an estimated 219,000 pregnant women is threatened. Several cold chain facilities have been looted and damaged, including the loss of over 600,000 vaccines intended for children. The central medical store warehouse is under the full control of one party to the conflict.The nutrition crisis in Sudan is dire, with UNICEF reporting the highest rate of child malnutrition globally. Approximately 3 million children under age 5 are affected by malnutrition, including over 610,000 suffering from severe acute malnutrition. The suspension of aid has disrupted treatment program for 50,000 children suffering severe acute malnutrition, putting their lives at risk.18 In addition, a factory that was producing vital supplies for the treatment of malnourished children has been burnt down, destroying supplies to treat 14,500 children.The factory produced 60% of ready-to-use therapeutic food used by UNICEF to treat children with severe acute malnutrition, as well as ready-to-use supplementary food provided by WFP for children with moderate acute malnutrition.Food production, supply chain disruptions, fuel shortages, inflation and currency depreciation have led to shortages and price hikes, making essential goods unaffordable for many. Imported staples such as wheat flour, oil and tomato paste are scarce. WFP expects the number of acutely food insecure to increase to 19.1 million people in the next 3-6 months.The destruction of critical infrastructure, including water systems and communications networks, is having devasting impacts across Sudan. Water short¬ages have been reported across Khartoum state, as urban water treatment plants remain non-functional. Water authorities are unable to repair damaged infrastructure due to access and security obstacles. As a result, more than a million people rely on unsafe drinking water from the River Nile, increasing the risk of water-borne disease and the need to undertake awareness raising around water quality and water safety practices.Education has been severely affected, with schools and educational institutions remaining closed in conflict-affected areas, namely Khartoum, Aj Jazirah, South Darfur, West Darfur and West Kordofan. With approximately 6.9 million children not attending school before the conflict of 23, the learning crisis has deepened with higher levels of risk of physical and mental threats, including recruitment into armed groups.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

AORD is committed to promote the Protection from and Response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Sexual Harassment (SH) and expects measures in support thereof to be mainstreamed throughout entire humanitarian programmes. To ensure gender and protection are mainstreamed in the program; AORD will be abided by Do No Harm principles. In terms of staffing, AORD will strive for gender balance in the recruitment of staff at all levels. Project staff and community leaders will be trained on gender issues PSEA and gender-sensitive approaches Protection will be incorporated across all sector interventions to promote meaningful access, safety and dignity during the implementation of the project. The approach will be based on Protection Principles as per the Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS) including prioritization of safety & dignity, and avoiding causing harm by preventing and minimizing any unintended negative effects of the interventions which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Targeted people will be granted access to assistance and services based on identified needs and without any barriers or discrimination. The project will provide special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable to accessing assistance and services such as disabled individuals, women and children. Community groups and targeted individuals including children will be supported to develop their self-protection capacities and ability to claim their rights to all services. In this regard, children and adolescents will be engaged in the planning, implementation and monitoring of this project through child-friendly feedback and complaints mechanisms, as well as conducting consultation with children during the implementation on issues in implementation, monitoring or evaluation targeting girls and boys collectively or individually to inform decision-making processes concerning their care, protection and other rights.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

AORD is responsible and will be held accountable by the people it seeks to assist. In order for humanitarian assistance to be effective, individuals accessing it must be able to receive clear, concise communications, input into planned activities, feedback on the provision of humanitarian assistance, and participation in decisions affecting their lives are all important. AORD will ensure AAP in line with humanitarian standards. To ensure “Do No Harm”, methods of receiving feedback confidentiality will be identified and communicated to community members appropriately to avoid exposure to further risks from sensitive issues. As appropriate, mechanisms and approaches will be tailored to the needs of both IDPs and host communities. AORD is committed to aligning to the CHS focusing on standard # 5 Community feedback mechanism and response. To ensure the quality and accountability of the project, AORD will promote transparency, meaningful participation and inclusion through the following steps: 1. Enable the targeted populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence 2. Share project information at an early stage and on an ongoing basis with girls and boys, parents and caregivers, community leaders, and government stakeholders; 2. Host regular consultations/ listening forums with established groups, including women, boys and girls; 3. Ensure partner communities are fully aware of PSEA, Protection and Safeguarding Policy including how to make complaints or raise issues. 4. Share information on progress and lessons in the relevant coordination groups and key messages on child participation 5. Ensure a robust and accessible complaints mechanism and response procedure is put in place and appropriate for different groups. The mechanism and timelines for expecting responses and how complaints and feedback are managed to inform programming; 6. Ensure that all staff, and stakeholders, are familiar with existing referral pathways both to service providers for supporting identified cases and referring to other agencies. In facilities, posters and leaflets will be posted and shared on program objectives, key activities, and ways in which beneficiaries can provide feedback to the implementing partner. Feedback will be collected where appropriate via FGDs, interviews, boxes, and a dedicated call-in number and meetings. Compliance boxes at the community level are the mechanism put in place by AORD for suggestions and complaints(SGBV), ensuring proper periodic review, analysis, reporting, and documentation.Through capacity building and referral pathways, AORD will increase knowledge of AAP, GBV, and disability among service providers and community nutrition workers. AORD proposed some of the interventions including training of nutrition staff on GBV and PSEA.
