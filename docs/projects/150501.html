Project Summary

The proposed project will target the most vulnerable families in Area C and in Hebron’s H2 area. These groups suffer from no, or limited, access to water and sanitation services due to Israeli restrictions on construction and development in the area. This makes them particularly vulnerable to the threat of forced displacement. The target communities are farming and herding families dependant on livestock and agricultural activities to maintain their livelihoods and, as such, heavily reliant on water availability, quality and affordability. Lack of water has forced families to adopt negative coping mechanisms such as reducing their consumption to an average of less than 30 l/d/c (Area C Water Master Plan 2017) depending on unsafe water resources or reducing their expenditure on basic needs such as health and education to afford the high cost of water.
(1) By constructing and rehabilitating water networks, filling points and household cisterns, the proposed project will decrease the target communities’ dependency on trucked water as well as deter them from sourcing unsafe water.
(2) The project will also improve the communities’ access to sanitation facilities through the construction and provision of household WASH facilities such as latrines and plastic tanks. Families often disregard these facilities and prioritise other basic needs such as food and water. This negative copying mechanism has left many households in poor health, hygiene, privacy and dignity conditions.
(3) Thus, to complement these activities, AAH will also carry out training and awareness workshops on subjects ranging from hygiene promotion to water saving techniques and cistern management.
Ultimately, by mitigating the effects of the Israeli occupation on water access and thus improving the target communities’ livelihood prospects, the proposed project will reduce their exposure to forced displacement and provide them with the means to show resilience in the face of these challenges. The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment.

Needs

According to the 2018 HNO, 156,000 people across the West Bank are either unconnected to a water network or receiving water once a week or less. Area C communities in the West Bank, particularly Southern Hebron, East Jerusalem periphery and the Jordan Valley, are particularly affected by this issue. Approximately 270,000 Palestinians in Area C are directly affected by the Israeli restrictions and control over WASH-related infrastructure, such as water and sanitation networks. Assessments completed by AAH, in August 2018, in Area C, indicate that water scarcity is one of the main factors leading vulnerable families to poverty and food insecurity, as well as further deteriorating living conditions and hygiene practices. Most Bedouin and herding communities in Jericho, Ramallah and the East Jerusalem periphery receive water through old water pipes laid over-ground. Irregular water supply, the small size of old water pipes, high water losses and the destruction of water pipes by settlers and the ICA (e.g. in the R317 communities of South Hebron) restrict access to safe, sufficient and affordable water. This places families in a situation of great financial vulnerability and forces them to displace closer to filling points and water sources during the summer. This movement complicates their return to the communities and places them at risk of permanent displacement due to settler harassment and settlement expansion. Data from AAH’s 2017 Area C Water Master Plan and Water Distribution Modeling, validated by the PWA, highlighted that most of the targeted communities consume less than 30 l/c/d and use rain harvesting cisterns and trucked water as coping mechanisms for the water shortage. Thus, adequate water storage capacity is vital for vulnerable families to harvest rain water in winter and store trucked water during dry months. In July 2018, AAH, through the West Bank Protection Consortium, used a community-based approach to assess 78 communities in the southern, central and northern West Bank. It established that many families in Area C lack latrines and do not have sufficient water storage capacity (< 5m3). Most of them must buy water using small tanks (3.7m3 tractors) instead of large tanks (10m3) with prices up to 30 NIS/m3 as the trucked water cost is affected by road conditions, tank capacity, distance and filling time. Distance from consumer to water source and road conditions also considerably affect the price of trucked water. High water prices and limited income force families to reduce their water consumption to as little as 20-30 l/c/d. Women, many of whom are widowed, disabled or divorced, must borrow water from relatives and neighbors, which is then stored in small containers or kitchen pots. According to AAH’s sex and age disaggregated data and gender analysis, men and women have distinct tasks: men provide payments for water truck services, while women are responsible for the storage and use of water. Women are often the most disadvantaged when families face water shortages or when latrines are not available, specifically for specific needs such as menstrual hygiene. AAH and educational directorates conduct regular assessments and coordination meetings on WASH facilities in schools in Hebron, Bethlehem, East Jerusalem, Tubas, etc. These meetings, field visits and interviews with women, men, students and teachers indicated that many schools are not connected to a regular water supply and/or have poor WASH facilities. Substandard sanitation facilities in schools increase children’s exposure to health risks, unhealthy social environment and the school dropout rate among girls.

Activities

Coordination meetings with the PWA, Ministry of Education (MoE), UNICEF and other international agencies (GVC, CISP and PAH) will avoid gaps and overlapping. Local partners in the targeted locations (municipalities, village councils and RCSD), will collect baseline and end line data. Said partners have been fully involved in the project’s design (identification of target communities and development of technical components). Together with AAH, they will be responsible for monitoring the implementation of activities and for the collection of feedback from project beneficiaries. RCSD staff will be trained on designing and monitoring WASH activities in sensitive areas, their field officers will work directly with AAH technical staff and the targeted groups to ensure the quality of implementation and further empower the beneficiaries. Based on their extensive experience in implementing cistern and road rehabilitation activities, RCSD lead in assessing, designing and implementing said activities. RCSD will also be involved in the final handover of the implemented activities. (1) Rehabilitation and construction of filling points and simple water networks for Bedouin and herding communities in southern Hebron, Bethlehem, Jordan valley, and East Jerusalem periphery. The pipelines will be connected to the PWA and municipality systems according to the Area C Water Master Plan. AAH will also rehabilitate and/or install 25 simple water networks using high-density polyethylene (HDPE) pipes. Wherever possible, pipes will be buried below ground level by the village council or the beneficiaries themselves as a protective measure against ICA uprooting or cutting, for H2 AAH and RCSD will target the families who have poor domestic WASH facilities (internal pipes and fittings) and will also target the water distribution network in the movement restricted neighborhoods of H2 area (2) Construction and rehabilitation of household cisterns in Area C and seam zones. According to the Area C Water Master Plan endorsed by PWA, 30 household cisterns will be constructed in South Hebron and the Jordan valley for communities that cannot be connected to a piped water service. Based on a socio-economic baseline survey, female headed households and families with disabled members will be prioritized. The activities will be implemented directly by the households to reduce the risks and increase time and cost efficiency. (3) Rehabilitation of 9.5 km of roads in Masafer Yatta, road 317 and north Jordan Valley clusters. This will facilitate access to the community and reduce the cost of trucked water by enabling access of larger volume water tankers, which are significantly cheaper than smaller tractor tanks. (4) Construction and rehabilitation of toilets, 6 cisterns and drinking fountains for 8 schools in Hebron H2 and Area C communities. This will improve student’s access to adequate, safe and healthy WASH facilities, and will also improve the general educational environment, thus upholding the children’s right to education. (5) Supply and installation of WASH facilities for the most vulnerable groups in Area C:- 130 latrines (compliant with SPHERE standards and with gender and age disaggregated analysis of hygiene practices and community social attitudes);- 141 plastic tanks (1.5m3/tank) provided to families affected by drought or when security constraints prevent the construction/rehabilitation of cisterns. (6) Community training and awareness workshops on (i) good hygiene practices (prevention of water borne diseases such as diarrhea by promoting hand-washing); (ii) water saving and recycling techniques and; (iii) and cistern management (chlorination). (7) Increase duty bearers’ awareness on causes and solutions to water scarcity, and the related health and economic impacts, through evidence-based advocacy tailored to influence their policies and practices.

Indicators

These interventions are expected to contribute to the safety and dignity of women, men, girls and boys, to do no harm and improve the overall protection of the targeted communities. They will do so by improving access to basic WASH services to sustain livelihoods and avoid/respond to displacement, support the WASH advocacy strategy and improve the coordination platforms. The expected results are the following: 1. Improved water quality for vulnerable families: construction and rehabilitation of water networks will decrease the dependency on trucked water and will deter families from sourcing unsafe water. Catchment area improvement, chlorination of cisterns, hygiene promotion campaigns on hygiene practices and cistern management will encourage behavior changes and contribute to a reduction in waterborne diseases. 2. Construction/rehabilitation of filling points and water networks to increase the availability of water supplies, particularly for women and children in southern and central West Bank. Men and women will participate in the planning, design and implementation of the activities. Families will reduce their expenditure on trucked water, consequently they will have additional disposable income for other basic needs. Women will also need less time to fetch water and will be able to spend more time caring for their children. 3. Improved awareness, knowledge and hygiene practices: Hygiene promotion activities and awareness raising will be provided at household level, with a special focus on women for topics regarding domestic consumption management, as per the previously conducted gender analysis of household roles and responsibilities. The core hygiene promotion messages will be hand washing, efficient use of water, domestic hygiene, cleaning and maintenance of WASH facilities and water storage tanks. 4. Strengthened capacity and resilience of vulnerable communities to respond to adverse environmental conditions resulting from natural disasters: The distribution of water, mobile latrines, shelters and non-food items (plastic tanks, kitchen kits), as well as maintenance training for cistern management and chlorination will improve and strengthen the capacity of vulnerable communities to withstand adverse environmental conditions. 5. Improved information management, coordination and dissemination of information among the cluster members and other stakeholders: The use of GIS and statistical software in data collection will facilitate data comparison and analysis; ad hoc databases will be set up to collect and compare information collected during the project implementation; complemented by AAH’s data management system. Elaborated data will be shared with relevant stakeholders and made available through regular reporting. KAP surveys and general project monitoring and evaluation will be carried out throughout the entire project in coordination with other agencies operating in the area; Action Against Hunger will contribute to exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community and to be used for advocacy purposes. Expected indicators and targets for the project are:• Water expenditure decreased by 30%• Water consumption increased by 30%• Interruptions of water supply in the network decreased by 30%• Water loss in the network decreased by 30%• Number of small expensive water deliveries (with tractors) decreased by 60%• Water storage capacity for 171 families with be improved through the construction/ rehabilitation of water cisterns and the provision of PE tanks• WASH facilities in 8 schools improved through construction/rehabilitation of toilets, cisterns and drinking points• Living conditions for 650 women, men, girls and boys are improved through the supply of mobile latrines• 25 communities improved their access to water through the construction/ rehabilitation of water pipelines• 7 communities improved the accessibility to water resources through the rehabilitation
