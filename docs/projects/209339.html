Project Summary

This a complex +12-month intervention that aims to improve access to emergency services, address urgent needs and provide lifesaving and life sustaining emergency support within WASH to conflict and/or displaced-affected populations and vulnerable host communities through various areas in Darfur, Khartoum and Jazeera. Also, the project aims deploy life-saving WaSH interventions to ensure conflict-affected populations have access to safe drinking water, as well as minimum hygiene standards to mitigate the risk of waterborne diseases and protect people’s dignity. The project design integrates WASH with other sectors in different localities throughout Sudan which corresponds to the most recent crisis to an entrenched regional and national crisis. 3month into the conflict, the total estimate of IDPs across Sudan reached 2.6 million. The critical needs of 5,855,848 IDPs and 1,532,350 mixed cross borders movements (Dec 2023, IOM) add onto those of 25M overall identified by OCHA, and revolve around food, water, hygiene and sanitation, health, nutrition, and emergency shelter. If not addressed, they will lead to crisis into crisis, preventable outbreaks and an increase in the mortality due underlying causes.
Considering the inconceivable suffering of the Sudanese population, TGH have coordinated it efforts to present a comprehensive WASH package of support to the internally displaced population as well as those hosting them in Darfur, Khartoum and Jazeera, as there are the most affected states since the beginning of the conflict in Sudan.
TGH aims to support the communities through:
- Rehabilitation, operation & maintenance of water points.
- Construction and upgrading of hygiene facilities.
- Capacity building and raising awareness towards hygiene promotion, best practices and technical aspects of maintaining WASH facilities.
- Distribution of WASH supplies and solid waste management.
- Water quality monitoring.
The intervention strategy is based on the expertise and local capacity of the implementing partners and aligns with needs illustrated in the revised HRP.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection mainstreaming is a longstanding commitment of TGH, typified by programs which comply with the do-no-harm principle, ensure relevant targeting and promote the inclusion of persons with special needs. This commitment is supported by various policies, in particular Child Safeguarding Policy, Policy on Gender Equality and Protection from Gender Based Violence and Policy for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse – all of which are available upon request. Also, These policies are promoted on a continuous basis, through staff sensitization and training, and through a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism, including a specific mechanism for addressing PSEA-related issues.Within the proposed action, TGH endorses the principles defined by the Global Protection Cluster guidance on Protection Mainstreaming, namely:1. Prioritise safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimise as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks.2. Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services, in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.3. Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.4. Participation & empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education.TGH will work closely with all women, men, girls, boys, including persons with disabilities, minority groups, host communities, IDPs and will ensure service delivery is safe and accessible to all. TGH ensures that its services can be accessed to all based on their vulnerability situation, including people with disabilities. Information sharing and awareness-raising in communities of implementation will target all community members cutting across age, gender, residential status, especially new arrivals in displacement settingsTGH strives for the safety and dignity of the people concerned by the intervention, and to minimize unintended adverse effects of its actions, applying a do-no harm principle to all projects, and protection mainstreaming throughout and across all activities.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

TGH’s feedback mechanisms are designed to allow people of all ages, genders and abilities (including marginalized communities) to voice their feedback, suggestions or complaints. The current mechanism allows individuals to voice their feedback in a secured and anonymous manner. Once the complaint is received, it goes through a loop following a standard operating procedure. Feedback is systematically categorized, answered and closed once the beneficiary is satisfied with the answer. The mechanisms aim to guarantee the availability of appropriate communication channels between the beneficiaries and the project team, while ensuring effective handling of complaints. This reinforces understanding of and trust towards the proposed mechanisms.Depending on the context (e.g. availability of phone coverage) and to accommodate beneficiaries’ preferences and abilities (e.g. literacy skills), the CFRM mechanism in each location consists of one or more of the following channels:Regular consultations with community focal points / community committees by project teams, including on the best way to communicate with them - responses and ideas will help tailor the CFRM to this specific action.Suggestion boxes: will be available in sites where TGH & PUI intervene e.g. health/WASH/youth facilities. Project teams will explain their function. Feedback forms will be available by the box, can be left anonymous and will be collected once a week by the project team.Surveys and group discussions: will be organised jointly by the MEAL and the project team during and after the implementation of activities to monitor whether the assistance was delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner. This feedback channel is punctual and allows TGH & PUI to cross check information received through other channels.TGH team oral feedback: TGH teams are regularly present in the field and collect oral feedback from beneficiaries. This ensures regular feedback and cross-checking of information.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Beneficiaries’ participation is one of the TGH’s principles of action. Involvement of the affected populations ensures that the emergency response is tailored to actual and most urgent needs. Partners will engage with the community at all stages of the project cycle, including the needs assessment, project implementation and any adaptation or adjustment needed based on lessons learned. Participation is ensured mainly through 1) regular discussions with different stakeholders (community leaders, beneficiaries of the action, community members, etc.), 2) a comprehensive Complaint Feedback & Response Mechanism (CFRM); and 3) the involvement of the community in the implementation of the activities, either through their recruitment in the project team as volunteers or through community-based approaches. The CFRM will enable partners to collect beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints and respond to them. The MEAL teams will increase the opportunities for monitoring field visits, interviews and have Focus Group Discussions with beneficiaries to ensure that all of them are given the opportunity to voice their feedback, concerns or suggestions. The involvement of beneficiaries enables Partners to design, and continuously adjust, the project to adapt it to the context and to beneficiaries’ choices.The involvement of the affected population remains at the core of this action to ensure that the emergency response is tailored to actual and most urgent needs. The involvement of local partners, including neighborhood and community committees supports this purpose. Particular attention will be given to conflict sensitivity aspects and to ensuring that affected populations are assisted proportionally to their needs and regardless their origin, tribal ethnicity, etc.Beneficiaries are targeted based on needs and vulnerability criteria and not on status and vulnerability criteria and such situation should be as readable and objective as possible to be fully understood by local communities and checked by program teams Vulnerability criteria are discussed and agreed upon with local stakeholders and community representatives in order to avoid conflict and to foster project ownership. Project adaptations take place during the course of the project, based on the actual interaction between the project and its environment and based on the evolution of the context itself.TGH strictly enforces PSEA and Safeguarding policies. Data protection systems are also in place and explained to the community to ensure the processing is understood by beneficiaries. SEA complaints and fraud/corruption suspicions are treated when detected.
