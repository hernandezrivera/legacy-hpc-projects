1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

TO IMPROVE THE NUTRITIONAL STATUS OF VULNERABLE POPULATIONS (PARTICULARLY PLWS, INFANTS AND YOUNG CHILD UNDER 2, AND CHILDREN 3-5 YEARS), REDUCING ACUTE MALNUTRITION, MORBIDITY AND MORTALITY. Building on FHI 360’s well-established presence and existing service provision points, FHI 360 proposes to expand its comprehensive support to IDPs and affected host communities by providing emergency nutrition prevention and treatment support. The nutrition activities will follow a three-pronged approach through FHI 360’s static facilities, IMMUs, and community mobilization through supported APE networks. Throughout its Nutrition interventions (especially under MIYCN-E activities), FHI360 will also support the Ministry of Health and the Nutrition Cluster in monitoring and reporting violations of the BMS Code. Beneficiaries are host community members and IDPs living with host families or resettlement sites in the target geographies. Most beneficiaries self-select by seeking nutrition and/or medical care at IMMUs or supported static facilities. PLWs and children U5 are specifically targeted with particular attention to adolescent mothers and infants <6 months and under 2 years of age, given their heightened vulnerability. Number of People Targeted: 19,400 (15,400 women, 4,000 men); Number of IDPs Targeted: 5,900 (4,800 women, 1,100 men);

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Geographical areas: Ancuabe and Metuge; Promote and Support Recommended IYCF Interventions: FHI 360 will promote maternal nutrition, recommended IYCF practices, and hygiene/sanitation behaviors with a particular focus on early initiation of breastfeeding, exclusive breastfeeding and introduction of appropriate/safe complementary foods. FHI 360 will support quality IYCF-E services, with micronutrient supplementation and maternal nutrition support. Establish IYCF Corners and Pilot Mother to Mother Support Group: To increase integration with health and protection services, FHI 360 will establish IYCF corners in both of its supported health facilities, as well as at women and girls’ safe spaces. FHI 360 will also establish MTMSGs in the two communities supported by the IMMUs. Pregnant women will be referred to the nearest MTMSG for counselling/peer support on breastfeeding, infant feeding, and maternal nutrition. The IYCF corners will be dedicated, private spaces, comfortable for women and their children where trained IYCF counsellors will be able to provide personalized individual counselling services to those needing some specialistic support. Community-level IYCF-E Counselling and Outreach through APEs: Complementing the above interventions, at the community level, FHI 360 will work through its supported APEs to deliver key IYCF-E messages to households and at the community level. APEs will be trained to conduct rapid assessments and identify caregivers of infants and young children in need of immediate IYCF counselling/support. Pregnant women will be referred to the nearest MTMSG for regular counseling and peer support on breastfeeding, infant feeding, and maternal nutrition. Distribution of micronutrient supplements to pregnant women and children aged 6-59 months: FHI 360 will provide iron folate supplements (for pregnant women), Vitamin A supplements (for children 6-59 months), deworming tablets (for children 6-59 months) twice annually to prevent anemia, as well as micronutrient powders, in line with the Ministry of Health’s minimum nutrition package. To ensure groups targeted for micronutrient supplementations are food secure or are connected to appropriate services, FHI 360 will link recipients of micronutrient supplies to the nearest partner or government-run food security projects and initiatives. Community mobilization, screening, and referrals for community-based management of acute malnutrition services: At the community level, FHI 360 will cover CMAM through community mobilization, active screening through APEs, and growth monitoring at facilities/IMMUs. APEs will be trained/equipped with MUAC tapes to identify, screen, and refer malnourished children U5 using MUAC and checking for bilateral edema. CMAM in Health Facilities and IMMUs: FHI 360 will support its two selected health facilities to manage malnutrition cases of children U5. Both FHI 360-supported facilities will provide OTP for children with SAM with no complications (typically an estimated90% of SAM cases). Meanwhile, cases of SAM with complications will be referred to the nearest SCs, and based on consultations with the Nutrition Cluster, and FHI 360 will also aim to support one SC with incentives, trainings, and quality assurance to improve their quality of services in line with MoH priorities. Trainings of health workers and APEs to manage SAM and MAM: FHI 360 will train medical doctors, medical assistants, Mother and Child Health (MCH) nurses (based in the health facilities and members of the IMMUs) and APEs on screening, case management, and referral of SAM cases using the CMAM approach. Training on FAMILY MUAC for members of the mother supports groups: To increase coverage of the CMAM program and ensure prompt and timely treatment, members of the MTMSGs (and other primary caregivers) will also be trained on Family MUAC and how to regularly assess the nutritional status of their children using MUAC tapes.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Prioritize Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm: To prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the proposed intervention, FHI 360’s Nutrition program will conduct a Do No Harm assessment related to the health activities which can increase both physical and psychosocial vulnerability of the beneficiaries. The assessment will be a series of FGDs with groups disaggregated by vulnerability and gender. The assessment will take place in conjunction with the project’s baseline assessment. Findings from the Do no Harm assessment will inform the development and revision of SOPs and protocols to mitigate protection risks. Examples of programmatic adjustments would be adjusting health facility hours to ensure meaningful access for vulnerable populations and adjusting IEC materials to avoid harmful stereotypes.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

Safe and Meaningful Access: The majority of FHI 360’s nutrition programs occur at static health facilities and in IMMUs. The health facilities and the IMMUs that are supported by FHI 360 are located in resettlement sites in rural areas, over difficult terrain. FHI 360 will work with the staff at each facility to accommodate physically disabled persons. Ramps exist at the static facilities to accommodate wheelchairs which were provided by FHI360. Protocols exist at each facility for accessing outpatient for patients with disabilities. Patients will also be provided support for using the latrines. Adequate spaces are provided for female beneficiaries, the elderly, and people with special needs. Existing toilets are not provided with disability friendly features because it is not feasible to retrofit the size of the doors within the scope of this project, however, newly constructed latrines under the WASH section will consider disability and provide at least one accessible latrine. Trained staff members will be involved in identifying specific health needs for children as well as responding appropriately to cases of orphan or unaccompanied children. All unaccompanied minors will be referred to the Protection Unit to facilitate family tracing and reunification with the family or caregivers. FHI 360 is prepared to support any expansion of these efforts by WFP, UNICEF, or other partners and if in line with the MoH guidelines. Participation and Empowerment: FHI 360’s Nutrition program will work very closely with the SDSMAS. The SDSMAS is obligated to provide Nutrition services to the population of Cabo Delgado according to Human Rights covenants and agreements. FHI 360 will inform the DPS if segments of the population are denied or experience barriers to their right to Nutrition services— either through access, acceptability, quality, and availability. Household Resources and the Role of Women in Caretaking: The proposed activities are aimed at demonstrating the importance of women’s roles in household management and decision-making. One anticipated challenge related to the SC referral is that the activity usually involves the caregiver or mother spending about one week away from her home, leaving other children and household members who rely on her (e.g., elderly, and disabled family members) during her absence. To address this challenge, during IYCF sessions, FHI 360 program implementers will encourage male involvement in childcare so that one parent/caregiver can take the U5 child to the TFC and the other parent/caregiver can remain at home to care for other family members.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The Nutrition activities, both preventative and treatment, will particularly focus on facilitating and ensuring access and inclusivity for women and children with disabilities. At the same time, older people caring for younger children will also be prioritized and supported to ensure an inclusive approach. Nutrition activities at the two supported health facilities, IMMUs, and community levels will integrate disability considerations, and nutrition-related trainings and materials will ensure that all the relevant staff are equipped to provide nutrition support and recommendations that include people living with disability. FHI 360 continuously seeks to mitigate any physical barriers to facilities and supports safe and easy access for PLWDs.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

FHI 360’s environmental health component has primarily focused on solid waste management and drainage around six IDP resettlement sites within Ancuabe and Metuge Districts. Experience from MERCAP and the current IRAMP project indicated that solid waste management and drainage do not get adequate focus and hence the environment soon presents potential health risks. Thirty Activistas from within the displaced community were selected and trained to manage these potential health risks by mobilizing communities to practice good environmental health behaviors by the inhabitants. FHI 360 has procured the tools/kits which will be distributed to the resettlement sites and the tools will be under the custody of the trained Activistas and community leaders for keeping the sheltering environment clean, including solid waste collection containers and transport to a disposal area, and hand tools to manage any drainage and stagnating water problems. Under this modification, FHI 360 will continue with the same approach to support Activistas and community leaders in their efforts to mobilize communities to conduct periodic clean up works. Additionally, to provide access to a safe solid waste management area, FHI 360 will create a safe solid waste disposal area within each targeted accommodation shelter. A similar approach will be taken when the resettlement of IDPs transition to more regular IDP sites within the operational areas. Under this modification, FHI 360 will establish solid waste disposal facilities at resettlement sites, given the continued flow of new arrivals and resultant solid waste management needs. FHI 360 will procure and distribute 100 disposal bins and 60 waste collection/disposal kits and train new Activists and community leaders on waste management (with refreshers for existing Activists and community leaders). FHI 360 will also maintain rehabilitated medical waste management at supported health facilities through training for medical staff, APEs, Activists, and cleaners.
