Project Summary

In 2024, 16.7 million people (8.4 million female, 8.3 million male) are assessed to need humanitarian assistance across Syria, up from 15.3 million in 2023. Of the 16.7 million people in need, 5.5 million are displaced, including over 2 million who live in last resort sites.
The earthquakes on 6 February worsened a protracted humanitarian crisis in northwest Syria, where 3.4 million people are displaced and 4.1 million people are reliant on humanitarian assistance.. Most displaced families were hosted in shelters or by the local community and relatives. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs.
SECD is planning to sustain and save the dignity and lives of the targeted people by implementing timely, needs-based, and effective interventions the people in north-west Syria are living in a very difficult situation, and the area experienced one of the biggest displacement waves, and through these interventions, SECD is planning to support 13475 People ( 2413 Men, 2477 Women, 4173 Boy, and 4412 Girl) based on in-depth need assessment, with focusing on responding to the critical needs of people with considering the access, the functionality of
the markets and the community engagement approach by consulting the people in need about the proposed interventions.
SECD is planning to build its response plan based on the following interventions:
1- Undertake the rehabilitation of 1450 shelters as shown below :
-Implementation of minor repairs to damaged or unfinished housing: About 650
-Rehabilitation of damaged or unfinished housing (Moderate and Severe): About 650
2- Installation and/ or construction of temporary transitional shelters: About 150
3-Provision of core NFIs: About 1000
The shelter intervention will be based on the following aspects:
- Ensuring proper HLP situation for the targeted shelters
- Prioritize the cost-effective interventions
- Ensuring the participation of the beneficiaries in all the intervention stages
SEECD is aware that needs are different amongst gender and age groups, and other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities. SECD aims to provide the most needed assistance to such groups through, Establishing beneficiary selection criteria to prioritize the inclusion of those most vulnerable and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. The households and/or shelters selected for this project will be chosen based on need and without the interference of authorities or armed actors.
-Communities will take the lead in setting beneficiary selection criteria and undertaking beneficiary identification: SECD will explain clearly the type of assistance then it will agree with the targeted communities on the selection criteria, SECD will allow all the affected persons to register themselves to benefit from the project then and based on the agreed selection criteria, SECD will announce the selected beneficiaries transparently, throughout the process, opportunities will be provided for community members to ask questions or complain and these comments and complaints will be responded to and rectified where necessary.
