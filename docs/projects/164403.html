Project Summary

In 2020 Shafak will continue work in the current locations under the UNICEF , UNFPA, WFP and SCHF and the expected targets will be the following locations if the security situation will not be changed and the agreement with the donors will not be terminated:
Aleppo,Jebel Saman, Atareb (3 RRTs, 2 OTPs, TSFP)
Idleb, Idleb,Maaret Tamsrin (1 SC , 3 RRTs, TSFP)
In 2020, Shafak is going to establish additional number of Rapid Response Teams and ensure presence of emergency lifesaving integrated nutrition services the operational areas where there is no any service present. With the new Rapid Response Teams, Shafak will fill the current service gap and provide emergency integrated health, nutrition and child protection rapid services such as triage, emergency aid and referral services, Shafak proposes to scale up the nutrition intervention to cover new areas, those areas were selected according to the new areas that will be covered under the partnership with WFP (GFA and BSFP project) and after the coordination with nutrition cluster to avoid any duplication to cover the gaps:

4 - Needs

According to the HNO- Syria 2020 The primary nutrition needs identified for 2020 are based on nation-wide SMART surveys and MUAC assessments in 33 IDP camps, informal settlements and collective centers in north-west Syria. Availability of accurate and timely SMART data across Syria in 2019 as well as a combination of continued hostilities in north-west and north-east and lack of sustained humanitarian access in areas such Manbij, Ain al-Arab, and east of the Euphrates resulted in increased severity. Available data suggests that the nutrition status of populations has become more severe in some sub-districts in Aleppo, Idleb. Chronic malnutrition is evident in most sub-districts throughout Syria – notably in the Northwest of Syria (Aleppo,Idlib) and North East Syria (Deir Ez Zor, Al-Raqqa and Hasakeh). Chronic malnutrition is also on the rise with almost half a million children stunted (low height for age) in 2020 and face losing their future due to hindered learning and physical developments, as well as early death. According to the Whole of Syria Humanitarian Situation Report shared by UNICEF Reporting Period: 1 January—31 December 2019, In 2019, UNICEF supported over 1.5 million children under five (U5) and pregnant and lactating women with screening for acute malnutrition—of these, more than 8,200 children U5 were identified with severe acute malnutrition and received life-saving treatment. UNICEF also supported more than 1 million children U5 and pregnant and lactating women with micronutrients and Vitamin A, which has been proven to improve learning and prevent visual impairment.8 In addition, nearly 574,000 caregivers benefited from Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) counselling and more than 774,500 people received essential nutrition supplies. Insufficient funding, reporting delays and the limited availability of partners, particularly in hard-to-reach areas, constrained the full achievement of targets.

5 - Implementation of activities

In accordance with Shafak's cross-border strategy, we will be responsible for directimplementation in the target locations coordinating with and gaining approval from localauthorities in Syria, building on their extensive network, and facilitating access. Shafakwill be responsible for ensuring that quality and accountability standards are met, willprovide required training to staff and ensure that contractual requirement are adhered toand met. Further, Shafak will be responsible for all program and financial reporting to thedonor and will also continue to coordinate with relevant cluster and other coordinationbodies in Turkey. This joint program will be conducted through three main phases as thefollowing: Phase I: - Recruitment of needed staff (Health/Nutrition) - Procurement ofneeded furniture, tools for training and tools for screening. - Training sessions. Phase II: -IYCF activities. - Facility and community based screening activities. - CMAM services. -Outpatient services. - Treatment. -Follow up/ -Referral. Phase III: - Reporting, dataanalysis, monitoring and evaluation. Shafak Organization will run the project and its activity with a complementary with the pre-existing nutrition teams, existing nutritionist, and community health workers who are already providing the nutrition service in Shafak health facilities. Service providers:The RRT service providers will be the team in each area; each team will consist of 1 nutritionist, 2 CHW and 1 data worker.During implementation of the proposed project activities, the project team will ensure community interests respected, and project objectives and activities are transparent and understood by the local population. The beneficiaries and affected population will have direct access to share their feedback and complaints. Shafak’s core staff will conduct regular visits and monitor project activities in the target communities to ensure smooth implementation and liaise with community members.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Shafak will apply the Nutrition sector’s Protection Risk Analysis for detailed guidance on the expected risks. The project overall objective aims at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individualwhere they need it the most. Throughout the project life cycle the principles of protectionmainstreaming are incorporated. All activities are designed to avoiding causing harm andprioritize safety and dignity of targeted populations, insuring meaningful access,accountability, and participation through several, action such as: • The project is designedto meet the needs of the community especially women, boys and girls considering thecontext, tradition, and norms in the community • Location of facilities will be accessibleand easy to reach for all people., also safe and away from any military location. •Community will be clearly and widely informed about the date, location and timing of theservice provision, and their opinion will be highly considered. • Team staff will be trainedto be skilled in delivering PFA, identifying CP&GBV cases and refer them to adequateservices. • Insure that the location is suitable for women, girls, people with disabilities,children headed household, old people, and other affected categories in the community---outreach. • Distribution and providing services for specific categories mentioned abovewill be to the door. • Teams will be trained on PESA, Child safeguarding, and will sign thecode of conducted related. • project team will set up appropriate mechanisms throughwhich affected populations can receive the information they need, provide feedback onthe activities. accountability officer will be responsible for information provision,collecting and responding to feedback and will report directly to HQ to ensuretransparency • Feedback will also be continuously gathered in line with standardizedcenter management protocol and monitoring and evaluation procedures, and doing soshall help improve protection mainstreaming.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Airstrikes and Shelling over NWSShafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk:• security assessment of the operational areas to avoid sites that have been targeted before.• Prevent and decrease crowds in front of the center.• Equipping the staff with VHFs.• Training the staff on how to deal in cases of shelling and conduct practical training.• Equipping the center with fire extinguishers• An evacuation plan for center ready and communicated to all staff• An emergency contact list is available at the center.• Staff to be aware of communication protocols Risk 2: closing of Bab al-Hawa BCP that can resulted in a shortage of consumables, equipment, and kits. Shafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk:• Monitoring the general security situation in the border area. • provide each center with consumables and medical kits that are enough for each center to work for 3 months at least.• using a different route for securing the supply chain process instead of Bab al-Hawa BCP by using Bab al-Slama BCP near Azaz in northern-countryside of Aleppo. Risk 3: Deviation of aid.Shafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk:• Informing field team not to comply with any request from a person or armed faction to give them vouchers.• Stopping the work and withdrawal from the center, relocation also can be an option according to the situation, if there is any threat from the military factions, and then report the subject case to our donor.• An ATC Check to be done for all suppliers\ vendors before signing the contract with them. Risk 4: shortage of qualified technical staff for recruitmentShafak would make sure to follow below measures in response to this expected risk:• Multi-shift approach will be followed to allow flexibility in recruiting part-time staff.• Salary scale will be in line with Health Directorate salary scale.
