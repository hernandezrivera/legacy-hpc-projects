Project Summary

The humanitarian context in Somalia has remained fragile; the impact of the prolonged 2016-17 drought and subsequent hurdles, like poor Deyr rains in 2018 (October-December), an unusually hot dry Jilaal season in 2019 (December-March), and abnormal, erratic rainfall during the same year's GU rainy season (April-June). flash flooding has also affected 540,000 people across Middle and Lower Juba, Bay, Lower and Middle Shabelle, and Hiraan; 370,000 have already been displaced. Climatic shocks, combined with other persistent drivers of needs - such as armed conflict and/or displacement - have left around five million Somalis in need of humanitarian assistance (HNO overview 2020 OCHA). In Banadir region, a rapid assessment of recent flood damage to WASH infrastructure and facilities conducted on 17 July by the WASH Cluster found that 130 latrines have been damaged or destroyed, affecting over 1,200 people, in IDP sites in Kahda, Daynille and Garasbarley districts. IDPs are currently residing in urban and peri-urban informal crowded settlements, with insufficient access to WASH services that raise protection and health concerns. DRC proposes to undertake a life-saving and sustainable assistance to 131,250 individuals (18,375men, 21,300 women, 41,892 girls, 44,733boys, 5,250 EVIs) Disaster-Affected Communities IDPs, returnees, refugees and host communities through provision of emergency and sustainable WASH interventions (water, sanitation and hygiene) geared towards improved well-being of people and prevention of morbidity and mortality from WASH-related disease outbreaks. DRC will prioritize the displaced in affected by droughts, floods and armed conflict but excluded humanitarian assistance, in DRC operational areas particularly those experiencing shortage of water and communities; and service levels are putting the health and of populations at risk. Interventions will focus on marginalized families and vulnerable female-headed households. DRC will also include Persons with disabilities challenged physical access and information in WASH interventions. DRC will carry out Joint analysis prior to implementation to inform the neediest sites and priorities. Predetermined beneficiary selection criteria will be used to reach the most vulnerable HHs/individuals. Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities. Life-saving WASH interventions include; water trucking where there is no safe water source, fabrication and distribution of portable water tanks, emergency latrine, sanitation and hygiene kits and rapid hygiene promotion and durable WASH solutions of improved and equitable access to safe, of adequate yields. DRC will rehabilitate or construct water supply infrastructures such as strategic boreholes focusing on optimizing outputs through upgrading, rehabilitations/ of shallow wells, water networks, water supply schemes extensions or surface water catchment systems, Berkads/Hafir Dams. DRC will engage the community’s ability for O&M of sources and assess water sources available to choose suitable water supply technology. DRC will encourage existing water source owners – both private and government, to extend water services to the settlements of DACs. Gender-balanced and inclusive committees water will be established for smooth O&M of water facilities. Hygiene promotion awareness will be done to enhance WASH behavioral change on key WASH behaviors andCovid19 prevention and distribute 3000 household hygiene kits, IEC messaging and dissemination, mass media awareness sessions and campaigns. For sanitation, DRC will encourage a market-based approach – looking at creating demand for latrine construction and building capacity of the supply side to ensure enterprise-creation and therefore sustainable services. DRC will construct gender segregated latrines, provide tools and support solid waste management.

Mitigation Measures

DDG is a department of DRC and has developed community safety tools used to create awareness of beneficiaries to landmines and ERWs. DRC has strict selection criteria for beneficiaries that identifies marginalized groups as vulnerable groups. DRC has trained field teams in ensuring that these groups are targeted to avert discrimination. DRC will ensure that they take services close to the beneficiaries to prevent Exposure of displaced women and girls to sexual violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements, distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services - DRC will ensure that they take services close to the beneficiaries to stop external stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection.DRC criteria specific to targeting IDPs, Refugee Returnees and vulnerable Host Community, a Do No Harm approach that is seen to promote peaceful co-existence of Displacement-Affected Communities (DACs). DRC will ensure the selection/targeting criteria is shared with camp/IDP management committees while DRC staffs will ensure that the selection criteria. The committees must be selected based on AGD criteria to ensure all affected groups are involved in the intervention. DRC will ensure that land occupied by IDPs is allocated by government or that the landowners agree to accommodate them for a reasonable time to remedy any forced evictions. For protracted IDPs seeking durable solutions, DRC will ensure that they have land allocated by government or they have land ownership documents like title deeds that guarantees them reasonable lease of 5+years before any shelter is constructed. DRC will ensure that there are IDP committees who organize people in order to reduce chances of scrambling during distribution. DRC will also ensure that vulnerable groups (women and children, PLDs and elderly) have special queues to minimize physical injuries or that they have delivered to their places of settlement.
