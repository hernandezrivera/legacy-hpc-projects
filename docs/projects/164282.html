Project Summary

UNRWA estimates that 418,000 Palestine refugees in Syria will continue to require humanitarian assistance in 2020. To contribute to mitigating the consequences of deepening poverty and increasing vulnerabilities, the Agency will provide cash assistance to up to 418,000 Palestine refugees through a targeted approach, which UNRWA introduced in the second half of 2019. This approach assists only vulnerable refugees, and prioritizes those most vulnerable. The four main groups that constitute the most vulnerable group are as follows: families headed by a person older than 60 years; families headed by a person with disability and people with disabilities; families headed by a female; and orphans. The groups can be identified through UNRWA’s registration system. UNRWA estimates that 126,000 Palestine Refugees fall under those four categories.
This system will offer two different values of cash assistance to Palestine refugees according to two tiers of vulnerability. The most vulnerable will receive cash assistance amounting to US$ 28 per person per month – thus covering 100% of the official abject poverty gap of US$0.94, while the remaining Palestine Refugees categorized as less vulnerable will get assistance equivalent to US$ 14 per person per month – covering 50% of the official abject poverty gap. The cash assistance aims to help vulnerable refugees meet their basic needs of food and NFI. Through its partners, comprising a network of banks and private companies present in all regions of Syria, UNRWA will be offering cash assistance in a ﬂexible and timely manner with minimal ﬁnancial risk. In 2020, UNRWA also plans to extend the use of ATM for its cash distribution. Some 40,000 ATM cards will be issued, distributed and credited to selected beneﬁciaries, on top of the 10,000 cards that were already issued in 2019.

4 - Needs

Out of the 438,000 Palestine refugees currently estimated to be inside Syria. As of the end of 2019, an estimated 40 per cent of Palestine refugees continue to be displaced within Syria. According to UNRWA’s observations, many of those who have returned are suffering a secondary displacement. Unable to return to their habitual residence, they often move in with extended family. The lack of privacy and limited available resources particularly negatively affect women and girls. Many are forced to rent houses, which creates a huge financial burden on an already poor population. The latest socio-economic assessment of living conditions conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in absolute poverty (less than US$ 2 person/day). It also found that households headed by older persons, females, persons with disabilities or orphans are among the most vulnerable Palestine refugee groups and suffer from high unemployment rates and poverty. This supports HNO 2020’s findings that indicate: “among affected population, female-headed households, youth, elderly and people with functional difficulties have reduced access to basic services including shelter, as well as reduced livelihood opportunities and income that supports access to NFIs.” HNO notes also that many life-sustaining, basic items continue to be unaffordable for people. Deteriorating socioeconomic conditions continue to undermine household resilience and coping capacities. It is expected that the reliance of Palestine refugee population on UNRWA humanitarian assistance to meet their most basic needs will persist in 2020. Families' inability to meet those needs can increase their vulnerability and force them to adopt harmful coping mechanisms, especially female-headed households and youth. Accordingly, UNRWA will be providing cash assistance to 418,000 vulnerable and poor Palestine refugees, 126,000 of which are categorized as the most vulnerable.

5 - Implementation of activities

Multipurpose cash assistance remains the most critical form of UNRWA emergency support to 418,000 conflict-affected and vulnerable Palestine refugees in Syria, helping them to meet part of their essential humanitarian needs while providing flexibility and dignity of choice.. Currently, UNRWA distributes cash through three partners (Al Baraka Bank, BBSF Bank and Al Haram Company) in addition to its own cash distribution centre located at filed oﬃce. As part of the planning phase, UNRWA generates a list of all eligible Palestine refugees and provides the list of beneficiaries to the partners ahead of the starting date. Once the local currency is availed in the outlets and following intensive coordination with the partners, UNRWA sends SMS to each beneficiary communicating the amount, location and time. UNRWA assigns dedicated technical UNRWA staff to each of the cash outlets, allowing the Agency to ensure strict compliance with financial rules and regulations for the distribution of cash and also for verification. In 2020, UNRWA plans to extend the use of ATM for its cash distribution. Some 40,000 ATM cards will be issued, distributed and credited to selected beneﬁciaries, on top of the 10,000 cards issued as part of a pilot conducted in 2019. The pilot helped UNRWA identify and address the following issues: 1. Issuing cards: enough time and staff are needed to sort cards and follow up technical issues when issuing a large batches of cards, as matching cards with PIN numbers has been time consuming 2. Bank’s daily withdrawal limit and fees: UNRWA had to liaise with the bank to waive the daily limit and the fees imposed on ATM withdrawals to allow beneficiaries to withdraw their full assistance all at once without bearing any additional costs 3. Time needed to verify lists: To ensure that assistance to beneficiaries is not delayed, enough time must be allocated for verifying lists before they are uploaded against the ATM cards.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Beneﬁciaries face exploitation or abuse travelling to and from distribution points. o Likelihood: medium o Impact: medium Mitigating measures: - In order to minimize problems in accessing distribution points, UNRWA allows ﬂexibility within the families as to who collects the assistance and a delegation system allows individuals of families to nominate a trusted person to collect the assistance on their behalf. Risk: Discrimination of vulnerable groups and risk of GBV and sexual harassment, and SEA particularly at locations with high number of beneﬁciaries. o Likelihood: medium o Impact: medium Mitigation Measures:- UNRWA staff prioritize vulnerable groups such as women, older persons and persons with disabilities in order to reduce their waiting time. - UNRWA provides training on protection mainstreaming - UNRWA is conducting protection audits on a regular basis to get feedback from the staff and the community on how to improve its services and how to optimize its compliance to the main protection principles. - All UNRWA staff sign the code of conduct on commencement of their contracts. - Training to staff on the code of conduct and PSEA and regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA. - Reinforced messaging to community members on the importance of reporting SEA and ensure mechanisms are in place to ensure conﬁdentiality and set up accessible and eﬃcient complaint mechanismsRisk: Explosive remnants of war (ERW) are recognized as a protection risk for Palestine refugees across the country, including when accessing UNWRA services. o Likelihood: low o Impact: high Mitigation Measures: - awareness-raising and training to staff, community members, including children, on reducing exposure to risks related to ERW. - UNRWA sends text messages to beneﬁciaries to advise them on changes to services to minimize movements.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A mix of humanitarian and post-conﬂict interventions: Whilst Damascus, Rural Damascus and most of the major urban concentrations experienced relative calm throughout 2019, hostilities continued in other areas and the overall situation remained tense and unstable. In 2020, the Agency anticipates that a mix of humanitarian and development approaches that are supportive of post-conﬂict rehabilitation will be needed to respond to the different needs ofPalestine refugees across Syria. High levels of humanitarian needs, especially amongst the most vulnerable Palestine refugees:UNRWA anticipates that the deteriorating socioeconomic situation and the depreciating value of the local currency will continue to produce high levels of humanitarian needs among the Palestine refugee population.. Increase in returns; a large proportion of Palestine refugees in Syria will remain displaced: the levels of displacement are foreseen to remain high among Palestine refugees, as access to Yarmouk is still restricted and the level of destruction there is signiﬁcant. Returns to Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps, which have sustained major destruction, remain slow, due to the level of damage to basic infrastructure and the lack of public services. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns from out of Syria in 2020 will increase. Secondary displacements are also anticipated among Palestine refugees due to their legal status. Risk of duplication and errors: To mitigate this risk, UNRWA established a separate unit that deals with cash assistance. The registration of cash assistance beneficiaries is separated from the list for payment. The database is reviewed on a monthly basis to ensure that all information pertaining to the status of the needs of beneficiaries remains accurate and up to date. Beneficiaries are identified in the database through unique registration card number. Assistance is recorded immediately after assistance is provided to avoid duplication
