Project Summary

The unique socio-economic conditions of blockade, with its movement restrictions and destruction of productive capacity, are further compounded by repeated cycles of conflict and political instability and now the COVID-19 pandemic, undercutting employment and livelihood opportunities in the Gaza Strip and abroad. UNRWA’s emergency food assistance operation provides a critical humanitarian lifeline to over one million Palestine refugees who depend on food aid to meet their most basic food security needs. It accounts for approximately 200,000 Palestine refugee families including families with persons with disabilities and female headed households. In quarterly bases, eligible families will receive a food basket that is composed of a range of basic food commodities. Through its well-established monitoring system, UNRWA will continue to evaluate the food distribution system including the distribution modality that follows strict safety and security measures responding to changing environment around COVID-19 emergency, while guaranteeing a fair, equal and consistent support to food-insecure Palestine refugees in Gaza.

1 - Needs

Food assistance is a vital element of social safety for Palestine refugees living in Gaza as well as contributing to wider market and food security under economic blockade and amidst widespread poverty. While the limited economic access to food is the main cause of food insecurity driven by unemployment and poverty, both indicators are in the rise due to the overall slowdown of the economy following the restrictions imposed to contain the COVID-19 pandemic (HNO 2021). According to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the overall unemployment rate in Gaza reached 49.1 per cent during the second quarter of 2020. Palestine refugees, who account for around two-thirds of the total population in Gaza, are facing even higher unemployment rate of 49.9 per cent compared to 47.7 per cent for non-refugees. These trends have a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups including women and young people. Unemployment rate is especially high among women with 66.8 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2020), facing both lack of opportunities and social barriers to employment in Gaza. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also significantly affected with average unemployment rate of 73.6 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2020). Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. 68 per cent of households in Gaza are food insecure, and poverty rates increased from 38.8 per cent in 2011 to 53 per cent in 2017 (SEFSec Survey, 2018). Conditions have since continued to deteriorate, including most recently due to the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. PCBS identified 17 per cent decrease in the already low number of employed persons in the local market between the first and second quarter of 2020 in Gaza confirming the impact of COVID-19. Persistently high levels of food insecurity in Gaza are not due to the unavailability of basic commodities in the local market, but rather to households’ lack of economic access to food, which, if unaddressed, would have lasting consequences on both the extent and severity of food insecurity. If to date hunger and undernourishment are not widespread in Gaza, it is because of the assistance provided by UNRWA and other humanitarian organizations. In terms of operation, food distribution will pivot between different modalities as the situation with COVID-19 evolves. UNRWA have successfully implemented a home delivery modality when a lockdown was imposed by local authorities. At times when movement is allowed, distribution takes place through UNRWA distribution centers that are expanded and adjusted to accommodate the health and safety measures recommended by WHO and the MoH. During the third quarter in 2020, UNRWA provided in-kind food assistance to about 1.04 million Palestine refugees, which is expected to increase in coming quarters.

2 - Activities

UNRWA will provide unified food basket to all registered refugees physically present in Gaza, with certain exclusions around employment status and stable income. The unified food basket is composed with basic food commodities – wheat flour, rice, sunflower oil, sugar, whole milk, lentils and chickpeas. The working assumption is of provision around 1.2 million individuals in 2021, while a greater emphasis on physical verification and refining employment exclusions may mean that this figure fluctuates upwards or downwards. The food basket will be distributed on quarterly bases. While all Palestine refugees who meet the specific criteria are eligible to receive food assistance, approximately a half of the beneficiaries benefiting from the food assistance are women. This also includes around 40,000 persons with disabilities and around 17,000 female headed households. Under the supervision of Relief and Social Services Programme (RSSP), trained social workers make systematic home visits to the beneficiaries including specific cases of wives from polygamous marriage, divorced women and separated or abandoned women to guarantee that they receive assistance fairly with dignity. Particular attention is given by the social workers to the identification and assistance of “abandoned” women and their children who, beside social stigma, are also exposed to specific risks such as not receiving any regular support from the husband and often have to rely on ad-hoc and weak social networks. UNRWA makes sure that vulnerable families do not fall through the system and are able to receive assistance independently. Amidst COVID-19 emergency, UNRWA provided part of its food assistance using home-delivery modality in 2020. As of October 2020, UNRWA increased the number of distribution points to avoid crowdedness at the distribution centers. In case of further restriction of the movement due to the COVID-19 emergency, UNRWA is ready to adjust its food distribution modality ensuring the protection of public health and safety of the population in the Gaza Strip. In 2021, UNRWA will continue to distribute using a safe modality with enhanced measures according to COVID-19 health recommendations, this will include controlled management of crowds, adjusted spaces to ensure physical distances and shading in open areas, strict use of PPEs and enhanced hygiene and cleanliness measures. The UNRWA Monitoring and Evaluation team, in close coordination with RSSP, will continue to regularly monitor the quality of the food distribution, ensuring the continued feedback from the community through focus group sessions and regular communication with beneficiaries.

8.6.b - If 'Other', please specify :

UNRWA Relief Social Services Programme (RSSP) regularly conducts focus groups to discuss key items such as the quality of the food assistance, the quantity of the food basket and the process of food distribution. UNRWA-RSSP has a complaint system in place to receive beneficiaries’ feedback. An appeal mechanism as well as a committee feedback system are also in place for the families who were confirmed “not-eligible” from the intervention based on the results of the criteria. Anyone can access to RSSP area offices across the Gaza Strip. UNRWA RSSP replies to the families regarding their status based on their complaint or appeal. UNRWA’s Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) unit is also involved through regularly conducting interviews with beneficiaries across all distribution centres as well as other distribution points, beneficiaries’ homes, or via phone calls, to collect their feedback regarding the quality of the received commodities, the behavior of the staff in charge of distributing the food and any other issues related to their satisfaction.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. Distribution centres and social services are regularly monitored against the four main protection mainstreaming pillars, as defined by the Global Protection Cluster and echoed by UNRWA, the assessed principles are: (i) Prioritize safety, dignity and do no harm; (ii) Meaningful access to services; (iii) Accountability; (iv) Participation and empowerment of refugees. During the food distribution cycle, UNRWA staff based at the food distribution centers including social workers and M&E teams, ensure that the targeted beneficiaries receive their food assistance with dignity and respect. In addition, regular monitoring work is conducted to collect beneficiaries’ feedback on key items of the programme e.g. the quality of the food distribution process, the content of the food basket, the overall quality of the food distribution or the behavior of the staff involved in the food distribution. These feedbacks help to continuously improve the intervention. All staff are required to take mandatory courses on prevention of SEA, and any beneficiaries and staff can report SEA concerns directly to the investigation office.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is targeting all Palestinian refugees living under the poverty line with food assistance, without any discrimination. Information to beneficiaries, eligibility criteria and other services are available to all refugees where application and claim can be made to Relief and Social Services Programme offices, often located in the same area of the distribution centres. Location of distribution centres are following safety measures, especially for women and girls, recognizing that catchment area, distance and potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. The distribution takes into consideration specific vulnerability concerns and adopted specific SOPs to provide meaningful access and prioritize assistance to vulnerable groups (abandoned women, pregnant women, persons with disabilities, elderly, etc.). A follow up system is also in place whenever assistance is not received in order to identify and follow specific concerns and potential protection cases. Furthermore, distribution centres are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. Staff in the distribution centres, whom is regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection and gender mainstreaming, are able to refer specific protection cases to social services for follow up. A crowd control system is in place and a specific calendar is set up to avoid and limit potential incidents and/or disturbance, especially amidst COVID-19 emergency. The unified eligibility criteria system enables vulnerable groups such as widow or divorced women as well as people with disabilities or anyone not able to go to the distribution center to be provided with specific assistance in order to receive their food basket. In order to ease the process for targeted persons with disabilities, there is a delegation system in place that enables those people to delegate one of their family members to receive the food assistance on their behalf. The delegation is set during the home visits of the social workers and following their reports’ recommendations. UNRWA will use home delivery modality for those who are under home quarantine and home isolation to make sure that they will continue to receive food assistance.
