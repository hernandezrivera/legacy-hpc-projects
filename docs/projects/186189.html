Project Summary

It will not be strange should we receive news of Cholera outbreak in Njikwa Sub Division soon. Njikwa has been highly hit by the crisis as fierce fighting left trails of destructions and pains in the communities and in the hearts of the people with all social services shutdown especially in central town of Njikwa Administrative area, Ngwo and Oshie villages. The Water systems in these three communities were highly vandalized with untold damages ranging from breaking of pipes, taps and catchments points putting the population more vulnerable to sicknesses like Cholera, Typhoid, Malaria and other dirty water associated diseases. There are 10 water collection points including 3 standard catchments that are dilapidating in the three highly affected villages.
About 10 000 people have limited or no access to safe drinking water, sanitation, or hygiene services. As of July 2020, typhoid and malaria affected all the 3 villages in the sub division with almost 80% of the population having traces of malaria or typhoid. A good number of cases of cholera noticed and managed at local levels. Between June 2018 and July 2021 over 300 people died in Njikwa from what the local people called "high fever" - Typoid and Malaria; Many children died of high running stomach and vomiting - "diarrhea" as community health volunteers reported, many people complained of belly bite. No medical report was available to attest to the cause of the deaths since the health services were not functional to determine the actual cause. The toileting system of the people had collapsed as many people had ran into the bushes, you could see open defecation at most back yards and roadsides.
The Comprehensive WASH support project intends to improves access to safe and adequate water supply and basic sanitation infrastructure for 2000 households with a population of 10 000 people, 4 health facilities, 8 schools in community learning spaces. 10 water collection points will be rehabilitated, fenced and 200 taps and100 wash basins will be provided and others rehabilitated. 10 community latrines will be built in community public places as samples to encourage the households to construct theirs in like manners under the supervision of the Community WASH Committees. The project builds resiliency through complementary hygiene practices to maximize and enhance health-seeking behaviors among the most vulnerable communities.
Rollout typhoid and bar cholera outbreak project will supports and improves sustainable environmental health conditions of vulnerable communities in Njikwa by improving water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) infrastructure and service delivery. The project addresses the key challenges to long-term sustainability by enabling (a) behavior change, (b) community ownership, (c) local capacity to manage
infrastructure, and (d) sensitize and provides the communities with Covid 19 preventive measures and gadgets.
Community Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Committees (C-WASH-C) will be created in each community. This is a component of our community emergency response team programme as a water management board, sanitation and hygiene issues in the communities of Njikwa council areas. This project aims to train 20 Hygiene and sanitary inspectors who will also serve as first aiders in the 3 pilot villages. 10 Community based water maintenance personnel with be trained on basic water repairs and maintenance and provided with basic plumbing tool kits. They shall organize regular clean up campaigns, do repair works on broken pipes, drinking points (spring), inspect homes and the latrines, do disinfection services in latrines and slums clearances. 3 community volunteers shall be specially trained to do chlorination of the water systems for the sub division on regular basis. The project will also target 1000 vulnerable girls and women for the sensitization on good practices on menstrual hygiene and to provide and train them on the use of reusable sanitary dignity kits.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The most urgent need for safe and clean water in Njikwa now is the protection of water collection points and repairs of broken pipes as they are attractive points to cows and other animals in 3 crisis affected communities. Recently, cows have invaded 6 water points and catchments broken and more pipes destroyed for the quest of water as the dry season approached. Catchment areas need to be fenced and broken pipes repaired. This will go a long way to evade cholera and typhoid outbreak that are associated with unsafe water. Cows and pigs stray in to the leaking areas where the drink, bath, sleep and defecate. This goes directly into the broken pipes. It will not be strange now to receive news from Njikwa of cholera outbreak as the people are most vulnerable now than before. The crisis has left a trail of destruction and pains in the community with most social services shutdown especially in central town of Njikwa Administrative area, Ngwo and Oshie villages. The Water systems in these 3 communities were vandalized with untold damages ranging from breaking of pipes, taps and catchments putting the population vulnerable to sicknesses like Typhoid, malaria and other dirty water associated diseases. There are 10 water collection points in the 3 villages that have all broken down and water not chlorinated for 5 years. The fighting has caused significant damage to essential infrastructure, including water and sanitation systems and health facilities. Access to basic needs has reduced with increasing rates of food insecurity, malnutrition, the reemergence of Typhoid, Malaria and Cholera.About 10 000 people have limited or no access to safe drinking water, sanitation, or hygiene services. Typhoid and malaria have been the major health challenges faced by people of affected villages in the Sub Division with almost 80% of the population having traces of malaria or typhoid. A good number of cases of cholera noticed and managed at local levels. Between June 2018 and July 2021 over 300 people died in Njikwa from what the local people called "high fever" - Typoid and Malaria; Many children died of high running stomach and vomiting - "diarrhea" as community health volunteers reported, many people complained of belly bite. No medical report was available to attest to the cause of the deaths since the health services were not functional to determine the actual cause. The toileting system of the people had collapsed as many people had displaced, you could see open defecation.Water catchments have been abandoned with broken pipes. All regular technicians fled and no one to clean or repair them. In other places water points are not cleared or drained. No knowledge of filtering water before drinking. No chlorinated water for over five years. Typhoid and malarial very prevalent. The communities use mostly pit latrines of which many have been abandoned and have collapsed, the people do open defecations in back yard bushes. The Mbororo Fulani community in Njikwa is highly affected and threatened for their purportedly supporting the government. They need of CRI and NFI.Women have resorted to the old traditional methods of managing menstrual issues. Some use pieces of clothes in turns while others use untreated traditional leaves and back of trees exposing them to diseases and infections. The project targets 1000 including 200 IDP, 225 Returnees vulnerable girls and women to sensitize on good practice on menstrual hygiene and provide reusable dignity kits. 2000 households of 10 000 people including 2000 IDPs and 1000 Returnees, 4 health facilities, 8 community schools need safe and good drinking water. 10 water collection points need rehabilitated, protection and 200 taps,100 wash basins be provided and others rehabilitated. 10 community latrines needed as samples to the communities. There is need to create Community WASH Committees to serve as WASH Inspectors. Community based water maintenance personnel be trained and provide plumbing kits
