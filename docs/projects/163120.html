Project Summary

A second consecutive year of drought has resulted in wide spread hunger and suffering for poor and vulnerable people in Zimbabwe. The combination of back to back poor harvests, a challenging and
deteriorating macro-economic environment and forecasts for reduced rainfall during the 2019/2020 season point to the high likelihood of yet another poor agricultural season with serious consequences that will persist throughout 2020 until the next harvest in 2021. The prices of basic agricultural inputs such as seeds and fertilisers have sky rocketed beyond the reach of most farmers. Moreover availability of traditional seed varieties at household and community levels has been eroded due to recurrent droughts. Post-harvest losses remain a major challenge for many smallholder farmers. According to study conducted by FAO About 30 percent of the harvested grain is lost due to lack of appropriate storage facilities. Thus any efforts to support the reestablishment of crop production needs to tackle issues of enhancing access to basic inputs, provision of training and addressing post-harvest losses.
Availability of water and grazing for livestock is very low across most parts of the country and this is affecting productivity and cattle sales. Most poor farmers cannot afford livestock supplementary stock feeds. More so it is not readily available on most markets. Poor cattle conditions coupled with above average disease prevalence are leading to an increased number of livestock deaths, mainly in the low rainfall parts of the country .
Evidence generated by FAO has shown that when there shocks within a community or household, the worst affected are women who are primarily the providers of household food security.
FAO is proposing a project that will "Prevent further deterioration of living standards for acutely food insecure population, by providing emergency agriculture support aimed at ensuring they can achieve food security and resilience to repeated exposure to multiple shocks and stressors. The project will be implemented in 40 districts targeting 100, 000 households (500,000 people) in IPC phase 3 & 4.
FAO’s Response strategy is to provide emergency livelihood support that will increase sustainable, climate smart agricultural production while promoting the growing and consumption of nutritious and diverse crops. The expected outputs of the project are
• Crop and livestock production capacity restored
• Access to water resources for agricultural production increased
• coordination in the food security and agriculture sector strengthened

1 - Activities

Output 1: Drought affected farmers have increased access to crop and livestock inputs: (starting by September 2020)The beneficiaries will be selected geographically (most affected wards according to IPC 3 and 4) and according to capacity criteria (e.g. for crops: access to land, available labour in household), as well participatory community based approaches, with local traditional leadership (e.g. village Heads) Engage implementing partners• Mobilize and sensitize project stakeholders and beneficiaries• Procure seeds for field crops (sorghum, millet cowpeas)• Procure various vegetable seeds (cabbage, tomato, onions, covo, beans)• Procure supplementary stock feed• Provide subsidised crop and livestock inputs to drought affected farmers• Provide training to farmers on utilization of crop and livestock inputs and good agricultural practices• Capacitate and train Agriculture Development Associations• Monitor and report project activitiesOutput 2: Increased access to water for crop and livestock production ( Activities will be implemented at any time of the year according to the identified needs.)Beneficiaries will be selected according to geographical consideration as well as according to the status of the infrastructures to be rehabilitated (e.g. bore holes, troughs) and number of beneficiaries able to use the rehabilitated assets.• Procure and engage service providers to rehabilitate and install solar powered boreholes, livestock drinking troughs and community gardens• Rehabilitate non-functional boreholes• Rehabilitate/construct livestock drinking troughs• Establish community gardens• Train community members and pump minders• Inspect and certify solar powered boreholes• Monitor and report project activitiesOutput 3: Strengthened Agriculture and Food Security Sector Coordination and early warning information systems• Provide financial and technical support to national and sub national crop assessments, vulnerability assessments and studies • Provide financial and technical support to operationalize the food security cluster• Document and facilitate the dissemination of agriculture and foods security information to facilitate programming and policy decisions
