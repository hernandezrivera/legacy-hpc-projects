Project Summary

The project seeks to enhance the resilience of food insecure IDPs and host communities recently displaced by conflict in Terekeka and Lainya. The target beneficiaries are residing in four locations namely Siremon, Lainya, Rokon and Kuda in Juba County and in Lainya and are considered food insecure. In this intervention, a total of 1667 Households constituting of 6500 Females and 3500 Males will be reached through a set of activities geared towards enhancing their resilience. Needs

Widespread displacement, the massive destruction of productive assets, the severe disruption of input and output markets and the continuing impact of climate related shocks and crisis has severely tested the resilience and livelihoods of rural and urban households in South Sudan. Livelihood loss, unemployment, deepening poverty, inflation and steep depreciation of the South Sudan’s pound have further eroded the capacity of families to meet basic needs and cope with the crisis. Household food security and economic situation of the families remains dismal, with an estimated 6.1 million people (59% of the population) having faced food crisis (IPC 3) between July and August 2018, of this population, 47,000 individuals were classified as being in IPC 5 (South Sudan’s Key IPC Findings: September 2018- March 2019).According to the same IPC findings, Central Equatoria reported 225,000 IPC3 cases between July and August 2018.The number is expected to decrease to 170,000 by December 2018 with a further decrease to 155,000 cases by March 2019.Between October 2018 and March 2019, IPC2 cases will increase from 250,000 to 285,000 in March 2019.Within the same period,IPC4 cases will decline from 15,000 cases in by December 2018 to 10,000 by March 2019.The reported decrease in IPC3 and IPC4 cases will occur if the state remain stable. However, the current displacements from Terekeka County and the expected increase in the number of returnees may arrest the decline. For example, IDPs in rural as well as urban areas in Central Equatoria are in dire need of food and livelihoods or emergency employment and sustainable income generating activities. Agricultural production in the state is hampered by a number of factors including; decreasing availability and increased prices of key agricultural inputs; continuing population displacement and insecurity creating a shortage of labor force and by extension rise in daily wages beyond the financial means of many farmers. As a result, farmers have abandoned their lands. In light of this, support to agriculture based livelihoods and cash support to most vulnerable population through support to income generating activities, establishment and implementation of conditional cash transfers and labor intensive schemes for the rehabilitation of damaged critical agricultural infrastructure will be key in strengthening the resilience of both IDPs and host communities. The proposed project aims at using a combination of in kind support and conditional cash transfer interventions. This will improve production capacities, address economic vulnerabilities, and complement the income of vulnerable IDPs and host communities households. Through the conditional cash transfer component, MaCDA aims to provide temporary employment and a source of income for vulnerable households, while focusing on the rehabilitation and reparation of community assets important to agriculture. The project will utilize intensive unskilled labor in order to reach the maximum possible community participation and the widest possible dispersal of cash. Women associations (WA) have been created in target locations to diversify and increase community and household income. The project will strengthen the capacity of women associations to increase food production and income. The proposed project will be implemented through women associations, farmers groups and beekeepers association. An assessment conducted by UNOCHA, MacDA and other agencies in the month of September 2018 has identified 5,040 IDPs displaced into Rokon, Siremon, Kuda and Dolo in Juba County from Terekeka County. The IDPs have integrated with the host community (estimated at 8,000 Persons by RRC).The project will target these IDPs plus another 7,000 IDPs currently residing in Lainya, Kenyi, Kupera, Wugi and Mukaya. As such, the proposed project will target a total of 1667 most vulnerable HHs (840 IDPs HHs& 827 Host Communities HHs).This translates to 10,000 beneficiaries.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Link between National and Local Conflict: After 2013, the local conflict in Central Equatoria has assumed a deadlier version. Proliferation of weapons from other areas including Juba has emboldened young men to conduct raids and revenge missions in rural counties such as Terekeka and also in neighbouring counties. This has resulted in death, destruction and displacement. Other factors driving conflict in Central Equatoria include Land issues, border issues, competition for grazing pastures, and unresolved grievances. Populations that are currently displaced in Siremon, Kuda, Rokon and Lainya are survivors of these deadly conflicts. Having participated in UNOCHA assessment in September 2018, MaCDA is well informed about these dynamics and has fashioned this intervention cognizant of risks in target location. The project design has also relied on Protection cluster protection analysis of threats and opportunities Present in target locations. Also, UNOCHA/MaCDA and other agencies have identified various protection concerns facing children, women, and the elderly as a result of the displacement. Displaced Women and adolescent girls face potential GBV risks while boys face the risk of recruitment. Further, the elderly people lack able bodied members of their families or caregivers to provide and support them. They face the risk of being exposed to exploitation or missing out on humanitarian services due to lack of insufficient information or inability to walk to distribution centres. Further, Gender norms continue to diminish the rights of women among target communities. In light of this, the project is designed to minimise these risks on beneficiaries as well as staffs. As such, the proposed intervention will factor in security concerns of various categories of populations and more so protection needs of women, children and youths.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

MaCDA is well versed with barriers that hinder access to assistance for various groups .For example, gaps in service accessibility for children and adults’ living with disabilities is wider compared to those with no impairments. High illiteracy rate in rural South Sudan may bar individuals deserving assistance from accessing information on available services. Other factors may include distance covered to access services, age of the beneficiaries, and protection concerns. Aware of these barriers, MaCDA will seek to minimize the impact of these barriers by implementing activities closer to settlement areas, awareness raising using local languages on services available, ensuring all individuals living with disabilities are registered among IDPs and Host communities including children in order to comprehensively meet the needs of the beneficiaries.
