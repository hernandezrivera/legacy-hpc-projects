Project Summary

The proposed project aims to reduce key mortality/morbidity rates among targeted women, men and young children in Area C of South Hebron by providing mobile health clinic services. Through this one-year project, MAP will partner with PMRS to contribute to improving the health status of 5 targeted communities in Area C of South Hebron which are Al Jawaya, Tarrama, Om Al Amad, Al Ridwana and Arabia., by providing essential PHC services; including preventive, diagnostic and treatment services, growth/developmental checks for young children, health awareness raising sessions, NCD care, pre and post natal care, reproductive health services and first aid training. There are two main objectives:
• To improve access to quality PHC services within the targeted vulnerable women, men and children
• To increase health resilience (ability to cope with disease, illness and trauma) within the targeted women, men and children
The provision of dedicated clinical team will ensure continuity of care. The team will include a general physician, women’s health care doctor, two community health workers, lab technician and driver, and will be equipped with a vehicle, clinical equipment, essential medicines and consumables, and have access to key diagnostic tests.
People with disability will be particularly targeted under this project, as MAP assigns budget for clinics accessibility. The location of each clinic will be assessed in terms of accessibility for all community groups including people with disability, then actions are taken to make sure it matches accessibility standards including, bathrooms, entrances and check-up and waiting spaces.

1 - Needs

In early 2020, WHO highlighted the lack of mobile clinics for vulnerable communities in the West Bank, stating that “At the end of 2019, mobile clinic coverage of communities in Area C fell from 85 to 60 per cent of people in need, according to WHO’s tracking system”. According to the 2021 HNO, there are about 150,000 Palestinians in marginalized areas including Area C, have limited or no access to fixed health care services. Additionally, in the Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment, 64% of households reporting access barriers in the three months prior to the data collection in July 2021, reported high cost of services and medications as a major barrier. In the West Bank, MAP is one of four mobile clinic service providers, who are targeting 44,372 individuals according to the Health Cluster bulletin as of June this year, out of 127,255 of identified people in need.Women and girls in the target communities are a particularly more vulnerable groups as they lack access to Sexual and Reproductive health services, as well as pre and postnatal care which impacts the health of both women and children. Moreover, health sector is often the first and sometimes only point of access for GBV victims, therefore there is a need to improve the detection, treatment and referral of GBV victims and survivors to ensure that they can access specialized protection services.People with disability in marginalized areas suffer from lack of access to health facilities. Transportation is not available or accessible to them in addition to lack of accessibility in the facilities themselves. Moreover, some people are in need for home visits for check ups and treatment as they are not able to reach clinics due to disability or injury.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

MAP staff will share our safeguarding policy with the local partner. Training on safeguarding and GBV will be conducted. MAP staff will conduct regular site visits to ensure proper beneficiary feedback. Direct interviews will be conducted as well as focus group discussions. The target communities are under continuous attacks by the occupation forces, and are under the risk of home demolition or property confiscation. For this reason, the clinic’s services will be provided in already existing structures in the community while the staff and equipment/supplies were transported via vehicle.MAP’s partners understand that any actions taken that could result in a breach of any of the provisions of this policy may lead to an immediate termination of the agreement. MAP and the partner will take all measures possible to ensure that all activities are always conducted according to MAP’s Safeguarding Policy and to ensure the safety and protection of children and vulnerable adults participating either directly or indirectly in the project. MAP will work with the partners to ensure that:1. the partners safeguarding policy, code of conduct, investigation procedures and beneficiary feedback/complaints mechanisms are consistent with global and regional guidelines/best practice.2. All the partners’ staff, trustees and volunteers sign the code of conduct in relation to safeguarding beneficiaries and fully understand their responsibilities within the code.3. All the partners’ staff, trustees and volunteers are provided with suitable safeguarding 4. All services provided for beneficiaries are assessed for the potential risk to those beneficiaries, with review of the risks/preventive action incorporated within the project’s action and monitoring plans.5. There is a shared understanding of what safeguarding concerns need to be reported to MAP immediately: a. Any allegation or disclosure of abuse that concerns a beneficiary.b. Any allegation of abuse made against a staff member, trustee or volunteer.c. Any observed or reported breach of the safeguarding code of conduct.d. Any other safeguarding concern they may have in relation to our project/beneficiaries.6. There is close collaboration between the Partner and MAP in any safeguarding investigation.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The mobile clinic is accessible for all members of the target communities. The community leaders communicate the mobile clinic schedule with all community members. Those who are unable to go to the clinic are provided with home visits by the mobile clinic team. Further, health services and education will be provided according to need and access for all will be ensured. MAP will ensure people with disability are included in project activities through providing regular home visits to cases identified in the target communities. People with disability will receive required checkups and test and will be referred to other health services providers as needed. An indicator will be set to measure number of people with disability reached. Regarding awareness raising, the clinic team will include people with disability in the door-to-door awareness visits with their families and will provide them with produced awareness material. Moreover, feedback from people with disability will be particularly encouraged and the clinic staff and MAP will ensure to collect complaints during home visits.
