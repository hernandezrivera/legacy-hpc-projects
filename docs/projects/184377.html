Project Summary

Deteriorating food insecurity and the resultant food consumption gaps owing to depletion of livelihoods has significantly impacted into the lives of vulnerable communities and displaced households in Banadir District ,Somalia. The key drivers of acute food insecurity include combined effects of poor and erratic Gu 2021 rainfall that was poorly distributed and further started very late and ended very early. Furthermore, floods damaged crops and farmland and further displaced local populations in riverine areas of Hiiraan, Shabelle, and Juba regions hence below-average Gu crop production whereas communities are yet to recover from impacts of past climatic and socio-economic shocks while impacts of desert locust continue to pose a serious risk to both pasture availability and crop production.

Current weather forecasts indicate an increased likelihood of below-average rainfall during the 2021 Deyr (October-December) season across most parts of the country which will adversely affect food security and nutrition outcomes where food insecurity is expected to further deteriorate among poor rural, urban, and displaced population. Approximately 3.5 million people in Somalia face acute food insecurity Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes in late 2021 (FSNAU-FEWS NET 2021 Post Gu Technical Report of September 9, 2021).

The project is aimed at supporting the most vulnerable persons in Banadir particularly in the Mogadishu and Afgooye with food interventions. The project will improve the lives of the IDPs by providing unconditional food vouchers to improve the immediate food security, health and nutritional well-being of IDPs, mainly by tackling the immediate food needs and reducing the percentage of people in severe acute food insecurity phases (crisis and worse). Food security interventions is aimed at preventing malnutrition in the population, especially among women, children, older persons without support, persons living with disabilities, female or child headed households and pregnant and lactating women. Priority will be given to households with malnourished children, female headed households (widowed divorcees, separated and without adequate support from extended family members), elderly headed, child headed households and Socially excluded groups (abandoned women, intellectual/mental disability all with no economic active members. The project will use the most appropriate form of food assistance through vouchers depending on the market establishment and accessibility and vendors will be selected from within the selected target areas with close proximity to the beneficiaries to reduce protection risks.
SSWC will conduct market assessment after every voucher distribution and cash transfers to see what are the market prices for the predetermined food ratio for the beneficiaries. This will track the availability of food and the fluctuation of the market prices. SSWC will coordinate the distribution of the food through the qualified vendors who meet the standards to distribute food to the beneficiaries and on the Cash transfer SSWC will use the available e-money transfer services within Somalia. SSWC will buy SIM cards that will be distributed to the beneficiaries to receive the money. There will be complaints and feedback registers at the camps and the distribution centres beneficiaries will call a free toll number that will manned by the project staff. Immediate solutions will be given to the beneficiaries who have complaints regarding the cash transfer and the distribution of food by the vendors. Voucher distributions will take place in the target communities, ensuring that the elderly, pregnant women and women with young children all have easy access to the selected distribution points. Post distribution monitoring will then be done to capture the impact and exact feeling of the community. This intervention will be organized to assess and monitor the food security status of the population.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

By using cash and vouchers, Save Somali Women and Children will allow the beneficiaries to have :-Flexibility and choice: Using cash or vouchers recognises that beneficiaries are best placed to decide what their households need, and gives them the flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities.Empowerment: The availability of cash gives households a sense of restored power over their immediate situation. In addition, there is evidence that receiving cash can at least temporarily empower women within the household. Access to financial structures such as bank accounts may empower beneficiaries, in particular women, in the longer-term as well. Supports or revitalises local trade: Injections of cash or the use of commodity vouchers with local traders benefits the recovery of trade and the local economy. Cash-based support for livelihood rehabilitation can potentially also provide a link between relief and longerterm economic recovery and may offer the opportunity for beneficiaries to increase income and create savingsDignity: In allowing beneficiaries to control what goods and services their households need, cash transfers are considered by many as more dignified than receiving goods in kind, and recognise beneficiaries as active participants in providing for the welfare of their families after a disaster.Supports or revitalizes local trade: Injections of cash or the use of commodity vouchers with local traders benefits the recovery of trade and the local economy. Cash-based support for livelihood rehabilitation can potentially also provide a link between relief and longer term economic recovery and may offer the opportunity for beneficiaries to increase income and create savings.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

During delivering assistance, exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance is possible and SSWC will prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm. Save Somali Women and Children will ensure the location of and access routes to distribution points and services are safe with enough space for orderly distribution. The distribution centers will be central and the distribution will take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. The risk of minority groups not being included in consultations or humanitarian services may occur. Save Somali Women and Children will consult marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific food requirements and preferences for distribution locations. SSWC will also ensure that a certain percentage of the selected vulnerable population is from minority groups.
