Project Summary

The proposed intervention links with and contributes to the Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) Sector strategic objectives for the upcoming humanitarian cycle, such as improving the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance; supporting the self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives; and improving communities’ capacity to sustain households’ livelihoods through the rehabilitation/building of productive infrastructure as well as supporting services. It aims to support displaced populations and host communities to meet their basic needs and access to essential basic services in West Darfur, West Kordofan, and South Kordofan States.
The recent conflict has exacerbated an already dire humanitarian situation, sending fuel and food prices soaring, and leading to stark reductions in food and commodity availability on the markets. The security situation has forced many households from their homes, with approximately 1,042,114 individuals (209,136 households) internally displaced and a further 319,194 individuals forced across the borders to neighbouring countries. This has meant that people have abandoned their farms, left their livelihoods and means of income, and has put stress on those of the host communities. The severe lack cash availability and restrictions on imports has hampered market functionality. This intervention will address this by providing life saving emergency responses to the affected population as appropriate. It will also contribute directly to the rest of FSL sector sub-objectives by addressing life threatening and life sustaining needs. In addition, it will indirectly contribute to the sector’s third strategic objective by providing investment in rehabilitation and construction of productive agricultural infrastructures and supporting initiatives related to addressing the negative impact of climate change.
In the latter half of 2023, Concern aims to reach and additional 56,595 individuals (25,468 men and 31,127 women) across West Darfur, South and West Kordofan through a set of multi-funded interventions combining in-kind food assistance, cash, and vouchers to respond to the specific food security and nutrition needs of internally displaced persons (IDPs), hosting and returning population groups, in addition to agricultural and livestock interventions (including animal vaccination campaigns).

1 - Needs

On the 15th April 2023, Sudan’s Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) launched a conflict which has seen one month of intense fighting across the country. Conflict has been ongoing in multiple cities across Sudan, including Khartoum and in West Darfur. The intense fighting has led to thousands of civilian deaths, over 1 million people being internally displaced and 319,194 individuals have crossed into neighbouring countries - primarily to Chad and South Sudan. The highest figures of displacement have been reported in West Darfur (23.86%). While West Kordofan and South Kordofan have not yet seen clashes, the wider conflict has had an impact on the state, including an increased number of IDPs, interruptions to supply chains to markets, fuel shortages and an increase in food prices. The fighting has continued at high intensity in West Darfur, specifically in Al Geneina, which has seen widespread violence, looting and restrictions to movement with all roads being blocked in the town. There have been significant disruptions to the markets, leading to increased food insecurity. Despite a commitment from both sides made on 12 May to protect civilians and allow aid in, they have failed to agree on a ceasefire. As the crisis is in the early phases and shows little signs of abating in the coming weeks, the situation remains volatile and changeable and the full impact on the operating environment is yet to be understood. The main planting season, which is set to begin at the end of May, is being threatened by the ongoing situation. If the season is missed, the number of people going hungry is set to increase even further. This additional threat to Sudan’s food system will impact women particularly. Before the crisis, 42% of female headed households (HHs) headed women already experienced food shortage, compared to 31% of male headed HHs, and women are reported to be eating less since the conflict began. In addition, according to FEWS NET, prolonged suspension of food assistance is anticipated to increase food insecurity in areas where aid was previously assessed to be preventing the worst outcomes—especially in greater Darfur and parts of greater Kordofan. Access to cash remains a significant issue, in West Darfur, while the increase in fuel prices and transportation costs has hindered both daily life and the ability of people to move out of insecure areas.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Lifesaving food security and livelihoods activities will be prioritised in the latter half of 2023 in the wake of the ongoing crisis. Concern will focus activities across West Darfur, West Kordofan and South Kordofan where the needs are great and rising. Support will be provided through in-kind food assistance, cash, and vouchers to respond to the specific food security and nutrition needs of the conflict affected population, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and hosting communities. In addition, agricultural support will be provided in the form of seed provision, extension services and pest control where appropriate. 56,595 individuals (25,468 men, 31,127 women) will be reached. Gender inclusion through specific targeting criteria and overall protection will be mainstreamed and communicated to partners/programme participants through training and sensitization sessions.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communitieswe assist; ii) the specifi c risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. Among other vulnerable individuals, refugee mothers and their children from the host communities who walk many hours to reach the centers will be oriented about ways to protect themselves on the way. Each HF, where the mobile services will refer to, will maintain the availability of a protection focal person and the communities will be informed about the role of this person. Traditional healers will be targeted by behavior change activities at community level. Focal persons in each HF (one male and one female) will be responsible for dealing with GBV cases and related protection issues. In the community where refugees settled, there will also have one-one protection focal point (possibly one female, and one male) for dealing with sensitive issues. Children will be in high focus, with field staff duly reporting on safeguarding. Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding the project objectives (e.g. access to information, access to services). In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical group sand backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, and host communities), information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a selected focal person in each group. Understanding of "Do No Harm" approach will be mainstreamed through training sessions and sensitization across project activities. In order to create a constant dialogue between connectors and dividers for conflict-sensitive decision making, conflict-sensitive activities are to be mainstreamed across all activities to promote community interaction between different groups e.g. agro-pastoralists and farmers through facilitation of conflict sensitive dialogue platforms; identification of existing community structures for dialogue (e.g. CBRM, VDAs, Pastoralists Associations, CPC, etc.); sensitization and strengthening of these groups to undertake project activities in a conflict sensitive way through Do No Harm workshops, social cohesion building through cultural and locally sensitive events; and training for the groups on identification and mapping of conflict-triggered hazards, with round-tables to be organized upon drafting of risk hazard mapping plans and related action points (ensuring cross-gender and inter-tribal participation)

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As a member of the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS), Concern continuously strengthens and expands systems and processes to ensure accountability across its development and humanitarian programmes. Concern first certified in 2017, and a re-certification audit took place in 2022 with re-certification confirmed in April 2022. Concern’s approach to accountability is based on the four pillars of: 1) information sharing; 2) participation; 3) feedback mechanisms; and 4) continual learning. Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) is in place for each area of operation, through which queries are invited, feedback provided and complaints are tracked. At the end of each year, country offices prepare a CRM report, which analyses the information regarding complaints received and highlights any learning and/or changes made by the country teams as a result of complaints received, as well as some of the successes and challenges reported by country offices. Country Management Teams provides overall direction and leadership support to implement CRM. Relevant Country Management Team members initiate investigations where necessary and are involved in decision-making based on outcome of investigations. All staff are sensitised on the CRM system and accountability principles, and information sessions and consultations are carried out with beneficiaries in relation to their rights and methods of access. All sensitive cases including sexual abuse, sexual harassment, fraud, and any serious breaches of Concern polices fall under a system of direct referral to senior management, specifically, to the Country Director. The remaining valid complaints/feedback coming to the CRM are responded or referred to the relevant programme teams for follow-up. In the event of low literacy level among programme participants, Concern will consult them to design an alternative accessible. This will be tailored to FSL project activities and ensured at every project site. Specific sector activities will be undertaken to ensure AAP, e.g. PDM after livestock/seeds distribution to ensure that recipients can express their opinions on quality of the assistance received.
