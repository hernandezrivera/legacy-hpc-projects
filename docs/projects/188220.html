Project Summary

The proposed intervention's overall objective is contributing directly to the education sector 2022 HRP objectives by improving equitable access for school-aged children to safe, inclusive, and quality learning environments to foster their resilience. This will be achieved through implementing a package of education activities to 50 schools in NW Syria which are located in underserved and high-needs areas, where 25000 children (11000 boys and 14000 girls 'including 600 CwD ~2.5%', 5-17 years old) are enrolled (70% IDPs). By supporting a quality learning environment, White Hands (WH) seeks to increase children's enrollment in the targeted schools.
The project formulation has been based on needs identified by the Education Cluster, supported with RNA conducted by WH in Dec 2021, and firsthand consultations with camps management and local authorities/stakeholders.
Based on 'WoS_PiN_Severity_2022' statistics; 20% of the project total direct targeted beneficiaries are within severity scale 5 (Catastrophic), and 80% are within severity scale 4 (Extreme).
WH aims to meet the objective of this program through the following interventions:
1. Provision of formal and non-formal education for children.
2. Small-scale rehabilitation of damaged schools and establishment of other safe temporary/alternative learning spaces through a gender-sensitive and disability-friendly approach.
3. WH will conduct a BTL campaign at the community level and in the targeted camps. Activities will target children, community leaders, and caregivers.
4. The project will cover the schools' monthly operational costs, including recreational materials, refreshment, cleaning, hygiene materials, stationery, fuel for heating, and the provision of students' and teachers' kits.
5. All teachers and education personnel (550: 300 men & 250 women) will be provided with monthly incentives to increase their motivation and ensure their retention in schools.
6. The quality of education will be improved through contextualized and targeted teacher training and further support to their professional development within each school by delivering (CSG, TiCC, PSEA, COVID-19) trainings.
7. Provision of schools’ furniture and students’ desks.
8. Providing weekly PSS sessions to overcome the harsh experience of war conditions and to motivate them to continue their studies at schools.
Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs) will be a component in the activities of this project. Quarterly meetings for PTAs members will be held to discuss what was achieved in the last period and set with WH’s staff plans for the upcoming period.
To ensure safe and easy access for CWD (boys and girls), WH will provide schools with tools and equipment for CWD such as ramps, Iron stair handrails, special toilets, not connected desks and chairs with height adjustable function in addition to providing transportation for CWD.
Due to COVID-19, there will be comprehensive precautionary measures in the 50 schools, including periodic sterilization processes for classrooms and WASH facilities, awareness sessions for students, training for teachers and educational staff, and sterilization provision materials for all schools.
Stakeholders in the project implementation site, including children, adolescents, and older persons, including PWDs, and their opinions are taken into account while developing the project design, implementation of activities so that they have better access to humanitarian services and information in accordance with their specific situation.
The project will make sure that children engaging in child labor or recruited for military purposes, or living with older or disabled caregivers or adolescent girls at risk of early or forced marriage are given special attention and have their own space to participate in the project activities and are properly referred as per Protection cluster guidelines.
Note: 50% of this program will be implemented during 2022, while the other 50% are planned for 2023.

4 - Needs

According to the 2022 Syria HNO education chapter, 6.4M children (96%) & 200K school personnel (4%) are estimated to be in need of humanitarian education assistance. Of these, 0.5M (7%) are PWDs. The protracted nature of the conflict, economic duress, and the Covid 19 pandemic, continue to impact overstretched education services. With 25 verified attacks on schools and seven verified instances of military use of schools in nine months, attacks affecting schools remain high. At the national level, the data reveals that 18% are out of school, and half of the children who are not in school are never enrolled. Rates of non-attendance were highest in Idleb (28%) and Aleppo (26%). Over a quarter of households with school-aged children indicated that their children are not in school because there is no school to send their child to. In 1,017 assessed campsites in NWS, there are only 196 schools. Idleb has a critical confluence of negative factors: low attendance rates, the highest classroom to school-aged population ratio, the only governorate with triple and quadruple shifts, nearly half the schools are non-operational and the largest proportion of children traveling +45 minutes to school and the highest rates of attacks on education. Limited availability of non-formal education services will continue to reduce the opportunities for children to return to learning and be brought to age-appropriate learning levels.

5 - Implementation of activities

WH aims to discourage drop-out & increase meaningful quality learning as well as increase enrollment of out-of-school children; also, to secure the functioning of schools through -Promoting child safeguarding & Edu quality in 50 schools in NW Syria by delivering training for teachers on TiCC, CSG, PSEA, & COVID19 by the WH field team. -Increase awareness among parents & local communities on the importance of education & how to be protected from the COVID19 pandemic by conducting digital Back to Learning campaigns within the targeted communities.-Determine children's educational levels & enable the teachers to plan & focus on the needed educational activities for each student by undergoing an ASER test. -Improve children's well-being & learning capacity through recreational activities -Ensure safe & quality learning environments by the provision of monthly required operational supplies. -Conduct small-scale rehabilitation works. -Provision of Education & COVID19 prevention materials to schools, teachers, & children, -Conduct periodic sterilization process. -PTAs establishment per school. -Quarterly meetings for PTAs members to set plans for the upcoming period. - Trainings for PTAs about safety and protection-related topics. -Providing weekly PSS sessions. -Providing monthly incentives to teachers and education personnel. -Conduct MEAL activities: field visits, PDM, MEAL report, feedback mechanism.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk1: Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff, or related entities.MS: Learning facilities should have Child Safeguarding policies and systems in place by the WH should be regularly monitored and corrective action is taken when needed. The goal of child safeguarding is to create and maintain a safe learning environment through preventive actions that protect children from actions, or inactions that may lead to harm.Risk2: Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment, act violently, bully and neglect students. MS: All teachers will sign a code of conduct once hired, the code of conduct will be explained carefully. Principals/headmasters, teachers, and caregivers will be made aware of positive discipline methods. Teachers and school personnel will be trained on the prevention and reporting of violence, physical punishment, and bullying.Risk3: Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe.MS: Learning facilities will be improved to ensure that buildings and grounds are safe and protected including stairs, walls, roofs, flooring, electricity, water, etc. All works will be supervised and monitored by WH technical staff. WH will ensure CSG measures are in place before work starts.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts& Address any SEA complaints.A child safeguarding risk assessment, gender, GBV audit, and conflict sensitivity analysis are conducted for each program. These identify gaps, inform the project design, and mitigate protection risks for children and their families while accessing WH facilities and using WH services. In addition, WH has prioritized more sustained capacity-building for staff related to disability and disability inclusion, as well as on gender mainstreaming and GBV.Specific attention concerning complaints/feedback mechanisms (CFMs) will be given to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Following the ‎PSEA Network recommendations, WH's focal points will ensure the availability of information to the beneficiaries ‎concerning CFMs through informative sessions at the beginning of the activities, making sure that beneficiaries are ‎aware and engaged, if necessary in improving the design, monitoring, and review of the CFMs.
