Project Summary

This project aims at restoring and improving food and livelihood security conditions of the most vulnerable displaced 15,000 individuals (3750 men, 5250 Women, 2550 boys, 3450 girls) in Terekeka (CES), Kapoeta South and Magwi (EES), Mundri West and Mvolo Counties (WES), affected by conflict, by addressing their immediate and long-term food needs through resilience and recovery approaches. The conditional general food distribution approach will address the immediate needs by enhancing skills, through training in improved agriculture practices, like value addition and post-harvest handling, as a conditionality for accessing the food rations. At the same time, the cash-for-work approach is intended to restore basic services that are key to effectively stabilize the affected communities and encouraging many displaced populations including refugees to return back to their original settlements, thus facilitating early recovery and sustainable development. Besides, short-term work created through emergency employment recovery projects in the cash-for-work projects can be a catalyst to enabling access to markets and other social services while enhancing asset creating for improved standard of living.
The choice of the above modalities is based on the fact that, since the onset of conflict in South Sudan, over 80% of emergency food and livelihood assistance has been through in-kind modality, with no sustainable gains in the long-term rather exacerbates the dependency syndrome. It is therefore against this background and in response to the immense calls from the FSL Cluster and the Cash Working Group, South Sudan to find innovative solutions that LCED proposes a cash-based intervention modality to scale up FSL assistance in a more yet robust sustainable manner. LCED has vast and greater experience in implementing cash projects in Greater Equatoria under the shelter & NFI Cluster for the last two years and will use this expertise; the existing stricture and strong working relationships with the targeted communities; strong presence presence blended with the contextual knowledge of the targeted locations to successfully implement this project. Furthermore, this specific proposed project has been informed by the findings of various assessments and post-distribution monitoring conducted by LCED that still reveal the need for long term solutions to food security and asset creation (Ref; LCED needs analysis, market assessments and post cash distribution monitoring, 2019 – SNFI South Sudan Cluster website).
LCED will ensure that the gender aspect is incorporated in the project cycle for female inclusion and participation. Project beneficiaries will be mainly women and youth (60% women, 15% IDPs/returnees, and 35% youth) selected from households with malnourished children, female-headed households, pregnant and lactating mothers as direct beneficiaries of both food-for-work. Further, the village committees will emphasize female representation to ensure their unique needs, voices and feedback are considered. Gender appropriate and agreed project in consultation with women and youth like rehabilitating community assets like existing roads, schools, and water points will be emphasized during the project identification to minimize GBV and child labor risks. Monitoring will form integral part of the project, and will be done jointly with community representatives to ensure quality assurance and adherence to agreed guidelines, also involving a Third Party Monitoring Agent to give an independent and informed view about the project and recommending areas for future improvement. Lastly, an agreed Complaint Feedback Mechanism will be established in consultation with the community to encourage the community to provide and share feedback about the project activities.

1 - Needs

South Sudan remains one of the most food-insecure countries in the world, due to conflict, displacement, restricted movement, and disruption of trade/markets, long dry spell, recent floods and Fall Army Worm (FAW) attack on crops (FAO, 2019). The impact of displacement on cultivation activities continues to exacerbate food insecurity and humanitarian needs situation leading to persistent hunger, large-scale asset losses and livelihoods disruptions (USAID, Aug 2019). Besides, multiple displacements coupled with the absence of social safety nets have exacerbated poverty and increased food insecurity. Acute malnutrition level among children in South Sudan has also increased from 13% in 2018 to 16% in 2019 (FAO, Sept 2019) and about 54% of the population is reported being food insecure (IPC August, 2019). It is therefore imperative to prioritize food production and market functioning in areas at risk of food insecurity as they have a strong linkage to humanitarian situation throughout the country. Overall, agriculture production and markets have been negatively affected by rapid inflation, insecurity, and logistical constraints when supplying goods, and the high market-dependency in areas of low food production is a further driver of food insecurity due to increased food prices (JMMI Report, 2019). In the face of strained coping mechanisms, absence of external assistance over a large area for an extended period of time, Famine (IPC Phase 5) would be likely to lead to increased levels of conflict over remaining scarce resources.Currently, Greater Equatoria is characterized by limited food supply and basic goods; loss of livelihoods/income-generation opportunities; reduction in source of income; and damaged infrastructures for service delivery (over 30% of health, education, facilities are either destroyed or damaged) which makes the available services more expensive for the majority of vulnerable population. IPC analysis (Aug 2019) reports that in Central Equatoria alone, an estimated 400,000 people (27.5% of the population) are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and all counties are classified in IPC phase 3, except Terekeka which is in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) which is likely to be the most food insecure county in CES (IPC August 2019). In Eastern Equatoria State, an estimated 560,000 people (52.6% of the State population) were facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in August 2019. Magwi (EES) and Terekeka (CES), among other Counties, were classified as IPC AMN Phase 3. The same report however also notes that there has been a significant improvement in the security situation in Greater Equatoria, and especially Western Equatoria State, following the signing of the Revitalized Peace Agreement which has resulted in an influx of returnees resuming farming (REACH, Aug 2019), contributing to new cultivation, which is likely to increase food production. However, agricultural-related challenges and inability to work due to lack of tools, seeds, access to markets and health services will continue to reduce food availability for the affected Counties if an immediate solution is not provided. According to LCED findings (LCED PDM Report/Market Assessment, Mundri West, July 2019) diminished activities in the market due to degraded infrastructures (caused by conflict and climate change) are likely to cause food shortages and lead to an increase in food prices. Limited employment and income generating activities in the current context of economic collapse will also limit households’ consistent ability to access adequate food. Additionally, extremely high food prices has limited household food access, while malfunctioning agricultural production, dysfunctional market supply chain system, overwhelming agri-business operational challenges faced by small holder farmers and market traders lead to reduced food production/livelihoods.

2 - Activities

(1) Conditional General Food Distribution;(1.1) Inception meeting/stakeholder engagement meetings; Community-based consultations to identify food security gaps, beneficiary selection criteria (guided by FSL vulnerability criteria), and conditional activities in this response plan (FFW). (1.2) Beneficiary Registration: identify FSL needs and beneficiaries of the FFW jointly with local authorities, community leaders, NGOs implementing FSL activities, to ensure participation and avoid duplication. The program will target 1,667 HHs (10,000 ind).(1.3) Community awareness; beneficiary sensitization on what to expect from the project, their roles and any reporting channels and procedures agreed upon. Project beneficiaries will be informed further of the different activities to participate in including the conditions to be met to receive the food rations.(1.4) Identification & training of Project Management Committees (PMCs); PMCs comprising of 8-10 members (60% female) will be formed at boma level to oversee the implementation of project activities, including meeting participation, monitoring jointly with LCED staff, and handle beneficiary feedback. These shall undergo 5 days basic training in providing support to selected HHs to implement the various capacity building sessions, and to the LCED staff during food distribution.(1.5) Identification of project sites & ration cards distribution; identification of distribution sites and schedule by LCED jointly with PMCs and local leaders, in order to ensure access to all beneficiaries. Ration cards/tokens (with name and HH number) will be distributed by PMCs to registered beneficiaries, to receive the food rations.(1.6) Training; As a precondition to receive food ration, the beneficiaries will have to successfully complete trainings in improved farming practices, selection of quality seeds, improved fishing methods, value addition, post-harvest handling, improved hygiene practices, gender and protection mainstreaming among others. (1.7) Seeds & tools trade fairs; Identification of suppliers of quality tools and seeds, conduction quality testing and sensitization of the suppliers and the community at large on the implementation of trade fairs. (1.8) M&E including PDM, Third Party Monitoring, End of project evaluation; Monitoring will be done throughout the activity implementation to measure project achievements, identify and address gaps; share key lessons for proper and informed decision learning. An end line evaluation will be conducted by an external consultant at the end of the project to measure effectiveness, efficiency and impact of the intervention. (2) CFW:(2.1) Inception meeting/stakeholder engagement meetings; see 1.1(2.2) Beneficiary Registration; see 1.2. The program will target 834 households (5,000 individuals). (2.3) Community awareness; see 1.3.(2.4) Formation of Cash Transfer Committee (CTC); CTCs of 5-8 members (60% female) will be nominated from within the community at boma level, and together with the LCED team and the local authorities, will identify locations for cash distributions within a maximum of 1 hour walking distance from the beneficiary community. Targeted household, verified by the CTCs, will receive a cash voucher from LCED which will be used alongside during the redemption process.(2.5) Identification of CFW projects; LCED working with local leaders/community representatives will identify at least 4 CFW projects, aimed at employment generation and improving household access to cash and asset creation (rural road rehabilitation, clearance/repairing of community water points/sanitation facilities, repairs of health/education facilities), targeting 500 households (70% women and youth). (2.6) Distribution of Seeds and Tools Voucher; all beneficiaries (2,500 HHs) will also be recipients of vouchers for agricultural inputs so as to assist them in ensuring their own food security in 2020, through organization of seeds fairs. (2.7) M&E; see 1.8

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Risk analysis through safety audits is a precondition for LCED project staff before any field engagement, and this project will not be an exception. Project locations will consider accessibility; area distress index, security situation; high concentration of IDPs and or returnees that are food insecure and impact of the conflict of community infrastructures. The project will be implemented based on a contextual analysis to be conducted before hand to ensure that the interventions do not cause or escalate conflicts in the target areas (do no harm principle), closely monitored to identify and mitigate possible conflicts and associated risks. The selection of distribution sites and sites for trade fairs will be done taking into consideration issues of access; safety and security to ensure that marginalized groups like women, girls, and persons with special needs are considered and not discriminated. Gender and age appropriate activities will be identified in consultation with women and youth to encourage their participation while minimizing risks of GBV and child labour. Work norms will also be developed with the community to guide activity implementation and adherence. Additionally, the Project Management Committees and Cash Transfer Committees formed, including female and youth representatives (60% women), will emphasize women representation to break the gender-cultural norms of stereotyping women for income-generating tasks, and will also act as Feedback and Complaint Mechanism. The LCED staff will also ensure gender balance while hiring enumerators, local laborers, and volunteers, in order to provide a source of income to both. Appropriate complaint feedback channels will be agreed upon with the community to ensure they freely provide feedback/information about the project activities including conduct of field staff. Besides, GBV referral pathways and PSEA reporting channels shall be shared with the community at inception phase.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Community engagement in project implementation is paramount for ownership, encouraging participation and sustainability of projects intended to benefit them. LCED will employ a community -based approach throughout this project through community consultations in defining the selection criteria, community projects, work norms, food security gaps (VAM), and formation of community committees. Community members will also be engaged during monitoring of project activities and providing feedback through an agreed established beneficiary complaint feedback channels and referral pathways. Project beneficiaries will be informed further of their roles, what to expect from the project, the different activities to participate in and conditions to be met to receive under each modality. Additionally, PMCs will be formed at boma level and engaged throughout the activity implementation through supervision of community activities, monitoring and reporting. To avoid conflict between beneficiaries and other community members, the beneficiary registration will be done jointly with local authorities and community leaders later displayed at county headquarters to ensure transparency, accountability and good working relationships within the community. Additionally, trade fairs are intended to give an opportunity to farmers from within the community to show case their capacity, and others to easily access a variety yet quality items at affordable prices, so as to have the community at large benefiting from this project while injecting money to boost the local economy.
