Project Summary

Under this project, NRC will train a team facilitators to conduct Education in Emergency (EiE) activities in collective shelters where newly displaced children and their families are seeking safety. NRC will use the Child Education Package – Emergency (CEP Emergency). The CEP Emergency is a 1 to 20-day programme designed to provide safe and welcoming spaces for children and youth when newly displaced (i.e. within the first 90 days of an emergency). Key activities that NRC will carry our under this programme include life skills, psychosocial skills and recreational skills for pupils and educators and providing a safe space where children and youth can connect, make friends, discuss age-appropriate issues and concerns and feel a sense of connectedness in what is often a new and very unfamiliar setting.

1 - Needs

An estimated 400,000 Libyan, migrant and refugee children and education personnel have been affected by the protracted crisis, continuous fighting and the lack of legal status in Libya. According to HNO 2020, around 82,000 school aged IDP, returnees, non-displaced, migrants and refugees are in need to safe and protective access to quality formal and non-formal education. School-aged children across Libya are exposed to violence in and outside the classroom, ranging from bullying and corporal punishment to witnessing the horrors of war. The IOM DTM Round 27 data on schools shows that across the country, 268 school buildings were affected while 197 are partially damaged, 47 are totally destroyed and 24 are used as collective shelter for IDPs. Findings from MSNA 2019 and JENA 2019 show that access to education is constrained due to displacement, schools being closed and damaged, lack of teaching learning materials and security concerns linked to the commute to and from school. The wellbeing and learning ability of children has been severely affected due to conflict induced trauma and stress. Six percent of total HHs with children have observed negative behavior and emotional changes in their children because of conflict and displacement. The teachers require capacity building to teach in emergency context and identify and address the psycho-social needs for their students and colleagues. Although 96% of Libyan children are enrolled in schools,35% of HHs with school aged children reported their children faced issues in schools. According to affected populations, one of the biggest issues faced by children in schools is the lack of teaching quality, which actually prevents some families from sending their children to school. By the end of 2019, the escalation of fighting in Tripoli has caused the suspension of all public and private schools in Abu Salim and Ain Zara municipalities in addition to 4 schools in Souq Al Joumaa. According to the last statement given by the Humanitarian coordinator in Libya, over 210 schools and kindergartens are closed, denying more than 113,000 school-aged students the right to education.

2 - Activities

1- Train formal and non-formal teachers for emergency in education response: In coordination with the MoE and the School principals, NRC will provide 5 days + 1 day mock sessions to 10 teachers from formal schools that are selected to benefit from the “Creating Safe and Inclusive Programme”. The training will cover modules from the ‘Teachers in Crisis Contexts’ training pack (especially child well-being and pedagogy), NRC’s Better Learning Program (BLP) and Teacher’s Code of Conduct. NRC will provide training to 26 recruited incentive teachers that will be providing the EiE and remedial classes. The training will be 5 days of training + 1 day of mock sessions and will cover modules from the ‘Teachers in Crisis Contexts’ training pack (especially child well-being and pedagogy), NRC’s Better Learning Program (BLP) and Teacher’s Code of Conduct. In addition, NRC will provide a 2 days’ induction on the CEP emergency programme and remedial programme for the incentive teachers.2- Provide non-formal education in emergency classes to newly displaced children in collective shelter: Education in Emergency (EiE) classes will be provided for 3,000 newly displaced children in collective shelters in Tripoli. The materials of the curriculum reflect the Middle East context, and are both in Arabic and English. Displaced children will be able to have some normalcy where they connect and learn with other peers in a safe environment. For EiE, any child who attend one day of the 1 to 20 days programme will be considered as a beneficiary. Since this an EiE that is mobile and responding to newly displaced children, the collection of data might be sensitive in some cases so the source of verification shall be attendance lists and photos if unable to collect attendance data.
