Project Summary

Most vulnerable school-aged children including those with special needs/ disabilities (3 to 17 years) continue to face an array of shocks and have been impacted by prolonged closure of schools in 2020 and 2021 due to COVID 19 pandemic, and now 2022 this has been compounded by school destruction by floods and displacements due to conflict (MOGEI, 2022; UNICEF Country Reports 2022; Floods Updates 2022). At least 2.7 million additional children were impacted by the prolonged closure of schools in 2020 and 2021, with current estimated number of out of school children is 2.8 million (HNO, 2022).
Recent waves of insecurity and flooding in Pibor, Wau and other counties continue to negatively impact education with consequent destruction and occupation of schools and learning spaces, with the two counties ranked severity 5 and 4 respectively in 2023 (Education Cluster Nov 2022). This is in addition to conflict induced displacement of teachers and children, killings of teachers and the risk of revenge killings of boys in schools which have further contributed to lack of retention and access to schools. With the country facing unprecedented food insecurity levels (7.76 million likely to face severe food insecurity in 2023 lean season) in addition to at least 1.4 million children at risk of malnutrition, could further hamper school attendance in addition to significant impacts on brain development of learners, with some resorting to other livelihood activities instead of schools. Girls have in addition been victim of early an forced marriages, attributed to cultural norms tied to high dowries, further impacting their access to education. Children with disabilities continue to face additional risks attributed to lack of assistive or mobility devices further leading to low retention rates among children with special needs; further compounded by irregular payment of teacher salaries, inadequate learning spaces, lack of school supplies and untrained teachers (Education Needs assessment 2021; HNO 2022; IPC Nov 2022).
Stop Poverty Communal Initiative (SPOCI) has recently successfully implemented an SSHF funded project in Wau in addition to recently completed EiE action in Pibor, that will scale up 2023 initiatives, that will support a total of 5,056 people comprised of 2000 girls and 2000 boys including those with disabilities, 528 men and 528 women which are in line and correspond to the three cluster objectives aimed at improving equitable access to safe and protective education services for emergency affected IDPs, returnees, spontaneous refugees, and host community children including children with disabilities and youth (girls and boys); Improve the quality of gender and disability-inclusive education services including where appropriate use of assistive devices for those with disabilities and teachers' capacity to protect the wellbeing of all children and prevent the negative impact of the emergencies on learning environments; and strengthening the education system’s response capacity of the school community to mitigate the impact of existing emergencies on learners, teachers, including PTAs and communities.
Proposed actions will be integrated with WASH, Protection, Health and Nutrition integrated programming while taking into consideration Accountability of Affected Populations, Gender Equity, Protection concerns including GBV and sound environmentally friendly approaches. SPOCI is a national NGO with over 7 years programmatic presence in Pibor and Wau and is a member of National NGOs Steering Committee, member of the ICWG and AAP Technical Working Group and Monitoring and Evaluation Working Group. In addition, SPOCI has fostered strong linkages with communities, state/county line ministries in addition to strong and competent team including staff redeployment to scale up interventions based on needs.

1 - Needs

Most vulnerable school-aged children including those with special needs/ disabilities (3 to 17 years) continue to face an array of shocks and have been impacted by prolonged closure of schools in 2020 and 2021 due to COVID 19 pandemic, and in 2022 school destruction by floods and displacements due to conflict (MOGEI, 2021; UNICEF Country Reports 2022; Floods Situation Updates Nov 2022). At least 2.7 million additional children were impacted by the prolonged closure of schools in 2020 and 2021, with current estimated number of out of school children is 2.8 million (HNO, 2022). Recent waves of insecurity and flooding in Pibor, Wau and other counties continue to negatively impact education with consequent destruction and occupation of schools and learning spaces. This is in addition to conflict induced displacement of teachers and children, killings of teachers and the risk of revenge killings of boys in schools which have further contributed to lack of retention and access to schools. With the country facing unprecedented food insecurity levels (7.76 million likely to face severe food insecurity in 2023 with IPC 5 pockets in Pibor) in addition to at least 1.4 million children at risk of malnutrition (IPC November 2022), could further hamper school attendance in addition to significant impacts on brain development of learners, with some resorting to other livelihood activities instead of schools. Girls have in addition been victim of early an forced marriages, attributed to cultural norms tied to high dowries, in addition to gender issues related to menstrual hygiene management, further impacting their access to education. Children with disabilities continue to face additional risks attributed to lack of assistive or mobility devices further leading to low retention rates among children with special needs; further compounded by irregular payment of teacher salaries, inadequate learning spaces, lack of school supplies and untrained teachers and PTAs (Education Needs assessment 2021; HNO 2022). The situation of girls and boys, including those with special needs has been exacerbated by cyclical waves of inter-communal violence and age set fighting among youth (OCHA Updates, 2022). This is in addition to related protection concerns facing girls and boys, including abduction and use of children in armed hostilities (NGO Forum Updates, 2022; REACH Situation Overview Reports, 2022). According to the latest HNO 2022, Pibor and Wau are among other counties facing severe food insecurity levels which contribute negatively to the wellbeing/health and subsequent malnutrition which has consequent cognitive development among children (girls and boys); in addition to 2023 severity 4 and 5 respectively (Education Cluster 2022). Poor access to safe water, hygiene, sanitation and feeding practices, particularly in schools, with heightened COVID rising cases, are likely to further aggravate vulnerability of girls, boys, men and women. School WASH facilities were submerged due to floods in 2021 and 2022 compounded by collapsed latrine facilities in addition to widespread open defecation with potential risks exposure to water borne diseases; coupled with poor sanitation coverage including schools and lack of hand washing facilities, poor knowledge and practices on personal and environmental further fuel the situation (IRNA Report, 2022; SPOCI Situation Reports 2022). SPOCI recently successfully completed an SSHF funded action in Wau in addition to recently completed EiE action in Pibor, that will scale up 2023 initiatives, that will support 5,056 beneficiaries comprised 2000 girls and 2000 boys including those with disabilities, 528 men and 528 women which are in line and correspond to the three cluster objectives.

2 - Project activities

SPOCI will support;• Enrolment of children (girls and boys) in pre-primary, primary and secondary schools/learning centers• Establishment of temporary learning spaces and classrooms (established in line with Inter-INEE's Minimum Standards for Education in Emergencies and Standards in Education Cluster Strategy)• Rehabilitation of temporary learning spaces and classrooms• Establishment of gender segregated latrines and water points• Rehabilitation of gender segregated latrines and water points • Provision of sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers. • Provision of emergency-affected children quality education recreational materials, teaching and learning materials for ECD, primary, and secondary school education• Training of teachers on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centred methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children• Training and updating of teachers and learners on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition. • Train SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support, and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change• Support prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets) and support conducting back to learning campaigns.Routine monitoring and reporting including Cluster reporting, 5Ws submission.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Implementation committees shall include other vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities, women, girls, boys and men including illiterate ones who shall be guided and supported to ensure their views and concerns are taken into consideration. These will have an equal opportunity to provide feedback and complaints including key contacts their can use for confidentialreporting and feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Stop Poverty Community Initiative (SPOCI) will select and brief AAP project implementation committees who will be elected from the community and will include people with special needs. These committees will be mandated oversee implementation in addition offer means for communities to provide feedback on the project and to submit complaints, and to ensure that they receive a timely response; in addition to the program responding and changing design in respect to feedback; review how complaints are received and handled, consult with communities on how they could best submit complaints, and develop a plan for establishing mechanisms that shall be discussed and implemented in consultation with other key stakeholders.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

SPOCI shall refrain from, and shall take all reasonable and appropriate measures to prohibit its employees or other persons engaged and controlled by it from exchanging any money, goods, services, or other things of value, for sexual favors or activities, or from engaging in any sexual activities that are exploitative or degrading to any beneficiary. All SPOCI staff have receivedtraining on PSEA and have signed organization’s PSEA policy including newly recruited staff. SPOCI does not tolerate any form of discrimination against ethnic groups, clans, minorities or communities. SPOCI shall ensure that the selection of beneficiaries is transparent and conforms to established selection criteria and is applied objectively and without prejudice.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Stop Poverty Community Initiative (SPOCI) will select and brief AAP committees who will be elected from the community and will include people with special needs. These committees will be mandated oversee implementation in addition offer means for communities to provide feedback on the project and to submit complaints, and to ensure that they receive a timely response; review howcomplaints are received and handled, consult with communities on how they could best submit complaints, and develop a plan for establishing mechanisms that shall be discussed and implemented in consultation with other key stakeholders.
