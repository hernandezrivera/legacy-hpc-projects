Project Summary

Grace Chin Foundation through this project aims to provide re- usable sanitary pads to school girls in formal and informal learning centres in the crisis areas of Cameroon. This is to increase the rate of school attendance despite the period flow which causes discomfort during classes when there are no sanitary pads. Since the outbreak of the Covid-19 pandemic which has left 6,369 schools (3,692 in NW and 2,677 in SW) and about 4,200 Community Learning Spaces (2,436 in NW and 1,764 in SW) closed to contain the spread of the Covid-19. Over 1,033,000 (570,000 from NW and 463,000 from SW) school aged children are out of school due to the Conflict and COVID-19, a total of 28,866 (16,742 from NW and 12,124 from SW) teachers are not in schools. With the presence of these two emergencies in these parts of the country, about 3 adolescent girls out of 5, face the challenge of providing proper menstrual hygiene and sanitation to themselves because of limited education on it due to little or no guidance from parents for the shame of discussing what they believe is a taboo to talk about. Adolescent girls in these crisis areas of Cameroon find it difficult going through extremely painful periods, which affects their daily life, attendance and performance in school. The limited knowledge on menstrual hygiene and sanitation and the inability to provide sanitary pads restricts movements of the girls during their periods; they feel ashamed and unclean which also contributes to them skipping classes, hence poor performances in their various schools/place of learning. With such an emergency in our communities, it is important that these young girls get educated on how to manage these situation so as to have a clear focus in school while learning in comfort. They will be educated on menstrual hygiene management, how to make reusable pads to reduce cost of buying ones that are not reusable and how to maintain the hygiene level of the pads. Before these trainings in various schools, these adolescent girls will be given a” My Pride Box” each which contains menstrual hygiene and sanitation items they can be using while taking the training. The age group of girls to receive these “My Pride Box” ranges from 10-18years; girls from all ethnic groups will be part of these including victims from the crisis, most especially the girls from the bushes, orphans and displaced girls. Covid-19 measures will be put in place for a proper and effective training in various classrooms. This project will cover the whole of Fako division including towns and villages like Buea, Limbe, Ekona, Bonakanda, Bokwai, Bova, Muea, Tiko, Tole and a host of other communities in this division. The project also aims at training young girls from ages 16 and above including teachers and parents on producing organic sanitary pads that are reusable in communities, this is to ensure a constant supply of comfortable and healthy sanitary pads to the girls to help boost their confidence and participation while they study. Parents and guidance will be sensitized on the importance of educating their children on menstrual hygiene and sanitation, how it will boost their confidence and create a better world for them to not be ashamed but stay confident in comfort. Putting in place all covid-19 measures for the safety of the girls and other beneficiaries, girls, boys and other community members like in Tole, Buea Town, Limbe will be sensitized on menstruation, to curb the myth, stigma and harmful gender norms that are affecting girls in this region. To teach them on menstrual hygiene and curb the negative ideas they have about this natural occurrence. The project is targeting 10,000 girls and boys (6,000 girls and 4,000 boys) to receive the “My Pride Box” in 1,00 schools and safe spaces in the SW. 2,000 participants including teachers will be sensitized on producing re-usable pads so they can replicate it in their different communities.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Girls from the ages of 10-18 years in the crisis affected areas of Cameroon undergo a lot of struggles in terms of providing sanitary pads for themselves especially orphans who have lost their parents in the crisis. This affects the level of interaction and performance in schools as many find it hard sitting without sanitary pads in class for fear of embarrassment; they chose to stay at home during school hours to evade the shame. Many of these girls miss school during their periods. A research done by United Nations estimates suggest that 1 in 10 girls from Sub-Saharan Africa miss school during their menstrual period, with some missing out on 20% of their education, this is a general estimate without a crisis but in these two crisis affected regions in Cameroon, the case is worst as most of the people are displaced, some not working, others are orphans and single mothers who can barely fend for themselves, adding to it the coming of the pandemic (Covid-19) which has put a lot of things to a halt, causing many families to lose their jobs and source of regular income. This has rendered many adolescent girls in these regions helpless as they are confronted with two giant challenges (conflict and Covid-19) that seem to have changed the normal balance of their lives. With such great burden on their shoulders, providing sanitary pads which are expensive and inaccessible to the young girls attending school is difficult, maintaining the general hygiene is also a challenge which is why there is a need to educate them on how to produce reusable sanitary pads and how to live a healthy/hygienic lifestyle that will also help prevent them from contacting diseases that will make living unbearable for them. There is also a need to share Covid-19 materials and sensitization to these girls for proper hygiene and clean up to help curb the spread of the virus amongst themselves. 80% of young girls in these crisis affected areas have little or no knowledge about menstruation and menstrual hygiene, most parents find it hard discussing the natural occurrence with the young girls for fear of spoiling the girl child as they say and those without parents/guardian to teach them end up confused. Menstruation is seen as a taboo to discuss within the people including the young girls in Africa most especially in Cameroon, it is seen as an embarrassment, a shameful thing which should be cast aside. It causes exclusion from school, family life and social activities as some of the girls feel ashamed and unclean to even participate in class most especially when they lack sanitary pads to hold in the period flow. This is the case in most areas affected by the crisis. Since the ending of 2016, schools have not been effective with most of the girls living in bushes using leaves to pad themselves, in the long run may cause health challenges if not properly handled. With the resumption of some schools, they need to be educated on menstrual hygiene and sanitation so as to help them know more about menstruation and reduce their fear of embarrassment and shame. More than 30 million girls and women are currently displaced due to conflict and disasters across the world. A research done by Plan International shows that 1 in 2 girls experience issues around the affordability of sanitary products and also, 1 in 2 girls did not find school helpful for providing information on menstruation reason why we need to teach teachers on the sensitive nature of menstruation to these young girls. Plan international also concluded from its research that 6 in 10 girls are embarrassed to talk about their periods with family or their teacher. With adequate knowledge and a period-shame free society, we can improve attendance and performance at school, break down taboos and misconceptions around menstruation, raise the self esteem of girls and enable girls to fully participate in all aspects of society.
