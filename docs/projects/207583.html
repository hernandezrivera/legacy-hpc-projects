Project Summary

The BRAC WASH project for 2024 is designed to align with the WASH sector objectives, aiming to provide adequate and equitable access to inclusive and culturally acceptable WASH facilities. This is achieved through regular repair and maintenance of the facilities and the improvement of WASH behaviour practices among the targeted people. Following the WASH sector mega rationalization process, BRAC's geographical coverage includes 8 camps (1E, 2W, 3, 4 extensions, 5, 10, 13, and 20 Ext) as well as the host community.
The BRAC project design includes comprehensive WASH services in camps with an emphasis on the operation and maintenance of the WASH facilities, Solid Waste Management (SWM), faecal sludge management, new construction and upgradation of latrines, bathing cubicles, and tap stands to address the needs of age segregated diverse groups including men, women, persons with disabilities, , children and others. BRAC will ensure community engagement in repairing and maintaining the WASH facilities through capacity building of the user groups and WASH committees while providing necessary repair and maintenance materials to the community. Additionally, women's leadership will be promoted in each WASH user group.
In the host communities, BRAC will install water points, latrines, and bathing cubicles, as well as address menstrual hygiene management in schools and solid waste management. Additionally, BRAC will work on new initiatives, such as the installation of rainwater harvesting systems and wastewater recycling. Water quality will be monitored in both host communities and camps.
BRAC will involve local and national NGOs in different camps where the WASH gaps are significant, aiming to develop the capacity of the local NGOs in terms of WASH humanitarian response under the pooled fund. Furthermore, BRAC will exclusively work on plastic waste management in camps to mitigate physical and long-term environmental impacts. The overall objective of the project is to contribute to the reduction of plastic waste pollution in camps by developing a comprehensive and human-centric waste management system that involves all relevant stakeholders in the waste management value chain. BRAC will also work through partner NGOs for the improvement of the solid waste situation in camps 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, and camp 3.
The BRAC WASH project design also includes integration with different sectors within BRAC, especially Health and Nutrition, Livelihoods and Skills Development, Education, and Protection, to deliver integrated services in an efficient and cost-effective manner. BRAC will address the solid waste problem at the marketplace and adjacent host community with the involvement of CBOs. To manage non-valuable waste and dispose of it environmentally, BRAC will take the initiative of joint advocacy along with the WASH sector and fundraising to construct a new sanitary landfill and will be continuing the regular operation of existing sanitary landfill sites. BRAC will make a contingency plan 2024 to address outbreaks of Acute Watery Diarrhoea (AWD), Scabies, Dengue, and other hazardsincluding heavy rain, cyclones, etc. in host and camps.
In terms of budget allocation for 2024, BRAC plans to allocate 24% in host communities and 76% in camps. BRAC has project implementation quality (PIQ) indicators based on which a project monitoring plan will be developed in alignment with the Joint Response Plan (JRP) monitoring framework and will be periodically monitored by the BRAC MEAL team for improvement and necessary adjustments to the WASH program. During the implementation of the project, BRAC will ensure proper coordination and collaboration with different stakeholders, such as RRRC, CiC, DPHE, UNO offices, UN agencies, NGOs, and the WASH sector. BRAC will follow WASH sector strategies and guidelines in all stages of the implementation, avoiding any overlapping while considering Core Humanitarian Standards and Accountability to Affected Population principles.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In the 7th year of the Rohingya response, the WASH sector has yet to meet the necessary standards for sustaining the quality of life in a protracted refugee situation. The increased cost of WASH management, due to the high maintenance frequency of semi-permanent WASH infrastructure, combined with the ongoing global crisis and donor fatigue, has resulted in a significant decrease in funding, while rising inflation also exacerbated the situation. In the third round of the WASH overview and monitoring, elevated user-to-latrine ratios were identified in several camps. This has placed a burden on existing facilities, leading to increased repairs, maintenance, and desludging, and has led to heightened volunteer engagement. The increased quantity of sludge has also impacted the operation and maintenance cost of FSTP. Furthermore, 5% of host community households, still lack access to improved latrine facilities, posing a threat to public health and lowering the community's living standards, as indicated by J-MSNA. This assessment also found an additional 12% of host community households do not have their own latrines and are forced to share communal facilities with neighbouring families. Therefore, there is an urgent need for new latrine facilities in camps and host communities. Another alarming issue in the camp context is the lack of safe access to latrines for female beneficiaries at night. Ensuring gender-inclusive safe access for female beneficiaries, along with regular sanitation activities, including repair, upgrade, and need-based decommission of WASH facilities, will be a priority in 2024. One of the crucial areas of focus will be ensuring reliable access to water to alleviate water anxiety. J-MSNA identified that 38% of refugees and 9% of host community households frequently worry about having enough water for drinking and other domestic purposes. Additionally, 13% of host community households still rely on shallow tube wells as their primary water source. The third round of the overview and monitoring survey found that, on average, 10% of households have faecal contamination in their drinking water, with a high contamination rate of 67%, posing a risk of AWD outbreaks. Therefore, in 2024, the top priority will be to provide both communities with access to sufficient quality and quantity of water through installation, upgradation, operation, and maintenance of Water infrastructures. The overcrowded living conditions, lack of space for waste management facilities, and a high volume of plastic waste, combined with household-level waste management practices, make waste management a significant challenge. A substantial amount of plastic waste ends up in drains, causing blockages, restricting water flow, and contributing to vector breeding and inundation of WASH facilities during the monsoon. Addressing the increased operation and maintenance costs of SWM facilities and the need for more solid waste collection volunteers to transition from communal collection to household waste collection will be a focus in 2024. Combining the hardware component with appropriate WASH behaviour and practices is crucial for ensuring the optimal utilisation of WASH facilities. There is ample room for improvement in hygiene practices in both the camp and host community settings. Therefore, regular hygiene promotion interventions, day celebrations, cleaning campaigns, and the supply of hygiene and menstrual items should continue as needed. In past years, the presence of local NGOs in the WASH sector has been minimal, with limited funding directed through them. In 2024, capacity strengthening of local NGOs in the WASH sector should be a priority to capacitate them to raise funds directly from donors. Given the vulnerability of the camps to both man-made and natural disasters, contingency plans should also be established to respond immediately after the disaster.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Following the WASH sector mega rationalization process, BRAC has refined its geographical coverage for the JRP 2024. This revised coverage includes eight camps: Camps 1E, 2W, 3, 4 Ext, 5, 10, 13, and 20 Ext, as well as the host community. The project is now strategically concentrated within these specific geographical locations.BRAC will take a proactive approach to ensure coordination and collaboration with various stakeholders. It involves close partnerships with RRRC, CiC, DPHE, respective UN agencies, and AFAs. The primary objective of this collaboration is to prevent potential duplication of efforts during the project's implementation phase. BRAC's project design places a strong emphasis on integration with different sectors. These sectors include Health, Education, Livelihoods and Skills Development. The aim is to deliver comprehensive services while also maintaining cost-effectiveness in the program design.In addressing the urgent needs of the Rohingya communities, BRAC is prioritising multi-year funding. This approach will allow sustained and effective support over an extended period.Additionally, BRAC is committed to enhancing the capacity of local NGOs in the field of WASH. This capacity-building effort is part of the pooled fund initiative, ensuring that these NGOs are well-equipped to respond to the crisis alongside larger implementing agencies.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

BRAC will ensure protection mainstreaming by ensuring the participation and representation of marginalised groups, alleviating discrimination based on gender, disability, sexual orientation, and other contributing factors while ensuring dignity. Protection, safety, and privacy considerations will be integrated into WASH services to ensure safe access to the WASH facility for all beneficiaries and to refrain from physical and psychological harm while accessing these facilities. Messages targeting menstrual stigmas will be disseminated among the community people to eliminate the stigmas regarding menstrual practices in the community.

3.3 - Age & Disability

BRAC will focus on conducting sensitisation activities to reduce stigma and discrimination related to age and disability, promoting inclusivity within the community. BRAC will train WASH staff, caregivers, and community members on how to assist people with disabilities and elderly people in using WASH facilities in safe and comfortable manner. Physical barriers will be addressed through modifications of infrastructure, such as adding ramps or tactile tiles in latrines, low-height facilities and taps for individuals with mobility challenges etc. Social stigma will be addressed through community engagement, advocacy and training.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

BRAC will ensure systematic and meaningful engagement of the affected population in designing WASH services to ensure sustainable wash service. Projects will ensure equal participation and opportunities for people of all genders in the decision-making processes. A robust feedback mechanism will be implemented to address feedback and incorporate changes suggested by the community in the service strategy. Program participants will be communicated clearly on the goals and outcome of the project, and what services they will receive throughout the project.

3.5 - Is your agency/organization and are your IPs members of the PSEA Network?

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Being a member of the PSEA Network, BRAC will adhere to the policy and procedures and respond accordingly. BRAC has a zero-tolerance stand against activities violating safeguarding and PSEA standards. BRAC will ensure regular and mandatory training on PSEA and Safeguarding emphasizing the importance of upholding these values and principles in program planning and implementation. All WASH staff and volunteers who will be in close contact with the program participants will be oriented and sensitised on PSEA through BRAC Safeguarding Policy, Sexual Harassment Elimination Policy; Child and Adolescent Protection Policy; and Prevention Against Workplace Bullying and Violence Policy, Whistleblowing Policy and BRAC Code of Conduct . These initiatives will ensure the safety of all program participants, especially children, adolescent boys and girl, women and elderly, etc., in all wash interventions. In case of any safeguarding incidents, the victim will be promptly referred to the safeguarding focal and/or protection unit (at camp level), ensuring confidentiality and dignity. BRAC Safeguarding and PSEA mechanisms include not only the program participants but also staff, volunteers, vendors, partners and stakeholders. BRAC has an established Complaint Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) within the organisation, and a strong culture of prevention of SEA, support and redressal of these issues. This WASH program will ensure that each WASH facility will have an accessible CFRM mechanism, such as a complaint box, PSEA focal information, etc, to all the target participants, irrespective of their gender, age, ethnicity and culture, without facing barriers and disclosure of confidentiality.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

In 2024, BRAC will be focusing hugely on comprehensive plastic waste management strategies. Recycling, reducing plastic usage, promoting reusable alternatives, and raising awareness, of communities can significantly contribute to creating a safer and healthier environment for current and future generations. Additionally, BRAC will create a contingency stock of WASH NFI and portable WASH facilities to provide emergency support in case of a natural and man made disasters.
