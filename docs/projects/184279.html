Project Summary

According to the JMCNA 2021, shelter is one of the top three priority needs of more than half of the entire population. An estimated 3.6 million people in Somalia need shelter and NFI assistance mainly due to poor shelter conditions, overcrowding and lack of space, poverty, and lack of security of tenure. Those in need of shelter and NFI assistance include displaced and non-displaced populations. The scale of Shelter and NFI needs in 2022 is expected to increase in comparison to the needs in 2021. Women and girls living in sub-standard and overcrowded shelters are particularly vulnerable and tend to feel unsafe in makeshift shelters which do not offer privacy and sufficient protection from weather and increases the risk of gender-based violence.
According to the UNHCR-led Protection and Return Monitoring Network (PRMN), more than 7,000 people have recently been forcibly displaced from Laas Caanood and surrounding areas in Sool region, Since October 2, more than 3,300 of those affected have arrived in Gaalkacyo, of whom 986 have proceeded to South West State, Hirshabelle and Banadir. Thousands of people have also been displaced by the recent government offensives against Al Shabaab in Wisil, Amara and Bacadweyne areas as well as the recent Government forces and Ahlu Suna fights in Bohol village and Guriceel districts. The fighting has been destroyed some in fractures such as hospitals, schools and homes. Authorities have appealed for urgent humanitarian assistance, particularly food, Water, Shelter and Health interventions. CoDHNet and other partners presence in the areas have done a joint assessment to determine the exact numbers of affected people, needs and response.
CoDHNet plans to undertake the provision of NFI kits and ESKs to 3800HHs comparing 22,800 people (2,394Women 11,970girls, 7,030boys and 1,406men) including 2,280 people with disability to affected conflict and droughts-displaced & non-displaced persons Galmudug, South West States and Benadir Region of Somalia. The organization will target IDPs and host communities. Newly displaced, evictees, IDPs affected by recent conflicts and offensives and IDPs living in sites without long term security of tenure and target areas with High Severity and People In Need (PiN) will be prioritized. A market assessment will be conducted prior to the purchase of the items to ascertain their market prices. The beneficiary selection criteria will consider newly displaced persons, female headed households, child-headed households, the elderly in the community and persons with disabilities to ensure the most vulnerable groups are targeted.
To ensure sustainability, the ESKs will be established on lands with relatively long land agreements (three years and above). This will be ascertained through solicitation of written agreements from IDP site leaders and the subsequent confirmation of their validity from respective local authorities. ESK construction will be keen on COVID-19 and basic site planning aspects such as distance between ESKs, roads, fire breaks, drainage and distance from toilets. To improve accountability CoDHNet will monitor effectiveness of delivered assistance. Programe design will be consider the specific needs of women and, where appropriate, women’s dignity kits are also distributed. CoDHNet will use hotline numbers for complaint and feedback mechanisms for Shelter and NFI projects implemented and conduct to analyze PDM and use the findings to improve the quality of future projects and share lessons learned. PDM and Post construction monitoring (PCM) will be conducted after every distribution and construction, this will provide beneficiary feedback on the effectiveness of the activities. 70% of the beneficiaries will take part of the exercise to ensure proper representation and that exact feelings are recorded about the processes and quality of distribution. This will ensure that community participation and AAP is at the center of the project.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

According to flexibility, control, empowering and minimizing the risks; Use of cash or voucher has shown greater impact compared to in kind approach, mainly because it gives flexibility to the beneficiaries to allocate and design the limited resources available to their needs appropriately, it also contributes sustainability and ownership. For the beneficiaries it is easy for them to control what goods and services their households need. However quite substantial considerations have to be looked in to including market access to inform which approach to be used and where. In this application, CoDHNet foresees applicability of cash in some areas particularly the big towns, hence where possible CoDHNet will adapt cash approach in order to ease access for the households. In addition to that, CoDHNet has recently implemented similar cash approaches where the proposed items (ESKs and NFIs) were locally available as well community’s preference shown cash or in kind. There is evidence that receiving cash can at least temporarily empower women within the household. Also, the cash transfers modality reduces the negative coping strategies used by project beneficiaries. To minimize risks associated with Cash approach, CoDHNet has in placed mitigation plans including splitting the transfers and forming respective committees to monitor the implementations. However, market assessment will be carried out to inform like this decision.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

CoDHNet will conduct market assessment to determine availability of standard ESKs within the local market and/or nearby markets. When goods are available and accessible locally, cash or voucher-based interventions have the potential to be more rapid than bringing supply from out of country. CoDHNet procurement sector will check the availability of Shelter and NFIs materials in the local market, available local materials will be prioritized. This will help CoDHNet to design the intervention in terms of whether in-kind distribution will be undertaken or cash/voucher system will be used.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance: Mitigation measures: (1) rotate distribution teams so no one team constantly visits the same settlements; (2) provide clear information to distribution actors and affected populations explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services / favours to anyone in exchange for receiving SNFI assistance, ensuring that this information is communicated to women and girls. Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure and/or contamination by explosive hazards in targeted project area: Mitigation measures: (1) Consider the land tenure arrangements of IDPs, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land and inheritance rights. Advocate with land and property owners and local authorities to avoid eviction or disproportional rising in rent; (2) obtain permission before using or building on any land or property, in writing where possible; (3) avoid community tensions by providing the local/host community with access to camp services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of non-food items; (4) consult both displaced and host communities about shelter and NFI needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment, or conflict between the two communities; (5) keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use – apply effective data protection measures.
