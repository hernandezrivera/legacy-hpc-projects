Project Summary

In 2024, PIB will focus more on fostering safe and equitable access to quality, gender transformative and inclusive learning opportunities for a total of 17,646 learners of 3 to 18 years aged Rohingya refugees and host community children, adolescents and youth following 50% ratio for girls. PIB will reach out to 2,996 learners of aged 3 to 4 years, 1,150 learners of aged 5-14 years along with 177 learners for SEL in host communities and 13,323 leaners of aged 15-18 years through different education interventions implemented by local implementing partners. PIB will continue with its community-based learning facilities (CBLF) modality and establish and refurbish new integrated ECD, Myanmar Curriculum (KG, primary and Secondary), Adolescent and Youth learning facilities. This project aims to reach most vulnerable and dropout adolescent girls along with disability through organizing gender segregated classroom, recruiting female facilitator for adolescent girls’ learner and female Monitor to support adolescent girl's trip to and from the Community Based Learning facilities and ensure their regular attendance in learning facilities. A total of 26,469 Parents and community people and other stakeholders will also be engaged and their capacity will be strengthened to promote girl’s education, empowering adolescent girls, GVB risk mitigation and prevention of bullying and harassment.
PIB will build the capacities of ECD, MC (KG, primary and secondary), FLN, host primary and secondary school teachers/facilitators and sector education partners on gender responsive pedagogy, inclusive education, theme-based training, crisis sensitive education and planning to become better teachers and improve learning outcomes of refugee and host communities’ learners. PIB will also scale up the PSCD (Pre-Service Capacity Development) course package to build the capacities of Rohingya women to become Myanmar Curriculum teachers and create a pool of potential female facilitators. PIB also focus to strengthen coaching and mentoring approach through capacity building of Rohingya Resource Instructor/master trainer, education administer/supervisor to provide hands on support to the teachers in a real classroom setting. To ensure students learning progress is assessed and tracked, PIB will conduct regular learning assessment and Introduce and strengthen formative assessment system and capacity building of teacher, Rohingya Resources Instructor and teacher's administrator and track learners progress.
In this JRP 2024, PIB will continue to support the community engagement and capacity strengthening of Education Sector partners and affected communities to enhance their participation in the education of children, adolescents and youth. PIB will train CESG (community Education Support Group) on DRR to jointly design emergency preparedness and response plans to equip LF and communities with required skills to respond to potential emergencies. Furthermore, PIB education partners will be benefiting from capacity building on gender responsive pedagogy and gender mainstreaming, SEL, inclusive education and crisis sensitive education planning and management etc. PIB education partners will also be benefitted on planning and implementation of education project throughout the project period. To undertake community consultations, advocacy initiatives, dialogue and strengthen their capacity to promote social cohesion and co-existence, this project will work with both host community and camp through supporting key community leaders, including members of education support groups.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Through this proposal, PIB aims to discourse the main barriers and needs of Rohingya refugees and host community children, especially girls and children with disability, to access gender transformative quality inclusive quality education.According to the J-MSNA- 2023, 46% Rohingya and 78% of host community early education aged children aged from 3-5 years not attending early education program during the 2022-2023 school year. Besides, education enrollment in particular among adolescents (especially girl) remained low (only 20%)in both the camps and host communities. It’s been found that adolescent girls aged of 12 to 18 are more likely than boys to be missing schools. J-MSNA 2023 data shows that the reason behind missing schools by girls is due to education not being considered as priority, household chores, protection risks, cultural norms etc. whereas boys are missing school due to attending Madrasha. Limited data exists on the number of Rohingya adolescents are currently engaged with education opportunities including pre-vocational program especially life skill. However, the limited range of educational opportunities exposes them to exploitation, increased exposure to protection risks, and can lead to negative coping mechanisms. For the holistic development of children, ECD interventions need multi-sectoral support, including early learning, responsive caring, health, nutrition, education, and protection, which is not available in both camp and host community settings. In cox’s bazar response, there is a need of developing appropriate guidelines for supporting and enabling young children’s holistic development in camp and host communities following the context of Bangladesh and customizing Early Learning competency framework of education sector, Cox’s Bazar. Also, there is no such referral mechanism or established linkage with the sectors of health, nutrition, WASH, education and child protection to ensure screening and services for young children including children with disabilities.In the Camps there has been some success at recruiting a high percentage of female teachers, with 56% of teachers being female. However, the majority of female teachers are from the host community and are Bangladeshi, with female Rohingya teachers only comprising 25% of the total. This presents a challenge in the roll out of the Myanmar curriculum, with greater numbers of female teachers with sufficient Myanmar language skills needing to be recruited and trained. Besides, children with disabilities face significant challenges who attend learning centers at much lower rates than their peers across all age groups for both girls and boys. This is contributed to by insufficient skills and training of the educators, and poor awareness, negative attitudes and stigma related to children with disabilities attending the same learning centers as children without disabilities. A lack of inclusive learning and teaching materials, including teaching materials suitable for children with learning difficulties, is an additional barrier.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Following the rationalization framework developed by education sector, PIB scaled down the number of camps that it is operating in to strategically reduce the footprint and aims to cover this project in camps 1E, 1W, 3, 6 & 7 in Ukhiya and 21, 22 & 24 in Teknaf to implement project activities where PIB has the maximum camp coverage and education intervention along with great reputation of work among both CiC and community level stakeholders. PIB will ensure the implementing partners consolidate their services in specific blocks and camps following the integrated approach. This project targets 3-18 years learners to implement Education intervention like Integrated ECD, Myanmar curriculum, Adolescent programming focusing on Functional Literacy, Numeracy and Life skills including Capacity building of relevant project staff, teachers’ professional development, Community engagement, youth led activities etc. Furthermore, all the implementing partners participate in camp-level coordination platforms and also maintain a good relation with Government officials.The project is designed for multi years with intention to secure donor fund for multi-year program and to serve in the same Sector in the same camps recognizing that it takes time to build relations with the government agencies, the refugees, and other implementing partners in the camp. Project will also carry out all the education activities through quality implementation that will be finalized by education sector through rationalization process.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project will integrate protection principles into all education interventions and ensure the interventions and activities will be targeting the most vulnerable individuals, enhance safety, dignity, and promote and protect the rights of refugees, encouraging inclusiveness without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect, and exploitation. It will be followed at every stage of project cycle. Following the FOUR PRINCIPLES in all of its activities, the project will strongly Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended adverse effects of its intervention, which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Access to assistance and services will be ensured and paid special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services, e.g Persons with disability, elderly, children, etc. Throughout the project, child Protection will be mainstreamed by training and sensitizing the project and partner staff in line with CP minimum standards. To protect girls and boys, as well as parents and caregivers, the programme will link to the Plan International’s established GBV and CP services in all target locations. Community-based CP and GBV mechanisms to monitor, identify and address CP and GBV concerns will be supported and strengthened and community/ parents support group will help monitor, report and refer identified protection cases within education interventions. Safe learning spaces will be provided and facilitators/instructors will be trained in CP principles and ways to identify and refer cases. To equip girls and boys with key knowledge and skills, SEL and life skills interventions will be considered that will help them to mitigate, prevent and respond to GBV.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project will ensure various initiative to create safe, disability inclusive, accessible and child friendly learning facilities for children and adolescent (aged 3-18 years) including girls and Children with Disability; This project also aims to ensure accessibility of learning environments and facilities are equipped to meet the needs of children with disabilities and that assistive devices are provided to decrease physical barriers; Community based approaches minimize access barriers, especially for the most vulnerable children; and, blended/multi-modal learning modalities are implemented to provide layers of support for vulnerable children, critical for reinforcement and learning engagement, specifically for children with disability, where layering can ensure audio, visual, and traditional learning approaches are applied. In addition, training on disability inclusive pedagogy for facilitators and teachers in host community and camps will be conducted, to ensure they have the pedagogical tools required to differentiate instruction through understanding different learning capacities to improve classroom management techniques to engage all learners, will be critical to improving inclusion. Furthermore, the programme will ensure all the teaching materials, centres and services are disability inclusive. There will be a strong capacity building plan to build the capacity of programme staff, teachers, administrators and volunteers on disability inclusion issues. Additionally, disability inclusive community monitoring system will be established. In order to gather comprehensive data, sex-age-disability disaggregated data will be collected and analyzed. Based on the findings, necessary initiatives will be taken to ensure attendance of Children with Disability in learning facilities in regular basis and improve their learning outcome.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

PIB will ensure accountability towards the affected beneficiaries and targeted communities through this project and will assure that their perspectives are systematically listened to and their feedback are used to make critical programmatic decisions in addition to communicating and explaining how their feedback is being used to inform actions. Specifically, Plan’s child-friendly accountability and feedback mechanism will be rolled out and through this mechanism, feedback will be collected and responded accordingly through different channels, including mobile phone calls, SMS, community consultation, and Post Distribution Monitoring. There will be dedicated persons with dedicated phone numbers to receive phone calls and SMS from the community. Bi-monthly community consultations will be organized at the community level to listen to the children, young people, and other community members. The targeted communities will be informed about the feedback channels beforehand so that they know how to share their feedback. The accountability focal in each location will be trained on CFRM and will proactively engage with project beneficiaries for suggestions and feedback on the project interventions. Overall, all the relevant issues like participation of relevant stakeholders, access to information, capacity building and compliance, the project team will ensure appropriate practice among the team members.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Plan has a policy of zero tolerance of behavior that is discriminatory, harassing or bullying, including, but not limited to, sexual harassment, sexual exploitation and abuse, or any other behaviors which may jeopardize safety and wellbeing of all Plan’s workplace participants, children, young people and beneficiaries. Plan promotes child and youth safe practices, approaches, interventions and environments which respects, recognizes, and responds to the specific safeguarding needs and addresses the protection risks of the differing gender and other identifies. Furthermore, Plan promotes the active involvement of children and young people in their own protection. This project aims to take a comprehensive approach to address Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming by embedding principles of safety and accountability throughout its program. The project will actively engage diverse stakeholders and promotes inclusivity in its approach, focusing on the community and resilience of adolescent girls and young women. Providing awareness session on GBV preventive and responsive services and creating a protective environment where girls are better equipped to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse. In addition, Risk management plans adequately address all issues raised in the risk assessment, and include contact details of safeguarding and PSEA focal points and other key personnel managing the activities. In summary, this project mainstreams Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) by fostering a culture of safety and accountability within the communities it serves and empowering local communities to advocate for improved educational services. By doing so, it contributes to a protective environment that safeguards the rights and well-being of adolescent girls and young women in Cox's Bazar, aligning with its core mission to promote gender equality, human dignity, and address sexual gender-based violence.
