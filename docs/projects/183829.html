Project Summary

This project aims to provide humanitarian assistance to refugees and hosting communities under all three Strategic Objectives of the Humanitarian Response Plan 2022: Objective 1: Provide timely multi-sectoral life-saving assistance to crisis affected people to reduce mortality and morbidity. Objective 2: Contribute to building resilience to recurrent shocks and improving vulnerable people’s access to basic services. Objective 3: Enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks, and respond to protection needs through quality and principled humanitarian action.
IOM will provide multi-sectorial interventions including Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH), Emergency Shelters (ES)/ and Non Food Items (NFIs), Durable Shelters, and Health based activities to respond to the urgent needs of refugees and hosting communities. IOM will focus on providing the multi-sectoral assistance in several states including South Darfur, North Darfur, Central Darfur, East Darfur, West Kordofan, Khartoum, Blue Nile, Kassala and Gedaref. Numerous instances of displacement and increases in the number of refugees influxes have been reported in 2020 and 2021 or are expected in 2022 in these states. The proposed WASH activities will increase access to safe drinking water, adequate quality sanitation facilities and improve personal and public hygiene practices. The proposed ESNFI and durable shelter interventions will be used to improve living conditions for vulnerable refugees and host communities that receive high rates of displacement and offer protection to vulnerable people from the harsh elements. The proposed health activities will focus on delivering a basic package of primary health services (BPPHS) to improve access to services and reduce risks associated with morbidity and mortality and support more favorable protection outcomes. During these intervention IOM will follow national guidelines on COVID-19 preventive measures, such as physical distancing, use of personal protective equipment (PPE) and avoid large gatherings. IOM's project will focus on targeting both refugees and hosting communities in an effort to mitigate the risks of tensions over scarce resources, increase the capacity of hosting communities to absorb population influxes. However, lifesaving assistance will be prioritized for new arrivals and vulnerable groups including women, children, the elderly and people with disabilities or other specific needs to ensure that the most urgent humanitarian and protection needs are addressed.

1 - Needs

Sudan hosts one of the largest population in Africa with over 1,111,329 refugees and asylum seekers from South Sudan, Eritrea, the Central African Republic, Ethiopia, Chad, Syria and Sudan. Since the outbreak of the conflict in South Sudan in December 2013; a total of 786,524 South Sudanese refugees have fled to Sudan as of October 2020. South Sudanese refugees have crossed the border at different sites into Abyei, South and West Kordofan, White Nile and East and South Darfur due to continued instability in South Sudan. While there is a major humanitarian presence in these states, significant gaps remain in EastDarfur, South Darfur and West Kordofan, where humanitarian interventions have been more limited. At the same time, localities like Um Dafoug in South Darfur, have witnessed the influx of over 27,000 refugees from Central African Republic due to the clash between Sara and Kara tribal groups that broke out in the Central African Republic in June 2019, and access to basic services and shelter is very limited. Multi-sector needs of the population are high both in and out of camps. Many refugees are living among host-communities that already have very limited access to basic services; for example, in Keliak, West Kordofan, approximately 4 Liters of water are available per person per day (l/p/d), which is below the Sphere standard of 15 l/p/d and the influx of South Sudanese continues to create tensions between the community and the refugees. A lack of proper water management, sanitation facilities and poor hygienepractices have also been observed among these communities’ waste management is limited, and open defecation is widely practiced. Therefore, there is a great need to support these communities with construction, rehabilitation and maintenance of water structures and safe sanitation facilities; as well as facilitating hygiene awareness campaigns and waste management campaigns. The health and nutritional status of refugees entering Sudan is also dire as they were already facing serve health issues and acute malnutrition before they arrived in the country. As of November 2021, and as per the figures released by UNHCR and the Commission of Refugees (COR), 64,180 Ethiopian refugees (57 per cent men, 43 per cent women) have fled the Tigray region and crossed into Sudan since the crisis began on 9 November 2020 and 11,254 into Blue Nile. Influxes of refugees continue arriving through the primary border points, including Hamdayet in Kassala state, in Algazira, Yagolo, and Yabisher in Blue Nile state, and in Lukdi and Abderafi in Gedaref state, which also act as a transit point for transportation and relocation to camp sites and settlements. As tensions continue in the Tigray and other Ethiopian regions, it is expected that the number of refugees crossing into Sudan will increase. Needs remain vast and there is an urgent need for interventions across all sectors, especially WASH, health, shelter and protection.

2 - Activities/Outputs

WASH Activities:1. Monitor water depletion levels and construct or rehabilitate solarized water points to improve access to safe water sources and increase quantity from 7.5-15 l/p/d (Sphere standards). 2. Assess area needs and provide gender-segregated and sensitive sanitation facilities (including household and communal latrines, shower blocks, and bathing facilities) constructed in well lit areas with ramps and railings to provide access for people with disabilities, and with locks to ensure privacy and reduce personal security risks. 3. Construction of hand washing facilities in communal set ups especially near communal toilets in camps, schools, markets, public places and health facilities, where hand washing takes place. 4. Ensure the Operation and Management (O&M) of WASH facilities including the provision of tools and training of community committees and mechanics on water quality treatment and monitoring for water sources and desludging for sanitation facilities.5. Distribute context appropriate Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits, jerrycans and soap to vulnerable households (HH) requiring essential NFIs. 6. Conduct hygiene and cleaning campaigns including the mainstreaming of COVID-19 mitigation measures to encourage the community to develop waste management protocols that prevent environmental pollution and promote the use of better hygiene practices. ES/NFI Activities:1. Based on initial DTM assessment conduct ES/NFI specific vulnerability assessments to identify and select vulnerable refugee and host community HHs in need of immediate shelter and NFI assistance. 2. Identify suppliers and procure materials for emergency and durable shelters (plastic sheets, poles, ropes grass mats, tukuls, bricks, etc.) and NFI kits (containing - plastic sheets, sleeping mats, jerrycans, blankets, kitchen sets and mosquito nets). 3. Purchase and distribute NFIs and environmentally friendly materials for emergency and durable shelters from the local market based on the needs and shelter designs. 4. Provide training to beneficiary HHs on proper usage of the NFIs and construction techniques and including fire and flood mitigation measure, as well as assist in the assembly/construction of shelters, to ensure durability and sustainability. 5. Carry out post distribution monitoring (PDM) missions for the selected vulnerable HHs that received the assistance.Health Activities: 1. Conduct needs assessment in coordination with MoH and the sector lead to ensure that IOM and health partners do not duplicate services and focus on emergency response and recovery plans for refugees and host communities in the target areas.2. Increase access to health services by rehabilitating and refurbishing health facilities with the provision of medical equipment.3. Provide outpatient consultation and treatment for communicable diseases and non-communicable diseases and support BPHHS including conducting occupational visits to provide essential primary and secondary health care services, especially supporting maternal health care including antenatal and postnatal care sessions and ensuring that skilled attendants are present at deliveries. 4. Support the referral of specialized cases for further treatment. 5. Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services through training of staff, midwives, and community health workers. COVID-19 mitigation measures will be streamlined across all sectors including: Utilizing infection prevention and control measures protocols for WASH and Health activities. Utilizing PPE and physical distancing and other guidelines for activities where staff and beneficiaries or groups of people are closely interacting such as distributions, trainings, hygiene and cleaning campaigns. Providing C-19 mitigation, health, and hygiene, related messaging to direct beneficiaries and wider community during the hygiene/cleaning campaigns.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project will have a neutral impact on the environment with mitigation and enhancement measures put in place - IOM will employ environmentally friendly solutions for the implementation of the activities, for example the use of solar panels for the rehabilitation or construction of new water sources. New water sources will only be constructed after a geophysical study is conducted and ground water levels are monitored. IOM will also work closely with the WASH Sector and the targeted communities to ensure that Integrated Water Resource Management Systems (IWRM) are established. Hygiene and garbage collection campaigns will also be designed in a way to encourage land preservation through proper/ responsible garbage disposal methods and systems. Furthermore, climate change, agricultural development and population growth have made water resources extremely important to Sudan’seconomic development and social cohesion; IOM will focus on finding alternatives from fuel powered water pumps to systems which are affordable, scalable, environmentally sustainable and climate smart. Experience has shown that solar powered systems reduce running costs by two thirds unlike motorized pumps, which are also noisy, polluting (emitting greenhouse gases which contribute to climate change) and are highly susceptible to fluctuating fuel prices and availability. Solar powered water sources will be prioritized where possible. Primary health facilities will have proper medical waste management according to WHO and MOH standards. IOM will also provide health programs that raise awareness among refugees and host community about environmental health risks and causes and mitigation measures for diseases like, malaria, cholera, and COVID-19. The awareness campaigns will focus on issues like preventing the spread of infectious disease through vector control, proper wastedisposal and improving personal hygiene practices.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project activities will be designed in a way to support women, children and people with disabilities. Special attention will be paid to women, lactating mothers or pregnant women most vulnerable to illness and severely lacking in access to basic services and support needed. For WASH activities, consultative participatory approaches will be employed to allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identification of priority needs based on gender and vulnerability, especially for the distribution of the Menstrual Hygiene Management kits. IOM will undertake participatory assessments/exercise to identify gender and age specific needs and context specific risks of different groups, especially women and girls, related to access to and safety around service provision. This inclusive participatory approach assures the inclusion of ideas from vulnerable women and girls, and people with disabilities, who are often excluded from community decisions or under represented during decision making processes, especially with regard to selecting the location for the construction/rehabilitation of latrines, bathing facilities and water points. The facilities themselves will be built with gender sensitivity, accessibility, privacy, and safety in mind considering protection risks are addressed and mitigated and chosen locations in nearby, well-lit areas with ramps and railings, and locks on the doors of the latrines and bathing facilities and for water points to decrease walking distances to reduce associated risks of gender-based violence (GBV) especially for women and children. Locations of shelter structures will also be chosen through community consultations and built in areas where there is access to other basic services and possible land tenure / ownership issues are discussed and resolved between local authorities and among the community members before shelters are constructed. Based on needs assessments, IOM will ensure that most vulnerable HHs with people with specific needs including women, children, persons with disabilities, will be prioritized to receive emergency and durable shelters shelters. During the construction of the shelters the most vulnerable will receive additional support to assemble emergency and durable shelters. Privacy and safety standards will be a top priority for the shelter construction activities especially emphasizing construction techniques and training of community on disaster risk reduction measures that address fire and flood related risk to the shelters.Health services will provide support to the most vulnerable beneficiaries but also wider host community which will reduce community tensions over resources. Pregnant women will prioritized for assistance including antenatal care, skilled deliveries, and postnatal care.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Under this project, consultative participatory approaches employed will allow for the communities to participate directly in the activities, in the decision-making process and in the identification of priority needs based on gender, disability, and vulnerability. The participatory approach assures the inclusion of beneficiaries and the available avenues of feedback and complaint mechanisms, which IOM and its partners can use to address activity design flaws or other issues. For instance, individualized interviews with vulnerable beneficiaries especially women and girls during certain activities like PDM missions opens communication channels for beneficiaries to discuss topics they may not want to discuss in a group setting. IOM is part of the inter-agency coordination groups and will ensure to follow the relevant guidelines on accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms as agreed by the inter-agency groups. As for the prevention of SEA, IOM has zero Tolerance policy towards SEA and has institutional guidelines on PSEA and reporting mechanism in case of suspected SEA as well as guidelines on how to manage GBV related cases during crises. IOM staff will receive refresher training on how to report SEA and ethical and appropriate ways to refer GBV cases through the referral pathways. These guidelines will also be included and highlighted to any implementing partners to ensure compliance.
