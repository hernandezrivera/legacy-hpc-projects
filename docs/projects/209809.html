Project Summary


Twelve years into the conflict, the humanitarian situation in Syria remains alarming as violence and displacement continue to have a devastating impact on the lives of millions of people. Most of the inhabitants in northwest Syria are living in narrow and crowded houses, unable to meet basic needs, in extreme poverty, limited or no labor market opportunities, shortage of food and clean water, lack of accessibility to health care services, and Inadequate finances to meet their medical needs. The earthquake and cholera outbreak heavily impacted northwest Syria, a region where 4.1 million people depend on humanitarian assistance, with the majority of women and children.
According to HNO 2024, 67 health facilities were damaged by the earthquakes, of which six were destroyed. not only damaged health facilities but also fragile medicine equipment and devices. 40 percent of primary and secondary care health facilities are either partially functioning or not functional. A key concern is the limited access to antenatal (ANC) and postnatal (PNC) healthcare. 2.3 million women of reproductive age, including 500,000 PLWs, are losing access to reproductive and maternal health care. More than 218,000 children under one year did not receive routine immunization services due to access constraints, supply chain interruptions, and funding shortages. Moreover, approximately 1 in 10 people in Syria lives with a mild to moderate mental health condition, 1 in 10 children need focused mental health care.
Human Appeal (HA), to respond to the above needs and in line with the Health Sector Strategic Objective 1, proposes to support and operate five healthcare facilities: one maternity and pediatric hospital, three primary healthcare centers, and one mobile clinic in Idleb governorate (Dana and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts) for 12 months, namely:

The above well-equipped facilities during the project period will provide primary (EHSP) healthcare services for people in need within catchment areas as follows:
RH services ( ANC, PNC, family planning, treatment of STI…etc.).
Child health consultations and treatment.
General medicine consultations and treatment.
Nutrition services ( screening and treatment for CU5 and PLWs for malnutrition, IYCF-related counseling and
awareness through CHWs).
MHPSS services.
Basic trauma care.
Lab tests.
Natural Deliveries ( only Iman Hospital).
GBV case management ( only Iman Hospital).
EPI (only Iman Hospital).
In addition to that, Iman Hospital (CEmONC) will provide secondary health care services as follows:
In-patient admissions for RH and pediatric.
C-Sections.
ICU services for newborns.
Infant incubators.
Phototherapy for jaundice in newborn.
blood transfusion.
Uterus curettage for miscarriages.
HA, during the proposed project, will support the health facilities with staff incentives, pharmaceuticals, medical and lab consumables, maintenance costs, electricity, cleaning materials, stationary, and other operational costs, as well are technical supervision.
HA, during the design and implementation phase, was very keen to coordinate and communicate with the partners and coordination bodies, including WHO, IHD, SRH TWG, and local authorities.
The project will provide health services for 254388 beneficiaries including 29972 men, 99424 women, 64892 boys, and 60100 girls.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risk Analysis:Risk 1: Women of reproductive age, the elderly, PwDs, and other vulnerable groups might face challenges accessing health services. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: HA selected the facilities’ locations to be inside the communities and camps areas which enhances access and also provides transportation as needed to elderly people and PwDs besides mobilization of some activities to the communities through outreach team and mobile clinic, training all the staff on humanitarian principles including engaging with vulnerable groups, kept Analyzing and disaggregating all data by sex, age, and disability with analyzing vulnerable group’s needs. Risk 2: GBV survivors may arrive at the health facility but may not directly disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Human Appeal will train the staff on the identification of GBV cases, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, and safe referrals as applicable to their respective role, and will ensure that the affected populations have access to reproductive health services. PSEA strategy:HA has already trained all staff on PSEA. However, HA during the project, will conduct refresher training on PSEA training by Human Appeal ToT trainers for all project health staff. HA, to protect from sexual exploitation, is keen to hire female health staff such as midwives, doctors, and nurses to deal with female patients. Additionally, the HA team will ensure Effective and clear complaint mechanisms are available in the health facilities, feedback/complaints box, and posters with PSEA hotline number. confidential and independent proper investigation and follow-up of each reported case, including referrals to specialized GBV and health services. Risks, Assumptions, and Mitigating Measures:Risk 1: Severe reduction in the funding allocated to Syria by the main donors. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: Approaching new donors. Advocate with other organizations to enhance the fund. Better financial management of resources. Rely on the gift in kind. Rely on HA private fund to fill the gap. Risk 2: Non-renewal of UN cross-border resolution. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation: Rely on AFNS and INGOs to replace OCHA roles and responsibilities. HA has multiple channels of funding in addition to good cross-border access for staff and commodities to Syria through its coordination with local authorities. HA also relies ongifts in kind and better financial management of resources. Risk 3: Deterioration of the security situation puts project staff at risk. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: Continuous monitoring of the security situation through different indicators and preparing evacuation plans in advance. Maintain good relations with local authorities. Respect local culture and build acceptance with host communities. Training the staff on security topics and first aid. Follow the common and safe roads.
