Project Activities

According to education cluster needs analysis 2019 an estimated 1.37M school aged children (including 691,295 girls) need humanitarian assistance of 396,987 are children from displaced families, IDP children are more likely to be at risk of not accessing education with 37% of displaced children in need compared to 25% of non-displaced children. UNHCR’s post-return monitoring indicates that in 61.2% of returnee households, at least one child is not attending school regularly. Several cluster assessments (Education Cluster, Multi-Cluster Assessments and Baseline Survey) have identified specific educational gaps and obstacles which may be contributing to the low enrollment rates. These include lack of adequate learning facilities, lack of qualified teachers and availability of basic teaching and learning materials. The presence of returnee children is only adding pressure to an education system already ill-equipped to respond to needs: 90% of schools do not have access to safe drinking water and 61% of schools do not have functional latrines across Central and Southern Somalia. Other gaps include inadequate learning equipment, laboratories and other furniture.There are limited intermediate/ secondary education opportunities for IDPs and returnees. Tradition, culture and religion make it difficult to reach the grass-root level, and females are constrained from expressing their ideas and needs.ActivitiesIn addressing the key gaps identified in the assessments, UNHCR will assist returning refugees in accessing education through provision of education grants and scholastic materials (books, uniforms) to the parents of each refugee returnee child enrolled in school. UNHCR will distribute these materials bi-annually in form of conditional and restricted cash/vouchers of $20 each, per school going child. UNHCR will also conduct teachers' training and awareness raising for Community Education Committees. In coordination with the Ministry of Education and other actors, UNHCR will support interventions aimed at greater school attendance and retention of students, in particular of girls, including through providing sanitary and hygiene items to girls attending school.Inclusion will be key to UNHCR’s approach. As such, UNHCR will support the Ministry of Education in integrating returnee children into the national public school system to prevent the continuation of NGO-managed parallel systems. Overall, UNHCR will work closely with the Ministry of Education and the Education Cluster, adopting a local area based approach, where the emphasis is placed on supporting existing systems to increase capacities. This will in particular entail:1. Training of teachers, including on INEE, and awareness raising for Community Education Committees (CEC) on safe learning environments – contributing to Outcome 2.1 of the Education – Child Protection Response Framework2. Provision of teacher incentives. UNHCR will engage with the Ministry of Education to provide incentives to teachers in public schools where returnee children are enrolled. 3. Distribution of teaching and learning materials in support of returnee children4. Conduct back to school campaigns and other social mobilization to further retention, in particular of girls – contributing to Outcome 1.1 of the Education – Child Protection Response Framework

Mitigation Measures

Lense 1 Protection of civiliansThe recruitment of children into armed groups is widespread in Somalia, and increasingly well documented. The targeting of children for recruitment is a major source of family separation, both when children are forced to join armed groups, and when parents and caregivers send children away from areas subjected to child recruitment as a safety strategy. As of 1 July 2019, CTFMR figures indicate that a total of 819 children including 8 girls were recruited by armed forces and groups in various areas, compared to 1381 (34 girls) in the same period in 2018. However, the actual number is believed to be much higher. In general, the most common method of recruitment of children between 7 and 17 is persuasion by local leaders, relatives or parents to join armed forces or groups (46%), forceful removal from their homes by armed groups (40%) of verified cases and forceful removal from schools (16%). According to the 2019 Child Protection Assessment in Somalia, children who have joined or have been recruited into armed groups was most regularly cited in Daynile (66.7%), Kahda (61.1%), and Beletweyne (53.8%) and least mentioned in Merca (10%). Pull and push factors leading children to join armed groups. include radicalization, forced recruitment, abduction and parents facilitating their children’s recruitment. 27.3% of respondents stated more boys have been recruited into armed groups than girls (9.5%). Returnees and IDPs are affected in line with the general population by the ongoing insecurity and fragile political environment in Somalia. The insecurity results in restrictions in humanitarian access and restraints for persons of concern to move freely and in safety, further exacerbated by inter-clan conflicts. Threats targeted at beneficiary communities often force humanitarian organizations to postpone the implementation of affected projects until they receive security assurances for their staff and the beneficiary community. Due to practical considerations related to humanitarian access, concrete reintegration support and assistance can only be provided in areas of the country with relative stability and where UNHCR and partners are currently present and/or can establish operations.Lense 2 Preventing exclusionVoluntary returns are on-going despite the conflict, violence, insecurity, absence of functioning and effective institutions, impunity, and lack of infrastructure in Somalia. While there is increased effort to promote sustainable return and re-integration, a substantial number of returnees have limited access to basic services and are constantly exposed to protection risks. Lack of proper legal, psychosocial and physical support also remain a challenge in most parts of the favorable return areas. Many returnees choose not to return to their areas of origin, preferring to settle in urban areas. Many of these already face pressures on basic services, further compounded by recurrent influxes of IDPs. Support to enrollment of returnees and IDPs allows them to integrate in the local community and counters exclusion. The support to the education infrastructure also benefits the local populations, which promotes better social cohesion and counters exclusion. UNHCR applies vulnerability criteria for the selection of beneficiaries of all its activities, which takes into account specific needs that are related to gender and age. Application of the vulnerability criteria ensures an equitable approach, while also addressing specific risks of exclusion. For example, provision of sanitary kits to girl pupils to encourage girl-child education and minimize the number of girls dropping out of schools and absenteeism thus enhancing retention and completion of schooling. Also, UNHCR supports the construction and rehabilitation of gender sensitive latrines and water points/hand washing sinks and sporting facilities in schools.

Please provide further explanation

UNHCR aims to enroll returnee students in the existing public schools with the aim of integration and ensuring continuity of their education after the end of the one year education grant. UNHCR will endeavor to sign an MoU with MoE in FGS and FMS for smooth transition of the returnee children into the public schools. UNHCR also aims to design and implement programmes and to pursue partnerships that feed into wider longer-term solutions objectives, while also ensuring a focus on immediate needs. The education activities planned for 2020 are strongly aimed at sustainability and handover to the authorities. Ministry of Education at the Federal and the State levels are actively involved in the activities as UNHCR aims to mainstream education activities and phase out parallel education structures that were put in place to ensure access to education for beneficiaries. The grants provided to returnees are not impacted by the security situation.
