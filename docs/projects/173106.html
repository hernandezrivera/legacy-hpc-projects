Project Summary

The goal of this project is to enhance the wellbeing and safety of conflict affected population in Libya with improved and more inclusive access to services. The project will target highly vulnerable, crisis-affected internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities living in Tripoli, Misrata and Benghazi districts as well as local and national service providers.
The project will improve physical and functional independence and psychological wellbeing of the most vulnerable, conflict-affected persons with functional limitations and their caregivers thanks to an integrated approach to rehabilitation services, alongside raising awareness on risks posed by Explosive Ordnance (EO) for conflict affected population as well as promoting a more inclusive humanitarian action within coordination bodies, humanitarian community and partners. Finally, it will also seek to enhance the capacities and empower local and national services providers and institutions.

1 - Needs

In the 1st half of 2020, the deterioration of the conflict, causing an increased number of internally displaced people and worsening the already extremely precarious situation of migrants, has resulted in mounting health and protection needs across Libya. In the context of this deteriorating situation, even more worsened since April 2020 with the inexorable onset of Covid-19 pandemic, the overall needs of persons with functional limitations in Libya can be summarized as the following:- Lack of access to services: persons with disabilities, injuries or living with a chronic disease are often not only physically unable to access services, but also regularly report barriers such as fear for safety and financial constraints. In some cases, such as persons having severe psychosocial distress and mental health disorders, there is also fear of stigma.- Lack of availability of services: specialized services for persons with physical and functional limitations and/or psychosocial distress or mental health disorders are increasingly unavailable due to severe shortage of trained staff and long waiting lists, while the cost of private services is in itself prohibitive for vulnerable populations.- Lack of information about available services for persons with disabilities and injuries and persons living with psychological distress or mental health disorders: there is also lack of understanding and knowledge about the specific needs of these population and how they can access appropriate services. Caregivers and family members struggle with the physical, financial and emotional burden of caring for the individual, lacking the time, energy and ability to seek out information about what assistance is available to them or to afford it.Persons with disabilities living in emergency contexts are more likely to have limited or inadequate access to essential basic services whereas this relates to the humanitarian principles of humanity and impartiality as well as the human rights principles of equity and non-discrimination. Furthermore, recent Covid-19 response shows that prevention, hygiene and safety messaging system was not adapted for persons with disabilities, demonstrating a clear gap in mainstreaming disability inclusion and a lack of coherent and comprehensive approaches in the humanitarian response for Libya. With a humanitarian situation deteriorating and recent disruptions of service due to the Covid-19 crisis, continued efforts in addressing the needs of persons with disabilities – with a focus on providing care and improving functional independence in daily living activities - are crucial.In parallel, Explosive Ordnance contamination in the greater Tripoli area has increased considerably since the onset of conflict in April 2019, with a 131% rise of civilian casualties from explosive weapons, making of 2019 the deadliest year since 2011 . Meanwhile most humanitarian mine action operations were suspended in areas affected by the hostilities in the second quarter of 2019. Since March 2020, the humanitarian situation in Tripoli and its surroundings has deteriorated even more as a result of the intensification of fighting, exacerbated by the Covid-19 crisis. Land clearance and Risk Education (RE) activities have been severely limited since then in this area. Indiscriminate shelling, EO left by the various forces involved in the conflict] and the increased use of improvised explosive devices (IED) caused new displacements, a new layer of land contamination, and a rise in EO related accidents, killing and injuring civilians and non-civilians, including mine clearance and humanitarian workers .

2 - Activities

- Identification, assessment and Provision of direct physical and functional rehabilitation, PSS services and guidance to vulnerable persons and to their caregivers, through home visits or at a fixed point, alongside with provision of assistive & Mobility devices;-Provision of direct MHPSS services to persons with psychological distress or mental health disorders (psychoeducation, low intensity psychological interventions)- Provision of external referrals to facilitate access to basic and specialized services;- Training to health care providers on early rehabilitation and inclusion of persons with functional limitations, psychosocial distress, most common Mental Health disorders, referral mechanisms; PFA, PM+; Provision of basic rehabilitation equipment to supported PHCs and CBOs- Training of senior mental health professionals (supervisors) on delivering training and coaching sessions (ToT)- Training of Mental health professional on specialized and community intervention- Training of 3 CBOs on provision of MHPSS community intervention (with the support of HI)- Awareness sessions for community focal points including IDP leaders, and local and international NGO staff on the identification, inclusion and referral of people with functional limitations and psychological distress- Provision of training / refreshers to staff (PHCs, CBOs) on basic Covid-19 prevention measures.- Emergency risk education sessions to most at-risk groups in Greater Tripoli, through a public/social media campaign and through training of first responders (increase knowledge of the risks posed by explosive ordinance and support safe behavior and practices).- Support to humanitarian partners to promote effective and meaningful inclusion of extremely vulnerable persons and persons with disabilities, toward a more inclusive humanitarian action as well as participation in humanitarian assistance and protection of crisis-affected persons.

5 - Mitigation measures
5.1 - For each risk mentioned under 4 above, please describe the ways you plan to mitigate, prevent and/or address these.

4.04 HI has developed in the course of 2020 a full set of SOPs / Protocols for appropriate and safe office work but also implementation of activities in the field, may it be in households, fixed points (PHCs, etc.), or for meetings / trainings with partners and stakeholders. These SOPs/Protocols are already in place for the whole Libya mission. HI is also providing PPE as per needs to its staff.4.10 HI activities are tailored to answer age, gender and disability specificities of beneficiaries through both its unique approach of provision of services through home visits and fix points, and implementation modalities based on individualized needs assessments and tailored treatment plans. The age and gender composition of HI teams and the experience of team members in addressing age and gender concerns is taken into account when recruiting staff. Gender diversity in the composition of HI teams is essential to ensure the access to males and females to the integrated rehabilitation services. HI thus aims to ensure the presence of female teams along with male teams for both physical rehabilitation activities (sensitive due to the physical interactions required) and in the psychosocial field (individual sessions being often facilitated if the beneficiary and workers are of the same gender). This allows a better adaptation of the modalities of intervention upon gender sensitivities, through individual or groups sessions, home-based or in fixed points.4.14 Institutional policies on anti-fraud and corruption, child protection, and protection of beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse are deployed in Libya, and are shared with new staff, partners, and suppliers. Moreover, all HI staff have signed and follow HI Code of Conduct, which prevents HI Staff and beneficiaries from protection risks.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

HI ensures accountability towards beneficiaries, having in place a hotline and Facebook page to receive potential complaints and feedback. This mechanism follows specific communication channels according to the degree of gravity of the feedback, triggering a series of immediate corrective measures. Regarding the dedicated mental health services (standalone component), rehabilitation services and psychosocial support, the beneficiaries are fully involved in the action, starting with their consent to benefit from the project's activities. Following an initial assessment to better understand the medical history, functional limitations and psychosocial issues, the physiotherapist and the psychosocial worker suggest and build with the beneficiary a common treatment plan. The beneficiaries are also systematically asked to give feedback on the quality of the intervention and the improvement of their access to other services.
