Project Summary

This project aims to provide a layered and creative approach to re-introducing school-aged girls and boys to learning opportunities within the North-West (NW), South-West (SW), Littoral, Ouest regions of Cameroon. An estimated 17000 girls, boys, women and men will be target in this project. The project also targets teachers and plans to build their capacities in providing quality education to boys and girls in a safe and clean environment as well as provide comprehensive school material and curriculum for both boys and girls and teachers. The specific needs of girls and boys, including children with disabilities and other vulnerable groups where appropriate and possible will be provided.

Besoins / Needs

Since 2016, political and social instability, exacerbated by sporadic violence, has had a negative impact on the civilian population of Cameroon’s South-West (SW) and North-West (NW) Regions which host a total of four million people (16% of the total population). Due to political grievances within the education system, schools in the NW and SW regions have been ordered to close by separatist groups; thereby resulting in a significant number of children including boys and girls throughout the regions missing out on an extended piece of their education. More than 470,000 girls and boys are in need of humanitarian assistance in the area of education.The attacks extended to schools destroying more than 77 school structures, educational workers and children and the eventual shut-down in educational services, among other factors, within the NW and SW regions. This has contributed to the displacement and relocation of significant proportion of the population particularly women and youths to neighbouring villages and towns first of all to seek safety and to access education and other social services. This humanitarian crisis has had a growing impact in the neighbouring West and Littoral regions. Clashes have hit neighboring areas, particularly in the Bamboutos, Menoua, Noun, Wouri and Moungo divisions in the West and Littoral regions respectively. In the run-up to the elections, these two regions saw a rise in the number of Internally Displaced Persons (IDP) (86000 as of November 2018). While some have returned, many women, men, boys and girls remain displaced. As a result, many schools in Littoral and West are hosting a large number of displaced children either at the primary or secondary level. This explosion of displaced children in the host schools is not without effects. In the Littoral region, the findings highlighted the challenges schools and school staff face as IDP children continue to enroll. Classrooms are extremely crowded, there is a lack of teaching staff and teaching resources, and poor Water Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) facilities and practices are all impacting access to - and the quality of – education. The study carried out by Plan International equally found that, most of the teachers in these schools were government teachers who did not receive sufficient training. Teachers lack the basic competences to differentiate learning for displaced children, children with disabilities and the necessary pedagogical practices in an Education in Emergencies (EiE) context. In the West region, findings revealed that schools often do not have appropriate classrooms to accommodate students to facilitate learning. The dirtiness and maintenance of some of the schools is also a major concern. Classrooms are over-flowing with children, coupled with lack of benches, and insufficient space for the teacher to move around in the classroom – making formative assessment and support to individual or groups of students difficult. Teachers lack sufficient teaching resources and textbooks are almost non-existent. The lack of potable water points in some of the schools, the lack of gender-appropriate toilets for boys and girls, as well as hand washing facilities is also a regular phenomenon. Again, most of the teachers in the schools assessed are Parent Teacher Association (PTA) -appointed teachers – as opposed to government-appointed teachers - which can affect the motivation of these teachers.
