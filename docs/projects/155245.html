Project Summary

Goal: Contribute to enhance resilience of vulnerable hurricane Mathew affected households in Les Nippes.
Objective: Reduce shelter related vulnerability of the hurricane affected population in the municipalities of Anse-a-Veau, Arnaud L’Azile through access to safe, healthy, and appropriate living spaces, and safe building skills.Results: R1: Vulnerable families from the rural areas in Le Nippes have increased access to adequate and disaster resilient housing. Under this result, the project will respond to housing need of most vulnerable families through the repair of damaged houses and the construction of new chore houses for the families whose house were destroyed. Under this result HFHH will also work with family to increase understanding about safe construction method and therefore increase theirs and community resilience to future shocks such as Hurricane, earthquake and flooding’s. The selection criteria to identify the most vulnerable ones will be guided by (5) majors’ phases: 1. Mapping of most affected localities (number of houses damaged or destroyed) – 2. Census of households -3. Preselection – 4. Community validation – 5. Final verification 6. Publication. Based on the data collected on the households related to their socio-demographic status, assets and income, HFHH will rank the most vulnerable families affected by combining, Technical assessment, qualitative and socio-economic criteria. Key selection criteria are as follow: Head of family/member Handicap; Woman headed family; Minor headed family; Large family more than 5 people; Breastfeeding family leader; Pregnant women Elderly people without support; Family income. The assessment will be guided by the approved Template by UCLBP and the draft list will be submitted to both Mayor office and community representative for validation. After local validation, Habitat for Humanity Haiti will submit the full beneficiary’s dossier including the assessment, BOQs and other relevant documents for UCLBP approval. To ensure transparency in the project implementation as well as active participation of community in the monitoring, a complaint Mechanism will be established where beneficiaries and other community member can report their concern in regard to the project implementation. . Cross-cutting issues: Protection / (do no harm): HFHH will place emphasis on community engagement and participation to minimize potential tensions. Result 2: knowledge and competences of community members and Construction professionals from les Nippe on safe and disaster resilient constructions is Increased: Under this result, HFHH will implement action to improve the skills and competencies of construction professional on safe and resilient construction and therefore promote safer construction culture in the project intervention areas. theoretical and practical training will be giving to Masons and carpenters. The professionals who successful complete the program will be used in the construction and repairs of household under this project. the list of trained masons and carpenters will also be shared with Mayor offices and community representatives as well as with UCLBP and shelter working group. The project will target 350 most vulnerable household families (250 new construction and 100 house repairs.
Coordination and Collaboration with local authorities and Shelter working group: HFHH will continue attending the shelter working group meetings and shared lessons learned with other shelter focused actors. The project will also work in close collaboration with local authorities will jointly with habitat propose the list of potential beneficiaries, based upon which Habitat will conduct the need assessment. final project beneficiaries list will be validated by community-based structures and local authorities. To ensure UCLBP oversight of the project, a full package with all beneficiaries’ information, including assessments will be shared with UCLB for approval.

Needs

Haiti remains the poorest country in the Americas with 59% of the Haitian population living under the national poverty line of US$ 2.42 per day. Haiti is in the hurricane corridor, and annually exposed to a long hurricane period (June to November), and subsequently exposed to high risks of recurrent natural disasters. On October 2016 the category 5 hurricane Matthew hit the southwestern part of Haiti, leaving a death toll of 546. 2.1 million persons were affected by hurricane Matthew, and it nearly or destroyed around 200,000 homes. Amongst the 2.1 million people who were affected, over 1. million still need some type of humanitarian assistance in various sectors, particularly shelters. One year after the hurricane, durables repairs have been provided to an estimated of 23.000 HH covering 30% of the need at national Level and 3.500 HH benefitted of a new construction out the 103.000 identified by the PDNA (3%of the estimated need). In 2018 with CERF fund, HFHH conducted the repair of 900 Houses in Les Nippers, and additional 350 repairs with ADH fund through Habitat Germany, totalizing 1250The communes of Anse-a-Veau, Arnaud and L’Azile, are in the department of Les Nippes, which has been severely affected by the hurricane Matthew and where up to 60% of the population were in need of humanitarian aid. A total of 6711 houses were destroyed and 9621 have been severely damaged by the hurricane Matthew in the Les Nippes Department. During the assessment of the level of the damages, across the commune of Arnaud, Anse-a-Veau and L’Azile, the Habitat team found out that more than 60% of the houses were deeply damaged and had to be knocked down to be rebuild from ground. In spite enormous effort from government and NGOs, UN and others, to address the issue of houses affected by Mathew, overall response is still far from address the needs. Over the 2018 HFHH supported the repair of 900 houses with fund from CERF and other 350 with fund from ADH, enabling 1 250 families to go back to their homes. HFHH response reached less than 20% of needs, meaning that high number of the families are still in need of profound repair or reconstruction of their home. In a bid to reach the most vulnerable families, who have their houses destroyed and have been grossly sidelined in the response, HFHH decided to pursue its strategy of serving this most vulnerable among vulnerable ones by focusing on the construction of core houses with the possibility of extension, in a progressive building model as well as the repair of damaged houses.Needs assessments highlight shelter as one of the key priorities needs of the hurricane Matthew affected households Anse-a-Veau, Arnaud and L’Azile, Petit-Trou and petit Reviere which had severe impacts on housing in all three (5) municipalities. Preliminary assessment data indicates that damage caused by the Hurricane combined with existing challenges such as low income, and limited resources has resulted in drastically reducing households’ physical protection and further exacerbated the likelihood of exposure to weather conditions such as cold temperatures, rain and storms and mosquito born-diseases, and outbreak of diseases. This problem affects particularly the most vulnerable ones, including young children, pregnant women, elderly, people living with disabilities or serious specific medical needs. The assessment of the need was done by HFHH through a combination of methods including desk review, individual interview with potential beneficiaries and local authorities and observation. The assessment of the needs has also been based on the results of the rapid assessment conducted earlier in 2017 and additional assessment conducted in August-September 2018 by HFHH team of engineers coupled with the results of the housing assessments from HFHH, OCHA’s Situation reports and the PDNA. Also, the identification of the needs included consultation with Mayor Office, CASEC and community.

Activities or outputs

Activities: R1Land assessment: to mitigate the risk of future housing problems related to flooding, land erosion and hurricane, Habitat for humanity Haiti will conduct a thorough analysis of land appropriateness for safe and disaster resilient construction. Construction and repairs of houses: Homeowner-driven construction approach will be used for constructions following TCLA model, the project will support 350 Households from Grand-Anse to build new Core house of 27.88 m2, comprised of a bed room, a multi-functional room, a veranda, rain harvesting system and a toilet. Home owners will be involved and take the lead in the construction process to define the locations for construction, design and technical characteristics, selection of the local trained masons, negotiations of the labor cost, supervision of the work of the masons etc. each core house will cost as follow: Construction Materials funded by the project 3,048.88 and Beneficiary contribution = 384,26 USD a total of 3,469.14 USD. Labor funded by the project 912.43 USD and beneficiary contribution 35.74. total house cost to be funded by the project is 3,997.03 USD. The total cost of the house with beneficiary contribution is 4,417.31 USD (91% funded by the project and 9% from Beneficiary contribution). The repairs of the damaged houses are estimated at 900 dollars per house. Supervision of houses’ Constructions: To ensure strict application of technical guidelines, HFHH engineers will be based in the community to guide the households and trained local masons in safe construction process to ensure houses fully meet safety and security standards and they are disaster resilient Activities: R2Safe construction Training: to fill up the existing skills gap on safe construction, the project will support to increase local technicians’ construction skills and knowledge on construction techniques, incorporating disaster risk reduction practices, while respecting local/traditional norms (and culture). Technical Construction training for masons will be conducted through a mix of theoretical and practical sessions, facilitated by HFHH civil engineers. Safer construction awareness campaign: Building prevention culture and resources represent the starting point to develop vulnerability reduction strategies strongly rooted into local conditions and practices. Housing construction in rural areas of Haiti is by default owner-driven combined with the use of local masons.
