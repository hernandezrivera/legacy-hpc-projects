Project Summary

The proposed project aims to enhance agricultural production, the food, and income-generating opportunities of affected households and communities in North-East Syria especially among the most vulnerable households and communities. The proposed interventions are three folds:
1. To improve the marketing of wheat products of farmers and support wheat/bread availability in conflict-affected regions;
2. To improve the access of small livestock breeders to animal feeders;
3. To restore income-generating assets and increase agricultural production and improved economic conditions and labor market opportunities in the targeted communities.
SR intervention will be aligned with the wheat crop calendar and improve the purchasing power of 500 households. 1000 tons of wheat seeds will be purchased; 400 tons of wheat will be used as seeds for the next season’s planting, after sifting and sterilizing. The other 600 tons of purchased wheat will be supplied to the contracted mill to be grinded, and, as a result, 480 tons of flour will be obtained and supplied to the bakery contracted by SR. Consequently, bread for 3500 households can be produced and distributed free of charge. The remaining amount (120 tons) is used as bran to be provided to 1200 livestock breeders as feeders for their animals.
On the other hand, 2000 farmers will benefit from the distribution of wheat inputs, including seeds, fertilizers, pests control materials, and harvesting costs, among which 350 farmers will receive TOT and capacity building on wheat crop integrated management through the establishment of 12 farmer field schools in the targeted areas.
Moreover, 240 cash-for-work workers will be selected to implement activities related to firefighting and integrated pest management.

4 - Needs

The HNO 2021 states that 14.2 M people in need of food and agriculture assistance, at least 12.4 M are estimated to be food insecure, of which 1.3 M are considered severely food insecure. Furthermore, 1.7 M living in camps are deemed to be 100 % food insecure, and 1.8 M are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity.SR conducted Wheat value chain assessments in proposed locations. The main purpose was to get a better understanding of the respective domestic agricultural value and supply chains and the challenges which in particular smallholders are facing along the chain. The assessment covered 28 sub-districts in Tell Abiad, Ras Al Ain, Azaz, Maree, Suran, Mhambel, Maarat Timsrin, and Armanaz. Interviews conducted with small and medium-scale actors engaged in the value and supply chain. The main findings were: Low-quality seeds and crop diseases, Lack of pest control materials due to high prices, Lack of fertilizers due to high prices, Lack of marketing information, Lack of pastures and fodders for livestock breeders, Lack of awareness for seed processing at the farmer level, Malfunctioning mills, Malfunctioning bakeries as a lack of flour and operational cost flour baking/bread producing. SR assessment findings matched to a great degree the ones of FSL cluster, ATWG wheat cultivation assessment carried out in NWS and NES, Dec 2020, as 71% of interviewed stated the unavailability of agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizers, pesticides, and fuel) was the main factor towards the reduction in wheat cultivated land and productivity. Aiming to get a more detailed picture, SR conducted FGDs in December 2020 in the proposed areas. The information collected was analyzed in the form of a value chain mapping for the wheat sector.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Although all Syrian population has been heavily affected by the current crisis, due to traditional local norms, women are exposed to additional protection-related risks. All types of violence are cited as occurring in opposition areas of Syria, with domestic violence, sexual exploitation, and child marriage as the top three. While sexual exploitation is somehow new, child marriage and domestic violence are often normalized and ingrained in the culture. Women and girls are exposed to structural and societal risks as the lack of safe spaces and public transportation, limited freedom of movement, poverty, unemployment, displacement, the stress associated with war, gender role change, lack of documents. All these pose enormous pressure, men feeling they failed in protecting and securing a good life for their families. With men perceiving women as a threat to their control due to gender roles change, domestic violence has increased in Syria. Whereas men's actions are explained as a result of stress, women are often blamed for provoking the violence or allowing it to happen.The planned intervention will mainstream protection principles throughout the project cycle by considering the different needs of men, women, boys, and girls, therefore before starting the activities SR will provide a package of training for field staff including GBV guiding principles, child protection minimum standards, referral pathway, and protection mainstreaming.This training designed to enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection risks by supporting the protective environment in Syria. SR designed the safety audit to check with women and girls, specifically, if they were consulted in the design phase of the project and express their fears.
