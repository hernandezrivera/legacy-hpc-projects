Project Summary

The overall aim of the proposed project is to ensure Access of Health to the most Vulnerable Migrants in Lebanon especially the individuals with Irregular Status (non-ID/ undocumented).
This project is proposed for a duration of 12 months starting from 1st January 2023 until end of December 2023.
Amel efforts in primary health care date back to its establishment in 1979, with the recent national efforts played a major role in the national response to the pandemic, cholera outbreak including vaccination, surveillance, response, policy involvement among others.
Amel has been working in the support of Migrants since 2011 with established connections and outreach to almost all migrant communities of 28+ nationalities and 80+ migrant leaders through its specialized team. Outreach is conducted by Amel team specialized in the support of migrants’ cultural sensitivity, including migrants all over Lebanon, and related to embassies, GOs and other stakeholders. Migrants reach out to Amel through 30 field centers all over Lebanon, through migrants’ hotlines, community leaders, diplomatic representations and referral by other organizations. Amel is also specialized in identifying victims of trafficking and ensuring their livelihood.
Locations of the project includes AMEL PHCCs all over Lebanon that are part of the MoPH accredited network, and in the process of application or that Amel is managing within national consortiums located in Beirut, Beirut Suburbs, Bekaa and the South. The largest requests for access to health are located in BML and specifically in Amel centers in Ashrafieh, Bourj Hammoud and Burj El Barajneh.
This includes primary health care support in Amel PHCCs with referral to advanced lab and Diagnosis, when needed, for effective follow up to health counselling. The support is conducted through a comprehensive approach of humanitarian assistance that starts with registration/screening and support to basic assistance of the case through the migrant sector directives and channels of support. Since 2011, Amel succeeded to support thousands of migrants through social support, Voluntary return to their home countries, Cash for Rent Assistance, legal support, empowerment, capacity building, awareness raising, advocacy, access to health, support of basic assistance, food security, NFIs, support of migrants in detention among other activities.
The project aims at ensuring access of health to the vulnerable migrants in Lebanon especially with irregular status through:
Outcome 1 – Ensure Access of Primary Health Care support to Migrants in Lebanon including provision of essential and chronic diseases Medications, and Advanced Diagnosis
Obj. 1.1: 1200 patients/visits supported with chronic disease medicines in Amel provided in designated Amel PHCCs upon prescription by the doctor consultation
Obj. 1.2: 2500 Migrants receive health consultation and medication upon prescription in Amel PHCCs through GP and specialized doctors. This includes cases referred through different referral pathways from other Response or Community agencies (NGOs, CSO, INGOs..), GOs, and diplomatic representations of Migrants, among others.
Obj. 1.3: 50 patients supported with advanced secondary care diagnostic (outside ER/Hospital)
Outcome 2 - Enhanced protection environment in humanitarian health response
Obj. 2.1: 100 frontline health workers trained on culturally sensitive approaches to improve health access for migrants
Obj. 2.2: 1300 women and girls receiving dignity kits, including personal hygiene items
Population includes migrants that are female, male, children and older persons.
The target population includes migrants of African and South-East Asian nationalities, including Sudan, Iraq, Egypt and Afghanistan.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The project is designed to be responsive to the emergent needs the target population, whilst ensuring that this is conducted in a comprehensive approach provided through Amel various programs. No intervention will be taken without the expressed will, consent and involvement of the targeted individual, conducted on a participatory and within Amel respect to Human dignity and humanity of each, ensuring inclusivity, non-discrimination and PSEA. Empowerment activities and awareness raising are provided to ensure a protective approach with livelihood when possible to ensure a sustainable framework. The activities are rolled out by trained and specialized staff. Confidentiality of cases is ensured where out and the do-no-harm principle is embedded both within this project as well as more broadly throughout Amel's program design. Gender sensitivity is also engrained within Amel's approach - and the team is able to provide gender-sensitive services throughout its centres.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Amel is a member of the CHS alliance and a strong advocate of Humanitarian Accountability to beneficiaries, in addition to donors. Amel has a number of well-practiced quality assurance mechanisms and accountability strategies in place. Constant communication between the project coordination team and field staff, as well as regular field visits ensures good interaction between all staff involved in the project. This allows feedback for rapid responses to changing dynamics in the field. Additionally, this program has a hotline for (P)VoHT, including vulnerable migrants at risk of sexual/labor exploitation. The Do No Harm principles are embedded in the project design, and social stability factors are taken into account. The project builds on existing deep-rooted relationships developed with local communities. Services have been identified by engaging with over 28 migrant communities in Lebanon. The team who developed the proposal has a deep understanding of the context & risk mitigation
