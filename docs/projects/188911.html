Project Summary

The project aims to continue and increase the accessibility to essential life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services for all boys, girls, pregnant and lactating women, and the caregivers (other women, and men) with attention to people with special needs without any sexual discrimination, among IDPs and host communities in Aleppo governorate. The project activities will be implemented by supporting 2 stabilization centers (SC) in high severity communities and IDPs hosting areas in Azzaz.
The SCs will be providing infant and young child feeding interventions, Micro-nutrient deficiency prevention, control, and improve equitable access to high-quality, lifesaving, curative nutrition services through early identification, referral, and treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW.
The nutrition team will provide nutrition services integrated to the health services provided in Al Amal hospital and Al Amal PHC in Azzaz.
A mobile network of community health and community outreach workers linked to the HFs will Screen of children, pregnant and lactating women for malnutrition signs and will be conducting outreach activities in the communities around the health facilities to deliver key messages to community members with special focus on pregnant and lactating women, along with disseminating IYCF-E messages among mothers of children under five.
All activities and nutrition intervention will be provided according to nutrition cluster recommendations and CMAM, IYCF- E protocols, including the adapted interventions in the context of COVID-19 such as simplified CMAM protocol , etc.
All the prevention measures from COVID-19 infections will be applied during providing nutrition services, the nutrition staff will encourage beneficiaries to get COVID-19 vaccination as the main prevention measure, and disseminate the communication key messages about COVID-19 preventions that are recommended by the RCCE.
The project will also focus on building the capacity of the health and nutrition staff on IYCF counseling to provide integrated health and nutrition services. The health facility staff and CHWswill be trained in aspects related to child health care and nutritional screening and referral, they will also be provided with sufficient education to help to raise the awareness of the related communities.

4 - Needs

As per HNO draft for 2022, 12.23 million people are in need of health services in 2022, including 4.4 million displaced persons, 1.33million children under 5 years – including an estimated 503,000 live births expected16, and 3.38 millionwomen of reproductive age (15-49 years). With more than 164,000 confirmed cases and close to 5,800 deaths4, COVID-19 continues to disrupt the already-fragile health services and systems due to low levels of COVID-19.More than 1.8 million people have 'critical' needs whilst almost 1.9 million have 'severe' nutrition needs. According to the SMART survey conducted in June 2021, the prevalence of chronic malnutrition among children aged 6-59 months increased from 19 % to 24.5 % over the same period The prevalence of GAM increased from 0.9 % to 4.7 % between May 2019 and June 2021 in Northwest Syria in Aleppo and Idlib governorates. Among PLW, the prevalence of acute malnutrition across northwest Syria is 12.4 percent. There are also significant differences across northwest Syria. Maternal Malnutrition continues to be a major public health problem in NWS especially in areas affected by displacements where 10.8% of Women of Reproductive Age (WTA) are malnourished, while this rate was higher between PLWs 12.4% According to the nutrition, surveillance report had conducted in September 2021 that included 9 districts, 35 Sub-district, and 50 spatial points in the northwest Syria surveillance team had reported.

5 - Implementation of activities

VO will provide integrated primary and secondary health services with nutrition and protection. This will be implemented in all levels of supported health facilities; All supported health facilities will provide free of charge preventive and curative services. These facilities will provide integrated nutrition services for communities affected by the water crisis, socio-economic challenges and vulnerabilities related to war and displacement. The nutrition program will systematically prevent, identify, treat, and refer, where required, acutely malnourished children between 6-59 months by providing integrated nutrition interventions of CMAM and IYCF. Referral and follow-up mechanism will be established for providing specialized care when needed. The supported facilities will receive payment of salaries for staff (medical and non-medical), monthly operational costs, professional training and monitoring, provision of pharmaceuticals, and other medical and non-medical commodities (including equipment, medicines, and consumables). MEAL activities include a) daily visits to the facilities and observation notes, b) patient satisfaction surveys, c) AAP, d) monitor number of visitors/patients, e) conducting verification checks and f) exit interviews with patients. The team will prepare the IPTT, observation reports and means of verification. Plus uphold the AAP mechanism and learning.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination if the service is provided to a specific group and others are excluded Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Violet conducts assessments and selects vulnerable communities based on severity; selection communicated through the AAP team. Service mapping to inform about alternative services. 2) PWD or at-risk groups might be excluded from services or facilities. Likelihood: Low; Impact: High Information sharing, services and facilities are suitable for at-risk groups, conduct AAP sessions to inform about requesting special assistance for PWD/elderly/pregnant BNFs, consultation with stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training staff on inclusion. 3) Risk of GBV to/from/in overcrowded health facilities Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Selection of suitable locations and facilities, referral of special needs cases, avoid long queues and allow clear space for waiting, provide child care services, suitable service times, guarded facilities. 4) At-risk group do not receive needed services Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium Coordination with sectors to avoid duplication, collaborate with protection and GBV teams to ensure access, consult BNFs in sex-segregated FGDs, use Washington Group Questions as tool to include disability in selection. 5) Risk of COVID-19 Upholding WHO recommended measures, provision of relevant information to BNFs, increased GBV response to mitigate domestic violence as result of economic pressure on the family.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements. Potential risks: 1) Request of favors, e.g. SEA, for selecting BNFs to the services Mitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasise on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative, ensure appropriate staffing during any distribution or service provision. 2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staff Mitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender balanced project and MEAL teams, clear visibility of staff, avoid overcrowded facilities and allocate clear waiting areas, identify at-risk households and provide special services e.g. transportation if needed. Violet will conduct sex-segregated FGDs and KIIs to ensure participation of women and girls during all phases of the project, especially in design of the activities and monitoring.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Staff infected with COVID-19 do not exceed 2% at all times. - Community acceptance for this project continues. - Current conflict doesn't affect program activities. - Border crossing from Turkey is maintained. - Drugs and medical supplies continue to be received through WHO medical supply-line. Risks: Unstable security situation: VO regularly monitors the local context, and employs security focal points. VO staff complete mandatory security trainings. Lack of the health services quality: VO has a health technical team who will be responsible on following up the quality of the health services provided in the health facilities as well as link the staff with the updated international guidelines and protocols. Moreover, a training plan for the staff will be set in coordination with health facilities teams and the staff needs. Complaints mechanism is already set in the health facilities and the patients can raise any issue regarding the quality of the health services and will registered and directed to the relevant department to deal with. the Cross-border resolution is not renewed: VO will advocate to the importance of the cross border operations to the people in NWS and will shed the lights on the humanitarians need that still exist in the area.
