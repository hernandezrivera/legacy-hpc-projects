Project Summary

2. Libya continues to be a complex crisis. It continues to be one of the most fragile and conflict-affected countries in the region due to the ever-present threat of large-scale hostilities, the presence of foreign armed groups, the trafficking of drugs and migrants, uncontrolled borders, organized crime and corruption. The dire humanitarian situation has been exacerbated by the continuing spread of COVID-19 in Libya and the efforts to reduce transmission is hampered by the weak health system and by the difficulty to ensure prevention measures such as social distancing and proper sanitization amongst the 392,241 internally displaced people (IDPs) recorded by DTM, and the 584,509 migrants and refugees. Many migrants, displaced populations and refugees live in cramped locations with poor sanitation and hygiene. This is only likely to increase as the socio-economic impact of COVID-19 unfolds. Many also suffer significant barriers when trying to access public or private healthcare, including, but not limited to, stigmatization and discrimination.

1 - Needs

The country health system is fragile and fragmented as protracted and concurrent political, health and humanitarian crises converse to weaken the technical and operational capacities of health care delivery across the country. According to WHO’s recent assessments, only 40 per cent of communities have child health and emergency services, only 35 per cent have general clinical services, and only 15-20 per cent have services for reproductive health care and noncommunicable and communicable diseases. Although noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) account for 72 per cent of the burden of disease in Libya, NCD treatments are widely unavailable. The availability of essential medicines is also a major challenge (70 per cent of functioning Primary Healthcare Centres (PHC) facilities do not have any of the top 20 essential and most in demand medicines). Services for the survivors of gender-based violence are inadequate and difficult to access (especially for female migrants) due to movement restrictions, curfews, closures of services and fear of social stigma. Disability services are available mainly in Benghazi, Misrata, Sabha and Tripoli. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) is chronically neglected in Libya due to underdeveloped community-based and specialized services, shortage of qualified workforce, lack of facilities, and social stigma towards people with mental illness. Migrants face psychological distress due to their precarious living conditions in Libya, experiences of conflict and displacement as well as en-route to Libya, or due to mental disorders which require significant MHPSS services. Migrants and refugees’ vulnerabilities have been also exacerbated by the pandemic which has reduced their access to casual jobs. These groups, by virtue of their displacement or their legal status, have limited access to health care services. Migrants who try to return home find they can no longer do so because their sources of income have disappeared, and travel is extremely difficult due to ongoing COVID-19 mobility restrictions. Although migrants and refugees in detention centres (and other people in Libyan prisons) are particularly vulnerable to COVID-19, humanitarian agencies lack sustained and unrestricted access to these facilities. While migrants and refugees have the most severe needs, IDPs, returnees and non-displaced Libyans in the worst-affected areas also need humanitarian assistance. COVID-19 lockdowns and curfews have further reduced people’s access to primary health care (PHC). The health needs are not met at the health facilities level and there is very limited capacity to reach out to people in need of health services through mobile medical teams. The capacities of local health care facilities to meet life-sustaining needs has eroded and an increasing number of municipalities are referring patients for treatment to the main cities of Tripoli, Benghazi, Misrata and Sabha. The referral system needs to be urgently strengthened throughout the country. Reliance on lifesaving and life-sustaining health care services supported by the humanitarian response will continue across the country in 2021.

2 - Activities

This project will support a total of 125,427 individuals, which include 30,701 IDPs, 20,774 returnees, 73,951 migrants for a total budget of $2,475,600 through the following activities: •Mobile health outreach services through the deployment of mobile clinics in the areas hosting high concentrations of IDPs and migrants. The services will include primary healthcare and referrals to specialized facilities and providers • Mobile healthcare clinics in migrant detention centres that will provide an integrated essential health service package including MHPSS services •Assist in strengthening the existing referral system and develop linkages at different level of health care system including community, primary health care centers and secondary hospitals •Provide direct and remote MHPSS services to migrants, IDPs, and vulnerable members of host communities and build referral linkages for long-term care through MOH facilities, private practices, and local and international NGOs •Strengthen the capacity of healthcare providers to deliver the essential package of health services including MHPSS in areas with high concentrations of migrants and IDPs. IOM through the MHPSS TWG chairing role will organize capacity building activities for health service providers to ensure mainstreaming MHPSS within the national response •Support selected public health facilities to improve delivery of essential services, primary health care centers and secondary hospitals by supplying the most essential, unavailable equipment and medicines •Support the ongoing disease surveillance system by strengthening the Early Warning Alert and Response Network (EWARN) and District health Information system (DHIS-2) •Strengthen coordination mechanism among partners delivering health services to vulnerable migrants, IDPs, returnees and refugees by continuing to co-chair the Migration Health Sub-Working Group •These activities will support men, women and all age groups

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

Accountability to the affected population is a key concern for IOM programming in all its projects, especially in the humanitarian settings. IOM will also collect information on how beneficiaries perceive services provided by IOM and other service providers from the complaint and feedback mechanism jointly developed by partners and integrate the feedback to adapt and improve project activities during the implementation period.
