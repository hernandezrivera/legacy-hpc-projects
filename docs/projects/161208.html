Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to help improve access to safe and clean drinking water, sanitation, and hygiene of targeted IDP and host individuals, households, population/communities affected by impacts protracted and complex crisis in Yirol East, lake state, South Sudan. The affected population includes Internally Displaced Peoples (IDPs), and most vulnerable host communities’ households and individuals including not limited to poor female headed households, households with persons living with disability, those with low income, and the most food insecure households suffered with malnutrition cases particularly Pregnant and Lactating Mothers and under five children.
Women, men, boys, girls, and Children, are at the center of this project. Targeting will be based on humanitarian principles of humanity and impartiality to save lives and alleviate suffering. And, aid will be provided based on need alone. For this, efforts will be made to deliver assistances in proportion to identified needs.
To achieve this, CAFOD protection and safeguarding framework will be used which considers safety, meaningful access, dignity, and inclusivity. A participatory and consultative approach will be used to agree on targeting criteria in the way it can ensures most vulnerable individuals/households are identified and benefited from this support. This will be implemented by building on past targeting methodology which has been used in previous CAFOD response and recovery interventions. To realize the inclusion of persons with disability, the project will make, at least, 10 percent of the beneficiaries’ households will be targeted according to membership of person living with disabilities.
The project planned will allow CAFOD to provide immediate lifesaving assistance as per the needs and this will be delivered through repair and rehabilitation of critical broken boreholes, house-to-house hygiene promotion through project volunteers, training of water users committees to aid sustainability of access to safe drinking water and refresher training for handpump mechanics which will be conducted by CAFOD humanitarian staffs. To support girl-child education, the project will provide sanitary kits for adolescence girls in primary school to curb menstrual hygiene-related absenteeism from school and mediocre performance. The project will also train women, girls and boys on the making of re-usable menstrual pads (RUMPS). This will be a source of income generation for the concerned individuals.
Children being social agents for change, the project would establish and train school hygiene clubs to pass on good hygiene practices to their peer in school and eventually to the entire community. The main messages would revolve around personal, food, environmental hygiene, safe water chain and safe disposal of solid and human waste amongst others. Mass hygiene campaigns in public places such markets, health facilities, village centers and schools would also be conducted to enlighten the communities on environmental and public hygiene. At the same time, the project will engage strategically to address the drivers of vulnerabilities and underlying structural issues across the targeted state. Besides, the project will collaborate and complement with other stakeholders implementing in the area to address WASH in Nutrition, WASH for GBV mitigation and WASH for IDPs and vulnerable host communities.

1 - Needs

CAFOD’s recent WASH needs assessment conducted in March 2019 in Yirol East, Eastern Lakes State indicated that forty percent of the population have no access to safe drinking water meanwhile, existing handpumps are either poorly maintained or broken down. Lack of access to spare parts, and/or ownership of waterpoints by the community and trained personnel to maintain/repair handpumps are the major contributing factors for high downtime of handpumps in Yirol East. This has left a large proportion of the population at risk of preventable waterborne related diseases. Open defecation is widespread and the most predominant practice of human wastes disposal among the internal displaced persons and the host communities. About 75% of the population has no access to functional latrines and handwashing practices is as low as 28% (CTP WASH KAP Survey, 2018). Yirol East was one of the worst hit counties in South Sudan when cholera broke out and declared in June 2016 that claimed lives of more than 56 people and 856 cholera registered cases in Yirol East alone. Currently, Yirol East is classified in catastrophe in the recent released IPC with 10,000 people on the brink of starvation. This will further exacerbate the WASH situation. Yirol East is hosting more than 16,000 IDPs (CTP needs assessment, 2015) displaced from Panyijar County in former Unity and others displaced from Bor and Duk Counties of former Jonglei State during the 2013 conflict and the ongoing widespread violence across South Sudan. CAFOD & Trócaire has been active on the response in coordination with other partners in Yirol East. However, the situation remains dire and continues to be confirmed by prominent levels of malnutrition, and hunger. Beyond infrastructural deficiencies, traditional beliefs, cultural practices, and lack of information are seen as the highest contributing factors to poor hygiene behaviors in Yirol East. This project will complement the ongoing CTP response in the area to address identified gaps.

2 - Activities

As mentioned above, forty percent of the population have no access to safe drinking water whilst existing handpumps are either poorly maintained or broken down. Lack of access to spare parts, and/or ownership of waterpoints by the community and trained personnel to maintain/repair handpumps are the major contributing factors for high downtime of handpumps in Yirol East. The project would therefore interevent in the following ways; 1. Rehabilitate 10 boreholes in critical areas at health facilities, schools and underserved communities.2. Construct new water points in the area where access to safe water is an issue. 3. Establishment and training of water users’ committees (WUC) on operations, maintenance, sustainability and promoting good hygiene practices at the water points.4. Provide refresher training of community handpump mechanics on repair and preventive maintenance of handpumps to promote functionality of water points (2 per payam). 5. Establishment and training of school hygiene clubs to promote good hygiene practices in schools and as well as agents of hygiene behavioral change in the community.6. Distribution of culturally appropriate menstrual hygiene kits to 200 adolescent schoolgirls to improve girls’ attendance in schools during menstrual cycles. Train girls and women to make re-usable menstrual pads (RUMPS) as a sustainable and cheaper way to avail menstrual towels to girls and women. 7. House-to-house hygiene messaging to 3000 households to trigger positive hygiene behavioral change. 8. Support government in providing spare parts to rehabilitate the water points9. Train hygiene and sanitation volunteers for undertake mass and house to house hygiene campaign.10. Develop hygiene and sanitation IEC material for promotional activities11. Provide appropriate WASH NFIs to 500 households. 12. Train 10 gender sensitive community hygiene promotion volunteers to conduct hygiene sessions at household levels and public places such as in health facilities, schools and markets.13. Lead and co-chair monthly WaSH cluster coordination meeting at Yirol East.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

CAFOD operate in the framework of Safe Dignified Access and Inclusive framework which recognize the right of disaster affected individual and groups to have access to safe and adequate basic services and protection. The beneficiaries CAFOD works for are citizen of South Sudanese and they are also legally recognized (both IDPs and Host communities). CAFOD has included a strong component on safe and responsive feedback and complaints handling and response in its SADI Framework which is rolled out across all its programme. The main emphasis of this is on ensuring that systems are safe, accessible and dignified for every group in a community, particularly the most marginalized, and that there is consultation in the project design, the feedback loop is closed, and that the effectiveness of the mechanism is monitored. This allows room for flexibility in programme to ensure they have a system that is appropriate and adapted for their context. Requirements to have a feedback and complaints mechanism in place have also been incorporated in every partners operation, with requirements for any sensitive / serious complaint to be shared with CAFOD within a set time limit.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project design puts IDPs at the heart of this intervention. The main beneficiaries of this intervention include both IDPs and host communities. The approach CAFOD implements focuses on meeting basic needs of the most vulnerable including IDPs, to ensure their access to safe and adequate water, promote good hygienic and sanitation practices, screening, and referrals for GBV and PSS, Cash/Voucher for food insecure households, and improved access to basic services. The above-mentioned actions will support those impacted mostly by conflict and dry spell and this include Internally Displaced People. Thus, main beneficiaries of this intervention include both IDPs and Host Communities. In proportion, the beneficiaries will be 75% IDPs and 25% host communities. This project will make efforts to address the needs of both Host and IDPs. CAFOD continue to support IDPs. The project design has taken into consideration the needs and the expectations of different community groups such as women, men, boys, girls, and children through participatory approaches that have been used to determine the community’s needs. The Displacement Affected Communities will have safe and dignified access to this support. To materialize this women groups containing 5 members will established as targeting committees (2 from host, 3 from IDP HHs) in various project locations. These committee will identify most vulnerable IDP and Host communities as per the set and agreed criteria with big community meeting.

State ministry of Rural Water will be the forerunner of this intervention and will be contacted at all levels of the implementation to provide technical advice and support to the targeted communities. The project will also work closely with Relief Rehabilitation Commissions. This approach will ensure the long-term sustainability of the project in terms of smooth implementation the project activities. To avoid duplication of efforts, actors mapping will be conducted at the operational Payam and County Level. The project implementation process put communities at the heart of its intervention. For this, strategies for assurance of active participation of women, girls and boys, men will be employed. The programme design has taken into consideration the needs and the expectations of different community groups such as women, men, boys, girls, and children and for these participatory approaches have been used to determine the community’s needs. In addition, accountability mechanisms will be developed to ensure beneficiaries are able to provide feedback (both positive and negative) on the implementation of the action. Project beneficiaries will provide information and stories to input into our external communication to highlight the impact of the crisis in Yirol East. The information collected on communities’ protection concerns will also be used to adapt the programme and improve the level of safe programming to ensure CAFOD fully adheres to the Sphere Protection Principles. To address existing barriers that hinders equal participation of women, in depth analysis of women’s domestic roles and responsibilities will be conducted. 6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

After South Sudan descended into civil war in December 2013, the humanitarian situation remained dire and the programme being implemented by CAFOD has taken note of provision of life-saving WASH support amongst others through multi sectoral responses.This would enable most vulnerable households to recover from crises by building capacity to withstand acute and chronic shocks through well designed and integrated programming to support positive coping capacity and promoting sustainable access to safe drinking water for all. As a result of ongoing peace negotiations, the organization expects that there might be increased caseloads of returnees and efforts to support these groups is vital to supplement the already overstretched WASH services in Yirol East for households, and individuals both from host communities and IDPs. Through its operation on peace building initiatives, the programme will make effort to address the root causes of conflict and drivers of displacement, which can provide the basis for further returns and stability. This intervention will be complemented by the ongoing intervention being undertaken in collaboration with South Sudan Council of Churches. This is being undertaken through other funding sources and contributes toward peaceful coexistence through conflict prevention and mitigation measures. Consequently, this programme plan together with ongoing peace building intervention, expected to address stability and security of crisis affected populations. The intervention CAFOD implementing through and with its implementing partners benefits internally displaced people, returnees, and vulnerable host communities, that have been affected direly and needing timely and quality humanitarian assistance and protection.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

CAFOD Humanitarian programme logic focus on the Linking Relief Recovery and Development (LRRD) approach. CAFOD have been implementing in the area focusing on capacity development of communities and institutions to foster a long-term solution to lack of access to safe drinking water. This will be achieved through various trainings conducted at community, payam and county levels. All the repair and rehabilitated boreholes are handed over to the water users committees in present of community leaders and communities are encouraged to provide their own resources to repair broken boreholes. Hygiene promotion volunteers are community-based and would always be available even after the closure of the project. CAFOD has an exit strategy which ensure sustainability after the withdrawal of external support.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project will make a deliberate effort to explore, understand and mitigate programme related risks leading to the development and implementation of strategy that minimize and address all ranges of threats both external and internal. In practice, this means that all intervention responses will be guided by Do-No-Harm and conflict-sensitivity approaches through analysis of risk to programme participants and environment. This is accompanied by appropriate information sharing, active communities’ engagement, and complaints/feedback-handling mechanisms and other safeguarding procedures. To minimize interference of elites and interest groups the project will undertake fair and impartial targeting mechanisms using its past experiences. Community power dynamics will be analyzed to help curb interests of various groups at community level. CAFOD uses its SADI (Safety Access, Dignified and Inclusivity) framework to mitigate exclusion or lack of consultation or participation of all marginalised and vulnerable groups in a project. The framework carries out a comprehensive assessment of at risk groups and how they are affected differently by a disaster or conditions of their vulnerability using such diversity statuses such as sex, age, disability, being a refugee, or IDP, minority status and others. This leads to design of projects that meet the specific needs of particular groups of people. Women and girls are not included in decision making in civic matters of the community and at family level as a result of the patriarchal nature of the Yirol East community. In order to mitigate this CAFOD has through this project and other interventions designed specific project to address gender inequality and women empowerment through various activities including women capacity building for leadership, confidence building, access to economic opportunities. The project will enhance protection especially of women and girls against SGBV.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?
