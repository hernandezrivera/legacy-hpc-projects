Project Summary

Kuwaiti patients Helping Fund (KPHF) proposes to assist vulnerable population affected by conflict, displaced and malnutrition by supporting 128 OTPs and TSFPs integrated including FBMAM & HF and 3 SCs in the following States: North & South Darfur, North, South and West Kordofan, Kassala, Red See, Gedaref, Sennar, Gezera, River Nile and Khartoum States. The project is proposed to treat 20,000 children <5 of age from SAM cases at OTPs and 4,000 children <5 of age from SAM with medical complications at SC. In addition, the project will treat 20,000 children <5 of age and 10,000 PLW from MAM cases. Moreover the project will prevent 20,000 children aged from 6-23 months and 10,000 Pregnant and lactating women from MAM by FBMAM. Beside that the project will fortify 15,000 children 6-59 of age who are not acutely malnourished through Home Fortification (HF) intervention service. Finally, the project will provide 90,000 caregiver with IYCF counselling. KPHF will provide health services through SCs and nutrition services through OTPs and TSFPs. Nutrition services to cover SAM and MAM cases for U5 and PLW which include screening at admission, medical treatment of severely acutely malnourished (SAM) children with and without medical complication, dietary management and individual child follow up, among others. It will also cover the administrative (support) cost of case management of moderate acute malnourished (MAM) girls and boys of 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Furthermore, nutrition education and counseling will be provided for mothers/caregivers in Infant and Young Child feeding (IYCF) practices and hygiene promotion as well as MSGs will built to facilitate of nutrition education and contribution in MUAC screening activities. Selected trainings will be provided on SC case management, CMAM, NiE, identification of medical complications at OTPs and TSFPs level, community screening and follow up. In addition to, women club, women cornea and community awareness session campaigns will be conducted under nutrition program.

1 - Needs

The nutrition situation in Sudan in 2022 is projected to remain at elevated high levels withoverall GAM levels of 13.6 percent. The situation is more critical in 64 localities that areclassified with very high prevalence of acute malnutrition of 15% and above as per WHOemergency levels classification of which 9 localities fall into catastrophically levels i.eGAM 30% and above. The projected worsening of drivers of malnutrition: food insecurity,deterioration in provision of Health and WASH services in 2022; increasing inflation whileno significant improvement is projected on caring and feeding practices for under-fivechildren. All these drivers will contribute to further deterioration of nutrition situation. Apartfrom acute malnutrition, the sector analysis revealed very high prevalence with over 36%stunting and micronutrient malnutrition affecting over 48% of under-five suffering fromanaemia. Given the high risks of mortality and morbidity associated with acutemalnutrition in normal and humanitarian situation, the sector has developed threeobjectives, interventions and targets as summarized below guided by envisaged responsecapacity. Approximately 522,000 children under 5 require treatment for SAM, andapproximately 2.2 million children require treatment for moderate acute malnutrition(MAM). The prevalence of MAM among pregnant and lactating women is 6.3 per cent.The prevalence of underweight children in Sudan is among the highest in the world 12under nutrition exacerbates morbidity and mortality, and contributes to underdevelopmentand a weak immune system. Additionally, in the case of maternal nutrition approximately883,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) suffer from acute malnutrition annually inSudan. Also, one million mothers need appropriate infant and young child nutrition (IYCN)counselling services. In North Darfur, South Darfur, Kassala, Red See, Sinnar, Gedaref andKhartoum States the nutrition needs continue to persist in on the background of lowcoverage and access of nutrition services and inadequate response capacities. HNO 2021showed that North and South Darfur States are the highest malnutrition rates with GAMrates ranging between 16 – 19 per cent and SAM between 3.7 and 4.9 per cent andKassala has stunting rates above 30 per cent. 6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

KPHF recognizes the importance of mainstreaming protection in its projects in order to make sure that its efforts would ensure full respect for the rights of individuals and groups. The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of IDPs, returnees and all groups especially the most vulnerable groups such women, girls and minority groups. While implementing all projects, the organization will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations which expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights. Through the Community Committees, partner and staff members, KPHF work to foster trust and mutual understanding between various groups and to minimize conflict and reduce or stop violence. Also KPHF focus on building the capacity of the staff to strengthen their understanding on the fundamental human rights and serving beneficiaries with dignity, gender and protection mainstreaming to ensure that vulnerable beneficiaries (women, girls, men and boys and minorities) are respected and feel protected.
