Project Summary

In the year 2023, Syria faces a concerning escalation in the prevalence of wasting, stunting, and anaemia affecting children under the age of five. Elevated levels of anaemia for women of reproductive age, particularly among adolescent girls (15–19 years). The prevalence of GAM has increased from 1.7% in 2019 to 4.7% in 2023. The burden of wasting in children under five is affecting a total of 506,529 children. Of these group 107,974 children are grappling with severe wasting. Presently, an estimated 650,000 children under the age of five in Syria are stunted. Stunting rates have consistently risen across Syria, increasing from 12.6% in 2019 to 16.1% in 2023. Aleppo reported unacceptably high stunting rates exceeding 20%. Al Hasakeh reported catastrophic levels of stunting exceeding 30%, while Lattakia Governorate reported an emergency-level GAM prevalence of 10 per cent. An estimated 153,000 children in Syria are living with disabilities . Children with disability are 34% more likely to be stunted 53 per cent more likely to have symptoms of acute respiratory infection. While exclusive breastfeeding for infants under six months has shown improvement over the past five years (28.5% in 2019 to 45.5% in 2023), certain parts of Northern Syria have witnessed a notable drop, with breastfeeding rates as low as <30% reported in Aleppo, and Al Hasakeh over the past years. Almost half of the surveyed caregivers confirm the use of infant formula for infants aged 0–6 months (HNO 2024).
ACF Nutrition Strategy for 2024-2025 align with sector strategy, mainly tire 1. Emphasizing life-saving nutrition prevention services, all sub-districts (Severity 2, 3, and 4) and providing curative services for wasting, focusing on children 0 – 59 months and pregnant/lactating women in high-severity (3 and 4) sub-districts. CVA for malnourished WRA and HH with CU5 will also be considered in Severity 3 and 4 districts. Further prioritization can follow tire 2 due to budget limitation. In 2024-2025, ACF's Health and Nutrition projects demonstrate multiple synergies and complementary actions. Emphasis will continue to be placed on ensuring a comprehensive approach to primary care services, including malnutrition assessment, prevention, and management, integrated with infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices and mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). Gender responsive counselling will be promoted, while applying gender lens through all activities. Detecting malnutrition early and expanding community-led screening will continue to be a fundamental aspect of ACF's Nutrition Strategy for 2024. ACF will support the creation of a close mapping mechanism for identifying malnutrition cases and setting up a more robust referral pathway. A thorough analysis of barriers to accessing nutrition treatment services will be conducted, and community-based initiatives will be tailored to address these barriers in all four targeted governorates (Aleppo, Hama, Hasakeh and Lattakia). Treatment facilities will be supported in fixed and mobile settings. ACF will continue its endeavors in updating national guidelines on malnutrition management and providing required technical support and mentoring of national health workers capacities.
Gender sensitive approaches and safeguarding of beneficiaries are integral to ACF programming, with dedicated staff upholding gender minimum standards and mainstreaming safeguarding processes. ACF recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. ACF’s policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

ACF provides compulsory training to each new staff member and refresher sessions to existing staff on organizational ethical policies. Internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power and whistleblowing/reporting mechanisms are followed with employees receiving instruction on these internal policies. ACF employees and any third parties contracted by ACF sign a Code of Conduct and are inducted on its contents. There are various gender-balanced focal points against harassment at both capital and base levels in Syria, as well as a Safeguarding lead for ACF Syria based in Damascus.ACF staff are trained in Gender and Protection mainstreaming. Protection standards are followed to ensure safe access to Nutrition services. Child Protection charters are included in contracts and HR procedures, with zero tolerance for child labour and abuse. ACF upholds the IASC principles on PSEA and has a zero-tolerance policy on SEA. ACF programming such as during assessment, consultation, or feedback mechanisms. All employees are trained in safeguarding and have access to reporting systems. ACF also upholds a multi-modal and accessible Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism, through which people can freely provide feedback to ACF, which is handled according to a stringent internal protocol. Assumptions are related to securing timely approval for proposed projects, including by the concerned Line Ministries. An unstable security situation, restricted access to targeted locations, and lengthy administrative procedures may cause a high impact. Mitigation strategies include advocacy, ongoing context analysis and monitoring, and coordination and access negotiation with local authorities. Additionally, there is a risk of currency devaluation, fraud, lack of data access, and ethical concerns. Mitigation strategies for these risks include close and direct monitoring, training staff members on organizational policies, transparency, and trust-building.
