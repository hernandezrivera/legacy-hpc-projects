Project Summary

This project targets youth and adolescent women and girls aged 15-24 through a community-led education approach. Rohingya facilitators will receive comprehensive teacher professional development through a continuous process that will enable them to undertake the bulk of the teaching tasks. They will then implement the teaching responsibilities through a home-based learning approach, in co-shared learning facilities, and through community engagement activities to meet the unique needs of women and girls.

Needs

Over 900,000 Rohingya refugees currently reside in and around Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. The Rohingya population continues to face significant hardships and challenges, and the scale, severity and complexity of the needs remain overwhelming. Critical services including education for adolescents and youth are insufficient. Women and girls, particularly, have been exposed to sexual- and gender-based violence due to the lack of appropriate shelters, lack of female-only latrines, and damaging coping mechanisms such as child marriage. The preliminary findings from the 2019 Education Needs Assessment underscore that protection risks are further compounded by movement restrictions for female refugees and cultural practices, both of which greatly impact access to education services. While there has been incremental growth in education programming for adolescents and youth, the 2019 Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment estimates that 89% of adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years old don’t have access to any educational activities. This exposes this already vulnerable demographic to increased protection risks. Therefore, interventions must take into account these challenges while also working to strengthen the capacity for Rohingya teachers to deliver quality education programming.

Activities or outputs

Adolescent and youth remain a critically underserved group in the education response. Specifically, girls and young women continue to face barriers in accessing education, challenges that are compounded by a scarcity of available meaningful education opportunities for this age group (15 to 24). A lack of income-generating opportunities for women and resistance from the community on acceptance of girls’ education exacerbates the existing barriers. To successfully address these challenges, the proposed project aims to strengthen the capacity of female Rohingya teachers to ensure quality, community-led education for female youth. Brief overview of the proposed project activities: • Intensive, continuous teacher education training for female Rohingya who are not yet qualified as teachers• Implementation of community-led education for female youth aged 15 to 24• Regular awareness raising and meetings with the communityMany of the Rohingya community, particularly women, have previously been deprived of education while in Myanmar. Education for adolescent girls and young women remains often inaccessible due to lack of cultural acceptance and lack of gender and age sensitive services available. DCA seeks to identify Rohingya women who have a basic level of education but would not yet qualify for the role as a teacher. These women will be trained in an intensive pre-service teacher training as well as ongoing training throughout project implementation. Research indicates that the workshop approach to training has many gaps and that teachers need repeated exposure and practice to new concepts. In order to ensure that the trainings encourage a constant learning process, the crux of the project will focus on reinforcing capacity through longer-term TPD, guided practicums, and mentoring and coaching. The project ultimately will focus on providing evidence-based teacher professional development to better support teachers and effect meaningful change. After successfully accomplishing this training, the Rohingya women will take the lead in educating adolescent girls and young women conducting activities in co-shared spaces and home-based facilities to account for the scarcity of space. The home-based approach is an effective avenue to reach those students with certain disabilities that prevent ease of mobility. Inclusion components are interspersed throughout DCA’s curriculum and awareness on issues of inclusion will play a prominent theme in all teacher trainings. All activities will be aligned with education sector standards and designed to build the capacity of stakeholders such as government educational institutions and NGOs to promote positive adolescent and youth development outcomes. DCA education personnel will continue to participate in camp level coordination meetings and engage with relevant CICs to work together for more seamless implementation. Regular, structured feedback will ensure the quality of the delivered classes. Young girls aged 15 to 24 will then benefit from the basic literacy, numeracy and life skills lessons. Participants in Focus Group Discussions for the Education Needs Assessment expressed frustration with the fact that there is little structuring of curricula for older learners. As a comprehensive, standardized curriculum for youth and adolescents does not exist, DCA developed its own foundational skills curriculum for youth and adolescent girls and women, and is currently utilizing it at camp level. As Bangladesh is a disaster prone country, life skills lessons will include disaster preparedness messages and all teacher professional development opportunities will incorporate messages on DRR. Activities will be complemented by parenting sessions for caregivers and the set up and training of Alternative Learning Committees to ensure community participation, accountability, and capacity strengthening.
