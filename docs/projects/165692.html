Project Summary

The effectiveness of actions in the area of food and nutritional security necessarily requires a thoughtful coordination of the interventions of national and international actors, a clear political arbitration to bring together all the actors and partners involved in this thematic for an effective governance of the food security and nutrition.
The absence of a functional and responsive information system capable to anticipate crises (drought, price increase, etc.) through early warning must be resolved. In this context the CNSA plans to revitalize its information system by setting up an alert system. This project proposal aims to support this approach.

1 - Needs

Haiti ranks as one of the countries with the highest exposure and vulnerability to multiple hazards, including hurricanes, floods, earthquakes, landslides and droughts. After three years (2014 2016) of consecutive drought, Haiti was further devastated by Hurricane Matthew in October 2016. A new episode of drought recently affected the country in 2018-2019. Currently, many Haitians have seen their livelihoods disappear. The situation does not seem to be improving, according to the latest IPC analysis (CNSA, October 2019), 4.1 million people in Haiti were estimated to be in need in the projected situation (February – June 2020). Since June 2017, the food security response continues to decrease even if humanitarian organizations remain on the ground to help vulnerable people across the country, including in urban settings.

2 - Activities

- Ensure a harmonized approach is used for all sector’s partners by providing appropriate tools and capacity building for assessment, analysis of data and monitoring.- Maintain a mechanism for sector partners to share the tools and findings and update assessment registry.- Develop sector contingency plans and report their emergency response.- Promote alignment and harmonization of partners’ geographical targeting and selection criteria with sector guidelines.- Promote harmonization of partner’s assistance/response package as per sector technical guidelines and ensure that early recovery approach is embedded in the response from the earliest phases as a guiding principle of the humanitarian action. - Provide trainings to partners in sector’s related topics, including food security concepts, analysis and programming.- Update WOS 4W regularly by partners for response and gaps analysis.- Provide an inclusive platform for all cross learning initiatives and advocacy (policies and strategies assistance/livelihoods linkages, monitoring, transfer modalities, gender, protection mainstreaming GBV, accountability and other people centric issues).- Ensure the coordination and collaboration with other sectors (e.g. nutrition, early recovery, protection, S/NFI, Wash, CCCM) through inter sectorial working meetings.- Support CNSA to develop and implement an effective food security early warning and monitoring system to guide the sector's interventions.- Support the IPC process. At least two IPC analyzes are planned per year to update the national food insecurity classification and to enable food security emergency response planning or simulation exercises. A CNSA staff will be trained by FAO to ensure the sustainability i.e. continue the activities at the end of the project.
