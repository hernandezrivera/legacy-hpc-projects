Project Summary

Many years of conflict have filled Al Hol Camp in northeast Syria to double capacity — with thousands returning to already unsettled and divided areas of return. An assessment carried out by the partner Khotoat identified that returnee children from Al Hol camp that have returned to Baguz - the last IS stronghold to be liberated in 2019 - are marginalised for their association with the camp, and have severe education needs that minimise their ability to reintegrate into society. There are no specific, concentrated areas set aside for returnees in Baguz (the last IS stronghold to be liberated in 2019), and they have formed no recognizable social clusters.
The purpose of this two-year project is to reintegrate children that have been released from Al Hol Camp into society in Baguz, DZ by providing immediate psychosocial assistance and non-formal remedial education to the most vulnerable children (returnee and local) in Baguz. The project will involve 8 schools in Baguz and include both returnee from Al Hol as well as local vulnerable children to mitigate community tensions. To ensure inclusion and representation of all sections of Baguz, the project will independently engage with all heads of tribes and clans who serve as “guarantors” of the returning families and prominent local professionals. An open and productive partnership with the commins, Education WG and education authorities, as well as PWG/CPWG/ CAAFAG TF to streamline the effort of assistance to will also be integral to the education focus of the project for improved reintegration. This project is a multi sectoral response (see project HSYR22-PRO-188443-1 'Reintegration of Al Hol Releasee women and children in Baguz, DEZ'.)
This project will advocate on behalf of returnees with local stakeholders and work with the education WG and education authorities to ensure that children are better equipped to deal with the needs of the returnee population who are marginalised against, including the provision of psychosocial care to local and returnee children, and remedial education through the SLP (Self-Learning Program) curriculum.
This project will focus on the children of the returnees from Al Hol to reintegrate and access education using a cross-communal approach.
• Up to 150 children between the ages of 6 and 15 who have been unable to successfully reintegrate into formal education as a result of their experience at Al Hol.
• 16 facilitators at partner schools in Baguz. Trained facilitators will set up and facilitate safe spaces in schools where psychosocial assistance can be provided to vulnerable children.
• 6000 students at 8 partner schools in Baguz (including returnees and residents). Students at partner schools benefiting from the use of “safe space" and rehabilitated sports facilities.

3 - Needs

Barriers to reintegration for returnees from Al Hol camp include lack of access to essential services such as education for children, which is compounded by no reintegration facilities for Al hol camp returnees in NES. In Baguz specifically, a CAID needs analysis (implemented by Khotoat with the commendation of the Baguz council with whom they have an established relationship) of returnees from Al Hol Camp identified that their living conditions were so poor, access to food, and basic services was so restricted due to a combination of challenges that returnees face, including fear of the community which has resulted in children not going to school and not going outside. The Syria HNO 2021 reports the the ability to cover basic needs has deteriorated underlining the difficulties or re-integration and securing a livelihood. Loss of income was cited more frequently by returnees than by other groups as an important factor in a deterioration of their ability to provide, linked to a lack of employment opportunities and the increasing gap between price increases and wages which is affecting returnees more severely than other population groups. In parallel, returnee children are not returning to school because of stigma and the need to provide for the family (often single headed female households). This project will therefore take a cross-communal approach to improve children's access to education, as well as provide much needed psycho social support and remedial education services.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will work with eight selected partner schools to set up “safe spaces” to provide psychosocial support to vulnerable children (both returnee and local). due to the lack of appropriate safe spaces, a rehabilitation package will be provided whereby Khotoat will provide minimum rehabilitation and maintenance support to the schools to ensure their safety and to ensure proper provision of safe spaces. Rehabilitation will cover basic toilet maintenance and replacing white boards. This will promote the project so that both boys and girls will attend as lack of separated toilets is a factor why there is less inclusivity among girls. The safe spaces will have 16 facilitators to implement rights and PSS sessions in Al Baguz schools. There will be 2 in each partners schools. An activity coordinator will be responsible for on supervising the implementation of programs within the schools. An advocacy officer will be responsible to leverage knowledge and insights into the needs of returnee children and other vulnerable resident children to advocae for their needs with local governments and the wider aid community in close coordination with Education and Child Protection Working Groups for NES.M&E follow up of the project will ensure that activities are implemented and barriers of groups addressed for better reintegration and social cohesion. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of community tensions. Mitigating measures: PSS and remedial education will also be open to local vulnerable children those that suffer from severe trauma. We have established and developed relationships with the 8 partner schools for the project and will liaise with other actors and the Education WG, Baguz education council to ensure that the most vulnerable attend. To safeguard the beneficiaries and further reduce community tensions, there will also be no branding of the organisations implementing the project at the school. Risk of discrimination/perceptions of discrimination if referral services are provided to specific groups/individuals at the exclusion of others. Mitigating measures: 1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed which will include vulnerable local women in the assistance too; 2. Ensure consultation with local councils, imams, commins and guarantors of the returnees understand the individual community perspectives of the returnees; 3. Carry out safety audits for beneficiaries’ access to locations of services. Risk of abuse and assault from host community. Likelihood: Low. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: Ensure children are never alone when seeking services or travelling to and from the school; Consultations with women and men should be organized among the project and school staff (which will be staffed by women facilitators and councillors.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project has a Community Response Mechanism (CRM) where participants can directly provide feedback or complaints about the school and project activities and staff and systems via email, whatsapp messaging, telephone and designated complaints boxes open to caregivers and children. Continually over the two year project, the facilitators and advocacy staff will be responsible for disseminating CRM cards to beneficiaries and their caregivers and give clear guidelines on why and how all feedback should be reported. CAID and Khotoat's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are in line with the principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Khotoat safeguarding and child protection codes of conduct are vetted by CAID HQ DD team. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate risk analysis and mitigation measures identified together with the partner which includes the promotion of female staff at the school to ensure a space of safety. PSEA focal points will be trained by specialist staff on safeguarding and PSEA measures and appointed to the schools who will engage with the wider PSEA network in NES in liaison with the PWG. They will be responsible for enhancing prevention strategies. Khotoat staff will be trained on checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in activities. Further, all personnel will adhere to and sign the CAID PSEA code of conduct prior to contracting.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk of sexual exploitation and abuse among staff towards beneficiaries. Mitigating measures: CAID Best Practice Guidelines agreed by all stakeholders; Clear anti-fraud, corruption, abuse and harassment SOPs and training; bribery and corruption clauses included in contractual commitments and policies; Sensitize stakeholders and community about PSEA and how to report incidents of harassment SEA, as well as abuse. Lack of buy-in from stakeholders - Identify linkages with other CSOs/ International agencies whereby support can be scaled up for children to access formal education. Identify new sources of services, build relationships between service providers, institutions and the community. Explain to stakeholders importance of reintegration in target area. Difficulty targeting children - Initial outreach will be important to ensure buy-in and the targeted participant groups are included in the program with the support of caregivers. Returnee children are also likely to have connections to other returnee children, which will have a snowball effect for generating participation of the correct target group. Psychological “brain drain” on school facilitators - x 16 facilitators will mean remedial educational will be fast and readily available.
