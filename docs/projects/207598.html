Project Summary

Green Hill (GH) has established a Health Post (HP) in camp 1W (UID# 418) and implementing Community Health Outreach activities in camp 1W and 4 providing continuum of care from community to facility. Through this health post primary health care, health education & counseling are being provided, including but not limited to maternal & child health/sexual & reproductive health (SRH) with a focus on availability of long-acting reversible contraception, communicable and non-communicable diseases, emergency care, immunization, laboratory, pharmacy and GBV/MHPSS services & referrals. All activities will be implemented with a focus on PSEA, accountability to affected populations, protection, age, gender and diversity, disaster risk management, disability and environmental ecosystem with the concept of do no harm. The Health Post and Community Health Outreach program jointly will scaleup the availability and equitable access to a range of preventative and curative services for FDMN and Host Community population.
The semi-permanent HP operates 6 days a week for 8 hours a day. The HP serves approx. 21,000 beneficiaries with staff consisting of a manager, 3 medical doctors, 2 midwives, 2 nurses, 2 medical assistants, 1 pharmacist, 1 dispenser, 1 laboratory technologist, 1 psychologist, and 18 support staff volunteers. In camps 1W and 4, we have 65 trained Community Health Workers/Volunteers providing health education and awareness, referrals with emphasis on SRH services and facility-based deliveries, maternal mortality surveillance/verbal autopsy surveillance, and routine EPI mobilization. We plan to continue the capacity building of the same CHVs in both camps in 2023. Green Hill is the Co-Chair of the Community Health Working Group and an active member of the Health Sector and sub-sectors incuding SRH Working Group and Epidemiological Working Group.
The project has a focus in gender, age diversity and persons with disabilities by including them as a contributor to the project implementation. Patient information at HP is managed through 4Ws, DHIS-2 and EWARS. Ongoing patient feedback is collected as a routine job to measure the quality and patient satisfaction. GH coordinates with the Health Sector, Government Authorities (Civil Surgeon, UH&FPO, CICs, Site Management, RRRC, UNO and DC office), ISCG, relevant cross-cutting sectors, and working groups to ensure required coordination as well as smooth implementation.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

At least one health post is required for a population of 10,000 beneficiaries, whereas in Camp 1W, block A, B and C comprise of more than 17,000 people who are taking service from the health post. Additional gap analysis identified the need for comprehensive health service delivery to improve their health status. The rate of facility-based deliveries in Camp 1W were relatively low and vaccination drop out was high compared to other camps which has been significantly improved by the health post and our community health outreach efforts. SRH Services: Access to essential comprehensive reproductive, maternal, and newborn health services is a major concern in Camp 1W & 4. Community feedback demonstrates that the husbands or in-laws often make decisions for pregnant women. GH will emphasize including the household decision makers in the door-to-door awareness sessions through CHVs. Family planning needs remain unaddressed, particularly for the most vulnerable populations which will be a focus area for GH community health outreach programming and health post. NCD and Specialized Services: Availability of NCD treatment and specialized services (eye-care, geriatric care, oral healthcare and services for people with disabilities and palliative needs) were limited which have been addressed through the HP and community health outreach program of GH. Health Promotion and Health Education: In the past, there were outbreaks of diphtheria, cholera, measles, and varicella. Early health promotion messaging is needed to prevent these outbreaks. Awareness messaging on vaccination, hygiene, nutrition, waste and water management, clean drinking water, etc. is essential to improve the overall health condition. Awareness and Vaccination for Vaccine-Preventable Diseases: Persistent misinformation and misconceptions within the FDMN community continue to influence their healthcare-seeking decisions. There remains a substantial need for ongoing risk communication and community engagement efforts, contact tracing for vaccine-preventable diseases, and the facilitation of referrals for cases and their contacts. Furthermore, the health post has served as a pivotal vaccination center for the vaccine-preventable disease immunization campaign, bolstered by beneficiary sensitization and mobilization through the community health outreach program. Laboratory Services: Quality service delivery and diagnosis greatly depend on the laboratory investigations whereas camp 1W had limitations to provide sufficient laboratory-based diagnosis. The health post established by Green Hill is contributing much to full-fill the gap with additional laboratory services (biochemical tests, automated CBC, RDTs and Microscopic examinations) in coordination with the Health Sector, which has improved the diagnosis and quality of service delivery.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

This project aims to improving camp based coordination with different sectors to ensure the proper service delivery, referral and linkage. MHPSS and GBV services at the health post coordinate with the protection based organizations in the camp level. Nutritional screening is conducted in the health post. Referrals for malnourished children are being made with the coordination of nutrition sector. We also have good coordination and collaboration with WASH and Shelter sectors. This project ensures service delivery to both refugees and host communities with a focus on improving health status of the targeted population. Our community health outreach efforts work to establish proper health promotion, sensitization and mobilization, whereas the health post works on primary healthcare, heath education and family counseling. GH program staff actively engage in the general coordination meetings and established good communication with the camp level government officials as well as other stakeholders. This program aims at continuity and stability by adapting the feedback we get from the community level. The community health outreach program reaches out to the households and community leaders including majhis, imams and other influencers of the community to ensure proper feedback and implement findings for proper adaptation of services. To improve relations with government and other partners we ensure timely report submission, following the government rules and regulations and implementing any new instructions provided by the government. We also maintain good collaboration and communication with other partners by supporting them in any need.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

This project is committed to provide primary and emergency healthcare services, delivering impartial care without distinction, based on need. Ensuring confidentiality, data protection and privacy is crucial to protect individuals from violence, abuse and other problems which has been considered in this project. Green Hill ensures adequate training for the staff to identify and refer cases to social welfare or protection actors using confidential communication and referral systems. To assist the unaccompanied or separated child in need of critical healthcare but without a legal guardian to consent, the project will ensure prior consultation with the child and relevant local authorities. In applying the Minimum Standards, the project has been designed considering all nine commitments in the Core Humanitarian Standard and will respect them during implementation. Feedback response mechanisms are well maintained during healthcare service delivery.

3.3 - Age & Disability

In mainstreaming age and disability, this project aims to employ screening tools like the Washington Group Questions (WGQ)-Enhanced Set or Child Functioning Module (CFM) to identify persons with disabilities, including children. Setting a target of around 12% of persons with disabilities in each activity, providing sector-specific inclusive programming training, conducting accessibility audits, and modifying health facilities to meet universal design guidelines are essential steps where required.. Additionally, establishing a referral mechanism for rehabilitation services, providing customized assistive devices, and prioritizing service provision for women and adolescent girls with disabilities, particularly in sexual and reproductive health, ensure a holistic approach towards inclusivity and accessibility in the project's services.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

This project has a great focus on AAP. Green Hill strongly believes that the affected population should have the opportunity to register complaints, provide feedback and to get a response and include proper mechanisms in this project. During the project implementation, the collected feedback from the affected populations will be analyzed and required actions will be taken on a regular basis. This project will assess potential PSEA risks in program delivery settings and identify mitigation measures. Green Hill also circulates the PSEA Code of Conduct and other safeguarding measures for all staff and reminds them about their obligations. The M&E department is assigned to monitor AAP-related outputs and indicators through the project results framework. Green Hill also involves affected populations in the monitoring process, to ensure active participation as well as effective community feedback system.

3.5 - Is your agency/organization and are your IPs members of the PSEA Network?

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

In a commitment to upholding the highest standards of safety and accountability, the mainstreaming of protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) is a paramount focus in this project implementation. Firstly, being a member of the PSEA Network, this project will ensure that the entire network is aligned with the PSEA principles and best practices. This project integrates PSEA at every stage of the project cycle management, from planning and design to implementation and evaluation, to create a protective environment. A key aspect of this commitment is the mandatory PSEA training, which is provided to 100% of our staff, including volunteers, to enhance their awareness and understanding of these critical issues. This project will ensure that PSEA awareness materials, such as posters, fliers, and videos containing key PSEA messages and information about available Complaint Feedback Mechanisms, are prominently displayed and accessible to the project participants. Finally, this project will provide confidential, accessible, and culturally appropriate Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms, establishing a secure channel for individuals to report any concerns or incidents related to PSEA, thereby ensuring that the safety and dignity of all those this project serves remain paramount in all operations.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project will not make any negative impact on the environment, rather maintaining the clinical waste of health post in a systematic way as well as a proper safety system for the toilet pits, a positive impact will be created. Green Hill planted an adequate number of trees at the health post premises and considered the average flood level during the construction of the facilities to mitigate the upcoming disaster risks.
