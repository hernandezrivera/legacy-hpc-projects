Project Summary

"As of 29th of April, total confirmed cases of COVID-19 in Bangladesh is 7103 and out of them 163 died. Till Today, a total of 21 cases has been confirmed in Cox’s Bazar district and out of them, one patient has recovered (2 from Chokoria, 4 from Teknaf, 2 from Ukhia, 10 from Maheshkhali, 2 from Cox's Bazar town, and 1 from Bandarban district). In total 1031 test has been performed in Cox’s Bazar and at present the lab has the capacity of performing maximum 96 tests per day. The overcrowded and unhygienic conditions increase the potential for the rapid spread of disease and non-availability of basic measures to prevent the spread of the virus will result in a large-scale outbreak in the densely-packed refugee camps. Rohingya refugees in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas who are currently residing in 34 overcrowded, makeshift camps are highly vulnerable to COVID-19. The nearby host community people, especially people living in lower income quartiles in these Upazilas are also vulnerable due to proximity and similar living conditions.
UNHCR indicates that there are more than 31,500 refugees age 60 or older who are at higher risk for severe illness. The refugee population relies on humanitarian assistance and the preparedness for a wide scale outbreak and continuation of lifesaving services is critical. The construction of isolation and treatment facilities for refugees and adjacent Bangladeshi communities has been highlighted as an urgent priority by the Inter-sector Coordination Group (ISCG). Large-scale outbreak of coronavirus in the densely-packed Rohingya refugee camps is highly likely and would lead to thousands of deaths.
IRC is going to establish a 60-bed SARI Isolation and Treatment Center (ITC) as per WHO guidelines within the refugee camp, which will be accessible to both refugees and host communities. The location of the ITC will be camp 23 (Shamlapur). There will be separate beds for women and girls to maintain the privacy and the dignity. Focus will be given on the clients with any disabilities, and also for the vulnerable sex and age group. Any GBV related issues will be addressed by the protection case worker who will be visiting the ITC from the PHCC. Separate Community health volunteers (CHV) will be recruited for the SARI ITC and will be capacitated on Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE), enabling real-time information to community members, overcoming negative perceptions, improving health seeking behavior and compliance with COVID-19 preventative and mitigation measures.
The project will works with and supports boys, young men and adult men increase their understanding and involvement in promoting gender equality. It will addresses the needs of children, adolescents, youth and adults in all their diversity (gender, age, disability, ethnicity and other exclusion factors relevant to the project scope and context) during this COVID-19 crisis time.
The project design is informed by community engagement, collection of feedback, and assessments. The IRC is currently operating one Women safe space (WFS) and one Primary Health Care Center (PHCC) which form the basis for community engagement. During this Covid Period as the community engagement activities are limited due to applied restriction from the GoB, the PHCC CHVs are conducting awareness session and Health Education activities inside the Health Facility. The community leaders (majhi), local camp authorities (CIC) and local administration (sub-district and district administration) are involved throughout the project. The IRC will develop a detailed M&E plan in line with the Logic Model and Performance Measurement Framework of this project. M&E data and information on beneficiaries will be disaggregated (by sex, age and disability and other exclusion factors).
Besides, IRC is also supporting the MoH led Ramu Isolation and Treatment Center by providing HR support (2 Medical Doctors, 4 Nurses, 3 Cleaners, and 2 Security Guards) for three months.

Needs

Recognizing the risks that the COVID-19 outbreak could pose, the Government of Bangladesh and the humanitarian community including International Rescue Committee (IRC) in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh are now delivering a wide array of supports to ensure that health services in place for both Rohingya refugees and the host community are ready. As part of the response plan, the establishment of isolation and treatment facilities continues to be an urgent priority, with planning underway to prepare an initial 1,700 beds across the District. This includes the expansion of capacity in government facilities, including 200 at Sadar Hospital and support to the facilities established at Ramu and Chakaria (50 beds in each facility). In and around the camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf, health partners are now preparing all possible beds in existing facilities (329 isolation beds for potential COVID-19 patients, with 636 general beds still reserved for other health issues, in line with lessons learned from other outbreak responses). The rapid identification of appropriate sites to establish new isolation and treatment facilities is ongoing, led by an ISCG-led inter-agency team and the local authorities. Setting up of 1,000 additional beds is planned on six sites, land availability and operational capacity permitting. IOM, UNHCR, UNICEF, MSF, SCI, IFRC and IRC are taking the lead in setting up facilities in few identified sites.

Activities or outputs

"1.1 Establishment of 60 bedded Isolation and Treatment Centre (ITC). Supplemental oxygen therapy will be provided to patients with SARI (Severe acute respiratory infection) respiratory distress and patient with hypoxaemia. The IRC supported ITC will operate on a 24-hour, 7 day per week basis and will be staffed by IRC staff in line with standard guidelines. Clinical staff (Medical Consultant 1, Medical Doctor 12, Nurses 24, Medical Assistant 8, Midwives 4 etc.), WASH personnel, laundry personnel, security guards, and cleaners working in both the low- and high-risk zones will operate in set hour shifts on rotation, in accordance with IRC global policy.1.2 ITC is equipped with standard Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) materials and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE). 1.3 Provide training on IPC, PPE and Case Management to health care workers as well as for ITC support staff. The ToT will be received from WHO and ToT trainers will cascade these training accordingly. 1.4 Provide COVID-19 case management services for both Rohingya and Host Community1.5 Hiring two ambulances to support referrals of COVID cases from IRC center as well as to support health sector DRU to strengthen referral capacity.1.6 MHPSS counselling services for COVID positive cases in the ITC as well as for facility staff who need this services. IRC will hire two MHPSS counselling during this project period. 2.1 Regular community engagement sessions through IPC and house to house sessions on preventive measures on COVID-19 and to sensitize community people to reduce the stigma against COVID-192.2 Engage the key stake holders to enable security and safety net2.3 Community sensitization for women and men about provision of gender-segregated health services2.4 Recruitment of 10 volunteers who will be working closely with CHW WG to support the risk communications and 4.1 Two Primary health care service centers (in camp 23 & Camp 25) will continue providing essential and lifesaving health services (e.g. outpatient services, inpatient services, SRH services including BEmONC services etc.) 4.2 SRH services including BEmONC services (e.g. ANC, PNC, NVD, FP, STI, PAC, MR, CMR, Referral) will continue from SRH center in Camp 264.3 A total 24 women friendly spaces will continue to provide FP, STI, PAC, MR, CMR, Referral from these centers. 4.4 A total 8 referral hubs will continue to provide emergency maternal and newborn referrals as well as any other emergency referrals from community to health facilities and facility to facility.
