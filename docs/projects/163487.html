Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to Improving living condition, promotion of rights and physical safety to promote dignity and protection of returnees, displaced people and hosts communities South West and North West Regions of Cameroon. The project will provide NFIs and emergency shelter kits for the construction of temporary shelters to a total of 10,000 households (with 51% of women head of households). The NFI distribution will be carried out in synergy with WASH hygiene kit distributions in order to provide a comprehensive WASH/shelter/NFI kit to beneficiaries.
NRC will also support with electronic vouchers for rent when displaced populations are living in rented housing in peri-urban areas in South West and North West, respectively Mezam and Fako Divisions. E-Voucher will be distributed for average 03 months per households assisted.
NRC programme will target IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host community’s households who need shelter assistance to improve their living conditions in the project implementation locations.
For IDPs living in the bush in the South West region, the NFI kits will be easy to carry as they move back to their original homes.
Beneficiary targeting will also aim at reaching the most vulnerable households based on agreed upon selection criteria.
NRC will prioritize the most vulnerable groups that will include young mothers, persons living with disabilities, women headed households, child headed households, orphans, the elderly, and pregnant women

Besoins / Needs

The humanitarian needs in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon are concentrated in divisions such as Fako, Meme, Ndian, Lebialem, Kupe Manenguba and Manyu Divisions in the South West Region and Mezam, Ngoketunjia, Momo, Bui, Menchum, Donga Mantung and Boyo Division, in the North West Regions, Cameroon. To date, the displacement situation has been on the increase with the number totalling up to 536,107 IDP’s in the South West and North West Region as at the end of September 2019. This depicts a 69.8% increase in the displaced population as compared to the 160,000 IDP’s within the same timing of the year 2018. The main subdivisions are already characterized by poor living conditions especially to the population who have fled to the bush. The instability in the area has affected timely humanitarian interventions especially where access to IDP sites in the bushes is hampered by the different security developments which remain harsh and unpredictable. Due to the continuous burning of houses down to ashes and occasionally entire villages in the South West and North West Region of Cameroon, there is an increase in the need for shelter and NFIs for the IDP population who have fled to the bush in search of security. Rehabilitation and construction of new facilities is thus required for displacement affected populations to have access to safe and protected social services. The locations where the IDP’s have fled for safety include areas where they are living with relatives and friends in homes whose space is insufficient to provide the needed separation and privacy between the different ages and sex. In some situations, it is still common to find a home hosting almost 22 persons as depicted by NRC’s profiling data. The same was witnessed in past assessments conducted in July 2018 in Buea, South West Region. The situation is reported to be worse in the bushes where the IDPs’ sleep on the bare ground with no privacy and are exposed to harsh weather conditions and protection issues.Their vulnerability is further compounded by the lack of adequate household items, and the use of the provided plastic sheets (tarpaulins) to act as cladding material, which can easily be cut to allow unwanted intrusions. Besides, the plastic sheet cladding requires replacement after every few months as it is easily degraded by the hot climate. Inadequate lighting at night coupled with weak doors and windows and wall cladding materials for the IDP shelters, increases the risk of theft, night attacks and protection concerns.In addition to limited access to livelihoods, IDPs, and their host / surrounding communities face high unemployment rates remain high and low income low. As a result, most IDP households cannot afford appropriate shelter materials or rent.
