Project Summary

HI began providing services in Cox’s Bazar prior to the 2017 crisis, & has been adapting ever since. Continuing to address complex, acute, & multi-layered needs, HI proposes to foster a safe & protective environment with the provision of specialized & community-based services to Rohingya refugees & host community through a twin modality:
Multi-sectorial (disciplinary) Mobile Units (MU) to address mobility or social barriers. Integrated outreach teams (PSS + Physiotherapist protection officers, sports & recreation officers) deliver home-based services focused on education for beneficiaries & their caregivers on safe, effective identification & safe referral internally & externally for therapeutic exercises & other interventions. Currently, HI has thirteen MU which are teams of trained staff providing direct specialized services (including protection, mental health & psychosocial support, physical/functional rehabilitation, & basic health care/SRH) to vulnerable persons & persons with disabilities in a comprehensive approach.
At HI service centers (static points), teams support beneficiaries with enough mobility to access the facility with rehabilitation, PSS, basic healthcare, safe space.
HI will continue to provide individual life-saving protection assistance by safely identifying, assessment & referring individuals being most at-risk or survivors of violence, including women & girls with disabilities. Within its ongoing activities, HI has established strong referral pathways (using Inter-agency forms as per Sector recommendation) with specialized camp protection actors in each of its activity sites. HI developed awareness messages to address gaps identified on the following topics: Human Trafficking & Exploitation, PSEA, Inclusion, Children’s Rights & Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Violence (included IPV & GBV), Gender Equality & General Protection. HI also actively participate in the PERU teams, are fundamentally intended for deployment during disasters or emergencies in camp settings. If circumstances dictate, PERU teams can act as an integral component of emergency response teams assigned by other authorities. PSEAH focal in HI are responsible for extensive training of all staff & volunteers using PSEA Network materials & recommendations to ensure adequate level of practice within the organization. Inclusive Feedback & Response Mechanism is in place where feedback is regularly elicited through suggestions boxes, a dedicated hotline for host & refugee communities, community discussions & 1:1 interview. A dedicated Focal Point to receive complaints is assigned in the Cox’s Bazar base to coordinate with camp level team leaders to ensure referral for needed services & ensure survivor advocacy as needed. HI is currently implementing four projects that addresses above mentioned services. HI focused on trainings to humanitarian workers on inclusion of persons with disabilities in services provision, to increase accessibility & inclusiveness of the humanitarian response.
HI created & supports community groups (in form of peer support groups) composed of persons with disabilities, to enforce self-resilience & participation/representation to the community. HI has inbuild capacity to respond to disaster which has been demonstrated in recent fire & flood response. COVID19 protection measures will continue to be adopted & its awareness will be prioritized especially for people with limited access to information.
HI works in partnership with the other actors present at camp level, to ensure the best response to the needs reported. HI coordinates with both NGOs and government bodies, depending on the areas of intervention. HI through his Compliance & Liaison department, is engaging with all relevant authorities (RRRC, DC, UNO, DSS, CiC, NGO AB) to ensure timely coordination & proper communication. HI is part of part of GO & NGO coordination groups at DC & UNO Levels.

1 - Identified Needs

In 2017, 721,641 Rohingya refugees fleeing violence and persecutions in Rakhine State, Myanmar, have sought protection in Cox’s Bazar district, triggering the largest and fastest refugee influx into Bangladesh. Over three years after the influx from Myanmar, around one million Rohingya refugees live in extremely precarious conditions in 34 camps spread between Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas in Cox’s Bazar district. As per PWG-REACH study, 2021, persons with disabilities and specific needs in both camps and host communities face the risks disproportionally compared to the rest of the population, given the fact that intersectionality of factors (gender, age, disability, ethnicity, religion, and other factors) lead to further exclusion, discrimination, hence increase vulnerabilities. Rohingya households reported high levels of insecurity related to limitations on movement, discrimination, harassment, and physical violence prevailing in camps. As per the JMSNA, 2021, 10% of the interviewees indicated that the safety and security situation in their area of residence deteriorated, with a 65% of the responders in need of more protection, in particular improved safety and security. In addition, anecdotal information on the deterioration of the perception of the safety and security situation by refugees has been reported by partners throughout the year, based on the activities of consultation conducted with the communities, before and after the lockdown period. (UNHCR 02/2021, 01-06/2021, 07/2020; IOM 01-03/2021, 04-06/2021; IRC 01/2021 03/2021 06/2021 09/2021; ACAPS/2021,). Persons with Specific Needs including single-headed households, elderly, and persons with disabilities, transgender population, and adolescent girls continue to require a community-based approach and individual case management support to respond to their needs. Additionally, social cohesion – already precarious – has deteriorated as tensions rose between the camp and host communities, owing to fears and misconceptions surrounding COVID-19 risks. Containment measures have hampered access to services; safety, mobility restrictions, limited transportation options, and limited access to information presented additional barriers for persons with disabilities and women and girls. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support: Findings from a rapid needs assessment conducted by IOM (May 2020) show that stress (39% of respondents), anxiety (30%), and depression (11%), sleep behavior troubles (31%), and somatic complaints (19%) are faced in both refugee and host communities. Comprehensive psychosocial services remain highly necessary to address the mental distress faced by many Rohingya. As such, supportive community-based mechanisms are needed to develop the community resilience to moderate mental health issues. The COVID 19 situation continued to exacerbate pre-existing protection risks, including neglect, violence, and exploitation and is reflected in the perception by refugees of a deteriorated security situation. Increased safety and security concerns, caused by the socio-economic impacts of lockdown measures and suspended protection programming, accelerated already rising protection risks, including GBV and child protection, while community protection structures weakened and inter- and intra-communal tensions exacerbated. About 33% of refugees reported experiencing insecurity incidents in 2020, compared to 15 percent of 2019 (The REVA 4 WFP 04/2021).
