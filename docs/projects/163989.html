4 - Needs

Gender-based violence, GBV, especially child/forced marriage, sexual harassment and sexual violence (including rape), denial of resources, emotional/psychological violence and physical abuse continue to pervade the lives of women and girls, resulting in very few spaces where they feel safe. Perpetrators are often people known to the survivor; in many instances the same people that survivors count on for their protection and support, such as intimate partners or family members. While the reach of UNFPA's GBV interventions have expanded to more then 1,000 communities across Syria (as of Nov 2019 4W data), this is still only 10% of all communities. Sudden displacements and shifting lines of control continue to put women and girls at risk and women, girls, boys and men in Idleb, Deir Ez Zor, Raqqa, Rural Damascus and Rural Aleppo are likely to require emergency interventions. Deep-rooted social norms, compounded by the length of the crisis and mounting lawlessness in some areas, are normalising this violence, severely affecting the detrimental psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights. Sexual harassment and violence are concerns raised by consulted women and girls that contribute to psychosocial distress and limit their movements. These restrictions inhibit their access to services, humanitarian aid and ultimately curtail their rights. Various means of technology, including social media, are increasingly used to sexually harass, blackmail and exploit, especially targeting adolescent girls and divorced and widowed women. Reported incidents of rape are on the increase, which does not necessarily mean an increase in prevalence but signifies an improvement in the quality of services . Where GBV services exist, women and girls have confirmed that they have a positive impact on their lives with Women and Girls Safe Spaces and Community Well being Centers serving as a place to seek safety and to receive essential life-saving services.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to increase by 25% availability of and access to specialized GBV services across Syria through static facilities and mobile teams. UNFPA will increase its support and services to female headed households and adolescent girls, widows, divorcees and women and girls with disabilities (as those identified as the most at risk of GBV in the HNO) and focus on empowering them through women and girls safe spaces (WGSS), community wellbeing centers (CWC), safe spaces within health facilities and integrated RH/GBV outreach services. Special attention will be made to increase the inclusiveness of GBV services to people living with disabilities. UNFPA will continue to lead the GBV coordination mechanisms and enhance the quality of the GBV response through building the capacity of member organizations, strengthening and updating referral pathways and SOPs. 1.1 / 1.2 Provide lifesaving and quality GBV response services including case management, psychosocial support and reinforce referral pathways throughout Syria. This will translate in an anticipated 775 community reached with GBV response services and 365,000 GBV response services provided. 1.3 The capacity of 950 service providers will be built to ensure quality GBV services are provided in a confidential, safe, respectful and non-discriminatory manner. Services providers will be trained on CMR and case management (both of which includes specific provisions for men, boys and girls having experienced sexual violence), adolescent girl friendly programming and disabilities 2.1 UNFPA will engage in strategies to empower women and girls and prevent GBV, with a focus on changing negative attitudes that foster harmful social norms and implementing the WoS adolescent girls strategy. UNFPA aims to reach 1,250,000 beneficiaries (500,000 women, 375,000 men, 250,000 girls and 125,000 boys).3.1 UNFPA will train up to 1,100 humanitarian actors in GBV risk mitigation. 4. Coordinate the GBV AoR at the WOS level

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Providing GBV services are critical to ensuring survivors are able to recover and heal, if these are not confidential, they may put survivors at further risk of stigmatization or even death. Communities may be resistant to engaging around topics of GBV and resist there being Women and girl safe spaces and ultimately prevent women and girls from attending. As a result, UNFPA carries out thorough risks analysis of its interventions and works with communities to reduce risks of GBV. UNFPA strives to ensure that all services are confidential by building the capacity of its partners on the guiding principles of working on GBV so that survivors of GBV will feel comfortable accessing them but also to make sure that there are no negative repercussions in accessing them. Communities are always engaged when WGSSs and CWCs -which target additionally men- are established to ensure their acceptance. UNFPA is leading efforts to mitigate the risks of GBV. As such UNFPA has organized and facilitated trainings for a variety of sectors to ensure that actions to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV are included in their responses. UNFPA has established strong M&E systems. In addition to UNFPA’s regular M&E standard reporting practices such as onsite visits, through third party facilitators, monthly and quarterly partner meetings, UNFPA conducts quarterly assessments using client feedback forms at health facilities and WGSSs, key information interviews with service providers, and FGDs at WGSSs. Third party monitors are used to verify program quality and implementation through its cross-border operations.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access, permissions and bureaucratic hurdles may slow the implementation however, UNFPA actively works with its national partners to over come these challenges. Assumption that communities will continue to engage in preventing GBV. While GBV remains a difficult subject in many communities, this can be overcome by actively engaging with communities. Another risk associated with the project is mainstreaming fatigue as various projects aim to mainstream GBV, gender, disability and other protection issues. The GBV AoR assumes that this fatigue will occur and has prepared to provide tools, checklists and technical support (Skype calls, email, meetings) to facilitate technical support in developing and implementing mitigating measures across sectors. The GBV AoR, as a mitigating measure, will work with colleagues addressing gender, disability and protection risks in order to synthesize and streamline processes e.g. conduct a FGD that addresses the GBV risks associated with person(s) with disabilities or pilot an indicator that addresses GBV and disability for women, men, boys and girls.
