Project Summary

SAMS Nutrition program aims to continue its focus on strengthening lifesaving preventive and curative nutrition services for vulnerable population groups (Children U5 and PLWs), in Aleppo, Idleb, Raqqa, and Al Hasaka governorates in 13 sub-districts (Sarmin, Ma’aret Tamsrin, Ariha, Dana, Idleb, Afrin, Jandairis, Atareb, Raju, Jarablus, Ras Al Ain, and Tell Abiad) targeting:18,659 males, 33,171 females, <5 females 24,066, <5 males 19,362, <5 Young children 43,428 and PWDs 881).

SAMS seeks to strengthen CMAM/IYCF services and improve their quality, by hiring qualified (Nutritionists and CHWs) at SAMS-supported health facilities, and building their capacities; Provision of outpatient treatment (OTP) at SAMS-supported health facilities; Provision of Inpatient treatment (SC) in two of the SAMS-supported health facilities; Provision of targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP); Provision of community outreach services through the CHWs; Provide emergency IYCF counseling for mothers by trained nutritionists; Disseminate messages on IYCF-E, by nutritionists and CHWs; Establish IYCF corners in SAMS-supported Health Facilities and promote the Baby friendly hospital approach. The targeted facilities are located in Aleppo, Idleb, Raqqa, and Al Hasaka governorates and all of these HFs will also be part of the nutrition surveillance system. For more than four years, SAMS has been applying IYCF-E in its supported facilities. In coordination with the Nutrition Cluster and nutrition actors, SAMS conducted training on IYCF-E for health practitioners and medical staff in the HFs (Pediatricians, midwives, and nurses) and CHWs. Within this program, the outpatient departments of the supported HFs and CHWs will provide nutrition activities that include IYCF components such as MUAC screening to children under 5 and PLW in all health facilities, reach the families with IEC materials, provide preventive and curative nutritional supplies to all health facilities, IYCF counseling, and group sessions. It is important to mention that the CHWs will be working and delivering their tasks separately from RRTs. Where possible, a dedicated space will be made available in the supported HFs for promoting IYCF practices, especially lactating exercises. Private space to conduct IYCF activities, particularly for teaching mothers how to breastfeed or manage re-lactating issues, is provided in some health facilities depending on the availability of space. In smaller health facilities that do not have space available, IYCF activities are conducted in reproductive health clinics or labor rooms.
SAMS will continue to coordinate with other health actors through the Nutrition Cluster, Health Cluster, and the NGO Forum where routine data is gathered and shared through the 4Ws and HeRAMS, and the DHIS2 system. In addition, SAMS is also coordinating with other humanitarian actors on the field level to assess the needs, strengthen referral pathways, and respond to emergencies, among other ways. SAMS will coordinate with UNICEF for the micronutrients and nutrition supplies; and in case of nonrenewal of the XB resolution, SAMS will coordinate with the alternative channel for delivering the nutrition supplies and will try to reach GIK donors that support nutrition supplies.
Moreover, SAMS will closely coordinate to share information about needs, ensure an effective and coordinated response to new emergencies, and harmonize the salary scales and staffing structures across HFs.

3 - Needs

Nutrition status in NWS continues to be concerning, unstable and needs scale up of high impact nutrition interventions to meet the increasing needs especially for early detection and referral for acute malnutrition among women and children, integrated malnutrition prevention interventions focusing on the first 1000 day of life, quality and timely treatment of all forms of malnutrition. The number of malnourished children in NS has increased in the past six months and more children now facing acute malnutrition which is relatively high among IDP children and mothers; chronic malnutrition and stunting are major concerns, affecting annually more than 0.6 M Syrian children and 1 of every 3 displaced children according to Nutrition C & Smart Survey 2022.Project targets 13 sub-districts in NWS&NES (Sarmin, Maaret Tamsrin, Ariha, Dana, Idleb, Afrin, Jandairis, Atareb, Azaz, Raju, Jarablus, Ras Al Ain, and Tell Abiad) The poor nutrition situation is largely attributed to continued exposure of children and mothers to unsafe living conditions, diseases (including impacts of C19 and Cholera threats), sub-optimal IYCF practices, high maternal malnutrition, family separations and poor care practices, and early marriages, causing limited access to health services, and inadequate WASH supply. Surveillance (NWS) 2022 Oct which indicated the most three GAM underline causes to be both Diversity of food problems 92.6% and Meal frequency problems 80.2% and both combined 76.9%

4 - Implementation of activities

Within SAMS plans in 2022-23 are to integrate nutrition services in 20 HFs. The provided nutrition activities include: • Hiring qualified (Nutritionists and CHWs) at SAMS supported health facilities, and building their capacities. • Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) screening for Children Under 5 years old (CU5) and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs). • Provision of outpatient treatment (OTP) for malnutrition cases at SAMS-supported health facilities • Cases management and follow-up cases • Provision of Inpatient treatment in Idled and Raju hospitals • Provision of targeted Supplementary feeding program (TSFP) • Provision of community outreach services through the CHWs which include MUAC screening for CU5 and PLWs, referral of suspected cases, IYCF activities (raising awareness sessions, providing IYCF messages), and follow-up of the referred cases. • Provide emergency IYCF counseling for mothers by trained nutritionists • Disseminate messages on IYCF-E, by nutritionists and CHWs MEAL Plan: Besides SAMS CRM channels, the MEAL team will be responsible for ensuring the quality of Nutrition services provided to beneficiaries by conducting: 1) patientsatisfaction surveys (exit interviews, SAMS incorporated the protection mainstreaming indicators within this tool), 2) spot-checks according to EHSP standards, 3) key informant interviews (KIIs) with local stakeholders including health facility staff, 4) KAP baseline/end-line.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Gender inequality and GBV. Possibility: high. Mitigation: Ensure IYCF programs have private spaces for breastfeeding mothers and help decrease the risk of harassment or violence against females. Train IYCF counselors on how to supportively engage with GBV male and female survivors, provide information in an ethical, safe, and confidential manner about their rights and options to report risk and access care, and ensure referral mechanisms to appropriate support. Provide a confidential environment for at-risk groups seeking information about where to report risk or access care for exposure to GBV. Provide nutritional support to survivors. Ensure locations times and methods of nutrition services are safe and accessible for women and other at-risk groups. This includes distance to be traveled, times convenient and safe, treatment is offered at the same time as children, avoiding long waiting periods, nutrition sites are designed to ensure accessibility for male and female, including PWDs.Risk: Children are not sufficiently protected. Possibility: High. Mitigation: Have Child Safeguarding policies and systems in place and these should be regularly monitored and corrective action taken when needed.Risk: Lack of nutrition supplies as of UNXB non-renewal will affect the nutrition supplies provided to parters.SAMS will coordinate with alternative funding mechanisms to procure those supplies and cross them to Syria, as SAMS can do that.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: Beneficiaries are subject to PSEA risk. Possibility: high. Impact: High Mitigation: The availability of a clear complaint mechanism within the service centers and the definition of it during the definition of the beneficiaries of the services of the Center and explain the mechanism of its flag "complaints box and mechanisms follows the complaints section directly to the private line and mail moderator complaints section and brochures and the mechanism of regular complaints, in addition to the availability ofperiodic training for service providers and ensure supervision of effective technical service.SAMS displays various posters to enhance the awareness of both the health facility staff and the beneficiaries regarding the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), the services provided, the patients’ rights and responsibilities, and the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). The CRM poster includes the complaints/feedback channels (Phone hotline number, WhatsApp, and email), and SAMS installed a designated ‘Questions/Comments/Concerns’ box. Moreover, SAMS developed a short animation video displaying patients’ rights and SAMS’ CRM channels, the video is published via SAMS social media channels.Risk: SEA - Possibility: High. Mitigation: Policies should be in place, practiced, and monitored. Polices should cover standards of behavior for SAMS Male & Female staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability
