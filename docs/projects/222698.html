Project Summary

Amidst the challenges and vulnerabilities facing South Sudan, a nation grappling with profound humanitarian crises, a stark reality emerges: an estimated 9.4 million people, including 4.9 million children and over 300,000 refugees, are in dire need of assistance. These staggering figures paint a sobering picture of a population struggling against the backdrop of conflict, displacement, and scarcity. Disturbingly, UNICEF reports that approximately 52% of girls in South Sudan are married before they reach 18 years of age, while data on boys in similar circumstances was not provided. Gender norms in the region contribute to the acceptance of violence as a means to control female decision-making, mobility, and sexual activities.
A baseline report from the WVI-SS Joining Forces Phase I project in 2023 revealed alarming statistics, with 76% of girls experiencing physical or sexual abuse at home, and a staggering 86.9% of girls getting married before reaching adulthood. The situation is exacerbated by food insecurity, with an estimated 7.1 million people needing food assistance during the lean season from April to July 2024 according to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC). Children are particularly vulnerable, with 1.6 million children aged 6 to 59 months at risk of acute malnutrition, including 480,000 at risk of severe acute malnutrition.
In Northern Bahr el Ghazal State, heavy rains have caused widespread flooding, impacting communities and disrupting livelihoods in areas such as Malual West, Malual North, Malual Centre, Malual East, and Ariath. The flooding has compounded existing vulnerabilities, particularly affecting women and girls who are often unemployed and vulnerable to sexual violence. This increased vulnerability makes them targets for recruitment into armed forces or militia groups, perpetuating cycles of poverty, gender-based violence, forced child marriages, and psychological abuse.
Regions like Panyikang County, Fashoda County, and Melut have been significantly affected by floods and armed conflicts, resulting in mass displacements of host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and refugees. These crises have exposed women and children to exploitation, abuse, and neglect, further exacerbating their already precarious situations. World Vision, operating in Greater Bahr al Ghazal, Upper Nile, Central Equatoria, and Western Equatoria, is implementing multisectoral projects focusing on emergency response and resilience-building to support affected communities.
World Vision's proposed project aims to reach 83,957 beneficiaries, including 50,374.20 females (including 30,225 girls) and 33,583 males (including 20,149 boys), with a specific focus on including 907 girls and 605 boys with disabilities. The project seeks to provide sustainable solutions to the protection concerns of host communities, IDPs, returnees, and refugees, addressing critical issues such as gender-based violence, exploitation, and displacement.

1. Needs

In the midst of ongoing humanitarian crises in South Sudan, where critical needs persist, the Humanitarian Needs and Responses Plan for 2024 projects that 9 million individuals, including refugees, will require vital assistance. The lean season from April to July 2024 is anticipated to see 7.1 million people in need of food aid, with children at significant risk of malnutrition. Shockingly, 1.6 million children aged 6 to 59 months face acute malnutrition, including 480,000 at risk of severe acute malnutrition. Additionally, UNHCR's 2024 estimates project that approximately 520,000 returnees from Sudan will have sought refuge in South Sudan since the onset of the Sudan crisis. South Sudan currently hosts over 500,000 refugees and asylum seekers, with Sudan being the primary country of origin for 95% of refugees, followed by the DRC (3%) and Ethiopia (1%). A concerning 78% of these refugees are women and children, who have endured harrowing experiences of sexual and gender-based violence during their journeys to safety and at various checkpoints. Reports from UNHCR in 2023 highlight instances of harassment, abduction, rape, sexual assault, and exploitation, with a majority of cases reported by women and girls. World Vision International - South Sudan (WVI-SS) has been actively involved in supporting multisectoral programs, including Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and Child Protection projects in Greater Bahr al Ghazal and Upper Nile States. WVI-SS's focus is on safeguarding vulnerable children by providing dignified access to life-saving assistance and aiding survivors of gender-based violence in accessing specialized health care, mental health and psychosocial support, child protection, and GBV services. To achieve these objectives, WVI-SS's strategies involve providing case management services, family tracing and reunification for unaccompanied and separated children, community-based mental health and psychosocial support, strengthening GBV referral pathways, conducting awareness campaigns, training humanitarian workers on GBV integration, and offering GBV services to individuals in transit. Notably, WVI-SS's programming in Renk, Melut, and Twic has reached 500 individuals, providing training, awareness campaigns, and counselling to support survivors and enhance community resilience. WVI-SS plans to establish Child Friendly Spaces to provide recreational activities for children whose education has been disrupted by crises, offer referral, treatment, and material support to survivors of sexual and gender-based violence, and collaborate with relevant authorities to provide family tracing and reunion services. Preventative measures against sexual exploitation and abuse will be implemented, alongside training for healthcare workers, social workers, and community volunteers in GBV and child protection case management. Individual counselling and group therapy sessions will be conducted to aid survivors in coping with traumatic experiences, disseminating mental health messages and promoting early support-seeking behaviors. GBV survivors will also receive financial assistance and training in income-generating activities to support their recovery and self-sufficiency.
