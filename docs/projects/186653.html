Project Summary

This project will improve the nutrition status of children under five (CU5) and PLW/Gs by providing treatment and preventive services. Using CNMs, SC will facilitate identification of acute malnutrition among CU5 and PLW/Gs using mass MUAC screening and family-led mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) approach. CU5 with SAM will be referred to OTPs to be treated using ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF) and routine medications, those with SAM and complications will be treated in the stabilization centres. Access to the stabilization centres from the communities will be supported. CU5 and PLW/Gs with MAM will be treated with locally produced nutrient-dense Tom Brown Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP). SC will support mothers of INFANTS <6 months who are nutritionally at risk through the community management of at-risk mothers and infants (C-MAMI) - an integrated approach to managing infants at-risk of acute malnutrition through assessment and monitoring of anthropometry, clinical feeding, maternal nutrition, and referral of mothers to other services; the intervention will be integrated with MIYCN counseling. SC will also address MIYCN gaps through one on one counselling, mother to mother and father to father support groups and MNP supplementation. SC with EYN and Greencode will strengthen the knowledge among HWs (f/m), CNMs, community and religious leaders, lead fathers and mothers on MIYCN. Father-to-father (FTF) and mother-to-mother (MTM) support groups will be leveraged as platforms for MIYCN behavior change. Further, cooking and food demonstrations for MTM and FTM support groups will be conducted to enhance dietary diversity. This project will mainstream WASH, protection, CVA, and PSEAH to address the underlying causes of malnutrition and also ensure safe programming. Feedback mechanisms will also be put in place to ensure that beneficiaries are able to provide their feedback efficiently.

1 - Needs

The 2020 Nigeria MSNA conducted in BAY states revealed that most households (>90%) had multi-sectoral needs with Borno state being the epicenter of the crises. Based on the assessment, nearly 6.5 million people have acute needs across BAY states with a MSNI score of 3 or 4 severe or extreme severity of needs . MSNA classifications further indicate that more than 2.2 million people face extreme degree of needs and more than 4 million have severe needs in the BAY states. Further, a MSNA-Borno conducted by SC in 2021 indicated that less than 50% of the household met the minimum dietary diversity . Further the Minimum Acceptable diet was also below the SPHERE standards. This is a clear indication that most households do not have adequate food and a diverse diet, with the most vulnerable being children < 5 years and PLW/G. The ongoing insecurity, displacements and population movements in Northeast Nigeria have increased the burden on the existing nutrition services. These have resulted to the adoption of negative coping strategies by the affected households. Further, According, UNICEF FIT Sentinel alert report, September 2021, there has been a deterioration of the nutrition status of children less than 59 months. The global acute malnutrition (GAM) and severe acute malnutrition (SAM) rates have increased by 1.5% and 0.5% from the previous round. The report also reported a higher morbidity such as diarrhea has been on the increase. There is therefore the need to provide live saving interventions that will reduce the level of mortality during the first 5 years of life and also interventions that will focus on behavior change to acceptable practices that will contribute to healthy practices at household level and at the community at large.

2 - Activities

SC will provide treatment interventions for MAM and SAM children in Borno. SC will support 25 OTP and 2 Stabilization centers sites in Mafa, Jere, MMC, Kaga and Konduga LGAs. The OTP component will provide medical and nutritional treatment for severely malnourished children 6-59 months with MUAC <11.5 cm and/or bilateral pitting edema (+; ++), who have an appetite and are free of medical complications through weekly monitoring visits. The SC Nutrition team will provide support supervision with LGA officials. To ensure that quality treatment for SAM is available at OTP sites that operate within existing health facilities, SC will build the capacity of health facility staff (MOH) to deliver CMAM, and to ensure that an adequate supply is available. Children with SAM admitted to the OTP will be monitored on a weekly basis using MUAC and Weight. The stabilization centers will treat children SAM and medical complications while the OTP centers will treat children without medical complications. Services will be provided 24/7 to ensure that treatment is continuous until the medical complications are treated. SC will also admit infants <6 months who are malnourished in the stabilization center. MAM cases will be managed using the Tom Brown- nutrient-rich supplementary food for children with Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) children between 6 and 59 months of age and pregnant and lactating women. Using the locally available foods, caregivers of children presenting MAM will participate in weekly meetings and supported by a Lead Mother, they will make the premix to take home and prepare Tom Brown for their children. Children with MAM will be identified through MUAC screening and their caregivers will be enrolled in Tom Brown groups, comprised of ten mothers each. During the weekly meetings, the MUAC of children will be monitored and it will also be an opportunity to provide IYCF group counseling. Participants will be put on the program for 8 weeks after which they will graduate if they have shown successful recovery (MUAC over 12,5cm). SC team, EYN, Greencode and government officials will monitor activities. This project will target MAM children 6-23 months by providing micronutrient powders (MNP) supplied by UNICEF. Micronutrient deficiencies, also known as hidden hunger, are highly prevalent in malnourished children. The national protocols for MNP supplementation will be adhered to. Caregivers will be encouraged to add MNPs to complementary food to improve their nutritional value. MNPs will be provided for the 8 weeks that the child is in the program.Caregivers (f/m) and PLW/Gs will be sensitized on gender-specific MIYCN practices through MTM and FTF support groups in accordance with C-19 adaptations. SC will also provide IYCF support to all caregivers of children with SAM and MAM through one on one counseling.SC will support nutritionally vulnerable girls and boys U6M and their mothers through C-MAMI approach - an integrated approach to supporting infants U6M at-risk of acute malnutrition. To improve early identification and treatment, CNMs (f/m) will be trained on screening and referral and HWs (f/m) will be trained on screening, assessment and providing support and referral (to infant feeding programmes, integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI), MHPSS). The intervention will be integrated with MIYCN counseling; referral to other sectors will be promoted for continuum of care.SC will link households with SAM/MAM children to the CVA interventions that are supported by SC and further where possible collate with other partners in intervention locations To ensure that program decisions are made based on need, SC will regularly analyze program data, conduct nutrition assessment like SMART surveys and KAP surveys. SC will integrate will mainstream protection, WASH , CVA in its interventions to address underlying causes of malnutrition

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SC will sensitize nutrition staff, health workers and CNMs on Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA), child safeguarding, child rights violations, psychological first aid, SGBV, child safeguarding (CSG), referral pathways as well as on reporting channels. This is to enlighten them to identify children at risk, including survivors of SGBV, refer them to appropriate services and effectively develop a child safe programming approach in nutrition programming. Furthermore, SC will conduct risk assessment are conducted for locations where nutrition activities are implemented to ensure OTP/TSFP centres are free from harmful substances. Where possible, SC will also ensure that the of beneficiaries needs are met within their community to reduce distance travelled by them hence reduces protection risk which can occur through trans-community migration. Also, SC has an existing reporting platform that enables that every case is reported on time and acted upon. Our accountability mechanism provides beneficiaries with a safe, transparent and confidential system of information sharing that will encourage the participation and empower beneficiaries. The Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM) has clear, transparent procedures and provides a safe, confidential channel for children and communities to voice their complaints and feedback on issues concerning the organization, the project and project activities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SC will apply a range of mechanisms including SC’s Accountability Framework and the Core Humanitarian Standards to ensure that accountability to the affected population is upheld. SC will ensure that information about planned activities will be made available to target communities through appropriate media (verbally and written in local languages) and in good time for active participation. We will also create safe and accessible opportunities for communities – including children – to participate in programming decisions and provide with feedback throughout the project cycle such as: toll-free hotline, community meetings, focus group discussions (FGDs) help desk and child participatory activities, strengthening community-based groups to gather feedback, suggestion boxes and routine monitoring. Program staff and volunteers will be sensitized on their responsibilities in collecting and reporting community feedback in real time. To ensure confidentiality, transparency and that feedback is closed or resolved, SC has put in place Protocols on complaints and feedback handling. SC’s accountability mechanism will provide beneficiaries with a safe, transparent and confidential system of information sharing that will encourage the participation and empower beneficiaries. The Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM) has clear, transparent procedures and provides a safe, confidential channel for children and communities to voice their complaints and feedback on issues concerning the organization, the project and project activities. SC will ensure that complaints are resolved within 7 working days providing appropriate responses to the complainants’/feedback providers with except of complaints that require investigation.
