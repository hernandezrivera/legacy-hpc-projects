Project Summary

PUI has an extensive experience in documenting and responding settler violence (SV) and related military operations, which has been increasing in the last three years. The purpose of the project is to actively facilitate the participation of communities to ensure their contribution to the methods of mitigating and/or preventing the risk of settler related incidents and thereby support the resilience of the most exposed communities. Needs have been identified to strengthen, improve and increase awareness among exposed communities on accessing legal aid, reporting and documenting settler violence incidents, increase the community participation and feedback on the type of responses needed, accountability from the international community towards the affected communities and on methods to hold duty bearers accountable on IHL and IHRL violations in relation to settler related incidents. The project will target 354 direct beneficiaries, but the entire population of the 53 targeted communities are estimated to be indirect beneficiaries.
The action will contribute to the protection cluster strategic objective SO1: “Increased respect for International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL) and accountability for violations” and will be both remedial and environment-building in nature.
The settler violence community based awareness and participation project contains three layers of action designed to support the inter-cluster mechanism led by OCHA, which was established with the support of PUI and OHCHR, to trigger and coordinate protection as well as material responses to SV incidents.
The first part of the action will focus on strengthening policy and advocacy efforts in this sense through the collective work launched recently under the Working Group of Settlement Practices and Policies co-chaired by PUI and OHCHR, under the lead of the Protection Cluster. The second part will supplement the first part by strengthening alerting skills at community level to support the inter-cluster mechanism the ‘OCHA settler violence tracking tool’. The targeted communities are considered to be heavily impacted by settler violence, but without having incidents comprehensively triggered and recorded in the tracking tool either because of normalization of the violence, because of lack of awareness and knowledge on how to document settler violence or because the incidents mainly takes form of harassment and intimidation not leading to property damage or casualty. The communities targeted are either located close to new outposts that are thus expected to face severe threats of settler violence and military related operations, or they are communities identified by key stakeholders as highly affected by settler violence, but with a very limited documentation of incidents in the inter-cluster tracking tool. Identification of needs for capacity building and activity implementation will be planned and coordinated together with community members, putting great attention to the community representation reflecting the diversity of the community in age, gender and diversity. The Action will specifically emphasize women participation in identifying protection threats, and in decision making processes on designing collective strategies, including ways to tackle specific risks faced by most vulnerable groups such as children.
The third part will target clusters of communities identified as highly affected by SV “so-called hot-spots” and exposed to the same sources of threats arising from specific settlement and outposts. The project will facilitate joint discussions on the threats of SV (similar in types and origin) - to foster joint reflection on possible methods of mitigating threats and holding duty bearers accountable.

1 - Needs

The project is designed based on needs assessments conducted by PUI during the last year and will complement the extensive work done in the last 5 years tobuild the network of Community Eyes Watch currently covering 71 communities in theWest Bank and under handover to the Colonization and Wall Resistance Commission(CWRC). PUI updated its settler violence survey in the summer of 2018 and reviewed therankings of 281 communities to list the most at-risk communities as identified by keyStakeholders. In June 2019 PUI conducted three regional meetings with main stakeholders monitoring and responding to settler violence. The meetings covered the vulnerabilities of communities across the WB. The list of most exposed communities, when triangulated with incidents documentation from sources such as PUI Early Warning System (implemented under the WB Protection Consortium) and the Protection of Civilians database managed by OCHA, indicates gaps in incidents reporting from communities highly affected by settler violence. The targeting will also include communities identified as in proximity to new outposts mapped by the Applied Research Institute–Jerusalem and the recent report of Peace Now: “Return of the Outpost method”. PUI’s past and current experience shows a crucial need to improve the representation and engagement of communities exposed to settler related incidents to ensure the core protection mainstreaming principles in the process of mapping protection needs and possible ways of mitigating and preventing these and their consequences. This lack of engagement has been further confirmed and reflected during focus group discussions (FGDs) conducted all over the WB since August 2018 and continuously in 2019 to date 17 communities. A participatory community approach designed to identify threats, vulnerabilities and capacities, and design protective measures to build on the capacities at community level and mitigate the threats and vulnerabilities. These FGDs have confirmed the need for activities specifically targeting the most vulnerable groups and the mitigation of their exposure to settler violence. Currently, most responses to settler violence focus on households or single communities, but the clustering methodology has been identified as an approach that will improve the cooperation between communities exposed to the same sources of protection threats. This will allow communities to share experiences, lessons learned and develop effective strategies to strengthen their resilience and collectively engage in ways of holding stakeholders accountable for IHL/IHRL violations and for them to contribute to advocacy strategies. The Action will ensure an inclusive approach of diverse members of the communities and the participants will engage in analysing vulnerabilities of gender, age and diversity. In order to actively work with communities on improving the equal access to information and decision making the Action will emphasize the active participation of women and men as well as all age group. Both children and youth are in particular vulnerable to settler violence. Many communities see patterns of settler violence targeting schools as an example and when settlers enter communities, youth are often the first on spot to prevent the settler's entry. Settlers are often accompanied by soldiers which brings great risk of casualty and violence and following search and arrests by the Israeli army. Furthermore, isolated vulnerable households are more likely to be attacked by settlers and most often in times where only a few people are around, in many cases only lonely women, elderly people and children. As stated in the ToRs, endorsed by members and the ICCG WB, the Working Group of Settlement Practices and Policies has identified the need for joint efforts to analyse settlement related threats, in order to provide strategic direction for HCT advocacy, feed the HNO and HRP, as well as for inter-sectorial responses through the ICCG.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Community outreach to 30 pre-identified communities with the purpose of increasing the community participation in identifying needs and improve the accountability towards highly affected communities but with little or no reporting of settler related incidents in the OCHA tracking tool. Meetings will be held with the communities to introduce community members to protection principles, presentation of settler violence trends and objectives of the project. Community representatives will be nominated ensuring a diverse representation in age and gender including persons with and without disabilities, and they will discuss protection threats for their community and needs of support in terms of awareness raising for community members or types of assistance coordinated through the OCHA tracking tool. Activity 2: Awareness raising sessions will be conducted for the selected community members and a number of teachers from each community (to ensure that the knowledge will be passed on through the school system as well), covering the subjects of accessing legal aid in context of IHL/IHRL violations and displacement, reporting and documentation of settler violence incidents, settler violence trends and settlement expansion trends, analysis and observations, tailored to accommodate the needs highlighted in the initial meetings. The teachers will be included for improved awareness not to support the triggering system, since the Education Cluster is working on a separate system with DCI recording incidents linked with the education sector that will also feed the PoC. Sessions will be facilitated by PUI team and Al Haq will provide trainings on legal aid, IHL/IHRL and documentation and reporting on alleged violations. The training will involve 5 members per community and 2 teachers (total target: 210 community members/teachers). PUI and Al-Haq plan to build on already existing means for referrals within the inter-cluster settler’s violence triggering system, so the community members trained in legal awareness and in reporting and documentation of alleged IHL/IHRL violations will be included in the WhatsApp group established by PUI and already being used by OCHA sub-offices as one of the sources of information on incidents occurring in the West Bank. Activity 3: Joint resilience building activities for clusters of communities (11 clusters pre-identified including 36 communities). Workshops will tackle the need for joint and collective efforts from communities facing similar threats (in their types and origin) to share strategies to prevent and mitigate the risks and consequences of settler violence and related military operations and hold duty bearers accountable. Initial discussions with community representatives will focus on identifying key members across communities to participate in workshops. Participants (4 members per each community, divided in 11 clusters total target 144) will jointly identify collective needs, preferred responses and ways to strengthen resilience across the cluster of communities. Activity 4: The project team will support the clusters in designing strategies, plan implementation, and support with resources by establishing connections with relevant stakeholders or train community members in tools to build advocacy campaigns and communication strategies. Additionally the Action will have an increased focus on intimidation and harassment and the effects of the communities. Besides having communities carefully reporting on all such incidents, Al Haq will complement by monitoring emblematic cases to highlight and further explore this aspect of settler related incidents. Activity 5: Beyond coordinating regular and ad hoc meetings, along with OHCHR, PUI as co-chair will contribute and facilitate other members to provide the deliverables of the subgroup identified (i.e., quarterly updates, factsheets, policy brief and other materials).

3 - Indicators

A monitoring and evaluation plan will be developed at the beginning of the project to manage the process of assessing and reporting progress against project targets. This plan will describe the whole M&E system, including indicators, tools, breakdown of responsibilities and communication channels within the organizations and between them.Monitoring visits will be conducted by PUI M&E team to follow up on the outcome and impact of project activities. A complaints and feedback mechanism will be set up to make sure that the opinions of target population are heard and taken into consideration.Complaints and suggestions related to the design or the implementation of project activities will be timely responded to. Focus group discussions and/or semi structured surveys will be used to assess the impact of project activities by the end of project implementation. Indicator 1: (outcome indicator) 80% of supported communities increase their rates of SV incidents reporting Indicator 2: (impact indicator) 75% of the community-cluster participants report improved coordination between communities exposed to the same sources of threats. Indicator 3: (outcome indicator) Each cluster of communities targeted by the project implements at least one key action of their designed collective strategy to address the threats of SVIndicator 4: (outcome indicator) All meetings and capacity building sessions include a diverse representation of the communities Indicator 5: 80% of communities/project beneficiaries reporting that they have meaningful access to participation. Indicator 6: 80% of communities/project beneficiaries reporting that they feel involved in the way the humanitarian assistance is conducted.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The primary concern of the Action is to respond to the needs of the population affected by protection risks, expressed and identified during consultations, needs assessments, as well as through focus group discussions (FGDs) and surveys. The Action is designed to increase the participation of highly affected communities by settler violence to improve the accountability towards their needs and priorities. During the entire action PUI and Al Haq will ensure the continuous collection of feedback from the targeted communities during the implementation of the project through PUI Feedback & Response Mechanism (FRM). Information cards on the mechanism will be handed out during the initial community outreach and at the beginning of each training and awareness session. Feedback will be collected on regular basis throughout the project, through verbal communication in the field, or text messages, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards), and responses will be provided for each in a timely manner. Where necessary, adjustments to the implementation of activities may be made based on the received feedback. Feedbacks may originate from beneficiaries, non-beneficiaries impacted by PUI / Al Haq interventions as well.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

While the proposed intervention falls under the protection sector, specific attention will be given to ensuring the fulfilment of the four protection mainstreaming principles in order to ensure the best “good programming” standards:1. Do no harm principle, promoting safety and dignity: for each phase of the proposed intervention, PUI will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks associated with the action. This will be done through assessments, continuous evaluation and consultations with the communities. Special attention is especially placed on the identification on participants to the awareness sessions where CBOs and PDOs are consulted to insure best possible inclusion, and to include different and diverse perspectives on protection risks.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In line with the strategy of community participation and commitments to “Do-no-harm” the focus on a diverse inclusion will support the meaningful access of a diverse representation of the 53 communities throughout the Action. Through the FRM, the advocacy and capacity building components of the proposed intervention, and through regular community consultations (FGDs, etc.) with a specific focus on vulnerable and possible marginalized groups, PUI will contribute to the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights and contribute to the project at all stages of the implementation.Throughout its intervention, PUI will pay specific attention to possible specific needs connected to gender, age and/or persons with and without disabilities and will ensure their equal representation in all proposed activities. Opportunities for community representatives to express their specific perception of the settler related incidents, through advocacy efforts for instance, will contribute in providing a voice to marginalized groups/communities which will also strengthen the outcome of the action as well as the broader analysis of the phenomena of SV, and to provide specific evidences to feed general advocacy efforts.The action will put special attention to create a safe and dignified environment during all activities as well as attention to any forms of exploitation or abuse. A safe environment to nurture constructive dialogues and discussions will be guaranteed through the different activities and also the possibility of all participants to take an active part in the activities.
