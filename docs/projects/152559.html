Project Summary

Nile Hope seeks to implement GBV prevention and response and by providing quality and timely appropriate case management and psycho social support to GBV survivors in in Uror, Leer, Akobo and Nasir Counties of Jongle, Uppernile and unity States. This project is designed to improve responses to cases of GBV, enhance the ability of survivors to recover from GBV, and make progress towards changing the social norms that perpetuate GBV, with the intent of fully mitigating the effects of GBV and preventing future cases. It will provide GBV response services and address critical gaps in information and knowledge among women and girls on GBV response, which limit access to services. The project will focus on provision of Psycho social support for women and girls through cases management in women and girls friendly spaces. This will be in line with minimum standards on case management. Access for services for survivors will be promoted through community wide campaigns on availability of services. Women will also be provided with some little cash for income generating activities to support their healing process and resilience. GBV risk mitigation will also be conducted through joint safety audits with different clusters to identity risky areas for women for redress. Therefore, the entire project will address the GBV issues affecting both Women and girls in the Communities. Through this project Nile Hope will target a total population of 10968( 6268 Women, 1200 Men, 1500Boys and 2000Girls) direct beneficiaries ion the all the project locations that is Leer, Akobo. Uror and Nasir of Jonglei, Unity and Uppernile states.

Needs

As the country yearn to return to stability Leer, Uror, Nasir, and Akobo Counties of Unity, Jonglei and Upper Nile States. Counties will reverse gear to become the hubs of return and repatriation routes from the refugees and IDPs camps as they have served the exit routes to refugee and reception camps. The issues of tribal violence mostly perpetrated by youth have not been addressed well, especially between the communities. Exacerbated by dismal function of lack of law enforcement institutions, coupled with unruly armed youth, and the impeded traditional leadership and customary law, communal revenges over past killings, cattle rustling between communities, clan feuds, etc remained potential threats to many lives of women and girl and a genuine concern to the at risk communities. The situation remains unpredictable in Jonglei state, Upper Nile and Unity States with reported humanitarian response disruptions resulted into mass population displacement. The number of IDPs increased from 1.85 million in December 2016 to 1.9 million in December 2017. The number of IDPs was highest in May with over 2 million. In Leer, a SMART survey conducted in Leer by Medair in April 2018, Leer county remains a concern more so because immediately after the assessment fighting broke out again resulting in loss of lives and livelihoods, displacements and disruption of humanitarian services, women and girls are highly exposed to sexual violence as most them were survivors of gang raped by the armed actors and killing of the civilians. The islands where most people hide have poor hygiene and sanitation challenges and morbidity is a going concern. Culturally, women are charged with the responsibility of taking care of the whole family and are exposed to high risk of GBV because of spending all the time trying to fend for the family, fetching firewood, water and going for food distributions. . In mid-April the SPLA advanced from Yuai into areas previously controlled by the SPLA-IO in northern Jonglei and ultimately gained control of Waat by 26 April (According to protection trend 2017). This advance resulted in large-scale displacement, with 102,625 civilians displaced to Lankien and payams in Uror, Akobo, and Duk counties. IDPs fled to areas off of main roads that were perceived to be safer. Smaller numbers IDPs went to the Bor POC in addition to Ulang, where IDPs began arriving by foot in May. Onward movement of the population toward Akobo was compromised by increased inter-communal attacks between the Murle and Nuer, scarcity of water along the route, and expense of travel. With this high displacement of the population from their origins, Akobo is currently hosting more IDPs which results into Host community and the IDPs sharing scarce resources, as result of this, women and girls are exposed to high GBV risks while accessing where they could find food, firewood and water collection points. Nile Hope needs to runs GBV services in Leer , Nasir, Uror and Akobo and will ensure that those affected by protection risks and threats are provided with timely protection response and prevention services through a multi sectorial approach with other sectors; Health and Education( involve the women and girls in PSS activities) to ensure that survivors needs are met when referrals are done, this will be achieved through monthly coordination meeting where referral pathway will be enhanced to ensure the needs become effectively addressed, we shall have an in-built Log frame that will be the focal reference for measuring project progress and results; this will be coupled with additional tools and approaches including FGDs with community beneficiary members, case study profiling, interviews with key informants and community feedback engagement forums. The field staff led by the Field Coordinators and with the support of the MEAL Officer and Programs Coordinator will be vigilant to track the implementation process and the timely realization of results.

Activities

Activity 1.Provide comprehensive quality and timely services provision to individual of GBV survivors including management of child survivors. Nile Hope will quality of case management through training and coaching of case managers, social workers and community outreach workers on the guiding principles of GBV, PFA and referral pathway to enable them respond to the cases of GBV survivors. Activity 2.Provide psycho social support to GBV survivors using the appropriate case management tools, make appropriate referrals and document using Gender Based Violence Information Management Systems (GBVIMS). The psycho social support will target individual and groups psychosocial support activities which includes skills and life skills building activities for women and girls. Activity 3: Establishment of women and girls friendly spaces in the conflict affected locations to enhance accessing of GBV services to women and girls. Nile Hope will establish new WGFS in the new locations and will refurbished the existing WGFS where we already existing CentresActivity 4: Train front line service providers (Case managers, police, community leaders, community support teams) on GBV, referral pathways, survivor centred approaches and psycho social first aid.Activity 5: Conduct regular community mobilization and awareness raising consequences of GBV, information dissemination on availability of services and importance or benefits of accessing GBV services within 72 hours and community awareness raising and sensitization on women’s rights. Activity 6: Conduct GBV assessment and quarterly coordinated safety Audits to identify the protection risks and concerns for women and girls and disseminate the findings and recommendations with concerned actors and sub clusters to mitigate the identified risks and plan on how to address these challengesActivity 7: Establish community based mechanisms to provide protection assistance and psycho social support to women, girls and vulnerable people affected by the conflictActivity 8: Establishing and supporting of referral pathway mechanismsActivity 9: Produce, Print and distribute IEC and visibility materials material including messages on how to use the content of the kits (promoting positive health habits), where to access GBV servicesActivity 10: Procure and distribute women and girls psycho social items (bed sheet for embroidery, cotton thread wool, and beads)Activity 11: Establishment and strengthening of women’s participation to the protection coordination structures Activity 12: Provision of Cash for IGAs for protective purposes for persons of concern (including CBi)
