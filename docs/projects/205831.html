Project Summary

The project will seek to provide access to safe, inclusive, and quality basic education to vulnerable children, out of school children and youth aged (3-18) in South Sudan for the year 2024. The project will be implemented in Akobo, Nyirol, Bor South counties of Jonglei State, Maban county of Upper Nile State, Kapoeta East and Magwi counties of Eastern Equitoria State; and Abyei Administrative Area. The education project will target a total of 27, 301beneficiaries (24,360 learners, 3,398 B & 10,962 G; 1,575 Teachers, 866 M & 709 F; 1,365 PTAs/SMCs, 751M & 614 F). The project goal will be achieved through three objectives:
The first objective: Emergency-affected children and youth have access to safe, protective, inclusive education services that are appropriate to their age, learning level and need. This objective will be achieved by supporting the enrollments of 24,360 learners (3,398 B & 10,962) in pre-primary, primary and secondary schools/learning centers; establishing & rehabilitating 49 temporary learning structures for the children affected by various emergencies; constructing 49 gender segregated WASH facilities in schools and learning spaces; conducting 7 BTL campaigns to support in enrolling out of school children in 7 counties; mapping teacher/tracking for re-integration into education system to support continuity of education in 7 counties; establishing child help desks in 105 schools; establishing and support CP/GBV community protection committees in 7 project targeted counties; Providing and distributing sanitation and hygiene materials to 105 schools; and providing of dignity kits to 10,500 girls in schools.
The second objective: Children and youth are provided with quality teaching and learning services that support retention and progress through the education cycle. This objective will be achieved by training 1,575 teachers (866 M, & 709 W) on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children; providing to 4,128 (2,270 B & 1,858 G) emergency affected children quality education recreational materials; providing 21 education kits to leaners in schools; training 210 (105 M & 105 F) teachers on MHPSS to support mental health healing of learners; and supporting catch-up classes in 105 schools and learning centres affected by various emergencies.
The third objective: Coordination and crisis management capacity is strengthened to promote efficient, accountable, and timely education in emergencies response. This objective will be achieved by training 49 (28 M, & 21 W) SMCs/PTAs and education authorities on school management, governance, psychosocial support and Disaster Risk Reduction.

1. Needs

According to HNO 2022, 3.6 million people, 97 per cent of whom are children, are estimated to need education services. 2.8 million Children are currently estimated to be out of school. Furthermore, gross enrollment fell by about 7% between 2020 (2,727,209) and 2021. (2,523,204) (HNO, 2022). The IPC classification and inter-sectoral classification classified Akobo, Nyirol, Bor South, Kapoeta East, Abyei administrative area, and Maban as geographical areas of high priority with high level of severity, targeted for humanitarian intervention for Education in Emergencies services; through a multi-sectoral approach to address the holistic needs of vulnerable children (South Sudan IPC classification 2023). The Education Needs Assessment (ENA) conducted in 2021 indicates that in targeted locations, 8% of schools were non-functional, of non-functional schools; most (61.3%) were in rural areas which majorly was attributed to flooding, looting by armed group and insecurity in or around schools hence contributing to high number of school drop-outs and out of school children. Out of the functional schools, 29.3% head teachers reported missing at least one week of education in the Academic Year 2020/2021 due to insecurity in or around schools and unpaid teachers. Of the schools that reported on their accessibility, the majority are between 1-2 hours walking distance in the dry season from the nearest county capital and most schools can be accessed by foot, motorbike, and car in the dry and rainy reasons (though less schools reported being accessible in these ways in the rainy season). In terms of accessibility for Children with Disabilities (CwDs), 40.9% of the surveyed schools reported that their classrooms were not accessible for CwDs.The South Sudan HNO indicates that more than 3.5 million people, of whom 2.9 million are children and adolescents, including those with disabilities, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation. According to CPIMS+ report 2021, the number of documented child protection cases increased from 26,009 (12,128 girls and 13,881 boys) in 2020 to 30,441 (14,185 girls and 16,256 boys) by the end of 2021, UASC, child neglect, child labor, domestic violence, and child trafficking (CPIMS+ report, 2021). The ongoing displacement caused by multiple shocks in the country, including conflict and climatic vulnerabilities; has given rise to new patterns of risk among children posing risks of family separation, physical and sexual abuse, violence, and vulnerability to recruitment by armed groups, among others, the destruction of property due to these crises, have weakened the protective environment, undermined resilience, and hampered recovery; with less than a quarter of schools having a functioning general protection referral mechanism. FSNMS+ report stated that children associated with the armed forces and armed groups have experienced multiple human rights violations or abuses and the initiative to release children from armed forces and groups have been inadequate (FSNMS+ Report, 2021). Teenage pregnancy is estimated to be 30% among girls aged 15 to 19 contributing to high drop-out rates, the ability to cub teenage pregnancies is worsened by the contraceptive prevalence rate for all methods at 6%, with modern methods accounting for 5%, and the unmet need for family planning is estimated to be 29.7% by 2020. The ENA also indicates that early marriage equally contributes to high dropout rates of girls in schools. According to a report authored by UNICEF, young people living in targeted locations are among those most at risk of the impacts of climate change, threatening their health, education, and protection, (UNICEF report, 2021). Schools' functionality has also been severely impacted by the country's consecutive three years of flooding, impeding learner access to school and closing over 190 schools due to flooding, according to the Education Needs Assessment, 2021.
