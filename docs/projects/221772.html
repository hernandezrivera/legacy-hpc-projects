Project Summary

The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1
million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer
elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June
2025, out of which 650,000 are cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition
(SAM). About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women
(PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of
acute malnutrition in the same period. An estimated 67 percent
of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states
of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. Major driving factors of acute malnutrition among children in the State are poor food consumption, high disease prevalence and feeding practices are related direct causes of malnutrition. The communities of Panyinjiar and Rubkona Counties of Unity state have experienced unprecedented humanitarian crisis which has been heightened by the recent heavy floods that has seen more than 50% of the households displaced, loss of harvests, destruction of farmlands and livestock that has plugged these counties into severe food insecurity that has led to high prevalence of malnutrition. The emergency nutrition response is targeting children under five years, adolescent girls and women most especially pregnant and lactating mothers from internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host communities in Panyinijar and Rubkona County with chronic emergency nutrition needs. The planned nutrition intervention activities will as well be integrated with Health, WASH, Education, FSL and GBV prevention interventions by creating synergies and coordination with Health, GBV and WASH, FSL and Education clusters through Health, GBV and WASH, Education and FSL implementing partners so as to prevent and reduce malnutrition related morbidity and mortality by reducing the prevalence of global acute malnutrition in children under five years and pregnant and lactating mothers (PLWs) in Panyinijar and Rubkona County. Therefore, the proposed project activities and targets will be including prevention of malnutrition through, provision of MIYCN counselling to 12,000 caregivers of children under five years, provision of micro nutrient supplementation (Vitamin A) to 15,000 children 6-59 months, deworming for 10,000 children 12-59 months, malnutrition screening for 15,000 children 6-59 months and 8000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW), conducting 12 monthly monitoring and supportive supervision visits, provision of capacity building through on job training and mentoring for staffs, provision of hygiene and sanitation messages to the target beneficiaries, establishment and maintaining of demonstration kitchen gardens at 5 nutrition sites, conducting GBV, conducting cooking demonstration sessions at the nutrition site and community, supporting 60 community nutrition volunteers to undertake home visits, provision of long lasting insecticide treated mosquito nets to 13071 SAM and MAM children and 3000 PLWs with acute malnutrition among others in the project period.
The project intends to support nutrition needs of 25,000 beneficiaries from most vulnerable (IDPs), returnees and host community beneficiaries of Panyinijar and Rubkona County majorly children under five years, adolescents and women mostly pregnant and lactating mothers. This project comes at a time when Panyinijar and Rubkona County is struggling with continuous inter communal conflicts causing internal displacements worsening the vulnerability evidenced by high rates of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) due to food insecurity caused by lack of enough food stocks among the internally displaced and returnee community increasing the vulnerability among the host communities and high food prices due to poor access to markets and inflation. 2. Project activities

1. Conduct early detection of malnutrition through nutrition screening at the nutrition site and community level and treatment of girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition though establishment of four nutrition sites per county. 2. Provide micronutrient supplementation to the under five children (Vitamin A) at every nutrition site during nutrition visits and growth monitoring. 3. Provide behavioral change messages to the mothers and care takers of malnourished children with maternal infant and young child nutrition (MIYCN) counselling to improve the nutrition at household level. 4. Conduct cooking demonstrations to the beneficiaries so as to improve on the nutrition diversity and nutrition status at household level. 5. Conduct deworming for children 12-59 months with albendazole tablets to prevent iron deficiency anemia due to worm infestation. 6. Conduct monitoring visits to the nutrition sites to make sure nutrition services offered are of quality following the ministry of nutrition guidelines. 7. Conduct testing and treatment of malaria for acute malnourished children under five years so as to reduce the prevalence of malaria among malnourished children. 8. Distribute WASH kits to the caregivers of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) children to improve on their hygiene and sanitation conditions at household level to reduce the prevalence of diarrheal diseases among these children. 9. Establish kitchen gardens at every nutrition site for demonstration to the mothers and care takers of malnourished children so as to improve the availability of kitchen gardens at household level hence improving food security and dietary diversity. 10. Stimulate play for acutely malnourished children by provision of child play materials at every nutrition site. 11. Conduct Gender based violence safety audits at every nutrition site to make sure child protection and gender-based violence mitigation measures are guaranteed. 12. Establish feedback mechanism. 13. Treat girls and boys admitted with acute malnutrition with diarrhea treated
