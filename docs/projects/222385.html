Project Summary

This project is seeking to respond to the critical integrated lifesaving, Nutrition, health and food security needs of the IDPs, returnees and host communities in Renk, Twic and Ulang Counties; in Upper Nile and Warrap States and Abyei administrative Area targeting a total = 66,347 Ulang= 44,212 and Twic = 97,742 and Abyei= 47,466)
Beneficiaries. Of these total targets MAM under 5= 72,674, MAM PLW= 38,703 and SAM under-five 45,868. All three counties and Abyei Administrative Area have a GAM rate above the WHO 15% percent thresh hold (Renk= 25.1%, Ulang= 26.4% and Twic 25.3%. all three counties and Abyei Administrative Area are categorized as IPC phase 4 and most of them are intersectoral priority 1 except for Ulang which is priority 2. The main objective is proving critical lifesaving nutrition support to vulnerable populations affected by multiple crisis and vulnerability with a focus under-five, pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) and adolescent, with the goal of reducing malnutrition prevalence rates amongst children 6-59 months and pregnant women (PLWs), providing life saving nutrition interventions to crisis affected population.
This will be implemented by GOAL as an integrated package of the Nutrition, health, WASH and the Food security and livelihood interventions. This will contribute to the overall objective of the nutrition sector and aligned with the South Sudan ministry of health strategic plan. This project is seeking to create a nexus between emergency, transition, and sustainable nutrition outcomes.
The proposed response aligns with the HRP 2025 priorities for Nutrition, that recommends multisectoral approach to tackling the causes and predisposing factors of acute malnutrition.
Strategies and the interventions will meet both immediate and lifesaving needs among affected populations while strengthening local capacity to deliver high quality service and contributing to reducing dependencies on food assistance as well as fostering longer term behavior change outcome.
GOAL currently have nutrition grants in Renk, Ulang and Twic and is seeking to build on those to reach more communities with an integrated package of interventions that will help to address the root causes of undernutrition. The plan is to integrate the health interventions in the same area. GOAL will capitalize on findings from formative research to propose evidence-based strategies and intervention with high impact but cost-effective interventions for greater positive impact.
This project will be implemented in all Payams in Ulang, Renk, Twic Counties and Abyei AA. GOAL is the lead Nutrition partner in three counties and implementing integrated health and nutrition activities. GOAL will leverage existing relationships with the CHDs in all the proposed areas and existing grants like the health Sector transformation Project (HSTP), UNICEF and WFP Nutrition grants.
GOAL is going to work closely with the County Health Departments in all counties and their respective State Ministry of Health making sure the capacity of these institutions is built as part of the system building resilient and sustainability. Key activities will include providing integrated preventive and curative nutrition services targeting the most vulnerable groups including responding to severe and moderate acute malnutrition and improving maternal infant and young child feeding practices. leverage on support of other sectors to provide a package that is not only cost effective but also high impact. This project will cater for people affected with the Sudan crisis, supporting nutrition screening for early case identification, referral and management of malnourish cases; integrate surveillance of any notifiable diseases through the training of the nutrition workers and volunteers on the case definition for any disease that may have reach an epidemic thresh hold; enhance the triage to optimized infection prevention and control.

1. Needs

Malnutrition remains a persistent problem in South Sudan, with millions of children and women still suffering.According to the South Sudan Nutrition Cluster HNRP, malnutrition crisis in South Sudan will continue to deteriorate in 2025, driven by multiple, converging humanitarian crises. Approximately 4.3 million children under five, as well as pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, will need life-saving treatment and preventive care. This includes 0.65 million children under five at risk of severe acute malnutrition, 1.4 million at risk of moderate acute malnutrition, and 1.1 million mothers requiring urgent treatment (IPC-AMN 2024). In all four location the , acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15%, with one county in Upper Nile projected to reach IPC-AMN phase 5. By mid-2025, it is estimated that 80% of children under five are anticipated to be at risk of acute malnutrition, with 7.69 million people, including 1.5 million young children, facing severe food insecurity. Repeated outbreaks of fever, diarrhea, and respiratory infections, coupled with poor nutrition, water, and sanitation access, add to the crisis. The situation is further aggravated by economic challenges, climate impacts, ongoing conflicts, heightened GBV risks, and spillover effects from the Sudan crisis. According to the October 2024 IPC result, the food security situation in Warrap State Upper Nile is expected to deteriorate even further during the lean-season (April-July 2025), with an estimated 790,000 people (59.2 percent in Warrap) facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food 21.8% in IPC4. Similarly in Upper Nile during the same period an estimated 1.02 million people (65.1 percent of the state’s population) likely to be in Phase 3 or above, of which 10,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5), 330,000 people will likely be in Phase 4 From the onset of the fighting in Sudan 15th April 2023 September 2024, the Government of South Sudan and humanitarian agencies recorded an estimated 629,000 South Sudanese nationals crossing into South Sudan from Sudan. 80% of these returnees have crossed into the country at the Joda point of entry in Renk County in Upper Nile State.An estimated 2.1 million children aged 6 – 59 months are suffering or expected to suffer from acute malnutrition and will be in urgent need of nutrition services from July 2024 to June 2025. This shows a 20.4 percent deterioration of acute malnutrition compared to 1.66 million registered in 2023. The total number of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM)and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) cases are estimated at 650,000 and 1.43 million, respectively. The analysis was conducted FSNMS survey, SMART surveys, and program admission trends. An estimated 67 percent of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unityand Warrap. Contributing factors of acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases with 49.4 percent of children reported to have been ill two weeks prior to the FSNMS Round 30 assessment Diarrhea, fever and cough were the leading symptoms reported at 18.3, 38.0 and 20.3 percent, respectively. Poor access to sanitation and drinking water compromises hygiene practices and increases the risk of high incidences of illness leading to acute malnutrition. Only 9.9 percent of the population reported having access to improved sanitation facilities and 74.3 percent reported using open defecation. Overall, 63 percent of the population reported having access to improved sources of water leaving 37 percent of the population depending on unsafe sources. Inadequate feeding practices of infants and young children are other high-risk contributing factors to acute malnutrition. All Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) indicators remain suboptimal at national level, making them another high-risk factor for acute malnutrition.

2. Project activities

The project activities are aligned to and contribute to the cluster nutrition cluster objectives and they include: Conduct MIYCN counselling seasons in all the nutrition sites using IYCF charts provided by MOH/UNICEF/WHO/WFP to caregivers and engage community gate keepers through inter-generational dialogues sessions. Train care givers on family MUAC screening. Conduct community or household level/Family MUAC screening of children 6-59mnth for acute malnutrition and refer to nutrition sites. Support community outreach, community screening for malnutrition for early identification and referral. Provide protective ratio to household with malnourish kids Build the capacity of nutrition providers to provide integrated management of malnutrition (IMAM) and sessions not covering not only the generic training on IMAM.Admit and treat all children with SAM in IMAM program Treatment of Moderate acute malnutrition in children 6-59month. Admit and treat all children with MAM in CMAM program. Conduct training to all the nutrition staffs on CMAM program. Support to VASD campaign, additional budget to supplement VASD NIDs campaigns. Treat Moderate acute malnutrition in Pregnant and Lactating women. Conduct GBV safety audits in all the nutrition sites in in the three Counties. Conduct training on protection/GBV to 130 nutrition staffs in across the three counties. Conduct malaria testing using RDT for children with SAM and MAM in all the nutrition sites. Integrate child stimulation and early childhood development services in the provision of care for children with acute malnutrition. Collaborate with health cluster to ensure prevention, screening and treatment of malaria and management of diarrhoea at the nutrition service delivery. Work with the WASH Cluster to ensure provision of WASH kits to children with acute malnutrition and sensitize communities with hygiene and sanitation messages- soap and improvement of WASH and IPC in Nutrition facilities.Deliver blanket supplementary feeding programmes to vulnerable childrenSupport micronutrient supplements to young children and women. Work with the Protection Cluster to ensure mainstreaming of services and referral of child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV) cases identified in the nutrition sites.Collaborate with health cluster in the management of diarrhoea, by providing Zinc and ORS to children with diarrhoea. Conduct regular program and field monitoring and joint supportive supervision to strengthen evidence-based decision-making.Support coordination meetings with all key stakeholders, This project will be integrated with Health,, WASH, Food Security and livelihood Cluster. With health, the project will seek to integrate with ongoing health programs that GOAL is implementing in the same area but also trying to implement nutrition activities in facilities where health is implemented. Specifically, we will integrate with EPI, maternal and child health intervention, deworming, SRH especially looking for opportunities to integrate family planning intervention during nutrition activities. With FSL, we will integrate on the establish kitchen garden demonstration in all the nutrition sites. Conduct cooking demonstrations in all targeted sites. Conduct sensitization to make raise community awareness of systems in place for representation of their different interests and needs. Establish QAAP committee in which MtMSG are part of it. Explore opportunities to improve infection prevention and control and WASH in the targeted facilities, including improving the water reticulation in the facilities. Establish a community complaint and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities, initiating community focus group discussions with members of the community. Provide training to the nutrition program staff to dignified service provision to nutrition beneficiaries using the QAAP approach. Raise awareness and provide infor to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement.
