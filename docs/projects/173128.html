Project Summary

The overall objective of the project will be to contribute to the stabilization of the
South West area, enhancing the access to local basic health
care services. Specific Objectives: 1) Improved health
services (infrastructure upgrade, including WASH interventions and strengthened capacities
of health staff) contribute to ameliorate life quality and
expectancy Specific Objective 2) Promote the
collaboration between local actors in order to foster better
public services and promote social cohesion/inclusion

1 - Needs

Health care system in Libya has always presented several weaknesses such as high dependence on foreign health workers, marginalized health services in some areas, weak Primary Health Care network, lack of effectiveness, transparency and accountability in government policy. Since the 2011 revolution and subsequent 2014 civil war, public services have never really resumed to an acceptable quality standard due to continuous instability, decline of national revenues and absence of effective public administration. The ongoing conflict has worsened the situation with mounting health needs and the direct targeting of healthcare professionals had led to foreign medical staff leaving Libya. The availability and readiness of various health care programs are seriously limited especially due to lack of qualified staff (43%) lack of resources to pay for care (37%), lack of equipment and supplies (32%), according to the findings of 2020 Multi-Sector Needs Assessment for Libya conducted by REACH. The most affected areas were found in southern Libya including Wadi Ashshati (in Barak occur hospital closure), Murzuq and Sebha. Sebha has less severe shortages, but it needs particular attention due to the fact that has to overcome the gaps of the surrounding areas. The hospitals are overwhelmed with trauma patients, which often limit the access to other patients in need of clean surgical facilities (i.e. pregnant women). Women are particularly affected by the current political context as well as by the cultural one. Critical specialized services such as sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services are weak across the country on multiple issues, for example, only 4% of all public health facilities are able to offer a diagnosis of cervical cancer. In Murzuk and Sebha, it is estimated that 1/5 of deliveries are assisted by relatives or friends. Many households face one member with chronic illness, while the national figure is 27%, it reaches 60% in Murzuk and 80% in Sebha between returnees. Many of them have limited or no access to medicines: 64% in Murzuk, 93% in Wadi Ashshati. Severe shortages of medication affect the public sector and many are obliged to buy medicines in private clinics at a very inflated price. Many people cannot afford to buy them. Treatment and storage of dead bodies awaiting identification is not sufficient to guarantee respectfulness and collection of all relevant data. As far as the Health Facilities concerned, main problems are: 1. Maintenance works have not performed since a long time, generators and pumps are not regularly verified and different degrees of renovations are necessary. Particularly problematic is the situation at the Al Mahdia Hospital where sewage water interfere with clean water. Moreover, some facilities are not suitable to ensure access for disabled people, for example the second floor of the Main Lab in Sebha; 2. Essential medicines are running low in the hospitals’ pharmacies as not regularly refurbished by the government pharmaceutical store. Chemical and reagents are needed in the Main Lab in Sebha, as well as consumables (bandages, syringes, needles, IV, etc.); 3. Personnel has not been supported and regularly updated and there is lack of knowledge and equipment for specific conditions on most recent treatments and technologies

2 - Activities

A1.1 Technical Needs Assessment: it will be carried out in order to update the final list of equipment, rehabilitation works and training topics. It will also outline the conflict sensitive approach to be followed; A1.2 Procurement, supply and set up of medical equipment: based on the assessment, the necessary medical equipment will be purchased, delivered and installed and staff trained on its management and maintenance; A1.3 Rehabilitation works of Hospital Facilities: based on the assessment, the necessary rehabilitation works will be carried out. During the rapid assessment, it was identified the need of improving especially in WASH (toilets, water sewage, sanitary sewers, medical waste management) A1.4 Specialized cascade and on-the-job training for medical and paramedical staff: addressed to health workers and medical staff, topics will include: Safety and Environmental sustainability of the hospitals to enhance their capacities to handle and prevent nosocomial infections, protection of patients and staff, disposal and management of medical and non-medical waste, correct use of chemicals, reagents and radiation in Labs; training on patient’s clinical management in emergencies, especially maternal care and neonatal resuscitation. (SO2) A2.1 Set up of a Multi-Stakeholders Group: the group will be composed by local authorities and local CBOs and will have the task to define the local health/awareness strategies; A2.2 Awareness initiatives: will focus on a) the new health services available thanks to the action; b) the importance of inclusive health services; A2.3 Final Conference
