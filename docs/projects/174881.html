Project Summary

The ongoing Anglophone crisis in the NW/SW regions of Cameroon has caused many individuals including women, men, children, and persons with disabilities (PWD) to be displaced to various locations in search for safety and security. Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and their hosts especially in hard to reach areas have right to live in dignity, protection and security as well as receive humanitarian assistance on the basis of need, without discrimination and exposure to further harm. According to UNICEF reports, there is limited access to safe water, and sanitation facilities by affected persons especially IDPs in hard to reach areas in the Menchum and Mezam divisions of the North West Region.
This project aims at ensuring equitable access to WASH services by IDPs and their host communities in four communities in the Menchum (Zaah-biya, Menkaff) and Mezam (Baba 2 and Alateneng) divisions. Specifically the project targets affected women, men and children by carrying out hygiene and health promotion, rehabilitation of water points, construction of gender sensitive latrines and distribution of WASH kits. In the course of implementing this project, aspects of protection, gender based violence and child protection shall be mainstreamed.

1 - Besoins / Needs

According to Cameroon NW/SW Response Dashboard November 2020, the conflict in the NW/SW and the COVID-19 pandemic has had a major impact on the WASH sector. Access to water is a key challenge both in terms of quantity and quality. According to MSNA (2019), only 50 percent of households in the NW/SW regions have access to improved sources of water. Almost 61 percent of households do not have access to functional latrines. In November, 12,000 people received WASH and hygiene kits, compared to 35,300 in October, while people who accessed improved sanitation facilities from WASH partners decreased from 9,000 in October to 6,400 in November. Access to improved water supply was lower in November with 5,900 people benefitting compared to 10,000 beneficiaries in October. Lack of funding remains a major setback in WASH partners’ ability to meet set targets. From the above statistics, IDPs and their host communities are in need of WASH facilities. As more IDPs settle in these localities, there is pressure on available WASH facilities such as water, and sanitation facilities (toilets). As a result of inadequacy, open defecation is rampant leading to the spread of diseases like cholera. The fact that there has been reported cholera outbreak in the SW is a reason to prevent as this disease can be transmitted through affected individuals travelling. The humanitarian response through this project will help address the needs of the IDPs and the host communities in the aforementioned localities in the Menchum Mezam Divisions.
