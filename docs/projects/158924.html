Project Summary

The project will be contributing to the current emergency response projects that MAP is implementing in coordination with the health cluster and stakeholders and will be in line with the
Strategic objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL and
Health cluster strategic objective #1: Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to vulnerable communities in Gaza and West Bank/EJ, including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building.
In Gaza, health related needs continue to be driven and exacerbated by insufficient access to health care due to a combination of chronically depleting medical supplies, insufficient human resources, and imposed access restrictions, including for patients referred outside of Gaza. Meanwhile, the constant surge of GRM casualties has created a double-burden on MOH and health cluster partners.
It was anticipated that the GRM demonstrations will scale down during 2019. However, the reality and the political atmosphere dictated that this would not be the case. The Friday demonstrations are still happening and the influx of injuries is still increasing.
According to the latest reports, more than 34,000 have been injured while 18,549 were transferred to the MOH and NGO hospitals.
Moreover, the lack of medical supplies, including diagnostic and therapeutic, alongside limited referral mechanisms, are the primary reasons for interruption in the treatment of chronic diseases who often get neglected in light of the GRM events since 2018.
Untreated non-communicable diseases lead to serious complications such as renal failure, disability, and amputations and may lead to death at the end.
In Gaza, interrupted treatment is common factor for cancer and kidney dialysis with 55% and 50% of medicines respectively depleted by end of Sep 2019. Those patients are considered to be at highest risk of complications and death.
Furthermore, the health cluster reported in 2019 that the provision of essential life-saving drugs, disposables and lab reagents should continue. No matter how much organizations intervene, CDS still reports almost the same levels of shortages range among the first 9 months of 2019 from 45-51.9% for drugs and from 22-28% for disposables.
Consequently, MOH is still in dire need of drugs and disposables to be able to save the life of Palestinian patients in Gaza. MAP is planning to purchase a list of essential drugs and disposables to be delivered to Central Drug Stores and prepositioned in order to ensure the access of trauma and non-trauma patients to the treatment and medical care they need even in time of emergency. The project is aiming to help mitigate avoidable mortality and morbidity among the trauma and non-trauma patients.
MAP will make sure that all the items are provided to all patients attending MOH facilities regardless their gender and age.

1 - Needs

In the wake of the ‘Great March of Return’ demonstrations since 30 March 2018, Gaza’s already overstretched health sector has been struggling to cope with the mass influx of casualties that exceed 34,000 (MoH and HC, Sep 2019). As reported by Health Cluster, 18,549 of injuries were hospitalized. Consequently, the stocks of drugs and medical supplies have significantly depleted and access to healthcare for trauma and non-trauma patients is being compromised putting patients’ lives at risk. To go deeper into the analysis, in August 2019, 51% out of the essential drugs list, were reported at less than one-month supply at MOH Central Drugs Store (CDS), out of which 225 items are totally depleted (90%), representing 44% from the essential drugs list. Additionally, 28% out of the essential medical disposables were reported at less than one-month supply. This is considered to be truly staggering keeping in mind that the health system is already collapsing in the wake of the now 12-year blockade, the deepening intra-Palestinian political divide, the energy crisis, inconsistent and reduced payment of public sector staff.It is worth mentioning that according to MOH in Gaza, only 29 types out of 65 cancer drugs are available in its stocks till Sep this year, i.e. suspending the treatment protocol of 8000 patients, in addition to the acute shortage of the essential drugs for the kidney dialysis patients that reached 50%, leaving the life of at least 1000 patients with chronic kidney diseases at risk. Those patients with life-threatening diseases or chronic illnesses who need maintenance medications, are highly affected by the shortages. Importantly, they face a high risk of complications from medical conditions that may deteriorate while they wait for drugs to be supplied, or for referrals and access permits to be approved.Now one would wonder why the levels of shortages remain the same since start of GRM despite the donations to CDS. According to reports and statistics shared by CDS; the donations were primarily for trauma and EDs while neglecting the NCD and non-trauma categories striking a weird balance in the levels. Striking a balance of supporting trauma and NCD patients is crucial at this stage not to further exacerbate the suffering of any patient. Subsequently, and following the continuation of the demonstrations beyond the initially expected period, as well as the volume and gravity of injuries; MAP is seeking to procure more items of essential drugs and disposables to continue supporting MOH and to ensure the availability of drugs and medical supplies along many stations of the trauma pathway for the affected GRM patients and for non-trauma cases regardless of their gender or age.

2 - Activities

- To procure and deliver the list of essential drugs used for Trauma cases- To procure and deliver the list of disposables used for trauma cases- To procure and deliver list of drugs used for non- trauma cases (oncology, kidney dialysis, cardiac diseases, etc...)- To procure and preposition items in coordination with MOH to be released during crisis times. Accountability:MAP has recently been active in terms of applying CHS standards as we are a member of the CHS alliance and currently preparing for the assessment and we have a dedicated officer who follows up on accountability issues with the beneficiaries. In this case too, CDS can come back to MAP with any complaints or comments about the drugs and disposables provided to take actions with suppliers. In addition, MAP applies rules and regulations in the contracts signed to make sure that the MoH and CDS are benefiting to the maximum limits in terms of coverage of test costs, and the provision of free samples for analysis. In addition, MAP tends to procure items with 75% of it's shelf life . To ensure the safety of the propositioned items, MAP has an open contract with Al Awda hopsital to store MAP"s medical items in good conditions and also tend to release the propositioned items at least six month before its' expiry date if the items were not needed for emergency (though it's rarely happening). To add, the CDS collaborates and participates with MAP in every step of the project from the design phase (identifying needs) to implementation (technical committees, selection, etc). Protection:MAP has always been promoting for impartiality, and neutrality. While the GRM patients are predominantly males, the services we provide are accessible to all inside the health care facilities without any discrimination. MAP is also independent and not influenced by any commercial power, or political party.

M&E:MAP has been releasing and prepositioning drugs and disposables, even NFIs, since 2008 and was able to respond to the crises in Gaza in 2008-2009, 2012, 2014 and to the natural disaster in between. MAP has a proven and strong record for being one of the best respondents during emergency situations as evident in situational reports by the health cluster, and the MoH's records of respondents to emergencies. This all does not work without a strong M&E system in place. For this type of action, MAP has many staff members collaborating to ensure immediate response to emergencies, the programme manager, the programme officer, the programme assistant and the procurement officer. All collaborate with the finance officer to ensure sound and correct financial reporting. This is all done under the supervision of the director of office. MAP uses logframes to track the implementation of the project via indicators and base line data. MAP uses quarterly reports, internal correspondence and financial reporting as forms of M&E. MAP also performs regular visits to the locations of intervention, Al Awdah warehouses (where MAP preposition its' items) and Central Drugs Store, for this specific action, and keeps track of the activities implemented. MAP will also use the records from the cluster and the MoH as proof of its activities in this instance. CDS will also indicate to us the numbers of patients that will benefit from the provision of the drugs and disposables.
