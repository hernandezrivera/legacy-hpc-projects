Project Summary

The proposed project adopts a comprehensive approach to address critical humanitarian challenges in NE Syria for 24 months, focusing on protection, psychosocial well-being, and resilience-building initiatives. Aligned with the principled response to achieve protection overarching objectives, the project aims to establish 2 community centers equipped with integrated community based protection and Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services. These centers will extend outreach through mobile teams to reach vulnerable populations in informal settings, addressing gaps in assistance, including violations related to housing, land, and property (HLP).
Specific objectives include improving access to quality protection and MHPSS services, directly addressing challenges such as restricted resource access and protection risks. The project's holistic approach encompasses legal advice/counseling and cash for protection interventions for all affected population including the most vulnerable groups.
Additionally, structured awareness sessions, community-based educational campaigns, and information provision aim to enhance understanding of protection risks and mental health issues. Youth-driven initiatives focusing on social cohesion promote unity within diverse communities. The project also strengthens the capacity of humanitarian actors, engages with local organizations, and fosters community-based protection committees to build technical capacity and advocate for inclusion.
In summary, the proposed project's comprehensive interventions aim to effectively respond to critical humanitarian needs in NWS. Through tailored approaches, it seeks to provide essential services, empower communities, and promote long-term resilience in the region. Additionally, it places emphasis on peace-building interventions, coexistence initiatives, and transitional justice measures to foster sustainable peace and reconciliation.
Needs assessments:
The project is built on specific needs assessments conducted by Bahar and it is based on the MHPSS consultation research conducted by WCH.
(M&E) mechanism
The proposed monitoring and evaluation (M&E) mechanism for this project adopts a multifaceted approach to ensure accountability, learning, and continuous improvement. The monitoring strategy encompasses regular field visits, standardized data collection tools, feedback mechanisms, activity logs, progress reports, and partner coordination meetings. Through these measures, project staff can closely monitor the implementation of activities, assess progress, address challenges, and maintain alignment with partners and stakeholders. Key components of the monitoring mechanism include conducting regular field visits to assess progress and identify emerging risks, establishing feedback mechanisms to solicit input from beneficiaries and stakeholders, and maintaining detailed activity logs and progress reports. Additionally, regular coordination meetings with implementing partners ensure alignment of activities and facilitate knowledge sharing.
The evaluation strategy involves conducting baseline and endline surveys, outcome mapping, case studies, stakeholder interviews, focus group discussions, beneficiary feedback surveys, and mid-term and final evaluations. These methods enable the project to measure changes over time, assess the impact of interventions on protection outcomes and psychosocial well-being, gather in-depth insights into stakeholders' experiences and perceptions, and identify lessons learned and recommendations for future programming.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Protection Risks Analysis:Access and Security Risks: Operating in NE Syria, particularly in conflict-affected areas, poses significant security challenges. The project must assess and mitigate risks related to access to communities, staff safety, and the security of beneficiaries.Ethnic and Sectarian Tensions: Given the diversity of communities in NE Syria, there's a risk of exacerbating tensions or conflicts, especially if interventions are perceived as favoring one group over another. Strategies to promote inclusivity and neutrality are essential.Vulnerability of Mobile Teams: Mobile outreach teams may face risks while reaching vulnerable populations in informal settings, including potential exposure to violence or harassment. Adequate training and security protocols are necessary to safeguard team members.Data Privacy and Protection: Collecting sensitive information during legal advice/counseling sessions poses risks related to data privacy and protection. Safeguards such as anonymization and secure data storage are crucial to prevent unintended harm.Gender Discrimination: Protection services must address gender-specific risks and vulnerabilities, including discrimination and gender-based violence. Ensuring gender mainstreaming in all project activities is essential to mitigate these risks effectively. GBV Risks:Power Dynamics: GBV risks may stem from power imbalances within communities, households, or institutions. Awareness sessions and community-based initiatives should address underlying gender inequalities and power dynamics to prevent GBV incidents.Community Stigma and Barriers to Reporting: Cultural norms and stigma surrounding GBV may discourage survivors from reporting incidents or seeking support. Strategies to overcome barriers to reporting, such as providing confidential and accessible support services, are crucial.Protection of Vulnerable Groups: Women, children, and others may face heightened risks of GBV in crisis settings. Tailored interventions and specialized support services are necessary to address the specific needs of these vulnerable groups.Livelihood and Economic Empowerment: Economic instability and dependency can exacerbate GBV risks, particularly for women and girls. Cash for protection interventions should be complemented with livelihood support and economic empowerment initiatives to reduce vulnerability to GBV.Capacity Building and Coordination: Strengthening the capacity of local organizations and community-based protection committees is essential to effectively prevent and respond to GBV. Coordination with existing GBV response mechanisms ensures a comprehensive and coordinated approach.
