Project Summary

The project aims to provide a wide-ranging support from emergency to regular food assistance as well as household based agricultural and livelihoods assistance. The project aims to target populations who are both food insecure as well as at risk of food insecurity through multiple but mutually reinforcing activities. The project will contribute to the following two objectives of Food security cluster: Sector Objective 1: To Scale-up and sustain minimum food consumption needs through life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance. Sector Objective 2: Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives.
A total of 25,000 households (125,000 individuals) will be supported through multiple but integrated activities.
Under ﬁrst objective for life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance, the project will aim to meet the immediate food needs and also seasonal/supplementary food assistance. The Emergency Response will aim to reach crisis-affected people within 72 hours of the onset of a crisis to cover a minimum of one to four weeks of their immediate food needs through short-term support such as Ready-to-Eat Rations, cooked meals and/or baked bread. Baked bread will also be provided to the displaced families living in the camps. this will help to stabilize the market prices. further supplementary food assistance will be provided to improve the dietary diversity. such as Ramadan food distribution and Qurbani meat distribution
Under the second objective; The proposed project seeks to improve the livelihood situation of 2,000 households through livestock and agricultural support in Harim and Idleb District of Idleb Governorates. The project is developed based on the primary need assessment conducted through focus group discussions and key informant interviews in nine communities. 1,000 families to be provided with agricultural input (winter) including wheat seeds, fertilizers and basic tools. These families will be provided with ongoing technical support to improve their knowledge and practices to get best yield. The estimated yield will be sufficient to cover the food needs of the household and any excess can be sold in the market to create income generation. In addition, 1,000 families (who have existing livestock) will be provided with concentrated feed. During the gestation period sheep will be provided feed. This will be in beginning of the winter period when the feed is most expensive and unaffordable. The sheep will be vaccinated against prevent them from contagious diseases. The households will also receive livestock health and milk kit. The expected milk production is sufficient to cover the household diary needs and any excess can be sold in the market to contribute to the income.
Both beneficiary groups (livestock and agriculture) will continue to receive follow-up advice and training over the course of the project from field project team hired for this purpose.

4 - Needs

According to the 2021 Need and Response summary report; The Humanitarian situation continues to deteriorate drastically with an estimated 14.2 million people in need of some form of food and agriculture assistance, compared to 9.8 million in the previous year. The food insecurity PiN has risen by 45 percent in the last year. Furthermore, a recent assessment was conducted by IR with the communities, the report indicated that the current crisis has devastated the previously flourishing agricultural sector. Some of the agricultural findings include the following: The general scarcity of inputs, combined with the low value of the Syrian Pound (SYP) relative to 2011, continues to push up input costs to the farmer in SYP terms. The use of fertilizers has declined significantly since 2011, with many farmers applying no fertilizer at all to their crops because of low availability and high prices. In Northern Syria, since the government-subsidized fuel is not available, farmers had to depend on often poorer-quality and usually more expensive fuel for their land preparations for the 2021/22 season. As for the livestock: Idleb Governorates, reported livestock numbers are constantly fluctuating, partly as a result of IDP movement and partly owing to the difficulty of measurement. The majority of the female-headed households and IDP prefer sheep breeding as it is much easier for them to take care of their herder compared to others. Breeders try to reduce their stock, by either sale or slaughter, in response to the current shortage and high price of feed. The failure of large areas of cereal crops during the prolonged dry spell decreased the availability of grazing, especially for sheep and goats, early in the year.

5 - Implementation of activities

The following are the main project activities. A total of 25,000 households (125,000 individuals) will be targeted. 1.Emergency response to crisis affected vulnerable people with short term food assistance through appropriate modalities. This intervention will target 5000 households (25,000 people). 2.Supplementary food assistance to Persons with Speciﬁc Needs (PSN) through appropriate modalities (complementary to above and inter linkage with nutrition sector). This also include seasonal food support such as during Ramadan and Qurbani. This intervention will target 10,000 (50,000 people). 3. Support to bakeries (wheat ﬂour, yeast, etc.) through appropriate modalities. This intervention will target 7,000 households (35,000 people). 4. Distribution of agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment. This activity will target 1,000 households (5,000 people). 5. Support to asset building and asset protection (small livestock and animal feed distribution) including winterization activities. This intervention will assist 1,000 households (5,000 people). 6. Emergency livestock treatment, and training for veterinary services of which 1,000 households (5,000 people) will benefit from this activity.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. There is a risk of Community tension arising from the beneficiary selection. IR will ensure to inform the beneficiary selection criteria clearly to all the stakeholders. Local councils and community committees will be engaged in beneficiary selection. 2. Targeting for IDPs versus Host communities. The risk includes inclusion and exclusion errors leading to tensions and sexual exploitation of IDP female. This will be mitigated through local beneficiary selection committees with equitable representation of both IDPs and host communities. Clear/precise guidance to the staff on the beneficiary selection process and criteria. Effective communication as regards the guidelines include communication materials in local language. 3. Targeting for IDPs/Returnees for livelihood projects. The risks include access to land/assets. Risk to secondary rights holders e.g. female headed households. This will be mitigated through engaging with local authorities to verify status and through work alongside other complementary programs on HLP. In provision of general food assistance there is a risk of violence from the beneﬁciaries among themselves as well as the humanitarian staff. Also there is a risk of SEA by humanitarian workers male dominated, gate keepers and potential other beneﬁciaries. This risk will be mitigated through establishing a robust complaint and feedback mechanism so that any such cases are appropriately reported and followed up. The organisational staff will be trained on the Inter Agency Coordination in Community Based Complaint Mechanisms as well as code of conduct. To the extent possible, female staff will be recruited and community committees will be formed for women. post distribution monitoring will be conducted.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Conflict settings place women and girls at acute risk of poor health outcomes and sexual and gender-based violence. IR’s CFM uses multiple channels to collect beneficiary complaints and feedback including a complaint box, hotlines, during-distribution and post-distribution monitoring, community committees, casual enumerators and program staff. In addition, during action activities, random interviews, spot checks and dialogue will allow beneficiaries to report concerns. The received complaints and feedbacks will be classified and handled as per IR policy for CFM and high-risk complaints, such as those relating to PSEA, child protection, or corruption, will be prioritized and promptly managed. Confidentiality and data protection are ensured as per Islamic Relief’s data protection policy. IR’s commitment towards Safeguarding against misconduct will be upheld, and options for safe access to IR’s feedback mechanism, especially to the most marginalized groups such as women, children, persons with disability, and those who cannot read and write, will be sought through WhatsApp, telegram, Skype and SMS.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Potential conflict between the beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. This is a medium level risk. Islamic Relief, in cooperation with the Community Representative will choose the beneficiaries according to specific criteria to serve this community and will adopt a clear and transparent policy in project implementation.2. Takeover of trucks moving the agriculture and livestock inputs. This is a medium level risk. IR will make continuous assessment on accessible safe routes prior to transportation. Appropriate time of truck movement will be assessed and chosen. The movement will take place only on the main and frequently used roads during daylight and journeys will be planned beforehand and based on the latest security and accessibility information.3. The existence of many checkpoints inside Syria, which may limit the access to the target area. This is a medium level risk. IR will coordinate with local partners and local committees in facilitating the movement of the items to the target areas. All required documentation will accompany all shipments of goods along with experienced staff who are familiar with the situation.4. Medium level risk of Spread of COVID-19 in Northwest Syria: IR staff will take the following measures during implementation and monitoring: organize an awareness session for staff, use hand sanitizer that contains at least 60% alcohol, avoid close contact (distance of 6 feet), wear facemask, avoid crowded distribution. Fix awareness posters at the distribution points in order to increase awareness on the pandemic.
