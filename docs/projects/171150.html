Project Summary

The main objective of the shelter coordination is that vulnerable Palestinians who are displaced or at risk of displacement, under the risk of COVID 19 pandemic or protection concerns obtain secure and adequate shelter solutions, through coordination and capacity building of actors working in the shelter sector. 33 and 15 various shelter member organizations registered at shelter cluster in Gaza and West Bank respectively, including INGOs, NNGOs, UN agencies, and Government counterparts are benefiting from the shelter cluster services including coordination, information management, strategy and standards, monitoring and evaluation, contingency planning, advocacy, communications, and cross-cutting issues. The Project is in line with the HRP 2021 strategic objectives and allows Shelter Cluster to continue its role by ensuring that the IASC cluster core functions are fulfilled and well designed to suit the context in Palestine.

1 - Needs

The Shelter Sector faces different challenges in Gaza, the West Bank, and East Jerusalem that requires continued collaboration between actors as well as representation to the HCT and donor/diplomatic community to remove barriers and ensure assistance to the most vulnerable Palestinians. The Cluster collects regular updates from partners to identify challenges and outstanding needs, as well as using data from cluster and partner evaluations/assessments, and OCHA data such as IDPs and demolition databases as well as PCBS information. In Gaza, the high unemployment rate and poverty exacerbated by the spread of COVID19 pandemic hinder the people to meet their basic needs including adequate housing. The vulnerable people (9,356 families) suffer from multiple displacements due to inability to pay the rental costs, the IDPs (4,000) from the frequent conflicts adopting different negative coping mechanisms, part of them living in store or one room shelter with unhygienic overcrowded conditions. While the ongoing blockade, frequent escalation and the risk of large scale conflict combined by other unforeseen natural disasters and the threat of Covid-19 pandemic are always expected, continued information tracking, and emergency preparedness is required to promote adequate and timely support to various vulnerable people in Gaza Strip. The Shelter Cluster has played a vital role in gathering and triangulating data and sharing this to internal and external stakeholders. Chronic housing shortages and the dire economic situation as a result of the blockade, pandemic spread and loss of jobs, frequent conflicts, salary cuts, and poverty have compounded concerns for adequate shelter, protection of acutely-vulnerable families, as identified in the MPWH assessments and MoSD updated database.Continued efforts are needed to consolidate and improve databases and information sharing to ensure prioritization and efficient use of resources. In the West Bank, the unprecedented rate of housing demolitions during 2020 and the annexation plan continues to put Palestinians at risk of displacement, while restrictions on construction due to a range of administrative building barriers imposed under the occupation also challenge the living conditions of Palestinians in the West Bank and East Jerusalem, mainly the Bedouin and herding communities. As a result of the restrictive planning environment and threat of demolition and displacement, over 3,300 households in area C, are in urgent need for upgrading or improvements to achieve the minimum protection and acceptable living conditions, in addition to the 800 housing units yearly shortage. Careful coordination is required to address this need. Several agencies are currently working to respond to housing demolitions in the West Bank through emergency shelter provision as well as advocacy and legal advice. Inter and intra cluster work is ongoing to increase predictable and coordinated response, improve technical standards and engage with donors and authorities on finding ways to avoid re-demolition. Like Gaza, the West Bank also faces winter storms and possible forcible transfer requiring preparedness and continued engagement to implement and improve contingency plans and prepositioning of different NFIs. In both contexts, children are particularly vulnerable, as are the elderly and those who are disabled. Women face a particular burden as primary carers and face specific vulnerabilities accessing their housing land and property rights. A cluster gender and protection focal points were appointed to address different concerns and to provide support and guidance to partners on gender-responsive interventions and protection mainstreaming, as well as revision of NFI standard packages and kits is made frequently to fit with the needs of various vulnerable groups including children, Women, girls, elderlies and PWDs. Shelter sector coordination across the oPt has contributed to improved collaboration, common standards, and clearer guidance, and will continue to work on greater preparedness and coordinated response. There has been good engagement and coordination with local government which should continue to increase understanding of humanitarian principles and overcome barriers to shelter provision to the most vulnerable.Across the oPt, the outbreak of COVID-19 generated additional hardship for families living in substandard shelters, as members who contracted the virus could not be properly isolated, increasing the risk of transmission. 276,447 individuals went to home quarantine since the start of pandemic across oPt, such families are in need of shelter support to adapt their housing for the isolation of members and to access hygiene items and disinfectant materials. Similarly, institutional quarantine and isolation centres require support to 2000-3000 individuals monthly with the supply of a range of non-food items (NFIs) to accommodate those referred to them. Shelter cluster is on top of the coordination and design of such activities which comes in response to urgent needs as a result of the Covid-19 pandemic.

2 - Activities

NRC has led the Shelter Cluster for several years, bringing together the work across Gaza and the West Bank and supporting the ‘one Palestine’ approach. Throughout the current and previous years, the Cluster has produced regular information updates that have been highly valued and relied upon to track the status and progress of the shelter response, COVID19 shelter related needs, and support advocacy and resource mobilisation. The cluster hosts different Working Groups (WGs) like Demolition WG, Contingency and NFIs WG and Assessment WG. The Cluster is a respected and relevant forum through which relationships between key stakeholders are strengthened, reliable information is collected and disseminated and common approaches and standards are developed that reduce inequity and provide safe, adequate and dignified shelter solutions to affected and displaced populations in Palestine. The overall objective of the program is to ensure that displaced Palestinians and Palestinians at risk of displacement, and people living in unhygienic substandard shelter conditions in oPt obtain secure and adequate shelter/NFIs solutions, by continuing to support service delivery through monthly Shelter Cluster meetings, clear and regular information sharing, addressing COVID19 emergent Shelter related needs and informing strategic decision making through assessments, strategy development, conducting assessments, developing different standards, activating different working groups to complete specific tasks and ensure stakeholders engagement. The Cluster will continue to provide a forum for data collection, analysis and dissemination; contingency planning and preparedness; and information management support. The Cluster will promote the mainstreaming of protection and gender in cluster activities through dedicated workshops and tailored support from the gender and protection focal points. It will also continue to play a role in strengthening M&E to ensure adequate impact and outcome analysis, sharing good experience, while highlighting gaps and advocating for appropriate and effective solutions to meet the needs of affected populations. The Cluster will focus on technical support to partner organizations in relation to contingency planning, shelter impact, and gender needs. The cluster also aims to support the partners to target and prioritize the poor families and groups FHHs, PWDs, and the Elderly and keep monitoring and updating the gap and caseloads for different needs including IDPs. 3 - Indicators

The Cluster in 2021 will coordinate activities related to Strategic Objectives 1, 2 and 3 to mitigate the risks and impact of displacement, protect against the effects of severe weather and COVID19 spread, and build preparedness and response capacity. In addition, activities that focuson ensuring access to a basic level of adequate shelter will contribute to Strategic Objectives 1 and 2. The Cluster will also work to promote the inclusion of cross-cutting issues in the work of its members and continue to pursue transition options through building partnerships and linking with long term actors and government. Accountability to members will be fostered through regular feedback and internal performance mid-year review, while overall cluster and cluster members' performance will be monitored by OCHA through the Periodic Monitoring Review (PMR). The Cluster through the SAG will develop a strategic plan at the beginning of the year (Jan-Feb) to identify partner priorities and layout concrete and time-bound activities in an annual workplan. The cluster will continue to increase knowledge and capacity in national authorities, local NGOs and build relationships with NRC Global and the Global Shelter Cluster in order to access surge support where required and ensure cluster performance oversight. Selected indicators: • # of Shelter Cluster meetings facilitated (6 WB; 6-12 Gaza) • # of 4Ws updates on the online system (12 Gaza; 4 WB) • # of information management products shared (12 national) • # of joint advocacy initiatives undertaken (1) • # projects showing increased recognition of gender needs and response in Shelter & NFI sector • Contingency plans updated (1 WB; 1 Gaza) The budget includes a team consisting of 2 Coordinators and 1 Information Manager (all fully cluster dedicated). The coordinators to oversee the coordination of the current level of shelter activities and fulfill core cluster coordination functions, with some support from the Head of Lead Agency in representation to external audiences and championing the needs of the shelter sector. In addition, in order to achieve the above objectives and provide better analysis, technical assistance to partners, or additional support to the cluster team, the cluster in collaboration with the partners will provide the required technical support and capacity building for the existing and newly joined members.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Shelter and NFIs Cluster activities adopt an integrated protection approach to ensure adequate climate protection, safety and security, privacy and dignity and improved wellbeing for occupants. Protection is measured at the outcome level by ensuring accordance with minimum standards for which it’s reported through improvements in living conditions. In addition, the cluster has developed protection mainstreaming guidance to ensure a “do no harm” approach, non-discrimination and the inclusion of vulnerable populations. Gender equality and gender-responsive programming will be advanced through gender analysis; partners will address the gender needs based on the GAM system for of men, women, boys and girls through active beneficiary engagement with all affected groups (not only community leaders), disaggregated beneficiary data, and monitoring indicators and analysis. The Cluster team and focal points updated and disseminated the protection mainstreaming and GAM materials and tip sheets, and encouraging the partners to participate in the training and sessions related to this topic to strengthen gender integration in shelter projects.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

All shelter projects require community engagement at all stages of the project cycle, starting from needs identification, beneficiary selection, project design and implementation, and evaluation.In the West Bank in particular, where vulnerable groups are found in high-risk areas, community engagement is vital in identifying risks and ensuring collaborative decision-making and informed consent as to the appropriate actions. Communities are increasingly involved in the planning and implementation of projects, enabling the selection of the right materials and timing to prevent and/ or minimize exposure of beneficiaries to protection risks. The Cluster will ensure the inclusion of a sound M&E component in all the submitted projects asvetting criteria, to reflect clearly the complaint mechanism and how it will be accessible by beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries, and communication channels identified for this purpose. The cluster will ensure the partners comply with main factors to ensure equal and meaningful access to aid through: (1) Treat displaced persons equitably, whether they are living in host-family arrangements, collective centers, are self-settled in urban or rural locations, are self-settled in camps, or are living in planned camps; (2) Ensure that agencies consider the needs of different ethnic, racial, national or social groups in shelter allocation, ensuring that the quality of shelter is equitable across all groups; (3) Prioritize people and groups on the basis of need – do not prioritize certain groups because their solutions are easier to achieve; (4) Ensure that shelters are accessible and appropriate to all groups and individuals, note in particular concerns of persons with physical or mental disabilities and older persons– where necessary, make individual changes to household shelters, or build all shelters to be universally accessible; (5) People with disabilities and older persons may not be able to come to distribution/facility sites. Plan additional measures to reach persons with disabilities and older persons; (5) Ensure that bathrooms are accessible topersons with disabilities and older persons, and safe for small children; (6) Ensure particularly vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, older persons and persons with disability have equal access to Core Relief Items and ability to transport them; (7) Recognise the joint ownership rights of both male and female heads of household and prevent discrimination; (8) Provide both women and men with the same benefits for their input and their work in construction: e.g. if work is paid, ensure that both women’s and men’s work is paid and is equal; (9) Ensure that unaccompanied older persons are assisted in constructing their own shelters; (10) Develop appropriate response to help vulnerable groups to access aid, transport shelter material and construct shelters. Pay particular attention to assistance needed by persons with disabilities and older persons to ensure the construction of accessible shelters.
