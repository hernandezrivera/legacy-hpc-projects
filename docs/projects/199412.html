1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

Needs Chad ranks 187 out of 189 countries on the Human Development Index, and 6.5 million Chadians live in poverty . By the end of 2022, some 6.1 million people – over one third of the population – will need humanitarian assistance. Over the last few years, Chad has been experiencing three interconnected crises - food insecurity and malnutrition, population movements, and health emergencies - which were further exacerbated due to the COVID-19 pandemic and the impact of the war in Ukraine.The nutritional situation remains critical in most Sahel Belt provinces, even though the overall acute malnutrition has decreased to 8.6 per cent compared to 10.9 per cent in 2021, according to the SMART Survey conducted in November 2022. This decrease could be attributed to the survey being conducted at the end of the harvest season, when food is more available and accessible, and to the actions carried out by different humanitarian actors in 2022. The gender and equity analysis of the global acute malnutrition (GAM) at national level has shown that the boys are more affected than girls (9,8 per cent vs 7,4 per cent), the youngest child aged 6-23 months are more affected than the eldest aged 24–59 months (13.3 per cent vs 6 percent), and the Sahel Belt including fifteen provinces are more affected than the southern provinces (12.2 per cent vs 5.2 per cent).Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) at national is estimated at 1.5 per cent according to the 2022 SMART Survey, compared to a 2% prevalence in 2021. The December 2022 Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) analysis of acute malnutrition showed how serious the nutrition situation is in 3 provinces and 30 departments out of the 47 analysis units (9 provinces and 38 departments). In addition, it projected a continuous deterioration of the nutritional conditions in 2023, especially during the lean season (June to September 2022) where 6 provinces (Borkou, Wadi Fira, Batha, Salamat, Sila, N'Djamena) and 28 departments could be in a serious to critical phase.This worrying nutritional situation, which has lasted for years, is exacerbated by multiple recurrent crises, including armed conflicts with large population movements in the South (CAR crisis), in the East (Darfur and CAR crisis), in N’Djamena, Chari Baguirmi and Mayo Kebi Est (Cameroon crisis) and in the Lake area (NSAG attack, and BOKO HARAM), with the island populations who are the most affected and the least accessible by humanitarian aid for security reasons. As of December 31, almost 600,000 refugees reside in Chad, 101,500 returnees and some 380,000 Chadians are internally displaced, and require humanitarian assistance. These constant waves of refugees and returnees generate critical humanitarian needs in a context of low level of coverage with basic social infrastructure, particularly for displaced people and host communities. The massive displacements affect disproportionally children and women, with an estimated 21,113 children under five suffering from severe acute malnutrition and 39,429 mothers who will require lifesaving assistance. UNICEF will implement integrated and multi-sectoral interventions with strengthened accountability to affected people and protection. Specific strategies will be implemented for populations with disabilities. Health staff will be trained to ensure gender-based interventions and protection in the nutrition programs. They will receive guidelines on referral mechanisms to specialized protection services, including child protection and Gender Based Violence (GBV). An analysis of specific needs will be done to ensure that gender is effectively considered in the interventions. Related data will be disaggregated by sex and age. Outputs: 1.) Procurement and distribution of 21,113 RUTF boxes and others nutrition supplies, materials and equipment to health facilities.2.) Ensure training and refresher training for 64 health workers on the management of SAM and IYCF counselling and accountability for beneficiaries3.) Ensure training for 41 community health workers (CHWs) on MUAC screening of acute malnutrition and awareness raising activities using the promotion of optimal IYCF practices4.) Raise awareness of 39,429 mothers on IYCF practices5.) Conduct monthly monitoring of nutrition status for children using Mothers MUAC strategy for early detection of SAM cases Results 1.) 21,113 children suffering from acute malnutrition are treated 2.) 41 Community Health workers trained in the prevention and management of SAM, disaggregated by sex and age 3.) 39,429 caregivers have improved knowledge on IYCF 4.) 119,136 children screened for severe acute Malnutrition
