Project Summary

SPEDP proposes a 12 months HRP project aimed at combating malnutrition among children under the age of 5 years, reduce WASH-related morbidity and mortality and GBV risks among communities affected by multiple hazards including returnees in Fangak of Jonglei; Manyo and Fashoda of Upper Nile; Leer, Koch and Rubkona of Unity State . The approach is through WASH integration with nutrition, health and gender based violence (GBV). The project targets 23,408 vulnerable people (4121men, 8009women, 5618boys and 5660girls) among IDPs, returnees, host community and people with special needs.
Strategy 1: Integration of WASH with nutrition and health to combat malnutrition and reduce water borne diseases respectively.
The proposed intervention will integrate WASH with nutrition and health through construction/rehabilitation of water and sanitation facilities at community level and nutrition centers /health facilities. In all the project locations, water interventions will be complemented by hygiene education.
The featured solutions include drilling and construction of 6 boreholes and upgrading of 2 newly drilled boreholes to mini water yards , rehabilitation of 53 dysfunctional hand pumps, water quality testing and monitoring of water points, construction of 15 compartment of improved toilet blocks in 15 nutrition/health facilities. Training of water user committees and Community health promoters. Water and sanitation interventions in the targeted locations will be supported by various hygiene promotional interventions. By complementing all hardware with software activities, SPEDP will conduct hygiene promotion sessions tailored to specific target groups, using participatory hygiene and sanitation transformation methodology with materials provided by the WASH Cluster or adapted as necessary; oversee production of messaging materials printed in local languages on posters, banners, posters and thus will contribute towards working against malnutrition and water borne diseases among the affected population. In case of emergencies, SPEDP will distribute standard kits to SAM children/caretakers and pregnant lactating women (PLW) paired with hygiene promotion (hand, food and domestic hygiene) and water household treatment and safety.
Strategy 2: Integration of WASH with GBV and Inclusion
SPEDP takes into account women’s dignity and security during WASH service provision to reduce possible risks of GBV and fear of not using the facilities. Also protection of females against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse during access to WASH service will be closely monitored. During the project cycle, the project team will conduct focus group discussions to consider views and special needs of females, children and persons with disabilities are incorporated in project activities, which will improve services effectiveness, timeliness, usage of facilities and beneficiary participation. SPEDP will conduct separate discussions with women and girls for allocation of sites for of WASH facilities preferably done GBV female staff. We shall also set out a box for women and girls to give feedback and complaints. Conduct community meetings with target beneficiaries to ensure that designs meet their specific requirements, including women, adolescents, men, persons with disabilities, lactating women, pregnant women and the elderly; including safety audits and risk analysis for selecting locations.
The proposed intervention will contribute to the improved well-being of the people affected by disasters or chronically exposed to lack of basic water and sanitation services in the proposed project locations hence reducing water and sanitation related morbidity and mortality and also mitigate WASH related GBV and disability inclusion. SPEDP has static presence in the proposed locations with ongoing health, nutrition, WASH and FSL projects.

1. Needs

South Sudan is grumbling with multiple hazards i.e. climate-related shocks (severe flooding and drought), continuous pockets of insecurity due communal violence, disease outbreaks (Measles, Meningitis, Hepatitis-E, Cholera and Anthrax outbreaks) and high levels of acute food insecurity driven by the impact of the economic meltdown. Similarly, the causes of the malnutrition crisis are multifactorial, inadequate utilization of both preventive and curative basic health, nutrition, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), and protection services. Furthermore, the humanitarian situation in South Sudan is exacerbated by the fighting that erupted in neighboring Sudan in mid-April between the Sudanese Armed Forces and Rapid Support Forces in which over 101,000 people fled the conflict to South Sudan. The majority are South Sudanese returnees who had been residing in Sudan. While combating the impact of climate change, people of South Sudan face challenges to access basic services. Only 50% percent of the population of South Sudan have access to basic drinking water sources and just 10% of households in South Sudan have access to improved sanitation, while 75% practice open defecation (World Bank Press release October 9th 2023). Humanitarian and development needs in Jonglei (Fangak), Unity (Leer, Rubkona, and Koch) and Upper Nile (Fashoda and Manyo) are driven by multiple hazards i.e. flooding, conflict, displacement and disease outbreaks. Despite the worsening situation, many people are returning to their homes, as their situation in Sudan and other neighboring Countries or IDP sites is not any better. However, the host community members, IDPs, returnees and relocated persons are living in dire conditions with extremely limited-to-no access to essential services such as shelter, food, water and health care. Women and children often face sexual violence, forced labor and other protection risks. Most of the WASH facilities in the proposed locations have been abandoned and nonfunctional; the few functional hand pumps are over being used and frequently break down. Inadequate access to safe water supply is the underlying causes of frequent occurring of water related sicknesses especially among children under the age of 5 years. Lack of access to WASH can also affect a child’s nutritional status in many ways. Existing evidence supports at least three direct pathways: via diarrhea diseases, intestinal parasite infections and environmental enteropathy. WASH may also impact nutritional status indirectly by necessitating walking long distances in search of water and sanitation facilities and diverting a mother’s time away from child care.
