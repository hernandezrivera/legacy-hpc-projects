Project Summary

Mercy Corps (MC) will respond to priority needs with life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI assistance across the governorates of Al-Hasakeh, Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor and Idleb (estimated 75% NWS, 25% NES).Mercy Corps will target IDPs, returnees and host communities with the proposed interventions. Mercy Corps is a cash first agency and estimates 30% of the programme will be delivered through cash and vouchers. The modality will be decided based on detailed market assessments in the targeted communities, and therefore cash will be implemented when possible. MC will improve access to dignified shelter through rehabilitation of substandard shelters (collective shelters and individual units). And will also work on camp expansions and site improvement of the targeted informal IDP sites in response to overcrowded and flood situations in camps and informal sites. This will be complemented by tents provision through other Shelter partner. MC will also pilot different dignified shelter units to address major protection concerns in informal and formal camps. MC will take into account home and land ownership along with vulnerability criteria to ensure HLP rights are considered, and vulnerable HHs are not further displaced or taken advantage of after receiving assistance. In camp and out of camp interventions will enhance security, privacy and the provision of dignified shelter. MC will further provide NFI and/or winterization kits when needs arise which include: Winter kits, Clothing Kits, Basic Shelter Kits.

3 - Needs

Over the course of the crisis, shelter and infrastructure damage has occurred on a massive scale, with the scale of rebuilding and reconstruction needed going beyond the capacity and remit of the Shelter & NFI sector and the humanitarian response in general. HNO 2023 confirmed that shelter situation in Syria continues to worsen, in terms of the available shelter capacity, the adequacy of shelter conditions with Shelter assistance a top unmet need, particularly for IDPs in camps and returnees.More than 90 per cent of households reported a combination of shelter inadequacies such as the inability to lock their home securely (43 per cent), lack of insulation (50 per cent), leakage from roof/ceiling (50 per cent) and lack of privacy/space (61 per cent). Around 5.7 million people are in need of shelter assistance due to inadequate conditions in existing accommodation, and the overall lack of available, adequate and affordable shelters.The destruction and structural damage have also severely affected the housing stock across the country, leading to inadequate housing and shelter conditions for millions of people in Syria. Some of the displaced people are hosted by family and friends, some pay rent, others resort to illegal occupation of abandoned buildings. Poor shelter conditions, overcrowding and limited access to basic services in informal settlements and camps do not just negatively affect people’s living conditions but increase protection and public health risks specifically.

4 - Implementation of activities

1.1 Provision of core and essential NFIs. Provision of one-off, in-kind assistance to newly displaced HHs within 72 hours of displacement (access and availability permitting), composition in line with SNFI cluster guidance, taking into consideration age, disability, gender. 1.2 Provision of seasonal & supplementary NFIs and shelter assistance; including cash/voucher for provision of winter kits consisting of mattresses, blankets, floor mats, tarpaulin and a solar lamp, per SNFI cluster guidance. 1.3 Provision of life-saving and life-sustaining shelter: Provide emergency shelter intervention to improve shelter conditions of camp & informal sites, to facilitate tent installation and installation of dignified shelters per SNFI cluster. 1.4 Rehabilitation/upgrading of Life-saving and life-sustaining shelter spaces in collective centers, unfinished buildings, spontaneous settlements, other emergency spaces (cash for shelter, physical repair, etc.); 1.5 Support to sustainably repair/rehabilitate housing, related community/public infrastructure & facilities to owners/tenants/host families (materials, cash, voucher, labour, etc.): Selection criteria will consider gender, age, disability, and socio-economic vulnerability such as rCSI, shelter type, and dependency ratio of the household. Activities based on needs assessments and KIIs,. MEAL teams will monitor projects with TPMs. Sustainability and handover plans will be created in consultation with communities and updated regularly.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection, gender and inclusion mainstreaming into all S/NFI programming, will ensure participants’ safety and dignity, equal access and meaningful participation. MC will conduct protection, disability and gender mainstreaming and conflict sensitive refresher training for staff and partners prior to the implementation. MC will also complete a specific protection risk analysis (PRA) at the start of projects and review it during implementation. MC will deliver community awareness sessions on rights and entitlements, information on available services, selection criteria, complaints and feedback mechanisms and MC’ Code of Conduct prior to the implementation of field activities. Data collected in programming will be disaggregated by sex, age, and disability. Main protection risks and mitigating measures: 1. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: MC monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and MC has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. 2. Programming exacerbates community tensions due to perceived discrimination/favouritism: MC upholds a conflict sensitive and DNH approach throughout programming. 3. Local authorities restrict activities of partners due to lack of support for protection interventions.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Participant safeguarding is central to Mercy Corps’ values, and we uphold a zero- tolerance tolerance approach towards any form of sexual exploitation and abuse. In order to prevent safeguarding violations of any kind, all staff, casual labor and external contractors receive orientation and training, and are required to sign the Code of Conduct. The safeguarding training is comprehensive and is delivered to all Mercy Corps and partner staff. Where Mercy Corps and partner staff have higher levels of contact with participants, they will receive tailored capacity strengthening on Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and will participate in quarterly events, which address PSEA concerns in target communities, and facilitate the roll-out of PSEA tools and preventative actions. This process is led by our WoS Safeguarding Manager and is supported by a network of focal points within Mercy Corps. Partner staff will have the opportunity to risk assess their own areas in terms of safeguarding and create proactive action plans. Community participants will be informed on what safeguarding is and how they can report any concerns through sensitisation work across programmes. In addition, external contractors and staff such as suppliers or Third Party Monitors (TPMs) are regularly trained by department focal points who have completed tailored Training of Trainer (ToT) safeguarding training.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(1) The ongoing conflict in Syria presents a constant threat to the safety and security of individuals and groups across Syria, including prospective project participants and staff members. To mitigate this risk. Mercy Corps will conduct ongoing context analysis including security and risk assessments to inform decisions on location, routes and timing of program activities, ensuring such decisions are in the best interest of the safety and security of participants and staff. (2) Program implementation could experience significant delays due to the COVID-19 and cholera and other pandemics, occasioned by lockdowns and curfews. Mercy Corps will draw on experiences adapting program implementation over the last one year to ensure that as many activities as possible can be implemented. Simultaneously, opportunities for pursuing and strengthening remote program implementation capacities will be explored and harnessed. (3) Shifts in territorial control or major security incidents could temporarily or permanently impede access of Mercy Corps organization to project participants. Mercy Corps will continuously monitor the situation. (4) Mercy Corps will distribute cash in USD, as opposed to the Syrian pound (SYP) to ensure participants retain the value of the transfer, protecting them against the ongoing devaluation of the SYP and attendant market fluctuations.
