Project Summary

Youth development and empowerment are vital stages in life for building the human capital that allows young people to avoid poverty and lead better, and possibly have a more fulfilling life. The human capital formed in youth is thus an important determinant of long-term growth that a nation can invest in.
International organizations and governments have invested in the youth sector over the past decades, Where it has had a good impact on societies' political and social stability.
It also contributes to the end of the conflict and peace, whether as a result of external wars or internal conflicts.
In the areas where refugees and IDPs are hosted, Inside and outside Syria, the economic empowerment of youth contributes to reducing the financial burden of receiving refugees and IDPs and reducing tensions with the host community as a result of the depletion of existing resources, especially in economically vulnerable areas. Therefore, based on the difficulty of the situation of young Syrian refugees to find jobs because of the legal conditions and policies imposed in the host country to work with any official party or the establishment of private work, as well as young people displaced within Syria under conditions of war, and the waste of the ability of young people in work in the poor jobs that consume their energy. The QRCS has developed the following proposal to support a range of youth initiatives for internally displaced persons in Syria.
Through the provision of grants (2072 USD for each grant) that enable them to establish innovative, income-generating pilot projects to support themselves and their families. As well as providing them with the skills and experience necessary.
the project can foster social cohesion; by involving the community in program design (e.g. in determining frequency/timing/type of delivery) besides FGDs with different segments of the community, including one exclusively for women. and promote consistent gender balance among monitoring team.

3 - Needs

millions of Syrians continue to face challenges in accessing basic services, due to social fabric of the country has been severely damaged. According to HNO 2022 report, there are 13.8 million people inside Syria requiring Early Recovery and Livelihoods support HH’s ability to meet basic needs has deteriorated across all the governorates, particularly in As-Sweida, Dara, and Quneitra, where 55%, 51%, and 47% of HHs indicated much worse conditions compared to last year. Deterioration of the HH’s ability to meet basic needs is observed across all population groups, for IDPs (73% of HHs), residents (73% of HHs), returnees (74% of HHs). Nonetheless, the top need for 71% of HHs remain food baskets/bread, immediately followed by livelihoods (56%). This signals a widespread existence of “working poor”, whose aggregated income is insufficient to meet basic needs. This evidence is even more disquieting if we consider that in 38,1% of HHs more than one member is employed. Across all population groups, the two main causes of inability to meet basic needs are the price increases of food items and other essential goods, followed by insufficient income, reported by more than 80 % of households. Other causes are unemployment or loss of job, (IDPs living in camps are most affected with 35%); items being unavailable (returnees most affected with 23 percent); and lack of availability services, including electricity and loss of remittances (IDPs in camps were most affected with 17.5 percent)

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) considerations are overriding components of all activities. This project will take several steps (training staff, use of the feedback mechanism, recruitment of male and female staff, and clauses in contracts) to mitigate any susceptibility to such risks. QRC is an active member of the PSEA Network in Gaziantep and has supported the establishment of the inter-agency reporting mechanism to ensure procedures and materials are in line with community understanding and preferences. QRC staff sign a code of conduct, including protocols to ensure that sensitive beneficiary information is kept confidential. We have a feedback mechanism (FM) that includes a mobile phone and WhatsApp hotline number for beneficiaries, other stakeholders, and the affected population in general, to provide feedback (positive or negative). Information about the mechanism is distributed broadly through activities (i.e., posters in public areas and during outreach). One dedicated staff is responsible for monitoring the feedback number, logging all messages, and alerting the appropriate team member about relevant feedback in a way that is sensitive towards the complainant, and taking necessary action to respond. Where necessary, QRC will modify program implementation to address concerns raised by affected people. QRC FM receives all reports, including SEA. If a SEA case is received, the receiving staff will immediately refer it to the QRC PSE
