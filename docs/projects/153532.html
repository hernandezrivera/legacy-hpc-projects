Project Summary

On the 19 Jan 2018 armed clashes between SPLA and SPLA –IO forces occurred in Pogee Boma, Pajok Payam, Magwi County. SPLA –IO attacked SPLA again in Pajok Payam causing civilians in the area to flee. Fighting continued in the area of Lobone on February 5, 2018 continuing the displacement of populations. On 22 February Abara, Magwi County, a group of SPLA and armed youth were ambushed by IO forces. Three SPLA soldiers and two civilians were reportedly killed and eleven others wounded which further contributed to the displacement of civilians that began in 2017. Ambushes have been reported on the Juba-Kapoeta, Juba-Torit, Kapoeta-Torit, Torit-Magwi and Kapoeta-Ngauro roads, resulting in several civilian casualties.
After extensive access negotiations by UNOCHA and others, these areas of Magwi became accessible for humanitarians though low level clashes and ambushes continue to occur. Some IDPs that fled to Uganda are returning slowly as fewer large scale clashes are reported and the rainy season approaches.

Risk factors for developing mental health problems reported in previous South Sudan studies included being female, being older, single marital status, lack of access to basic needs (e.g. food, water, health), forced displacement, and number of traumatic events (Roberts et al, 2009; Roberts et al, 2010). According to the IRNA report from December 15-17, 2017 and update reports during coordination meetings held weekly at the SSOPO office in Magwi the condition of persons with special needs, particularly elders and people with disabilities, is dire and in need of response. SSOPO has previously responded to the needs of older households through nutrition support; SSOPO will use the networks and knowledge of services from prior projects to work to support this specific population.
The IDPs in these three areas of Pajok, Lobone and Magwi have suffered multiple displacements and have lost most of their properties. Due to inaccessibility of the area for months due to ongoing fighting, older people and people with disability have been underserved and untargeted by many humanitarian interventions. For the older persons, their age indicates having survived all the atrocities and fighting’s to protect their families and properties. It also means that they have been displaced multiple times, having lost many of their possessions and support structures impacting their psychosocial wellbeing . Many of them are frail and old mostly due to stress and trauma they have suffered. Along with their physical weakness associated with age, many suffer the pangs of starvation and disease, with no one to serve or protect them.
Unfortunately, some of these elderly persons especially the women are still primary care givers, giving care and protection to children of their daughters and granddaughters who passed on during the conflict. There are reported incidences of older men getting burned in houses, while being unable to escape.

Activities

1. Identify 30 community volunteers to support elderly and disabled IDPs/returneesThe volunteers that will be selected together with IDP/Returnees and local leaders will have to fulfil the following criteria: willingness to work voluntarily with or without financial incentives, has empathy, respected in the community, honest, and hardworking; able to read and write, experience having worked on PSS projects or in Peer to Peer support groups will be prioritized 2. Provide 3 day training for 20 community volunteers The selected 10 community volunteers (4 in Magwi, 3 in Pajok, 3 in Lobone, 4 in Nimule, 3 Pageri, 3 Mugali) will undergo 3 day training by the Program Officer and Project Assistants on the following topics: Identification of potential beneficiaries; Introduction to Psychosocial First Aid (PFA); Referral services available in the community; Coping mechanisms and support systems; and working with Peer to Peer support groups. 3. Create Peer to Peer support groups for each boma of Magwi Town (7 groups 30 people each), Pajok (5 groups 30 people each), Lobone (5 groups 30 people each), Pageri ( 5 groups of 30 people each), Nimule (5 groups each of 30 each) and Mugali (5 groups of 30 each).Project Assistants with Community Volunteers will work with identified elderly and disable volunteers to create Peer to Peer support groups to re-create community for displaced persons, share stories and work together to build resilience. Members will be supported through referrals and referral support (accompanying members to appointments as requested, assisting with transport, etc.) for the most vulnerable 200 members by Project Assistants. SSOPO is going to support the Peer-to-Peer support groups (who are expected to continue to work even after the end of the project) to work with the Community Volunteers (the Community Volunteers are project specific and they cease to exist after the end of the project) in the identification of vulnerable members of the older people and the disabled adults (IDPS, Host Community and Returnees), identification of the needs of the vulnerable groups and referring them to specialized agencies for specialized support and services. The referral supports in the context of this proposal involves engaging and building rapport with community and members, identification and registration of vulnerable groups, identification of the needs of those in need of support, keeping the information of those identified and referring those in need of support and specialized services to Organizations providing them and follow up of those referred.4. Establish Centres in Magwi, Pajok, Pageri, Nimule, Mugali and Lobone for meetingsPeer to Peer support groups in Magwi will meet in local centers, such as churches, that are already established. For Magwi, Pajok, Pageri, Nimule, Mugali and Lobone, plastic chairs, tables and other supplies will be purchased for the local center as these items are not available to support the Peer to Peer groups. 5. Awareness raising for community and for local health workers, influencers and church leaders.Persons with special needs in times of emergency may not be visible to health workers, local leaders and church leaders. In order to raise awareness about the special needs of displaced elderly and disabled persons, a half-day session will be held to identify the ways health workers can support referrals, local influencers and church leaders can advocate for inclusion in community activities. 6. Specific referral and support to 600 elderly and disabled IDPs, returnees.7. Provide psychosocial support services (PSS)

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

SSOPO worked in Greater Magwi County since, 2009 to date and it has wide knowledge about the community and beneficiaries targeted with various projects in the County. The projects implemented in the six Payams of Magwi County such as Food security and Livelihood, Peace Building, Awareness raising on Gender Based Violent (GVB), Civic education on referendum to encourage women participation in the 2011 referendum, Nutrition (IYCF) and Emergency Psychosocial support services to returnees, IDPs and Host communities. Throughout these period SSOPO had identified protection services as an appropriate intervention for the most vulnerable groups such the older people and people with disability. We implemented the PSS project in three Payams in Magwi which was support through the RRF and we would like to continue with the same activities with support from South Sudan Humanitarian Fund (SSHF) with extension to remaining three Payams of Pageri, Nimule and Mugali.
