Project Summary

The project aims to deliver essential emergency lifesaving Primary Healthcare services across five strategically selected counties in Upper Nile State namely: Longechuk, Maiwut, Renk, Malakal, and Fashoda. The project's service delivery model is to integrate 10 static healthcare facilities (2 per county) with mobile medical units (MMUs) to ensure maximum coverage and accessibility for vulnerable populations in both urban and remote areas. The project is to target 50,000 beneficiaries, with a demographically balanced distribution comprising 16,500 children under 5 years (8,400 girls, 8,100 boys), 11,000 children aged 5-17 years (5,600 girls, 5,400 boys), 20,000 adults aged 18-59 years (12,000 women, 8,000 men), and 2,500 elderly persons (1,375 women, 1,125 men). Special attention will be directed to approximately 4,000 pregnant women and lactating mothers through dedicated maternal and child health services, representing the most vulnerable subset of the adult female population. The service delivery framework is to encompass a multi-faceted approach to healthcare provision. The 10 static facilities will be strengthened through comprehensive equipment and supply support, ensuring they meet WHO IPC Minimum Requirements standards. Each facility will serve approximately 4,000 direct beneficiaries, with additional coverage provided through strategically deployed MMUs reaching isolated communities, particularly during seasonal accessibility challenges between July and November when flooding affects 40% of the region. To address the prevalent health challenges identified through recent assessments, the project will focus on managing and treating the leading causes of morbidity: malaria (14% of consultations, approximately 12,500 cases annually), diarrheal diseases (10%, approximately 9,000 cases), and pneumonia (6%, approximately 5,400 cases). The initiative will include robust disease surveillance systems, with EWARS implementation and outbreak response protocols. Protection mainstreaming is to form a crucial component of the project, incorporating Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services targeting 2,000 beneficiaries annually, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services with capacity for 300 cases, and established referral pathways. The project will emphasize gender-sensitive service delivery and ensure accessibility for 1,500 persons with disabilities, implementing specific mechanisms to address protection concerns affecting women and girls. Healthcare worker capacity building will feature prominently in the project design, with comprehensive training programs covering disease surveillance, immunization, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC), Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP), Sexual and Gender-Based Violence/Clinical Management of Rape (SGBV/CMR), Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI), and malaria Rapid Diagnostic Testing (mRDT). The project aims to train and support 100 healthcare workers across all facilities. The project will incorporate strong WASH integration, ensuring adequate water supply and sanitation facilities at all supported health facilities, maintaining minimum standards of 100L water per bed daily, and implementing comprehensive infection control protocols. Community engagement mechanisms will be established through 40 Boma health workers and 10 community health committees, ensuring local participation and sustainable health improvements. The project supports Health Cluster objectives 1, 2, and 3, with specific attention to achieving 100% compliance with WHO IPC Minimum Requirements in supported facilities.

1. Needs

Recent health facility assessments conducted in September 2024 across the five target counties of Upper Nile State reveal a critical healthcare situation requiring immediate intervention. Primary data collected through the Health Management Information System (HMIS) in these specific counties shows that out of 27 health facilities, only 12 are currently functional, representing a 45% functionality rate. This limited healthcare infrastructure serves a catchment population of 75,000 people across the target counties.Disease burden analysis from county-level HMIS data demonstrates that malaria remains the primary cause of morbidity, accounting for 14% of all primary healthcare consultations in the target counties. This translates to approximately 18,900 documented cases in the past year. Diarrheal diseases follow as the second most prevalent condition, representing 10% of consultations (13,500 cases), while pneumonia accounts for 6% of consultations (8,100 cases). These rates are exacerbated by a critical shortage of essential medicines, with facility assessments revealing stock-outs in 70% of functional facilities during the last quarter of 2024.Maternal and child health indicators in the target counties present significant concerns. Facility-based data shows that only 35% of deliveries are attended by skilled personnel, resulting in approximately 2,100 assisted deliveries out of an estimated 6,000 annual deliveries across the five counties. Antenatal care coverage is particularly concerning, with only 45% of pregnant women accessing these essential services. County Health Department records indicate that 60% of documented maternal deaths in these areas are due to preventable causes, primarily postpartum hemorrhage and hypertensive disorders.Disease surveillance data collected through the Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS) in the target counties between January and September 2024 confirms 6 laboratory-verified measles outbreaks, primarily affecting Longechuk and Maiwut counties. The surveillance system operates at 55% capacity due to limited resources and trained personnel, with only 8 out of 12 functional facilities regularly reporting through EWARS. Active case finding has identified 97 suspected cases of communicable diseases requiring further investigation.Protection-related health needs are substantial within the target counties. Gender-Based Violence (GBV) response data from county protection partners shows 400 documented cases requiring medical response in 2024. Health facility records indicate 2,500 children needing specialized healthcare services, 600 elderly residents with chronic conditions requiring regular medical attention, and 2,000 persons with disabilities facing significant barriers in accessing healthcare services.Infrastructure and resource gaps severely constrain service delivery capacity. County-level health workforce data shows a 65% shortage in skilled healthcare workers against WHO minimum standards, with only 2.3 health workers per 10,000 population compared to the recommended 23 per 10,000. Cold chain assessment reports indicate functionality at 55% capacity across operational facilities, significantly impacting vaccine storage capabilities. Emergency response capacity reaches only 40% of the minimum standard, with critical gaps in essential medical supplies affecting 7 out of 12 functional facilities.Geographic accessibility remains a major challenge in the target counties. GIS mapping shows 60% of the target population living beyond 5km from the nearest functional health facility. Seasonal flooding between July and November impacts access to 40% of existing facilities, while insecurity along supply routes disrupts medical supply chains to 35% of service delivery points. Community consultations reveal an average walking time of over 2 hours to reach the nearest health facility for most communities in the target counties.

2. Project activities

Activity 1: Comprehensive Primary Healthcare Service Delivery through Static FacilitiesThe project will implement six core healthcare activities across five counties in Upper Nile State, serving 50,000 beneficiaries through 10 static facilities and mobile outreach services. Static facilities will provide comprehensive primary healthcare, operating eight hours daily with 24/7 emergency response capability. Each facility will target 1,500 monthly consultations, delivering essential services including outpatient care, maternal and child health services, and immunization. The facilities will focus on managing prevalent conditions like malaria, respiratory infections, and diarrheal diseases while maintaining 90% immunization coverage for children under five.Activity 2: Mobile Medical Unit Outreach ServicesMobile Medical Units (MMU) will extend healthcare access to remote communities, with five units conducting bi-weekly visits to hard-to-reach locations. Each MMU will serve approximately 1,000 beneficiaries monthly, providing basic curative services, antenatal care, and immunization. The units will conduct 35-50 daily consultations during outreach visits and support disease surveillance in their operational areas.Activity 3: Maternal and Child Health Services EnhancementMaternal and child health services will form a crucial component, with four facilities providing 24/7 basic emergency obstetric care. The program will target 2,000 pregnant women for antenatal care, aims to assist 1,600 facility-based deliveries annually, and provide postnatal care to 1,500 mother-baby pairs. Implementation of IMCI protocols will ensure comprehensive care for 10,000 children under five.Activity 4: Disease Surveillance and Response SystemDisease surveillance systems will maintain emergency response teams capable of investigating suspected outbreaks within 48 hours. The system will verify 80% of disease alerts within 24 hours and maintains weekly EWARS reporting.Activity 5: Protection Mainstreaming and Mental Health SupportIntegration of protection services and mental health support across all facilities ensures comprehensive care for vulnerable populations. Services include Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) targeting 2,000 beneficiaries annually, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) services with capacity for 300 cases, and established referral pathways for specialized care. The program aims to provide mental health services to 800 individuals, support 300 gender-based violence survivors with medical care, and ensure protection services reach 1,500 persons with disabilities.Activity 6: Community Health System StrengtheningCommunity health systems are strengthened through 40 trained community health workers and ten health committees. Each committee will conduct monthly health promotion sessions reaching 300 community members and facilitates community feedback mechanisms. The program will establish functional referral systems between communities and health facilities, aiming for 80% community engagement in health activities.
