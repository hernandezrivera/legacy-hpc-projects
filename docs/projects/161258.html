Project Summary

Changes made include: EORE used consistently, updated figure for contaminated land used according to last monitoring report (11 November 2018), and confirmation that beneficiary data will be collected by disability as well.
In line with the Protection Cluster Objective 2 (prevent and mitigate protection risks through enhanced preparedness), the project seeks to provide lifesaving explosive ordnance risk education (EORE).
The project targets locations in Tier 1 (Juba), and Tier 3 (Fashoda, Melut), reflecting the needs and severity in line with Mine Action Sub Cluster, and aligned to World Vision’s area of existing operations to benefit from logistical and structural support. World Vision maintains Child Protection, GBV, Education, Health, and FSL in these areas for a multi-sectoral life-saving intervention; EORE teams would have full access to these sites and activities, ensuring that messages are shared broadly and with vulnerable communities.
The project will implement the following EORE activities, according to IMAS, NTSG, World Vision approved EORE SOPs and in alignment with the MASC – EORE Working Group and South Sudan National Mine Action Strategy 2018-2021: Training and accreditation of EORE teams through approved third-party trainer / training programme; EORE assessment; community engagement / mobilization of women, girls, boys, and men; leaflet provision of integrated EORE and community liaison, linked with child protection and education to particularly reach girls and boys in and out of school, and facilitate integration of EORE in schools; use of approved / standard EORE materials (i.e. banners, leaflets, warning signs, etc.) and age-appropriate/gender-sensitive means of communication; use of all IMSMA tools and forms for complete and timely information management / reporting per SOPs; implementation of quality monitoring system including quality assurance (internal, external) and quality controls; advocacy to greater prioritize EORE as a life-saving humanitarian intervention, especially for children.
The project reflects the unique needs of people in need, committing a gender marker that sees significant contributions to gender equality (including across age groups), prioritizing the needs of disabled populations (reflective of strong protection mainstreaming), and accountability to targeted communities (engaging stakeholders and conducting broad consultations to inform interventions and ongoing monitoring.
Finally, World Vision maintains zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, and has a robust Child and Adult Safeguarding Policy and approach to realize this at a national level across South Sudan, and globally.

1 - Needs

An estimated 8 million people live in counties impacted by landmines and Explosives Remnants of War (ERW). Over 79km2 of land is known to be contaminated, with shifting frontlines and historical conflict making it problematic for IDPs and host community members to be aware of mined areas. The areas of greatest contamination stem across the southern area of the Greater Equatoria region, which accounts for many of the primary return routes for expected South Sudanese refugees in Uganda and DRC. The presence of explosive hazards not only threatens the safety of children, their families, and communities overall in terms of physical harm from explosion, but such hazards present barriers to freedom of movement, access to resources and services these communities need, and the delivery of humanitarian aid. The 2018 Mine Action Review conducted finds that South Sudan is not on track to realize Article 5 of the Mine Ban Convention, and recommends: South Sudan should make every effort to minimise the risk to civilians from mines and UXOs; Continued efforts should be made to ensure accurate reporting by operators of mine action data and recording according to IMAS; Greater assistance from the government and international partners should be provided to strengthen capacity to develop and implement effective policies and programmes. South Sudan’s National Mine Action Strategy (2018-2022) has three goals: advocacy and communication of South Sudan’s mine/ERW problem to continue through national and international awareness raising; Size of the mine/ERW contaminated area is clarified, confirmed, and cleared; and, Safe behaviour is promoted among women, girls, boys, and men to reduce mine/ERW accidents and promote safe livelihood activities. Protection Trends data indicates the high human cost of UXO contamination, and underscores the life-saving nature of EORE to mitigate the risk of death or injury, especially for children. Since 2013, the largest proportion of incidents have occurred as a result of play/recreation, and more specifically has impacted children. Boys and girls are at risk of seeing UXOs and believing them to be toys to play with, or otherwise failing to recognize their danger. Due to their traditional roles and patterns of behaviour, men and boys are more affected by the risks of UXOs, recognizing their greater involvement in conflict, pastoral movement, and allotment of time for play and recreation, in comparison to women and girls. The scale of contamination, the high rate of unknown/unconfirmed UXO presence, the low levels of basic education and the social normalization of conflict combine to create a high risk for women, men, boys and girls to be killed or maimed. In this context, EORE is a life-saving intervention, especially for children. Many years of conflict have left many remnants of war and unexploded items to pose serious hazard for children and communities. The dangers posed by remnants of war to children has prompted WV, particularly as a child protection-focused agency, to develop specifically targeted Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) interventions, in direct response to the third strategic goal articulated above. Since the last quarter of 2018, WV has undertaken EORE in heavily affected counties of Ezo, Fashoda, and Juba. The main objectives are to: Advocate and communicate life-saving skills and education; and, Promote safe behaviour among women, girls, boys, and men, to reduce the risk of injuries and death caused by mines. In 2019 (up to September, per latest reporting), WV has reached 28,189 individuals with EORE messages (including: 8,262 boys, 7,690 girls, 5,390 men, and 6,947 women); during EORE sessions and assessments conducted, WV has identified and reported over 55 hazards (majority of which were discovered in Juba). WV will continue to prioritize advocacy for resourcing EORE, conducting outreach and awareness, and integrating EORE in existing operations.

2 - Activities

In alignment with the MASC & South Sudan National Mine Action Strategy, the project will contribute to Strategic Goal 3. As a non-technical actor, WV will continue to focus exclusively on EORE, integrating age & gender sensitive approaches to EORE with Child Protection, Education and other humanitarian programming. All EORE activities will be according to WV’s approved / desk accredited EORE SOPs (July 2018); further, at time of writing, WV is in the midst of planning refresher training/accreditation for mine action educators ahead of their mid-November 2019 expiry. The project will include dedicated EORE Teams to ensure prioritization of EORE objectives and compliance with International Mine Action Standards EORE Programming and Implementation (IMAS), Quality Assurance & Controls, reporting per IMSMA. Since the last quarter of 2018, WV had four EORE Teams in operation; EORE 1 in Ezo, EORE2 in Fashoda, and EORE3&4 in Juba. EORE Teams 1&2 are not currently active due to funding, but WV remains committed to advocacy and fundraising efforts to reach priority Tiers identified. EORE Teams 3&4 remain operational in Juba and are currently preparing for a refresher training for re-accreditation to be provided by approved UNMAS/NMAA trainers, with training plans to be reviewed and approved prior. Training will include Assessment and Quality Monitoring Systems. Each EORE Team includes 3 individuals (1 Team Lead, 2 Educators) who are mixed gender, local staff with ability to use local language/dialect. Currently, EORE 3&4 teams are comprised of 6 staff (2 male and 4 female). EORE will be based on completion of and informed by assessment, guided by EORE Working Group (NMAA, UNMAS) assessment tools and standards, and conducted inter-agency where recommended. EORE Programme Implementation will focus on an integrated EORE and Community Liaison approach, and integrated EORE and Child Protection (reference NTSG Chapter 4 Para 4.5.1). This approach shall ensure that there are complimentary activities to achieve the overall goal of reduction of mortality and morbidity from mines/UXOs. Stakeholder involvement and coordination with all relevant actors will be prioritized, ensuring those at most risk are identified by the local community. Communication strategy and channels use standard messages/materials and follow WV’s approved EORE SOPs, with specific consideration for communication strategies that consider the unique needs and patterns of women, girls, boys and men. For example, inclusion of the unique gendered risks women/girls are likely to face (ex. collecting firewood), compared to men/boys (ex. moving with cattle or small animals) in stories told. EORE session timing/location will also consider gender and age, for example targeting nutrition centres to reach women; farming plots/cattle markets to reach men; schools & CFS to reach in & out of school girls and boys. All EORE content and methods will follow approved IMAS/NTSG/EORE SOPs. The project will procure, print and/or receive in-kind approved EORE banners, leaflets and warning signs (international, local) to facilitate EORE. All EORE will be reported per EORE SOPs and IMSMA, including the completion and timely submission of EORE Weekly Report, Monthly Progress Reports. Reporting will also include for Hazards, Victim & Accident Reports per SOPs. In alignment with the NMAS 2018-2021, WV will also contribute to Strategic Goal 1 of increasing advocacy and communications on EORE, specifically the impact of mines/ERWs impact the safety and well-being of girls and boys. As a child-focused humanitarian actor with a global scope, WV will prioritize advocacy for resourcing EORE, streamlining in Child Protection and Education (schools), and other key priorities as identified by MASC, NMAA.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Providing EORE contributes to the realization of a number of civil and political, and economic, social, and cultural rights, which include the right to life and to personal integrity, freedom of movement, the right to an adequate standard of living, the right to education, among others. International human rights law goes on to afford special protections for persons affected by mines and ERW.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, prior to any EORE activities, assessments are completed to get buy in from communities, particularly communities who are displaced and / or who are planning to move in the near future, to ensure messaging is applicable, relevant, and timely.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

World Vision works alongside South Sudan's NMAA and as such is in line with the National Mine Action Strategy of 2018-2021.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

South Sudan’s National Mine Action Strategy (2018-2022) has three goals: (1) Advocacy and communication of South Sudan’s mine/ERW problem to continue through national and international awareness raising; (2) Size of the mine/ERW contaminated area is clarified, confirmed, and cleared; and, (3) Safe behaviour is promoted among women, girls, boys, and men to reduce mine/ERW accidents and promote safe livelihood activities. World Vision will be focused specifically on Goal 3, under which EORE activities fall, as well as contribute to Goal 1 via ongoing advocacy efforts.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

WV is proposing specifically to maintain EORE operations in existing areas of operation where WV has established presence, so that people in need / vulnerable populations have a comprehensive package of multi-sectoral life-saving interventions. As a community-based organization, World Vision has established community acceptance in the areas proposed, and applies ongoing context monitoring and crisis modification to respond to changes in the environment that would require hibernation or scaling back of activities, prioritizing the safety and protection of World Vision staff, volunteers, and communities. Given context outlined in the needs assessment and the sensitivity and risk of Mine Action interventions, the reality is that proposed areas are at high risk of contamination, but EORE teams are trained in safe practices.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project's sustainability is focused on the successful uptake of positive, protective behaviour practices that sees reduction of harm and death caused by tampering with UXOs. As World Vision proposed to conduct EORE in schools, teachers will be included in key messages, which they can reinforce once WV's sessions have completed.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Consultation is broad/representative through community mobilization/stakeholder engagement, particularly through CL and within Quality Monitoring System (QMS) implementation. EORE sessions areparticipatory and seek audience feedback from women, men, girls, boys during and at the conclusion of each session, not only to identify hazards but on the quality/approach of EORE interventions themselves. While Key Informant Interviews and feedback is collected from local authorities, community leaders and faith leaders, the majority of influencing data and feedback is sourced from women, men, girls and boys within the community broadly.
