Project Summary

Of a total population of over 4 million people in north-west Syria, it is estimated that around 2.8 million IDPs are Considered as people in need of humanitarian assistance. New and protracted IDPs share the same urgent needs, in an area that has limited shelter options and where the fighting has caused the destruction of civilian infrastructure, including health facilities, schools, bakeries, and has disrupted the provision of basic services. . With an increase in hostilities and deteriorating economic situation coupled with harsh weather conditions that have been severely damaging IDPs’ shelter and properties, the population is now facing more difficulties in addressing their basic needs. Bad weather conditions and fire-related incidents continue to affect the displaced population, while new displacements continue to be tracked. IDA as an organization that is working in the health sector and as the protection sector is considered integrated into the health sector IDA will provide integrated protection services which include specialized Gender-based violence (GBV) and Child Protection (CP) case management services, which will be focused on the vulnerability cases and those who are survived from the Gender-based violence- and will be for all the categories who are suffering from the violence and its type.
This program aims to build the resilience and affected from the war by providing a holistic approach of the Psychological Support intervention and Prevention, and for people who are suffering from psychological disorders because of the permanently displaced through the sessions and safe referrals which target Women, men, girls, boys, and PWSNs.
Group work will provide space for beneficiaries with similar issues, problems, or circumstances to come together for mutual support. Outreach services will spread information on positive mental health and protection risks, as well as assist in reaching the most vulnerable in communities and making appropriate referrals to services. Training will target both community members and front-line service providers to increase their capacity to help and support others. Awareness-raising activities aim to decrease stigma around accessing services by encouraging people to be more aware of protection risks faced. Activities will be accessible by all, efforts committed to creating an accessible environment for PWD and elderlies and balanced between the staff gender for better inclusion the communities.
The activities are covering; psychosocial support, community outreach, group work, awareness-raising activities, training for service providers and community members on mental health and protection topics, and integrated mental health services for the most vulnerable people, including people with disabilities, people facing mental conditions, psychological distress and trauma, also will target children and women assessed to be at high risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect or violence, children found to be associated with armed forces and armed groups, unaccompanied or separated children, persons with mental disorders or psychological distress, the elderly, chronically ill and people with disabilities. All activities will be based upon the principles of the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings, Minimum Standards for Child Protection, and Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action and GBV SCSOPs and guidelines.

5 - Implementation of activities

Case Management: Integrated GBV, CP, PSS CM will be provided to women, men youth, and children including CWD by Case Managers with close supervision by team leaders and Technical advisers. The services will be integrated into PHCs centers for access, confidentiality, with strong referral mechanisms to services such as CMR and specialized Mental Health and other services available in the areas. Community-based Awareness Activities and Outreach: Community-based group PSS sessions targeting the wider community will be facilitated by CM with technical support provided by TLs to response to collective trauma, lack of social cohesion, shared stressors and difficult living conditions, aiming to promote resiliency, limit psychological effects of conflict, reduce protection risks and stigma and provide an avenue for support adopting GBV SC awareness raising toolkit. Capacity Building,IDA will provide training to service providers, humanitarian staff stakeholders, community members in order to build capacity, raise awareness, improve referral mechanisms and strengthen coordination. Trainings include protection mainstreaming, GBVPFA for Children,Coping Skills,CSG,PSEA.MEAL team will conduct frequent field visits to collect data and monitor the activities implementation using checklists, observation and interviews with beneficiaries and staff and share notes with project management for corrective action and follow-up. reporting willbe to 4Ws and sub-clusters using their tools.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Maintaining a gender balance in the organization staff to ensure better inclusion and mitigate the optional risk of PSEA and in order to ensure a comfortable atmosphere for the beneficiaries. The organization has an established community-based feedback and response mechanism to include a PSEA hotline to report suspected cases and no tolerance towards SEA. The organization's staff, partners, and sup-partners are required to sign a code of conduct and receive training in child safeguarding and SEA protection with clear protocols on reporting potential SEA of beneficiaries, staff and children staff, and children.1- Active PSEA reporting system available in all the community centers and have clearly visible signs with guidance on how to register complaints and feedback.2- BNFs has access to reporting phone number and feedback boxes3- Training the staff and providing awareness to the communities 4- Mixed-gender follow-up committee 5- Coordination and communication with PSEA working group6- IDA PSEA and safeguarding policy is applied.7-accessible environment and CBFMs for PWDs including CWDs.
