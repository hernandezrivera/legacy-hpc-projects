Project Summary

Save the Children will support primary and community care services in underservices areas affected by food insecurity and at risk of COVID 19 in Kapoeta North and South and Budi in Eastern Equatoria, Nyirol and Akobo in Jonglei and Tonj East, North and South in Warrap state.
SC will focus on the following objectives:
Reduce excess mortality and morbidity in epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities.
Improve access and utilization of integrated life-saving quality health care services to crisis-affected and vulnerable populations through static, mobile and community-based health service delivery systems
Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled, mental health disorders and the elderly.

1 - Needs

The situation in South Sudan has worsened since 2020. Despite a progressive peace process and increased interest by development donors the number of people in need in South Sudan in 2021 has increased due to the food insecurity exacerbated by the economic impact of COVID-19, extensive flooding which has affected 800,000 people between July and October 2020 and continued inter-tribal conflicts. This has led to economic migration and conflict displacement resulting in large population movement, and a serious food security situation in 23 counties, with an estimated 4,024,000 now in IPC stage 4 with six counties reaching an estimated 1,785,000 people in IPC stage 5. 45 counties are predicated to be IPC 4 between April and July. 2020 saw an increased rainfall in 39 counties, most of which are still recovering from the effects of the floods of previous years. Affected communities have poor or no access to health services. There are increased rates of diarrhoea and pneumonia due to poor hygiene, malaria due to flooding other environmental changes which exacerbate increasing malnutrition. Malaria prevalence has almost double since 2017. Essential preventative measures against mortality associated with malnutrition include measles and pentavalent immunization which are at a low coverage rate of 45%, with key vulnerable states at only 20% South Sudan Covid-19 cases have risen to more than 8000 cases, almost double in the cases in 2020. in the first 3 months of 2021. Cases are under reported, given the national capacity to promote, conduct surveillance and test. A recent sero-prevalence in survey in Juba reported a rate of 22%. While there have only been 100 deaths, there are limited infectious disease units with limited oxygen systems and only 2 ventilators in South Sudan. Should there be a severe outbreak there would be limited capacity to respond. The impact of COVID-19 has worsened the countries already weak economy. COVID-19 has been of greater prevalence in men, this is possibly because men travel more but perhaps due to the difficulties women experience in accessing heath care but this gender balance is now changing. Poverty, food insecurity and conflict increase mental health and psychosocial issues and the need for services. A higher number of suicides were reported as a result of flooding, mostly among men. Domestic violence including sexual violence is linked to stress in the home. Health staff need training, and access to drugs, guidelines, technical support, referral pathway in order to support effective health services in communities. Women, particularly adolescent girls and women living with disability are more vulnerable to domestic violence, while adolescent girls are particularly concerned with the heightened risk of child early forced marriage. The ongoing political-economic conflict in South Sudan restricts public health funding whereby government expenditure on health remains under 2% and services are under-resourced. A number of important policies and plans are underway which need support, such as the national malaria strategy, the new GAVI strategy which includes introduction of two important vaccines, pneumococcal and rotavirus and the national family planning costed plan. Warrap State, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Aweil South and Jonglei state have the highest acute food insecurity level in South Sudan, with Tonj South, East and North in Northern Bhar el Ghazal, and Akobo and Pibor in Akobo at IPC 4 levels of acute food insecurity, with 10% of this population at IPC 5. Kapoeta East, South, North, Budi and Counties in Eastern Equatoria and Nyirol County in Jonglei are also vulnerable as estimated to be IPC 4 between March and July and with communities without access to health services.

2 - Project activities

1. Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and response SC will provide disease surveillance, prevention and control in all locations in which it will be operating and in collaboration with the MoH, WHO surveillance officers, community leaders and key partners. SC will ensure timely reporting through the EWARNs, IDSR and DHIS2. SC and cluster partners will support risk communication, surveillance, referral and case management for COVID-19 and other key communicable disease of outbreak potential notably measles, cholera and malaria. SC will aim to have an even gender mix in the surveillance teams. SC will support life-saving essential health care services including management of non-communicable diseases through primary health care centres, PHCUs and mobile healthcare services, community case management for children under five, procurement of essential medical kits and training of health workers. Warrap with HFO: Tonj East: Static sites:Akier PHCU-Palal Payam; Mariik PHCU-Ngapagaok Payam;Palam PHCU-Wunlit Payam; Mapara PHCU- Paliang Payam.Mobile Clinic Sites: Pan-Mourwel Malou Payam; Mangen, Paweng Payam Tonj South: Manyangook PHCC, Kilo 7 PHCU and Lingir PHCU. Tonj North: Madhiath, Manlor and Majok-nyan PHCUs. Jonglei with CRADANyirol: Keth and Rim PHCUs, 2 mobile clinics and outreach. Akobo East,and West: Walgak PHCC Yidit, Tangnyang Kaikuny Burmath Thoklil Thokwath PHCUs with outreach. Eastern Equatoria with OPENKapoeta South: NachekutNorth: mobile services in Lokwaor Lomyen, Karukomuge East: Magos Lolim, Nakabangole, Nanyangachapor,and LotimoBudi, outreach. SC will support the MoH basic service package, prioritising life saving activities, malaria in alland malaria, pneumonia and diarrhoea in children under five, through community case management and integrated management of childhood illness (IMNCI) SC will work with the National Malaria Programme and secure sufficient mosquito nets treatment. SC will ensure oxygen treatment at PHCC and intravenous treatment for severe pneumonia. SC will support referrals providing transport and funds.SC will support rational drug use and management with warehouse maintenance, pharmacy staff, staff training, temperature control mechanisms, monthly facility delivery, stock checks, supervision and consumption reports and ensure timely supplies. SC will support maternal and neonatal health services across the continuum, supporting ANC, skilled birth attendance, BeMONC services, PNC including community newborn care and family planning, SGV promotion and services. SC will prioritize immunization focusing on measles 6 months to 15 years, ensuring sufficient vaccinators per clinic, cold chain support, campaign and catch up activities. SC will integrate MHPSS services in health facilities and community services. SCwill hold specific records and clinic times for chronic disease management to facilitate continuity in care IPC in facilityies will include provision of supplies, guidelines and training focused on protection from COVID-19, monitoring using score cards, and including support and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities. Nationally approved IEC materials and guidelines will be used in facilities and community risk communication and community engagement activities. Views of the communities are taken into consideration in the provision of health services through community meeting, focus group discussions, exit interviews and facility suggestion boxes.Technical and operational assistance to heath facilities is monitored through supportive supervision visits with county MoH teams to ensure quality. Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using integrated community case management coordinating with Boma Health Initiative programmes. Provision of rehabilitation services and assertive devices to ensure access for disabled patients.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

• An initial consultative meeting with community leaders and representatives from different community members including gender, age and disabilities starts the project. Here, the focal person for PSEA discusses the confidential whistle blowing reporting mechanisms for sexual exploitation and abuse. SC contractors, staff and partners are trained and sign our policy. Throughout the project people of different vulnerabilities, focus groups and health committee are consulted to monitor PSEA breaches.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

SC community health workers and staff conducting outreach services, surveillance and will seek disabled, chronically ill, women head households and adolescent mothers. Community health committees will include vulnerable members. 20% of exit interviews will be from vulnerable patients. Clinic dispensers will support disabled and elderly patients to complete suggestion box recommendations and complaints, midwives will actively support illiterate women and vulnerable groups to feedback on services.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

• An initial consultative meeting with community leaders and representatives from different community members including gender, age and disabilities starts the project. The objectives, deliverable and accountability system is presented. A feedback and compliant mechanism is managed; beneficiaries are supported to feedback on complaints and recommendations through health committee meetings, suggestion boxes, focus group discussions and patient exit interviews.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

• Information is collated, analysed and added to project action plans monthly. Beneficiaries are informed of steps taken. The action plan is shared with MoH, partners and health committee. The health committee and MoH conduct a quarterly facility and service audit with SC to verify measures taken against the action plan and SC quality framework.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

SC’s multi-sectoral approach helps in ensuring that we meet most of the needs of the targeted beneficiaries including protection, inclusion and equality. Save the children during the initial stages of the projects conducts a general risk assessment and once the project is assessed to cause risks to both the beneficiaries and the organization, mitigation measures are always employed. In previous projects, mitigration measures such as clear and open communication with the beneficiaries will help in protecting the beneficiaries from being exploited, choosing locations for registration and distribution which are close and safe for the beneficiaries and an inlcussive decision making process with the beneficiaries. Save the children also works with the government and the community leaders to ensure that adequate information reaches the beneficiaries and the community leaders usually take responsibility of managing the crowds as the community members listen to them more. During distributions and registrations, vulnerable groups such as sick, disabled, pregnant and breastfeeding mothers, children and the elderly are prioritized so that they spend little time in waiting. Confidentiality is also key to protecting the beneficiaries and save the children normally conducts thorough training for the staff before any activities in the field which includes data protection policies etc.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

SC will support SGBV services, training staff on clinical management of rape, securing supplies through the RH working group, providing protocols and job aids, promoting th eservice in the community particularly with adolescent girls, vulnerable women such as women head households and disabled women. SC will integrate MHPSS services, training staff on psychological first aid and diagnosis and treatment of anxiety and depression with strong referral and follow up where possible. SC will be supported by SC regional MHPSS resources.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

Provision of public health support as close to the community as possible, focusing on morbidity associated with malnutrition, namely respiratory tract infection, pneumonia and diarrhea in children and measles and pentavalent immunization. Reproductive health services prevent unwanted pregnancies and support to women to prevent anaemia and obstetric complications, preventing maternal mortality and morbidity. Malaria, diarrhea and chronic disease, including mental health limit people’s ability to work and involve expenditure on transport to health facilities and purchase of medications if free medications are not available. Illness, disability food security and malnutrition perpetuate eachother in a vicious cycle. Increased cases of COVID-19 increase the risk of required lockdown measures limiting access to work and children’s opportunity for education increasing the risk of food insecurity in the short and long term. Risk communication and community engagement surveillance and protection of health staff prevents this risk.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

• SC are able to support an integrated services providing nutrition, WASH, protection and food security services in the same location, referring between sectores and linking sectoral community systems either directly with SC programmes and coordination with partners working in the selected operational areas. • The project will identify and address risks for people with disabilities and older persons and other age gender and socio-economic diversities. People with disabilities, older persons and/or their representative organizations will be consulted during the project cycle. SC will ensure at least 20% health committees are women representatives, people living with disability and adolescents and focus group discussions include vulnerable women, adolescents and people living with disability. Community health workers are required to find and visit people living with disability in their communities to ensure inclusion in surveillance and outreach services. • SC will support chronic disease, supporting health seeking behaviour in the elderly and effective diagnosis, strong referral systems and follow up. Training will enable staff to diagnose elderly patients correctly, addressing multiple symptoms, ensuring continuity of care with a in a dedicated chronic disease service system between clinic and community. • RH services will actively seek vulnerable adolescents and women living with disability to join focus group discussions and access health services. SGBV service training, protocols, job aids and promotion will focus on adolescent girls and vulnerable women such as women head households and disabled women. • Community health workers are required to find and visit people living with disability in their communities. Rehabilitation of latrines and water points will take disability into account. • Beneficiary data is disaggregated by gender, age and disability.
