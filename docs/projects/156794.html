Project Summary

Economic livelihoods across Syria have been severely impacted by the crisis. This impact is particularly apparent in the agriculture and livestock sector. Increases in input and production costs, and disruptions in value chains, coupled with reduced availability of power and water resources are all factors leading to decreases in production levels, poor availability of locally produced foods, rising sector-related unemployment, and placing added strain on already weak social structures and communities in general.
Taking into consideration the above trends, the project aims to improve livelihoods and food security of 1100 vulnerable female and male producers within rural Hama and Tartous through about 2000 interventions. Building upon the strong linkages between the rehabilitation of local economy and household livelihoods, and food security, R2R project aims to enable vulnerable households to protect, sustain and re-establish livelihoods within a longer-term resilience building goal. As such, this action is developed to:
1. Bridge the gaps within local value chains, namely knowledge, information and human resources by implanting the following activities:
1.1. Establish Farmer Field Schools (FFSs) in the targeted villages
1.2. Provide agriculture extension workers with technical training and equipment
2. Improve access to agricultural and livestock productive assets and inputs, particularly for females and youth. Under this target the following activities will be implemented:
2.1. Provide basic agricultural, livestock and food production inputs for rural households in the target villages.
2.2. Established and equippe community owned agricultural and livestock productive assets in the target areas villages of rural Hama Tartous
The villages of Qub Al Hat, AL Gawi, Marj Matar, Alkafat, and Jadooaa within the District of Salamieh/ Hama Governorate, in addition to Jemmaseh Keblieh, Bait Abo Khalil, Kawkab AlHawa, Kherbet Alfaras within Al Kadmous and Al Skeik Bader Districts/Tartous Governorate have been identified as project locations for R2R. those proposed villages satisfy a specific set of criteria that AKF (Syr) has established for communities where the resilience model could be employed with minimal risk:
• A mix of vulnerability segments and hosting IDPs with relatively a large number of vulnerable women headed households.
• A history of social activity and implementing community-based projects
• Agriculture and livestock are the predominant livelihoods activities
• Potential for diversified agricultural and livestock activities
• Accessible and safe (e.g. no mine risk)

Needs

The Syrian crisis is categorized as a Complex Emergency which continues to be characterized by major displacement, damage to vital infrastructure, and an increase in people’s vulnerabilities and poverty levels. According to the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (UNOCHA) 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) report, 13.5 million civilians are in need of humanitarian assistance while half the population forced from their homes, with many being displaced multiple times. The overall GDP loss of the country is estimated at USD 163.3 billion, out of which USD 49.7 billion was generated in 2015. Experts estimate that even if the war stopped today, reconstruction would take at least 15 years and about USD 180 billion to bring Syrian GDP back to pre-conflict levels. Even more, since the onset of the conflict, Syria has lost four decades of human development gains and fallen into extreme poverty, where almost 85% of the population are living in poverty, of which, 69% are living in extreme poverty and 35% in abject poverty. Within the Syrian context, agriculture and its related sub-sectors have always made critical contributions to national, regional, and local economies. However, surging input prices, abandonment of productive lands, disruptions in domestic supply chains, a poorly functioning market, and lack of access to new technologies and practices, are rapidly degrading and eroding the agricultural sector, placing immense strains on both urban and rural households and entrenching food insecurity. Fuel price has significantly increased from 9 SYP/L prior to the crisis to 200 SYP/L in March 2017 . Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) estimated cereal production to be 55% lower than pre-conflict averages, whilst livestock numbers have fallen by 30% for cattle, 40% for sheep, and 60% for poultry due to unavailability of livestock feed and access to pastoral lands. Estimates indicate that the sector has endured nearly USD 16 billion in revenues due to damage and loss since the onset of the crisis. The 5 targeted villages of Salamieh district cover an area of 8,150 ha with an estimated population of 11, 143 households, (55,715 people) with 5,075 IDPs. There are around 8,469 families who are considered vulnerable, with the key drivers of vulnerability being displacement, chronic illness, lack of livelihood opportunity, female headed households, and host family overcrowding. Out of the total number of vulnerable households, 5,572 are considered farming households. The target villages are mixed ethnically and religiously. There are urban dwellers, traditional village farmers, and Bedouin who reside in those villages. AKF has worked previously with farmers in these villages, finding that they are willing to try new methods to improve livelihoods. Prior to the crisis, the target villages boasted a diversified agriculture base, high level of food security, and strong trading relationships with other communities. The production of cereal crops, vegetables, olive and livestock are the main drivers of the local economy. The other location of the project is Tartus which did not witness armed conflict, and therefore, is not considered a high priority location. However, the economic implications of the protracted crisis is beyond geographies. This is particularly reflected in the 2019 HNO assessment which shows that in Tartus there are around 100,000 food insecure people, in addition to more than 36,000 people at risk of food insecurity. A large percentage of in-need households are female headed. The project will target five villages in rural Tartus, Sheikh Badr, and Kadmous. According to AKF assessment, the targeted villages have high poverty and low income levels, and the main source of income is olive production and cattle breeding.

Implementation of activities

In order to expand farmers’ agricultural and livestock technical knowledge and promote for best management practice, Farmer Field Schools (FFS) will be established and invested in as platforms for learning among local communities. Agriculture and flock management best practices will be introduced to farmers who are still largely reliant on traditional methods of farming through a partnership with the Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reforms (MAAR), 20 FFSs will be created in 10 targeted villages. 2 FFSs will be established in each village depending on the village need and catchment area. In total, 500 farmers, including 30% females, will be identified through FFSs and benefit from improved technical knowledge on agriculture practices in 2019. Furthermore, to address the limited agricultural service provision, agricultural extension systems will be strengthened to improve the quality of agricultural services. System strengthening will focus on improving the quality of agricultural services provided through extension workers by providing technical training and equipment for 40 agriculture extension workers. R2R will improve olive production by providing required inputs for 800 small-scale olive farming households. The target olive farmers will be supported by providing plastic jerrycans and olive milling coupons in order to enable them to press around 2 tons of olive produced. In order to improve overall health of household livestock, the project will support 200 rural livestock farmers (of which approximately 20 % are female) with veterinary kits and a total of 100 ton of balanced feed which meets the energy and protein requirements for dairy cows. Also, the project will endeavor to improve the capacity of 100 females from the target villages, out of which 75 are IDPs, to establish their agro-processing unites. Under this activity, technical and business training along with basic materials and equipment required to produce molasses, dry vegetables, Zaater , orange juice and milk products will be provided. R2R Project will establish and equip six community management groups (i.e. Community Based Organizations, CBOs) that are representative of the ethnic, social and gender composition of the 5 targeted village. the CBOs, assisted by the Project, will establish and equip 20 community owned agricultural and livestock productive assets groups with around 400 beneficiary households. Community assets creation (e.g. olive harvesters, shredders for olive pruning, refrigerators, electrical shearer machines) will facilitate community access to critical production equipment; consequently, improve their capability of food production and enhance farming households’ resilience against economic shocks. Moreover, the creation of community owned productive assets promotes social cohesion in the target areas. Group members from different ethnic and religious backgrounds will work together to manage community productive assets. This action is perceived as instrumental in strengthening agricultural and livestock value chains by increasing access to critical production equipment and maintaining productive assets.

Protection Risk Analysis

A: the project beneficiaries may be exposed to discrimination from other farmers due to not knowing the criteria of choosing beneficiaries and who are not supported or equipped with such agricultural inputs. Likelihood: Low; Impact: Low. With the help of CBOs, AKF can strengthen the social cohesion between these community groups by orienting the male and female beneficiaries to transfer knowledge of new agricultural methods to other farmers in their community.B: Women could face some difficulties in using the new agricultural equipment and seek the help from other male farmers at some points which make them exposed to exploitation. Likelihood: Low, Impact: Medium. Confidential regular monitoring assessments will help in investigating any cases and take actions towards this.C: Due to gender related traditional norms, female farmers could be underestimated in terms of farm work so they could suffer from verbal abuse or bothering by other male farmers during working together Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. AKF will monitor the implementation process particularly the women contribution and the restriction to it and take actions such as impeded awareness raising or orientation sessions to the targeted group in this term.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement of staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: AKFS regularly monitors the regional and local security situation using a variety of primary and secondary sources. In the event that mobility of staff and beneficiaries in program areas is deemed a high risk, AKFS will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is able to be reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Likelihood: Low, Impact: High Risk 2: Limited availability of quality goods, services, equipment and difficulties experienced in successfully procuring required items for delivery to Salamieh and other program areas.Mitigation: AKDN has extensive experience in rural livelihoods programming in extremely difficult environments. AKFS will seek to source from local suppliers and when required expand procurement tendering offers to within the Governorates. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Risk 3: Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Mitigation: AKFS regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the programme and communicate with EC as needed. Likelihood: High, Impact: low
