Project Summary

As per the HNO 2024 the number of people in need has increased to 16.7 million. 15.3 million people are in need for protection.
After 12 years of hostilities and persistent economic crisis, people in Syria saw their situation worsen when a series of earthquakes hit northern Syria and Türkiye on 6 and 20 February 2023. The earthquakes uprooted hundreds of thousands of families, resulted in almost 5,900 deaths and more than 12,800 people injured in Syria, and severely damaged infrastructures already weakened by the conflict. Many families lost their main breadwinner due to death or injury, at a time when the economic situation was already dire, increasing the vulnerability of millions of people previously unable to meet their basic needs. The ongoing economic crisis is also worsening negative coping mechanisms and particularly affecting female-headed households while contributing to the normalization of gender-based violence and child exploitation. Due to the difficulties that faced the Syrians the protection need became one of the highest needs especially in Aleppo and Rural Damascus. 1) SOS CV Syria aims to support people through having two community centers in Aleppo, AlShaar and Tal AlZarazer and one in Rural Damascus. SOS will specify a scoring system to assess people approaching the center in order to provide them with cash for protection. The scoring system will depend on the living conditions, psychosocial risks, and health. The center will provide sessions targeting pregnant women regarding reproductive health with dignity kits and will provide sessions for affected people related to GBV, parental care skills, MHPSS, and child rights as an activity of protection and prevention in addition to child friendly spaces, referrals, and case management. For legal activities their will be referrals to the local NGOs working on legal activities. The GBV, MHPSS and parental care skills trainings will be done based on the newly developed national manuals with consultations and referrals in safe spaces. 2) In addition to the community center, there will be a targeting for the schools to provide them with child rights awareness sessions for parents, teacher, and children. There will be recreational activities and school bags distributions for children and supplies for the school such as whiteboards, pens, papers, etc. SOS met different stakeholders in Aleppo to assess needs in the education system and it was noticed that the main risk is the lack of electricity. To ensure the stability of the education and the protection of the children especially with the risks related to harassment, and abuse, SOS will provide schools with solar system. The intervention will be implemented on two years, however, the target and the budget will be determined by end of 2024 to ensure the needs are aligned with HNO and sector needs.
PSEA is one of the main risks that can affect our participants and prevent them from receiving their assistance and their rights in a dignified way. SOS established a feedback mechanism that empowers the participants’ voice and allows them to reach the organization and report any possible incidents both anonymously and personally through the different channels. SOS staff are trained on the mechanisms and their importance, in addition to having an Accountability to Affected population assistant that is responsible for community briefings and ensuring the assistance is suitable, and reachable for different participants, and the feedback mechanisms are known, suitable, and reachable. Some major risks that might affect the program and are based on the sector risk matrix are as following:
-Risk: Child and Adult Safeguarding risks that might affect the participants
Likelihood: High, Impact: high
Mitigation: Continuous training and application of Child Safeguarding (CS) Policy and Sexual Misconduct Regulation, Feedback and Complaints mechanism in place, CS Focal Point actively consulting project plan and implementation.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Potential risks that might occur to the programme and can affect the quality of the implementation: -Risk: Participants are asked for a sexual or other favors in exchange of assistance Likelihood: Low, Impact: High Mitigation: SOS ensures all staff are trained on PSEA, aware of the SoPs and code of conduct. SOS has zero tolerance with such cases. - Risk: Participants are not able to reach SOS feedback mechanisms or are afraid of reporting. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Mitigation: SOS will ensure the different channels of the feedback mechanism that take into consideration the confidentiality and the protection of the reporter, as well as seeking the advice from the community on the preferred and most suitable mechanism. Ensuring the participation of participants in all phases. - Risk: Content of messages related to the sessions is not fully adapted to the social and cultural environment and creates misunderstanding or even tensions and has repercussions on the organization especially regarding child rights. Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Mitigation: The community briefings and FGDs will support to understand the community. In addition to depending on national manuals and expert people in the field with the support of the sector. -Risk: Exclusion of some groups due to the choice of the location Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Mitigation: SOS will coordinate with the key people with the support of the AAP assistant to ensure the inclusion of all groups and will design the programme and the location based on the targeted group. -Risk: Lack of a appropriate intervention due to inadequate service provision and inadequate capacity, high turnover and loss of capacity lack of specialized and well-trained staff/service provider in these particular areas of expertise Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation: Depending on HNO data, assessments, FGDs, and sector needs to determine the services needed. Adjust salaries to the market value, identify consortium partners and local NGOs to support the implementation and provide continuous trainings for staff. -Risk: Long administrative processes due to government regulations and delays in approvals Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation: Ensure close coordination with relevant governmental entities to prevent delay in issuing the approvals. -Risk: Breach of confidentiality putting at risk SGBV survivors due to lack of capacity on information management of service providers Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation: Case management with survivors to be conducted by trained personnel only with adequate and adaptable supervision protocols and data sharing policy in place. -Risk: Tension within the family, including risks to physical violence or safety and disruption of the previous family dynamic (esp. On cash assistance). Likelihood: Low, Impact: high. Mitigation: When targeting individuals for services, ensure a holistic family based approach where possible and proper consultation and communication.
