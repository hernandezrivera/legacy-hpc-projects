Project Summary

WFP and partners will implement Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programme (TSFP aiming to treat acutely malnutrition and improve nutrition status of children 6-59 months of age, pregnant and lactating mothers. TSFP prevents these vulnerable individuals from developing severe acute malnutrition, reduce morbidity and mortality in this group and provide follow up monitoring for the recovered children with severe acute malnutrition. WFP and partners will also implement a preventive Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme (BSFP) for children under 2 years of age, and pregnant and lactating mothers targeting vulnerable IDPs and rural livelihoods with high acute malnutrition rates and high food insecurity. BSFP will be implemented to reduce the incident of acute malnutrition and reduce excess morbidity and mortality among the vulnerable groups.

Needs

The recent post GU assessment results indicate that food security has improved due to average or above rainfall between April and June 2018. Overall, the level of acute malnutrition improved from critical levels in post GU 2017 median GAM was 17.4percent to serious levels in post GU 2018 with a median GAM of 14percent. However, acute food insecurity persists among the populations who lost most of their animals at the height of the 2017 drought. The low lying Shabelle region was affected by river and flash floods during the GU rains. FSNAU post GU survey indicated that twelve out thirty-three populations had critical levels of GAM between 15-29.9percent. Seven were IDP settlements of Bossaso, Baidoa, Dollo, Galkayo, Garowe, Mogadishu and Qadho. Nutrition actors on ground attributed this to food insecurity, limited health services, poor EPI coverage, increased morbidity, poor health seeking behaviour and lack of access to safe water. Five were rural livelihoods of Guban pastoral, Northern Inland Pastoral, Hawd Pastoral, Beletwein Riverine, and North Gedo Riverine. Morbidity rates were high >20percent in many parts of the country but the highest levels were recorded in North East and Central. WFP is seeking additional funding to complement and expand the current lifesaving curative and preventive services. Activities will include systematic screening through a network of community health workers who conduct active case finding at household level and follow up beneficiaries. At the health centre level all children and pregnant and lactating mothers are screened for malnutrition. Treatment services are offered through fixed sites and WFP has established outreach mobile treatment clinics in the hard to reach areas. In the IDP sites, WFP work together with UNICEF to offer an integrated service for treatment of malnutrition, WASH and hygiene, IYCF and health services to address the underlying causes. Gender equality will be a cross-cutting theme in TSFP and BSFP.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Beneficiaries/caretakers in the nutrition programme will be empowered to take part in solving the health and nutrition problems affecting them. Pregnant and lactating mothers will be part of the BCC program where they learn the causes of malnutrition and how to address them at their level. Part of this messaging will be eating a diversified diet, good hygiene practices, exclusive breast feeding, regularly attending ante-natal and post-natal clinics and being linked with livelihoods programmes that promote nutrition security at the household level. Taking part in the process of solving the health and nutrition problems affecting them enhances their ability, opportunity and dignity in the society. Men will also be targeted with BCC so that they are sensitized on health and nutrition problems. The nutrition program will build on the capacity of the affected people to take part in solving the health and nutrition problems affecting them. This will address resilience at individual, household and at community level. Beneficiaries discharged from the treatment programmes will be referred to food security and livelihood projects. Where feasible, employment opportunities will be fostered through skills training based on assessed market needs, taking into consideration the participation of women and addressing gender norms to promote gender equality. This approach will enable beneficiaries to move away from assistance once they obtain employment and sufficient income to meet their own needs, and it will build durable solutions by improving human capital. The urban safety net will inform the development of a future scalable national social safety net under the Government’s social protection framework and the resilience umbrella of the NDP. Collaboration with the government on programme implementation and capacity strengthening on policy development and implementation and monitoring of the nutrition activities. The government has taken the leadership of nutrition services delivery both at the Federal and at the federal member state level. WFP will work together with the government to support the holistic approach to nutrition programming. WFP share all the monthly reports with the government.WFP will combine humanitarian relief and development. In times of shocks and crisis, WFP will respond and continuously addressing the underlying causes of vulnerability while also building the capacity of people and the governments to better manage risks in the future.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

WFP Somalia receives regular feedback and complaints from the field during standard monitoring activities conducted by WFP and WFP-contracted monitors, and through third-party monitoring activities conducted by donors. To complement these essential sources of information and in line with the IASC principle of Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations (CAAP), WFP Somalia developed a complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) that enables a direct channel and two-way communication between affected populations and WFP. It aims to handle feedback and complaints from all stakeholders, including beneficiaries, community members, CPs and NGOs.Beneficiaries have the ability to report exclusions during monitoring visits, through the hotline. Sensitization strategy defines the plan for beneficiary sensitization about WFP programmes and the WFP CFM. Sensitization is carried out systematically by all parties involved in WFP project implementation (CPs as well as WFP staff at area office and liaison office levels), and it is part of the WFP Somalia Country Office communication strategy. Sensitization is done by way of disseminating sensitization posters at food distribution sites, by instructing beneficiaries during registration and enrolment activities. WFP plans to start releasing announcements through radio and other media. These sensitization materials inform beneficiaries and other project stakeholders about the beneficiary rights and their entitlements, about the WFP hotline numbers, and on how to report observed fraudulent conduct, possible corruption, abuse of power, sexual harassment or exploitation, discrimination or any other issues putting beneficiaries or WFP programmes at risk. The cases are followed electronically after being logged to ensure proper follow up.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk mitigation builds on WFP’s extensive experience in inclusive and transparent programme planning. Beneficiary targeting will be strengthened through greater community consultation and sensitization, drawing on the community based targeting guidance developed by the food security cluster and addressing gender inequalities in the targeting criteria. To address national partner capacity and presence, WFP has provided gender and protection training to staff and partners to strengthen their ability to deliver efficient and effective assistance in all locations. Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

To mitigate the risk of being unable to reach people in need, WFP engages with national and regional actors to underscore the neutrality and impartiality of its assistance. In areas where WFP has no direct access, WFP uses third party monitoring and deploys innovative transfer measures such as establishing catchment areas in accessible locations. All programmes are implemented in a conflict-sensitive manner (in line with the do no harm approach), accounting for local dynamics, through community consultations and partnerships that seek to increase access and ensure that WFP activities do not exacerbate tensions in the country. This also ensures WFP programmes link local dialogue into programme design, and reduce food insecurity as a driver of conflict, ultimately contributing to local peacebuilding. Poor infrastructure, conflict and road closures pose challenges to the delivery of in-kind assistance. To mitigate this, WFP uses alternative routes where feasible and adopts cash-based transfers where markets are functional. Additionally, WFP measures the feeling of safety and security of beneficiaries going to/at/coming from distribution sites as part of post distribution monitoring. This way, WFP knows if beneficiaries do not feel safe when getting their assistance and WFP can adjust project implementation accordingly. In addition, beneficiaries can report of conflict issues through the CFM.
