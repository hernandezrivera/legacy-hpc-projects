Project Summary

The intervention is based on the MSNA data (REACH, Sep. 2022) reporting that around 591.000 people in the Gaza Strip require humanitarian assistance to access adequate shelter which is an essential component of providing people with a minimum level of privacy, protection and dignity.
The actual needs and gaps were identified by PHC based on a needs assessment that has been carried out in April and September 2022 with the full and active participation of the stakeholders (MoSD, MPWH) who contributed to the identification of needs and actual gaps of interventions in different geographical areas. Available data shows that 16,005 families are living in substandard shelters in need of urgent rehabilitation and upgrading works, (27.6% in Gaza and North-Gaza).
The intervention aims to support vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity which is designed based on experiences of PHC and (WW-GVC) will entail the rehabilitation of damaged and/or substandard shelters to meet minimum shelter standards and respond to beneficiaries’ immediate needs, including an adaptation of shelters to meet the needs of PWDs, upgrade/separate shared toilet and kitchen facilities and improving the living area to include adequate isolation space for people at high risk of virus/diseases that may arise. The intervention aligns totally with the Specific Objective (SO2) identified by the Shelter Cluster.
The intervention will apply the Building Back Better (BBB) approach that commits to delivering quality, sustainable and resilient interventions to reduce future vulnerabilities of communities in the event of disasters. The project will significantly contribute to gender equality while including age and diversity considerations. The proposed intervention will involve three key components as described below:1. Responding to the need for shelter upgrading and rehabilitation for 100 vulnerable HHs living in substandard shelters at high risk of exposure to natural or manmade hazards, with the average cost of 2500 $/shelter; 2. Assisting 100 vulnerable HHs who live in partially damaged shelters due to May 2021 and August 2022 escalations. PHC will support the HHs to conduct rehabilitation works with an average of 3000 $/shelter. The rehabilitation or repairs works will include an adaptation of shelters to meet the needs of PWDs, elderly persons, increasing privacy, improving WASH facilities, repairing leaking roofs, doors and windows to protect the families from harsh weather. 3. Providing essential shelter NFIs restricted cash assistance (for an average of $500 ) the 200 HHs received the rehabilitation/ repair assistance. Each HH will receive a personalized NFIs package based on HH’s priority need, with coordination with Palestine Cash Working Group (CWG). The intervention will adopt the Self-help approach to achieve the maximum benefit of the allocated budget for each shelter. Direct beneficiaries include 200 HHs in 8 locations, a total of,1154 individuals. selected based on prioritization criteria of the Shelter Cluster based on HH and shelter vulnerability assessment tools (minimum standard), scoring, and ranking. Each HH will develop an individual rehabilitation plan based on specific needs to determine the cost of work. The AGD-specific needs will be transferred to engineers to conduct the relevant technical assessments before the implementation of the designed interventions.
The intervention will ensure that women, girl, children, PWDs and all beneficiaries are protected according to PSEA network recommendations and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) principles including training, dissemination of awareness materials, and safe access to complaints channel. PHC will carefully monitor the protection concerns (GBV, abuse, exploitation, and child labor) by building the capacity of the project’s team, partner, and targeted beneficiaries and applying referral approach.

1 - Needs

The right to adequate and protected housing is one of the basic human rights and it is one of the main priorities in the life of any human being “Everyone has the right to adequate living conditions”. Gaza is one of the most densely populated areas in the world, with a total estimated population of 2.17 million living on 365 km²of surfaces, hence with a population density of 5,936/km2 (PCBS, October 2022). Humanitarian needs and vulnerabilities in households in the Gaza Strip are mainly driven by the military occupation, the intra-Palestinian divisions, climate change, and, more recently the outbreak of COVID-19 and its consequences as well as May 2021 and August 2022 escalations. According to the (HNO) 2023 around 591,000 persons in Gaza lack access to adequate shelter based on the SC Minimum Standard on Shelter Repair and Upgrade. According to the (MoPWH) and the (MoSD) around 16,000 families are living in inadequate shelters not meeting the minimum standards of living space, risk of exposure to natural hazards, hygiene facilities and privacy. In addition to that, MoPWH declared that there is a shortage of 120,000 HUs which increases by 14,000 units yearly due to both the population growth and the repeated destruction of HUs in the event of escalations. Following August 2022 escalation, around 1,500 HUs were damaged (of which 97 units were severely damaged and 26 units were completely destroyed) causing the internal displacement of around 600 individuals. These HUs’ damages sum up to the 62,000 HUs affected by May 2021 escalation (of 916 HUs were severely damaged and 1,688 HUs were completely destroyed), which caused the internal displacement of 12,000 people of which 7,000 remain displaced in addition to 3,000 people still displaced from previous escalations. MoPWH reported that 924 demolished HUs and 12,300 damaged HUs during 2021 escalation are still lacking reconstruction funding as well as in need of repair of partial damages. These circumstances continue to put pressure on the available HUs, which therefore suffer from overcrowding, unhygienic and substandard conditions. Based on the (MSNA), 37.3% of the households in Gaza reported damage to their shelters due to the bombardment of May 2021. At present, 28.8% of those shelters are still in need of rehabilitation due to a lack of own resources or insufficient assistance received. In April 2022, the Palestinian Housing Council (PHC) conducted a thorough assessment to identify the “Housing needs of the most vulnerable families in the Gaza Strip ”. The survey reached 100 HHs living in inadequate shelters, namely suffering from water leakages, overcrowding, lack of privacy and/or safety which, in turn, increase gender and/or child protection risks. All shelters of the assessed HHs were found not to comply with at least one of the minimum shelter standards. Specifically, out of 100 HHs consulted: 32% needed urgent winterization interventions; 50% have toxic asbestos or metallic roof; 78% have leaking windows; 58% have leaking roofs; 47% have cracks or holes on the walls; 30% lack at least one room minimum with insulated roof against heat and cold; 47% have unfinished wall surface or very bad plaster; 24% have unsealed floor which causes the presence of insects and rodents; 23% do not have a kitchen with cleanable preparation counter or sink; 65% have broken or unfunctional WASH facilities; 50% toilets do not have tiled floors or walls which jeopardize hygiene conditions; 30% lack of sufficient water storage capacity ; 57% are not provided with solar or electric water heater, directly affecting daily personal hygiene, especially for women and children, as well as harming the proper cleaning and disinfection of the house environment. Overall, 59% of the targeted HHs live in overcrowded shelters, which do not guarantee the safety and privacy of the more vulnerable members; 39% of HHs suffer from unsafe electrical outlets and switches; 52% of HHs’ have no lockable doors and 55% windows for bedrooms; 22% of HHs do not have a door for bathrooms, thus do not allow privacy and safety; 91% of them present basic furniture in a bad condition.The records of (MoPWH) show that out of 844 affected families during the 2021 and 2022 escalations, 699 affected families are still living at partially damaged shelters in Gaza and North Gaza Governorates. Accordingly, the value of their shelter damages ranges between 2000-5000 $ for each unit. August 2022 escalation, PHC conducted a rapid assessment targeting 180 of these affected HHs (consisting of 1020 individuals, identified by MoPWH, to evaluate the immediate needs od assistance for shelter repair/rehabilitation. The field assessment visits confirmed that in addition to the damages that occurred in 2021 and 2022 escalations, the targeted HHs presented previous vulnerabilities of their shelters. Specifically, 78% of targeted HHs lack of access to adequate and safe WASH facilities (e.g. domestic water network, sewer system, gender-sensitive and PWD adapted bathrooms, functional kitchens with access to water), unsafe houses with bad conditions windows, doors, floors, ceiling, electricity networks, and painting as well as proper household equipment, furniture and appliances like mattresses, blankets, gas stoves, washing machine, etc.. All targeted HHs were exposed to protection risks linked to the inadequate shelter in which they are living. As such, 72% of the visited HHs reported negative impacts on the health and well-being of their members (e.g. physical and mental health issues as well as educational attainment) due to the poor housing conditions that expose them to lack of privacy, and harsh weather conditions among the others. 45% of the targeted families rely on the limited financial support provided by MoSD, which hardly covers their basic needs of daily living (like food, health, and clothing), but is not sufficient to rehabilitate and renovate sub-standard houses.

2 - Activities

The intervention will entail the rehabilitation of damaged and/or inadequate shelters (fully or partially) to meet minimum shelter standards and respond to beneficiaries’ immediate needs. Specifically, PHC and WWGVC will focus on the adaptation of shelters to PWDs’ needs, integrated interventions to protect HHs from extreme weather conditions and/or floods, upgrade/separate shared toilets, and improving kitchen facilities and the living areas in line with the “Cluster Objective 2” proposed by the Shelter Cluster for HRP 2023. While PHC will be the main applicant and will lead the overall project management, WW-GVC will contribute to ensure that WASH standards for HHs will be entailed durint the implementation of the action. Both partners will effectively collaborate in the planning, selecting of beneficiaries, technical assessment, implementation and monitoring of the activities. Preparation phase: The partners will coordinate with MoPWH and MoSD to identify 400 vulnerable and affected HHs, based on a set of agreed criteria among project partners and the ministries, and a sharing with the Shelter Cluster to avoid duplication.Building on this list of potential beneficiaries, PHC and WW-GVC technical and social teams (males/females) will conduct a joint verification field visits to the 400 identified HHs. This will serve to identify the HHs more in need based on the prioritization criteria of Shelter Cluster, based on HH and shelter’s vulnerability assessment tools (minimum standard), scoring and ranking.The PHC and WW-GVC will select a final list of 200 HHs that will be shared with the MoSD, MoPHW and Shelter Cluster. PHC and WW-GVC technical teams will then define precise needs for essential shelter NFIs, shelter repair and/or adaptation through the production of (BoQs) and clear work plans that are suitable to each targeted HHs’ needs. Activity 1PHC will provide (Restricted Cash Assistance) average of 2,500$/shelter to upgrade and rehabilitate shelters for 100 vulnerable HHs living in inadequate shelters at higher risk of exposure to natural hazards (weather extremes, flooding) or manmade hazards (escalations etc.). PHC will implement this component based on experience, technical guidelines and available shelter needs assessment of potential beneficiaries. The project team will provide technical support and supervision to the shelter upgrading through weekly field visits.Activity 2PHC will provide (Restricted Cash Assistance), average of 3,000$/shelter to repair and rehabilitate shelters for 100 vulnerable HHs living in partially damaged shelters due to previous Gaza escalations (May 2021 and August 2022). PHC will support the HHs to conduct rehabilitation works with a self-help modality. The interventions of repair and rehabilitation work for affected families will be conducted after the revision of the HHs nomination list by MoPWH and the field visits of the project team verifying the assessments of repair works and damaged items previously conducted by the MoPWH.In both activities 1 and 2 the work will include the adaptation of shelters to meet the needs of PWDs, elderly persons, increasing privacy, improving WASH facilities, repairing leaking roofs, doors and windows to protect the families from harsh weather and other summarization/winterizations intervention based on the technical assessment conducted in each HH. Activity 3:WW-GVC will provide the 200 HHs who received the rehabilitation/repair assistance and living in substandard and overcrowded shelters at higher risk an essential winterization/summarization assistance or shelter/household Non-Food Items (NFIs) average estimation 500 $ cash assistance) for manage overcrowding and improve access to basic household supplies and equipment. The value of the assistance will be determined based on HHs’ priority need according to the NFI Shelter Cluster standards, with coordination with Palestine Cash Working Group (CWG). NFIs cash assistance will be disbursed through two payments (50%,50%), the first one will be advanced payment after the preparing of NFI priorities plan, and the final payment will be paid after ensuring the purchasing process has been done through invoices and/or verification visits.WW-GVC communication and advocacy department will design and conduct a community awareness campaign and an advocacy program to amplify the outreach and impact of the intervention. The awareness activities will be directed to the targeted HHs member and will focus on personal and environmental hygiene (with separate sessions/informative materials that take into consideration AGD considerations), while the advocacy program will target communities, local authorities and donors on the right to proper shelter through workshops, advocacy events and production info-graphics showing statistics about the shortage of housing units, the high need for repair of substandard and damaged shelters. PHC will sign an agreement with the beneficiaries that will be enclosed with an individual rehabilitation plan. HHs will receive shelter rehabilitation through self-help and will be provided by basic technical instruction, and negotiation skills to strengthen their capacity in handling procurement of quality materials and managing rehabilitation work. PHC and WW-GVC will ensure that women, girl, children, PWDs and all beneficiaries are protected according to PSEA network recommendations and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) principles including training, dissemination of awareness materials, and safe access to complaints channel. PHC will carefully monitor the protection concerns (GBV, abuse, exploitation, and child labor) by building the capacity of the project’s team, partner, and targeted beneficiaries and applying referral approach.

10.1 - Do No Harm

The project design included a do-no-harm analysis to ensure full understanding of the context, power relations, and existing and possible threats to the various stakeholders. An effective and easy to use complaints and feedback handling mechanism is in place to allow vivid and transparent communication with the organization. In addition, the project staff are culture and gender sensitive as they are aware ad respectful of the local culture and are gender-balanced to ensure that women beneficiary are comfortable in communicating with PHC staff.PHC will ensure compliance by all parties to its Code of Conduct, as the staff will have to read, understand, and sign the Code of Conduct , while it will be shared and made public to all other stakeholders. 11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The Community Based Protection Approach (CPA) is a participatory methodology based on PHC and WW-GVC operational interpretation of the IASC definition of protection. An environment fully conducive to protection is obtained when multi-sector humanitarian and development needs of individuals and communities are met in a dignified and safe manner. As such, multi-sector needs are identified and ranked on the basis of the contextual protection risks of communities and individuals. A right-based analysis is used as entry point to elaborate local protection strategies to be then used as a programming framework for the definition of needs-based responses. The CPA was developed according to IHL, Humanitarian, Do No Harm and the following fundamental principles: 1. the safety of affected population (both of groups and individuals); 2. restore the dignity of affected population putting a stop to emerging or established patterns of violations (and/or alleviating their immediate effects); 3. place the empowerment of communities at risk at the core of any action to enable self-protection and realize their rights. The use of focus groups, random interviews and transect walks, ensures the involvement of the population in terms of location, age, gender, ethnicity, diversity, profession and power. According to the access to the population, the level of trust built and other do not harm considerations, multiple specific techniques will be applied to get deeper insight (bias reduction and/or data gap filling) of a particular group, a specific situation, a particular household. Throughout the assessment and planning phases, the needs of all community members will be taken into consideration. The proposed shelter and protection intervention address the urgent need of the targeted communities, accounting for non-discriminatory practices and considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. Providing a voice for each member of the community, and requesting their direct action in the project’s implementation, preserves the dignity of each beneficiary and fosters community empowerment. The distribution of Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) cards in the communities will ensure the accessibility to all beneficiaries to provide their feedbacks and complaints about the implemented project.Outputs of the assessment conducted in quarantine/isolation centres were verified with the health cluster and ICCG documents related to priorities in health sector; continuous coordination will be kept with these centers and related stakeholders to update the collected data and to respond to the most urgent NFI needs before designing and delivery of any response.
