1 - Needs

The proposed project will achieve its objectives through implementation of a package of interrelated activities in two sectors: Food and Livelihoods Security and Water, Sanitation and Hygiene, to timely address urgent needs of the affected population, and at the same time mitigate risks and stresses that could arise from the current crisis in Sudan. Shortages of safe water supply forces affected people to collect water for domestic use from unsafe sources, the communities are at high risk of water borne-diseases which in turn could aggravate the nutritional status of those who are vulnerable (i.e. children under five). Hygiene knowledge and practices are poor among the targeted people which also increases risks of diseases particularly for small children, pregnant and breast-feeding women and elderly. The risks of COVID-19 spread are higher in congested areas (i.e. especially with IDPs) with limited access to water supply and poor hygiene awareness and practices. So, the need to improve access to safe water and hygiene promotion, is very important under the current circumstances. according to CFSVA report (June 2022) the prevalence of severe food insecurity also increased from 5% to 15%. The localities with the highest prevalence of food insecurity are Kerenik (90%, also the site of the deadly April 2022 clashes); and Sirba (77%). The level of food insecurity in Central Darfur increased by 16 percent, from 43% in 2021 to 59% in 2022. The prevalence of severe food insecurity also increased from 5% to 14%. The localities with the highest prevalence of food insecurity are Bindisi (68%); Bindisi also has the highest level of severe food insecurity at 21%. The prevalence of food-based coping strategies increased by 32% (from 27 to 59%) and livelihood-based coping strategies by 33% (from 34 to 67%). In addition, food intake worsened, with the prevalence of inadequate food consumption increasing by 11% (from 42 to 53%) compared to one year ago. Fierce clashes erupted between SAF and RSF in multiple cities across Sudan (April 15, 2023) including Khartoum, Marawi, Al-Fashir, Al-Obeid, Nyala, Zalinge, Geneina, etc. This devastating development in Sudan resulted in killing of more than 3000 people, injury of 6,000 people, and displacement of about 2 million people inside and outside the country. Darfur and especially Geneina, West Darfur was severly impacted by the deadly conflict. Rports of thousands killed and hundereds of thousands displaced to other areas in Darfur, as well as to Chade situation is still very inflammed and unpredictable. The clashes characterized by looting and damaging public and private property by armed forces, including UN agencies and NGO offices. The international community is calling for a ceasefire and opening humanitarian passages to deliver aid to those affected, however so far there has been no positive reponse. The current disruption to lives, livelihoods, institutions, systems and programs will definitely have an impact on the fragile humanitarian situation in the target areas, and food insecurity has tremendously increased accorrding to the revised HRP issued on 17 May 2023. People in need of humanitarian assitance in Sudan increased fom 15.8 m to 24.7 m and people in need of FSL services from 11.7 m to 19.9 m. There are shortages in access to water, fuel, cash, and health services, which will exacerbate the dire situation of people directly affected by conflict and all across Sudan if this crisis continues. The proposed support is planned to provide live-saving services to people affected by the current conflict and to mitigate worsening of their humanitatian conditions.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The main activities under WASH include: construction of Sand dam in West Darfur Forobranga locality , upgrade of 15 hand pumps in West Darfur and 15 hand pumps in Central Darfur to solar operated mini water yards one in West Darfur and one in Central Darfur to increase the accessibility of vulnerable HHs to safe water for domestic use, the activity involves building the technical and managerial capacities of community-based water user committees to enable them carry out simple maintenance and to contribute to sustainable water resource management. Furthermore, under the hygiene promotion and WASH NFIs subsectors, the project will promote good hygiene practices among the communities through training of 60 hygiene promoters from the community, conducting awareness creation events using IEC/BCC materials, distribution of soap to 2,800 HHs and water containers to 1,800 HHs of newly displaced people or returnees. 15 HP Tool Kits will be distributed to West Darfur and 15 to Central Darfur. The project integrates WASH interventions with livelihood-based interventions under Agriculture and Food Security sector to achieve maximum benefits in terms of food and nutrition security; the main activities will be: provision of assorted seeds packages for 2500 in west Darfur 2500 in Central Darfur households combined with extension training on good agricultural practices. The project will implement emergency animal health and feeding programs to reduce morbidity and mortality of livestock belonging to 5200 HHs. The project will also provide conditional cash transfer to 2400 vulnerable HHs for agriculture/livestock purposes (5 USD per day per HH for 30 days). Protection will be mainstreamed throughout this project implementation, as well as specific GBV and child protection activities will be implemented based on certain needs and to build on VSFG previous efforts and achievements. Prevention and response to gender based violence subsector activities will be implemented in West and Central Darfur state to expand on our best practice there including: • establishment of one women multipurpose centre in each State • training of 900 women and girls in each state on GBV prevention and response• Incentive for social workers to follow-up cases, provide PSS & referral Logistical support to government staff to provide referral and psycho-social services to 80 victims/survivors through women centers.• Training of 1000 on prevention and response to GBV • Establish and train 40 community-based protection network; The protection networks capacity will be strengthened through training, provision with stationeries and logistically supported to conduct regular community awareness sessions and meetings & to support community-based referral mechanism • Support training of 100 government counterparts on Child Protection• Designing and printing IEC/BCC materials to increase awareness on child protection and cinduct awareness sessions targeted 5000 people in the targeted communities• Provide logistical support to 80 children to access PSS & referral services; children who reported abuse and require logistical and financial assistance to access referral services especially healthcare• FTR & foster care services to 40 separated and unaccompanied child and 60 child released from armed group• Training of 2000 child on leadership and communication skills • Socio-economic integration to 1200 child into FSL activities

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Selection and prioritization of all project beneficiaries will be through a participatory strategy that will engage beneficiaries at every stage of the project to make them of the active stakeholders in the project. During the first quarter of the project, a FGD will be formed in which the beneficiaries will meet regularly to discuss how they are progressing with What they learn , what kind of support they need from other stakeholders. This session will be led by the project beneficiaries with the facilitation of our local partner UPO . Feedback and complaints will be regularly collected from beneficiaries directly through community and 1-to-1 meetings in addition that VSF assigned number which is maintained by a Program Officer in Khartoum will be shared with beneficiaries in all project sites to provide feedback and raise complaints. All feedback and complaints received will be recorded on the form, categorized and accordingly shared with respective VSF staff for action. Details about the feedback and complaint cases will be logged on the Khartoum CRM for follow up and implementation. accountibility rolloing out will be in place in addition to sessions to the Community about PSEA and Sexual abuse and how to report such cases.one-on-one discussions will be conducted with each category to understand their special needs and concerns that may need the other stakeholders to be aware of. This document will be kept as a confidential document so that their personal concerns will not be exposed to others. Considering the sensitivity involved in dealing with HHs, beneficiaries will be formed into self-help groups 5 households in one group.
