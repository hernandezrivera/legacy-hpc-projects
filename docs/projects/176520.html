Project Summary

AVSI intervention aims at addressing the urgent needs related to water access, sanitation and hygiene through an intersectoral approach that involves targets typically related to food security, nutrition and health in Lakes and East Equatoria State.
The overall objective is to increase equitable and sustainable access to safe water to meet population basic needs and reduce morbidity of the most vulnerable in severe food insecurity. The interventions proposed, address two main urgent needs: water accessibility, of both the population and the communities of refugees returning to South Sudan and key institutions as schools, through the drilling and installation and rehabilitation of boreholes in target areas in high need due to the hydrological stress conditions and/or with increased water demand caused by the returned South Sudanese refugees. Prevention to water related disease outbreaks as diarrheal diseases, cholera and ebola and prevention of COVID19 through awareness campaigns at multiple levels (schools, health institutions and community). On the other hand, it aims at targeting a longer-term change towards good hygiene and sanitation practices in order to interrupt the circle of diarrheal diseases linked with malnutrition, to be achieved through the distribution and construction of hygiene and sanitation equipment and facilities (hygiene kits, hand washing, chlorine, soap, latrines) and the construction of latrines for rural institutions as schools and PHCUs. Specifically, hygiene kits are to be provided to screened in SAM children and their mothers, or caretakers, through the supervision of the already existing network of CNV (Community Nutrition Volunteers) and Nutrition assistants. Importantly. The hardware component is necessarily combined with awareness activities as campaigns at village level, the creation of hygiene clubs at school level and institutionalization of community groups, and the strengthening and of existing Community Water Community Management Committee (CWMC) or water bodies at the County or Payam and Boma level. Both type of interventions are characterized by the high involvement of local institutions and practitioners. Importantly, it is highlighted how in this approach, that involves local institutions as well as promotes key player for community good practices, AVSI relies on existing social structures, focusing on strengthening them in order to introduce elements of innovation that are welcomed by the communities through constant and persistent actions of sensitization (through field-based staff and constant visit to beneficiaries and awareness campaigns). With the lessons learnt from compounding effect of COVID-19 pandemic to already existing challenges, several revised/new guidelines and approaches shall be adhered and implemented in 2021 to ensure continuity of equitable access and utilization of quality WASH services in the context of COVID -19. The project will ensure existent of operational linkages and joint programing with other relevant sectors such as nutrition,, protection and food security through effectives coordination mechanism that will stimulate integrated approach and dignified access to mult-sectoral basic service. Accountability to affected population (AAP) will be mainstreamed by ensuring that the approach is community/people centered in such a way that those in needs are fully engaged throughout the program implementation. Consideration gender and age variation of the vulnerable groups will be prioritized as well for efficiency of the response, supporting sensitization of communities on SGBV issues specifically related to WASH context with SGBV participatory risk and assessment exercise at water access and sanitation sites.

1 - Needs

As reported in Humanitarian Needs Overview for 20211, an estimated 5.6 million people in South Sudan will be negatively impacted lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. Data from the 2021 severity mapping of the WASH Cluster4 show that Rumbek North County reports an overall Severity Level 4 (out of 5) with peaks of Severity 5 concerning access to sanitation and access to handwashing; in Cueibet, the overall Severity level is 5 with particular needs related to water quantity. Yirol West County, despite an overall level of 2, reports high needs (level 4) concerning access to WASH NFIs and access to safe water and severe needs (level 5) concerning access to handwashing. In Eastern Equatoria State, Kapoeta South County considered in level 5 regarding Water Related Protection Issues, while Kapoeta North and Kapoeta East Counties face a Severity Level 5 regarding access to sanitation with the latter facing the same level concerning access to handwashing and access to WASH NFIs. Ikwoto and Torit present Severity level 3, and with AVSI Multi-sector Households Survey in 20205 show constrained access to safe water, with 4% of the population in Torit County using surface water (pond) as source of drinking water and 75% of HHs in Ikwoto not accessing safe water sources, HH percentage walking more than 30 minutes to access water is 30% in Torit and 34% in Ikwoto.Generally, the main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Lack of WASH services does not only represent a problem per se but also a potential threat to peace building processes since the provision of life-saving water supply services to the host community is crucial in order to build and maintain peaceful co-existence. There are also protection concerns related to WASH: women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. Poor access to WASH facilities is a major cause of the worsening of malnutrition. In Lakes state, IPC data2 show that 55.6% of its population is facing Crisis (P3) or worse with the counties of Cueibet, Rumbek Center, Rumbek East and Yirol East facing Emergency (Phase 4). On the side of Eastern Equatoria State, the IPC data show that 41.6% of its population is facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, except for Kapoeta North which is classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) facing acute food insecurity. Concerning nutrition, in Lakes State 50% of children between 6 and 59 months old are considered to suffer General Acute Malnutrition with 43% suffering Moderate Acute Malnutrition and 7% suffering Severe Acute malnutrition while in Eastern Equatoria State State 46% of children between 6 and 59 months old are considered to suffer General Acute Malnutrition with 34,5% suffering Moderate Acute Malnutrition and 11,5% suffering Severe Acute malnutrition.The key drivers of WASH services gaps in malnutrition have been analyzed by AVSI in details for the targeted areas in the Multi-sector Households Survey in 2019 and 20203. Limited access to WASH sanitation services with risky health practices concur to determine the poor health outcomes. Indeed, women point to the lack of doctors available in proximity. From the hygiene point of view, the lack of awareness and knowledge of the families is still significant. It is highlighted the limited contribution that any awareness campaign on hygiene can have when considering the crucially limited accessibility to structures and items, which makes the new knowledge hardly translates into new habits.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

The numerous permanent bases that AVSI has throughout the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes; protection help desk points and suggestion boxes for unanimous and isolated reporting of abuses or complain. Moreover, prevention of GBV (Sexual Exploitation) is pursued by displaying SEA code of conducts at service access points (schools, health facilitates, distribution points and community).On the other hand, safety audits are conducted and multiple locations selected to avoid far travel of beneficiaries and reducing risks. Strengthening community, staff and stakeholder’s capacity through PSEA trainings.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

On regularly basis, AVSI conducts community engagement meetings such as awareness campaigns, innovative youth events that allows open participation/discussion over the quality of progress of the activity conducted with, for instance, CWMC, Nutrition volunteers and mothers benefitting from integrated WASH-nutrition services and AAPs who have built-in system of community monitoring. AVSI embrace a last-mile approach through field-based personal and daily visits to the field. AVSI reports to the WASH state cluster the result of its activities. AVSI conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, which are analyzed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention. For the subject who are found out to lag behind, a follow up interview of key informants, field personnel is conducted to review precisely the barrier that imped a more equitable targeting the intervention. Subsequent projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Yearly, AVSI conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community, the county and payam officials and traditional leaders (Boma and sub Boma Chiefs). During these events, AVSI explains to the community its plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. Last March, for instance, in Ikwoto, along with the results of the Great Ikwoto Multi Sector Household survey, the need to move toward more development-oriented interventions and thus to apply more demanding criteria for the selection of beneficiaries was discussed with the communities. In that occasion, AVSI received positive feedbacks from the community as well as suggestions regarding operative mechanism to employ for the implementation of projects. AVSI extension officers, AAP committees before selecting a person/group/community as beneficiaries, establish a case specific dialog where AVSIs expectations are compared with the one from the beneficiaries in order to evaluate possible challenges/ intervention failure chance. Beside the surveys, AVSI’s beneficiaries have access to information during participatory meetings which provided platform to address issues linked to power-relations whereby some strata of the population culturally marginalized can find the opportunity to express themselves, include the view of beneficiaries in programming and allow intervention in case of issues reported thus increase transparency and accountability through awareness of beneficiaries on protection-related issues as well as project-design. Allow information sharing at village level through creation of local key players as AAP committee members and suggestion boxes and protection help desks service points in the communities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beside the annual surveys, AVSI has a tested system of detailed post-distribution Monitoring which is used to gather precise feedbacks over the targeting, timing, transparency and overall implementation of activities oriented at the community at level. During each field visit, AVSI personnel is required to fill apposite forms that include a section for open-ended feedbacks and complaints. Finally, the numerous permanent bases that AVSI has across the targeted communities are equipped with mail boxes, suggestion boxes; focal point protection help desks for unanimous and isolated reporting of abuses or complaints.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

Protection sensitive services will as well be mainstream in the project, the activities will include; facilitation of child stimulation and ECD in all nutrition sites, GBV/PSEA awareness to nutrition workers and beneficiaries, referral of GBV cases to access service at the management sites.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

To improve on production and utilization of nutritious locally available food at household for optimal MIYC feeding the project will collaborate with AVSI food security team to establish demonstration kitchen gardens at nutrition sites, AVSI will also facilitate cooking demonstration to enlighten the mothers, fathers and caregivers on how to prepare nutritious foods harvested from the kitchen gardens and encouraged to replicate the same at home. Mothers and caretakers of children with acute malnutrition will be link to community general food assistant to improve on house hold food security. People with chronic illnesses and mothers of children admitted in SC for management of severe acute malnutrition will be link to institution feeding program to receive food assistance.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Given the fact drivers of malnutrition cuts across multi sectors the project will ensure operational linkages exist with other sectors such as health Food security, WASH and protection through improve coordination and convergence programing. The following activities will be implemented such that the vulnerable targeted groups (malnourished women, children boys and girls) have access to multi sector basic services; Joint health and nutrition education/promotion shall be conducted at all nutrition sites; provision of LLITNS shall be provided to children in OTP programs as a preventive measure of malaria, the project will ensure all children admitted in OTP are tested and treated for malaria, all children identified for medical complication shall be referred for medical care. Malnourished Pregnant mothers accessing treatment at nutrition shall be referred to ANC services. WASH kits shall be provided to children admitted in OTP program and all nutrition sites will be equip with hygiene equipment such as hand washing facilities and the project will ensure sanitation facilities exist in all nutrition sites.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The following approach shall be used; vulnerable groups PLW and children under will be provided with specialized food supplement through blanket supplementary Programs (BSFP) to prevent malnutrition; the project will emphasis maternal infant and young child nutrition counselling among pregnant, and lactating mothers/caregivers to children under five. Awareness on promoting health/ nutrition especially breastfeeding, hygiene shall be conducted. To optimize feeding practice among PLW and children the project activities will include conducting cooking demonstration to mothers and caregivers accessing services at nutrition site. Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies is very vital in reducing GAM prevalence the project will facilitate vitamin A supplementation campaigns and will refer children with identified deficiency for immediate treatment. The project will ensure access to early screening and timely quality treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition. Other key component that will contribute to reduced GAM prevalence is to ensure effective and efficient programing through strengthened program monitoring, ensuring community full participation and commitment and improve coordination and nutrition information management and utilization.
