Project Summary

UNHCR and partners (BRAC, CARITAS, DRC, NGO-Forum) will continue shelter interventions to improve the living conditions of refugees households taking into account AGDM and individual specific needs across all camps under UNHCR's AoR. Shelter Support is provided via the Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism and Active Helpdesk and this will continue into 2022. UNHCR operate a Phone Hotline managed by UNHCR Protection who refer relevant concerns and requests to Shelter Unit for follow-up. During the identification of beneficiaries for planned Shelter assistance, People with Special Needs and Extremely Vulnerable Individuals are prioritized based on the list provided by UNHCR. As with previous years, Shelter-NFI staff and Partners staff will complete the Annual PSEA full day training by UNHCR Protection Team in advance of 2022. PSEA assessment has been conducted for all UNHCR Partners with satisfactory results.
UNHCR works in collaboration with the Shelter/NFI Sector within the ISCG on the distribution and composition of non-food items (NFIs). UNHCR ensures refugees are participating in the process through focus group discussions ensuring their views and needs are captured in distribution programmes. NFI distributions will be planned in advance and managed in a functional and effective manner in coordination with the RRRC and CICs and site management agencies.
Refugee households will be informed on the distribution schedule with an appointment slip provided to them in advance. Distributions will continue to be administered through the Global Distribution Tool. Additionally, UNHCR plans to scale-up the newly introduced voucher distribution system or the “pop-up store” mechanism so that refugees can choose what items they need. Consulting the JMSNA and UNHCR's PDM, the lessons learned are incorporated into Project and will be part of the Participatory Activity Planning.
Refugees will continue to have access to cleaner fuel with limited exposure to protection incidents through the provision of LPG and pressure cookers. So far, at least one member of each refugee household has received training on the safe use of LPG, and UNHCR will expand user training to ensure that all refugees are trained and provided with a pressure cooker and a cookbook outlining how to use and maintain a new more efficient stove and pressure cooker technologies; in this way refugees will also benefit from a more diversified cooking practice with the distribution of pressure cookers. UNHCR will also continue to target households within host communities.
Shelter Activities will involve women, older people, PwSN and other forms of gender diverse population activities (i.e. women and youth involved in the production of bamboo items to cover walls and roofs, or women involved in the bamboo treatment plant related activities.

1 - Identified Needs

Ongoing use of temporary materials such as untreated bamboo and tarpaulin requires regular material replacement and structural shelter reinforcement. Reduction of shelter activities due to COVID-19 containment measures in 2020 and 2021 resulted in less shelters being upgraded and, without our regular assistance, the shelter conditions have deteriorated. The urgent need to provide shelter assistance remains, as well as more durable materials to improve the lifespan of the shelters. According to the Joint Multi Sector Needs Assessment (J MSNA) of Refugee and Host Communities Key findings, published on the 17 October 2021, gaps have persisted across sectors, such as, almost three quarters of households reporting issues with their shelters and a large proportions reporting having insufficient NFIs. 63% reported need for more shelter materials / upgrade, while 38% reported need of more solar lamps and batteries and 26% reported need of more household / cooking items. All newly arrived refugee households will be provided with non-food items (NFIs). Distribution and prepositioning will cover regular NFI distribution, seasonal assistance, ad-hoc and emergency distributions based on rapid assessments, and through the newly introduced voucher system modality or the ‘Pop-up Store’. The distribution modalities will be based on different types of distribution in a dignified method to ensure equalized distribution covering all households in a blanket manner and targeted households or groups of PoCs as per specific need. Continuous advocacy with the Government on predictable sustainable investment in infrastructure especially targeting the most vulnerable families. The continued provision of shelter materials, pre-monsoon kits, core relief items. The Global Distribution Tool (GDT), rolled out in UNHCR-managed camps, will be further developed to facilitate assistance delivery, an activity that will require increased investment in data management support and monitoring. The GDT in turn will support comprehensive monitoring and evaluation of the distribution programme.
