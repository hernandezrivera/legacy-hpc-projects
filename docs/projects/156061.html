Needs

Deir-ez-Zor According to UNOCHA, the United Nations (UN) and partners have significant concerns for the civilian population impacted by recent hostilities in south-east Deir-ez-Zor Governorate, with airstrikes reportedly contributing to significant civilian casualties and destruction of civilian infrastructure. Reports indicate that on average over 60% of the city is damaged and the lack of shelter available combined with the increased population in Deir Ezzor city has led to an inflation of rent prices. Pressure on available services (water, electricity, health, education, markets, bakeries) is also increasing. Access to health services is very limited across the governorate due to the lack of qualified medical staff. Unexploded ordnance (UXO) and other explosive hazards are present in Deir-Ezzor governorate. Access is severely restricted in these areas. Returnees are exposed to a particularly high risk of ERW. Roads in Deir Ezzor governorate are often contaminated with IEDs and inaccessible due to accumulated rubble. AlraqqaAcross SDF-controlled areas of northeast Syria there are an estimated 35,800 people living in IDP/ refugee sites as of the end of November 2018, of whom some 26,650 are IDPs. This represents a slight increase in the estimated IDP population from the previous month. While returns continue to occur towards Ar-Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor from all sites, new arrivals during the month have largely come from Deir-ez-Zor. Since November 2017 some 18,178 refugees have returned to Iraq with further movements anticipated. There continue to be significant concerns for the situation of IDPs in Ar-Raqqa, many of whom are reportedly not adequately equipped for winter. Heavy rains during the reporting period allegedly caused damage and destruction of shelters in several locations. In an IDP site near Al-Jayef village in western rural Ar-Raqqa heavy rains on 14 November damaged at least 13 tents, with the camp administration highlighting a lack of winter preparedness in the camp. IdelbDespite the recent ceasefire and de-militarization agreements, access to Idleb remains restricted. Insecurity in Idleb, particularly fighting between opposition groups and targeted assassinations, makes humanitarian access extremely difficult. IDPs living in desolate and hard-to-reach areas face severe restrictions in accessing aid and services. Over a million IDPs face regular restrictions when trying to access basic service. Half of the population of 2.9 million in Idleb and its border areas are IDPs. They live in poor, overcrowded conditions with high unmet needs, particularly WASH, health, food, and shelter. Idleb is experiencing very high levels of incoming and outgoing IDPs, particularly due to evacuations of opposition groups and civilians as a result of the SAA offensive in the southwest. Idleb has reported higher displacement numbers (357,000) than other governorates in 2018. In Idleb, where conditions are reportedly dire, healthcare workers have not reached many crowded IDP sites and are overstretched. Healthcare needs have increased in the governorate, while funding has decreased. The IDP influx in 2018 has doubled patient loads for doctors and left hospitals overwhelmed In light of the above needs, DSS undertakes utmost efforts to facilitate the operations of the UN, International Community and NGOs and help them in accomplishing their missions in Syria. However, currently the lack of extra-budgetary resources at our disposal cannot accommodate supporting all the non-UN mission requests for assistance that are being received. With the opening of new areas and the increasing additional requests of support from all involved in Syria, DSS resources are becoming rapidly exhausted and reaching the point of rupture/bottleneck. To sustain this additional requested supporting effort, unfortunately, it will no longer be possible without additional financial support.

Implementation of activities

Project Objectives and ImplementationObjective:Provide adequate security support for the UNCT and to enhance the security risk management measures in order to ensure the safety and security of UN personnel and continuity of humanitarian programme delivery in Deir Ez-Zor, Ar-Raqqa, and Idleb Governorates. Implementation for each of 3 locations mentioned above:Field Security Coordination Officers (FSCOs) and Local Security Assistants (LSAs) are essential to maintain and create the security risk management mechanisms and procedures as well as defining strategies and apply mitigation measures to enable the presence of humanitarian and development organizations and the International Community.Security Communications Centers are instrumental to enhance the awareness of the UN system in a location and to quickly respond to security incidents or emergency situations on the ground. They are part of the established Emergency communications System, integrating Smart phones, radios and satellite phones into a seamless 24/7 command and control room that can help to start the right reaction to contingencies in a minimum time.

Protection Risk Analysis

Low, since the project depends on UN staff protection, and there is no direct contract with the beneficiaries on this project.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The security situation in Syria continues to remain extremely volatile as hostilities continue throughout the country. With an increasingly complex security situation and heightened risks for UN staff and assets, there is a need for adequate security support for the UNCT and to enhance the security risk management measures in order to ensure the safety and security of UN personnel and continuity of humanitarian programme delivery inside Syria. UNDSS is responsible for providing oversight and operational support for the security management system in Syria, and plays a crucial role in supporting UN operations. Furthermore, it is crucial that UNDSS maintain presence in Deir Ez-Zor, Alraqqa, and Idleb to provide 24/7 security-related operational and analytical support to the UN Agencies and implementing partners and strengthen risk-based humanitarian delivery to the most vulnerable in Syria while keeping humanitarian personnel safe. In addition, UNDSS will continue to undertake awareness and training sessions (SSAFE, First Aid, ERW, Defensive Driving, ETB, etc.) for UN agencies’ staff and humanitarian actors.
