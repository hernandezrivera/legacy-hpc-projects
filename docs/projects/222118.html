Project Summary

The action will contribute to create an accessible response to GBV survivors, including case management, referrals and follow-up of cases and at the same time will set an enabling and inclusive protection environment to prevent and mitigate GBV risks within most vulnerable crisis-affected population in Cabo Delgado through accessible information and community awareness. The action will enhance resilience capacity, involving DPOs, women with disabilities, community members and local government authorities.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The action covers some of the districts most affected by the displacement of people in Cabo Delgado Province (Pemba, Ancuabe and Montepuez) and aims at enhancing community and government actors’ capacity on GBV prevention and response, focusing on women and girls with disabilities, contributing at creating an enabling and inclusive GBV prevention and response environment, promoting access to information, reporting and access to basic services. Moreover, the action will identify and remove barriers to access to information and services, advocating for universal access to life-saving and basic services and supporting stakeholders’ implementation of inclusive and accessible approaches for GBV prevention and assistance. The main target groups will be women with disabilities (of any age), who are the most exposed to GBV risks and experience multiple barriers to access life-saving services. The project will directly involve PwD who, duly trained, will identify and manage cases of GBV survivors with disability and raise awareness within communities of intervention. 40 Community officials, of which at least 40% are women with disability, will be trained to provide information and assist survivors. 8 sites for inclusive and accessible case management and GBV support mechanisms sites will be established – 2 for each intervention District.In order to empower survivors with financial autonomy, 80 GBV identified survivors (20 per District) will be provided with professional training and financial and technical support to start up small IGA.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

● Develop/integrate or strengthen disability-specific referral pathways for women and girls with disabilities; ● Establish and/or identify and strengthen existing 8 inclusive and accessible case management sites to provide GBV case management and referral services for women and girls with disabilities;● Conduct accessible GBV awareness raising sessions focusing on protection of persons with disabilities at community level, thereby ensuring proper engagement and support for women and girls with disabilities as well as awareness about accessible and inclusive reporting mechanisms.● Provide professional training and IGA in kind materials to selected GBV survivors with disability

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The project aims at establishing inclusive and accessible protection mechanisms related to GBV through accessible information, risk prevention, reporting and response, creating synergies with main specific protection and assistance providers, including CSOs and local institutions. The development and strengthen of disability-specific referral pathways for women and girls with disabilities will encourage meaningful access to assistance among one of the most marginalized and vulnerable group. The partnership DPOS, in particular DPOs of women with disabilities, will guarantee their active participation and involvement in the action.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The proposed project adopts a rights-based, inclusive approach to ensure meaningful access to assistance and active participation and empowerment, particularly for women, girls, and elderly persons with disabilities. This is achieved through several key strategies: 1. Enhancing Access to AssistanceThe project prioritizes the removal of barriers that prevent women and elderly persons from accessing critical information and services related to GBV prevention and response. Specific actions include: Inclusive and Accessible Services: Establishing eight inclusive and accessible case management and GBV support sites (two per intervention district), designed to meet the needs of people with disabilities, including the elderly.Universal Access Advocacy: Promoting inclusive service delivery by advocating for universal access to life-saving and basic services. This advocacy ensures that services are designed to address the specific needs of marginalized groups, such as women and elderly persons with disabilities.Community Officials Training: Training 40 community officials, with at least 40% being women with disabilities, to provide accurate information and direct assistance to GBV survivors. This ensures representation and empowers women in leadership roles.2. Participation and Community EngagementThe project emphasizes direct involvement of people with disabilities in program implementation to foster ownership and accountability: Peer-Led Identification and Management: Women and men with disabilities will be trained to identify and manage GBV cases within their communities. This approach empowers marginalized individuals to become key actors in addressing GBV while ensuring culturally sensitive case management.Community Awareness-Raising: Trained individuals will raise awareness on GBV prevention and response, fostering a supportive and informed community environment.3. Empowerment through Economic IndependenceTo address the financial vulnerability of GBV survivors, the project supports their long-term empowerment: Professional Training and Start-Up Support: 80 GBV survivors (20 per district) will receive professional training, along with financial and technical assistance, to establish small income-generating activities (IGAs). This economic empowerment fosters self-reliance and reduces dependence, which is a critical factor in breaking the cycle of GBV.4. Inclusion of the ElderlyWhile the primary focus is on women and girls with disabilities, the project indirectly benefits elderly persons by creating an inclusive environment and addressing systemic barriers such as access to services and information. The establishment of accessible sites and capacity-building among community officials ensures that the elderly can also benefit from these resources. 5. Creating Sustainable Systems for Long-Term ImpactThe project works closely with community leaders, local government, and other stakeholders to create inclusive GBV prevention and response mechanisms. By building the capacity of local actors and advocating for the adoption of inclusive policies, the project ensures sustainability and replicability of its approaches, benefiting marginalized groups in the long term.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Addressing Barriers and Promoting Inclusion:People with Disabilities (PWDs) are at an increased risk of being excluded from protection mechanisms due to a range of barriers, including physical/architectural, attitudinal/socio-cultural, economic, communication/information, and organizational challenges. While Mozambique has specific disability laws and policies, many line ministries at central, provincial, and district levels lack sufficient awareness and the necessary tools to effectively implement these frameworks. This gap undermines inclusive program design, particularly in critical areas such as GBV prevention and response, leaving PWDs without adequate support. Designing for Inclusion and Accessibility:This project directly addresses these challenges by ensuring that men and women with disabilities can access GBV-related information and services on an equal basis with others. Key strategies include: Collaboration with Disabled People's Organizations (DPOs): Partnering with local DPOs to identify PWDs and facilitate their active inclusion in project activities.Removing Barriers to Access: Establishing inclusive and accessible case management and GBV support mechanisms tailored to the needs of PWDs, ensuring that all interventions align with the principles of universal design.AIFO’s Expertise in Disability Inclusion:AIFO has decades of experience championing disability inclusion in Mozambique, with a strong focus on Cabo Delgado Province. In alignment with key international frameworks such as the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD) and the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Guidelines on Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action, AIFO integrates disability inclusion into all aspects of its programming. In 2021, AIFO conducted extensive capacity-building initiatives targeting local governments (at district and provincial levels), civil society organizations, and community leaders in Cabo Delgado. These efforts focused on promoting inclusive planning processes and ensuring PWDs’ participation in decision-making across various sectors.AIFO’s participatory approach fosters collaboration between government institutions and community structures, ensuring that disability inclusion is not only implemented but also sustainable.Commitment to International Standards and Best PracticesThis project reflects AIFO’s commitment to international guidelines and best practices, including: The CRPD, which underscores the right of PWDs to access services and participate fully in society.The IASC Guidelines, which provide a framework for inclusive humanitarian action.The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), particularly Goal 10 (Reduced Inequalities) and Goal 16 (Peace, Justice, and Strong Institutions), emphasizing the inclusion of marginalized populations in development and humanitarian initiatives.Through its expertise and alignment with global standards, AIFO ensures that this project not only addresses the immediate needs of PWDs in accessing GBV-related services but also strengthens local systems to sustain inclusive practices over time. By empowering local stakeholders and collaborating with DPOs, the project creates a foundation for a more equitable and inclusive response to GBV in Mozambique.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will be implemented in alignment with AIFO’s Environmental and Sustainability Policy, as outlined in the Manual of Operational Policies - Code of Ethics and Conduct 2020. In accordance with the key principles of AIFO's Environmental Policy, an environmental assessment will be conducted, incorporating a gender-sensitive perspective. This is particularly relevant as women in rural areas often face greater vulnerability to environmental degradation due to unequal access to land and resources. Additionally, the project will address community preparedness for natural disasters, recognizing the vulnerability of the geographic area to catastrophic events. This approach is integrated within the Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) framework. The findings from the environmental assessment conducted in the catchment area will be thoroughly considered to ensure an effective and sustainable response.
