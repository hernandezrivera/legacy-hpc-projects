Project Summary

A combination of extreme flooding, cyclical waves of inter and intra communal violence, pests and diseases coupled with an uptick in COVID 19 cases in the country, continue to exacerbate the needs of an estimated 5.82 million people who are currently (December 2020-March 2021) projected to face IPC Phase 3/worse levels of food insecurity. This is projected to rise to 7.24 million people with projected IPC phase 5 populations in Aweil South, Pibor among other counties. The recent Food Security Outlook (September 2020) Update has projected highest levels of food insecurity in 2021 ever recorded since 2014. This coupled with a persistently low food/ income sources in more than half of the population, urgent and sustained humanitarian aid through food assistance will be critical to preventing more extreme food security outcomes in 2021 including projected IPC Phase 5 across all the Payams of Aweil South County (South Sudan IPC, 2020).
Severe flooding extremes have led to disrupted livestock migration and consequent prolonged congregation of livestock in swampy/ marsh areas, further leading to livestock disease outbreaks such as Hemorhagic Septicemia, Contagious Bovine Pleuropneumonia, Contagious Caprine Pleuropneumonia among others with significant high mortality rates and subsequent erosion of the livestock sector contribution (VSF Suisse Livestock Disease Outbreak Reports, 2020; VSF Suisse Livestock Assessment Report, September 2020).
These, coupled by the COVID 19 Pandemic whose key government imposed restrictions have had consequent negative implications on vulnerable people’s livelihoods and food security, including a recent surge in confirmed cases in the country as at February 2020 (Ministry of Health COVID 19 Updates, February 2021).
Many vulnerable host, returnee and displaced families, especially women, widowed women, child headed HHs, the elderly, boys and girls including people with special needs solely depend on livestock, fishing, vegetable and crop farming among other coping strategies.
The project which will target 160,000 beneficiaries (30000 men, 30000 women, 50000 girls and 50000 boys) who will be supported through crop, vegetable and fishing and animal health services. Activities will be aimed at ensuring cost efficiency through complementarities by linkages with FAO pipeline for supply of crops, vegetables and fishing kits and veterinary vaccines in order to promote preparedness, household resilience and self-reliance through protection of livelihood opportunities. The project will ensure a multi-sectoral approach through inter-cluster synergies, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including Gender Based Violence mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

1 - Needs

An estimated 5.82 million people are currently (Dec 2020-March 2021) projected to face IPC Phase 3/worse levels of food insecurity. This is projected to rise to 7.24 million people with the recent Food Security Outlook (September 2020) Update projecting highest levels of food insecurity in 2021 ever recorded since 2014. This coupled with a persistently low food/ income sources in more than half of the population, urgent and sustained humanitarian aid through food assistance will be critical to preventing more extreme food security outcomes in 2021 (South Sudan IPC, 2020). The lean season is likely to see a deterioration in food insecurity particularly in market dependent counties such as Juba, Aweil among others as majority of households (HHs) deplete their food stocks and are unable to purchase sufficient food at extremely high prices (National Market Profile for South Sudan, December, 2020; FEWSNET Outlook, 2020-21). This coupled with unprecedented flooding extremes in 2020, attributed to rising of the waters of Nile and heavy rainfall, has had consequent implications on submerging of farm lands and multiple displacements of people and livestock. Severe flooding extremes have led to disrupted livestock migration and consequent prolonged congregation of livestock in swampy/ marsh areas, further leading to livestock disease outbreaks with significant high mortality rates and subsequent erosion of the livestock sector contribution (VSF Suisse Livestock Disease Outbreak Reports, 2020; VSF Suisse Livestock Assessment Report, September 2020). These, coupled with pests and diseases including desert locusts (FAO Desert Locusts Updates, 2020); diminishing consumer purchasing power and inflation, reduced market functionality, insufficient food availability, high disease prevalence, limited dietary diversity among others have further contributed to extreme GAM levels. Inadequate animal health services and livestock diseases threaten to erode livestock contribution to food security and coping strategies among agro-pastoralists. Throughout the crisis, livestock assets, crops, vegetables and fishing resources have provided a critical safety net to the conflict and flood affected communities, including the displaced populations in hard to reach areas (2020 FAO/ WFP Crop and Food Security Assessment Report). These, coupled by the COVID 19 Pandemic whose key government imposed restrictions have had consequent negative implications on vulnerable people’s livelihoods and food security, including a recent surge in confirmed cases in the country as at February 2020 (Ministry of Health COVID 19 Updates, February 2021). Many vulnerable host, returnee and displaced families, especially women, widowed women, child headed HHs, the elderly, boys and girls including people with special needs solely depend on livestock, fishing, vegetable and crop farming among other coping strategies. VSF Suisse 26 years of experience in the targeted states, presence on the ground and proven capacity will deliver high quality programming by leveraging on strong grass root networks, local knowledge, understanding of seasonal and location specific contexts. The project will be aimed at ensuring cost efficiency through complementarities by linkages with FAO pipeline for supply of crops, vegetables and fishing kits and veterinary supplies in order to promote preparedness, household resilience and self-reliance through protection of livelihood opportunities. The project will ensure a multi-sectoral approach through inter-cluster synergies, quality programming, cost efficiency in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of Accountability to Affected Populations principles, protection including Gender Based Violence mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conflict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

2 - Project activities

• Prepositioning, transport and distribution of crops, fishing kits and vegetable kits among 60,000 beneficiaries (10,000 HHs) – comprised of 10000 men, 10000 women, 20000 girls and 20000 boys)• Set up at least 60 vegetable demonstration plots/ kitchen backyard garden demonstration sites at nutrition facilities• Conduct agronomic trainings and post-harvest preservation techniques training for 10,000 household heads while observing COVID 19 mitigation measures in line with Ministry of Health/ WHO Guidelines• Training/ Refresher training for 300 Community Based Animal Health Workers/ Stock Persons (100 female, 200 male)• Conduct refresher training of 40 Stock Persons and Animal Health Auxilliaries on cold chain management and monitoring to support safe storage of veterinary vaccines/ integrity.• Install and maintain 20 solar vaccine fridges for livestock veterinary vaccine handling in Central Equatoria, Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Jonglei.• Carry out livestock disease investigation outbreaks in collaboration with line ministries, FAO among other key stakeholders.• Carry out livestock vaccination and campaigns targeting 800,000 heads of livestock against endemic livestock diseases in Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria and Jonglei.• Carry out livestock treatment and deworming campaigns through targeting 400,000 heads of livestock against endemic livestock diseases in Unity, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Central Equatoria and Jonglei.• Support 5000 households through input trade fairs using locally sourced seed inputs in Northern Bahr el Ghazal. • Support livestock and agriculture value chain addition and marketing among 2000 households.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

In line with VSF Suisse PSEA policy, the organization will sensitize targeted communities on the principles of Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and harassment including communities having key contact information they can confidentially report. Beneficiaries will as well be empowered through sensitizations and information dissemination including ensuring their meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled.Child/women/widowed/elderly/disabled headed and those with pregnant and lactating mothers will be prioritized and identified as primary beneficiaries to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed including advocacy against Gender Based Violence/ Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Most vulnerable members who shall include people with special needs, illiterate, women and girls will have an equal opportunity in active participation throughout the project cycle. These will be engaged and be part of implementation committees including ensuring assistance is offered including priority given to people with disabilities who cannot walk and ensure services are provided at their residences or ensuring activities are implemented close to their homesteads.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

VSF Suisse will adopt a consultative project implementation approach; by including equitable representation of male and female, youth, boys and girls, women and men (including disabled and elderly, illiterate); local community leaders, youth and women representatives among other relevant authorities in decision making as well and also ensuring their full participation in project planning, selection, verification, implementation, monitoring/evaluation. VSF Suisse field teams will also conduct weekly/monthly project management meetings to review and consider any issues emerging that may impact on the project implementation, in addition to mainstreaming of cross cutting issues such as gender, protection, HIV/AIDS and accountability etc. Activities including livelihood kits distributions and animal health campaigns/ vouchers etc will be conducted in presence of beneficiary trustees such as local community leaders, RRC and youth/ women representatives and facilitated by representatives of specific beneficiary groups; including inviting the Cluster in implementation and monitoring. Beneficiary distribution lists will be developed, verified and endorsed by beneficiary representatives and filed for records / references. Men, women, boys and girls are consulted jointly and separately including making arrangements to engage people sith special needs such as disabled. Sufficient information on the project, including sharing relevant documents with the local leadership, local State/County ministries will be shared to enable them understand and influence key decisions in the project including putting in place a feedback and complaints desk at Activity/ distribution sites and appropriate contacts.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

VSF Suisse will set up community engagement and feedback desks at targeted sites, which will be manned by male and female beneficiary representatives to collect feedback that shall be reviewed on a weekly basis including holding consultations and discussions on issues raised and way forward. These will then inform programming including relevant adjustments upon consultations with other key stakeholders including line ministries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Key risks such as insecurity and inter/ intra communal violence including congregation of beneficiaries during distribution, long distances covered especially by women/ girls to access services including PSEA; will be mitigated through conflict sensitive programming. VSF Suisse actions will ensure appropriate conflict sensitivity including dissemination of peace messaging to descalate tensions and working closely with key stakeholders on ground including relevant line ministries to ensure safety and access guarantees’. VSF Suisse will conduct routine gender and protection risk assessments and risk monitoring to understand and adapt to the evolving protection concerns during implementation. This is in addition to the project prioritizing beneficiary safety and dignity in consideration with the principles of ‘Do no harm; non-discrimination, equitable, gender segregated and meaningful access to proposed services/facilities and cater for specific needs of girls, women, youth and men including the elderly and disabled. Communities will also be engaged among otherrelevant stakeholders, including awareness and training on basic human rights, UXOs while implementing various activities. For instance, the primary beneficiaries for this project are identified as host, returnee and IDP communities. This is designed to deliver services in a non-discriminatory manner for all those who are most vulnerable, and to ensure any tensions between host communities and IDPs over access to facilities and services is avoided. The bulk of the program activities are planned to be delivered during dry periods, to ensure communities are not distracted from the core farming activities with potential to harm their productive livelihood engagements. Child/women/widowed/elderly/disabled headed and those with pregnant and lactating mothers will be prioritized and identified as primary beneficiaries of livelihood kits to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed including advocacy against Gender Based Violence/ Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Beneficiary information dissemination on specific support livelihood kits packages by the field teams will be done, in addition to instituting a “feedback and complaint mechanism”. Contacts of key project VSF Suisse will in addition monitor security trends and ensure conflict sensitive programming and linkages with other security focal points such as UN Department of Safety and Security Contingency Plans for South Sudan, South Sudan NGO Forum Security Working G and short message alerts and coordination with UN OCHA and UN HAS emergency relocation and evacuation plans. As cases of COVID 19 continue to spread, VSF Suisse will ensure implementation adheres to key Ministry of Health/ WHO Guidelines including provision of protective gears among frontline staff, ensuring social distancing and messaging on key preventative efforts throughout implementation.
