Project Summary

The project is designed to support the income of small farmers in 12 localities in Gaza. The project design is in line with FSS objective 1 "The resilience of vulnerable households are protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced ". According FSS-HNO-2022, Two million people (36.7% of the population) are estimated to be moderately or severely food insecure, (seventy-three per
cent located in the Gaza Strip and 27 percent in the West Bank). (HNO 2022) The blockade and recent aggression have directly affected the farmers in Gaza and put them under severe risk of poverty and food insecurity. The destruction of the agriculture infrastructure during last war has affected farmers across Gaza, particularly those who already have low incomes.
The project will directly enhance income generation and food security for HHs in 12 of most vulnerable agricultural communities in North Gaza, Khan Younis and Rafah through rehabilitation of agriculture assets, and improving access to irrigation for small farmers in the target areas. The Key activities include rehabilitation of 200 dunums of vegetable farming land for 100 small-farmers (90M,10F) and rehabilitation of 100 dunums of greenhouses (GH) for 100 small farmers (10F, 90M). Moreover, 13 wells will be equipped with solar energy units to improve access to irrigation for 260 farmers. The project will be implemented in partnership with two local partners: BAYDER in Khan Younis and Rafah and Beit Lahia development Association in North Gaza.
To ensure sustainable impact, SCI will coordinate with main livelihood stakeholders such as FSS, MoA, MoSD. The proposed project will build on SCI previous agriculture interventions which are being implemented with the same partners in eastern Khan Younis and north Gaza (2017- 2020). The project will be integrated with current SCI nutrition interventions through supporting the livelihood of HHs with malnourished children. In addition, the project will be integrated with current economic resilience
project through working with existing Community Protection Committees (CPCs) to enhance the communities’ participation and adapt the implementation based on the target Communities Protection Response plans.

1 - Needs

Gaza is facing a gradual process of de-development, which has been ongoing since the imposition of the blockade in 2007. This has led to deterioration of livelihood and increase in poverty, particularly for small businesses and daily wage laborers. Poverty is one of the main determinants of food insecurity. The poverty rate in Gaza was estimated at 53 per cent in 2017 and is expected to have increased to 56.4 per cent because of the May 2021 hostilities (FSS_HNO-2022). About 80 per cent of households were receiving some form of social assistance already before the escalation. (FSS-HNO-2022). Agricultural livelihoods are crucial to improving the food security of vulnerable communities. However, the increased costs of production inputs and poor productivity pose obstacles to any improvement in food security in Gaza Strip. The recent aggression in May 2021 has directly affected the targeted farmers and put them under severe risk of poverty and food insecurity. The destruction of the agriculture infrastructure during last war has affected farmers across Gaza Strip, particularly those who already have low incomes. According to World Bank (June 2021), the agricultural sector suffered a direct damage of USD 45 million, while the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) estimated it was accounting for USD 55 million (FSS-HNO). In addition, due to the loss of traditional crops this season, the low-income farmers do not have sufficient resources to rehabilitate their farms and resume their production. Moreover, the farmers’ production is also affected by the deteriorating land quality, water resources depletion and water salinity. The electricity outage leads to shortage of water for irrigation and irregular irrigation, which negatively affects the agriculture production. The result of two FGDs conducted by SC in October 2021 with 15 farmers (5 F, 10M) in North Gaza shows that the farmer income has fallen due to decreased farming profitability. Most of HHs suffering from the accumulation of debts due to the loss of their crops resulted from the last escalation and the harsh weather. The farmers facing challenges to resume their normal livelihood activities due to destruction of their farms, increase the cost of agriculture inputs, and poor access to irrigation due to electricity outage. The attendants said that many of farmers use their children as farming laborers to reduce labor cost, which negatively effects on the children growth and education. Severe pressures experienced by civilians in the OPT resulting from the occupation have een linked to gender-based violence (GBV); school dropouts; as well as child marriage, forced marriage, and early high-risk pregnancies while service providers struggle to meet the population’s needs due to limited resources (“Gender and Wars in Gaza Untangled: What Past Wars Have Taught Us?”, June 2021) The May 2021 escalation of hostilities and the COVID-19 pandemic have reinforced pressures facing women, girls, boys and men in the OPT. The pandemic has also exacerbated inequalities, inequities and discrimination based on location, age, and geography. Women and girls are disproportionately impacted by gender norms and pre-existing inequalities. Globally, COVID-19 has affected women and girls in multi-dimensional ways, particularly those facing multiple, intersecting forms of discrimination (GENDER ALERT: NEEDS OF WOMEN, GIRLS, BOYS AND MEN IN HUMANITARIAN ACTION IN PALESTINE –Sep 2021)In Khan Younis BAYADER conducted two FGs with 20 farmer (8 F, 12 M) who stated that they are suffering from low income. They do not have sufficient resources to rehabilitate their farms to resume their normal livelihood. Irregularity of irrigation due electricity outage causes to a decrease of production and sometime loss of the crops. Water salinity causes poor productivity and thus lower profitability of farmers. The increase prices of agricultural inputs and the decrease in the prices of agricultural products due to blockade and the difficulty of exporting led to lower profitability. The attendants stated that some farmers sold their agriculture assets to pay their debts. In many cases, irrigation water is not available except at night and farmers are forced to go to their farms at night, which puts them at risk, especially in areas close to the borders. During 2021, SCI and Ard El Insan conducted a socio-economic assessment for 580 malnourished children's families in three localities in Rafah and Khan Younis (Khoza, Al Shoka, Al Naser). The results show increase the extreme poverty as average monthly income per capita is 43.8 ILS. Results of former interventions implemented by SC showed that the rehabilitation of farms and provision of materials support was implemented according to technical standards and use of high-quality materials accordingly the rehabilitated farms will not need maintenance for at least four years. Also. The project contributed to alleviating the financial burdens of the beneficiaries of the project by increasing financial income from agricultural land or by providing a new source of income. Majority of supported farmers (more than 90%) agreed that the intervention resulted significant impact on the farming aspects such as: Reduce production costs, improve their accessibility to agricultural services "guidance, inputs ...”, Increase in production quantities, increase in quality of the agricultural product, using modern agricultural tools considering protection of environments.

2 - Activities

The proposed project will build on SCI previous agricultural interventions which were implemented with the same partners in eastern Khan Younis and north Gaza (2017-2020).The project will be integrated with current SCI nutrition, protection and economic resilience interventions in North Gaza, Rafah, and Khan Younis. The project partners will coordinate with MoA and FSS to ensure the quality of work. To increase the project impact, sustainability and do no harm, the project activities will be implemented in close coordination with target farmers and the target communities through the existing Community Protection Committees (CPCs) and ensure that intervention aligned with existing Community Protection Plan. SCI and the partners will select the farmers in coordination with MoA based on vulnerability criteria. SCI will implement the project activities in 3 localities North Gaza (Jabalya, Beit Lahiya, Beit Hanun)in partnership with BLDA and in partnership with Bayader in in 9 localities in Rafah and khan Younis (Abasan al Jadida (as Saghira), Abasan al Kabira, Al Fukhkhari, Al Qarara, Al Mawasi (Khan Yunis), Bani Suheila, Shokat as Sufi, Al Mawasi (Rafah),Al BayuK). The key activities are: 1) Selection of beneficiaries: SC will target the most vulnerable small-scale farmers. SC partners will obtain the list of nominated beneficiaries from MoA The beneficiaries be selected based on based of Socioeconomic criteria The selection criteria will include the HH income, family size, source of income (agriculture or non-agriculture), access to food, food consumption. Household outreach visits will take place to identify beneficiaries for participation. Group consultations will ensure community participation in beneficiaries’ selection and identification of needs. Although traditionally most farmers are male, but attention will be paid to targeting farms headed by women or those that employ women. The project partners will obtain the list of nominated farmers and agricultural wells to equip them with solar energy units from MoA. The partners will invite the nominated farmers to submit their applications and will screen the applications based on vulnerability criteria and through verification visits (focusing on prioritizing women headed farms). To have an efficient selection process, the partner will consult CPCs in screening the applications. The partner will provide SCI with the list of potential beneficiaries, and SCI will cross-check the list of potential beneficiaries with FSS to avoid overlapping.2) Technical assessment: SCI team will conduct technical assessmentto assess the needs of each nominated farm and agriculture well and prepare the bill of quantities of the needed rehabilitation works. To ensure the efficient design, the technical assessment will be implemented in consultation with the target farmers.3) Support farmers 100 farmers in north Gaza to improve the productivity of their farms. The project will target 200 Donums, with an average of 2 dunums of open farm per farmer (10F, 90 M), the work will include minor rehabilitation of irrigation system and provision of seeds, seedlings, such as, Tomato, Eggplant, cauliflower, cabbage, carrot. This will be based on the farmers needs and farming season) and fertilizer. The partners will implement the activity under supervision of SCI.4) Rehabilitation of 100 dunums of greenhouses (GH) for 100 farmers (North: 50, Khan Younis: 50), with an average of 1 dunum of greenhouse per farmer (10F, 90 M). The work includes fixing nylon sheets and protection net, wood and gutters and rehabilitation of GH irrigation network. Under SCI’s supervision, BLDA will rehabilitate 50 GH in North Gaza, while Bayader will rehabilitate 50 GHs in Khan Younis.5) Provision of solar energy units for 13 agricultural wells (5 in North Gaza, 5 in khan Younis and 3 in Rafah) The wells will be equipped with solar energy units total of 300 kW to improve access to irrigation for 260 farmers.. Based on the technical assessment, BLDA will provide the solar energy units for 5 wells in North Gaza while BAYDER will provide the solar energy units for another 8 wells in Khan Younis and Rafah. The provision of solar energy units for the target 13 wells will be implemented by contractors. SC will coordinate closely with MoA to ensure the quality of work and matching with MoA plans.6) Advocacy: SCI will coordinate with farmers, CBOs, and existing CPCs to produce a short study leading to publishing an infographic on challenges that households in target areas face, particularly impact of food insecurity on children’s health and their access to basic needs. The study will analyze factors leading to food insecurity, with focus on damage to livelihood assets and limited opportunities for marketing and export of agricultural produce. Findings of the study will be presented to FSS partners, national and international organizations, donors, and duty bearers. The findings will be used to produce an infographic and a motion graphic video for dissemination. The intervention will be built on the understanding of gender-specific needs and expectations as well as socio-economic factors and cultural sensitivities. Both partners also have a long track record of sound implementation and organizational capacity in delivering similar projects. Given that they have access to these communities, they will support SCI in the selection of the target groups, facilitate SCI access to these communities.PSEA and CSG: all project staff (SC, partners, contractors) involved in the project implementation and delivery of services to target communities will receive a training on PSEA and CSG policies, also, their contracts will include a special clause on PSEA and SG policies. Moreover, target communities will be oriented on PSEA and safeguarding concerns that must be reported and reporting channels.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

SCI has developed this integrated modality in collaboration with local partners to ensure meeting local needs and priorities among most vulnerable communities. 9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The MEAL system of SCI considers monitoring function as a live function for systematically tracking the implementation of actions that form part of project/program resulting in effective, performance-based project/program management. It provides basic tools to collect information to assess the efficiency and effectiveness of the project. SCI developed Quality Benchmarks (QBMs) lists to ensure high quality standards. QBMs monitoring visits will be conducted to project activities monthly and reported to program management for their considerations and actions. In addition, to ensure evidence-based learning and implementation, the project will conduct different assessments and studies to inform the continuous improvements and learning. At the early stage of the project, the team will be working on having the Baseline study assessment that will give the base-values of the different goals, outcomes, and output indicators. This includes Food consumption score, coping strategies index, and household diet diversity index SCI has established an accountability mechanism including complaints and suggestion boxes placed at CBOs’ offices as well as a Hotline. SCI staff and partners will make sure that targeted communities are informed on the types of feedback mechanisms available through publicizing materials and information sharing sessions. Any recommendations, suggestions, and concerns of the beneficiaries, along with the mitigation measures will be discussed with partners to be properly addressed. SCI will consult with the farmers in designing the rehabilitation in their farms and their expectations from this project. SCI in some cases follow an aggressive approach in Accountability by conducting specialized monitoring visits to the very marginalized communities to hear from them and to document any raised concerns, CSG or protection issues. SCI has CPCs and GPFPs that actively participate in design and implementation of interventions.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

SCI’s risk analysis ensures that all the activity risks will be identified and the relevant mitigation measures will be in place. SCI will follow a conflict-sensitive approach to ensure timely identification and mitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. SCI will conduct strong community engagement to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions. SCI will ensure that the project implementation aligned with the target communities protection plan and consider the risk analysis Beneficiary selection will be triangulated by field visits, cluster consultation, and local stakeholder information. SCI will maintain its independence and impartiality. Staff will not engage in discussions on sensitive political and community issues while working with beneficiaries. SCI will continuously monitor the development of the COVID-19 fragile context and risks. It will analyze the potential interaction between the planned activity, the changing context and potential implications for the relations between stakeholders and communities. SCI`s Social Tensions Tracking and Mapping system (STTM) will locate geographic areas with communities vulnerable to social tensions. SCI`s strong MEAL approach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce course correction to programming as required. SCI will strictly enforce its Child Safeguarding policies and processes including support to partners as needed.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Selection criteria will ensure that all the targeted beneficiaries have equal rights to the proposed assistance based on clear targeting criteria. SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living with physical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services outreach of both male and female people with disabilities tends to be limited. The project will prioritize for example PwDs and women with means to enhance their economic independence and productivity. The project gender sensitivity will be integrated and considered as a minimum standard. The project will be open to both women and men according to the vulnerability criteria, with the option to create women only working groups to facilitate their participation. The selection criteria will focus on female headed HHS. SCI, together with project partners, will check during the project visits the problems that women are facing and that the project activities are designed through gender and child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse by the family members. Both Men and women will be included in sessions with different interests and fields related mainly to enhancing their capacity in agricultural production. The project will give special focus to youth, including female farmers and HHs with members with disabilities (PwDs) . The Gender Advisor will ensure gender mainstreaming in the interventions. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to the project services.
