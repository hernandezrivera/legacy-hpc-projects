Project Summary

The proposed programme will increase access to improved and durable WASH services for conflict affected populations in Idleb and Aleppo governorates offering a comprehensive response to potable water, Solid Waste Management (SWM) and sanitation needs for estimated 471,541 conflict affected people, of which approx. 185,666 IDPs. Areas were assessed and selected based on People in Need (PIN) and Severity of Need as presented in the draft Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2020. Given the current context and mass displacement from southern Idleb, our organisation will reprogram activities as required in order to serve areas hosting the highest number of people recently displaced by current conflict dynamics. This will include areas in northern Aleppo where we are currently operational (Afrin and A’zaz districts).
Assessed areas are in urgent need of WASH assistance. The proposed action will deliver an evidence-based program of assistance in targeted areas to increase access to safe water and safeguard long-term service provision through establishing cost recovery mechanisms. Innovative approaches will be used to increase the sustainability of water service provision, including the use of solar power and other measures to reduce fuel requirements. SWM support and repair of sanitation infrastructure will also be delivered in targeted areas to protect and improve public health.

4 - Needs

The draft 2020 HNO estimates that 11.06 million people will require humanitarian assistance in 2020, of which 4.65 million will be in acute need. The Idleb Crisis has compounded an already desperate situation with humanitarian need reaching unprecedented levels. HNAP Mobility and Needs Monitoring overview for Dec 19 reports 196,067 newly displaced IDPs in Dec19. The WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant repair and operational support given almost nine years of crisis. WASH systems have suffered from damages owing to years of functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance, displacement of technical staff, the effects of climate change and poor water resources management. 42% of the population is relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs, at least 80% of sewage is discharged untreated while at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional, 26 per cent of garbage is inappropriately disposed of within communities. Such factors pose significant public health risks and have harmful cascade effects on communities in terms of nutritional status, inequality and poverty. (HNO 2020 WASH sect). A Countrywide WASH overview for Syria developed by the WASH Cluster and REACH to inform the 2020 HNO and dated Dec19 found 8% of assessed HHs were unable to access a sufficient quantity of water in the 30 days prior to data collection. The proportion of HHs reporting water insufficiency is noticeably higher in govs with a high proportion of the population reliant on water trucking as a primary water source. The highest proportion of HHs unable to access a sufficient quantity of water was found in Idleb gov, targeted by this project. Respondents in Idleb also reported the highest median % of HH income spent on water (6.7%). Problems often reported relating to the broader wastewater disposal systems include blocked networks, sewer overflows and a lack of desludging services.

5 - Implementation of activities

Funding will be sought to support 6 assessed communities in Idleb and Aleppo govs. Activities will focus on reducing oper costs and increasing sustainability of water provision through introducing solar powered systems. A techn assessment of solar power completed by our org early Jan 2020 confirmed solar can generate sufficient power to pump water for 6 months of the year. Solar power systems will be rolled out in targeted stations to reduce oper costs (fuel/ diesel), increase sustainability and reduce the end cost to water users to support CR. Oper material will be provided for 3 mos while solar panels are installed. Hybrid power systems (solar and generators) will then be used for water pumping. Water network rehab will also be implemented in targeted areas to ensure all the population has access to the network and to rehabilitate, with sewage pipes cleared and upgraded as necessary to protect public health. Fully funded by the SCHF up to 31Jan2020, access and population movement permitting, funding will be sought to maintain support to Al Mastumeh station in 2020.This will include operational costs (gradually reduced as CR is fully rolled out) and essential maintenance works. Oper costs (specifically fuel) will be reduced through the installation of electrical capacitors in control panels,. These have been piloted and found to result in the same volume of water pumped with fewer working hours, reducing oper costs and increasing sustainability. Water network rehabilitation will also be completed. Fully funded by SCHF up to 31Mar20 access and population movement permitting, funding will be sought to maintain adequate water delivery in Idleb City through operational support to 6 boreholes in Idleb City plus SWM services to meet the needs of an increased population. SWM will be implemented as Cash for Work (CfW) to create income generation opportunities.WASH services in Daret Azza town: Fully funded under the SCHF up to 31Mar2020 (water trucking + SWM services).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The 2020 Protection Risk Analysis for WASH highlights areas of risk for which we have already established effective mitigation measures. In line with the WASH sector, our organisation will ensure ‘Do No Harm’ principles are applied to identify and mitigate against potential protection risks that may arise from the implementation of the WASH sector strategy and related activities. We will ensure that SPHERE/sector agreed standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context during each part of project implementation, especially during emergency sanitation infrastructure implementation phase when the risk of GBV is higher. Measures will also be taken to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, female-headed households) to greater protection risks, and particularly to GBV. WASH staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of each project, with a need to put the necessary mechanisms in place to facilitate feedback from users.We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. We also ensure that all staff sign the organisation’s code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report abuse by staff or beneficiaries, including misuse of assets or funds. We have strict protocols against any sexual relations with minors or beneficiaries of any age.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

We are continually updating contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in NWS, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Real-time analysis of the security situation will continue to allow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary. During temporary, security-related suspensions, our field teams employ various techniques to maintain communication and support to project stakeholders and beneficiaries. Should security deteriorate, day-to-day operations will be managed by water unit staff under the remote management of our WASH staff. Planned WASH activities for 2020 have all been informed by recent technical assessments completed by our staff, and are therefore evidence-based, feasible and accurately budgeted. We also have well-established working relationships with the relevant Water Directorates and Water Units, which will again facilitate implementation - in particular planned activities to establish CRMs. We will continue to establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time. MoUs will be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated facilities. CR systems will be promoted for longer term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership. In line with current SOPs, proper storage of supplies will be ensured with clear provision of care and safety consideration. We will continue to coordinate closely with all partners on the ground and to work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared. We will ensure staff/contractors involved in construction works or working with chemicals wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations. Regular safety training will also be conducted for staff and contractors.
