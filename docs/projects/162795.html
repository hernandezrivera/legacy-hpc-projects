Project Summary

The proposed project builds upon NEF’s continuous presence in Numbers of states in Sudan (South Kordofan, North Kordofan and in 2020 will be operating in West Kordofan, while in Darfur, NEF presents in Central Darfur ).The Near East Foundation (NEF) proposes Humanitarian Support for IDPs and Vulnerable Populations in Water, Sanitation and Hygiene in South Kordofan State, Central Darfur, West Kordofan and North Kordofan. This project aims to provide life-saving activities to improve health and well-being among conflict-affected people (IDPs and vulnerable host populations) by improving their access to potable water and adequate sanitation and hygiene. The project will assist people in 4 states (mentioned above) and some localities in SKS, NKS, CDS and WKS.
However, the proposed project will focus on the same above states and will focus on communities with pressing humanitarian needs. It (a) capitalizes on NEF’s continuous presence, experience, and networks, (b) maximizes available local capacities (e.g., local community-based organizations and WSCs) and community assets (e.g., underground water sources, local construction materials), (c) builds local resources and capacities to ensure improved WASH conditions and minimizes dependence on external assistance during emergencies and impeded access to the region, and (d) mobilizes available resources (e.g., land, community contributions, local water supply and construction materials, local trainers and hygiene promoters) to immediately respond to help those affected by the conflict – giving priority to time-sensitive activities such as hardware-based interventions (e.g., latrines and provision of safe water) and integrating software-based interventions (e.g., behavior change promotion and community resource management) – to improve WASH access while improving transition strategies and sustainability of project results
The overall environmental health, hygiene and sanitation situation is particularly poor in target communities. Average water consumption level is 4.53 l/p/d – far lower that the Sphere and WASH Sector-recommended 7.5-15 l/p/d; 63% of the population has no access to latrines and hand-washing facilities. More than 75% of displaced populations are women, children, persons with disability, or elderly. Communities experience increased incidence of waterborne and water-related diseases (schistosomiasis, malaria, diarrhea), widespread open defecation, and decreased health and increased environmental contamination associated with poor sanitation and hygiene. Without this intervention, more than 180410 conflict-affected persons (indirectly 902050 persons) will have no access to clean sources of water, adequate sanitation, or awareness of proper hygienic practices. Their risk of morbidity and mortality will rise, waterborne and water-related diseases will become more frequent, and environmental contamination will increase, the project aiming at that, conflict-affected people (IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable host populations) in the above mentioned states will reduce their risk of morbidity and mortality through improved access to clean water, adequate sanitation, and good hygiene. Total number of beneficiaries expected to be targeted is 180410 conflict-affected persons (indirectly 902050 persons).

1 - Needs

Our project aims to provide life-saving WASH activities to improve health and well-being among conflict-affected people (IDPs and vulnerable host populations) by improving their access to potable water and adequate sanitation and hygiene. In February 2019, NEF conducted needs assessments in South Kordofan and Central Darfur based upon field consultation and desk review of existing assessments and surveys that focused on WASH, agricultural production, and food security, in coordination with the WASH Sector and the FS&L Sector, and with relevant line ministries. The assessments identified communities most in need of humanitarian assistance due to the degradation of their hygiene situation, water supply, sanitation, agricultural production, and food security situation. The assessments confirmed that current humanitarian responses are few and fragmented. The assessment team triangulated data with IOM, WES, MOA, WFP, UNICEF, FEWS NET, and other humanitarian assessments. Lack of basic services and water and sanitation infrastructure, along with hygienic practices, agricultural assets, and food production, continue to prevent the return of IDPs to their areas of origin and have created an acute emergency for returnees, vulnerable populations, and newly displaced persons (NEF NA, 02/2019).

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

NEF guiding principles. NEF mainstreams protection through training and enforcement of its Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct. Through these principles, and its adherence to Protection Principles as per the Humanitarian Charter and Minimum Standards of Child Protection in Humanitarian Action (CPMS), NEF resolutely ensures the protection of children and at-risk adults. NEF further ensures the safety and dignity of all beneficiaries through its strict adherence to the principles of Do No Harm (DNH). NEF actively looks for ways to minimize any threats and vulnerabilities of every group of the affected population. Protection is mainstreamed throughout project design and implementation by NEF’s promotion of meaningful, safe, and dignified access. Contractors and project partners need also to adhere to NEF’s guiding principles. NEF applies DNH and Sphere principles to its selection of participants, guaranteeing fair representation, regardless of their ethnicity, religion, or gender. NEF further reflects Sphere’s belief that all individuals have a right to assistance in our commitment to working with marginalized and vulnerable populations, including female head-of-households, survivors of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), youth, older people, and persons with disabilities. These considerations guide all of NEF to prevent exposing vulnerable and marginalized people to further harm. Consultation, feedback, and response. Through confidential and secure feedback mechanisms, NEF ensures that all beneficiaries are able to raise concerns and complaints. NEF responds to beneficiary feedback by adopting the DNH principles of constantly learning and improving. In addition to responding to and reducing risk, NEF assists affected populations to claim their rights and access appropriate remedies. As appropriate, NEF will establish community mechanisms (example, WASH committees) that are diverse and representative of the community. Women and girls are at disproportionate risk of GBV and SGBV. NEF strives to mitigate these risks through consultation in project design and implementation. NEF has a formal gender policy as well as a dedicated gender officer and it seeks to mainstream the participation of women in all activities. Throughout the project, NEF will give special attention to gender-based constraints and opportunities to ensure that activities and decision-making processes are accessible to women and girls and that they promote appropriate access to and safe use of NEF services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

NEF projects are designed, implemented and monitored with the participation of the beneficiary communities, government line ministries, HAC, donors and local partners. NEF ensures that affected populations are engaged at all levels of the project cycle. To help operationalize its commitment to actively seek, listen to, consider, engage, protect and act on feedback from the communities and individuals that are the direct recipients of assistance, NEF maintains an organization-wide Accountability to Affected Populations Framework linked to its organizational policies and practices and is built upon the 5 Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations. NEF expands upon this framework to include principles of protection, do-no-harm, appropriateness, and coordination. Leadership & Governance. NEF works in partnership with local NGOs and CBOs throughout the lifecycle of our projects. Where there is need and demand, NEF devotes resources to capacity building of local entities, aligning projects with relevant policies and priorities. All project staff, regardless of organizational affiliation, will work together as a single team. As the lead, NEF takes full responsibility for project management and implementation, for coordination among partners at an organizational level, for delivering results and for reporting. Transparency. Outreach events include information about the organizational stakeholders, criteria and processes for beneficiary recruitment, participation, and access to project benefits, project approach and objectives, and project sponsors, all in local languages, displayed in public, accessible locations. Community-based project association meetings and trainings will use culturally and linguistically appropriate communications. Feedback and Complaints. Beneficiary feedback mechanisms will be incorporated into program implementation, particularly through monitoring, evaluation and learning (MEL) processes. NEF will transparently communicate the purpose of all MEL activities with beneficiaries, and seek their feedback. NEF will consult communities to establish an appropriate feedback and response mechanism. All methods of feedback collection will involve sensitization of staff, partners, volunteers, and beneficiaries on the purpose of methods used and will communicate clear roles, responsibilities, and processes. Feedback will be collected legally, ethically, and with due regard to the welfare of those affected by the results. In addition, the feedback system will uphold confidentiality and non-retaliation principles as per NEF’s Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct. Participation. To verify needs, gauge acceptance, and ensure the target population understands the project, community consultation and orientation meetings will be held at the beginning of the project. These meetings will raise awareness of the communities’ participation roles and responsibilities and of the program partners’ responsibilities toward them.
