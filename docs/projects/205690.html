Project Summary

Ars to indirectly suppActed will provide WASH assistance through static presence in Upper Nile while providing emergency rapid response through mobile interventions in the country. Overall, Acted aims to reach 33,333 individuals with WASH assistance of which 20,000 returnees, 10,000 IDPs and 3,333 members from host communities. Acted objective is to cover immediate emergency needs through rapid response in vulnerable counties and in Renk County through Joda reception centre and Renk transit centre.
While the exact location of the rapid response component is yet to be determined as Acted deployment for these multi-sectoral interventions (including CCCM, SNFI, protection, cash, and WASH) is based on displacement triggers, in the current proposal Acted has estimated that it will be deployed in priority in the intersectoral priority counties 1 (Akobo and Ayod).
In line with WASH Cluster sectoral objectives, Acted interventions will aim to contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality (SO2), Provide access to basic WASH services in underserved locations affected by disaster (S03), increase disability inclusion and mitigating GBV at WASH facility (S04) and provide durable access to services in long-term IDP sites and communities chronically affected by disaster (S05). To ensure a comprehensive response to vulnerable communities’ needs, all WASH activities will be integrated with other Acted sectoral expertise with a focus on protection, food security, Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). Acted will also ensure to engage with health and nutrition partners to indirectly support the Sectoral Objective 1 Reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality.

1. Needs

More than a decade after its creation, South Sudan water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) situation remains dire, having being impacted by years of conflict, increased exposure to climate change effects and a chronic lack of investment in WASH infrastructures. In 2024, 5.59 million individuals are expected to need WASH aid including over 1,100,000 Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and 3,700,000 host community members and almost 700,000 returnees. Safe access to water is particularly challenging in the country as 42 per cent of the population do not have access to an improved water source and 45 per cent need to walk for more than 30 minutes to access its main water source (2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview). This is particularly problematic for persons with physical disabilities, as well as for women and girls who are increasingly exposed to Gender Based Violence risks while collecting water. Likewise, continual displacement, insecurity and a worsening economy have compounded the impact of a historically low coverage of water infrastructure. The economic downturn has exacerbated the situation, limiting the resources available for the maintenance of wells and boreholes, resulting in many communities having nonfunctional water sources and who cannot mobilize resources to repair them. This is particularly challenging when such a location hosts an increased number of individuals, either IDPs displaced due to man-made or natural shocks, or returnees, increasing pressure on the limited resources and threatening social cohesion. In addition, despite the repeated shocks, the limited WASH facilities have little resilience towards floodings and structures such as latrines often collapses during the rainy season, leading to increased open defecation and subsequent water-born diseases such as cholera. Displaced populations also often left with limited resources (both material and financial) and do not have the capacity to retrieve essential WASH items ensuring their safe access to water (buckets) and hygiene materials (soap). Due to Sudan conflict, the increasing returnee population (estimated at 270,000 persons in 2024) is also triggering significant needs in areas of origin and transit. Safe water supply and access to sanitation is a primary concern in Renk state, the first county recipient of returnees since May 2023 with 86% of the returnees as of October 2023. Due to high population density, water borne diseases such as Cholera could spread quickly with a high mortality rate among the vulnerable displaced population and competition over water access has already led to several security incidents.In 2024, out of the 5.59 million individual in needs of WASH assistance, 47% will be targeted with humanitarian assistance with a focus on covering the needs of IDPs and returnees. In line with these priorities, Acted will target host community members, IDPs and returnees in its response in Joda transit centre (Renk), Maban host communities, and throughout its mobile interventions.

2. Project activities

Acted will implement the following activities to increase vulnerable populations’ from IDPs and host communities’ access to WASH services throughout its mobile interventions (in line RRT triggers and intervention package):- Rehabilitation of non-functional boreholes and solar upgrading- Rehabilitation/repair of handpumps- Emergency latrine construction and decommissioning- Distribution of core WASH items- Hygiene promotion sessions/campaigns - Conducting safety audit and risk mitigation assessments to identify the key WASH resources, facilities, needs and service provision gaps- Installation of handwashing points- Establishing and training Water Committees Users- Implementation of community-led solid waste management In parallel, and in link with its site management duty in Renk transit centre and Joda reception centre, Acted will provide the following support to returnees: - Provision of emergency water treatment stockpile (Renk)- Waterpoint monitoring to ensure peaceful access to water (Renk)- Latrine maintenance (Joda)- Emergency communal showers construction (Renk)- Environmental sanitation (Renk and Joda)- Construction of waste pits (Joda and Renk) In Maban, to ensure safe access to WASH services and subsequently promote social cohesion between host and returnees communities, Acted will implement the following activities: - Borehole and hampump repair - Training of water management committee - Construction of emergency latrines for the most vulnerable - Construction for VIP latrines in institutions (including health facilities and schools)- Installation of hand washing facilities - Hygiene promotion - In kind support through the distribution of Menstrual Health Management kits and hygiene kits All activities will be coordinated with food security and livelihoods, nutrition and health partners to ensure that the most vulnerable individuals have access to a complete assistance package and improve their overall nutrition and well-being. In parallel, a specific attention will be granted to mainstream Disaster Risk Reduction component to ensure that facilities are resistant to floodings as much as possible.
