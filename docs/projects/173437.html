Project Summary

"The aim of this project is to ensure equitable access and quality education to adolescent girls and boys and young adults who have not engaged ever with education or currently not engaged in study. This project will provide Inclusive Education and Psych-Social Support (PSS) along with COVID-19 prevention and preparedness and Disaster Risk Reduction Strategies. FIVDB will adopt the Cox's Bazar Education Sector curriculum and primers for the Rohingya community and FIVDB’s adolescent primers (aligned with the NCTB curriculum) for the host community. The courses and contents and teaching-learning methodology will be adapted in a way that, the adolescent learners find it relevant for themselves. The adaptation has already been started as FIVDB is implementing 48 both established and home based LCs in the camps with FCDO and AHP funding and our education team is working with the beneficiaries at the learning center level for adaptation. 3200 Rohingya girls and boys will be enrolled in the learning centers in 7 targeted camps, while 25 government or non-formal schools will be selected for rehavilitation from 5 targeted Union in the host communities. The participants' ages will be between 12-24 years. The size of the students in a learning group would be 10 considering the COVID-19 situation so that physical distance could be maintained. Among the learners 70% will be girls and 7% will be differently able person. There will be hand washing facility and hygiene kits for girls and boys separately in each learning center and school. Teachers will be recruited from the communities and will be trained by the FIVDB education team with support from other sector partners, as it is done currently. There will be close monitoring and supervision of the learning outcomes and regular assessment of the progress in learning.
The teachers will be provided training on protection and inclusion, PSS and disaster risk reduction as they can ensure the participation of all learners including differently able persons and safety of the participants. In every LC, proper drainage system, waste management system and separate washroom facilities for males and females will also be ensured. There will be a 7-member learning center management committee consisting of members from the participants, guardians and community members. At least 3 members of the committee will be female. This committee will be involved in learning center management as well as all kinds of protection activities. Psycho Social support will be ensured by training teachers on PFA and LC Management Committees and SMC members"

1 - Identified Needs

"Despite significant progress during the last two years in providing safe and systematic access to learning opportunities, there are still gaps in rohingya community in education sector as well as in host community. 27% of household reported a loss or diminished access to education as an impact of the COVID-19 outbreak (J-MSNA, Preliminary findings, 1 October 2020). More than 30 percent of Rohingya children and youth aged 3-24 years old still require access to education. 69 percent of Rohingya refugee households reported at least one 5-17 years old aged child not benefiting from any learning opportunities. Furthermore, an alarming 83% of the adolescents and youth aged 15-24 years old don’t have access to any educational or skills development activities (Joint Multi Sector Needs Assessment, ISCG, September 2019). Impact of the lockdown on education is a general concern. There was consensus that the disruption of education poses a great concern and aside the loss of education, less regularly daily routine as a risk to children’s wellbeing of both rohingya and host community. All Learning Centers and Schools remain closed, continuing to hamper access to education for rohingya and host adolescents. There is a need to improve education technology to be used as a complementary solution for learning. (Situation Report, September 2020) For the rohingya 9% of individuals that attended any form of learning before COVID-19 outbreak who will not be sent back. And for the host 3% of individuals that attended any form of learning before the COVID-19 outbreak who will not be sent back . 40% of individuals aged 4-24 were reported not to have attended any formal learning before the COVID-19 outbreak. (J-MSNA, Preliminary findings, 1 October 2020)According to J-MSNA preliminary findings September 2020, the main barriers for rohingya community to studying effectively remotely are- Inability of other household members to support children; lack of money to pay for private tuition; support on remote education generally reported to have been limited across the surveyed camps; children needed to help in household. Main barriers for the host community in particular among poor families, inability of parents to support due to a lack of education and lack of money to pay for private tutor. Adolescent girls to be less likely to be sent back to school due to socio-cultural norms and Girls on the other hand may already be helping in the household and instances of child marriage. Children over the age of 10 reported to be less likely to be sent back due to a lack of appropriate learning opportunities. COVID-19 poses High rate of children drops - out and early child marriage. From our observation community people and parents are not engaged in education program in camps and host. There is lack of awareness about the importance of education of adolescents, that is why parents and community people should be engaged in the education program. In the 7 targeted camps (3, 4, 4 extention, 14, 18, 21, 27, NYP RC and KUT RC) total gap are 18202 individuals of 6-24 age groups. In the host community there are according to 2020 the target was 7674 approximately in 12-24 age groups and outreach is zero due to closure of the schools because of the COVID-19 outbreak (JRP-2021_EduSector_PIN_Target Calculation V8 (RC HC)). A noticeable fact is that gap between girls and boys are huge among the 6-18 age group. We have targeted 400 adolescent girls and boys (12-24 age olds) from each targeted camps. In the targeted host community 5 union would be selected for the school rehabilitation and 5 secondary registered schools would be selected from each union.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Site Selection for Learning Centers (LCs): LCs sites will be selected discussing with the community members in targeted camps . A Total of 160 LCs will be established in 8 camps considering accessibility for all including impaired people and factors regarding socio-cultural norms. Considering the COVID-19 , Safety instructions and hygiene would be considered strictly. 2 . LCs establishment and School Rehabilitation: The LCs will be well decorated and equipped with reach environment according to the sector standards. LCs will be home-based learning centers to mitigate the scarcity of available space to establish LCs. The size of the students in a learning group would be 10 learners considering the COVID-19 situation . Each LCs and schools would have separate hand washing point for girls and boys. There would be disinfectants, hygienic kit like soap, mask, sanitizer etc. and cleaning materials for learning centers and schools. Key messages on COVID-19 would be developed and disseminated. 5 schools (registered High School) would be selected for rehabilitation from 5 targeted Unions.3. Adaptation of curriculum: FIVDB will implement the Cox's Bazar Education Sector curriculum and primers for Rohingya community (English, Burmese and Math) and NCTB curriculum: English language skill will be focused ), adapted according to the need and learners interest in coordination with education sector and UEO . 4. Form LC/School Management Committee for LCs and Schools: There will be a 7-member ( At least 3 female) learning center management committee consisting members from the participants, guardians and community members. This committee from both host and Rohingya community will look after the LCs management and all kind of protection issues. This Committee in the government and registered schools will be capacitated to do the same. Monthly meeting would be conducted with education committee to know the progress, maintenance issues and what they have learnt to ensure community ownership and active participation.5. Program Participants Selection: For the program participants selection, our existing database would be used as well as sector data and various need assessments in consultation with the Education Committee from the community and approval of the CiCs and UNO. From 12-24 years old adolescent and youth will be program participants to reduce child marriage and child labour with a focus to school drop-outs and 70% of the learners would be girls because according to the sector gap analysis, girls have less access in education. 7% children and youth with disabilities will be selected among the targeted learners. adolescent with walking and climbing challenges will be provided porter to come to the LCs. 6. Select and Train the Facilitator and Teacher: Facilitators and Teachers (both male and female) will be recruited from the both Rohingya and Host Communities and will be trained by FIVDB education team with support from other sector partners. The teachers, LC management committee and SMC members will be provided training on Disaster Risk Reduction, Psycho-Social Support, Protection and Inclusion. 7. Deliver Session: After preparing the LCs and facilitators, learning session would be delivered in 2 shift in a group consisting 10 learners. The program participants will get lesson in English, Math and Burmese Language as well as they will get Psycho-Social Support (PSS) session. Awareness session on PSS will be accommodated with regular learning session. Adolescent with disability will get special care and attention as well as they will have additional support for their learning process.8. Psycho-Social Support (PSS): To Improve Psychosocial well- being and resilience among vulnerable adolescents and youth, program participants would be provided consistent awareness session on psycho-social support and wellbeing through drawing, group activities and session. 9. Monitoring : Community participation will be ensured .
