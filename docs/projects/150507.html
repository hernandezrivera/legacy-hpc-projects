Project Summary

Project Objective : Is to improve the lives of the most vulnerable families in the Gaza Strip through rehabilitating their houses and distributing NFIs.
The suggested interventions are linked to HRP as follows:
SO2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL. The project will will improve the lives of the poor by rehabilitating their homes, utilities, ceilings, windows. This have a direct impact on the health, hygiene, dignity & self respect of the target families especially children.
SO3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. The project will also protect the poor families to cope with the environmental threats and other shocks: winterization and summarization by improving the ceilings, walls, windows, etc.
The suggested project is also linked to the SC objectives 2: Vulnerable households exposed to harsh weather and protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity. This will be mainly achieved by improving/insulating the ceiling of the houses to prevent water from entering the houses in the winter and to reduce heat through the summer.
Project Summary : The project is mainly rehabilitating 200 houses for the most poor and vulnerable families in three governorates of the Gaza Strip: North, Gaza, and Rafah (60 houses in North governorate, 80 houses in Gaza governorate, and 60 houses in Rafah governorate). NDC will implement the project in partnership with three registered cluster partners. The three NGOs are located in the 3 governerates. Three local NGOs partners will be responsible for implementing the project activities on the ground while NDC will be responsible for the overall management of the project, coordinate with local NGOs, MoPWH, MoSD, shelter cluster, and INGOs. NDC will allocate in an average an amount of $2,500 for rehabilitating each house (maximum amount per house is $3,000) so that they will meet the minimum standards. The rehabilitation will include utilities rehabilitation such as plumbing, electricity works, and tiling, which are important for the hygiene and good health. The rehabilitation will also include fixing/replacing deteriorated doors and windows for safety and protection purposes, and roof rehabilitation or replacement if necessary to protect the families and their children from winterization and summarization. The rehabilitation of the houses and also distributing plastic films, especially for the roofs and windows, will protect the residents of the houses from the need to evacuate their houses in winter because of flooding.
In addition, the project includes distributing NFIs to those families to help them cope with bad climate conditions. NDC will allocate $300 as NFIs for each beneficiary such as mattresses, blankets, kitchen wears, fans, plastic sheets .
The project is designed and will be implemented with full consultation with the beneficiaries. In fact, 4 focus groups were conducted to learn about the beneficiaries main needs and how the project will better serve them. In addition, the project team visited more than 30 houses (10 houses in each governerate) and met with their owners to receive their feedback and main needs. Hence, the project was designed to deal with the most urgent needs of the poor and vulnerable families by rehabilitating their houses, providing more privacy especially for girls in terms of using bath rooms by providing locked doors, and taking the needs of people with disabilities into considerations especially for bath room uses, size of doors, and houses entrances. In addition, when possible, the project will try to overcome the problem of over crowdness and privacy by installing wooden separators in suitable rooms.
Furthermore, the beneficiary will fully participate in the M&E activities of the project. Full information is provided in the indicators section under M&E.

Needs

The Inter-agency Shelter Survey (March 2018) on Substandard Housing Conditions in Gaza indicated that: * 92.5% of the households surveyed were affected by some kind of shelter vulnerability. Of these, 6.2%, or the equivalent of 19,700 housing units, were classified as beyond repair, meaning that full reconstruction is needed. * Some 10.5% of the homes (about 33,000 housing units) were classified as either moderately or highly vulnerable. The remainder, 75.8 per cent, fell within the low and slight vulnerability categories. Of the approximately 33,000 housing units that are moderately or highly vulnerable, 24,000 are believed to require a prioritized shelter response. * About 13% of the cases from the MOSD sample were exposed to a kind of security risk or in flood prone areas or close to waste sites or waste water overflow. * On the average 24% of households in the MOSD sample lived in housing units that were lacking functioning and suitable external and internal doors at the housing units. * About 125% in the MOSD sample were living in housing units that were lacking proper area of windows suitable for natural lighting and ventilation. Furthermore 53% of the MOSD sample were living in housing units with windows that were not sealed from rain, water, and wind, and did not have solid panels (glass, polycarbonate sheet or equivalent). * In about 26% of the surveyed housing units from MOSD sample exterior walls possessed cracks or holes with no single room insulated against heat and cold, in addition to 45% of the housing units were not sealed and had water leaks. * In 9% of the MOSD sample, the housing unit had not 1 kitchen (inside or outside) with water tap and cooking space, and is separated from toilet by solid wall. Additionally about 26% of MOSD samples did not have a kitchen which had a solid and cleanable preparation counter and sink, with adequate space of minimum 3m2. * Arrange from 10% to 40% of the surveyed MOSD samples were lacking one or more items specified as minimum requirements for electric supply and lighting. The Humanitarian Need Overview (HNO) for 2017 indicates that "In Gaza, chronic housing shortages and the dire economic situation as a result of the ten-year long blockade, and three major escalations of conflict, have compounded concerns for adequate shelter protection for acutely-vulnerable families". The HNO also indicates that "over 4,725 acutely vulnerable families are living in sub-standard and unhygienic conditions that raise a range of humanitarian health and protection concerns". Moreover, NDC had conduced financing a project with its local partner SAWAD. The project managed to rehabilitate 104 in the eastern parts of Gaza City. The project evaluation indicated that such type of projects are well justified, have large impact on the lives of the target families, and their is a huge need for such interventions (house rehabilitation). During October 2018, four focus groups were conducted by NDC partners to discuss the main needs of the poor families. Two focus groups were conducted in the north and attend by 23 persons (11 males and 12 females). Two other focus groups were conducted in Gaza city and attended by (39) persons. (22 males and 17 females). The age of the participants started from 18 years and some participants were more than 60 years. The participants indicated that cash assistance and house rehabilitation are the most two top priorities for them. It is expected that the number of female beneficiary will be more than 60% since priority in selecting the house to be rehabilitated will be given to women headed households such as widows and divorced. In addition, priority will be given for house holds that have large number of children, and/or disabled, and/or elder people. Moreover, children, women, and elderly will participate in monitoring and evaluating the progress of the project.

Activities

The project will target 200 families from three governorates: North, Gaza, and Rafah. The beneficiaries have to be recipients of the MOSD cash transfer fund (the poorest of the poor).Furthermore, priority will be given to:1. Women headed households (widows, divorced, etc.)2. Families that have one or more children with special needs3. Families with large number of children4. Houses that suffer from GBV5. The ceiling of the house is from asbestos7. Crowded houses8. The house is owned by the family To select those houses that best fit the above criteria a three local committees will be formed (a committee in each governerate). The three committees will nominate target houses for the three partner NGOs who will validate the information provided based on the above criteria. Initial lists of the most suitable beneficiary will be shared and validated with the MOSD and MOPWH and shelter cluster. The final list will be developed by NDC and its partners and will be shared with the three local committees to spread among their communities for informing and any final feedback. NDC will be responsible for the overall management of the project activities, procurement, financial disbursement, monitoring and evaluation, and reporting. NDC will implement the project in partnership with three local registered cluster partner NGOs: Three local NGOs partners will be responsible for implementing the project activities on the ground, form the three local committees, and validate initial beneficiary lists submitted by the local committees. Each partner NGO will hire two engineers (project coordinator and field coordinator) who will follow up the rehabilitation works. NDC and the 3 local partner NGOs will finalize the beneficiary lists with MoPWH, MoSD, and Shelter Cluster. This will ensure good targeting for the poor and prevent any duplication of efforts and services received by beneficiaries. NDC will allocate an average of $2,500 for rehabilitating each house (maximum of $3000 per house) so that they will meet the minimum standards. The rehabilitation will include utilities rehabilitation such as plumbing, electricity works, and tiling, which are important for the hygiene and good health. The rehabilitation will also include fixing/replacing deteriorated doors and windows for safety and protection purposes, and roof rehabilitation and/or replacement if necessary to protect the families and their children from winterization to protect families from winter storms and flooding and displacement and also from summarization problems. In addition, the project includes distributing NFIs to those families to help them cope with bad climate conditions. NDC will allocate $300 as NFIs for each beneficiary such as mattresses, blankets, kitchen wears, fans, plastic sheets . Implementation Activities:1. Local committees will nominate potential houses for rehabilitation2. Local NGOs conduct home visits to beneficiaries3. Selection of beneficiaries base on the above criteria4. Finalize a list of beneficiaries with MOSD and shelter cluster5. Sign agreement with selected beneficiaries6. Conduct home visits to determine the required rehabilitation works and NFIs needs7. Prepare tender documents. Contractors will be used to ensure timely implementation of the rehabilitation and to be conducted on professional manner. In each area two contractors will be contracted (total of 6 contractors). In addition, the contractors will be asked to work on batches of houses (5 houses per batch). This ensures that any house will not take more than 7 days of rehabilitation to ensure the comfort of beneficiaries. NDC consulted potential beneficiaries on this mechanism who expressed their satisfaction. 8. Advertise the tender and award to the best responsive bidder9. Distribute NFIs such as blankets, mattress, fans, housewares for beneficiaries10. Hand over the rehabilitation works and beneficiary sign clearance documents.

To ensure good implementation and Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) the following steps will be implemented: 1. Each local NGO will hire two engineers (one project coordinator and one field supervisor) for implementation and M&E activities 2. NDC will hire two engineers (one project manager and one project coordinator) to ensure good implementation and solid M&E. 3. NDC's M&E Monitoring and Evaluation Officer will over manager the M&E activities and ensure that the protection standards are fully respected and followed. 4. A compliant response mechanism will be put in place to ensure that feedback and complaints from beneficiaries are reported confidentially. NDC management will ensure that these complaints are immediately investigated and addressed in the most appropriate manner. 5. NDC will implement its well-developed system of Community Score Cards (the system was developed with the support of the World Bank) to ensure the quality of service provided to beneficiaries. NDC will implement 6 sessions during the project implementation (two sessions in every governerate). The first session will be implemented at the start of the project while the second session will be implemented by the end of the project. The results will show how the beneficiaries assess the quality of the service provided to them. If the quality of the service is not as expected, the Project Manager will immediately take corrective actions. 6. Beneficiary satisfaction questionnaire will be distributed to selected representative sample of beneficiary to assess their satisfaction with the project activities. The findings of the survey along with the results of the community scorecard sessions will be used as learning tools to improve project implementation and the realization of objectives and indicators. 7. The 3 local committees which will be formed will actively participate in the implementing the project activities, monitoring and evaluation. The will visit the houses being rehabilitated to ensure good quality of work, listen to beneficiary compliance, and suggest interventions to overcome these complaints to the local NGOs and NDC.
