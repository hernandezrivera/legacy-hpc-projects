Project Summary

This project aims at building on and deepening on CAFOD on going response and prevention intervention in the Greater Tonj counties. This project will target directly 19,950 beneficiaries broken down as (10,000 women, 1800 men, 2500 boys and 5650 girls) in all the project locations (Tonj East, Tonj North and Tonj South - Warrap State). CAFOD will implement GBV prevention and response by providing quality and timely appropriate case management and psychosocial support to GBV survivors in Tonj South, Tonj East Tonj North Counties of Warrap State. This project is designed to improve response to survivors of GBV to enhance the ability of survivors to recover from shock, and make progress towards changing the social norms that perpetrators of GBV with the determination of mitigating the effects of GBV and preventing future cases. It will provide GBV response services and address critical gaps in information and knowledge among women and girls on GBV response, which limit access to services. The project will focus on provision of Psychosocial Support for women and girls through cases management in women and girls' friendly spaces. This will be in line with minimum standards on case management. Access for services for survivors will be promoted through community wide campaigns on availability of services. Women will also be provided with some cash support to safely access services with dignity to support their healing process and resilience. GBV risk mitigation will also be conducted through joint safety audits with different actors to identity risky areas for women and drive mitigation methods. Therefore, the project will address GBV issues affecting both Women and girls in the Communities.
In collaboration with peacebuilding project in Tonj North implemented by TOCH, this project will enhance women and girls participation in Peacebuilding for peaceful co- existence through capacity building to women groups and leaders on conflict resolution and staff will be trained on conflict sensitivity and Do No Harm. This is meant to create opportunities within the local community to eliminate local drivers of conflicts and Gender based violence.

Implementing Partners

The Organization for Children Harmony (TOCH South Sudan)
The Organisation for Children Harmony (TOCH) – registered in South Sudan - was formed in 2008 and operates in 5 counties of Warrap state, including Tonj East, Tonj North, Tonj South, Gogrial East and Gogrial West and Yirol East of Lakes State as an implementing partner to CTP. TOCH’s area of expertise Child Protection and Peace building programmes, and is the Co-lead of the GBV subnational cluster in Warrap State. TOCH also has experience implementing WASH and FSL activities
Under this project, TOCH is responsible for the day today implementation and management of the project across Tonj North, Tonj East and Tonj South. This includes community mobilisation and sensitization, beneficiaries targeting, registration, verification and distribution of project inputs. They also train community-level volunteers who support farmers to adopt good agronomics practices. They establish responsive feedback and complaint mechanism. They coordinate with local stakeholders. TOCH monitors implementation of the project activities, capture and share learnings.
Catholic Agency for Overseas Development Role:
CAFOD will be the lead agency and will provide strategic oversight of the programme and ensure alignment with the Protection cluster guidelines, core humanitarian standards and needs as identified through local assessments and humanitarian needs overview. CAFOD will draw upon its technical expertise in humanitarian capacity strengthening, environmental stewardship and assessments and Safe, Accessible, Dignified and Inclusive (SADI) approaches to support the project. CAFOD is CHS certified, and will work with beneficiaries to address any issues that may arise through the programme implementation. CAFOD will lead on capacity building of staff and stakeholders.
CAFOD will lead design and roll out of the project, inception and baselines assessments, and carry out regular monitoring visits to the programme sites. CAFOD will disburse funds to TOCH, and will be responsible for major procurements, while working with partner staff to build local capacity. CAFOD will also be responsible for accounting for the funds, including narrative reports.

1 - Needs

In 2022, the humanitarian Need overview showed a sustained deterioration of people’s physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms. This year, 8.9 million people, including refugees in South Sudan will need humanitarian and protection assistance, compared to 8.3 million people, representing a 7 per cent increase compared to 2021 among which 2.6 are at risk of facing Gender Based Violence due to climatic shocks, severe drought, massive flooding and conflicts contributing in different counties including Tonj north, Tonj East and Tonj South. Warrap state has been attacked with numerous emergency situations in the last few months causing displacement of communities, HNO 2022 indicates that Tonj North and Tonj East in IPC 4 and Tonj South in IPC 3. These counties continue to experience significant food insecurity, acute malnutrition, persistent insecurity, and armed conflict disrupting livelihoods, access to services like clean water, medical services and markets worsened with the recurring economic crisis that has affected household purchasing power. A recent protection need assessment and protection safety audits conducted by CAFOD/TOCH in December 2021 showed that multiple displacement have continuously exposed women and girls to rape and other forms of violence. This is exacerbated by major GBV services gaps in the three locations especially when collecting food, water, and firewood due to the unpredictable security and the consistent presence of armed actors in the surrounding areas, there are reports of GBV complications due to untimely GBV incidents response services because of poor access to GBV services. The increasing protection needs are far outweighing the available and limited resources for GBV programming for instance, women are finding significant challenges to meet their family needs, there are also reports of beating, looting, rape abduction and torture when accessing services. There is also lack of assistance for women groups that can help provide networks for mutual support and protection. Limited women and girls’ safe spaces for counselling services to especially survivors an provide referral information on available services and actors, there are only traditional court systems existing in the area with limited or no clear procedures to promote dignity and confidentiality for survivors. With the increasing trend in sexual violence, the recent scale of violence and the gaps identified justifies the prioritization of sectoral support for GBV prevention and response in the HRP to ensure violence prevention and response service provision is granted available

2 - Project activities

This project will assist crisis affected communities, households, individuals positively cope with situation and meet their aspiration. This will be achieved through establishment of women and girls safe spaces in Tonj North and Tonj East and Tonj South; Provision of support to existing women and girls safe spaces (2 in Tonj north, 2 in Tonj East and 1 in Tonj South) through structured life skills and psychosocial support (PSS) activities; Conduct community safety audits through focus group discussions with women and adolescent girls to identify GBV risk prevalence in the community; Support coordination/advocacy meetings with humanitarian actors to ensure needs of women and girls are identified and prioritized by other sectors such as WASH, NFI, shelter and food security and livelihood; Provide women and girls’ with cash assistance for risk mitigation support materials in times of acute crisis and also cover basic needs as part of case management; Provide age appropriate case management and psychosocial support services and referral services through a holistic and coordinated approach for GBV survivors for women, girls, men and boys; Conduct case management review meetings and follow up bilateral meetings with service providers, update, establish and disseminate functional, safe and immediate referral pathways for GBV survivors updated regularly (Quarterly) based on changing contexts; Ensure GBV survivors women, girls, men and boys accesses and receive appropriate comprehensive care they need; Provide training to Psychosocial support facilitators, case workers to enable them to provide quality case management and psychosocial support services to GBV survivors and people who need; Train local women’s groups on provision of psychological first aid and referrals; Train local service providers and frontline workers, community outreach teams of staff/volunteers on GBV, referral pathway Psychological first aid and GBV guiding principles; Conduct sensitization and training sessions on gender and GBV related topics targeting community stakeholders and parties to the conflict on trauma healing, conflict resolution and peacebuilding, establish functional feedback and complaint mechanism for provision of effective and appropriate services by this project including PSS services. This will include client satisfaction surveys, quarterly community group discussions, and conduct awareness raising using EMAP, SASA, and Community Cares programming to transform harmful social norms and contribute to gender inequality, regular community mobilization and awareness raising on consequences of GBV will be conducted, information dissemination on available services and benefits of accessing GBV services within 72 hours will be emphasized.

3 - Project indicators

CAFOD utilises the results-based approach to monitor its projects and track attainment of outcomes, outputs, and activities. The project will make use of a results framework with indicators and targets agreed with the Protection cluster. CAFOD uses the KOBO toolbox and Comm-Care applications for M&E assessments and analysis. Routine activity and output performance tracking is carried out monthly and this will be used to populate the 5Ws template and GBV Monitoring, and evaluation tools developed to accompany the existing tools in the GBV minimum standard. Attendance of women and girls in the women and girls’ safe space will be tracked to determine the numbers and percentages of persons who completed or drop out from structured Psychosocial support and life skills sessions, there will also be a baseline and end line survey for all women and girls attending structured psychosocial support and life skills sessions to determine their coping capacity because of attending to the support. The quality of case management trainings will be measured through the quality of knowledge and attitude of Case workers towards survivors of gender-based violence, services, levels of satisfaction for Gender based violence survivors from the services provided directly or through referrals, safety of the women and girls safe space will be assessed with the project participants participating in the activities by conducting focus group discussions. This project will modify and enrich the existing tools to suit this project. Findings of the post-distribution monitoring of dignity kits, surveys and key informant interviews will be used to adjust the project modalities. Project monitoring will be led by the Programme Quality officer and Monitoring and Evaluation section of partner office. There will also be regular joint monitoring conducted by CAFOD and community representatives. The indicators to be measured will include the number of men, women, girls, and boys supported with cash-based Individual Protection Assistance as part of case management, number of women, men, boys and girls with protection concerns supported through case management number of women, men, girls and boys provided with individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g., recreational activities,), number of women and girls who accessed services in WGFS, number of women, men, girls and boys reached through awareness-raising activities( GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting ), number of women, men, girls and boys reached through GBV prevention initiatives (disaggregated by type e.g. SASA, EMAP, community care etc.), Number of women and adolescent girls who received dignity kits (disaggregated by age) as part of addressing differential needs and GBV risk mitigation measure, number of community mobilisers trained/strengthened on GBV risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning, number of staff/frontline workers trained on GBV guiding principles and response services(Case management, Psychosocial support etc), number of women, men, girls, and boys referred for GBV response services, number of men, women, girls, and boys supported with cash-based Individual Protection Assistance as part of case management.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The project will make a deliberate effort to explore, understand and mitigate programme related risks leading to the development and implementation of strategy that minimize and address all ranges of threats and risks both external and internal. In practice, this means that all intervention responses will be guided by Core Humanitarian Standards to which CAFOD is certified and Do-No-Harm and conflict- sensitivity approaches through analysis of risks to programme participants and environment. In relation to risk 8.1 on long distances to distribution centres CAFOD has proactively mitigated this risk in its projects and will do so for this project. Additional distribution centers will be created to reduce long walking distances. As part of its responsibility of mitigating risk 8.2 and 8.3 on SEA, CAFOD has various policies on PSEA policy, code of behaviour and safeguarding policies which are signed by every staff member, volunteers and contractors before any official engagement to ensure protection of project participants. Project participants are also made aware of these policies through various information sharing ways including setting up of information kiosks and agents in the project locations. CAFOD and its partners have a robust complaint and feedback handling mechanism in place, and hotline to receive complaints and feedback. CAFOD carries out regular community awareness on the existing channels of airing out their complaints and feedback as well as making them understand that it is their right to complain. In addition, there is a policy on whistleblowing which protects the whistleblower. CAFOD has clearly laid out procedures on handling and responding to feedback and complaints. Complaints are categorised and serious, sensitive complaints such as SEA are handled by senior management with clear investigation and disciplinary protocols it must be resolved within set time limits and feedback provided to affected persons. In relation to risk 8.4 on no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors CAFOD has robust protection response and prevention activities in most of its projects. CAFOD programmes provided community based GBV protection measures such as case management, psychosocial support, creation of women and girls friendly spaces, CAFOD and its partners have mapped out referral pathways trained service providers on their use. There is also training of various service providers (health workers, police, chiefs etc.) in greater Tonj to strengthen their capacities on GBV case reporting, survivor centred handling and management of cases. As regards risk 8.8 where the risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs- based selection of beneficiaries CAFOD has employed measures to restrict or limit this influence. CAFOD makes use of a targeting committee comprised mostly of women from the community. Targeting criteria are agreed with the community in inception meetings and their inputs incorporated to ensure no vulnerable families are left out.When targeting is completed a public meeting of community members is called forpublic verification and acclamation of selected persons. CAFOD also uses its SADI (Safety Access, Dignified and Inclusivity) framework to mitigate exclusion of marginalised and vulnerable groups in a project. The framework carries out a comprehensive assessment of at-risk groups and how they are affected differently by a disaster or conditions of their vulnerability using such diversity statuses such as sex, age, disability, being a refugee, or IDP, minority status and others. This leads to design of projects that meet the specific needs of groups of people.
