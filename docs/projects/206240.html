Project Summary

This project aims to provide targeted support to returnees and refugees at entry points in Sudan and South Sudan. The project focuses on raising awareness about the danger of explosive ordnance and providing essential safety information to vulnerable populations. By equipping individuals with knowledge and skills to identify and respond to explosive hazards, the project aims to protect and safeguard the lives of returnees and refugees. Through coordinated efforts and partnerships, the project seeks to mitigate risks, enhance community resilience, and promote safe and secure environments for all affected populations.

1. Needs

Decades of conflict, still ongoing, have littered vast swathes of land with explosive hazards and 39.2 million square meters are thought to be contaminated by such hazards. The full extent of contamination is unknown with large areas of the country requiring survey. Approximately 150 previously unknown hazardous areas are discovered each month. In addition to the threats posed to the safety of conflict-affected communities, explosive hazards prevent the safe movement of the population including those who must flee active conflict. Explosive hazards preclude the delivery of humanitarian aid and inhibit socioeconomic development. There are currently an estimated 6.4 million people in need of humanitarian assistance across the country and nearly 3.5 million people displaced, including 1.6 million people who have fled to neighboring countries and approximately 1.9 million people who remain internally displaced and some are sheltering at UN Mission in South Sudan (UNMISS) protection of civilian (PoC) sites across the country. Due to the continued conflict, the contamination from the Explosive remnant of war has increased and affecting the livelihood and security of women, children and that requires a special intervention approach to mine action especially Nontechnical survey and Explosive Ordnance Risk Educationto ensure continuous and sustainable information collection, data gathering and Explosive Ordnance Risk Education for all age and generations. In this case SLI – SS will seeks to disseminate Explosive Ordnance Risk Education across Western Bahr El Gazal State to increase the safety of the most vulnerable Returnees, IDP’s and Refugees population.

2. Project activities

Activities will include the following:Capacity: a) Recruit a total of 12 Technical staff( 6 Female & Male 6) that will form 3 EORE/CL teams and managed and monitored by an experienced EORE/CL Project Manager. b) Conduct training for 3 EORE/CL and accredited by UNMAS/NMAA, deploy to the project areas. c) Development and distribution of EORE materials and messages to support EORE delivery ExplosiveOrdance Risk Education/Community Liaison (EORE/CL): d) Delivery of EORE sessions to 17,580 (Girls- 4,450, Boys-4,980, Men-3,940, Women-4,210) vulnerable populations at the communities, returnees and IDP camps and Refugees e) Organise and conduct training for 65 (Men 35, Women 30) Teachers in 95 Schools in order to deliver and integrate EORE in Schools (EORE School based program me) f) Organize and train 100 (Men 50, Women 50) Community focal persons in various locations of the project in order to coordinate dissemination of EORE and any reporting’s of hazards g) Organize Mass media campaign by organizing 10 Radio broadcast in order to target 30,000 indirect beneficiaries. h) Community liaison to gather initial contamination information for non-accessible conflict-affected areas j) Collect and reporting of Hazardous items using IMSMA reporting forms (20 HA forms through data gathering by the end of the project. Quality Management System (QMS): k) Conduct internal Quality Assurance Monitoring at least once in six weeks as per National Technical Standard Guidelines (NTSG) and SLI - SS Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) Mine Action Reporting: l) Reporting of Daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly EORE/NTS progress reports to UNMAS NMAA, protection cluster and donors.
