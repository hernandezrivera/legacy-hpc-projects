Project Summary

This project is designed to support Basena PHC, Harem Hospital in north of Idleb,and Kafar janna in Afrin and Kher PHC in Azaz area to improve an access of all population (female, male, children) (IDPs and host communities) to receive quality health services. will offer an essential package of PHC services, including: Integrated management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI), Reproductive Health, Nutrition and Non-Communicable diseases (NCD). Those requiring critical medical care will be served through an effective referral pathway linking the PHC with secondary health care facilities in Harem by ambulance. These interventions will be integrated by Community Health Workers (CHWs) at community level.
Through this project WATAN is responding to the most urgent needs with a focus on alleviating critical needs and laying the basis for more sustainable interventions that will strengthen people’s resilience. Women, children including for the children which special needs and elderly people with chronic diseases are considered the most vulnerable target groups. By implementing this type of project WATAN will be able to reach the people who most need the life-saving medical services

Needs

• Provision of health services continues to be disrupted by the ongoing conflict. Attacks on health have rendered 48 per cent of hospitals and primary health facilities in Syria as either partially functional or not functional while in some areas humanitarian partners have been unable to secure sustained and predictable access to populations in need.• Displaced populations and returnees, particularly those in northeast and northwest Syria, are vulnerable to outbreaks of infectious diseases due to unsanitary living conditions, overstretched health services, and low coverage of routine immunization.• Sustaining basic essential health services remains a key need across the country. Thirty-seven per cent of the affected population requires routine reproductive, maternal, neonatal and child health (RMNCH) services; an estimated 41 per cent of the population requires treatment for non-communicable diseases (NCDs) ; and at least 15 per cent of the population requires mental health and psychosocial services (MHPSS) , but the infrastructure and referral system that remains functional after seven years of conflict is inadequate to cope with these needs.• Access and referral to specialist health services are similarly affected. This includes dialysis, and cancer and thalassemia treatment, as well as trauma care and rehabilitation. The rate of disability in Syria is over 15 per cent of the population . Forty-five per cent of people injured by the conflict in Syria are likely to face a permanent impairment and require specialist support which will far outlast the hostilities (HNO 2019).

Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Data on attacks against healthcare collected through Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA)1 during the period of January - September 2018 documented 134 attacks across Syria, exceeding the incidents reported throughout the year 2017 (122 incidents). Although the rate of attacks against healthcare declined significantly over the course of 2018, violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers and affecting their ability to respond in contested areas, with a long term effect hampering the scale up effort in newly accessible areas that witnessed recent change of control. Probability: Moderate. Effect: High Mitigating measures: - Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG) and humanitarian leadership in their efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria. - Reporting from relevant hubs regarding health situation in reconciled areas with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase.Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) is a term used by the UN and NGO community to refer to measures taken to protect vulnerable people from sexual exploitation and abuse by staff and associated personnel. During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerable members of the community. The health sector and healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels. Probability: Moderate. Effect: HighMitigating measures: - A PSEA policy describes standard of behaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. - Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. - Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. - Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff. - Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver. - Design and implement appropriate programmes based on an impartial assessment of needs and risks, and an understanding of the vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting and feedback mechanisms.Provision of Healthcare for Vulnerable Groups and Equitable Access to Health Services: The health sector recognizes that seven years of ongoing crisis in Syria has impacted access to health of all the population on different levels.
