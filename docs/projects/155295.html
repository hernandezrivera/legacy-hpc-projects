Project Summary

Through the intervention, WFP will contribute to improving productivity, income and resilience capacity of the most vulnerable communities in disaster-affected areas. Conditional assistance will be introduced over time to support early recovery efforts, as well as helping restore or adapt basic infrastructure, which in some cases may also serve to reduce exposure to future shocks. Targeted communities will benefit from food or cash assistance for assets, from newly built or restored agricultural/rural productive assets to improve households’ resilience to shocks and ensure their food security.
Food Assistance for Assets activities contribute to creating, protecting or rehabilitating productive assets that improve local food and nutrition security, incomes, livelihoods opportunities, build resilience and adaptive capacity for vulnerable people including women and young people. It aims to create useful and sustainable assets so that communities are better prepared to handle future shocks and disasters that compromise their food security and livelihoods.

Needs

In recent years, food security in Haiti has deteriorated: the country’s Global Hunger Index score rose from 28 in 2009 to 34 in 2017, reaching the “extremely alarming” threshold according to the International Food Policy Research Institute. According to 2017 data from the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), 47 percent of Haitians are undernourished – one of the highest prevalence of undernutrition in the world. Among other factors, food insecurity is driven by the poor performance of the agriculture sector combined with the heavy dependence on food imports, which account for more than half of the food and 83 percent of the rice consumed. Consumer prices for major food products are between 30 and 77 percent higher than in the Latin American and Caribbean region, making them unaffordable for vulnerable populations. This disproportionately affects women, who have less capital (71 percent own neither land nor a home) and lower levels of formal education. High food prices are also fueled by logistics challenges, high production costs, inadequate market structures and food inflation in recent years. Haiti is one of the most exposed countries in the world to natural hazards that include hurricanes, floods, earthquakes, landslides, and droughts. From 1994-2013, Haiti was considered the third most affected country by extreme weather events in terms of lives lost and economic damages. More than 96 percent of the population is at risk of two or more hazards, and 56 percent of the country’s GDP is linked to areas exposed to risk from two or more hazards. The impact can be extensive. The 2010 earthquake caused over 200,000 deaths and damages and losses were equivalent to 120 percent of Haiti’s GDP , while Hurricane Matthew in 2016 caused damage equivalent to 32 percent of GDP. Hurricanes, droughts and other events also have significant impacts on food security; according to the latest IPC analysis (October 2018), 5% of the population in drought-affected areas (145,078 people) are currently in IPC phase 4 (emergency) and 25 (629,082 people) are in IPC phase 3 (crisis).

Activities or outputs

Haiti’s exposure to disasters combined with communities’ limited resilience force the poorest populations to depend on humanitarian assistance in the face of slow or rapid onset shocks and stresses from natural hazards or socio-economic instability. As such, WFP will seek to increase the self-reliance by developing sustainable community level disaster risk reduction solutions in support of rural development across Haiti’s fragile and degraded ecosystems. This activity will target the most food insecure, usually characterized by subsistence farmers and daily wage labourers. This activity will therefore focus on the most vulnerable rural households in areas with fragile ecosystems, taking into account sites of recurring disasters as well as the Haitian Vulnerability and Deprivation Index (HDVI) score used by the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labour. WFP will support beneficiaries with food assistance for assets, whereby women and men receive conditional cash transfers to meet their immediate food needs whilst boosting community assets that will improve their livelihoods by creating healthier natural environments, reducing risks and impact of shocks, increasing food productivity, and strengthening resilience to natural hazards. WFP aims to implement activities in irrigation systems and watershed management in order to build productive assets, but also to reduce flooding in the targeted areas. This will strengthen the resilience of the communities, participate in mitigating the effects of natural disasters and contribute to the recovery and response process for communities that are very vulnerable to hurricanes and other natural disasters. Organizational and technical trainings will be provided to especially dedicated maintenance committees or community groups, to ensure the sustainability of all the new or rehabilitated infrastructures assets. WFP will collaborate with UN agencies, international and local non-government organizations to ensure that beneficiaries receive an integrated package including technical assistance, non-food items, cash transfers and capacity strengthening. Specific attention will be given to women's participation in the activities to ensure that they equally benefit from the assets created and the cash distributed.
