Project Summary

The project will provide vocational trainings to 300 disadvantaged youth across four centres located between Ar Raqqa and Aleppo Governorates. As appropriate, the project will provide courses to groups outside of their traditional roles. For example, in 2021, three young women enrolled in mobile phone maintenance alongside their male counterparts in Raqqa. The project will also conduct labour market assessments and explore new vocations as needed. The Vocational courses will be delivered by technical specialists or practitioners in the relevant subject following the NGO’s standard curriculum. At the end of each month of training, the trainees will receive a stipend to cover transportation and other incidental costs.
Upon completion of the vocational training, participants have the opportunity to take part in an apprenticeship in a local business. The NGO’s livelihoods team will identify businesses who are willing to teach apprentices for one or two months. Vocational Training Assistants will follow the trainees during their transition from the centre to the apprenticeship providing supervision and collecting feedback. All the trainees will receive a stipend to cover transportation and other incidental costs.
The NGO will target 50 youth who have some vocational experience but require support to access the labour market. This apprenticeship component is designed to partner programme participants with potential employers and foster employment opportunities by improving their basic skills to succeed in the selected professions. The potential beneficiaries could be referrals from caregivers or recent young adults from the Education or Protection programme. The NGO will provide monthly stipends as the participants must attend full working days. Monitoring and supervision of this activity will be carried out in order to measure the apprentices’ progress in securing employment.
During the apprenticeships, participants interested in starting-up a new business will be asked to apply for a business grant that the NGO will grant to selected participants from the vocational training classes or from the standalone apprenticeship scheme. The application process consists of a basic presentation of the business plan, cost analysis, marketing and pricing strategy, detailed budget and forecast of expenditure and income. The applications will be evaluated based on specific criteria. The grant is designed to support the most promising and well-planned small businesses. The NGO will grant a maximum of 400 USD each to an estimated 150 applicants. The NGO’s livelihoods team will continue the follow up with the grant recipients, provide additional guidance and verify the adequate use of the grant.

3 - Needs

According to assessments carried out by the NGO many adolescents who have been out of school for a long period of time are looking for income generating opportunities. According to REACH’s March 2022 Labour market assessment for Raqqa City, lack of literacy (basic reading and writing skills) and absence of higher academic qualifications were also noted by a community leader KI as challenges that women faced in finding work. Without basic literacy or numeracy skills, the only option for finding work is in the informal sector, earning median estimated daily wage for unskilled labour of 8000 SYP (1.7 USD) (HSOS –September 2022). The deteriorating economic situation has also impacted the availability of vocational opportunities for youth. Since Raqqa city came under SDF control in October 2017, many markets have re-opened with a steady growth in the number of small businesses and a growing demand for technical skills, in particular in the construction and building maintenance sector. According to REACH’s March 2022 Labour market assessment for Raqqa City, vocational (60%) and computer/information and communication technology/ digital skills (44%) were reported as the main skills that workers would be most interested in learning. As a result, there is a strong need for linking vocational training activities with SMEs to enhance youth’s skills while supporting the local economy.

4 - Implementation of activities

This project will provide vocational trainings specifically designed to allow participants, including adolescents to acquire or reinforce skills to undertake technical vocations and to develop their business management skills, enabling them to start their own business, their home business or to find a stable employment in the labour market. This project will deliver micro-business management training which will help the trainee to create and monitor basic household/small business budgets. At the same time, participants have the opportunity to take part in an apprenticeship in a local business to practice their vocation skills and offer them exposure to the vocation.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The NGO will support home-based businesses tailored to the skills and capacities which the participant may already have and which the person can utilize at home especially for those with difficulty leaving the home due to childcare responsibilities (in the case of women and female-headed households) or disability. As with all the NGO's programming, this project will adhere to “do no harm” approach to ensure that any support provided will not put the participants at risk. Livelihoods staff will be trained on protection and identify protection risks and make appropriate referrals to the NGO’s protection programme.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The NGO's Code of Conduct and Associated policies are consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. Risks related to this project have been identified in a separate multi-sectoral strategy with appropriate mitigation measures identified. The latter includes, but is not limited to, the appointment and training of PSEA focal points in all project sites and offices and trained with supported from the Syria PSEA network. It also includes the use of the PSEA networks developed checklists for the observation of operations to identify, prevent and mitigate potential SEA and SEA risk factors in ongoing field activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: - Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence in Deir ez Zour with extension of attacks and activities to surrounding areas -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. - Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations - COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES - This project will be able to access beneﬁciaries, assess their speciﬁc needs and implement the programme accordingly - Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. • Flexibly shifting modalities as required between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods.
