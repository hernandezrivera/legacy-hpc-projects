Project Summary

Project proposal aims to improve safer and more dignified living conditions for 89915 beneficiaries (men 18369– women 20085 – boys 24775 – girls 26686) are suffering in 33 sites in Dana, and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts in Idleb with a set of integrated interventions in CCCM sector; supporting establishment of accountable, and competent management structures to promote the capacities of IDPs in camps to conduct care and maintenance for infrastructure and fire mitigation and response within these camps
To improve livelihood conditions of crisis-affected people, preserving their dignity, SCA is planning to fill gaps of basic needs from which 89915 beneficiaries (men 18369– women 20085 – boys 24775 – girls 26686) are suffering in 33 camps under Harim district.
Based on need assessment which SCA conducted, and coordination efforts with different stakeholders by data cross-checking of all implemented and planned response by humanitarian partners, IDPs sites showed the need for proper camp management structures since duration of displacement and live-stay in camp is longer than expected, with associated coordination of services, and fire mitigation and response, in addition to strengthening the IDPs capacities to conduct care and maintenance activities for camps facilities and themselves for better keeping for humanitarian assets and facilities.

4 - Needs

To understand the real needs for these camps' population and figuring out the uncovered gaps and problems they suffer from besides the root causes of these problems, SCA conducted a need assessment in 70 camps under Dana sub-district using many ways of collecting data in coordination with stakeholders (IDPs, local authorities and clusters), SCA conducted the following: - Rapid survey.- Conducting 13 FGDs that covered different age and sex categories of IDPs - Meeting with camp managers to specify current interventions by humanitarian partnersFollowing is the summary:- According to civilian defense there are no fire points near targeted camps in this proposal, a big difficulty to respond to fire inside these camps because of its nature and high density.- According to the initial information with camp managers and FGDs:- All targeted camps did not get any previous interventions in terms of fire, and there is no equipment or tools to fight fire.- The reasons of fire in camps maybe classified:1- related to beneficiary awareness towards fire and its causes, daily practices which is mostly categorized by neglect and lack of knowledge (use of primitive heaters, use of very dangerous flammable material for cooking and heating, messing around ash by children, use of candles for lighting without taking into consideration safety rules, storing flammable material inside the tent, smoking inside the tent, etc)2- Reasons related to camp structure which contribute enlarging fire when it occurs, these reasons include:Inappropriate design of camps in terms of distancing and high population density, Lack of consideration to fire separation lines during establishment, material used in tent establishment (very flammable sheets)

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to maintain safety of IDPs through the strengthening of fire prevention mitigation and response measures in 34 self-settled IDP sites in Harim district.Two interventions:1- Awareness raising, and training sessions provided on fire prevention, mitigation.2-Fire response resources provided and establishment of fire points/stands at IDP sites.Selection criteria through Cluster information and data base for sites with no fire systems.List of activities: 1. Establishment of fire units (fire points) and equipping them with necessary equipment and tools.2. Training IDPs to fire response, mitigation and raising their awareness along with first aids. average of trainees selection is one out of each 75 IDP in camp. 3. Distributing 19000 fire blankets for HHs in targeted camps, distribution will be door-to-door with average of 1 blanket each HH with providing instructions how to open and use these blankets for women particularly.4. Awareness raising for populations in camps especially for women especially in fire-related issues and ways prime protection, response and report along with training how to use fire blankets.5. Follow up with fire incidents reported in camps and evaluate the competency of equipment and Safety volunteers units and share information with CCCM Cluster .6. Follow up with fire incidents reported in camps and evaluate the competency of equipment and Safety volunteers units and share information with CCCM Cluster . Second output: Camp management structures: 1. establishment and support of camp managements. 2. coordination of servicesthird output: Care and maintenance activities of slight and routine maintenance for site facilities and infrastructure.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Due to the critical situation in the current allocation, and in a step forward to ensure integrity in provided service in targeted sites, and to mitigate risks related to implementation, delays or access issues, SCA has conducted wide-range analysis for expected risks for stakeholders to put strategies to manage them during design phase that will be implemented in practice and another one during project life-cyclesFollowing risks classified like following: • Access Risk: Prevention, obstructing or interfering in project activities by local authorities. Consequences and likelihoods: Medium Mitigation: to ensure save and continuous access to project implementation areas, SCA has informed and coordinated with local authorities about project activities on different levels starting with camp managers and ending with Humanitarian Affairs Minister at SSG to ensure no interference in project activities• Safety and security Risk: rapid updates and unexpected changes on the context and beneficiaries and staff are exposed to security threats. Consequences and likelihoods: medium due to de-escalation ceasefire currently in action and targeted areas are away from hot areas. Mitigations: close observation for security situation by safety and security teams and receiving periodical reports by field teams. Besides activating the role of security network report at SCA that ensures all targeted camps managers’ and other stakeholders are participating and finally planning for emergency evacuation for staff.• GBVAmong training and distribution sessions female trainees will be present. • UnderfundingIn case of underfunding, SCA will contact to cluster to refer to gaps and still fetch private funds and continue advocacy to needs

Since this project is based on an integrated approach between CCCM and FSL sectors, all proposed activities will be delivered in adherence to the principles of humanitarian neutrality and ensure equal access to all beneficiaries in need without discrimination. Practical measures will be implemented, such as training staff on protection core principles in addition to signing a Code of Conduct (CoC) and receiving training on PSEA measures. SCA will continue enhancing PSEA reporting channels and respond to the complaints related SEA in structured manner.The project will ensure that its staff mainstream protection principles in all components of the project which include ensuring that all beneficiaries have meaningful access to services prioritizing the safety and dignity of beneficiaries, ensuring non-discrimination and 'Do No Harm' in the delivery of assistance and seeking the meaningful engagement of the stakeholders and ensure that confidential reporting mechanisms are in place to address any potential abuse or conflict during project implementation.- The project was designed based on real need assessment conducted by SCA, all different groups aggregated by age and sex participated including women, men, children, the elderly and the disabled through FGDs conducted.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: COVID-19 spread in the area.Procedures:-All COVID-19 Prevention and Protection Recommendations will be applied according to COVID-19 Protection Guidelines during trainings, raise awareness sessions which include good planning for the training in terms of proper number of trainees in training location, keeping social distancing among beneficiaries, maintain personal protection means to trainers and trainees (masks – alcohol-based hand rubs).-During Firefighting Blanket Distribution: Blankets will be distributed through door-to-door mechanism, so that beneficiaries will not be crowded in distribution points, distribution staff will be provided by protective materials besides alcohol-based hand rubs to be used by staff and beneficiaries after pens exchange and distribution documents, during the distribution, distribution staff will explain the way of opening and using the blanket especially by women.Assumption: Availability of all necessary materials for firefighting points in the Syrian market except for firefighting blankets which will be purchased from Turkey and transported cross borders, this may delay distribution process because blankets should be distributed at the beginning of winter season.Procedures: -SCA indeed conducted market assessment that showed the availability of these materials in the Turkish market, its production sources, prices and time duration needed to supply.-As soon as the proposal and budget is approved (before signing the project agreement between SCA and SCHF) SCA will start procurement procedures immediately.
