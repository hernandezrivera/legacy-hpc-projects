Project Summary

3,600 children (1,764 boys and 1,836 girls U5 admitted for SAM treatment)
25000 people (M/W) sensitized on IYCF in emergency
Activities or outputs
• Caregivers of children aged 0-23 months are supported to adopt recommended infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices, including both breastfeeding and complementary feeding
• All children aged under five years in affected areas are screened regularly for the early detection of severe wasting and other forms of life‑threatening acute malnutrition and are referred as appropriate for treatment services.
• All children aged under five years suffering from severe wasting and other forms of life-threatening acute malnutrition in affected areas benefit from facility and community-based services (Fix or mobile) that provide effective treatment.

1 - Besoins / Needs

For all CAR refugees, the latest surveys available showed a global acute malnutrition rate (GAM) of 8.9% (Moderate acute malnutrition predominates and accounts for 7.6% of the sample, while severe acute malnutrition is at 1.2%).However, the observed rates of global acute malnutrition vary between regions and refugee status. Generally, they are higher among Old refugees than in new refugees.These rates are as follows:● GAM 10-14%, alarming situation: East and Adamawa regions, old refugees● GAM 6-9%, less alarming situation: East, Adamawa and North regions, new refugees.Regarding severe acute malnutrition, the results by region show that the prevalence of severe acute malnutrition is less than 2% in all regions, except in the Adamawa region (old refugees), where the rate is equal to 2%. In the East region (old refugees) and in the North, the rates are close to 2%For the Central African and Nigerian refugees, the main needs identified are to strengthen the analysis of the nutritional situation of the populations and their access to community nutritional care services (outpatient and hospitalization) while ensuring the strengthening of supply chains. Also, prevention aspects must be taken into account with the strengthening of good dietary practices and infant and young child feeding in emergency situations.The nutritional response, with the participation of various humanitarian and development actors, will be carried out in the following areas:- Strengthening of the nutritional surveillance system and food security through surveys;- Improve the management of severe acute malnutrition with or without complications in the Inpatient and Outpatient treatment facilities;- Promotion of good infant and young child feeding practices (IYCF)
