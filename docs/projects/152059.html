Project Summary

LPF will seek to address the WASH needs of displaced populations in southern Borno state through the provision of essential WASH support. This intervention will protect IDPs, Host Communities and returnees from water, sanitation and hygiene related diseases by ensuring that they have access to sufficient safe water and they interrupt the transmission of diseases through improved hygiene and sanitation infrastructure and practices. LPF will construct, rehabilitate and provide Operation and Maintenance support for water supply and sanitation infrastructures. To Improve access to safe drinking water and encourage good hygiene practices amongst returnees, IDPs and host communities in southern borno

Needs

Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) has been a paramount challenge in Borno prior to the emergence of the insurgency in 2009. As a result of over population in IDPs host communities with limited water infrastructures, camps and host communities alike have been strained with limited options to cope with water shortage. In 2018, more than 240,000 people arrived from hard-to-reach areas in camps and informal settlements in 43 LGAs across Borno and Adamawa states, further overcrowding existing WASH facilities in these sites. Furthermore the open defecation occurring in host communities and the lack of waste management are both sources of contamination to the surface water like the streams. Unfortunately some of the boreholes are poorly located owing to inadequate Ground Water Testing, only to fail in a short time after installation. As a result many IDPs are forced to rely on the water source from Streams. These polluted streams are in most cases far away thus exposing mostly women and girls to sexual abuses, harassment and abduction. Formal, informal camps and host communities stand at the risk of a WASH related diseases outbreak due to lack of access to safe drinking water and poor sanitation and hygiene practices especially during the raining season.In the return areas of Borno state, returnees have found up to 75% of the water supply infrastructure that existed prior to the insurgency damaged or destroyed. In 73 IDP camps across 11 LGAs in Borno State, the average allocation of water is 14 liters per person per day, below minimum standard of 15 liters, while IPDs are sharing a drop-hole latrine with an average of 72 people, below minimum standard of 50 people per drop-hole latrine.
