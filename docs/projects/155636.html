Project Summary

The objective/outcome of this project is “population (especially vulnerable groups) in the intervention areas are more resilient to environmental, social and economic shocks” in the Northwest and Southwest regions through the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus.
Output 1: IDPs and host communities are economically empowered
Activity 1.1 Provide temporary jobs and/or short vocational training
Activity 1.2 Facilitate income generating activities and access to land
Output 2: Confidence between communities and the government and among communities is built
Activity 2.1 Facilitate contacts/relationships between communities and the government
Activity 2.2 Rehabilitate small/social infrastructure
Activity 2.3 Support social cohesion initiatives
Activity 2.4 Support media to promote peace culture
Activity 2.5 Facilitate advocacy to protect the right of IDPs renting accommodation
Activity 2.6 Address security and safety concerns
Output 3: The capacities of key stakeholders (e.g. the government and CSOs) are strengthened
Activity 3.1 Support authorities to coordinate emergency and stabilisation responses
Activity 3.2 Provide training on core capacities to implement humanitarian actions for CSOs
Activity 3.3 Empower women to play a more prominent role in non-violent conflict resolution
Activity 3.4 Support authorities for good governance (esp. issuing ID cards and birth certificates)
Output 4: IDPs are safely returned and re-start their life (scenario of return)
Activity 4.1 Reconstruct/rehabilitate houses and fields
Activity 4.2 Support re-starting of agriculture/fishing activities
Activity 4.3 Support authorities for recovery
Main target: youth (18-34 years old) and women of IDPs and host communities, the authorities, CSOs, religious/community leaders

Besoins / Needs

The crisis of the Northwest and Southwest regions (NWSW) has deteriorated the security situation and affected the NWSW as well as neighbouring areas of the NWSW such as the Littoral and West regions and more than 530,000 Cameroonian have become internally displaced persons (IDPs) in the regions. Unemployment of people in the NWSW regions and IDPs in the four regions is alarming and social cohesion and the mutual confidence in the NWSW have been negatively influenced by the conflicts between communities and the government and among communities. A national dialogue was held from 30th September to 4th October 2019 but the crisis has continued. For instance, between 9 and 15 December 2019, estimated 5,475 people (782 households) were forced to flee their villages following a series of military operations and community clashes throughout the NW. Many IDPs in the Littoral and West regions rent houses. Basic services (e.g. water, health, education) are available but they need to pay for them. IDPs and vulnerable people, in particular in host communities need revenues to keep their life with basic services. Social cohesion and mutual confidence need to be improved. Women are more willing to participate in dialogue and focued groups than men who are scared by security forces and activists/separatists. The government, CSOs and media need to strengthen their capacity to respond to the crisis with promoting peace culture.
