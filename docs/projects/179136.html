4 - Needs

The war in Syria continues to negatively affect livelihoods opportunities for individuals and communities in Northwest Syria. Idleb and the surrounding areas hold nearly three million people, with almost half internally displaced. As per the HNO 2021 report, the 10 year old Syrian crisis, have continued to erode the socio-economic foundations of the Syrian people, in parallel to the fallout from the Lebanese financial crisis, the rapid depreciation of the Syrian currency, dramatic inflation, and the entry into effect of new trade restrictions in June 2020. Moreover, the global COVID-19 pandemic has led to additionalstress on social and basic services while also depriving Syrian people of their jobs and livelihoods. The pandemic has been affecting the entire socio-economic system and has led to a dramatic loss of livelihoods across the country. At the end of 2019, around 86 per cent of Syrians lived below the poverty line, and estimated just under 90 per cent as of mid-August 2020.288 A distinct category has emerged,the working poor, households unable to meet the cost of basic needs despite one or more member of the family are working. As per the UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment, before the pandemic, 41.9 per cent of households could cover their expenses, while currently, only9.7 per cent can do so. This is exacerbated by a severe lack of opportunities to attain employable skills and working capital. About two-thirds of households lost one or more income sources, and about half lost between 75 per cent to 100 per cent of their previous income.

5 - Implementation of activities

1) Labor intensive cash for work (CfW) projects aimed at improving community infrastructure while providing temporary employment opportunities for unemployed or under employed men and women ages 18 60 including PwD. 2) Market relevant skills training programs through two Livelihoods Center in Idleb governorate; the centers have one training space for women and girls, and one for men and boys. Activities will include business and life skills training and vocational training followed by an apprenticeship placement targeting males and females ages 17 35 years old. Specific sectors will depend on the findings of the labour market assessment, and projects for women will be based on the previously mentioned results from the FGDs to attempt to diversify vocational training projects targeting women. 3) Microenterprise grants and entrepreneurship training to both startups and existing businesses in the community; the IRC will provide business training and small scale cash grants to individuals who have developed a business plan. The project will provide specialized support women to start small home based projects, and will encourage youth (16 30) to gain practical work experience and start low risk projects that require little start up capital. All activities will adhere to the adaptive preventive measures for COVID 19The IRC will target at least 15% PWDs and through the partnership with Humanity and Inclusion, the IRC will ensure to address PWDs access challenges including adapting project tools, building of ramps, select accessible project locations, etc..

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

One risk is that delayed payments negatively affect house-hold dynamics, especially for women who are pressured into dropping out of projects if stipends are not paid on time. The IRC has identified multiple payment methods for livelihoods projects to mitigate this risk, as well as multiple money transfer agencies to ensure on-time payments. Another risk, also outlined in the Protection Risk Analysis (PRA), is the perception of biased beneficiary targeting and selection. IRC has found this individuals especially believe either host, or IDPs, are favored during humanitarian activities. During project design and planning, IRC considers do no harm principals and conflict sensitivity concerns, including a solid understanding of the number of IDPs in a targeted location, how long they have been there for, and the level of integration they have in the community and markets. IRC does not target activities solely for host or IDPs, but instead included and encourages both host and IDPs in every project. IRC delivers cash in its offices and considers multiple installments to ensure beneficiaries' safety when delivering the cash payments.The IRC has identified the risk of beneficiary exploitation that can include sexual exploitation by local authorities or staff. The IRC has conducted PSEA trainings for all staff, including field staff. Confidential and trust worthy reporting mechanisms have been shared with all staff, and both staff and beneficiaries are encouraged to use the IRC’s beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism. Another risk identified is an escalation of the security situation. IRC’s temporary income schemes through cash for work, also addresses community-level priorities.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks to project implementation include loss of access to project areas due to conflict (inter armed groups or due to Government of Syria offensives). As mitigation, IRC conducts pre-project security assessments that includes risk of future offensives. IRC will have ‘exit plans’ for all locations and planned measures to implement to ensure staff safety and duty of care, building on lessons learned from the South Syria offensive in summer 2018. Another high risk is interference by armed groups or local councils, who may attempt to control or influence projects by requesting or demanding IRC to take certain actions (aid diversion). IRC adheres to humanitarian principles and the established Joint Operating Procedures for northwest Syria. IRCs well-resourced humanitarian access team will conduct principled negotiation and conflict resolution in order to continue project plans and meet the identified needs of a community. There is a risk of local authorities or armed groups targeting IRC beneficiaries for taxation or exploitation. IRC holds open registration for most ER Cluster activities, and when this is not possible, does household-level verification of lists that are provided to IRC. This reduces the power that local councils or others have on applicants. finally, the IRC will adhere to the adaptive measures of COVID 19 to mitigate the risk of the spread of the disease, maintaining social distancing, wear of masks, sterilization of used facilities during project implementation and maintaining smaller groups during trainings and activity implementation. As IRC has a strong coordination with Humanity and Inclusion to support mitigate PWDs access challenges and help in adapting tools, building of ramps, etc...
