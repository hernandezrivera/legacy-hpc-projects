Project Summary

The conflict has widely disrupted markets and livelihoods, with negative consequences worsening year after year as the conflict ensues. Typical livelihood strategies – in particular agricultural systems – have been eroded or completely destroyed. Many families have been left without traditionally male breadwinners, leaving female heads of household to enter the labor market – in many cases for the first time, with lack of access to education, training and experience, and conservative social norms limiting opportunities. The main changes at HH level identified by participants were an increase in HH members contributing to HH income, including negative coping strategies engaged in by women and children. PIN will support communities in Northern Syria to enhance market systems and empower vulnerable, conflict-affected households to launch viable income-generating activities to meet their basic needs through activities including Cash for Work (CfW), vocational training, apprenticeships, and business grants targeting at-risk households, including those resorting to negative coping strategies. Cash for work approaches empower beneficiaries to work for the cash assistance received, promoting resilience and dignity while supporting communities and stimulating local markets. PIN favors high-value CfW activities that support broader communities by providing services, infrastructure, and/or production of products for sale or donation. PIN’s cash for work approach covers the most vulnerable HH for a minimum of three months, supporting them to meet their basic needs while minimizing resort to negative coping strategies. Initiatives supported will include public infrastructure rehabilitation and activities explicitly intended to support women, including education-oriented, knitting and sewing activities.
Furthermore, PIN will implement integrated Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET), Small Business Grants (SBGs) and Apprenticeship placements with local businesses. Target populations will include vulnerable male and female heads of household within host, IDP and returnee communities. PIN aims to provide the most needed assistance to such groups by employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet distinct needs, establish beneficiary selection criteria to prioritize the inclusion of those most vulnerable and adapt its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programmes are carried out in coordination with local authorities at various levels – from cross-border to hub, to district and village level. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at the planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation stages.

4 - Needs

Recent PIN’s assessments across Northern Syria highlighted that works for infrastructurerepairs remain a significant priority across all assessed communities, with all groupsassessed likewise ranking Cash for Work (CfW) in their top 3 priority assistancemodalities. In NES, during repeated meetings with LCs, municipalities and HAO, needs forminor rehabilitation of physical infrastructure including roads, sidewalks rehabilitationand lightening of streets were frequently mentioned. Women’s committees repeatedlystressed the necessity and usefulness of sewing and knitting activities for females as asocially-acceptably pathway into income generation. Recent Endline assessments fromCfW activities in NWS highlighted an 88% decrease of beneficiaries in the top 25% ofsevere negative coping strategies and a 27% increase in the number of beneficiaries withacceptable food consumption scores at the Endline phase compared to baseline. PIN’sassessments from 2018, demonstrated that the main employment barriers were lack ofemployment opportunities, lack of skills, low wages for unskilled labor and highcompetition amongst unskilled laborers. Due to the destruction of both livelihoodsthemselves, as well as vocational training opportunities contingent upon large-scaleemployers, formal education services or factories, respondents saw little chances ofacquiring well paid, skilled jobs. Women are facing additional challenges to participate inthe job market because of household obligations and child-care. This problem isparticularly severe in Raqqa, where there are high rates of widowed/divorced women, andFHH depend on support from the community or child labor to be able to survive.

5 - Implementation of activities

PIN aims to Strengthen access to livelihood by creating income-generating opportunities and improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economic recovery. The following activities are foreseen: Cash for Work (CfW), Delivery of Technical Vocational Education and Training (TVET), Apprecentships, Small Business Grants (SBGs). CfW targets the most vulnerable through cash for work activities based on a combination of eligibility criteria and at least one vulnerability criteria. PIN will strive to ensure inclusion of PwDs. The workers will be employed during 12 weeks (approx. 60 days), 5 days/week in line with local labor market trends as reported by PIN surveys or data from REACH or information provided by the Cluster respectively. PIN will provide temporary support for operational costs and/or staff incentives, as well as support the institutions/NGOs with the necessary training equipment. The training cycles will last for 3 months (up to 12 weeks), with the beneficiaries attending 4 hours a day, five days a week; an equivalent of a part-time job, allowing beneficiaries to maintain other income-generating activities and domestic or child care responsibilities. The most successful TVET graduates will be selected for SBGs. PIN will prioritize the SGBs that thematically match the vocational training provided. A call for submission of business proposals will be announced and the best one selected by the Evaluation Committee. The apprenticeship component is designed to partner workers with potential employers and foster opportunities. At the end of the mentoring cycle, apprentices who successfully finish their apprenticeship will receive a start-up kit.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

As per PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy for 2019-2021, PIN carries out regularprotection risk assessments, focusing on all assistance modalities. These protection riskassessments feed into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. Thefollowing major risks and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified: 1)R: Mostvulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1)MM: targeting criteria communicated withthe communities and LCs; advertisement in targeted public places; use of Washingtongroups Questions and advocacy for inclusion of PwDs by the local councils.;100%verifications of the beneficiaries; FGDs with representatives from a cross-section ofcommunity, wide advertisements and communication channels for PIN’s CFRM, 2)R:intergroupstensions over assistance; 2)MM:Community stakeholder meetings, harmonizationof the packages across partners, Provision of assistance to host communities andIDPs.3)R:PSEA 3)MM: Training of all staff and trainers; Gender balanced teams, Clearprocess to swiftly address PSEA case, Signs are posted throughout project sites statingthat assistance is free. 4)R: Project sites are not accessible/safe, 4)MM: distribution ofprotective equipment for CfW, collaboration with Security and Access teams for timingand place of CfW activities, Accessibility adjustments of TVET centers to ensure accessof PwDs.
