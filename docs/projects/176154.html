Project Summary

African Development Aid Interventions in this component are designed to increase demand for health services by educating individuals, households, and community and local leaders so that they can play their roles more effectively. The Project employs expanded health mobilization strategies that promote awareness, information, and advocacy to promote behavior change, adoption of healthy norms and practices and structural orientations, including supporting appropriate policies.
African Development Aid will collaborate with the ministry of health to sensitize PHCU, CHWs and WASH Committee members on Community Lead Total Sanitation and cascade to the household level. The Project will continue to promote access to improved water sources, sanitation and hygiene at community, schools and households. The Project will continue collaborating with other stakeholders to promote WASH activities and advocacy to strengthen the linkages and working collaborations.
The project will also build the capacity of WASH Committee members to develop community action plans on CLTS and for sub-committees comprised of the women, PWD, Youths, men and the elderly to monitor implementation. These structures will use community dialogue days and community outreaches to reach out to community members.
Through strategic partnerships, we will continue to capitalize on existing opportunities to make a lasting contribution to improved health outcomes with full community ownership focusing on: building local skills and supporting systems for sustainability; improving health service demand, access, and quality, reducing barriers for most vulnerable groups, and leveraging social determinants of health to improve health outcomes. The Project will continue this relationship with the states, counties and Payams but engage even much more with each of them in a dialogue that ensures that project resources will achieve the highest level of efficiency and effectiveness in contributing towards agreed goals and objectives.
A lean local staffing structure will be maintained to provide the skills needed for effective technical assistance. Senior managers will ensure that work plans are implemented in accordance with WASH Cluster guidance and with stringent adherence to time tables and budget ceilings. The location that characterizes ADA WASH with a target of 28,000 beneficiaries will be maintained and its contributions, with a focus on continuity of critical services rather than on expansion and scale up.

1 - Needs

The 2021 WASH needs in South Sudan has been greatly influenced by limited WASH infrastructure, heavy floods of 2020, a population affected by multiple displacements. Incidence of Covid-19 pandemic, less-than-adequate knowledge and good WASH practices in communities, exacerbated by a limping economy amidst intermittent inter-communal clashes increases vulnerability of the most affected populations. The 2021 HNO for South Sudan indicate that an estimated 5.6 million people (about 50% of the population) are negatively impacted by lack of adequate access to clean water. People returning to their areas of origin and urban areas are projected to be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices due to demand on already limited water and sanitation infrastructure. This is expected to increase in 2021. In addition, 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers lack access to sufficient and quality WASH services. As the access to water sources is predominately communal in South Sudan, the limited access to WASH services impacts all population groups. Those in need are not restricted to one specific group. IDPs, non-displaced and host community members, returnees from internal displacement and refugee returnees are vulnerable with limited access to WASH services. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. In addition, poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continues to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. South Sudan WASH Cluster HRP 2021 guide document on Severity Mapping of Populations in need indicate that ADA’s location of WASH intervention in Jonglei State (Ayod and Duk Counties) have populations in dire state of need for WASH services. Ayod is in severity level 4, while Duk is in severity level 5 (highest). Only between 21% to 40% of people in Duk have access to quality water. Amongst the 78 counties of South Sudan with lowest access to sanitation, both Duk (not more than 30%) and Ayod (20% to 30%) fall in needs bracket. Amongst the counties with the lowest handwashing levels, Duk (less than 30%) and Ayod (21% to 30%) are included. Access to WASH NFIs is still dismal in Ayod and Duk Counties (21% to 30%). With the arrival of COVID-19 and with no vaccine roll-out in the near future, the virus may also increase the demand for WASH infrastructure in both urban and rural areas. Poor access to water may drive people to collect water from potentially contaminated sources. Limited or no sanitation infrastructure will increase the need to construct new latrines and place an increasing demand on the frequency of faecal waste management of existing sanitation structures. Without adequate WASH infrastructure, the potential presence of water or vector-borne diseases, as well as the ability to contain them, may impact the well-being of the local people at risk of diseases. Against the foregoing litany of WASH needs, ADA would like to provide feasible, community-centred, but practical WASH interventions with high community impact in both Ayod and Duk Counties. These interventions will be guided by periodical technical guidance and priorities provided by the South Sudan WASH Cluster during 2021.

2 - Project activities

• Strengthened Infrastructure Maintenance: The Project will map out Water sources, Public Latrines and Boreholes in the community, assess the condition/status including vulnerability inclusion. Major disability inclusion renovations and Construction will be undertaken. Repair boreholes with rusted pipes; clarification of wells and other water points; Each Household or clan will be encouraged to construct a simple sanitary structure with a model Integrated design disseminated by ADA. Community and Public Institutions Health Promotion programs; establish water hygiene and sanitation clubs in the communities to continuously, encourage clean water supply points and storage facilities. The Project will supply tanks to Public facilities and schools for water harvesting techniques. • COVID-19 Adherence; Each program area will conduct community awareness on the preventive and management of COVID-19. • Stakeholder mobilization meetings: ADA through the coordination of the MOH and GoSS structures organize for a consultative and partnership meeting to share its WASH findings, achievements, challenges and best practices. • Recruitment and training of hygiene promoters: ADA will recruit hygiene promoters through consultation with the local authorities. • Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM): ADA with support from other static partners will conduct both mini (at end of each distribution) and comprehensive (after 1-2months of distribution). • Water quality monitoring at the household level: Water quality testing at the household level will be carried out by ADA WASH team to ensure that FRC (free residual chlorine) levels are still sufficient in the households that have benefitted from the distribution of household water treatment and safe storage products.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA is a universal humanitarian requirement that the project has internally adopted to protect its beneficiaries and stakeholders. Each community activity will be integrated with PSEA to ensure each beneficiary is sensitized on PSEA guidelines. All ADA project staff and volunteers will be provided with a code of conduct and enlightened on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse. In addition, mass awareness will be conducted to the beneficiaries and the community at large on PSEA and other protection concerns. ADA will provide a toll-free number for beneficiaries to report any cases of PSEA. The project will institutionalize PSEA minimum operating standards, and strengthen linkages to IASC thematic areas of intervention. Besides, the project will strengthen accountability to affected people (AAP) by ensuring its employees are trained and signs the PSEA code of conduct, and apply accountability and inclusion standards, taking into account gender, persons with disabilities, and mental health and psycho-social support. ADA shall establish coordination and support functions for PSEA and sexual harassment with field-level protection and child-protection sub-clusters and implementing partners. The project aims at conducting an awareness-raising and sensitization on the forms of sexual abuse and exploitation to community project committees, government authorities and opinion leaders in the affected communities. The project will address stigma and cultural barriers hampering reporting of PSE or SH cases through awareness-raising and also provide information on who to report to and how to report preparators. ADA will map out PSEA partners and establish safe referral pathways to services providers. Finally, ADA will ensure a zero-tolerance policy with regard to sexual exploitation and abuse throughout its projects as being a member of the protection and child-protection sub-clusters. It is considered serious misconduct and can have severe consequences, including staff dismissal and taking legal steps against preparators. Any suspicion of sexual exploitation or abuse of beneficiary or female employee will be reported immediately to the relevant authorities for action

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

ADA will prioritize delivering information to the beneficiaries using but not limited to, their most preferred channels of communication. This will include use of megaphones, help desks, radio calls, use of the local authorities or through public announcements. Based on whichever mode will be preferred, ADA will ensure to adhere to COVID 19 precautions. The field-based staff will be reaching out to the beneficiaries who are unable to reach the ADA offices or complaint desks as part of their daily outreach activities. This will help those with disabilities and others who cannot easily access the feedback and complaint desk to express their feedback on the services provided by ADA in the community. The outreach staff will fill for them and what they recommend to be done to at least satisfy their basic needs. A good practice that ADA has used in the past is to ensure that people with disabilities, different vulnerabilities, girls, elderly etc. are represented in the monitoring teams and specifically gather info from their own category. Special desks, lines, suggestion boxes that suit their needs are provided. Oftentimes, special people who can translate or have special ways of rapport with the various categories are identified to be representatives in a feedback loop on project matters (including complaints). These methods will be employed to ensure the project feedback mechanism is representative and inclusive of the various categories of beneficiaries.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through community entry meetings; Conduct stakeholders’ entry and introduction meetings. The project will use the existing WASH Committees as the voice for the beneficiaries and the Key HH members. Beneficiaries open forums will be avenues for information sharing and feedback, PDM to collect data and impact of the project. From the project inception period, ADA will consult the communities on the most preferred channels of communication to them. Information about the project will be communicated early and on a regular basis to ensure every community member (regardless of status, disability, age or gender) has received the correct information. ADA will establish a project implementation team that will comprise of stakeholder’s representatives, who will work with the project team to take deliberate steps to make sure the voices of the vulnerable groups are recorded and their needs are met. ADA project team will incorporate regular public community meetings, regular consultations; focused group discussions, community committees, micro feedback surveys. ADA has a policy on, and extensively uses feedback mechanisms in project implementation. This provides for a complete loop of communication between the field, programs and management. In essence, ADA employs four key steps in providing beneficiary feedbacks: design, implement, collect data and analyse; provide feedback through an agreed feedback loop. In this project: 1. ADA will use available procedures in previous projects to adapt to the feedback needs of the current project, by involving beneficiaries on how best they would like their feedbacks to be recorded, analysed, and handled in a manner that is satisfactory to them. This will be area-specific and handled with utmost confidentiality and practicality. The modalities used by ADA include suggestion boxes, phone calls, use of SMS, face-to-face meetings, focus group discussions, project implementation committee appointees, complaints and complements forms, and questionnaires. Beneficiaries make their choice and this informs the course of action. 2. At the implementation level, ADA will agree with the beneficiaries on how to participate in collecting/gather complaints and complements realistically and in a timely, realistic manner, meeting the needs of beneficiaries in specific locations (Ayod, Fangak, and Nasir) 3. ADA will agree with beneficiaries on how data collected (complaints and compliments) will be analysed, by whom and at what frequency to provide needed feedback. This stage includes concrete action points on matters raised and possible redress or way forward 4. ADA will agree in advance on how the actions generated can be shared internally or externally to create the needed change or course of action that was desired by beneficiaries. At this stage, the communities are informed of necessary changes, by when and how. ADA will use the beneficiary feedback to demonstrate its respect and promotion of human rights, foster good cooperation with affected communities, empower rights-holders, build trust and stronger relationships with project stakeholders. Also, the beneficiaries’ feedback shall help to strengthen project ownership amongst partners, improving the chance of long-term impact. The action points will provide feedback on what is working well and not so well, hence, strengthening the organizational understanding of the context in which the project operates, including identifying unintended consequences that are impacting negatively on some stakeholders, allowing for remedial action to be taken so that these do not become a risk to the project’s success. Positive feedback motivates staffs to put more efforts to assisting the affected communities, and also help to pick up on issues such as fraud and misconduct in beneficiary communities. Finally, feedback gives empowerment and voice –capacity to challenge and hold ADA and other decision makers to account for their actions against the affected communities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback is considered genuine and will be evaluated before implementation. The project will develop a structured questionnaire that will be done together with the current standard tool to capture fundamental innovations that will change the direction of the program. Routine data collection and analysis (face to face, help desk, key informant interviews, PDM questions, random household questions) will be done to evaluate the level of satisfaction from the beneficiaries. The analysis will be shared with the stakeholders and the management and incorporated in the program design.
