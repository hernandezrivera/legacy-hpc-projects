Project Summary

GOALPrime Organization Nigeria (GPON) targets to reach a total number of 47,000 people; out of which 600 (80% girls & 20% boys) who are survivors of sexual assault will be reached with psychosocial support services. 1500 people will be reached through house-to-house sensitization while 44,100 people (60% women/girls and 40% men/boys) will be reached with motorized GBV/COVID-19 risk communication with messages on the prevention and response of gender-based violence (GBV) in camps and communities of Kala/Balge, Ngala, MMC, and Jere LGAs.10,000 Information Education and Communication-IEC Materials will be printed and distributed to beneficiaries who will be reached with GBV sensitization as well as posted in all locations of engagement strategically. The IEC materials will be developed in Hausa, Kanuri, and English languages in collaboration with the GBV subsector, the Ministry of Women Affairs and Social Development (MWSAD), and Translators without Boarder. 10% of the total number of beneficiaries to be reached is targeted at people living with disabilities.
GOALPrime in collaboration with the Ministry of Women Affairs and social development (MWSAD) will develop prevention and response jingles/messages on gender-based violence which will be aired consistently on the Radio Station with coverage reaching Kala/Balge, Ngala, MMC, and Jere LGAs. GOALPrime will also set up GBV child-friendly reporting mechanisms and zero GBV clubs in schools and camps of its location of implementation, as well as provide phone booths services for case management support for survivors especially for cases of emergencies.
GOALPrime will carry out capacity building quarterly for 50 women and girls in each location of its engagement throughout the period of the project implementation, empowering them as a result to be the first respondents in their communities and camps, also GPON will work with the women leaders in these locations of engagements to create a “No GBV Women’s Network” in each location of engagement. GPON will also conduct capacity building and strengthening for GBV workers on proper case management in their field level.
Through Coordination with partners in the project locations, GOALPrime will identify livelihood actors for linkage of Survival of Sexual and Gender-Based Violence (SGBV) for livelihood support. Through this project, GOALPrime targets to identify 400 girls (SGBVs) with livelihood challenges and provide linkage to access livelihood support from livelihood partners across the project location.
GOALPrime seeks to strongly strengthen GBV referral pathways in each location. Through its ongoing GBV project in some of these locations, GPON has seen the need to strengthen actors' coordination and seeks to support quarterly coordination amongst actors in each of the locations. GOALPrime will be fully operational in all LGA throughout the implementation of this project. And continues assessment of camp and community response on the cases of GBV to inform subsequent project design and planning.

1 - Needs

An estimated 3 million people across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states need interventions for the prevention of and response to gender-based violence (GBV). The underlying drivers of the conflict and conditions of displacement continue to exacerbate the occurrence of GBV incidents. Half of this population in need consists of children (below 18 years) with the majority being adolescent girls. Sexual violence - including rape, sexual exploitation, forced and early marriage, survival sex and domestic violence, physical assault, denial of resources, and psychological and emotional abuse - are among the most frequently reported violations, recurrently perpetrated by intimate or former partners. Minors, particularly adolescent girls, are also at risk of GBV, which occur mainly in the context of child sexual abuse and early marriage. Also, an estimated 2.7 million children and adolescents (1.5 million girls and 1.2 million boys) in north-east Nigeria are in need of protection and cross-sectoral services. Threats and risks to the well-being of girls and boys include family separation, gender-based violence including child marriage and conflict-related sexual violence, recruitment and use of children, abduction and other grave child rights violations. At least 6,000 children (more than 50 per cent girls) are estimated to have been recruited, abducted, killed or victims of violent attacks by armed groups since the beginning of the conflict. Of those, approximatively 50 per cent are survivors of SGBV. The crisis also has caused severe psychological distress among children. The conflict has not only disrupted the social fabric but has also negatively affected the ability of parents and communities to appropriately care for their children.Moreover, the COVID-19 situation has more severe and sustained impact on people who experience multiple and intersecting discriminations, especially adolescent girls, women and girls with disabilities. The epidemic exacerbates sexual abuse, child marriages, denial of opportunities & resources, intimate partner violence, domestic violence within the household, and communities. As of 29th November 2020, a total of 1,123 confirmed cases have been recorded including 63 deaths, 26 active cases and 1,034 recoveries in the BAY states.The Humanitarian Response Plan for 2021 shows the critical Gender-Based Violence (GBV) needs in Kala-Balge, Ngala, MMC and JERE LGAs, with a PiN estimate of 30,198, 39,479, 156,665 and 113,939 respectively. This critical gender-based violence needs have also been confirmed by GOALPrime Organization Nigeria (GPON) through her activities of engagements in these LGAs and feedbacks from the community’s members, communities’ stakeholders and camp stakeholders. Hence, the need to build the resilience of survivors of Gender Based Violence by strengthening the prevention and response services through community sensitization, capacity building, PSS and referrals for specialized services in Kala/Balge, Ngala, MMC and Jere LGAs of Borno state.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

GOALPrime’s accountability to the affected populations spans through the Program Cycle from the Rapid Specific Vulnerability Assessment through to design, through to implementation, and through to monitoring. The proposed project prioritizes constant creating of opportunities for review and feedback from beneficiaries, community leaders, partners present in location, and donors. This integration of feedback and input from communities closely aligns with both the SPHERE Standards; Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Minimum Standard, and guidelines set forth by the InterAgency Standing Committee (IASC). The pre-design assessment on the feasibility of this project in target locations collected information from local beneficiary populations, IDP camp coordinators, host community Bulama, and a review of what other partners are doing in target LGAs via the WASH sector ReportHub partners update from target LGAs. As a result, the proposed project uses direct contributions in its proposed structure, scope, and proposed activities to allow a more community informed implementation process. Throughout the implementation of this project, GOALPrime shall engage participatory Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) and establish a platform that will allow participation and feedback from the community on project goals. GOALPrime shall conduct regular feedback surveys among the beneficiaries to gauge levels of satisfaction and solicit suggestions for project implementation and improvement.
