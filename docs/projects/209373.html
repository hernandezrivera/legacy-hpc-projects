Project Summary

Trafficking in human beings is a grave violation of fundamental rights, which causes great suffering and long-lasting harm to the victims.
Recent studies and surveys have shown that human trafficking is known to significantly amplify in times of war, crisis and conflict. A clear link is also established between human trafficking and emergency contexts with a critical gap in the humanitarian response system.
Evidence collected in the past decade illustrates that there are a variety of factors that intersect to enhance the risks of being targeted and recruited by traffickers. Armed conflicts, displacements, economic recessions, health issues, humanitarian and other crises notably contribute to increasing the vulnerability of individuals to trafficking, especially where these are connected to the erosion of the rule of law or a sharp increase in unemployment rates orwhere social safety nets are disrupted. Additional factors range from extreme poverty, marginalization due to social identity, financial exclusion, irregular migration status, low educational background to mental and physical disabilities, and/or dysfunctional family environments, among others.
Against this background, it is apparent that understanding vulnerability is critical in responding to and preventing the crime of trafficking in persons.
The abuse of an individual’s position of vulnerability is one of the means used by traffickers to perpetrate their illicit acts. Understanding the factors that can thus heighten exposure to trafficking in person (TIP) can improve the ability to identify victims of trafficking and develop strategies to prevent and respond more effectively to the Trafficking in Persons phenomenon. It is important to note from the onset that anyone can become a victim of trafficking, regardless of whether the individual may experience any vulnerability. Nevertheless, evidence shows that vulnerability factors characterize most trafficking cases.In Sudan, although there is very few evidence of the scale of human trafficking, the likelihood of a widespread phenomenon cannot be disregarded. The global slavery index places Sudan at 4 in the prevalence for modern slavery with the number of people victims of modern slavery estimated to 174 000 people before the eruption of the conflict. The internally displaced people, the migrants, the refugees but also all war-affected populations are particularly vulnerable to human trafficking, even more so in this time of emergency. Human trafficking is known to proliferate through highly adaptable modus operandi, such as fraudulent employment offers abroad, offers of support for transportation, accommodation and residence, as well as through work offers and online recruitment and disinformation.Other similar contexts have shown that the current situation in Sudan will increase both the number of victims and the number of traffickers, as affected people resort to negative coping strategies, and individuals not previously involved in trafficking or exploitation use the opportunity to take advantage of desperate people, setting up or extending trafficking networks into the affected areas and populations. The proposed actions aim to increase the preventive measures as well as the dissemination of tools for the prevention, the identification, the assistance and the protection of victims of human trafficking in the Republic of Sudan during the state of emergency. Given the emergency and based on the context and comparative advantage and challenges, two main goals for improving the functionality and effectiveness of the CSO consortium have been set:
(1) Strengthen the functionality of the CSO Consortium during the current emergency on preventing and countering human trafficking.
(2) Support the effectiveness of CSO consortium in making a significant and sustained programmatic contribution to the prevention, identification, assistance, and protection of victims of human trafficking in Sudan during the emergency.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Enhancing the prevention, identification and assistance of protection of victims of human trafficking would need to mainstream protection in its design and implementation phase so that protection risks and potential violations are taken into consideration. To mainstream protection, R&T and its partners will work to analyse and understand who is at risk, from what or whom as well as why, and the consequences their actions or inactions may have on the threats people experience and their vulnerability and capacity vis-à-vis these threats. This includes knowing how and where to refer people in need for specialist support to prevent or recover from violence and exploitation, as well as understanding when, how, and to whom to refer specialised protection issues. The 4 key principles of the protection mainstreaming framework would be considered, promoted and implemented: Prioritise safety and dignity, and do-no-harm; Meaningful access; Accountability;Participation & Empowerment
