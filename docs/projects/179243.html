Project Summary

Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are the leading cause of death worldwide, with
particularly higher rates in low- and middle-income countries. In Syria, an estimated 45%
of all deaths are related to NCDs according to the WHO. In addition, WHO estimates that
600,000 people in Syria are suffering from severe mental health (MH) disorders and
another 4 million from mild to moderate mental disorders. In the context of a
humanitarian and economic crisis, fragile health care systems are unable to respond to
the growing prevalence of NCDs and MH in a timely and proper manner, and where these
exist, vulnerable people have great difficulty in affording health services, treatment and
medication. Internal studies conducted in Salamieh District indicate that the top the
driving factor for households to engage in negative coping strategies is a drastic attempt
to access lifesaving health services and medicine.
The purpose of this project is to support vulnerable people with NCDs and/or MH to
access quality and lifesaving health services in Salamieh District. To do this, the project
will (1) improve the capacity and quality of health service delivery for AKF’s Health
Counseling Center (HCC) – the only facility in Salamieh District providing services for
NCDs and MH; (2) provide cash assistance for 9 months to 2,400 patients (targeting 50%
women) with NCDs and/or MH, prioritizing vulnerable women of reproductive age (15-49),
persons with disabilities (PwDs), and the elderly (50+); and (3) educate patients and their
families about NCDs and MH through awareness-raising sessions. The project will
establish high level of coordination with local stakeholders including health authority,
local NGOs, and SARC through establishing a joint committee (with 50% females’
representation) who will be involved in all project stages with supervision over the project
implementation.
The project is designed with close attention to the following:
• Active participation of girls and women with representation of females in clinical and
administrator positions.
• Establishing protocols for clinical care of GBV survivors and safe and ethical case
documentation.
• Raise awareness of all health personnel on basic issues related to GBV, women’s/human rights.
• Ensure safe healthcare environment with close consideration to patients’ privacy and
confidentiality as follows: i) providing safe and confidential escorts to HCC; ii) schedule
convenient opening times during daylight hours along with installing adequate lighting; iii)
ensure non-stigmatizing and confidential place such as planning for same entrance for
NCDs & MH services.
• As a part of NCDs services, AKF will provide counseling on healthy lifestyle which cover
nutrition recommendations.

4 - Needs

Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are the leading cause of death worldwide, with particularly higher rates in low- and middle-income countries. In Syria, an estimated 45% of all deaths are related to NCDs according to the WHO. In addition, WHO estimates that 600,000 people in Syria are suffering from severe mental health (MH) disorders and another 4 million from mild to moderate mental disorders. Worsening living standards and an increase in harmful coping strategies have led additional segments of the population to develop life-threatening physical and mental health needs (Needs and Response Summary 2021). Renewed attention on essential health services is required in 2021 to serve the needs of the most vulnerable, especially in the face of economic deterioration affecting key determinants of health. In Salamieh District, NCDs are significantly high, affecting more than 25% of people within a given household, and particularly those over the age of 50. Based on an AKF study conducted in 2019 in Salamieh District, an estimated 30,000 individuals have one or more NDCs, and/or MH disorders, and are desperately struggling to afford health care. Female headed households have slightly higher percentages of individuals with chronic illness (28.5% for FHHs, 24.2% MHHs), as well as per capita health expenditures. In Salamieh District, expenditure on Health is SYP 32,000/US$ 11.8 in 2020. A separate survey with 279 cancer patients, showed that average annual health care expenses were around SYP 614,000, with the top driving factor for households to engage in negative coping strategies is a desperate attempt to access lifesaving health services and medication, which is also the top households’ expenditure (84% of respondents).

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will support vulnerable people with NCDs and MH disorders to access qualitylifesaving health services in Salamieh District through a gender- and protection-sensitiveapproach. 1- Improving the capacity and quality of health service delivery of the HCC: (a)Support the HCC with human, administrative and operational resources needed for NCDand MH service delivery, which includes salaries for 12 current staff and volunteers, halfof whom are female. a) Develop HCC standard operating procedures (SOPs) and referralsystem. b) Establish a health service point to provide consultation and follow-up services,including home counselling sessions for patients who have difficulty with mobility 2-Providing cash assistance for 9 months to 2,400 patients (targeting 50% women) withNCDs and/or MH disorders, prioritizing vulnerable women of reproductive age, PwDs andthe elderly to help cover health costs such as checkups, lab tests, imaging, andmedicines, transport, and accommodation expenses. a) Identify list of vulnerable peoplewith NCDs and/or MH disorders using HCC records and AKF’s vulnerability database, andverify in consultation with specialized doctors b) Classify selected beneficiaries based onthe following categories: Category A) 1,000 patients with one disease (NCD or MHdisorder) to receive SYP 40,000/month; Category B) 800 patients with two diseases(NCDs and/or MH disorders) to receive SYP 60,000/month; Category C) 600 patients withthree diseases (NCDs and/or MH disorders) or cancer to receive SYP 80,000/month 3-Educating patients and families about NCDs and MH through awareness-raisingsessions, which will be preceded by developing materials and conducting ToT for 8health educators.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A: Access to HCC and/or cash distribution site may be physically challenging for patientsor may expose them to injuries or discomfort. Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium. AKF,in coordination with FMFI, will allow beneficiaries to assign a member of their families tocollect cash assistance for them in case of their inability to do so. AKF will assign avoluntary team to assist beneficiaries at the HCC or cash distribution site, and HCC willallow home visit for counselling when patients are unable to come especially for elderlyand people with disability. B: Access to HCC and/or cash distribution site may exposepatients to safety concern such as theft, GBV, or harassment. Likelihood: Low; Impact:Low. FMFI will ensure cash distribution site has suitable waiting space equipped withsufficient chairs, along with separate lines for women and men to receive their cashassistance. Cash assistance will be distributed in day hours prioritizing people comingfrom rural areas, people with disability, women, and elders. Compliant reportingmechanisms will be available to report any cases of violence or harassment, followed byappropriate responses. C: Mental Health patients may be subject to discrimination due tonegative cultural perception associated with Mental Health diseases. Likelihood:Medium; Impact: Medium. To mitigate such risk, HCC will take special measures tosecure the privacy for MH patients. Regular and confidential monitoring and compliant reporting systemwill help to identify and investigate any cases of discrimination and take actions againstit. AKF will facilitate awareness raising on MH as a way forward to change negativecultural perceptions and encourage positive interaction with MH patients.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Difficulty to reach eligible patients identified through AKF list of vulnerable familiesand HCC records due to change in patients contact information such as changingresidence. Mitigation: AKF will work through many communications channels such as keyinformants, CBOs to reach these patients and update their information contacts. Risk2:Death of patients receiving cash assistance. Mitigation: AKF will prepare additional list ofeligible patients to replace the diseased ones and receive cash assistance for theremaining period. Risk3: Lack of local qualified physicians. Mitigation: AKF invest incapacity building of available local qualified physicians. Risk4: Exchange rate fluctuationsand/or inflation may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Mitigation: AKFregularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses and market prices to consider theimpact on the project implementation. Risk5: The deterioration of the security situationlimiting movement of staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors the localsecurity situation. In the event that mobility of staff and beneficiaries is deemed a highrisk, AKFS will delay the implementation of activities until the situation is safe for traveland implementation.
