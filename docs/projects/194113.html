1 - Needs

According to UNHCR, 2,604 refugees reside in Babikiri and require comprehensive quality health services as the nearest MoH facilities are unable to serve this population. MTI/ZOA has been responding to the needs of these refugees in health and nutrition since May 2022. Following renewed conflict in the Northern part of Ethiopia the number of refugees has shown increase and existing refugees require a continued comprehensive health care services including Nutrition support. Currently Medical Teams is the only health partner providing H&N services. In East Darfur following MSF leaving the Kario Camp Health facility, the responsibility of providing health services is under the hands of MoH. MoH has limited capacity and funds to provide comprhensive Health Services. Moreover the number of health partners in East darfur and there is limited capacity to reach other refugees living outside the camps. Accordinlgly Medical Teams International (MTI) proposes to provide access to primary healthcare and integrated nutrition services directly to 78,851 beneficiaries (50,747 refugees (64%) in Kario and El Nimir Camps and to 22,971 of immediate host communities (36%)) in Bahr el Arab and Assalaya localities, East Darfur state. Accordingly MTI will work to adress assessed needs on" To improve limited basic health services in camps. Fill gaps and strengthen Basic Emergency Obstetrics and Newborn care (BEmONC) or structured PMTCT (Prevention of Mother to Childhood Transmission) of HIV services. However, there are weeks the vaccination team does not do outreach to the camp. Consequently, there is There is a need to strenghen child health and EPI services, significant potential for missed opportunities and hence under coverage, and risk for preventable disease outbreak. Though improvements in services on Non communicable disease more has to be done as NCD management were limited to basic oral hypertension and diabetes medications. Emergency referral system require continued support and build up on existing capacity and system set in place. Improvement of health infrastructure is also one of the needs on the ground which requires support. Malnutrition in under five children and PLWs is high there is a need for nutrition response including IYCF and community mobilization.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The following activities will be implemented to address the needs mentioned above, Output 1: Primary Health Care Services provided, which will include: • Provision of a comprehensive Outpatient package with IMCI, MHPSS and NCD (following the Package of Essential Non-Communicable Disease Intervention (PEN) integrated in all clinics. • Ensure Inpatient Services: Do inpatient treatment to provide essential primary and secondary health care services • Strengthen the capacity of health care providers and community health care workers to provide essential health services. • Do mental health consultations to provide essential primary and secondary health care services. • Support access to comprehensive SRH and HIV services to reduce maternal and new-born morbidity and mortality through: Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP), Provision safe motherhood awareness and services such as ANC, PNC, and family planning, • Attend (skilled attendant) vaginal deliveries to provide essential primary and secondary health care services. • Do antenatal care sessions to provide essential primary and secondary health care services. • Conduct postnatal sessions to provide essential primary and secondary health care services • supporting health facilities with basic and comprehensive emergency obstetric care, establishing and monitor access to BEmONC and ensure timely referral options for Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and New-born Care (CEmONC). Ensure clinical treatment and care services for survivors of rape and sexual gender-based violence (SGBV). Ensure HIV/AIDs and TB services are integrated within the PHC package in coordination with MoH/UNFPA/UNICEF etc. • Actively engage communities in activities to promote and sustain their health through recruitment of a minimum number of CHWs with regular training and supportive supervision and fully integrate them as the first level of health system. Output 2. Secondary Health Services Provided • Access to comprehensive health services supported through a functional referral mechanism shall be emphasized. MTI will build on existing programming to strengthen referrals systems. • Refer cases for specialized treatment (between levels of care across Sudan) to provide essential primary and secondary health care services. • MHPSS referrals will follow the MHPSS pyramid, with each clinic having a psychologist & a visiting psychiatrist to be planned on monthly basis to offer mental health treatment to the many cases in the Camps and host community. • Do occupational visits to provide essential primary and secondary health care services. Output 3. Nutrition services provided Effective treatment of acute malnutrition (through CMAM) and effective prevention of undernutrition and micronutrient deficiencies through: • Support to CHWs (trained in output 1) to conduct screening for acute malnutrition through monthly MUAC screening for early case finding, referring to appropriate treatment and they will also support community mobilization and follow-up defaulters. • Integrated curative nutrition services within health facilities using CMAM approach which includes managing children under five years of age with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) (through OTP) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) (through TSFP). • Outpatient management of severe acute malnutrition without for children 6-59 months. • Management of moderate acute malnutrition for children 6-59 months. • Management of moderate acute malnutrition for PLW: PLWs with moderate acute malnutrition will also be managed at SFPs. Children with SAM and complications will be managed at stabilization center. • Preventative nutrition programming will focus on infant and young child feeding (IYCF) at facility and community level (with support of network of Mother support groups), micronutrient (iron folate)/Vitamin A supplementation and deworming (1-5 years). • Infant and Young Child feeding (IYCF) counselling. • Homebased food fortification with micronutrient.
