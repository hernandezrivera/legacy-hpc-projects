Project Summary

HIHFAD seeks to target the people with disabilities with physical rehabilitation and psychosocial support services addressing their healthcare and psychosocial special needs. The intervention is planned to be implemented through reactivating two nonfunctional fixed facility based in Afrin and Kafr Nobol cities starting from the 1st of April till end of December 2019. The facilities are expected to serve individuals in need within their respective sub districts and potentially the nearby sub districts as well. The services are planned to be provided 8 hours/day, 5 days/week. The services will be made available for the individuals of both sexes across all age groups. HIHFAD will make sure that the facilities are adequately rehabilitated and well equipped to be appropriate for serving the people in need. In the same context, HIHFAD will support the referral costs of up to 100 patients requiring installment of prosthetic limbs at other facilities as well as the costs of the whole installment processes including those for procuring the limbs.

Needs

Nearly eight years into the Syria crisis, disability is increasingly prevalent due to a combination of war-related injuries and damage to the physical environment. A recent assessment on disability among adults in western Aleppo, Idleb and Ar-Raqqa governorates indicated an average prevalence rate of 30 per cent. Furthermore, the assessment revealed that an average of 45 per cent of surveyed persons injured during the Syria crisis are expected to sustain a permanent impairment (e.g. amputation, spinal cord injury, brain injury). Persons with disabilities are vulnerable to exclusion from the humanitarian response, despite their specific or additional needs. Over 60 per cent of persons with disabilities state that their disability has been exacerbated by the crisis, with a lack of access to healthcare and difficulties in meeting their basic needs listed as the most common reasons. Inclusive humanitarian response is a core component of principled and effective humanitarian action; disability mainstreaming is essential within the Syria Humanitarian response. This may include ensuring physical accessibility of activities or services, training staff in how to engage with persons with disabilities in ways that are respectful and maintain the person's inherent dignity, and/or the collection of disability disaggregated data. (The draft of HNO for Syria 2019)People with disabilities are among the groups prioritized by the health cluster/ Gaziantep hub, and consequently, more efforts has to be invested in addressing their needs.Afrin is a sub district located in Aleppo governorate and host some 94,855 population according to the latest data communicated by the health cluster/ Gaziantep hub. According to the HNO data for Syria 2018, the sub district suffers a severity of 3/6 in terms of healthcare and 4/6 in terms of protection conditions. Furthermore, and according to the latest mapping communicated by the trauma and rehab working group/ health cluster/ Gaziantep hub, currently there is only one facility that provides physical rehabilitation services across the whole district of Afrin, what rises the need for the establishment of additional facilities that can provide physical rehabilitation services for those in need in that area.Kafr Nobol is a sub district located far most south to Idleb governorate and host some 115,370 population according to the latest data communicated by the health cluster/ Gaziantep hub. According to the HNO data for Syria 2018, the sub district suffers a severity of 4/6 in terms of healthcare and 5/6 in terms of protection conditions. Furthermore, and according to the latest mapping communicated by the trauma and rehab working group/ health cluster/ Gaziantep hub, currently there is only one facility that provides physical rehabilitation services across the whole district of Afrin, what rises the need for the establishment of additional facilities that can provide physical rehabilitation services for those in need in that area.

Implementation of activities

Provide essential primary and secondary health care services, including trauma care, EmONC and referral: HIHFAD plans to support the operational costs of a facility that will be operating 8 hours per day, 5 days per week. The facility will be providing the presented people with disabilities with the physical rehabilitation sessions they need in order to improve their physical conditions. In addition and given that such people are among the most vulnerable population groups that are subject to compromised psychosocial difficulties, HIHFAD plans to offer them the psychosocial support those patients are in need of in the form of peer to peer sessions. Furthermore, and to ensure the comprehensive characteristics of the approach, HIHFAD will dedicate an appropriate proportion of the financial requirement for the purpose of assisting those with disabilities that undergo the loss of one or more of their limbs. That assistance is planned to be implemented by supporting the costs of referring those patients to a facility specialized in the installment of prosthetic limbs as well as supporting the costs required for the installment procedures. For the purpose of ensuring efficient utilization of the funds, HIHFAD will make sure that extensive evaluation is carried out individually for every candidate prior to the referral. Also, HIHFAD will continue to follow up with the patients benefiting from the prosthetic limbs installation and provided them with the physical rehabilitation and psychosocial support that is required beyond the installation procedures.Furthermore, and to help the presented individuals overcoming the barriers they might encounter while seeking healthcare, any person with disabilities presented at the rehab center and suffering any healthcare condition other than his/her disability will be supported through referring him/her to the appropriate healthcare facility according to his initial complaint.

Beneficiaries are affected by conflict related incidences while seeking healthcare at the Facility.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: Facility are based in areas included in the de-escalation zones agreement at adequate distances from gatherings of armed groups. Coordinate with the local authorities (Civilian LCs and HDs) to support the exclusion of the health facility from any possible clashes. Technical support by HIHFAD safety and security team. Training of staff on contingency plans. Ensure about the establishment of emergency exits within the facility.2. Patients are subject to harm due to low-quality healthcare services.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: HIHFAD follows strict HR recruitment procedures in terms of the qualifications and expertise required for medical personal. Also, HIHFAD consistently evaluate any gap of the technical capacities for its medical staff. Feedback mechanisms to be made available for the beneficiaries to ensure their participation in identifying any gap in the staff technical capacities that need to be addressed. In the same context, HIHFAD consistently seeks to ensure the standardization of the services provided in terms of medical equipment, medical supplies and pharmaceuticals.3. While at Facility, Beneficiaries are abused, harassed, undergo compromised privacy or dignity.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: Child safeguarding training provided for all staff. Establishment of feedback mechanisms with maintained anonymity. Ensure individuals of vulnerable groups (children, women, elderly and people with disabilities) are accompanied while at the Facility. Ensure adequate female staff representation. Application of privacy screens and separation of medical examination theatres. Establishment of breast-feeding corners. Separation of the bathrooms by sex. Label tags to be demonstrated within the Facility. Establishment of adequate waiting areas and ensure adequate lighting of the facility is in place.4. Confidentiality of the information provided by the Beneficiaries is compromised.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: staff of the Facility adapt HIHFAD code of conduct. Tags about confidentiality of patient records and information to be demonstrated within the Facility. Maintaining patient information through well secured and strictly monitored modalities inaccessible to public. Establishment of feedback mechanisms with maintained anonymity. 5. Communities are affected by medical wastes produced at the Facility.• Likelihood: L/ Impact: H• Mitigation: Securing the medical wastes prior to disposal. Application of medical waste management pathways for the purpose of proper disposal of them.6. Staff/ patients and their companions acquire nosocomial infections while at the facility.• Likelihood: M/ Impact: M to H• Mitigation: programmed use of disinfection measures. Programmed microbiological screening of the Facility. Establishment of clean and well-ventilated waiting areas. Proper handling of medical wastes. Use of personal protective equipment when indicated.
