Project Summary

Acute Food insecurity remains prevalent among agro- pastoral population in South Central Somalia. Most of Somalia acutely food insecure population are still in rural areas .They make 60% percent of Somalis
facing crisis and emergency (IPC phase 3-4) .The food security of this people can deteriorate very quickly as can seen in the first half of 2017 when rural people soared from 44,000 to 647000 people between Jan and May . Vulnerable Households lost most of their livestock ,poor harvest and became destitute during the 2016/2017 drought and subsequent conflict in the Area .
The Agropastrolist need urgent assistance to purchase & produce food .Cash transfers offers a vital safety net to families that lost production assets and income and cannot meet their food needs .For this reasons the programme combines Cash for work ,Unconditional cash transfers with agricultural inputs to ensure families meet their immediate needs, restore their own food production and rehabilitate communal assets
In response to this RAWA will implement extensive Cash for Work & unconditional cash transfer program for 1200 vulnerable beneficiaries (Female headed household, pastoralist who have lost most of their livestock ,Marginalized, low or no income Households) in Mudug and Haradhere District to work towards providing food access to vulnerable Households through a cash for work program enabling them to access the minimum food basket as well as rehabilitating community productive assets for a period of 4 months. In addition taking into consideration that other most vulnerable HH without labor force are excluded (Disabled, pregnant and lactating women, elderly and marginalized ) will be reached with unconditional cash transfers.
The programme will be closely coordinated with the input distribution in support of agriculture and resilience strategy targeting 800 vulnerable farmers (30% women/women headed households, Households who lost herd and have no food reserves, Beneficiaries with land, Households with one or more members malnourished, Residents and IDPS) in Haradhere and Hobyo District who will receive seeds (Sorghum, Pulses and vegetable seeds) as well as the unconditional cash transfer for 3 months.
Training activities will be conducted to improve capacity and resilience,intergrate Nutrition hygiene activities, good agronomic practices and integrated production.As well as mainstreaming Gender and protection guidelines throughout the project duration to maximize projects output.
A Do No Harm approach will be adopted in all our interventions. Contextual analysis is important in the implementation of this project considering the various potential sources of conflicts in the target locations both at leadership and community level. Regular assessments and community engagements will be done to learn if the project activities, approaches and methodologies are sensitive to the local context.

Needs

Acute Food insecurity remains prevalent among agro pastoral community in Galmudug District who lost most of their livestock & agricultural asset became destitute during the 2016/2017 drought and a recent large -scale protracted displacement of communities in Haradhere district who were forced to flee their homes .These groups are expected to face consumption gaps or engage in unsustainable coping strategies to meet their food needs and humanitarian assistance is required to prevent further deterioration of their food security & Nutrition according to the recent 2018 Post Gu- FSNAU assessmentThis is further supported by a recently conducted Joint Multi-cluster needs Assessment indicating there is an increasing level of food insecurity with Households reporting abnormal household hunger rates ,reliance on coping strategies and a reduction in the quality & variety of food .Additionally findings indicate decreasing levels of Household economic resilience as evidenced by a reported reduction in household income sources, rising food prices, and a reported lack of resources to purchase food as a key access constraint. Overall this indicates the gradual reduction of economic resilience of households especially the most susceptible (Female headed households, disabled, elderly and pregnant women)A rapid assessment done by RAWA indicates livelihood support played a fundamental role in restoring people’s ability to stabilize their own food security, provide for themselves and begin a recovery process . The protracted drought has had a drastic impact on the already fragile agricultural and livestock assets and has further eroded natural resources and the resilience of affected rural communities. Rural people & IDPs need urgent assistance to purchase and produce food. Cash transfers offer a vital safety net to families that lost production, assets and income, and cannot otherwise meet their food needs.

Activities

Outcome 1: Immediate access to basic (food and non-food) needs through cash transfers for a period of 4 months Activities 1.1.1:- Increased access to food and improve nutrition through unconditional cash transfers targeting 2,000 households representing 12,000 persons (3,638 women, 2,940 men, 2,448 boys and 2,976 girls) arrived IDPs and host community who are classified as phase 3, phase 4 and phase 5. RAWA will prioritize unconditional cash transfers, especially for the vulnerable Household, Female headed household, Pregnant and lactating women, the elderly, disabled, IDPs and pastoralist who lost a huge number of livestock through the voucher system. The cash transfer rates will be determined by the recommendations of the Cash Working Group (CWG) and based on 100% of the food component of the MEB for food security objectives. Outcome 2: Critical livelihood assets are restored and economic recovery is supported through provision of conditional cash transfers for a period of 4 monthsActivities 2.1.1.:- Provision of conditional cash transfers (cash for work), which will target key infrastructure of community assets rehabilitation during the lean seasons to 1,200HHs. The transfer will be conditional to completing prioritizing of community infrastructures (water catchment rehabilitation, Feeder roads, and key community assets) work in Mudug region.Activities 2.1.2 Distribute farm inputs 800 drought affected Agropastrolist (450 women and 350 men) the farm input and tools will be composed of farm seeds.Activities 2.1.3 Train 500 farmers (200 men and 300 women) and 100 pastorals (50 men and 50 women) for the best farming methodologies and technologies and animal husbandry respectively.Activities 2.1.4 Train Community/WASH committee in administration, management, operation & Maintenance of the rehabilitated community assets.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Multi-layered conflict & climatic shocks compounded by emerging but still weak governance structures massive displacement and persistence exclusion & marginalization have led to a complex humanitarian environment where rights are regularly violated. There is a likelihood IDPs, marginalized communities may be excluded from accessing the assistance provided. The project has mitigating measures put in place to counter this. There will be a systematic participation of representatives of marginalized IDP communities in consultation with the community as well as equal direct and non-discriminatory access to the program.The 2000 Beneficiaries will be selected through Community based targeting to promote participation & ownership of the programme further reducing risks of exclusion.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Gender disparities are visible in the operational communities, where women usually take a back seat in issues of community development. The project, through community mobilization and awareness, will ensure women are empowered and represented to influence decisions and outcomes. Women will account to 30% and above in both the CFW activities and Input distribution. While the elderly who are not physically capable will be eligible for the unconditional cash transfer. This will ensure no vulnerable individual is excluded from the programme.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

There is always a likelihood of Complaints/violence from individuals who do not qualify/eligible for the project to create chaos since he /she was not registered since the project will not cater to everyone.Beneficiary selection process will be done through a transparent community based targeting to avoid this types of scenarios.
