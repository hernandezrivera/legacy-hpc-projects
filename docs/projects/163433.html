Project Summary

Under the leadership of the Regional Education Authorities (DREB) and the various ministries who cather for the education of adolescents, the project aims at providing access to protective learning routines for a minimum of 10,000 out of school crises affected children. In 2016-17 UNICEF designed and piloted the first-in-its-kind emergency Radio Education in Emergency Prototype: 144 episodes on literacy and numeracy for all contexts where crisis affected children cannot physically access schools. A prototype for illiterate school aged children was also tested in DRC, CAR, and Burkina Faso, NWSW crisis affected communities, and is under recording in Douala (ready to be narrowcast). This model is unique in targeting children for individual listening or in groups – without the help of an adult or a teacher – from within their communities or from their new location. This is different from other radio education programs, usually designed to help teachers teach (i.e. radio assisted instruction). In the current phase, the emergency program prototype does not substitute classroom education but constitutes a powerful educational and protective platform for hard-to-reach children in emergency and conflict contexts, until a safer learning environment is provided, and children can be integrated into the normalcy of the national education system.
Preliminary evidence confirms the complementing protective nature of the Radio Education Program prototype for children living in high insecurity: besides life-saving messages, it offers psychosocial support. Children listening are brought back in time to when they were in school before displacement. It gives them hope.
Evidence from Niger and Cameroon (2017/18) suggests that children who follow the program in listening groups improve their competences in literacy, numeracy and life-skills. Pilot studies on learning (based on pre-tests and post-tests) are being planned in all countries, targeting children who listen in group, as well as children who listen individually from home.

Besoins / Needs

About 400,000 school-age children (49% of girls) need assistance in the education sector in the Far North region, particularly in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava, and Mayo-Tsanaga. Due to the displacement of families, the host schools integrate additional displaced students, which constitute a burden for already week education services. According to the 2018 report of ministry of basic education, the student/teacher ratio in the Far-north region is 127 children per teacher. The project, which is not new to this region, as the MOE has already broadcast this service for children in Mokolo and Kousseri, targets a minimum of 10,000 children (listeners) who are out of school, and that will be reached initially with narrowcasting, and - depending on the situation - with broadcasting. Targeted children will be identified amonst the displaced and crisis affected population.
