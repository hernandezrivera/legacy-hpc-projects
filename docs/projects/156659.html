Project Summary

The conflict has severely disrupted the health system in Syria, leaving less than half of the country’s facilities in full service and resulting in thousands of avoidable deaths from injury or illness. Displacement continues to drive health needs, particularly in northeast Syria where the influx of displaced persons has left over 484,000 people requiring urgent assistance and further challenged an already overstretched health infrastructure. [HNO 2018]
WATAN is one of these actors, that provides health services inside Syria for more than six years.
The mission of the Health programme is to contribute to the building of a stable health system in Syria through the provision of health-related humanitarian assistance, focusing on the most vulnerable people. In addition, it aims at strengthening the coordination and health information systems for improving the effectiveness of life-saving health response.
Within the current proposal, WATAN seeks to deliver essential health services for the affected population by supporting medical facilities for operating and continuing to offer health care to vulnerable people.
• Objective 1: Provide life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian health assistance for those most at risk and in need, including IDPs, to decrease the morbidity and to prevent disease outbreaks.
• Objective 2: Improve health system capacity for support of continuity of care by collecting and reporting on health situation of the people assisted.
In reaching the objectives, WATAN will support 6 medical facilities to maintain and even increase access to primary and trauma health care services especially in internal medicine, pediatrics, orthopedics, dentistry, gastroenterology, gynecology, otology, physiotherapy and radiology. The final goal is to help people in need facing many shortages caused by the conflict, in providing them good health service with impact on reducing the number of deaths and diseases/trauma.
The targeted areas by WATAN are the facilities from Idleb, Aleppo and Rural Damascus.
The results of the project envisage to:
- Keep on providing the diagnostic and therapeutic medical support for free to more than 122,000 beneficiaries in the targeted areas.
- Raise the medical efficiency of WATAN’s and partners’ staff.
- Fully equipped health centers with well trained staff capable of providing the healthcare services with the best quality.
- Running facilities without interruption which will be an asset in the future during the stabilization period.
The project duration is 12 months with an estimated budget of 1,2 million $.

Needs

The current project is based on the information collected from the supported centers in addition to data processed from official reports.WATAN identified the following problems on the field:- Limited access of the population to health services due to the decreased number of functioning facilities operating in opposition-controlled areas; large distance for travelling implying money for transportation and danger encountered during the trip by patients;- Lack of specialized medical staff caused by the brain drain of doctors and continuous targeting of them;- Limited support to run medical facilities, in terms of financial, equipment and human resources; continue targeting of medical centers;- Limited access of the staff inside Syria to training/courses meant to improve their capacity and align/update the information with the ones available on the medical market.

Implementation of activities

-Khayr1 center is classified as PHC level 3 (according to WHO classification) and provides the health services in internal medicine, dentistry, gynecology, orthopedic, otology and pediatric through 30 staff, 19 of them medical staff. The center also aims to complete the healthcare service by providing the needed medications for patients for free, in addition to technical training for the center's staff. The center provides essential services like non-communicable diseases services (including hypertension, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, chronic respiratory diseases….), child health services (including IMCI and IYCF) and reproductive health services (including gynecology diseases, monitoring of pregnant…). The center provides additional services such as ENT, orthopedic, and dentistry, in addition to investigation services including laboratory and radiology. - Khayr2 center includes a central laboratory that conducts most laboratory tests (hematology, biochemistry, immune hormones, germ culture and additional tests). The center contains a Blood bank for providing most of blood products (whole blood, Red cell concentrate, concentrate of platelets and fresh frozen Plasma), and an oxygen generator to provide Oxygen cylinders for health facilities. Currently, the center is operated by 16 staff members, 8 of them medical staff. -Al-amal orthopedic center is specialized in orthopedic surgery where most orthopedic surgeries (major and minor) are conducted in the center in addition to orthopedic clinic through 26 staff, 16 of them medical staff. The center is part of the health referral network in Saraquip district which is supported by WHO. This coordination increases the stability and sustainability of the local health system in the district of Ariha and in Idlib governorate. More, this would increase Health governance actors and local organizations management of complex medical cases and help medical service providers improving the quality and efficiency of the provided medical services. - Khayr3 center will include central blood bank (providing most of blood products (whole blood, Red cell concentrate, concentrate of platelets and fresh frozen Plasma) and specialized thalassemia center.

Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers:Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL).- Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare, and enlisting support of the Strategic Steering Group (SSG) and humanitarian leadership in their efforts to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria.- Reporting from relevant hubs regarding health situation in reconciled areas with continuous monitoring of health status of population in these areas especially in the immediate post-reconciliation phase.Gender Based Violence GBV:- Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package.- Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals.- Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor.- Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services.Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA:- A PSEA policy describes standard of behaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures.- Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.- Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic.- Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff.Duty of care:- Establishing “duty of care” policies detailing clear implementation procedures, is the responsibility of each individual organization in a manner ensuring the resilience and wellbeing of contracted health staff.- Staff Care programs which provide psychosocial support services and resources to staff.- Measures ensuring regular and consistent delivery of salaries, bearing in mind the ethical and logistical imperative to go further than basic labor rights.- Engagement with donors on the financial aspects of “duty of care” in early stages of project design to guarantee payment flexibility, and availability of severance payments in cases of unforeseen termination of contracts or re-location of the programs resulting from attacks on healthcare or change in control scenarios.Community Engagement and Participation:- Establishment of a common feedback mechanism utilizing available communication means, social media, in addition to the face-to-face approach.- Production and dissemination of key messages to ensure affected communities have access to information needed to receive humanitarian assistance and provide feedback.- Establish local partnerships, building the capacity of partners to effectively engage with communities, and strengthen community based health services to facilitate dialogue with communities.- Ensuring that community voice is included in the Third Party Monitoring mechanisms for health programs.- Regular follow-up and reporting on the results of accountability mechanisms in place.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Unforeseen changes in the conflict: Conflict is such that organization cannot safely access locations, staff get besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost.-Management and mitigation of this risk: Continue to gather information from a range of sources. Develop a contingency plan to work in alternative location if conflict continues. Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space. 2-Detention / Arrest of the staff: This has been one of the most reoccurring security incidents in Syria since 2011 in regime, opposition and ISIS controlled areas. This also includes Syrian staff in Lebanon and potentially Turkey. Detentions can be for any reason or duration. All must be managed on a case-by-case basis. Detention can be both of individuals and of materials. The mistreatment of detainees is a real threat. - Management and mitigation of this risk: Staff tracking and daily check-ins. Actor mapping and connections established in all areas. Staff training on visibility and communication practices. 3- Loss of acceptance or registration by Turkish authorities in the destination area: Beginning in the fall of 2014, Turkish governmental relations are good and increasing, for the work in Turkey and in north west Syria areas controlled by Turkey.- Management and mitigation of this risk: Continue generating options: local procurement, stock-piling inside certain areas in Syria and seeking alternate border possibilities.
