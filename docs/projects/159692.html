Project Summary

As per Somali -2019-post-Gu -FSNAU-FEW Technical release: Current high levels of food insecurity are driven by drought conditions from late 2018 through mid-May 2019, exacerbated by below-average and erratically distributed rainfall during the 2019 Gu season (April-June), which began late and finished early. Many households are still recovering from the severe 2016/2017 drought or have been affected by conflict, and 2.6 million people remain displaced.
Vaccine preventable diseases are prevalent in Somalia and child mortality is 200 per 1,000 live births. In Somalia WHO and UNICEF estimates of immunization Coverage: BCG 37%, DPT1 52% DPT3 AND IPV 42%.
The under-five and maternal mortality rate in Somali are amongst the highest in the world; one out of every seven Somali dies before seeing their fifth birthday (137 deaths/1,000 live birth) with higher number in south and central Somalia. The leading causes of infant and young child mortality are illness such as pneumonia, diarrhea and measles.
One out of 12 women dies due to pregnancy related causes (maternal mortality rate is 732 death of mother for 100,000 live birth down from 1210 in 1990). The average fertility rate is 6.6 children per women.
The continuous need assessments with stakeholders in Banadir, Lower Shebelle, Galgaduud and Mudug during ongoing interventions by CISP indicate that basic health services are needed by the vulnerable conflict affected populations, including urban and rural IDPs and host communities. Health care provision remains dismal with huge gaps in facilities offering maternal and child health and nutrition services including deliveries, vaccination, treatment of childhood illnesses and micronutrient supplementation. This is attributable to the overburdened health capacities, services disruption in conflict and disaster and population influx (IDP and Returnees).The challenges have exposed vulnerable populations to the risk of communicable disease and other morbidities. CISP seeks to build on her previous experience in Health specific and sensitive integrated programming to step up provision of lifesaving health in mentioned areas. CISP is currently working in the above mentioned areas in Nutrition, Health, Protection and Education.
The project targets a total of 65,182 under 5 children (35,198 girls, 29,984 boys), 57,388 WCBA and 10,000 men to address health needs by providing outpatient consultation in 15 MCHs, in patient care services in the 2 Hospitals, integrated management of childhood illnesses, immunization, maternal health services, emergency reproductive health screening of acute malnutrition and referral and health education. Some services, targeted areas women and children, including IDPs and returnees, will also be integrated in offered in 7 Mobile facilities linked to existing static health facilities for better service coverage. CISP will leverage on the EPHS strategy and structure to implement the project while building on existing C4D interventions in Shibis to ensure sustained healthy behavior change and adoption of ideal practices. CISP will sustain her coordination with the Health cluster to ensure minimal duplication and maximum impact of interventions.

Mitigation Measures

The target population faces continuous displacements due to armed conflict betweenSNA/AMISOM on one side and the Militant group on the other. The target population also faces forceful recruitment of young boys to the militia of the militant groups, although some local communities fought back and resisted they could not sustain and were ultimately defeated like what happened in some areas in Mudug region like Harardhere. As per the current situation the only way to mitigate is to families to send the young to government controlled districts and for long term solutions is to liberate those areas from the iron grip of the militants. The risk involved lack of gender equity and social inclusion: This will be mitigatedCISP will prevent exclusion and inequality by ensuring equal participation of all people, including individuals at risk of exclusion and ensure they reach their full potential, both in terms of its economy and its level of governance. Social Inclusion will be promoted through: •When hiring incentive staff equal opportunities will be given to all irrespective of their social background, gender and disability. • Equal representation in the Community Health Committees (CHC) and Community Development Committees(CDC). • Training Health staff on do no harm The proposed project will equally target women, boys and girls, particularly those from vulnerable families affected by ill health and drought whose vulnerabilities are exacerbated by the worsening drought, water scarcity and displacement into IDP settlements where children live in extremely precarious conditions with only limited community support. This project will support the MOH to identify the staff with optimum qualifications and experiences taking into account the different capacities and needs of men and women. As well as assigning equal opportunities, tasks and responsibilities to both men and women. Community Based workers selection and trainings will also consider gender sensitivity including both men and women. All this will ensure gender issues and equity is addressed. –CISP will prioritize safety &dignity, and avoid causing and any unintended negative effects of its intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and Psycho-social risks. CISP will ensure people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services.
