Project Summary

Due to recurrent drought and conflict, the recovery within many communities in Somalia remains fragile. GU 2019 assessments conducted by FSNAU indicate at national level, the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has remained Serious (10–14.9%) over the past three seasons (13.8% in 2019 Gu, 12.6% in 2018 Deyr and 14.0% in 2018 Gu). Certain population groups such as Galkacyo, Mogadishu, Jowhar and Baidoa IDPs are of concern due to chronically high rates of acute malnutrition with GAM rates of 20.2%, 16.0%, 25.9% and 14.5% respectively. Results from 33 separate nutrition surveys conducted by FSNAU and partners in June and July 2019 indicate the persistence of widespread acute malnutrition in Somalia due to a combination of factors, including food insecurity, high morbidity, low immunization and vitamin-A supplementation, and poor care practices. FSNAU assessment projected an estimate of 1, 008, 500 children under the age of five years (total acute malnutrition burden), would likely face acute malnutrition through June 2020, including 178 400 who are likely to be severely malnourished. Surveys recommended Integrated interventions to be provided to support recovery and prevent deterioration in the nutrition situation.
A SMART Survey conducted by IMC in Galkacyo in April 2019 found low coverage of immunization, deworming, vitamin A, iron multiple micro-nutrient supplementation. More than one quarter (28.3%0 of HHs had low dietary diversity scores and almost of half (47.7%) of the household fall under poor and borderline FCS categories. Poor water and sanitation facilities and poor storage of complementary foods exacerbates the situation due to resultant diarrhoeal disease. MIYCF Survey conducted by IMC in May 2019 revealed that almost all indicators of breastfeeding and commentary feeding are below the recommended standard. IMC programme data shows high prevalence of anemia among PLW which contains nutrient insufficient in diet, even in the absence of drought conditions.
This project aims to contribute to reduction of infant morbidity and mortality through improved access to screening, treatment and prevention of acute malnutrition; as well as availing preventive micronutrient supplementation among some of the most vulnerable communities and regions in South Central Somalia.
To enhance detection and regular identification of acutely malnourished children and PLWs, IMC Female Health Workers (FHWs) and CHWs will disseminate culturally appropriate promotion/prevention messaging and conduct community based screening and referral of malnourished children and PLWs using W/HZ, MUAC and oedema measurements. Family/mother lead MUAC strategy will be used as a simple and cost-effective way of increasing coverage of case detection. It also ensures caregivers/mothers have knowledge to identify malnutrition early.
Children who are severely malnourished or at risk of being malnourished will be referred to OTP and TSFP treatment centers, and in the hard to reach areas, by mobile health and nutrition units. Children with severe acute malnutrition with medical complications will be referred to nearby stabilization centers in accordance with national guidelines. BSFP activities will be implemented where targeted supplemental feeding programs do not meet the needs of MAM children; relapse after having recovered from SAM is high and urgent and untraditional action is required.
In addition to Individual and group IYCF counselling, IMC will promote optimal IYCF practices and positive deviance through Mother to Mother Support Groups. Within these groups, nutritious recipes will be formulated and tested through cooking demonstrations.
Other high impact nutrition interventions will include: multiple micronutrient supplementation for women of reproductive age; Vitamin A Supplementation; deworming for boys and girls aged 6-59 months; and Zinc for treatment of diarrhea.

Project Activities

1.Conduct individual and group IYCF-E counselling among PLWs and caregivers of children aged 2 years and below. 2. Establish community based Mother-to-Mother support groups for PLW 3. Conduct Market survey and food attributes exercise for listing food items locally available and classify based on their nutrient contribution in diet and develop six recipes out of it and trained mother to mother support groups 4. conduct complementary cooking demonstrations 4. conduct community dialogue.5. Scale up community based malnutrition detection and identification of acute malnutrition through FHWs and Mother MUAC 6. Provide lifesaving therapeutic treatment for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) 7. Provide Deworming, Multiple micronutrient and Vitamin A supplementation as part of Outpatient management of acute malnutrition. 8. Intensify breastfeeding counseling and re-lactation in stabilization centers for acutely malnourished children under 6 months of Age. 9. Conduct community mobilization activities for screening and referral of malnourished children. 10. Conduct Family MUAC screening. 11. Health, Nutrition and hygiene education. 12. Capacity strengthening of health workers (such as midwives), CHWs and FHWs to implement CMAM through classroom and on-job mentoring and refresher trainings. 13. Conduct blanket supplementary feeding to prevent deterioration into acute malnutrition or relapse in children discharged from OTP.

Mitigation Measures

1b. • For Children and mothers with disabilities and or with limited mobility, nutrition staff in the mobile teams will make home visits to provide treatment and IYCF counseling. Those needing transport for specialized treatment will be transported using IMC ambulances. • IMC will ensure there is adequate integration and linkages with Health/WASH/Protection services in the targeted areas to minimize distance travelled to access complementary humanitarian services. 2a. • Beneficiary selection will be systematic to ensure vulnerable persons, such as marginalized minorities, single/child headed households and Mothers/caregivers/PLWs with disabilites are not excluded. • Different sociodemographic groups will be consulted on their specific food and nutritional requirements and preferences for distribution points. • During treatment, distribution and mobile outreach, beneficiaries with disabilities & other special needs, who cannot stand in line for long periods, will be identified and given priority. • Outreach messaging will be respectful and inclusive to ensure that beneficiaries from different sociodemographic, economic and cultural groups know about nutrition services, distributions times/sites. • CHW and Mobile Health and Nutrition teams will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, socio-demographic groups, disability). 2b.• Mother to Mother support groups will be formed within different vulnerable socio-demographic groups within the target communities. • IMC will collect and use data disaggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. • Malnutrition and IYCF awareness messaging will involve women and men and will be timed to fit around women and men’s other work schedules. • All beneficiaries seeking treatment will be treated equally without discrimination strictly following protocols and guidelines for treatment of acute malnutrition as outlined in the Somali IMAM guidelines. 3a. • Safe programming and mainstreaming gender at all stages will be ensured. The integration of IMC Protection staff in the project team will help ensure the implementation of key activities beyond basic safe programming and Do No Harm approaches. • IMC will engage with local IDP and community leaders and community representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and the decision-making processes that affect nutrition practices, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build caregiver self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. • IMC will train all nutrition staff involved in care and treatment of acutely malnourished children on psychosocial support (PSS) including identification and support for disabled beneficiaries. 3b. • All IMC Nutrition staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, will sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries. • All IMC Nutrition staff and volunteers involved in distributions and in direct contact with beneficiaries will receive training in prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) 3c. • IMC will locate distribution points and OTPs in locations that are accessible by vulnerable population groups from the catchment population. • Mobile health and nutrition clinics will be located close to beneficiaries as possible and will open at times that allow mothers to walk to and from the services safely. • BSFP distributions to take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. • Mother to Mother support group sessions will be held in a central place that mothers choose and are located near caregivers’ homes to ensure short safe distances travelled to and from the meetings. 4b. IMC maintains a complaints and feedback phone number that allows anonymous submission of feedback. Timely corrective response or feedback will be maintained.
