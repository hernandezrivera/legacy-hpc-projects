Project Summary

The main objective of the project is to provide life-saving humanitarian assistance for most vulnerable adolescents and children in Idleb , A'zaz , Afrin and Al Bab. The Syrian crisis causes many negative psychological, social and economic effects in society, especially children. And as a result of these tragically situations, those children are considered the most vulnerable in society such as unaccompanied and separated children, child laborers, children associated with armed forces and groups, children deprived of their liberty, child survivors of violence, including sexual violence, children with disabilities, and other high-risk cases. The project will target the most vulnerable children and adolescents in Idleb , A'zaz , Afrin and Al Bab and will intervene to protect these categories, by means of specialized protection services, where provide case management services to physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, and lack of appropriate care, family separation and immediate risks of worst forms of child labor, child recruitment, and child marriage. 4 - Needs

The HNO 2020 identified that children throughout Syria continue to face pervasiveviolence and a range of protection risks. Grave child rights violations remain a significantconcern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of killingand injury, recruitment and use in hostilities, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence,attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access by parties to theconflict. In the first nine months of 2019, the UN verified 1,792 such violations againstchildren in 12 of 14 governorates of Syria, illustrating persistent trends of violence thoughnot the full scope of protection concerns affecting children. Masrrat has conducted manyinterviews with families and Key informant members, as a rapid assessment in Idlib andRural Aleppo to meet their needs. The results indicate cases of child abuse, labour, earlyand forced marriage, and minors being recruited, in addition to the neglect of childcarewith disabilities and Lack of specialized centers dedicated to the care of thesecases. There are many cases of separated and Unaccompanied children boys and girlsthat need urgent Family Tracing and Reunification. Also, the result indicated that, manyunaccompanied and children who are separated from their families sleep in garbagedumps and abandoned places, which makes them at high risk. Temporary shelters forthis group must be, therefore, provided for them, in addition to the health andpsychological care (wherein, the majority of children who are separated from theirfamilies or unaccompanied have lost their caregivers as a result of death or abduction).Also, all unaccompanied children are not enrolled in education and work in the collectionof garbage and plastic from the streets or in dangerous places. Also, Families withindividuals suffering from physical or mental disabilities indicated that their need was toobtain medication and artificial limbs for amputees.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will include establishing a static facility (center) that will provide several services like PFA, PSS, case management and basic necessities of life. The project contains implementation of the following main activities: - Case management services to most vulnerable children such as physical/psychological injuries, severe neglect, and lack of appropriate care, family separation and immediate risks of worst forms of child labor, child recruitment, and child marriage. - PSS and PFA individual services and recreational activities to the most vulnerable children and their caregivers. - Interim shelter services to unaccompanied, or separated children,. - Conditional cash transfers (CCT) service: the main purpose of (CCT), address the roots of the problem in child laborers and children associated with armed forces and groups during the interim period while communicating and collaborate them with NGO, local authorities, local traders and other traders to support them permanently. Where will follow the following advice for using (CCT)to ensure the safety of children: Also, the project will provide specialized services to (245) of Children with Disabilities (CWD) and their caregivers, where the activities will be: - Case management services to CWD. - Distribution of essential supplies that will remove CwDs from (potential) harm like (Chair for CwD, Hearing Earphones, Smart blind stick, Eyeglasses for blind, Crutch and Walker) where this equipment will be distributed under the supervision of specialists and in coordination with specialized organizations. MASRRAT MEAL team will review and assess the actual outcomes and actual inputs of the team, as compared with the expected outcomes and expected inputs defined by the project plan and logical framework, taking into consideration any unforeseen circumstances on monthly basis. Lessons learned will be included so as to ensure that subsequent activities are conducted with these in mind.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers if access to services or information is linked to a fee or limited to/excluded for specific persons/groups. Likelihood: High. Impact: High Mitigating measures: Communicate regularly and clearly to the community about what services are available and who services are for. Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code ofconduct and PSEA. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption (1): The security situation will not deteriorate. Risk (1): Shift in power balance, increased airstrikes and proximity to ground battles severely compromising access and implementation.Mitigation (1): Continuous monitoring of the situation and provision of services where accessible. Set the response plan and designing the activities in a flexible way where MASRRAT can change the way of conducting the activity according to the situation to achieve the objective, including the possible temporary suspension of activities.Assumption (2): the Targeted population is willing to actively engage in activities. Risk (2): unwillingness or inability to participate in decision-making mechanisms due to cultural norms. Mitigation (2): building the trust of the population through awareness-raising initiatives, leadership training and develop a sense of ownership to the project.Assumption (3): No tension occurs between communities due to the implementation of the project activities. Risk (3): Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. Mitigating (3): 1. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed. 2. Ensure consultation with communities and neighboringcommunities related to the location of services to understand the perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. 4. Support national actors and community groups toensure the sustainability of protection intervention. 5. Established committees to enhance community-based protection mechanisms. 6. Ensure information sharing with community members on services available.
