Project Summary


The overall objective of the emergency intervention proposed by Action Against Hunger (ACF)
and its 2 implementing partners Nutrition Action Zimbabwe (NAZ) and Africa Ahead Zimbabwe (AA), is to address multisector acute needs of the most vulnerable population affected by the ongoing protracted drought and socioeconomic crisis
The specific objective is to ensure the access to food and nonfood basic needs and protect the livelihoods of the most affected women, men, boys and girls in the Southeastern part of Zimbabwe. The expected outcomes are:
1. Men, women, boys and girls are able to cover their minimum basic needs (food and non-food)
2. Targeted households (HH) are able to bounce back and recover their livelihoods
3. The targeted households are able to prepare themselves for future shocks and/or for potential worsening of socioeconomic crisis
The intervention will target 2 districts including both urban and rural areas in Manicaland, Midlands, Matabeleland South or Masvingo in coordination with other stakeholders and based on ongoing field needs assessment.
The target groups will be poor urban and rural HH at risk of negative coping mechanisms. Special attention will be given to female, child and elderly-headed HHs and to HHs with high number of U5 children, PWD and chronic illness as more vulnerable to shocks. Targeting the participants to community-based groups will ensure that women, youth, PWDs and elderly are represented and equally benefit from the intervention. Women and youth will be specially targeted as agents of change to improve healthy behaviors, but men will be also engaged, as they are often the final decision-maker.
ACF and its partners will use flexible approaches to be adapted following the rapidly evolving context and in coordination with national and international stakeholders.
All activities will be refined based on beneficiaries’ main concerns and following the ongoing feasibility study outcomes.
Gender and age /Protection :
Due to the on-going situation (protracted drought and macroeconomic crisis) women, children, youth, elderly and persons living with disability or chronic illnesses are exposed to increased risks of mortality and morbidity and to negative coping mechanisms. Children suffers from poor diet (only 13% of children consumed minimum acceptable diets at national level according to 2019 ZimVac RLA), level of stunting prevalence reached the high prevalence threshold in some rural areas like Manicaland), jeopardizing the future development of the children. Adults are also affected and reduced the frequency or diversity of meals (only 23.8% of rural households had an adequate HDDS). With a drop in casual labor opportunities - main source of income in urban areas - and of agricultural livelihoods in rural areas, children, youth and adults have no choice than to resort to negative mechanisms. Those include migration (women and youth), begging (elderly, adults and children), sex work (women and children), interruption of medication (for HIV/AIDS, TB, hypertension, diabetes), early marriage (32% of women are married before 18 yo) and taking children out of school. With water shortage, children and women wait long hours at water points with higher risk of sexual harassment or violence. Cases of human trafficking, targeting women and children, are frequent in Zimbabwe, and it is likely that those will increase.
All ACF partners adhere to the do no harm principle and will implement preventive and protective measures. They will also ensure to know what do in case they face any protection issues – endangering particularly women, adolescents and children, people with disabilities or chronic illnesses and elderly – through a referral system with protection actors.

1 - Activities

For the urban areas : - UCT: to complement the WFP response where needs are uncovered. A feasibility study currently ongoing will identify the possible modalities in the fast evolving situation, considering the lack of liquidity, the limited capacity of the e-money service providers. The modalities being explored are e-mobile services, direct cash distribution or/and vouchers. The value will be based on the MFB updated in Dec.2019: 16.54 USD/individual- Saving and loan schemes (SLS): based on partners’ experience, SLS will be setup with 30 women groups, so they can purchase productive or economic assets and cope with the financial crisis. For rural areas: - MPCT: For the most vulnerable HH with limited access to the land, MPCT is planned to help them to cover their basic needs (including non-food) beyond the lean season, since the country will still rely on grains import and the HHs would not have recovered their livelihood. The MPCT will help the targeted HHs to pay for school fees, medical care or treatment and food depending on their priorities. MPCT will be based on the 62% of MEB updated in Dec.2019 (73.78 USD/HH)- Agricultural support: to help the HHs to cope with low crop season and to prepare themselves to next crop season through IK distribution or voucher system or fairs to access agricultural inputs for off-season agriculture (vegetable) and for the next season (maize, millet, sorghum). Voucher systems and fairs would have the advantage to link small farmers with suppliers (strengthening the market linkage). In addition, on-site training on best agricultural practices (including fight against FAW) and cooking demonstration will be provided. - Rapid and simple construction or rehabilitation of water schemes to ensure water for human, animal and agricultural use with significant training of water committees on maintenance and repair. - Support to VFNC for disaster risk management: in coordination with DFNC and WFNC, the intervention will support VFNC to conduct capacity and vulnerability analysis at community level and provide with equipment and tools to prepare for future shocks. For both urban and rural areas: - Awareness raising for healthy behaviors (nutrition practices, food diversity, hygiene, etc.): diet diversity is still an issue in urban areas, level of stunting is close or beyond the high WHO threshold in some areas (Manicaland urban: 27.8% rural 34.8% - ZimVac ULA& RLA 2019), feeding practices for mothers and children are key for the future cognitive development of the children. Hygiene also is an issue especially in poor areas, which have high risk of waterborne diseases’ outbreaks. Through behavioral change strategy, awareness sessions (mass campaign, care groups, etc.) are to be implemented with gender and age-specific groups (men, women and youth) to overcome the barriers to healthy practices.- Mass screening of U5 children: although the level of GAM is not high, the prevalence between 2018 and 2019 increased by 40% between 2018 (2.5%) and 2019 (3.8%) According to UNICEF, 98,112 U5 children are suffering from acute malnutrition. In addition, health services are not properly functioning as doctors/nurses are on strike (although they seem to slowly resume the work) disrupting the HIS. Thus, the invention will conduct mass screening during all “mass” activities.- Protection and feedback mechanism: Mapping of protection actors for the referral system. The selection process of beneficiaries will be explained to non-eligible persons through community leaders. ACF and its partners will pay special attention to PSEA through the setup of measures protecting women and girls from risks of survival sex when accessing services. To ensure people’s participation and for accountability and learning purposes, ACF and its partners will set up ways to collect feedback from beneficiaries (via VC, a hotline) in order to mitigate risks of violence/women and child abuses.
