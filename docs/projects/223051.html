Project Summary

Since 1992 Program for Helpless And Lagged Society (PHALS) has been working in livelihood and skill development sector for the disadvantaged and lagged communities’ in Cox’s Bazar with the support of the Government of Bangladesh, UN agencies and different national and international donors. PHALS has extensive experiences in the implementation of vocational training (Sewing, Tailoring, Handloom, Handicraft, Agriculture, Salt Processing etc.), cash for work, livestock and kind support (Sewing Machine distribution among the course graduate), linkage and sales and marketplace development. The activities implemented for the Forcibly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (Camp-8 Extension) and Host Community in Cox’s Bazar). Around half a million people were directly and indirectly involved with the livelihood/skills development program, benefited and develop their social status. Through different training and effective linkage, number of entrepreneurs and farmers increase their knowledge in daily activity operation. PHALS skill development program one of the signature program which includes computer training (Basic and fundamental using, software, hardware and programming) and Life skills to the community.
Currently PHALS has been implementing the livelihood, skill development and income generating program for the host community at Khurushkul and Chowfoldondi Unions in Sadar Upazila, Cox’s Bazar. The main objective of the project is to support climate migrants and vulnerable peoples through vocational training, market linkage, product selling as well as marketplace development in local market. PHALS maintained a strong relationship and effective coordination with RRRC, CIC, DC, UNO, local administration elected members and relevant other stakeholders. PHALS also the member of different network and committees in local, national and international level.
The goal of the proposed project is to “Enhance skills through vocational training for improve livelihood and well-being of Rohingya and Host community. The project will achieve outcomes: (1) Promote self-resilience and livelihoods intervention: this will include to empower the trainees on trade-wise vocational training, knowledge improvement, awareness on life-skill education and women-men participation. Outcome (2) Enhance skills and capacity building: The project will conduct vocational training programs and workshops that align with the market demands in the Rohingya and host area. By equipping refugees with relevant skills, the project will enable them to access better employment prospects and contribute to the local economy. Outcome (3) Income generation and family well-being: this will include production, sales, market linkage and improve regular income ensuring the family well-being and sustainability.
The project will develop local service providers from the FDMN and train on different selected trade (Off firm- Tailoring, Handicraft, Mobile servicing, Plumbing, solar/electricity, On firm-homestead gardening, and poultry). Post-training support and the effective market linkage will help the trained beneficiaries for self-resilient. The project will be created many cash-for-work opportunities by engaging them in different sector activities, tree plantation, small trading, day labourer and etc. This program will help to building sustainable livelihood which means the long-lasting improvement that will promote in community integration and social cohesion between Roahingya and Host community in Cox’s Bazar.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

The Multi-Sectorial Need Assessment (MSNA) was conducted by UN agencies coordinated by UNHCR to understand the situation and needs of Rohingya Refugees and Host communities in Cox’s Bazar. The key highlights of the assessments and initially proposed response activities are summarized by sector in this brief. Here are the needs of the Skills Development/Livelihoods/Environment sector:• There are limited livelihoods or cash-for-work opportunities for refugees. • There are limited skills development activities offered by local NGOs. • There is a lack of household income that is resulting in reduced food security and health outcomes. • There is limited access to land for agriculture. Considering the above-mentioned scenario, Program for Helpless And Lagged Society - PHALS is already working in Cox’s Bazar. PHALS has experience in conduction of different trade-base trainings for Rohingya and Host community. This initiative earned appreciation from RRRC, DC, UNO, CiC, and other actors working in Cox’s Bazxar. With the limited resources, PHJALS has been operating Handloom, Embroidery, Boutique-Block print, handicrafts, homestead gardening with a very clear intention of expanding the services, which subject to availability of donor support.The JRP project is a design based on the UN Inter-Agency Multi-Sector Needs Assessments. The major recommendations PHALS taken from this MSNA report are as follows:• In close cooperation with Government and local actors, support resilience and capacity development of refugees including adolescents, female-headed households, persons with disabilities and the elderly. • Provide access to skills development, vocational and life-skills training relevant to camp-based needs and to prepare for repatriation and reintegration in Myanmar. • Increase food security through household-level and community-based gardening and small poultry practices. • Provide cash assistance through various modalities including cash-for-work opportunities and multi-purpose cash assistance.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Aligned with the 2025 JRP Guidance Note, the project integrates protection mainstreaming by embedding safety, access, accountability, and empowerment in all activities, ensuring humanitarian support respects dignity and inclusivity, especially for vulnerable groups. The project will enhance beneficiary safety and dignity by conducting regular risk assessments to identify physical and psychosocial risks, implementing measures to minimize harm. Staffs are trained in safeguarding and psychosocial support, ensuring interactions uphold well-being. Safe spaces and support services are provided where needed.The project engages in community consultations to understand and address specific barriers faced by diverse groups, such as those based on age, gender, or disability to ensure equitable access. Information is provided in accessible formats and languages, and project sites are chosen for maximum inclusivity.Feedback and complaint mechanisms allow beneficiaries to share concerns confidentially, influencing project adjustments. Responses are communicated back to the community, fostering transparency and trust. Regular monitoring of feedback helps us continuously adapt based on beneficiary input.Community members are actively involved in project decision-making, fostering local ownership. Collaborating with local leaders and supporting community initiatives strengthens self-protection and skill development, empowering beneficiaries at each project stage.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

PHALS is committed to establishing and maintaining robust referral channels and responsive feedback mechanisms. By ensuring effective referral systems, PHALS will provide timely and appropriate services, linking affected individuals to specialized support across sectors. Feedback mechanisms such as suggestion/feedback boxes, hotline numbers, email, physical visits, and community meetings will empower beneficiaries to voice their concerns, provide input, and hold stakeholders accountable. Through continuous engagement with community members, PHALS will foster trust, encouraging active participation in project design, implementation, and evaluation.PHALS will provide training to its staff on AAP principles, CFRM, resource allocation for transparent communication, and integrate feedback into adaptive program strategies. Regular monitoring and reporting of AAP activities will ensure alignment with sectoral commitments, promoting equity, inclusion, and responsiveness to the needs of vulnerable groups. This comprehensive approach will uphold the dignity, protection, and empowerment of affected populations, contributing to sustainable and meaningful impactThe project strengthens Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) by establishing safe, confidential feedback mechanisms that enable community members to share concerns and suggestions. Additionally, the project will coordinate closely with local leaders to ensure culturally appropriate and respectful engagement with the community.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, the project promotes self-reliance and resilience among refugees by linking them to skill-building programs, income-generating activities and small-scale business support within the camps. Through vocational training and access to microgrants, refugees acquire practical skills and resources to pursue sustainable livelihoods. The project collaborates with local development agencies to create pathways for integrating refugee-led businesses into the wider market. These efforts not only enhance economic independence but also foster positive relations between refugees and host communities, contributing to long-term resilience and development.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

Yes, project activities in the host community align with Cox's Bazar's District Development and Growth Priorities (Dec 2022) and other district-level plans. The project focuses on sustainable livelihoods, skill development and social cohesion, which support the district's goals for economic growth, poverty reduction, and enhanced resilience. By promoting skills development, small enterprise growth, and income-generating activities, the project contributes to employment opportunities for local youth and vulnerable populations in Cox’s Bazar
