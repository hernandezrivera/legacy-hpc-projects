Project Summary

The project tackles three main pillars; providing quality and comprehensive remedial support to adolescents boys and girls at risk of school drop-out, rehabilitating conflict affected schools and lastly, supporting schools with emergency preparedness.
First, the project aims at decreasing the risk of Palestinian students’ of dropping out of school by enhancing their access to inclusive, safe and quality learning opportunities to improve their literacy, numeracy and socio-emotional learning (SEL) skills. The project will target 1,200 vulnerable boys and girls as direct beneficiaries in grades 7-9, ages 12-15 (age group at most risk of drop out), over 20 locations in the West Bank, spread through different governorates located in Areas C and H2, and East Jerusalem, in addition to the Gaza Strip. This component will also target children who have already dropped out of school. These children will be identified with schools, communities as well as the MoE/UNWRA and reached through national reading campaigns at schools and communities in an effort to bring them back to school.
The project will follow a community-based approach that builds on the national curriculum to design and implement interactive activities to improve adolescent’s literacy and numeracy skills; it will also focus on improving their social and emotional learning to ensure that the learning environment responds to their needs and that learning is interactive, relevant and memorable. The project will focus on targeting vulnerable students who are at risk of dropping out in close coordination with the Ministry of Education (MoE) and the UNRWA, as well as the principals of the targeted 20 schools. The project’s activities will be designed to enhance adolescents’ engagement in the learning process and motivate them to express their ideas, perceptions and understanding in Arabic, Math and SEL, while improving their critical thinking and analytical skills.
Secondly, the project will improve the school learning environment through minor rehabilitation of these 20 schools, focusing on infrastructure, adaptations and WASH. These improvements will ensure children and people with disabilities can adequately access schools, and that both boys and girls feel safe and protected in their learning environment, from external and internal threats.
Thirdly, the project will support these target schools and community’s emergency preparedness through establishing and building the capacity of school-community emergency committees, for example through risk mapping workshops. Finally this project will be closely coordinated with the MoE, in particular the Supervision and Building Departments, from the selection of schools to school preparedness. We will also coordinate closely with the Education cluster to maximize resources and avoid duplications.

1 - Needs

In 2022 we have seen a serious deterioration in the socio economic and political situation in the oPt, with education related incidents on the rise as per the latest education cluster report. The following are most critical needs in the sector in 2022 as identified by Save the Children and the Education Cluster: School dropout and poor learning outcomes:According to the MSNA 2022 (Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment) 19% of school children, ages 5-17, reportedly dropped out of school during the year 2021-2022 in the West Bank, where 13.6% is the drop out percentage among girls and 19.8% among boys. In Gaza Strip, the percentage of drop out among students of the same age is 5.7%. The reasons behind this percentage in the West Bank are various; the most influential are the protection and safety risks (settler attacks, demolition of schools, etc.), followed by school closures and the lack of interest respectively. In Gaza, the main driver is the poor economic condition of households; for girls, the main reason is the inability of families to cover for education expenses while for boys, the main reason for drop out is child labor to support their families. In the same assessment, 70.1% of households in the West Bank reported that there is an urgent need for catch up learning due to COVID-19 school closures, with H2 areas reporting the highest percentage (83.5%) followed by Area C (72.9%). In the West Bank, 88.9% of households perceived that children with mental or physical disabilities face additional challenges in accessing education, with bullying reported as the main challenge. A particular group at risk is girls with disabilities. According to 2018 UNICEF Country Report on Out-Of-School Children in the State of Palestine1, 36.6% of girls with disabilities between the ages of 10 and 15 are out-of-school compared to 26.3% of boys with disabilities. Humanity and Inclusion own program evidence confirms this finding: parents tend to prioritize the enrolment in the education of boys with disabilities over girls with disabilities. In terms of age, adolescents boys and girls are at a higher risk; according to Marsad’s study on “Drop-outs in Governmental Schools” conducted in 2020, drop-outs are higher at secondary (grades 1-7 drop out stands at 7%, while in in grades 7-9, it stands at 44% .) Save the Children has conducted consultations with the MoE over 2022 as part of our education program that have highlighted the urgent need for remedial education to mitigate the increased the risk of schools drop-out among adolescents. Our own program evidence confirms the findings of the MSNA. Students are performing worse in their literacy and math exams due to the reasons explained above (school closures, poverty, teachers strike and protection related incidents). It has also been observed that following Covid-19’s crisis, the communication and life-skills of adolescents have been deteriorating. In addition, Tamer Institute, who has a national network of school and community libraries spread all over the West Bank and Gaza in more than 70 locations, has observed that literacy, numeracy and life skills among adolescents students has decreased due to different reasons, the main of which was COVID-19 and the teachers’ massive strike that took place the last year. There has also been an increase in the number of boys and girls dropping out of school. For this project, Tamer has had consultation with the MoE in terms of mapping the schools where adolescents are at a higher risk of drop out or have dropped out. Damaged and unsafe school environment:Schools in the West Bank and Gaza continue to face longstanding serious infrastructure and material limitations due to demolitions, attacks and limited public investment. This continues to affect the overall learning and safety environment for children to learn effectively. For example, the Education Cannot Wait baseline report, revealed that more than half of all schools have no internet connection, 23% have no playgrounds, and around 150 schools serving around 30,000 children lack adequate water and sanitation facilities in the West Bank alone. UNDP’s EJ Schools’ survey revealed that almost 59% of schools in EJ have no playgrounds, libraries, or science labs. Around 40% of schools lack adequate functioning drinking water fountains. Children with Disabilities (CwD), are the most affected by the deteriorating situation. Furthermore, many of these schools are not adapted to children and people with disabilities, which limits their ability to attend school. Some of them lack latrines with locks with boys and girls. Therefore, there is an urgent need to conduct minor rehabilitation to schools to ensure they are safe and accessible to all boys and girls. Lack of school emergency preparedness:While since 2017 there is a national emergency preparedness plan, not all schools have developed their own, contextualized plan to help them identify and mitigate risks in and around schools. Many schools have developed these plans without consultations with teachers, children or communities. In other instances, however, school development committees are not in place and when they are, they are not always or aware of their emergency preparedness role. Given the threat of attack and demotions are on the rise, there is an urgent need to conduct a thorough and consultative risk mapping which considers the different risks for boys and girls with and without disabilities face in and on their way to school. Based on the risk mapping, we will support target school develop and/or enhance their emergency preparedness plans in consultation with children, teachers and communities.

2 - Activities

Remedial support to ensure adolescent boys and girls learn; In coordination with the MoE and UNRWA and our partner, SC will identify vulnerable children and public schools in the WB and Gaza that are struggling to perform effectively and are in need of remedial support. The project will provide a comprehensive remedial approach that focuses on educational, social and emotional skills to enhance the cognitive, behavioural and emotional developmental aspects of the adolescent boys and girls. Special attention will be given to adolescent boys and girls with disabilities given the higher risk of drop out. The remedial sessions will be structured weekly literacy, numeracy and SEL sessions to targeted students at the selected schools; the activities will include utilizing different tools like book discussions, drama theatre, shadow theatre, puppets making, creative writing, life-living simulations to boost adolescents’ literacy and numeracy learning skills. The activities will be various such as making theatrical shows presenting Arabic or math content, utilizing visual art (e.g. collage) to present geometrical concepts; adolescents will be engaged in developing podcasts on math and Arabic, which will require preparation, research to look for information to present for other Palestinian adolescents. The SEL curriculum is endorsed by MoE. The curriculum explores gender dynamics and how to prevent the use of violence. (lead by SCI in terms of the SEL and roll out by TAMER) Targeted support to out of school adolescents; To ensure we reach the most vulnerable and out of reach students, who have dropped out or struggling to learn, we will conduct outreach back to school campaigns with schools and communities. These campaigns are complementary to Tamer’s work with schools and the Palestinian community through its annual campaigns. The campaigns will allow the project to reach adolescents that are out of school through the community itself in order to bring them back to school. Through these campaigns, we will also decrease the likelihood of school dropout and improve school performance. These campaigns will specifically target children with disabilities and boys, both at a higher risk of drop out. These campaigns help identify out of school children and once identified and assessed, targeted support will be provided to return to school. (lead by TAMER) Community participation to support adolescents learning; We will be facilitating discussions between students, parent, teachers and communities through structured sessions to enhance adolescents’ academic performance.We will be conducting interactive learning sessions with groups of parents of the targeted students, in order to build their capacity in supporting their adolescent’s literacy, numeracy and SEL at home, ensuring a complementary role of providing a positive learning environment at home. WhatsApp groups will be established to share games, ideas, questions with parents; monthly sessions will be arranged for parents on different topics like utilizing art in learning, boosting life skills of adolescents, PDEP digital and media literacy. (lead by TAMER) Remedial activities during the summer holidays:During the summer break, remedial support will continue in the form of summer camps. Here students will work on producing art performances and outputs like geometrical shapes, Descartes space, Pythagoras Triangle through visual art, plays, and shadow theatre among others. The targeted adolescents, both boys and girls, and parents will join Tamer’s National Reading Campaign through the network of community and school libraries to boost their Arabic and Maths skills. (lead by TAMER) Emergency rehabilitation of critical school infrastructure; One of the first activities under this component will be the final selection of schools with MoE and the Education Cluster. This exercise will be done with the MoE’s Supervision Department and the Buildings Department to ensure there is no duplication. The project will provide essential and basic emergency rehabilitation support to schools in the WB and Gaza to ensure that school environments are accessible and safe for students and teachers of different abilities and are gender sensitive. Part of the assessment will include assessing the WASH facilities and repair them if needed. Examples include rehabilitating and ensuring there are separate bathrooms for boys and girls, well lit and with locks. School staff and children, as well as MoE, will actively participate in mapping out and identifying key risks, including areas where girls and boys might be at particular risk. The mapping will also look on how accessible the school facilities are for people and children with disabilities, for example ramps and rails, assistive devices or pedagogical aids. This will be followed with specialized engineering assessment, to highlight main interventions priorities within the intervention scope, with involvement of children and school staff to agree upon final interventions to improve school environment. The final selection of schools will be done with the MoE and the Education cluster to ensure there is no overlap. Support target school emergency preparedness:In order to better plan and respond to external and internal threats in and around schools, notably settler attacks, live ammunition or demolitions or internally, bullying, the project will facilitate the establishment of school emergency committees and the development or review of the school emergency plans to ensure they are comprehensive, inclusive of the different threats boys and girls with and without disabilities face in and around schools, with engagement from students, teachers and community members. SCI already established a number of community-based committees and will work through this funding to create linkages and connections between schools and community committees to provide sufficient preparedness planning and guarantee active participation.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

SC and Tamer will set up individual, project level feedback mechanism mechanisms in the target schools. Details of this complaint and feedback mechanisms are included in the project level MEAL plan which will be developed at the start of the action. This plan also includes measurement at activity level, called Quality Benchmarks (QBMs) reports, which are used on a regular basis by Save the Children MEAL staff during quarterly monitoring visits. QBMs include PESA questions to monitor the adherence to the SC Code of Conduct and Safeguarding policies where PSEA is included. In addition we will track progress of project indicators through our indicator performance tracking table (IPTT) and analyse the disaggregated data by sex, age and disability to understand who and how are they benefiting from the project and if gaps are identified, discuss with staff to take corrective actionSC pays special attention to ensure that beneficiaries are encouraged to raise any concerns and make use of their preferred communication channel. Therefore, the sensitizations sessions will be included before the delivery of key activities and in these sessions we will explain beneficiaries the objectives of the project and explain our feedback mechanisms to ensure they know how to share feedback. We will distribute project brochures during these sessions and stick these brochures in the target schools to ensure information is available throughout the intervention. In these brochures we also include information about SC Code of Conduct and information on PSEA and how to report it. To ensure children use the feedback and complaint mechanisms, we will deliver specific child friendly accountability sessions, where through art, storytelling we will encourage them to share feedback about the project.

Feedback will be received through field coordinators who will supervise implementation of activities, and logged in to our online accountability matrix, managed and analyzed by our MEAL staff. 10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Activities will take place at school and community spaces through cooperation with school principals, community members and town councils, who will ensure the best interest of adolescents’ remains at the heart of this intervention. We will prioritize the safety and dignity of beneficiaries by engaging them in activities, for example deciding on the schedule, feedback on the remedial education or the back to school campaigns or having beneficiaries identify the emergency risks in and around school. They will lead in identifying risks and mitigation actions that will then be captured in the emergency plans and lead by the school committees. Facilitators will be hired from the target area where possible so they understand the local context.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

We will ensure equal participation of boys and girls, men and women, as well as those with disabilities. For example during the beneficiary selection process, we will ensure there are same amount of men/women/boys and girls. In some cases we will prioritize one of the sexes, for example we know there are more out of school boys or boys struggling at school, likewise for children with disabilities, so more attention will be given to these two groups in terms of targeting in the remedial support component or the campaigns. Tamer will work with 2 organizations working with people with disabilities, who will support in the pre-assessment and targeting, as well as in outreach and networking with school principals and community leaders (town councils’ personnel). Indicators will be disaggregated y sex, age and disability to better understand if the project is benefiting boys, girls, women and men and those with disabilities in an equal manner. This ongoing analysis will enable us to adapt and course correct the intervention to ensure it is providing support in an equal and inclusive manner.
