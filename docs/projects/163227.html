Project Summary

The project on reducing the vulnerability of CAR refugees and IDPs has as objective to Improve resilience through accessing livelihood opportunities, sufficient access to energy for the population, support for the protection of natural resources and the environment, and promote the social cohesion of Central African refugees and host populations in the divisions of Kadei and Eboko Bomba. The project targets about 30000 women, men, girls and boys (including 16200 refugee and host communities’ youths and adolescent aged between 18 to 25 years as well as adults) refugees and IDPs in host communities with the support of local authorities.

Besoins / Needs

According to the Cameroon HNO for 2019, 65 - 80% of CAR refugees ( 52% women and 48% men) still depend on food distribution as they have lost their means of subsistence with limited employment opprtunities exacebated with high level of ethnic clashes and insecurity across the borders for those who depended on trade. Refugees and IDPs are forced to adapt their livelihood according to the new environment.The most vulnerable population who are women and children (58% of people below the age of 18, 42% of those between 18 - 54 years and 8% of those above 54 years) need support specifically to sustainably recover from the crisis and be more resilient to environmental, social and economic shocks which if not taken seriously will further lead to other challenges such as child marriage, sexual violence child labor in the mines, the prostitution of young girls in the zones of mining and timber and HIV aids in boys and girls because of the lack of opportunity forout-of-school youth and adolescents. The issue of access to livelihoods remains a major concern for all the refugees men, women, girls and boys consulted during the need assessment, regardless of their category or age group. Some activities aimed at building resilience to achieve self-reliance are conducted in the field, but only target a small number of refugees. The challenges differ depending on whether or not one is benefiting from the empowerment program developed by the partner in charge of this component. For those who are part of the empowerment program, the inputs provided by the partner on the chosen activity are often not in line with the initial demand of the parties concerned, distributions are unfair and sometimes not adequate.Those who do not benefit from the empowerment program continue to face the lack of capital for starting micro-projects, the lack or lack of skills training, the availability of agricultural inputs, tillage equipment and livestock. In both cases, these refugees in the working age group still face the limitation of their movement, the low access to paid employment, the difficulty of accessing agricultural areas and pastures to exercise farm and farm work, especially in developed sites and mining areas where pressures on resources are increased.With regard to young people, the observation is that most of them are idle with few job opportunities and limited access to vocational training, which does not allow them to meet their basic needs. Faced with these difficulties, they engage in activities that expose them to delinquency, sex for survival and abuse of any kind to be able to take charge. Women,Men, boys and girls expressed the need to carry out agricultural activities (community fields), to be included in apprenticeship programs for small trades and literacy, and to be supported in IGAs for petty trading.
