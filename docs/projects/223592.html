1 - Needs

The active non-international armed conflict in Nigeria continues as the Nigerian Government faces off against Boko Haram, Islamic State West Africa Province (ISWAP), and various non-state armed groups (NSAGs) in Nigeria’s northeast states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY). As a result of the conflict, over two million people left their homes and sought refuge as internally displaced persons (IDPs) in camps or camp-like settings and host communities across the BAY states. When the camps are closed and IDPs are forced to relocate to or near to their places of origin, the presence of explosive ordnance (EO) on roads poses a serious risk and, once the IDPs seek to recover their homes or grow subsistence crops, explosive ordnance denies safe access to agricultural land (food security), freedom of movement (protection), and is an obstacle to early recovery. The risk from EO is mitigated through the delivery of explosive ordnance risk education (EORE), ideally before the dispersal of IDPs from the camps. EORE is part of the broader mine action programme and goes beyond the communication of safety messaging.

2 - Activities

EORE will primarily be conducted in IDP camps, schools, child friendly spaces and in host communities. At the community level, implementing partners will engage in community outreach and coordination efforts to identify and collaborate with relevant stakeholders and actors. This approach aims to maximize the dissemination of EORE messages and enhance community confidence and trust in the activities. For example, in schools and other child friendly spaces, EORE will be delivered with the support of teachers who have previously benefited from the EORE training-of-trainers delivered by UNMAS. Out-of-school children and youths will also be targeted with EORE messages. The project will team up with other protection actors and local organizations (i.e. youth organizations, women’s organizations, and organizations of persons with disabilities) to organize sessions targeting different at-risk groups. The project will utilize appropriate and context specific methodology, improved from previous experience and use the revised EORE messaging that is gender, age and diversity inclusive. Methods of delivering the EORE will include community based face-to-face EORE using a variety of techniques, including songs, drama, puppet shows, and peer-to-peer EORE presentations. The average size of each audience group is between 15 and 20 people, to ensure a clear understanding of the message and the participation of everyone, as per IMAS standards. EORE information, education and communication materials will also be distributed after each session, to consolidate and increase its diffusion. The teams providing the EORE sessions will maintain gender balance to ensure diversity and will be able to communicate in local languages in an efficient and culturally sensitive manner. To ensure effective communication and comprehension of EORE messages, pre- and post- tests will be systematically conducted on a sample of EORE beneficiaries. These assessments will helpgauge the understanding and retention of the information delivered. Furthermore, the results of EORE activities, along with relevant data, meticulously recorded in the Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) database managed by UNMAS.During EORE sessions, victim and accidents data are systematically collected and subsequently registered into IMSMA. The implementing partner refers identified victims as requiring services to the relevant service providers. In addition, implementing partners will engage in, document and report on community liaison and perform community mapping activities to identify explosive hazards and/or hazardous areas. If a hazardous area is suspected or confirmed, the teams will report it using the appropriate IMSMA hazard forms. This will be then entered into IMSMA, creating a better understanding of the impact of explosive ordnance in the northeast.Information on the location of suspected items will be reported to the relevant actors that have the technical capacity for explosive ordnance disposal (i.e. the EOD police of the military). Teams may also mark the items when it is safe, considering conflict sensitivity concerns and the conflict's active nature.UNMAS will use radio messaging, which has already been developed and translated into six local languages, to reach people in need in hard-to-reach areas and areas of return following the 2024 planned phased camp closures. These messages will be disseminated through selected local and community radios chosen for their extensive reach, large listener base and strong community connections.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

In 2021 and 2022, UNMAS conducted a Knowledge, Attitude, Practices, Behaviour (KAPB) survey and explosive ordnance impact assessment in Borno State. In 2023, the KAPB survey was expanded to include the states of Adamawa and Yobe. At-risk affected local communities, through a sample of individuals, were involved during the assessments. Their involvement provided insight and deeper understanding of the most at-risk target groups, informed on their behaviour towards explosive ordnance, identified occupations at-risk and their beliefs. These enabled UNMAS and its partners to plan and tailor the EORE action accordingly. UNMAS has PEAS policies and feedback mechanisms in place. In the IPs selection process, UNMAS makes sure that also IPs have PEAS and feedback mechanisms in place. Moreover, updated messaging on marking and reporting have included conflict sensitivity concerns, the increased use of IEDs, the fear or risks of retaliation while reporting the presence of an EO or a suspected item to security and defence forces. As mitigation measures, the new messaging and visual toolsidentify as a safer reporting channel the reporting through local community leaders only. Community leaders, through their network and practice, are then able to safely relay the message to the appropriate actors for further action, butdirect reference to security and defence forces has been excluded from the new tools.

9.1.a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

Between February and April 2021, UNMAS conducted a survey aimed atassessing the Knowledge, Attitude Practices and Behavior towards explosive hazards among at-risk communities in Borno. Building on the outcome of the KAPB survey, in Feb 2023, UNMAS, through its implementing partners, expanded the KAPB survey to include Adamawa and Yobe. Interviews were conducted through focus group discussions,key informants and through household questionnaires. Through these, UNMAS adopted and used a combination of qualitative and quantitative data collection tools as well as use of secondary sources from literature that were reviewed.Questionnaires were distributed and were semi-structured and included close ended and open questions to allow for flexibility when answering. The population interviewed through the focus groups discussions and household groups includedmen and women aged between 18 and above, teenagers and students, including boys and girls between 11 and 17 years. Leaders such as Bulamas, religiousleaders, health center personnel, representatives of women’s group as well assecurity personnel, whose opinion shape the action within the local communities, were also included. This expanded KAPB survey and enabled UNMAS to tailor its EORE activities to the context and needs of the target population.
