Project Summary

The conflict that erupted in Tigray Regional State on November 4, 2020, has extended for more than one year and the impact of the conflict has recently spilled over to Amhara (North Wollo, South Wollo, North Gondar, South Gondar, Tegedie, and Waghimera, and North Showa zones) and Afar (Zone 2 and Zone 4). This has resulted in significant damage to the lives and livelihoods of the communities. According to the UNOCHA situation update report, on February.3, 2022, the situation in northern Ethiopia remains tense and volatile.
Due to continuing hostilities in the border areas of Tigray and Amhara, 12,000 IDPs (3,500 HHs) were displaced from Raya Alamata woreda in the Southern zone, Tigray region to Kobo woreda in North Wello zone, Amhara region which has increased the humanitarian need in North and South Wollo. Even though the situation now looks relatively calm in the majority of the zones, 17 woredas and 304 kebeles are highly affected by the conflict as reported by the UNOCHA update on Feb. 3, 2022. As the Es/NFI sub-cluster gap analysis has shown, there are 1,990 fully damaged houses and a gap of 10,393 for NFI kits in Habru woreda alone, with a total gap in the zone for 154,500 HHs’ who need NFIs. The main priority for assistance is shelter and NFIs. The damage to shelter and NFI can still increase, since there are still some areas inaccessible, and conflict undergoing in border areas of the North Wollo zone. As the Amhara scheme damage report shows, more than 89 schemes were damaged due to the conflict. The living conditions for both recently displaced people and the host community remain extremely critical and require immediate support, more specifically in the areas of shelter/NFI, agriculture/food, WASH, and protection.
The overall objective of the proposed project is to address the emergency basic needs of the conflict-affected displaced, returnees, and host communities through shelter repair kits and NFI, Agriculture and WASH assistance.
The Shelter/NFI project will reach the most vulnerable conflict-affected individuals and households. The goal is closely aligned with the 2022 shelter/settlement strategic objectives and key activities. ZOA will target North wolo zone (Gubalafto woreda - NFI highland V1- 686 HHs and repair kit-highland V2- 686 HHs) individuals, and the South wolo zone Sayint woreda- 840HHs NFI highland V1 and 839HHs repair kit- V2 in the Amhara region. the response will include disabilities through providing Non-food Items (NFI V1) Kit Disable Highlands. of this 10% (339 HHs) of the total beneficiaries will benefit disability groups with from Non-food Items (NFI V1) Kit Disable Highlands.
The proposed intervention will address cross-cutting themes through activities such as awareness raising on protection and gender mainstreaming and the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) in collaboration with organizations to reinforce referral systems in the project sites. Moreover, through the establishment of strong Complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFRM), target beneficiaries concerns and issues will be addressed in time and in a safe manner. Our beneficiary targeting approach will encourage the active participation of women, girls and other marginalized groups.
Furthermore, this intervention will be complemented with WASH and Food security and livelihoods activities to address the most urgent needs of the conflict-affected returnee and IDP communities in Tigray region. The intervention also includes a capacity strengthening element in both the WASH and Food Security and Livelihoods sectors. ZOA will provide some resources, as well as training to relevant government stakeholders, to capacitate them to fulfill their roles as duty bearers and service providers towards their constituencies.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

ZOA has strengthened compliant feedback mechanisms (CFRM) in all areas at all levels. ZOA is committed to Accountability to Affected populations. This is manifested in the Accountability Framework of the Organization which clearly lays out the Complaints and feedback reporting and handling procedures. The complaint and feedback response mechanism will be established in consultation with the target community considering their level of literacy and access to the various mechanisms especially for women, girls and disabled community members. Once the mechanism is agreed, Complaints and Feedback Response systems that are safe and confidential will be established. The MEAL officer with the support of Programme Area Manager will take the lead in the establishment and operationalization process. Complaints will be addressed at the field level in a timely manner by relevant staff in keeping with the confidentiality commitment . Our Code of Conduct (CoC) will be shared with project beneficiaries during information sharing sessions to ensure target communities are aware of expected behavior of ZOA staff who are also trained on the CoC. Complaints on the breach of the CoC and other complaints that demand the attention of Country office senior management will be escalated using our internal complaints reporting mechanisms guided by our Integrity Framework. The CoC clearly indicates that there is Zero-tolerance for Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Any SEA case will be reported directly to the Country and Headquarters Integrity Coordinators for immediate investigation. Hence, it is guaranteed that beneficiaries complaints will be properly handled at all levels in adherence with appropriate age, gender, safety and confidentiality considerations.
