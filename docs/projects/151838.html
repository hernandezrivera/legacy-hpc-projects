Project Summary

Northeast Nigeria is facing a landmine crisis as a result of the ongoing conflict between non-state armed groups and the Nigerian Army. According to MAG research carried out in April 2018, 144 accidents involving landmines and unexploded bombs between January 2016 and March 2018 have caused 439 casualties. Casualty figures reached 19 people per month in 2017, with thirty percent of civilians killed known to be children. Nigeria now has one of the ten highest casualty rates in the world.
Through the provision of Mine Risk Education sessions to internally displaced persons and host communities in Borno State, and the collection of information on the level of contamination, MAG proposes to continue its life saving mission started in June 2017. The proposed project aims to reach 130,000 beneficiaries through 6,500 MRE sessions. MAG's approach aims to have an equal number of boys and girls attending the sessions, including 20% of caregivers. Furthermore, MAG will provide risk education for 200 humanitarians who are exposed to the risks of contaminated land while supporting the response to the on-going humanitarian crisis.

Needs

MAG has collated data on incidents involving explosive items from different sources, such as INSO and UNDSS, and analysed it to better understand the situation. Based on data covering the period from 1 January 2016 to 31 March 2018, the database includes 285 incident reports and 439 victims (including both killed and injured). 33% of the victims (144) were civilian, while 67% (295) were military. Of the 144 civilian casualties, 30% (43) were confirmed to have been children, while for many no information on age is available. Of the 43 children identified as victims, gender information is available for 49% (21) casualties; 42% of total child victims were boys and 7% were girls. According to MAG’s incident database, the five LGAs with the highest number of incidents, Bama (59), Gwoza (49), Konduga (36), Dikwa (22) and Damboa (20) are all part of Borno State and include those close to Sambisa Forest, the stronghold of Boko Haram until recently. Gujba (18) is an LGA in the south-west of Yobe State bordering Borno (Damboa and Kaga LGAs). Ngala (12) and Kala/Balge (7) are bordering Cameroon. LGAs in Adamawa State (Numan, Hong, Madagali and Michika) register three or fewer incidents each. UNHCR also reports that 39% of households in Dikwa, Ngala and Munguno LGAs in Borno State, have witnessed or heard reports of mines or unexploded devices around their current displacement sites. In addition to improvised items deployed by Boko Haram, contamination from conventional weapons (mortars, projectiles, grenades etc.) is also likely to be found in these areas, as a result of clashes between government and coalition forces. MAG has found through its ongoing activities that on average, only 32% of conflict affected communities can identify a mine or ERW before receiving RE sessions, indicating the low level of knowledge of the threat they are exposed to.

Activities

Activity 1 - Updating and procurement of Risk Education materials. These materials will be used during Risk Education sessions, including for children, and include banners, posters, and leaflets, in addition to child specific RE materials such as flipcharts, maze games, picture and colouring books. For the broader community, MAG will use permanent billboards. For humanitarian workers, materials used consist of MAG’s Explosive Hazard Awareness Aide Memoire. Activity 2 - Selection of target areas and beneficiaries for RE sessions. Building on experience, MAG plans to target 19 LGAs in Borno State with RE sessions as well as conduct non-technical survey (NTS). These LGAs are AskiraUba, Bama, Biu, Chibok, Damboa, Dikwa, Gubio, Guzamala, Gwoza, Jere, Kaga, Konduga, Kukawa, Magumeri, Maiduguri, Mobbar, Monguno, Ngala and Nganzai. Activity 3 - Facilitation of RE sessions for 130,000 conflict affected individuals.CL teams will be deployed to IDP camps, host communities, and schools across Borno State to hold 6,500 RE sessions to reach 130,000 beneficiaries (80% children and 20% caregivers). Each CL team will facilitate at least two sessions a day on average. These individuals are accessed through schools, child friendly spaces and community focal points including LGA Chairmen, Wards Heads, IDP Camp Chairmen, Bulama (and other local leaders), and civil society organizations. Activity 4 - RE sessions provided to 200 humanitarian staff.MAG will provide scheduled mine and ERW safety briefings for interested agencies at regular intervals throughout the project period. Using established coordination networks amongst humanitarian actors such as sector working groups, the HCT and INGO Forum and government bodies such as SEMA for NNGOs, MAG will effectively ensure that actors are aware of the available safety sessions for their staff. Activity 5 - CL monitoring plan.MAG's Community Liaison Manager will closely monitor the progress of the CL teams. Specifically, the monitoring plan will include weekly field visits and quarterly refresher training. Furthermore, MAG employs tools such as surveys to identify changes in RE beneficiary knowledge, immediately before and after RE activities. For MAG, it is vital that impact-monitoring activities remain gender sensitive, taking into account the different effects of contamination on women, girls, boys and men. Activity 6 - Non- Technical Survey (NTS).NTS is practically undertaken by the CL teams using a ‘Dangerous Area’ form. The information collected in these forms is later analyzed. MAG’s NTS activities support wider planning processes by identifying the location, scale and type of contamination that is present, providing data on the impact this contamination is having on local populations. NTS requires access to the location that is surveyed. MAG's ability to carry out this activity will be determined by the accessibility of potentially contaminated areas. Activity 7 - Carry out remote baseline assessments.In areas that are inaccessible, and therefore unsuitable to carry out NTS, MAG will gather information on contamination using focus group discussions among IDPs having fled from the same area and by triangulating information that they give on contamination they have encountered before or while fleeing from their former place of residence. This information will be recorded and used as a baseline for NTS once these areas become accessible. Activity 8 - Analysis of collected data.All the information gathered by CL teams will be analyzed by MAG and will be used to populate the database. Activity 9 - Publish map of identified/suspected contamination.Using the data collected through NTS and remote baseline assessments, MAG will publish a map at the end of the project, showing the contamination as identified or suspected. This map will be shared with humanitarian actors and government stakeholders and will help plan humanitarian and government activities in North East Nigeria.
