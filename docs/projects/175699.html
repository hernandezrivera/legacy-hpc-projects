Project Summary

South Sudan continues to grapple with multiple shocks that exacerbate existing acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition. In 2020 alone, communities were hard hit by multiple shocks ranging from the intensified conflict and sub-national violence, locust invasion, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. (HNO 2021). Notably, 7.24M (60% of the population) people will be facing severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+) and in need of urgent humanitarian intervention and close monitoring. (IPC projection April – July 2021)
With the hope of a hunger free South Sudan and lowering the IPC, RCO seeks to provide Agricultural support to populations affected by crisis in Jonglei & WBeG states. The support will be delivered through provision of main and seasonal crop kits and agricultural tools to food insecure and displaced households and with an additional input of either vegetable or ﬁshing kits depending on the suitability of the additional inputs to speciﬁc locations, formation of farmers’ producer groups and formation of fisher folk groups.
The project will target 12,000 HHs (72,000 beneficiaries) vulnerable populations by building their resilience and restoring their assets that were depleted by drought, floods, or conflicts. This will be done through supporting the identified beneficiaries with conditional cash-for assets activities, including cash for restocking. Target group will also be supported with agricultural inputs including Crop and Vegetable kits and tools taking into consideration the seasonal calendar activities whilst riverine beneficiaries will be supported with fishing kits. RCO will aslo promote the use of improved agriculture technologies through the distribution nutritious dense vegetable kits that are early-maturing, high yielding, drought-tolerant & disease tolerant crop varieties to gain short term results coupled by capacity training on improved agronomic practices and ﬁsh preservation/handling techniques and training of farmers on land –harvest, storage and market linkages that will develop communities’ knowledge and skills that will improve food access, increase and build their resilience and future self-reliance by creating and expanding delivery models that support community empowerment and accountability. This will further strengthen their capacity to produce enough food for household consumption, mitigate irreversible coping mechanisms and build their resilience to withstand future shocks and meet their long-term needs.
RCO is cognizant of the various gender challenges in the context and will duly target specific groups particularly identified as being vulnerable such as IDP households headed by women and children. In addition, an inclusive community-driven approach will be applied to ensure that women and men are involved in the planning, selecting, implementation, and approaches of programmes, especially on decisions of the selection of cash programming schemes, taking into consideration the different obligations, challenges, time constraints and priorities of women and men across the seasonal calendar.
Lastly, RCO will integrate and synergize multi sectoral response by coordinating with counterpart agencies implementing Protection, Health, WASH, Nutrition, WFP GFDs to ensure effectiveness and referrals of unmet objectives in addition to mainstreaming cross-cutting programmatic approaches that include application of AAP principles, COVID-19, protection including GBV/ Sexual exploitation mitigation measures, Do No Harm, conﬂict context analysis, gender equity, HIV/AIDS awareness and prevention, environmental conservation and sustainability.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan continues to spread and became more complex as a result persisted insecurity, cattle-raiding, high food prices, ﬂood related destruction of crops & livestock and disruptions to market access and now COVID-19 pandemic. (IPC Oct 2020 – July 2021). The persistent communal ﬁghting and spats of tit for tat revenge killings in Jonglei state have forced people to ﬂee their homes, many of them multiple times. The compounding effects of widespread violence and sustained economic decline have further diminished the capacity of people to face threats to their health, safety and livelihoods. With 7.24M (60% of the population) people facing severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3+) and clearly identiﬁed as likely be at least one phase worse without current humanitarian support. (IPC projection April – Jul 2021).In Jonglei, the situation continued to deteriorate for the better part of 2020. A range of compounding natural, man-made, and macroeconomic shocks have resulted in mass displacement rendering communities to limited access to crops, livestock, and markets. These shocks have simultaneously eroded the capacity of the already vulnerable and food insecure households to employ coping strategies, exacerbating already widespread, severe, and multisectoral vulnerabilities. The 2020 harvest is unlikely to result in any substantial or long-term improvement in the food security situation and, moving forward, humanitarian food assistance will be essential to offset a continued and widespread deterioration. (South Sudan Food Security Outlook June 2020 to January 2021).This frequent displacement witnessed in Jonglei state coupled by the massive floods have undermined already compromised agricultural production with floods submerging crops and granaries as well as drowning livestock of the already vulnerable communities and causing food shortages. These have forced the affected populations to adopt to negative coping strategies by reducing meals, going without food, surviving on wild fruits or selling their last livestock which has led to food consumption gaps, reduced dietary diversity and increased household hunger scores. (REACH_SSD_Situation-overview-Jonglei-state-2020). WBeG state has not been spared either, the situation there has been compounded by a soaring inﬂation, communal conﬂicts, prevalent cattle raiding incidents, below average harvests due to recent Fall Army Worm, prevalent and fresh outbreaks of livestock diseases such as Foot and Mouth Disease. (FSNMS Round 24, HNO 2021). These events continue to negatively impact food security and livelihood options among the most vulnerable populations in WBeG state with a signiﬁcant proportion of populations at risk of sliding into IPC Phase 4 and beyond (June 2020 FEWSNET Food Security Outlook). The aggregate cereal harvested in WBeG in 2020 was about 9.1 percent below the previous year’s level, currently most of the HHs have depleted their food stocks and they are completely dependent on aid and market purchases. The contraction in the harvested is attributed to by the high cost of ploughing with A plough this a single one-acre plough costing about SSP 50 000 which is not affordable by most farmers compared to SSP 35 000 in the previous years. (FAO_SSD-Crop-Watch-June-2020). Needless to say the prices are expected to rise in 2021 and persist at extremely high levels, above those observed in 2020. Concern is highest for the many HH in need who do not have access to assistance. It is expected some HHs will exhaust the capacity to cope during the outlook period and be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) as hunger and malnutrition have escalated on an unrelenting course with about 1.5 M children under age 5 estimated to be acutely malnourished. Food insecurity is expected to be more severe during 2021 as conﬂict persists & macroeconomic conditions further deteriorate. This calls for immediate and unhindered humanitarian support and close monitoring.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The project has considered market functionality through conducting assessments, including market and seasonality analysis. The cash will be provided based on the minimum expenditure basket, ensuring that while the beneficiaries' purchasing power is increased they will be able to get access to livelihoods from the existing markets. The results of the seasonality analysis will inform the appropriate time of the year to implement the interventions, to ensure that the markets function as normal and beneficiaries are able to access goods and services from the markets. Besides, the amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market. The cash amounts distributed with be guided and based on the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) per region of interventions, as defined by the Cash Working Group and FSL cluster. During the implementation of the project, the team will liaise with other stakeholders to monitor the price behaviour and supplies for commodities to inform the effects the cash intervention is having on the local economy. Decisions will be made appropriately to ensure the cash is used for the right purpose, with no or little effect on the market functionality.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of cash enhances the decision-making and dignity of the bene\0ficiary as they can make an independent choice, with the \0flexibility provided by cash, to purchase what they need. While cash enhances the benefi\0ciaries' income level, it also contributes to strengthening local market functionality with the injection of the cash into the local markets. Further, cash transfer is quicker and more cost-ef\0icient than providing in-kind commodities, in addition to the items purchased by households being culturally appropriate and suit their spefici\0c needs.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

RCO has internal mechanisms in place to provide beneficiaries information on PSEA during project implementation. This is achieved through periodic PSEA sensitization to all staffs and volunteers after which they all sign the PSEA policy as a commitment to adhere to it. During project implementation, RCO will place hkey contact information they can confidentially and confidently report. All staffs are expected to sensitize the beneficiaries on SEA in order for them to understand the adverse effects SEA may have on them and to the organization and also to help them know their rights and entitlement of service delivery especially during beneficiary selection for the distribution of FSL inputs. Child-headed HH, women-headed HH, the elderly, PLW and people living with disabilities will be prioritized and identified as primary beneficiaries to ensure their dignity and safety is guaranteed including advocacy against Gender Based Violence/ Sexual Exploitation and Abuse.The beneficiaries will also be provided with information on how to report cases of SEA whenever it arises through our established complaint mechanisms in the areas of operations. Notably, PSEA information will be shared to the beneficiaries at the inception of the project to ensure they understand it and can give feedback and make complaints.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

RCO will be flexible with its mechanism models to accommodate the people with special needs and those whose access to feedback and complaint mechanism is limited. For instance, the beneficiaries with various disabilities will be visited at households’ level and communicated to in their local languages to ensure that their ideas are heard and considered during an assessment at the time of intervention. For the illiterate, RCO will encourage use of local language so that every person understands and contributes in the decision making. Equal consideration and participation during all sessions will be a priority so that the minority and vulnerable group can feel to be part of the group. The project will avoid, minimize or reduce any unintended negative consequences or impact of their assistance or interventions are committed to a ‘do no harm’ approach to programming. The project team in consultation with community leaders will engage in regular discussions with the most vulnerable groups to receive their feedback on all aspects of the project. RCO will ensure equal access of the most vulnerable including persons with disability older people to tangible assistance and implementation, all the implementing staff member will be aware of their specific needs, sex and age sensitivities within the targeted community. The project locations, to and from where the project is implemented will be safe and accessible to everybody. The provision of assistance will not put the beneficiaries at any kind of risk but reduce the threats (Do No Harm) and will ensure vulnerable and those who cannot go through usual procedures will be given equal chances. The project will further ensure that vulnerable groups and individuals are able to access services equally without any discrimination and service prevision process does not generate any Sexual Exploitation and Abuse against girls, boys, women or men. The feedback complaint mechanisms will be easy to use and impartially accessible by all including the marginalized and those who are illiterate.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

RCO has in the past and will be transparent with the beneficiaries by providing sufficient information; guidance and support that will enable them understand and influence key decisions in the project including consulting with the beneficiaries to enquire on how they want to communicate (feedback, complaints, general inquiries) with the organization before putting in place a feedback and complaints mechanism/desk. RCO will form AAP committee that will conduct monthly focus group discussions and community meetings and participatory activities to share information and solicit feedback from beneficiaries about the support they receive, to generate useful information for quick decision-making. The Project will ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members, through: - Surveys and FGDs with specific age groups, women, men, and potentially vulnerable or marginalized youth (e.g. with disabilities) will be conducted on a regular basis. Monthly FGDs will be conducted with children and communities to assess protection knowledge, attitudes, and practices; including focused FGDs with girls and young women with the participatory assessment. Moreover, RCO has established clear guidelines and practices on engaging the target population by ensuring that the most vulnerable in hard-to-reach areas are represented with full participation and influence. All the beneficiaries and other key stakeholders including the local leaders will be directly involved throughout the entire project cycle. The beneficiaries will be taught on their roles and responsibilities before the launch of the project and they will have an opportunity to ask questions and get clarifications concerning the project. In addition, RCO will establish a multi-layered feedback mechanism which include a complaint desk, suggestion box and providing the primary contact number which will give the beneficiaries a multiple avenue to channel their concerns and complaints. RCO has been doing this so that the community gets timely information which enhances involvement and sense of ownership of the projects.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

RCO as in the past and will be transparent with the beneficiaries by providing sufficient information; guidance and support that will enable them understand and influence key decisions in the project including consulting with the beneficiaries to enquire on how they want to communicate (feedback, complaints, general inquiries) with the organization before putting in place a feedback and complaints mechanism/desk. Moreover, RCO has established clear guidelines and practices on engaging the target population by ensuring that the most vulnerable in hard-to-reach areas are represented with full participation and influence. Beneficiaries’ feedback especially during project inception and assessment visits will be an integral part during the project development and implementation as it will provide a platform to accommodate new ideas and different perspectives and improve programming. In doing so, RCO will have an opportunity to demonstrate its accountability to the community and ensure that the project reflects local needs, has local support and does not have any adverse unintended consequences.
