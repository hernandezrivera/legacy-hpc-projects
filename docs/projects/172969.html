Project Summary

The project aims at continuing and strengthening IOM’s protection and MHPSS response in north-east Nigeria through the following areas of intervention:
1. IOM’s MHPSS activities will consist of services to populations in need as per standard service package and access to safe spaces, which act as resource centers to the community host IOM’s MHPSS activities, including lay counselling, basic emotional support, life-skills development, language classes, and socio-relational and cultural activities. Small-scale livelihood activities shall be integrated with mental health and psychosocial support to vulnerable groups.
IOM will create and ensure pathways to prevent further family separationand facilitate safety referrals to other
services and MHPSS teams will provide Psychological rst Aid (PFA) to the new arrivals, identify the ones in need of further support, link and guarantee safety referrals to other sectors. IOM will continue to enhance accountability to the affected population by strengthening Protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and feedback mechanisms.
IOM will, in close partnership with the MAPTIP, continue to build the capacity of counter tra cking stakeholders with the intent to localize and increase ownership of the counter tra cking response in north-east Nigeria. IOM will a;sp seek to improve prevention and response services targeting at risk sub-groups of the crisis affected population and victims of tracking.
2. To support GBV survivors consenting to access services in camp and camp-like settings, IOM’s Psychosocial Mobile Teams will provide the following array of services: survivors of GBV will be provided with PFA (Level 1 and 3 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid) in an effort to cover their acute needs; targeted survivors of GBV will be provided with structured psychosocial counselling, either as a stand-alone service or to complement other specialized services (Level 3 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid); With their consent, survivors of GBV will be referred to specialized services for case management and dedicated follow-up (Level 4 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid); Families of GBV survivors will be provided structured psychosocial counselling and family support sessions (Level 3 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid). IOM will ensure that safety, confidentiality, respect, and non-discrimination principles are adhered to, to protect the rights and dignity of survivors and promote resilience ..
3. To mitigate the risk of GBV and other protection threats in camp and camp-like settings,

IOM psychosocial Mobile Teams will conduct sensitization and awareness campaigns on GBV and protection threats in the targeted camp and camp-like settings (Level 1 and 2 of IASC MHPSS Intervention Pyramid). Campaigns will be tailored to the contextual needs of the community and take into account age and gender categories as well as socio-cultural, ethnic and religious aspects. Moreover, campaigns will be designed in participation with the community community, ensuring the participation of persons facing heightened vulnerabilities and more at risk of being excluded.
IOM will jointly work closely with the IDP safety committees to conduct safety audits and safety mapping to identify and highlight protection and safety concerns within the camp, disseminate protection focused messages, and increase awareness on protection risks.
4. IOM will build the capacities of humanitarian actors, incl. IOM staff, in the areas of MHPSS and Protection, with the overall aim of mainstreaming protection principles in programming and strengthen GBV risk mitigation. IOM will also prioritize strengthening capacity of the community-based structures such as women committees and IDP safety committees.

1 - Needs

The crisis in north-east Nigeria has impacted women, men, girls and boys, and people with special needs differently, and their vulnerabilities, as well as coping mechanisms, vary. Displaced populations face grave protection risks and abuse such as exploitation, family separation, restrictions on freedom of movement and insecurity. Vulnerabilities of the crisis affected population are exacerbated by pre-existing and emergency induced problems faced by the affected population, notably for the more vulnerable sub-groups of the population, including the elderly and chronically ill, persons with disabilities, female and child-headed households, unaccompanied and/or separated children, adolescent boys and girls, pregnant and/or lactating women and people returning from captivity. The population displaced due to the conflict is facing additional stressors created by the displacement itself. Stress factors include family separation, diﬃculties and sometimes denial of access to services, limited opportunities to livelihood, breakdown of family and community support mechanisms, potential stigmatization, secondary and repeated displacement. The above factors impact the conflict affected population’s mental health and psychosocial wellbeing. Vulnerable people, particularly female-headed households and unmarried women and girls in inaccessible areas are at higher risk of GBV. Further risks relate to the lack of access to safety measures (including safe energy options) and protective livelihood opportunities. Furthermore, widespread intimate partner violence, including physical assault, denial of resources and psychosocial and emotional abuse, have been exacerbated as a result of the conflict and economic decline (HNO 2021).Human trafficking by armed groups manifests itself in brutal forms such as the use of children in combat roles, the sexual enslavement of women and children, forced marriage to combatants of Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs), suicide bombers and other ancillary activities such as cooking, cleaning and farming. While in captivity, survivors report being subjected to rape and forced marriage amongst other types of GBV, which occurred in the context of possible sexual slavery and sexual exploitation.. Various data sources reveal that Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities in captivity are being exploited in several forms including, but not limited to, forced labour, forced begging, organ removal, sexual exploitation and forced marriage. Victims who are released or escape from these trafficking situations suffer from stigma and possible rejection from the community upon their release as well as insufficient attention, care and support. Meeting their medical, psychosocial and other needs is a significant undertaking needing intensified and targeted support. Failure to do so may result in re-trafficking. However, the limited response to TiP in northeast Nigeria makes it difficult to provide comprehensive support to victims of human trafficking.As COVID-19 intensifies in Nigeria, it is expected that vulnerabilities of women and girls, men and boys will increase with the outbreak of COVID-19, including GBV and other protection concerns. The potential low or loss of household income may have a longer-term economic impact on women compared to men and may increase the risk of exploitation and sexual violence (Nigeria GBVSS COVID-19 guidance note). Awareness raising activities represent an opportunity to include joint messaging and an occasion for MHPSS actors to provide psychological first aid (PFA) adaptation to the COVID-19 to alleviate the stress and anxiety resulting from the situation, mental health and psychosocial consequences of COVID-19 and dissemination of information on positive coping strategies and address social stigma.

2 - Activities

1. Deployment of male and female psychosocial mobile team members in project locations including hard to reach locations in Yobe, Borno and Adamawa states.2. Provide culturally appropriate mental health and psychosocial support activities targeting women, girls, men and boys across all the four layers of the pyramid of intervention. These include; provision of PFA, recreational activities, informal education for the youth, sensitization and awareness raising, remote lay counselling for Victims of Trafficking (VOT) and GBV survivors and specialized services. 3. Provision of remote counselling to the distressed population including GBV survivors, VOTs, care givers, vulnerable children and families to strengthen outreach approach while adhering to COVID-19 guidelines.4. Provision of small-scale livelihood support (such as vocational skills training and start up kits) integrated into MHPSS approach and human development to promote their psychosocial wellbeing and increase self-reliance among the women and men.5. Provision of an integrated Counter Traﬃcking in Crisis response through provision of direct victim assistance and capacity building for relevant actors in collaboration National Agency for the Prosecution of Traﬃcking in Persons.6. Develop responses to address the immediate needs of GBV survivors/women and girls formerly associated with armed groups & forces/victims of traﬃcking and make appropriate referrals to access immediate emergency response.7. Strengthen community-based activities in providing training to community-based women/men committees and organize structured discussion with adolescents’ girls and boys, women and men including persons living with disability within existing or establishment of new safe spaces.8. Conduct outreach activities and dissemination of protection focused messages including PSEA, how to report risk, traﬃcking in person and access to care targeting conflict affected population.9. Organize community dialogues and consultations with religious, traditional leaders, community members including marginalized population, community-based structures, youth, and women organizations to positively change perceptions of community on GBV norms.10. Provide protection training (MHPSS, GBV, PSEA and protection) for relevant partners and stakeholder including local authorities and committees on their roles and responsibilities, GBV concepts, how to handle a disclosure and facilitate safe and confidential referrals of GBV survivors. 11. Facilitate training sessions for staff and implementing partners (social workers, case workers and MHPSS teams) on protocols, procedures, GBV prevention and response including GBV case management, care for child survivors (CCS), child safe guarding incorporated, PSEA and mental health and psychosocial support. 12. Strengthening access to specialized services for children, prevent family separation and facilitate family tracing and reunification13. Conduct roll out trainings for the Nigerian Police Force and Nigerian Civil Defence Corps on GBV, TiP prevention, response and community engagement using the already existing training manuals.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Through this project IOM will prioritize engaging its staff and other humanitarian actors including government agencies through awareness raising and trainings on PSEA and child safe guarding incorporated in all other training sessions. Additionally, IOM will support in reinforcing two main aspects towards a prevention model: 1- Through dissemination of harmonize GBV and PSEA harmonized messages to the community and ensuring inclusion of vulnerable groups.; 2- Support in strengthening existing pathways for any referral activities to ensure appropriate access, observing do not harm principles and appropriateness of the response. Further special training will be provided to all staffs working at the PSEA community based-complaint and feedback mechanisms (CBCM), which can be entry points for reporting, on how to respond to disclosure, facilitate safe and confidential referrals, this will be done in coordination with the PSEA Network. IOM will ensure PSEA posters printed and put in place in offices and community level as well as disseminate PSEA key messages are disseminated to community members including information on reporting channels.
