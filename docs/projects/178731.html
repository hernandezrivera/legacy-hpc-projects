Project Summary

In 2020 ZOA implemented projects in multiple sectors including NFI, reaching a total of 35,081 beneficiaries. In 2020 ZOA was able to secure $4,439,160 in funding, of which $1,331,000 was SHF funding. In 2021 ZOA has to date secured $3,888,000 with around 2 million USD secured for NFI interventions. The cost per beneficiary is estimated to be $68.
Currently, ZOA is distributing around 4,000 NFI kits in Quneitra Governorate. The price for each NFI kit is about $150. For the winterization planned activities the kits estimation prices are as follows: Winterizationa kits - $140 (Jackets, boots etc.), SOK (Light) - $ 40 and heaters (multifunctional) – Although it is in kind, but the collected prices from the markets range is $48-$135.
ZOA is proposing winterization activities to reach households susceptible to the harsh winter conditions in underserved conflict affected communities in Rural Damascus, Aleppo and Quneitra. Based on community based needs assessments and sector guidance, ZOA will target the most vulnerable households with an emphasis on those in areas with high severity of needs in the target locations of Rural Damascus, Aleppo and Quneitra. Proposed activities are aimed at minimising vulnerable households’ exposure to harsh weather conditions, negative coping mechanisms as well as health concerns especially in light of the COVID-19 outbreak. Interventions will ensure that households susceptible to the harsh winter conditions are well equipped with the necessary resources to survive the winter period. Proposed activities based on needs, market assessments, sector guidance and beneficiary preference include the distribution of heaters, vouchers for heating fuel (coal), provision of commodity based vouchers for sealing-off kits and winterization kits and/or MPC, the modality will be decided once the locations are finalised and based on the rapid market assessment. ZOA has wide experience in CVA activities worldwide and in Syria. We also have prepared a full risk analysis for CVA activities. The assistance will be prioritised for newly displaced vulnerable families (including people displaced multiple times), people living in sub-standard shelters and spontaneous returnees. Assistance will be prioritised to the most vulnerable groups that include female-headed households, persons with disabilities and the elderly. Assistance is targeted at about 7,000 households.
The proposal is aligned with Sectoral Objective 1 which is to provide life-saving and life sustaining shelter and non-food items (NFIs) support.
All the targeted sub-districts have the severity score 3-4 and this is according to the HNO 2021 Severity Mastersheet. Apart, during the past months ZOA has implemented different projects in the same subdistricts and done assessments, therefore we are aware of the needs in the targeted areas. The number of the targeted beneficiaries are also identified based on these assessments, sector needs as well as feedbacks collected from different ZOA projects.

4 - Needs

NFI support during winter is vital for coping with harsh conditions. ZOA’s past experience and assessments show the varying NFI needs by gender and age, and the need for clothing and shoes has been highlighted as a primary concern for girls and boys. HNO 2021 records increase in PiN for the NFI and also confirms the importance of winterization considering similar interventions as emergency, “Access to NFIs is seen as lifesaving, especially during winter when vulnerable families are unable to access basic items to keep them warm, such as heating fuel, blankets and winter clothes. Despite the improved stability, the overall NFI PiN has increased by 37 per cent from 2020 to 4.69 million PiN in 2021. Of this, 2.70 million people (57 per cent) are residing in areas of severity 3 or higher with a concentration in 73 out of 270 sub-districts. According to the latest assessment, 90 per cent of families report diminished access to NFI’s with unaffordability being the main constraint” (HNO 2021 p. 79). It is expected that during 2021 and especially under the economic downfall and COVID-19 impact, access to NFI will be more difficult for the people in need. “Ongoing economic crisis compounded by COVID-19, will continue to negatively impact household resilience through all areas, further reducing families’ ability to pay rent, address shelter issues, meet their basic needs…. This would further exacerbate the need for shelter and NFI support as living conditions of the families is expected to continue to worsen. Emergency shelter and NFI support will continue to be a vital life-saving assistance while further support to improve shelter conditions will continue to be vital” (HNO 2021 p. 81).

5 - Implementation of activities

For optimum effectiveness & efficiency, the proposed winter intervention will use a combination of modalities- commodity vouchers for personal insulation, heating fuel (coal), in-kind distributions of coal heaters and sealing-off kits, the MPC has also taken into consideration depending on the location. A total of 7,000 HH are targeted and will receive commodity-based vouchers for winter clothing and coal. The seasonal voucher assistance is for vulnerable families to access core relief items to meet their additional needs during winter months. The commodity vouchers are exchanged for a fixed quantity & quality of specified goods participating vendors at local markets. The vouchers restrict & specify the assistance received. The commodity vouchers will be distributed in the form of paper vouchers, a low-tech, readily-available delivery mechanism. Each HH will receive a one-off NFI commodity-based voucher for personal insulation distributed in November; & a one-off distribution of a coal heater (in-kind) also distributed in November. Additionally, 2 fuel commodity-based vouchers (for coal) per HH will be distributed during the months of Nov. & Dec., (and Jan-Feb 2021). Assistance will be prioritised to the most vulnerable groups that include female-headed households, persons with disabilities & the elderly. Monitoring is the responsibility of the country based Manager of Programme Quality, supervising MEAL officers in-country. The collected data will be sex & age disaggregated. Quantitative indicators will measure quantities or amounts & can be expressed as numbers whilst qualitative indicators will measure people’s judgments or perceptions & will capture constraints & the perceived quality of any aspect of the project.

R1: Tension in community, for perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism, including risk for project staff. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation measures: 1. Establish adequate selection criteria mechanisms based on vulnerabilities and tools made available by the NFI sector; 2. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to establish targeting criteria that are as harmonized as possible throughout protection service providers; 3. Consult with actors on the ground through operational coordination mechanisms to ensure that selected beneficiaries to which ZOA/partners just provides three months of assistance can be covered through other actors for the remaining months; 4 Consistent M&E and feedback mechanisms that contribute to project development/ changes. R2: Selection criteria influenced leading to project not accessing most vulnerable. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: 1. Clear complaint and feedback mechanisms; 2. Ongoing engagement with community leaders and clear community communication; 3. Humanitarian standards, accountability and PSEA training for staff. R3: Physical safety of beneficiaries and risk of exploitation/abuse Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigating measures: 1. Liaison with communities and local stakeholders to develop detailed implementation plans; 2. Project coordination, sharing and seeking lessons learned with other actors; 3. Amending project plans based on identified protection vulnerabilities, such as disabilities, age and gender; 4. Regular staff training; 5. Raising awareness for all beneficiaries on complaint mechanisms.
