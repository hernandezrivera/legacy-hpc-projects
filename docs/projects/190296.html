Project Summary

The proposal is in line with the HRP 2022 project module designed to prevent further deterioration of food security and protect livelihoods of vulnerable households who has been the worst drought since four decades after loss three consecutive rain seasons. ADRA will implement under this proposal three major livelihoods in four Woredas (Adadle, Gode, Kelafo and Mustahil) of Shebele Zone Somali Region.
1- Provision of Emergency Agricultural Input (locally adoptable Maize seeds and Sudan grass seeds for fodder production with fuel for irrigation and necessary Farm tools for Total population of121,799 people in (Adadle 34350 people), (Gode 24,950 people), (Kelafo 39,975people), and in (Mustahil 22,524 people).
2- Provision of emergency Animal health intervention Livestock treatment, CAHWs training, Voucher system implementation, livestock disease surveillance Livestock Vaccination campaign for Total population of 81,636 people in (Adadle 2,746 people), (Gode 24,566 people), (Kelafo 39,974 people), and (Mustahil 14,350 people).
3- Provision of Emergency Livestock feed intervention Seed for fodder production and Concentrated Animal Feed provision for a Total People of 128,713 people in (Adadle 54,258 people), (Gode 25,140 people), (Kelafo 34,975 people), and (Mustahil 14,350 People).
The project will target where ADRA has a permanent presence implementing Humanitarian Livelihoods long term programs while responding to the recurrently arising humanitarian emergencies in the target area. This is in line with the recent document of the HRP 2022 location and activities prioritization Guide for the Clusters.
To achieve this objective, ADRA will focus lifesaving interventions for 7 months with possible Cluster decision of flexibility to adjust in case of new life threatening crises occurs. To ensure smooth implementation and complementarity ADRA will closely coordinate with the DRM-ATF Cluster, Federal, Regional and zonal authorities, and other operational partners in the targeted communities as the needs are massive and will require concerted efforts. Continuous monitoring will be undertaken in the implementation period to ensure all emerging trends are captured and reported to stakeholders. Community participation will be incorporated during the project design with women and children needs emphasis who mostly form a great percentage of the displaced populations. This approach will continue in the project implementation and lessons learnt incorporated in future responses.
In addition, ADRA will establish a protection committees involving men, women, a community leader, Religious leaders, a representative from the local authority and the police aimed to raise awareness on sexual violence and its impacts in the community; connect GBV and HIV; denounce any abuses of human rights; and share knowledge on how people could protect themselves.
Humanitarian crises increase the risk that GBV will occur—compounding the challenges already faced by people. Girls and women are disproportionally affected by Livelihoods and Food security issues that arise during humanitarian emergencies. Forced to travel long distances to Aid Items distribution points with existing sanitation facilities often lacking privacy, lighting, or locks—they are at increased risk of sexual assault and harassment. Livelihoods and Food security scarcity can create tension in the home and heighten the possibility of domestic violence. And a shortage of culturally appropriate and accessible NFIs/Dignity Kits supplies, services and other support materials can increase all of these risks. ADRA’s proposed emergency Livelihoods and Food security NFI/Dignity Kits intervention will be integrated with gender-based violence (GBV) protection, mitigation and reduction activities, that will play a critical role in enhancing the safety and well-being of those most vulnerable people affected by humanitarian crises.
