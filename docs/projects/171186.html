Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to improving children’s access to quality education in selected schools in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem. The selected schools were prioritized by the Ministry of Education and NRC, in the most vulnerable areas in Area C including East Jerusalem. The proposed project is in line with the MoE Strategy and Plan to guarantee children’s right to education, including the return to school plan, due the COVID- 19 strategy. The project- fits under a multi-year NRC education intervention of integrating psychosocial support in formal education, and supporting the MoE distance education efforts. The project aims to target 66 most vulnerable schools to support children through: i) Provision of psycho-educational “Better Learning Program” (BLP), NRC PSS flagship programme institutionalization process, by training a new cohort of BLP Master Trainers, General Directors and subject supervisors; and capacity building of teaching and non-teaching staff in MoE schools; ii) Provision of emergency kits to the 66 schools; awareness sessions for parents on PSS and mental health; and provision of a “Supporting the Supporter” package for teaching and Non-teaching staff that are in need; and iii) Strengthen the capacities of teachers and supervisors to carry out remedial/catch up classes for children that are not active in virtual classes and who are stuck in disrupted academic functioning, including by providing tablets to specific group the most vulnerable children; printing self-learning materials; and rehabilitate the schools’ ICT infrastructure. The distance learning component, will be implemented in full coordination with the Ministry of Education, at national, directorate and school level, to have a clear criteria on ICT equipment distribution, the use of tablets, including the different programmes and content to be utilized. NRC will also distribute the equipment to teaching and non-teaching staff in coordination with the MoE. NRC will develop clear procedures and SOP with the MoE on how to distribute and use the ICT equipment for teaching, non-teaching staff and students.
School facilities attempt to relieve pressure of running blended education while facing an acute shortage of qualified teachers, therefore capacity building will be tailored per school, and include the Better Learning Programme, following the International Network of Education in Emergencies (INEE) criteria and standards.

1 - Needs

Fifty-three years of occupation and thirteen years of siege imposed on Gaza have led to increasingly disintegration of the Palestinian territory and of its people. In particular, the right to education was significantly hampered by clashes in and around schools, search and arrest operations, restrictions of access to schools, settler violence related incidents against schools, arrest and intimidation of school children and teachers. In a study (Ann Strømmesen, 2018) carried out in four most vulnerable schools, it was found that almost half of the students expressed challenges with social relationships and/or communication, and finding difficulties in talking to others. Stress and anger constituted the largest emotional subgroups while another characteristic found was the feeling of lack of knowledge, resulting in students feeling helpless. The protracted conflict has long lasting negative effects on the psychosocial well-being of children and their caregivers. Teachers and counselors often lack the skills and response methodology to identify these children and respond in an appropriate manner. During 2020, and the COVID- 19 outbreak in Palestine evidenced the weakness of the Education system, and the lack of leadership at school level to ensure the continuation of the education services. In April 2020, NRC conducted a multi-sector rapid assessment through phone survey with 760 individuals split equally across Gaza and the West Bank. The rapid assessment aimed at gaining a better understanding of the context due to the impact of COVID-19 restrictions on vulnerable households. NRC conducted the rapid assessment with a representative sample of its own beneficiaries across Gaza & WB at household level. The key findings of the rapid assessment show that the negative impact on households is significant and that continuity of learning is severely impacted. The analysis of the data showed that 72% of interviewed households have school-aged children at home of which 55% have at least one child not following any kind of education system. There is an expected increase in inequality among children, with the most vulnerable at increased risk of dropout or reduced academic achievement. As per Cluster information and through NRC’s long-standing presence in the sector and continuous interaction with local educational institutions and authorities; there exists a need for a clearer and improved technical capacity and leadership in designing and rolling-out the distance-learning scheme, including catch-up classes for those at high risk of dropout. Support is particularly required in regards to content, pedagogical approach as well as tools and materials for education professionals and students. At household level, material support (ICT) to families with school-aged children who have limited access to distance learning possibilities is a must. As per the aforementioned NRC rapid assessment, 45% of school-age children have access to distance education, being the PA education system followed in the West Bank and 30% of the consulted households confirmed attendance. The remaining 55% are completely excluded from any distance learning programme. NRC's direct work and communications with more than 65 schools all along the West Bank, will guide the implementation of this project. NRC has experience in the most excluded areas of the West Bank has shown the challenges that many families face to follow the education system include lack of ICT equipment. The need for ICT equipment is greater now with the spread of C-19 and closure of schools/depending on distance learning. In addition, the teachers require support to lead the distance education process, including psychosocial support.

2 - Activities

NRC has in place a MoU with the MoEHE for collaboration and engagement across the West Bank and Gaza. A Framework Agreement will be drafted between NRC and MoEHE outlining an overall commitment to ensure the adequate implementation of the current programme. The activities are clustered in two main results: (i) Continue providing Psychosocial support at all levels, by teachers and counsellors at school level: a. Vulnerability/need assessment at school level and selection/confirmation of targeted schools. b. Grant opening meeting with schools, directorates and MoE key staff. c. Define and implement the training package for each school and directorate on BLP 1, BLP 2 and BLP 3. The training package is based on a minimum package, but tailored per each school. d. Define and implement the training package for Supervisor and key staff in the Ministry of Education, to ensure the continuation of the programme. e. Identify basic supplies, in partnership with the MoE and Directorates, to ensure the schools have basic equipment and supplies to implement the PSS activities. f. Support supervisors, teaching and non-teaching staff to implement BLP 1, BLP 2 and BLP 3 at school level. g. Provision of remedial learning opportunities to children at risk of school drop-out.h. Awareness raising to enhance children access to quality learning opportunities. (ii) Support school teaching and non-teaching staff to have a more inclusive and child-centered education:a. Identify the specific needs at school level (pedagogical gaps and needs) b. Support trainings at school and directorate level on distance and inclusive education, following basic INEE and MoE recommendationc. Develop a community engagement component, in order to improve the quality of the educationd. Support the adoption of blended plans at school level, ensuring the continuity between in-person and distance educatione. Define with the MoE the SOP on ICT equipment distribution and use, including the definition of the educational programmes to be installed in the ICT equipment. f. New SOPs will be defined based in the existing cooperation between NRC and the MoE, as well as the experience developed by other partners. During the C- 19 Emergency, NRC worked very close with the MoE to define and discuss different ways and approaches to reach the most vulnerable students. The SOPs will take that experience as well as other partners’ experiences. g. In close dialogue with the MoE, Directorates and Schools, NRC will identify the most excluded and at risk children, and ensure they have access to distance education, with tablets and self-learning materials. These criteria will be alignedwith the list MoE and Cluster list of Most Vulnerable schools, and will be discussed with key partners to avoid duplications. h. students of catch up and support classes, will be selected by each school following basic criteria discussed and agreed first between NRC and the MoE, including at national, directorate and school level. The criteria will prioritize those at risk of drop out or already out of school. i. Support teaching and non-teaching staff in the implementation of the blended educational programme. j. Distribution of learning, teaching and PSS material

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

NRC recognises that safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults engaged in their programmes is a fundamental responsibility. All staff must sign and are trained on NRC’s Code of Conduct, and are now required to sign and be trained on the organisation’s new Child Safeguarding Policy. The training for Education staff and staff with regular contact with children includes content on child rights, understanding child abuse and sexual abuse, reviews behaviour and conduct required from staff and how to receive disclosures and report and refer cases. BLP content is also designed to promote safe and inclusive learning environments for children and should support staff and teachers’ understanding of child protection. NRC Action trusts working on incorporating protection principles and prompting meaningful access, safety, and dignity in the proposed humanitarian aid. At the very beginning of the action in the needs assessment phase, the NRC will identify the people at risk, kind of risks they face and expected to face, and why they face such kind of risks, as well as activating a structured referral system who people who seek more help and support. The NRC action follows the participatory approach, where beneficiaries will be included from the early beginning of the action, participating in the needs assessment, monitoring and evaluation phase, and in identifying the area of improvement. Moreover, all beneficiaries will have the same chance of receiving the service (e.g. school children will receive the service at a voluntary manner and with a respected informed consent from their parents). Both teachers, and counselors as a main beneficiaries in NRC action will participate in a voluntary manner, and due to their interest. A follow up mechanism will be shared with all NRC suggested beneficiaries, where open channels of communication will be offered by NRC (e.g. Generic emails, field visits….etc) with all beneficiaries that guarantee the confidentiality.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The NRC action focuses on three dimensions of intervention: i) a general class room based intervention approach targeted at all children in target schools, its suitable for all children in any context, as its designed to reach larger numbers of children through classroom delivery. This dimension is designed to ensure and guarantee the equal chances of access for all children both males and females and their specific needs. ii) The second dimension offers a special kind of support to children who need additional support, to support resilience a mong them for the early recovery or protracted displacement when conditions for learning have stabilised. iii) The third dimension is a specialised, clinical one designed to address nightmares, which many children experience as a chronic symptoms of traumatic stress. The equal chances of both males and females, boys and girls, special need or without special need children are affordable based on the real needs, beneficiaries interest and potential. As well as without any kind of discrepancies among the beneficiaries either they are children or adult. The NRC action welcomes all children being part of the service delivered regardless of the differences among them based on inclusion approach.For the inclusive education component, catch up classes, the project will target the most excluded children (boys and girls) at school level. The project team will work at directorate and school level, to define the criteria for those children involved and benefited by the project.
