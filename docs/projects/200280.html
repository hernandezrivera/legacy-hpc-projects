Project Summary

For the past 3 years Lebanon has been grappling with a multifaceted socio-economic crisis that has left its mark on the different communities living in the country, affecting different populations while increasing violence, popular anger, needs and vulnerabilities. This crisis has further heightened the pre-existing vulnerabilities in Akkar, a rural, conservative area with difficult socio-economic conditions (originally), limited access to services, and low exposure to education and opportunities for women and adolescent girls. Akkar, one of the biggest poverty pockets in the country, with exacerbated vulnerabilities by the impact of the Syrian crisis, records at the moment the highest percentages of households living in multidimensional poverty (92%) and extreme multidimensional poverty (51.5%). The household economy analysis for Akkar and Baalbek conducted in 2021 by Save the Children had also found “that poor and upper poor families in these governorates show a Livelihood Protection deficit – that is, the inability to meet the basic cost of living to afford education, healthcare, and nutritious food...As they minimize spending further, their coping strategies are becoming increasingly crisis-oriented and irreversible.”
These conditions are driving more households to adopt a survival mode. The field team has reported witnessing decrease in food intake, child labor, early marriage….Adding to the above, a high level of mistrust in institutions and an increased feeling of aid bias along with a relatively easy access to weapons adds to the context in Akkar another conflictual dimension. It is worth noting that Akkar, home to a large population of civil servants and members of security apparel, has been slowly but steadily sinking into a state of a generalized acute poverty, drastically increasing the category of vulnerable population to include an exponentially large portion of the Lebanese community at this point. In this context the most at risk, and most affected are women, adolescents, children, PWD and elderly along with other marginalized groups still facing numerous barriers to accessing quality services.
This calls for a comprehensive intervention (combining prevention & response) in the area to provide quality services for women and adolescent girls while working with the communities to reject negative behavior, enticing them towards positive approaches and reduce risks of GBV.
AND will focus on the following components:
• Provision of comprehensive package of quality services for women and adolescent girls at risk and/or survivors of violence through mobile ( integrated in the community/ local CBO, community center....) and static safe spaces which include the following:
- Information sessions & PSEA dedicated sessions.
- Child care services for children coming to the safe spaces with their caregivers
- Referrals for children, adolescent girls and women to other service providers including health, youth and child protection services (AND is keen on adapting an integrated approach).
- Focused PSS curricula for adolescent girls and women
- Non-Focused PSS
- Case management services
- Mental Health support
- Legal support
- Lifeskills workshops
- Distribution of 4100 dignity kits to most vulnerable survivors including persons with limited access to resources as a result of increased poverty.
• Capacity development of rights holders, duty bearers and overall community mobilization
- Implementation of GBV risk mitigation activities
- Implementing risk mitigation tools (safety audits/GBV risk assessment – community mapping – community safety planning / different tools used between women and girls),
- Capacity building of community outreach workers, and community focal points
- Community-based committees
- Community referrals
- Sensitization of women and girls on GBV, including early marriage
- Mobilization through Social Behavioral Change activities

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

AND will rely on its static and mobile safe spaces ( within a community center, a local CBO...) to provide prevention and response activities. These activities include psychosocial support (FPSS and NFPSS), implementation of the I am here I am stronger approach, provision of information sessions, sessions on various protection and empowerment topics chosen from the “Facts for Life” booklet, and skills building sessions/workshops. These sessions, to be provided in static and mobile safe spaces, will also provide advice on mental health stigmas, symptoms of anxiety and depression, and parenting guidance, as they would serve as discussion fora for the participants on the above-mentioned topics. The services provided in the safe spaces also include case management for 150 women and adolescent girls along with individual psychological consultations legal awareness sessions and legal representation when needed in addition to SRH sensitization and provision of dignity kits.In an effort to enhance women's safety and agency, AND is looking into having GBV part of the daily overall discussion in the communities by conducting a set of community-based activities. Accordingly, AND shall be working with the scout groups, its community focal points, local authorities (when possible), and youth volunteers to initiate discussions about women's role and rights, concept of masculinity... through trainings, puppet show, community theatre, outdoor cinema, usage of the caregivers' toolbox...

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

AND is an equal opportunities employer, that places a strong value on diversity and inclusivity. We promote a culture of cooperation and opportunity for all, with zero tolerance for discrimination, harassment, sexual exploitation or any type of gender-based violence.In terms of prevention and response, AND conducted a series of PSEA trainings, in addition of a reporting framework to provide a guide to handling allegations of SEA, while including PSEA key messages in the beginning of all its community interventions ( all sessions/ workshops...), with the provision of specific PSEA sessions. AND commits to gender equality, throughout its projects design and implementation, to overcome and/or mitigate impact of structural barriers and unequal power relations, including all discriminatory practices: provision of transportation, and child care services in the safe spaces are one example. AND has been also working with its team on disability inclusion and intersectionality.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

For AND, the priority is exchanging information and communication channels between affected communities and service providers.It is important to always ensure that the people receiving support are heard, involved in the identification of their needs, and aware of all information related to the services being provided to them, with clear referral pathways, and with an access to effective feedback channels and complaints and response mechanisms (including comments, suggestions, and complaints).In addition, communication and transparency is important for effective AAP. To facilitate communication and dialogue between AND, and all community groups, information shall be shared in languages, formats, and and media that are culturally appropriate for, and accessible to, all groups in the community.A complaint mechanism is available along with hotline numbers and PSEA dedicated hotline number. AND will be using the feedback and complaint system linked to a hotline and referral system.
