Project Summary

NRC ICLA will be implementing activities across 20 camps and 5 host community unions ensuring protection and gender mainstreaming. ICLA will work with the Camp-in Charge’s, UNHCR and other actors in camps to secure identity and documentation of Rohingya refugees through existing registration and documentation guidelines. NRC will also work with Ukhiya and Teknaf government authorities e.g. UNO office, UP office to host communities. The project is designed to positively increase access to identity/civil documentation (mainly Family Attestation Certificate, Smart Card, birth, death, marriage, and divorce certificates), documents required for accessing essential services, and HLP including tenancy rights in the refugee camps and host communities, whilst also mitigating and addressing tensions between the Rohingya and local communities generated by HLP disputes. This objective will be achieved through the increased capacity of refugees and host communities to exercise/claim their identity and HLP rights and improved access to and use of collaborative dispute resolution mechanisms in communities. NRC will provide legal counselling and assistance based on individual needs to enable target beneficiaries to overcome legal, procedural, and documentation challenges, including referral to specialized case management actors as it may be required; combined with information dissemination and awareness-raising initiatives to empower target beneficiaries to claim their rights and access available justice mechanisms. Improvement of the relationships between refugees and host communities, and within the Rohingya community itself, will be achieved through the local leaders` capacity building in HLP dispute resolution. Key staff, volunteers, and other stakeholders, including target communities whenever possible, will be involved to identify existing and emerging threats to safety, dignity and rights faced by different sex and age groups among the refugee and the host populations. Special attention will be paid to threats and vulnerabilities generated or exacerbated by COVID-19 and the associated containment measures. This information will be analyzed and used for evidence-based advocacy and will also be shared externally with other protection actors and stakeholders.
ICLA will arrange information session, individual support, and counselling assisting refugees in obtaining and updating their civil documentation. These supports contributing to beneficiaries’ access to civil justice and other forms of humanitarian assistance, as well as better exercising of their rights. For both civil documentation and HLP, ICLA’s using general information sessions to raise community awareness of rights and to generate interest and awareness of the need for civil documentation and tenure security. Information sessions typically last 45-60 minutes and are open to all camp residents and are also conducted for vulnerable host community members. To address the widespread issue of refugees paying mahjis and others to complete their written applications, ICLA will supporting by the field staff to review refugee applications, assist beneficiaries in gathering supporting paperwork and witnesses, completing the written application, and submitting the completed package to the CiC office.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The proposed response is informed by an analysis of needs and gaps identified in NRC`s Information, Counselling, Legal Assistance (ICLA) Programme`s field experience. Overall findings on identity documentation and HLP in the Refugee camps and host communities: Both refugee and host communities, women in particular, are not aware of the application procedures for identity documents and the potential benefits of each document; Vital events (marriage, divorce, death, birth) registration practices vary between camps, with CiCs having varying capacities and understandings of this aspect of their roles; There is a lack of knowledge about how to obtain / correct errors / amend the “Smart Cards”, Family Attestation Cards, WFP Scope cards, and register vital events; Due to the low literacy level in general, and in Bangla language in particular, especially amongst women and elderly persons, most Rohingya are unable to complete the applications to CiC required to register marriage or divorce, or to amend the “Smart Card”, Family Attestation Card and WFP Scope Card. Individuals who are unable to complete the application forms pay Mahjis or other camp leaders to complete the application for them; Forgery, bribing of officials and marriages to Bangladeshi nationals are widespread strategies adopted by Roningya trying to obtain Bangladeshi NID. Local people living in rural / remote areas are having limited access to citizen services, including availing NID, marriage registration and inclusion in the updated voter list; With the resumption of birth registration for Bangladeshis living in Cox`s Bazar District, there is a lack of detailed information about how to apply for the birth certificate and/or how to check the birth certificate in the online Birth Registration Information System (BRIS), as well as on how to correct errors in the birth certificate; Rohingya face significant barriers to accessing formal justice system; There is an overall high level of reliance on informal justice. At the same time the quality of informal justice not aligning with basic human rights and principles, women’s interests not represented, lack of female representation in informal justice bodies; there are risk of corruption and power abuse; Many refugees, particularly those residing in the Teknaf camps, are engaged in tenancy relationships with host community landlords and face rent extortion and threats of forced eviction; On many occasions the land rented out to refugees and I/NGOs by local population is classified as a forest land, which is prohibited under the national legal framework for any form of settlement / development; Already existing tensions between Rohingya refugees and the host community have further increased because of the reduced means of livelihoods, as well as narrative that refugees are receiving most of the humanitarian aid. In the targeted areas ICLA will be using protection-sensitive vulnerability targeting. The individuals with highest risk of exposure to harm, limited access to services, and lowest ability to cope are among following categories: single women headed households, youth (female in particular), elderly and people with disabilities/limited mobility, and LGBTI persons. Most of the HH specially, women and those who have no income have to sell kinds to pay the rent monthly due to the fear of eviction from the landlord. As most of the rohingya beneficiaries have no income so paying rent is extra burden for them. On the other hand, as they have no written document with the landlord, whenever, the rohingya comunnity extend any kind of portion, they have to pay money for that individual portion. NRC ICLA advocating the issue with the CXB PWG and CICs with camps. Though the formal agreement not possible between RC and HC, NRC ICLA will continue informal lease agreement.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

NRC ICLA will work closely with the CiC office, site management, and protection focal points so that services can be tailored to reflect camp needs, in order to avoid duplications and create synergies, including the continuous development of efficient and effective referral mechanisms. In addition, NRC`s presence and provision of services would enable the organization to understand the protection risks, needs and gaps in services` provision in areas where it works. NRC ICLA coordinates the legal protection interventions with PWG, and its activities are reflected in the sectors information portal (4/5W). NRC is firm believer in integrated programming, and already integrates programmes across its sectors (WASH, Shelter, education, and legal protection). Furthermore, NRC has been helping other sector (SNFI, SMDS, WASH) mainstream HLP in their programmes through capacity building and technical support including conducting HLP due diligence for all constructions/land involving interventions. To strengthen social cohesion, and peaceful co-existence, NRC dedicates 25 -30% of its assistance to vulnerable host community members. NRC understands the importance of predictability, and continuity of its operations, and is fundraising for multiyear programmes. NRC’s current institutional donors have already expressed interest to continue funding, and managed to attract a multiyear funding from German federal foreign office which will continue until 2024. In addition to this, NRC has an exit strategy in place that includes transfer of materials, knowledge and skill to local civil society organizations that NRC works with.

3.2 - Gender

All NRC ICLA activities and reports will include sex, age and disability disaggregated data (SADDD). NRC understands the gender impacts, different needs and vulnerabilities of women, men, boys and girls. NRC acknowledges that women and girls (who account for 55% of Rohingya papulation) face particular socio-cultural barriers that restrict their freedom of movement, and access to and control over resources. The conservative culture of the Rohingyas places restrictions on girl’s mobility upon reaching the age of puberty in what is termed as Purdah practice which dictates that women shouldn’t be seen by men other than their husbands, brothers and fathers. This cuts off unmarried young girls from participating in most aspects of public life, including accessing education or employment opportunities. 3.4 - Diversity

Present situation of the response have specific risks for refugee women, girls, and persons with specific needs (PSN) along persons with disabilities (PWD), religious minority groups and gender diverse populations, continued to face heightened barriers in accessing services and information. Meaningful participation of women and female youth volunteers was challenging given their limited access due to the culture. NRC ICLA will ensure to address the issue by inclusion of the diverse groups.
