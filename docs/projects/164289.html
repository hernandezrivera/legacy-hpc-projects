Project Summary

Management coordination is essential to enable UNRWA to deliver timely and effective humanitarian assistance. The project consists of three main components: management and monitoring of humanitarian response, safety and security, and repair and maintenance of UNRWA premises in the new accessible areas. In April 2019, UNRWA received authorization to start carrying out works to its installations in camps previously not accessible (Dera’a and EinElTal camps).
One of UNRWA’s main priorities in 2020 is to resume its service in the new accessible areas to better respond to the needs of Palestine Refugees (PR) and ensure capacity in place to provide the service particularly in light of the spontaneous returns of IDPs and Refugees to camps and gathering areas. Contrary to the principle of the inviolability of UN premises, UNRWA facilities in Syria have suffered extensive damage throughout the conﬂict. Many PR camps have seen major hostilities, particularly Ein el Tal (Aleppo), Dera’a (South) and Yarmouk (Damascus) oﬃcial and nonoﬃcial camps, which have sustained large-scale destruction. In 2020, UNRWA will continue to focus on resuming its core services in areas where access has resumed and civilians are spontaneously returning. UNRWA will also make sure that beneﬁciaries and staff, including persons with disabilities, have safe access to its facilities by maintaining and rehabilitating its programme and operational support premises and community centres.
In order to ensure effective delivery of services as well as the support of operations in the logistic and administrative ﬁelds, deployment of local and international staff based in the target locations is essential. Considering the scale of the humanitarian needs, UNRWA will continue to require dedicated technical staff to manage, support and monitor multisectorial activities as well as emergency operations in Syria. International dedicated experts (humanitarian oﬃcers, monitoring and evaluation specialists, donor relations oﬃcers) will continue to be deployed at the main oﬃce to increase programme quality and neutrality. Adequate staﬃng is essential for UNRWA to implement its operations, especially with regard to direct implementation, as well as to ensure an eﬃcient coordination with all stakeholders and to enhance advocacy on behalf of PR.
Since the onset of the conflict, UNRWA has made significant investments in the safety and security of its staff, assets and facilities and security procedures. UNRWA requires funding to maintain and strengthen the existing security infrastructure. In 2020, the main investments will continue to focus on security personnel staﬃng costs, the procurement of security equipment and services, and uniforms for security staff. These investments will enable UNRWA to facilitate and provide security support for over 600 field missions conducted yearly in a medium to high-risk security environment, and to conduct security risk assessments of its installations. In order to ensure that UNRWA facilities are compliant with minimum operating security standards, the Agency will conduct security risk assessments on targeted facilities and will carry out necessary physical enhancement works to ensure a safe and secure environment for its staff and beneciaries accessing assistance and services. In addition, UNRWA plans to ensure the installation of a closed-circuit television (CCTV) room and Internet Protocol (IP) surveillance cameras at its oﬃces in Damascus and Central area. UNRWA will continue to train its staff in Safe and Security Approaches to Field Environment (SSAFE) and will also provide are safety training. Finally, through its commitment to ensuring gender considerations in security management, and recognizing that women are more exposed to risks of GBV, UNRWA in Syria will maintain a Women’s Security Awareness Training (WSAT) programme for UNRWA female staff members.

4 - Needs

Whilst some areas have experienced relative calm throughout 2019, hostilities remain intense in other areas of the country. This has resulted in deaths and injuries, continued internal displacement, largescale migration to other countries, lost livelihoods, mounting humanitarian needs and access restrictions. UNRWA has been assisting Palestine refugees within a volatile security environment. Investment in experts, security and safety of staff, assets and facilities, as well as in its security procedures, is critical for reducing the risk faced by staff and beneﬁciaries while maintaining operational integrity and neutrality. The latest socio-economic assessment of living conditions conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in absolute poverty (less than US$ 2 person/day). It also found that households headed by older persons, females, persons with disabilities or orphans are among the most vulnerable Palestine refugee groups and suffer from high unemployment rates and poverty. It is imperative for UNRWA to continue provide essential service to the PR community in Syria and to ensure operations are adequately supported. Support for UNRWA operations continues to be key. Staffing, emergency repair of UNRWA’s facilities, as well as investments in the safety of personnel and assets are a prerequisite for UNRWA to continue operating in Syria, and to deliver vital assistance to Palestinian refugees affected by the crisis.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project activities include 1. Recruitment of local as well as international staff. 2. Procurement of security and safety equipment items 3. Security enhancement of UNRWA’s facilities 4. Building the capacity of staff on safety and security including SSAFE training. 5. Electrical repairs and equipment for UNRWA premises and general maintenance 6. Maintenance/repair and reconstruction of UNRWA programme support and operational premises in newly accessible gathering areas and camps and ensuring structures are accessible to persons with disabilities. The project aims at ensuring that UNRWA support programmes are well equipped to provide operational support to the UNRWA implementing departments, to ensure the efficient provision of service to PR and a secure and safe environment for UNRWA staff and beneficiaries. Rehabilitated UNRWA installations will ensure the Agency’s ability to continue operate and provide assistance to PR. UNRWA remains the main provider for PR in Syria and it is mandated and responsible for the provision of education, relief and social services, health and Engineering and Camp Improvement services. Management, coordination and technical staff are essential for UNRWA to deliver humanitarian assistance, to ensure efficient coordination and to enhance advocacy on behalf of PR. Monitoring will be guaranteed through regular ﬁeld visits and feedback from implementing staff based in the ﬁeld and their respective programme. UNRWA will continue to build staff capacity regarding neutrality, to comply with the UNRWA regulatory framework in relation to AVAC and the prevention of SEA, and to participate in humanitarian coordination mechanisms at country level, including the humanitarian sectors and the HCT. While the vast majority of posts will continue to be filled by PR, UNRWA will maintain some international capacity, particularly specialist staff in the fields of humanitarian response, protection, logistics and grants management.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In 2020, UNRWA anticipates that a mix of humanitarian and development approaches that are supportive of post-conﬂict rehabilitation will be needed to respond to the needs of PR across Syria. Depreciation of the SYP, rising inﬂation, increasing prices of basic commodities, destruction and damage of houses, and limited livelihood opportunities continue to undermine resilience and coping capacities. The levels of displacement are foreseen to remain high for PR, as access to Yarmouk is still restricted. Returns to Dera’a and EinElTal camps, remain slow, due to the level of damage to basic infrastructure and the lack of public services. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation it is expected that self-organized refugee returns from neighboring host countries will increase despite remaining relatively low compared to the overall number of refugees. HLP and CD needs will also increase. Secondary displacements is anticipated among PR due to their legal status. It is expected that protection risks will persist and may intensify in areas where spontaneous returns, are expected to increase. Long term commitment of donors supporting UNRWA financially worsen 4. If the situation worsens in 2020 and given the current ﬁnancial situation, UNRWA might not be able to address the rising needs. Mitigation: 1continue monitoring the situation 2adjust plans accommodating most pressing needs 3Intensify fundraising efforts. Risk UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding: Last year, UNRWA received only around 30% of its requirements for Syria. Witnessing further shortfall in funding will lead to UNRWA’s inability to provide services and increase staff’s dissatisfaction. Mitigation: 1regularly engage with donors and partners and adopting a strategic approach to fundraising 2prioritize key projects 3Oversee robust ﬁnancial and management systems 4Maintain communication with staff on status of funding. RiskUNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats: If the security situation worsens in Syria, there will be a higher risk of exposure to violence and security threats for staff and beneﬁciaries. Mitigation: 1monitor security updates and conducting security assessments for facilities and missions 2advocate for the needs of its beneﬁciaries 3train staff and beneﬁciaries on ERW, and personal safety. UNRWA will continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. The RBM approach to programming ensures evidence-based decision-making and accountability. Risks are also mitigated by ensuring a sound and solid monitor of implementation
