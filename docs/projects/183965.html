Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work on improving access to WASH services for the Vulnerable population in localities of Rokero & Umm Dukhun (CD), El Ferdous (ED), Ed El Fursan & El Salam (SD), El Faw (Gadaref), and Abu Hujar (Sennar) that will contribute to 3 HRP WASH sector objectives for 2022.
In this project, HOPE will (1) 55,000 the crisis-affected population will have access to basic water services, (2) 6,160 crisis-affected populations will have access to adequate (basic or limited), protected, and gender-sensitive sanitation services, and (3) 81,930 crisis-affected populations will have access to hygiene promotion services, water quality, and environmental health interventions.
HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through full community participation, strengthen the current community mechanism/ committee of WASH committees and hygiene promoters as well as do follow up for all benefit targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

Based on WASH assessment findings conducted by HOPE: in CD Jan 2021, in SD Aug 2021, in ED Jul 2021, as well as observations during visits to AlFao & Abu Hugar, in these target localities, compared to a gap, the WASH interventions are very weak including a shortage of clean water drinking, deterioration of sanitation & hygiene; no latrines in most villages; open defecation is widely practiced increasing, drinking water is suspected to be contaminated meanly in the rainy season, no vector control was in place; increasing of flies and mosquitos was reported. Based on HNO – Sep 2021 by WASH sector, The WASH severity for 7 localities it is 4 or 5, the People In Need (PIN) are 206,229 in Rokero (40,313 IDPs, 121,483 Returnees, 46,191 vulnerable), 58,950 in Umm Dukhun (21,314 Returnees, 37,636 vulnerable), 25,862 in El Ferdous (all vulnerable), 87,156 in El Faw (all vulnerable), 74,091 in Abu Houjar (all vulnerable), 74,866 in Ed El Fursan (9,490 IDPs, 1,990 Returnees, 63,386 vulnerable), and 30,635 El Salam (SD) (4,490 IDPs, 4,539 Returnees, 21,606 vulnerable). The PIN/ target disaggregated to 19.6% of men, 20.4% of women, 29.4% of boys, 30.6% of girls, 12 % people with disabilities, also disaggregated to 41% male, 59% female, 8% elderly, 63% children and 29 adults.The main communicable diseases and outbreaks that related to the deterioration of WASH services of vector control, solid waste management, and contamination of drinking water in the target locality are Malaria, AWD, Fever, and Schistosomiasis as well as all localities at risk for COVID-19 due to the bad health system, lack of communities’ practices in term of handwashing and wearing face-masks in a crowded area such as a market or when visit health facilities. In targeted localities of Darfur, there is a high risk to expand the outbreaks of Chikungunya, and Dengue Fever that is happening in the border states of South and West Darfur. In Darfur, the inter-communal, inter-tribal conflicts, and violence that is happened in the last years which are affected the safety and security of people exacerbating the risk of sexual violence especially when displaced. Women and girls continue to face the risk of gender-based violence including sexual violence when they leave the villages. Fear of reprisals from attackers often prevents rape survivors from seeking medical help. The conflict in Darfur has also increased the number of women who are the sole providers for their families. El Fao of Gadaref is suffering from poor WASH services and a deterioration in services due to the floods that happened this year. Also, in October 2020 the arrival of Ethiopian refugees fleeing the fighting in Ethiopia began, as well as Gadaref state hosted more than 64,180 refugees (UNHCR - Refugees dashboard 30 Sep 2021) and expected increasing the new refugee's arrival, so there is an urgent need to improve the WASH services of the host communities. Abu Hujar of Sennar is also suffering from poor WASH services, in last years, registered the highest cases of Cholera and AWD due to a deterioration of WASH services, lack of sanitation, and hygiene practices by community people.Sudan also faces issues with more chronic levels of malnutrition with one in every three children under the age of five years affected. These children will suffer from more long term developmental, WASH impact resulting in both a shorter stature than non-affected peers as well as a significant impact on the development of the brain resulting in long term impact on IQ, learning potential, educational outcomes as well as poorer WASH in the immediate and longer-term future. As a result, these children will have lower lifetime earnings and lower productivity as adults, influencing the economic growth and potential of the entire country. Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for improving access to WASH services.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During design, HOPE assessed the safety, security and risk for target areas as well as types of GBV that facing women. During implementation, HOPE will support the capacities of committee members and assist to know their rights to WASH. All essential drugs at the pharmacy will be handled with the utmost care and storage - warehouses will be clean to avoid contamination. This project will include psycho-social stimulation activities for infants and young children will also be provided with psychosocial support to help mothers deal with their own distress and improve care for their children. Child protection messages on prevention and response as well as referral mechanisms will be included in activities related to WASH outreach and awareness-raising. All WASH volunteers will be trained on referral procedures and mechanisms linking patients directly to experts from other agencies and the government is already supporting the WASH sector. Children and women will be given priorities. Privacy for HH shared latrines will be considering. Awareness-raising during community mobilization on WASH, education a key component that HOPE has put in place for better understanding among the beneficiaries for self-protection on various programmatic matters. HOPE also will develop a system to collect feedback from beneficiaries to measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints.
