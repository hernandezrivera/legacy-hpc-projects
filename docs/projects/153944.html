Project Summary

1. The actions of this project aim to provide a response to the humanitarian needs of the 150 households identified among the displaced populations of the Koussérie site. (The choice made on the site of kafia is explained by the fact that it has a very large cultivable space with the possibility of making two harvests a year). In fact, affected by the crisis in the Lake Chad Region, thousands of refugees and returnees have settled in the sites, camps in the region and some are integrated into the host communities. However, there are men and women from more vulnerable households who are struggling to meet their basic needs such as feeding, dressing, and medical care.
In addition to those who are returnees and displaced, they are putting more pressure on already scarce local resources in communities suffering extreme poverty. They constitute a source of additional tension on local communities in a context of relatively fragile peace. Returned populations and host populations face extremely difficult living conditions. They fail to reach the threshold of survival, and livelihood, especially women for whom, economic opportunities are very limited.
2. This sudden movement has broken in these populations the system of organization of social life, the advanced economic imbalance. In addition to these upheavals, there is the precariousness of sanitary conditions, accommodation and food.
Unable to remain indifferent to this situation, it has been identified in these populations working households, physically valid, but limited by the means of ensuring their socio-economic resilience. the ASD under the auspices of PRM, plans to carry out this project of: Redeploy 150 households to a new site for those interested in agriculture in the valley of the kafia polder located 2km from the city of Baga-sola, according to the reality and the context of the environment. This project, given its relevance, is efficient and feasible, which will have positive impacts on beneficiaries with objectively verifiable indicators.
2. These are the following actions:
- Construct a dike with a length of 300m, 2m wide and 2m high in the kousseri polder.
- build shelters with semi-sustainable local materials.
- Install (1) borehole (a pumping station with solar panel) to supply relocated displaced people in the new site with drinking water.
This intervention will contribute to improving the living conditions of the vulnerable and affected populations of the Lake Chad crisis by covering essential needs and services and strengthening their resilience. The actions of this one will cover about 1000 people affected. . Through the construction of the dike, it is the development of a polder allowing beneficiaries and Aboriginals to improve their livelihoods. For upstream the Wal-Wal polder containment project was undertaken by indigenous people, but abandoned mid-term because of rising water and limited financial means. This is why the project comes in the hope of completing the book. Given the demographic concentration in the area, this action aims to reinforce peaceful coexistence between indigenous and returnees on the one hand, and access to arable plots on the one hand, which will reduce the problem factors to land between the two groups.
This wet, well-developed polder is a short, medium and long term productive resource for these populations. Under the management of SODELAC, parcels are rationally shared between identified beneficiaries and indigenous people. This project is of paramount importance because currently, arable land is not enough for the native population and will be beneficial for the returnees. They are only waiting for humanitarian aid to guarantee their subsistence and social reintegration.
3. Humanitarian assistance to ensure their livelihood and social reintegration.
4. The second part of the action consists of the construction of 150 shelters where each of the households identified will have to live on the basis of their socio-economic activities. In this section, the aim is to relocate them to a new site, located on the right bank of the Wal-Wal polder. These emergency shelters will be made from locally collected materials (wood, straws, maize stalks, matted tree branches made from date leaves, woven ropes, etc.), which do not require huge financial resources.
The site is chosen for the daily fishing, farming, small ruminant and poultry farming activities of the beneficiaries. This location will allow returnees to restore food and economic resilience through the practice of the activities listed above. Also, women will increase their income thanks to the tenuous waters used for market gardening. The construction work will be done by local labor, which will be made up of young people, so they will save unemployment. This will allow them to learn the cultural technique of ricultural and market gardening. This allows the unemployed to be occupied for a while. These shelters will protect the beneficiaries against bad weather and other dangers, in order to improve the health situation of children and women related to these bad weather.
5. Finally, the last part is part of the ultimate goal of making drinking water available and accessible to both target groups. Because the new housing site has no sources of water supply. Only the water retained in the polder is used at the same time to supply livestock and for human consumption. The realization of this book, will contribute to fight against the waterborne diseases related to the consumption of the unsanitary water, and consequently to lower the rate of diseases related to the consumption of surface waters among the returned ones installed on the site.

Needs

Identification and needs analysis on the Bagasola IPD sites was carried out through:- Since April 2017, during the deployment of ASD teams in the field to BAGASOLA, monitoring and evaluation of assistance needs have been carried out during the execution of the current project and have allowed to refine the analysis of needs- The state of play in water, shelter in the sites of the displaced and in the districts of return in the sub-prefecture of BAgasola realized by the ASD teams in collaboration with the teams of IOM in May 2017- The needs analysis also took into account the various meetings with the coordination on the country strategy on the evolution of the food access situation taking into account the evolution of the security situation in the sub-prefecture from Bagasola- Focus Groups Discussion on IDPs Sites by ASD Food Security Promotions Teams- The report of the monitoring visit of the food security situation- The database and CMP report- OCHA's humanitarian bulletins- The daily monitoring carried out by the ASD teams present at BAGASOLA in the Lake Chad region, All this monitoring and evaluation system has made it possible to highlight the needs below and requires the implementation of this project:In all the villages visited, the population consists solely of the indigenous people who were temporarily displaced by the attacks of BiH. These people started returning to their villages of origin in 2016. The people lost everything during the attacks and started their lives again without any assistance. Today, shelter needs are immense on all the sites visited, the houses were destroyed or burned by the BiH during their visit to these sites. These returnees are unable to obtain construction materials for new homes. Which leads to overcrowding in houses, some have no shelter.One of the most urgent needs in these villages are the MEAs. Activities or outputs

GLOBAL OBJECTIVE: To improve the living conditions of the most vulnerable men and women among host and returnee populations affected by the humanitarian crisis in Lake Chad by ensuring peaceful coexistence between communities. The project will achieve this goal through 2 key interventions - improving water, improving housing conditions and agricultural production, and preventing gender-based violence. Activity 2: Establishment / Recycling of WASH Committees10 health and sanitation committees will be set up or recycled. The WASH committees will be composed of 5 people (including 2 women). The committees will be equipped with environmental kits (wheelbarrow, mechanical pencil, shovel, dumpster, gloves, protective muffler, boot, rake), maintenance equipment and toolbox. The existing committees in PRM 2018 project villages will also be accompanied by the Action to strengthen their capacities, notably in the implementation of a cost recovery system. Appropriate treatments will be carried out to guarantee the quality of the water.
