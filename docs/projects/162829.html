Project Summary

The World Health Organization (WHO) preparedness and response to health emergencies are aligned with the national health sector strategies and adheres to national and international standards. The focus of WHO and health partners will be providing life-saving free of charge health services to vulnerable populations affected by natural and manmade disasters through strengthening the locality and community level structures to prepare, detect and respond to all types of emergencies with public health consequences.
The WHO in Sudan has well-established country office and 12 field offices located closer to the Federal and State Ministry of Health Offices across Sudan. Technical and operational support will be provided by the WHO to strengthen the SMOHs coordination and emergency response teams up to community level. The collection, centralization, analysis and dissemination of essential health data disaggregated by sex and age-groups will be maintained and strengthen by WHO field teams jointly with all partners. The WHO technical and cluster coordination team will ensure that the standard health cluster tools, such as Early Warning Alert and Response (EWARS), Health Resources Availability Monitoring System (HeRAMS), Who is doing What, Where and since When (4Ws), risk and vulnerability mapping, Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP), epidemiological briefings, periodic situational reports (SitReps) and health cluster bulletin are timely and regularly updated and utilized by health partners.
The WHO procure and distribute free of charge medicines to support the NGOs and SMOH in dealing with humanitarian case load at all levels, while ensuring a rapidly revolving pipeline for addressing acute events in 2020. Rapid Response Kits (RRK) adapted to the protracted crisis in Sudan addresses specific needs of children (boys and, girls), women in general and women of reproductive age, men and elderly. In addition, medicines for case-management and laboratory regent for confirmation of outbreaks will be provided by WHO. Training of health workers on topics directly related to prevailing humanitarian situations such as on surveillance and case management and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) will be provided based on the needs assessed. The project supports small repair and rehab of damaged health facilities; operational costs for scaling up the humanitarian health services as necessary. The project supports community mobilization to promote healthy behavior. Besides, the project adopts an integrated approach that facilitates access to health services to all age groups, male and female with their specific health needs adapted to the local cultural customs, including advocacy and synergic linkages with ongoing recovery-development interventions (GBV, FGM, Protection, FSL, WASH, Nutrition, NFI, and Education).
Optimal capacity for early notification, investigation, confirmation and immediate response to public health threats (outbreaks) is another priority contributing to the health security of targeted groups and beyond. This is achieved through technical and operational support for EWARS, epidemic alert investigation and response operations by supporting the RRTs; supporting response vaccination campaigns and strengthening the routine immunization; providing reagents and medicines to fill in the gaps during eventual outbreaks.
The overall target for the project is 5 million vulnerable communities and groups such as IDPs (1.3M), returnees, and children under five (860,000), pregnant women (2M), elderly (100,000) and people with disabilities in 112 localities of the 18 states in Sudan. The activities related to outbreak control are not restricted only to the 112 targeted localities, the WHO will intervene wherever the needs arise under its mandate for health security and protection of health.

1 - Needs

There are 8.6 million people in need for humanitarian health assistance residing in high risk areas, and lacking the basic lifesaving and life sustaining health services across Sudan. Over 175,000 people in need are facing a catastrophic health situation (severity 5), approximately 2.5 million people in extreme in need for health assistance (severity 4), and 5 million people in areas of severe need of health lifesaving activities (severity 3). Out of the total figure, around 3.5 million children under 5 years of age are in need of early diagnosis and treatment of life-threatening health conditions. Additionally, the vulnerability of these children is increased by low immunization coverage (EPI), which makes them at risk of future outbreaks of vaccine preventable diseases. Out of the total population in need of humanitarian assistance, there are currently 1,360,000 pregnant and women of reproductive age in need of sexual and reproductive health services. By September 2019, over 320, 000 people were directly affected by the floods across 15 states of which 7,648 are pregnant women in need of safe delivery services. With thousands of houses, latrines, and public facilities destroyed or damaged, the most affected areas were, White Nile, Khartoum, Kassala, North Kordofan, and North Darfur. In these states, existing health systems capacities are stretched and needs are high in trauma services, referral systems, mobile health services, and community-based interventions; water services are disrupted and the consumption of unsafe water is predominant among the affected population. As a result, people are exposed to water-born diarrheal diseases with a projected 40 percent increase in Dysentery and typhoid cases compared to last year, malaria cases breaching the epidemic threshold in several states, and an overall number of people vulnerable or directly affected by floods double the number reported in 2018. On the 8th of September 2019, the FMOH announced an outbreak of Cholera in Blue Nile state on the backdrop of the recent floods and deteriorating health and WASH situation in the state. Further, White Nile, Sennar, Jazeira, Kassala, Khartoum and River Nile states were identified as high risk states. A nationwide outbreak similar to the 2017/2018 outbreak can affect as many as 37,000 people. The later can be exacerbated by low coverage of facility based Surveillance system and a premature community based surveillance CBS. Therefore, there is a need to expand the coverage of extended program of immunization EPI supplemented by catch-up campaigns is essential to prevent outbreaks of vaccines preventable diseases especially Measles, Diphtheria and Whooping Cough. 300 thousands pregnant women live in areas where provision of maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health services is suboptimal (severity ranking 3-5), in addition, the overall lack of surgical supplies, safe blood banking services, and referral services can contribute to high maternal mortality in cases of complicated deliveries and the Hemorrhagic manifestations of viral HFs outbreaks. Shortages in essential medicines will affect over 150,000 elderly people with chronic medical condition, strengthening and sustaining the medical supply-chain is essential to ensure the availability of necessary medicines for all vulnerable groups. Limitation of the health system capacity to detect and manage major epidemics prone diseases such as Malaria which need strengthening the surveillance system and the capacity of case management.Hence, in 2020 health partners identified 8.6 million PIN, including 1.8 million vulnerable IDPs. Of which the current HRP 2020 target 5 Million people including 1.3 Million IDPs, 2million pregnant women, 860,000 under five children, and 100,000 elderly requiring humanitarian assistance. Of which, female and children constitutes 59% and 63% respectively.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The prioritization of vulnerable communities and geographical location are the most affected by the climate changes; drought and floods with consequent food insecurity and high acute malnutrition levels. Climate change also influenced the pattern of the outbreaks of vector and water borne diseases that indeed increased over the past few years with re-emerging and expansion to new geographical areas of Sudan of Dengue Fever, Chikungunya, Rift Valley and AWD Fever (suspected). There is sufficient evidence that climate changes also influence the transmission of measles. About 40% of the project inputs are factored in for the prevention, early identification, confirmation and response to outbreaks of water and vector borne diseases as well as water related diseases. Any health service delivery involves working with sick people, some of them suffering of communicable diseases with epidemic potential. Without implementation infection prevention and control IPC at HFs and during the mobile and outreach health delivery points. aggressive pathogens (microbes, Viruses, protozoa, fungus, etc) and medicines, sharp medical tools, disinfectants, lab reagents etc would contaminate/affect staff, other patients and environment outside the clinics creating severe risks for population living in the areas. The training program of WHO includes modules (initial training and re-fresher) on Universal Precautions that includes Infection Prevention, Injection safety, Solid and liquid medical waste safe disposal for all levels of care. The national guidelines are distributed to all participants to the training, and to the SMOH so all HFs in targeted area are covered. All WHO medical kits include barrier protection equipment, and disinfectants that are biodegradable. In addition, the WHO/MOH monitoring and supervision check list includes indicators for Universal Precaution, and the repairs of any HF takes into account the standards for environmental protection through correct medical waste disposal (pits for sharps and placenta, incinerators, fence, septic tanks, etc. The disposal of any expired medicines is done according with WHO/National Guidelines by incineration in the presence of Health Authorities.

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

By design, the project targeted vulnerable communities that requires protection intervention such as IDPs and women population. During the implimentation the project provides interventions to prevent gender based violence and ensure rights based service delivery that takes into account the need for privacy and confidentiality such as community awarness on protection of gender based violence, provision of psychosocial counselling by a women health worker in a facility that provides privacy. The project consideres provision of separate toilets and in patient rooms for women and men. Also, the project engages other agencies to ensure collaboration and referal services for victims of gender based violences. The project segregates reports based on age and gender.
