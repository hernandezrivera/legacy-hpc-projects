Project Summary

The proposed intervention's overall objective for 2022 is to improve equitable access for school-aged children to safe, inclusive, & quality learning environments to foster their resilience. This will be achieved through implementing a comprehensive package of education activities to 30 schools in NW Syria which are located in underserved and high-needs areas, where 17’000 children (7045 B and 9955 G 'including 444 CwD ~2.5%', 5-17 years old), plus 750 teachers and education personnel (425 males and 325 females) will be targeted (70% out of the grand total are IDPs).
Based on 'WoS_PiN_Severity_2022' statistics; 17.4% of the project total direct targeted beneficiaries are within severity scale 5 (Catastrophic), and 82.6% are within severity scale 4 (Extreme). These figures illustrate the population situation & the urgent need for direct action and support regarding education programs & services within the following sub-districts: Atareb, Daret Azza, Dana, Darkosh, Maaret Tamasrin, Qourqeena, & Salqin.
All proposed project activities are in line with the Education Sector’s HRP objectives. The project is going to address the education needs through a comprehensive package of support for 19 schools (12’888 children ‘5155 boys and 7733 girls’), in addition, to conducting cash for education intervention for 11 schools (4’112 children ‘1890 boys and 2222 girls’) in order to lighten the financial burden from families and send their children to school.
Ihsan Relief & Development (IRD) seeks to increase children's enrollment in the targeted schools by supporting a quality learning environment. IRD will conduct digital Back to Learning campaigns 'to minimize gathering and as a precautionary measure for COVID-19'. The enrolled students will receive formal/non-formal (SLP) education services. To improve the students’ well-being during the project lifetime, all enrolled students will receive recreational activities. All teachers and education personnel (750: 425 men & 325 women) will be provided with monthly incentives to increase their motivation and ensure their retention in schools. The project will cover the schools’ monthly operational costs, including recreational materials, refreshment, cleaning, hygiene materials, and stationery, and the provision of students' and teachers' kits. Adding, conducting small-scale rehabilitation works. The quality of education will be improved through contextualized teacher training and further support to their professional development by delivering (CSG, TiCC, PSEA, COVID-19). Parents Teachers Associations (PTAs) will be a component in the activities of this project. Quarterly meetings for PTAs members will be held. Due to COVID-19, there will be comprehensive precautionary measures in the 30 schools, including periodic sterilization processes for classrooms and WASH facilities, awareness sessions for students, training for teachers & educational staff, and sterilization provision materials for all schools. Plus, as the humanitarian situation for people in NW Syria remains severe, as the population already suffers from the effects of 11 years of conflict, insecurity, displacement, and economic hardship including the devaluation of the SYP; IRD will conduct a Cash for Education assistance that acknowledges the advantages of flexibility, free choice, and dignity of the beneficiaries for 11 schools (out of 30). This will be done through the 'Cash for Education SoP' that was developed by IRD & UNICEF 'jointly' targeting vulnerable households with children at risk of drop-outs due to financial constraints. The cash assistance option included the provision of cash to cover stationery and other costs. Children and their parents have the flexibility and free choice to purchase the needed items to continue their study.
Noting that for 2022/2023, IRD will provide an education response to "28'000 as overall beneficiaries" who are highly vulnerable children in communities of NW Syria (Aleppo & Idleb governorates)

4 - Needs

The education system, which has already suffered immensely because of this decade-long crisis "that also inflicted immense suffering on the civilian population", remains fractured & under-resourced limiting the ability of children to access education services. In reference to the 'Syria HNO 2022' report; education remains a critical need; where many students continue to learn in unconducive and/or unsafe learning environments with 25 verified attacks on schools. Additionally "per Idleb ED annual report" 20'000 teachers are working in NW Syria; around %50 are working voluntarily. Recent figures 'from Aleppo & Idleb EDs' show that 410 thousand children (95%) & school personnel (5%) are estimated to be in need of humanitarian education assistance, an estimated 145 thousand children are at risk of dropping-out, or out of school. Adding, COVID19 forced schools to switch between physical, remote, or blended learning modalities. IRD conducted a need assessment in 6 subdistricts; Idleb (Dana, Harim, Salqin, Maaret Tamsrin, Darkoush, & Qourqeena), & Aleppo (Daret Azza) to assess the educational situation. Based on this need assessment the project is going to address the education needs through a comprehensive package of support. The proposed project will be targeting (12888 children ‘5155 B & 7733 G’), plus, conducting cash for education intervention for 11 schools (4112 children ‘1890 B & 2222 G’) in order to lighten the financial burden from families & send their children to school

5 - Implementation of activities

IRD aims to discourage drop-out & increase meaningful quality learning as well as increase enrollment of out-of-school children; also, to secure the functioning of schools through -Promoting child safeguarding & edu quality in 30 schools in NW Syria by delivering training for teachers on (CSG), PSEA, & COVID19 by the IRD field team. -Increase awareness among parents & local communities on importance of education & how to be protected from COVID19 pandemic by conducting digital Back to Learning campaigns within the targeted communities. IRD will go with this method to avoid crowd gathering & achieve the widespread of the intended messages. -Determine children's educational levels & enable the teachers to plan & focus on the needed educational activities for each student by undergoing an ASER test. -Improve children's well-being & learning capacity through recreational activities -Ensure safe & quality learning environments by provision of monthly required operational supplies. -Conduct small-scale rehabilitation works. -Provision of Education & COVID19 prevention materials to schools, teachers, & children, -Conduct periodic sterilization process. -PTAs establishment per school. -Quarterly meetings for PTAs members to set plans for the upcoming period. -Conducting cash for education intervention for 11 schools in order to lighten the financial burden from families and send their children to school. -Conduct MEAL activities: field visits, PDM, MEAL report, feedback mechanism.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The number one risk is security, as schools have been one of the main targets in the conflict. Although the situation has slightly improved due to the cessation of hostilities, it is unknown how long it will last. The security situation is not one that IRD can play a major role in mitigating, besides providing additional safety and security training for teachers to use with their students if security concerns arise. IRD has chosen relatively safer locations and has historically been locations where IDPs flee to, not where people are fleeing from. In order to avoid any CSG violation by any education worker, IRD will deliver training on CSG & CoC for all educational staff. In case any education worker violates the CoC/CSG; IRD will assign a committee including ‘field education team, CSG focal point, CP advisor, and education supervisor’ to verify the incident of violation and communicate with the ED in order to take the appropriate disciplinary actions based on IRD’s CSG policy.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Awareness-raising sessions on PSEA will be conducted for children by classroom teachers. As all teachers will receive PSEA training in order to be able to deliver the sessions for the students. The sessions are being conducted by the start of academic year & repeated "for the newly enrolled children" by the start of the second semester. In case any education worker violates the CoC/CSG; IRD will assign a committee including ‘ field program edu team, CSG focal point, CP advisor, & edu supervisor’ to verify the incident of violation & communicate with the EDs to take the appropriate disciplinary actions based on IRD’s CSG policy. IRD has developed an action plan to fill in all the gaps related to PSEA & the Code of Conduct through revising & reviewing the potential risks that might occur in each department. Furthermore, for PSEA investigations, complaints are received through IRD channels, the complaints may be addressed directly to IRD focal point, who will communicate the case to the Executive Director.IRD has established a CRM in order to activate an early warning mechanism that can prevent, reduce, or resolve issues that may escalate into serious problems. In this project, CRM channels will be complaints boxes (in accessible places), email, & WhatsApp number, which allows receiving complaints & feedback directly from beneficiaries. These channels will be shared & announced in all activities’ locations, also, to conduct frequent FGDs with students to collect their feedback.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions1: The targeted areas are stable, and no large-scale military operations are anticipated, procurement and shipment roads/lines are stable, and relations with the Education Directorates and local authorities are accessible and supportive. Risk 1.1: A new wave of displacement to the project locations. L: Medium, I: High. Mitigation 1.1: Follow-up the security situation in the project locations, and integration with other stakeholders to provide emergency services. Risk 1.2: Deterioration of the general security situation in the project areas. L: Low, I: High. Mitigation1.2: Closely monitor and follow up security situations, coordinate with the education cluster, and follow the instructions issued to maintain the project's safety and employees. Risk 1.3: Schools’ lockdown due to COVID-19 pandemic. L: Medium, I: High. Mitigation 1.3: In case of COVID-19 outbreak in project locations, and as a mitigation measure: (A) schools might be suspended fully. The project will adapt the distance learning modality tested during 2020 & 2021 through 6 education projects implemented by IRD. Children will be provided with internet vouchers. WhatsApp groups will be created per subject and per class. Teachers will record the lessons to be shared with students. (B) If the closure was 'partial' then, the blended education approach will be applied, a mix of physical attendance and distance learning methods.
