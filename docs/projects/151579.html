Project Summary

This project is designed to address the humanitarian needs of IDP and host communities who lack access to basic healthcare services. Aid Vision planned to open health facilities that provides basic services like provisions of free medical consultations for under-fives and over fives at the outpatient unit, provide free antenatal and postnatal care services to all emergency affected pregnant women, provision of immunization services against preventable diseases six days a week, provision of free 24 hours maternity services to pregnant mothers and refer mothers with obstetric labour for further management. The Project has component of community health workers who will do active case finding, defaulter tracing, hygiene promotion and messaging at the community level. The proposed project will be implemented in Garasbaley of Banadir region, Warsheikh, Ragacel, and Balacad district of middle Shabelle to cover the health needs of the targeted population. For purpose of sustainability of the project, Aid Vision employs Community Based Participatory Approach through strengthening community capacity by capacity building own staff, local authorities’ staff, MoH staff, training of CHWs and establish community health committees. This approach ensures at the end of the project, a strong cadre of community-based structures are built for community ownership and sustainability of the program.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in Somalia remains grave due to residual impacts of drought, ongoing displacement, conflict and seasonal floods. According to UNICEF situational reports May 2018, More than 5.7 million people require basic health services, including critical needs in maternal and child health, as one in seven Somali children die before the age of five. Disease outbreaks such as acute watery diarrhoea AWD/cholera and measles continue to represent a major threat to children with over 5,892 suspected measles cases (72 percent being children under five) and 3,590 cases of AWD/cholera with 26 deaths reported in 2018.According to humanitarian health overview in middle Shabelle, healthcare provision for almost 700,000 who leave in the region is limited to 22 health facility across 7 districts. Areas of only two districts are in accessible due to insecurity. Parts of other district in the region are accessible, yet underserved in terms of numbers of functioning health facility. Where clinics are open, quality of services delivery is a major concern and it is due shortages of health supplies and human resources. Population movements in to the towns like Balcad and Jowhar has been driven by continued droughts across the region thus increase the humanitarian health needs of the communitiesWith the on going liberation efforts Aid Vision plans to move in and offer lifesaving health services focusing on displaced persons, under 5 children and pregnant and lactating women in Warsheikh, Ragacele. Aid will advocate for the integration of health with other lifesaving and essential services like nutrition, WASH, food security and Education. For effective and efficiency service delivery in the Health cluster, Aid vision will closely work with MOH, Regional Authorities and Local communities. Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Aid Vision will ensure that all segments of the affected population in the IDPs including pregnant and lactating women, children aged 6-59 months have equal access to health care assistance. The minority and marginalized tribes in the IDP camps who are at risk of being neglected are also given quality nutrition care services like the host community.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Aid Vision shall endeavor equal chance, inclusiveness and participation of minority and majority as well as well as disable groups in the implementation of the project and be able to be transparent in terms of employment.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Aid Vision shall employ community participatory approach through training s and workshops on peace building, conflict resolution management and establish community development committee (CDC) that will address the specific concerns of the vulnerable people like women and girls who are at risk of gender base violence in the community.
