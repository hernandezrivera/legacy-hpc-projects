Project Summary

Conflict in Ethiopia’s Tigray, Amhara and Afar regions has contaminated vast swathes of land with explosive remnants of war (ERW), since missiles were launched during ground operations and from the air. As many internally displaced persons (IDPs) people are starting to return to their homes, their movements increase the risk of ERW incidents. An immediate mine action response is needed to address the risks faced by returning refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs) and the local population. Under the proposed project, UNMAS will mobilize and train explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) operators, analyze the dynamics of the operational environment and develop culturally appropriate messages. The team will assess victims, pattern of conflict, the nature of weapons used and where ERW may be located, after which EORE will be delivered to communities in priority areas to reduce the risk of injury from ERW by raising awareness among men, women, and children in accordance with their different vulnerabilities, roles and needs.
Initial assessments by UNMAS have indicated a high rate of explosive ordnance contamination across Tigray, Amhara and Afar due to the ongoing conflict. IDPs and host communities have reported a lack of awareness of how to identify and mitigate risks from explosive ordnance, and in recent months there have been an increasing number of injuries linked to ERW. During protection monitoring, IDPs have expressed concerns about the presence of ERW in their areas of origin, which could expose them to further harm in returning home or resuming agricultural activities. At present, there is limited capacity for marking, survey, or clearance of unexploded ordnance; as such, UNMAS will focus initially on EORE to mitigate immediate risks, while strengthening the evidence base for future interventions.
Phase 1: Plan, mobilize and train EORE operators
The content of an EORE programme must be suited to the actual threat posed to affected people in the community where the EORE risk programme is being conducted. As such, it is important to prioritize people most at risk by linking EORE messages directly to casualty and contamination data, an understanding of the affected population’s behaviour, risk patterns and coping mechanisms, and, wherever possible, anticipated population movements. The intervention will ensure that all groups have equal access to appropriately tested and evaluated EORE messages, irrespective of gender or other factors.
Moreover, the team will assess the trend of casualties, pattern of conflict, the nature of weapons used and where ERW may be located. The team will triangulate this information with the structured and unstructured return plan, and operational plans of humanitarian partners to generate a list of priority areas that urgently require EORE intervention.
Phase 2: Implementation of Explosive Risk Education sessions delivery in the respective Area of Operation
EORE teams will deliver risk education to communities using training materials that are gender-responsive and contextually relevant. ERW affects women, men, girls and boys differently; each group will receive targeted messaging, the imagery used during the training will be inclusive, and a diverse range of voices will be heard to inform subsequent action.
EORE teams will deploy to priority areas identified in phase 1 to conduct rigorous EORE campaigns using various delivery methods to ensure all at-risk populations are reached, including delivery through communities, schools and the media. To amplify EORE messaging, UNMAS will identify focal points within community members and teachers to train as trainers, so that EORE can be incorporated and replicated into their communications with communities.
UNMAS’s EORE Officer will support the delivery of training and other types of support to humanitarian partners to mitigate explosive risk, including taking part in humanitarian assessments and accompanying convoys.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

UNMAS will work with national implementing partners who will have strong knowledge of the intervention areas and of the affected population’s cultural norms, languages and habits. For both explosive threat assessment and EORE activities, community liaison will be a key component as well as information gathering and sharing with CSOs, grassroots organizations and community leaders in the intervention areas. In addition, as EO affects women, men, girls and boys differently, each group will receive targeted messaging and the imagery used during EORE sessions will be inclusive. The EORE materials will be adapted to the local languages, field-tested to ensure they are easily understandable by the local community, and adjusted as necessary. Finally, affected populations will be able to report concerns and provide feedback to the EORE teams, and the AAP/PSEA reporting channels of the wider humanitarian system will be systematically shared.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Affected populations will be able to report concerns about explosive ordnance to the EORE teams. The teams will use a variety of awareness raising formats including community sessions, focus-group discussions (with groups of women, youth and the elderly for instance), child-friendly sessions at schools, door-to-door outreach to ensure people with mobility impairment or household chores are not left behind, and leaflet distribution and/or radio broadcast in harder-to-reach areas. The teams will also seek to collect feedback on EORE materials as well as on the content and methodology of the EORE sessions, to ensure that they are adapted to the affected populations’ needs. As women in affected communities often feel more comfortable to report their concerns to other women, implementing partners will be encouraged to recruit gender-balanced teams. Finally the teams wil systematically sare the AAP/PSEA reporting channels of the wider humanitarian system with affected populations.
