Project Summary

This project will support cross-border operations from Turkey into Syria to tap into sources of information and share it for use by the humanitarian community through a team of humanitarian field officers in NSAG-controlled areas. The team will be managed by the HFOs management team based in Gaziantep, Turkey in consultation with OCHA Turkey. The project is designed to broadly support the formal coordination system, especially the clusters/ICCG, as It is based on five pillars.
1-Information gathering
This pillar focuses on gathering data from the ground to produce reports about the humanitarian situation and context and inform response and advocacy. The data is shared with the OCHA coordination unit for use in various platforms and products. Info on unmet needs, including urgent cases, will be shared with clusters to address.
2-Support the monitoring of SCHF funded projects.
The HFOs will monitor projects funded by the SCHF. These monitoring missions will be conducted upon the request of the SCHF and based on SCHF monitoring criteria. Monitoring of SCHF projects directly improves the quality and accountability of the inter-cluster response, as projects funded by the SCHF are reviewed through the clusters.
3-Community Engagement
The HFOs will conduct community engagement to strengthen humanitarian response through facilitating meetings with the affected community and the humanitarian community and other stakeholders; obtaining feedback from the community on their needs and the response, and providing information to affected people through awareness sessions and other means on the humanitarian response and the community’s rights and role in it. Community engagement informs the overall inter-cluster response and in some cases is organized on behalf of clusters.
4-Service Mapping
The existing service map app for mobile phones will get an upgrade to enable users of better access and reach. The upgrade will include:
- Launching a version that is compatible with iPhone OS.
- Displaying notifications that are related to emergencies and important events (Roads’ status, Borders situation, etc.)
- Providing geolocation services operated by Google maps for certain locations.
- Designing an e-vouchers system for food security and livelihood projects (Cash and vouchers assistance). This will be piloted as a trial.
- Adding the English Language to the database to enable displaying the control panel’s reports in English instantly.
- New visual design.
5- Capacity Building for CSOs and CBOs in Northwest Syria
This pillar will fall under B&Z’s capacity-building program which strategically aims at empowering the Syrian civil society to take a leading role in Syria’s future and more importantly be active agents of change by gaining the skills and knowledge that enables them to advocate for change at the policymaking level.
The project builds on the outcomes of a previous project that is about to be completed in 2022. The information-gathering component is well-established and in recent months the mechanism for sharing specific needs data with clusters was streamlined and has resulted in individual cases receiving assistance. SCHF monitoring activities carried out so far have been well-received, resulting in one of the HFOs becoming largely dedicated to this work and the processes for monitoring becoming more formalized which will be further enhanced. Direct engagement with communities facilitated by the HFOs has allowed senior leadership, including the ERC, to provide high-level advocacy from key constituencies including women and PWDs. The service map app is now available in four languages and hosts over 4,000 distributed between medical, educational emergency, and other types of services. It is also hosting ads from humanitarian actors, and hundreds of people have registered seeking training.

4 - Needs

Information on the humanitarian situation in northern Syria is difficult to obtain. Currently, OCHA Turkey is reliant upon one-time and remote assessments, such as the SINA and the MSNA, as well as ad hoc data from partners. While useful, the frequency and quality of current information flow are not consistent. At the same time, there is general recognition among partners that northern Syria contains many sources of information, of which many are only accessible from the ground. The large displacement waves, the vast need for humanitarian assistance, COVID-19 impact on the affected communities under many sectors, all led to the need for an information-sharing system that is essential to provide local communities and IDPs with the information they need about various services.

5 - Implementation of activities

1- Ensure sharing of information collected and analyzed to OCHA Turkey on a daily basis. This will then be shared with relevant humanitarian actors to support principled humanitarian action. 2- Mapping of assigned OCHA HFU projects including identification of implementing partners, locations, and affected communities through an interactive online map that enables the HFAs to follow up on the SCHF ongoing projects per cluster/organization/location. 3- Engage with the local communities and the affected population to gather their inputs, and feedback and share them with OCHA Turkey and the wider humanitarian community. Note and report any specific needs as identified by the population, in particular for people with noted vulnerabilities, e.g. physically disabled; grandparent-headed households, etc. Provide feedback on these activities, identify opportunities, and trends. 4- upgrade the service map application including adding extra features. 5- Empower Syrian local CSOs and CBOs from Syria through an organizational capacity development program that includes training on NGO governance and administration, proposal writing, project management, NGO management, social engagement, communication, and advocacy, coaching throughout the program period and grant-making to enable them to implement relevant interventions and projects responding to their target community needs.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project aims to enable men, women, girls, and boys as indirect beneficiaries of the project in their right to receive humanitarian assistance. Cross-cutting issues and vulnerabilities (such as gender, age, disabilities etc.) will be focused on by ensuring gender balance among staff members to facilitate outreach and access to both male and female beneficiaries, taking into consideration existing traditions and cultural norms. In this project, 10 of 20 field officers will be female and 1 of 4 full-time Turkey-based staff. This will continue in the new project. In addition, existing staff members have already received training on protection mainstreaming, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), Do No Harm, and other related protection and GBV mainstreaming to ensure their awareness of their obligations and duties and to capacitate them in their community engagement. All new staff will also receive the same training upon their recruitment. The HFOs are also bound to sign a Code of Conduct highlighting protection and GBV related issues. While supporting the cross-border humanitarian community in their community engagement, the HFOs will raise existing protection and GBV concerns and will contribute to raising awareness among local communities about their rights as recipients of humanitarian aid, taking into consideration cross-cutting issues and vulnerabilities (such as gender, age, disabilities, etc.).


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

B&Z maintains a zero-tolerance policy on sexual exploitation and abuse. For this reason, B&Z has a child protection policy, code of conduct, and safeguarding policy where everyone within the organization plays a role and has a responsibility to protect the beneficiaries from any type of exploitation, harassment, or abuse. Therefore, staff and volunteers are required to follow all the guidelines and processes set in these policies. B&Z's zero-tolerance Safeguarding policy reaffirms and strengthens the commitment as an organization towards adults and children protecting them from all forms of harm and abuse. All B&Z’s staff have been informed, received training, and signed these policies to ensure the proper and dignified implementation process throughout the project period.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1- Military operations in the area and clashes based on scenarios anticipated by B&Z that might limit freedom of movement and ability to deliver 2- Limited access granted by authorities on the ground 3- Kidnapping risks 4- Casualty risks 5- COVID-19 outbreak. Assumptions 1- Secure the field staff to fulfill the tasks within the project period 2- Secure the information and equipment within the project period. Mitigations 1- B&Z developed a contingency plan 2- B&Z developed and secured duty of care policy 3- B&Z revised and kept the risks scenarios updated 4- the project field team will limit their meetings and use the internet communications methods (WebEx, Skype, etc.) and in case they are conducting site visits they will use Personal protective equipment (masks, gloves, rubbing alcohol) to ensure their safety and the beneficiaries safety as well. BZ will monitor the safety of practices and locations on an ongoing basis - and adjust project activities when threats to safety are identified in order to minimize risks. In case of severe degradation of the security conditions in northern Syria, the training center in Afrin will be used as a guesthouse where HFOs can seek protection. A safe and secure information exchange system will be established between the project field team and the team in Turkey. OCHA Turkey will support the field team in their engagement with authorities on the ground to ensure their safety and access.
