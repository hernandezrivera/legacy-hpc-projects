Project Summary

The 2023 Humanitarian Programme Cycle (HPC) is informed by lessons from programme implementation in 2022, the evolving context of nutrition in Northeast (NE) Nigeria, the 2023 Humanitarian Action for Children (HAC) and Humanitarian (HRP), and the NE Nutrition Strategy. The nutrition response in 2023 will focus on implementing lifesaving nutrition activities, namely inpatient and outpatient treatment for acute malnutrition, and improving complementary feeding through Maternal, Infant and Young Child Nutrition in Emergencies (MIYCN-E) and the provision of micronutrient supplementation for home fortification. The 2023 HPC is expected to cover the BAY states targeting all the LGAs as much as possible, focusing more on those with the highest burden of children under five suffering from severe acute malnutrition (SAM). Most of these 29 LGAs are also projected to be in the serious and critical phases of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) during the lean season, harvest, and post-harvest periods. High-risk and hard-to-reach locations (such as Gubio, Guzamala, Magumeri, Marte and Rann LGAs) with many Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and returnee populations will be prioritized for nutrition assistance.
In 2023, UNICEF will primarily target infants and young children with SAM and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) who are vulnerable and nutritionally at risk. The nutrition response will also target all children under five and PLWs with preventive nutrition services such as micronutrient supplementation, to ensure levels of acute malnutrition are not above average thresholds and to reduce morbidity and mortality. The response will prioritize households with persons with disabilities, who face barriers in accessing health, nutrition and water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services, and who are disproportionately food insecure due to high levels of exclusion.
The nutrition status of the targeted population in the BAY states significantly deteriorates every year between May and August, which is both the lean season and coincides with the rainy season. This period is characterized by very high rates of acute malnutrition, medical complications due to poor food access, and a high prevalence of diarrhoea, malaria and measles. UNICEF will need to adequately prepare for the lean season by securing a healthy pipeline for commodities and prepositioning supplies, to increase the overall sector capacity to treat acute malnutrition with a focus on inpatient care and IYCF-E.
The nutrition response in the BAY states will be contextualized in 2023, based on the following categories:

Protracted crisis: locations where the crisis persists, characterized by unstable livelihoods and high levels of acute malnutrition (above 10 per cent GAM), as reported in the Nigeria Food Security Strategy (NFSS), round 12. High acute malnutrition rates among vulnerable populations have been reported consistently in north and central Borno LGAs, worsened by poor access to food and health systems. The approach in these areas will include partnerships with International Non-governmental Organizations/National Non-governmental Organizations. Direct support to the affected population will be provided through the government and via prioritization of lifesaving nutrition activities, including inpatient and outpatient treatment of SAM, and improving maternal newborn and child nutrition in emergency feeding (MIYCN-E). Hard-to-reach/partially accessible areas under the protracted crisis comprise locations with a limited humanitarian footprint and access, such as Gubio, Guzamala, Magumeri and Marte LGAs. The approach in these locations will include a rapid response mechanism (RRM) and remote management strategy (where feasible), focusing on life-saving interventions. 1 - Needs

According to the Northeast Nigeria Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance conducted in October 2022, Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates (percentage of children that are acutely malnourished) have significantly increased in Borno state from 11.8 per cent in October 2021 to 12.3 per cent, whereas GAM rates in Yobe state have slightly decreased from 14.1 per cent in October 2021 to 10.6 per cent. GAM rates in Adamawa state (7.2 per cent) have remained relatively stable but are still above the acceptable threshold (less than 5 per cent). The October 2022 Famine Monitoring System (FMS) results show that levels of acute malnutrition among new arrivals from inaccessible areas are serious (IPC phase 4), with overall GAM rates of 20.1 per cent and rates of SAM at 7.2 per cent. The high levels of acute malnutrition indicate an extremely stressed population affected by food insecurity, poor water and sanitation access, and poor health conditions as the key underlying causes of acute malnutrition. In 2023, over two million children under five and 178,501 PLWs are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition and require highly specialized nutrition, supplemental feeding and health services to save their lives and prevent an overall spike in mortality. In addition, over 3 million children under five and PLW will require nutrition preventive services. To meet the targeted population's nutritional needs, the Nutrition Sector will need to strengthen and scale-up nutrition treatment services significantly, including outpatient therapeutic programmes, improving maternal and young child nutrition programmes, micronutrient supplementation, and promotion of maternal nutrition and IYCF practices.

2 - Activities

Strategic Objective 1: Strengthen the quality and scale of preventive nutrition services for the most vulnerable groups through supplementary feeding activities, appropriate IYCF practices, micronutrient supplementation and optimal maternal nutrition.- Provision of micronutrient powders (MNP) distribution and Small-Quantity Lipid-based Nutrient Supplement (SQ-LNS) to children aged 6 - 23 months for point-of-use fortification to prevent micronutrient deficiencies; - Iron and Folic Acid (IFA) supplementation for pregnant women; and adolescent girls- Skilled MIYCN counselling for PLWs to strengthen feeding practices. Strategic Objective 2: Improve access to quality curative nutrition services through the early identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases in collaboration with the health sector to enhance sustainability Procurement of nutrition supplies/commodities (e.g., Ready to Use Therapeutic Foods (RUTF), therapeutic milks such as F75 & F100, ReSoMal, weighing scales and medications); Treatment of SAM for children aged 0 - 59 months through Outpatient Therapeutic Programmes (OTP) and stabilization centres (SC); Enhance and improve community mobilization for active case finding and programme monitoring; and Ensure robust programme monitoring and data collection. Strategic Objective 3: Reinforce appropriate coordination with other sectors and strengthen situation monitoring by undertaking nutrition surveys, joint assessments and analysis, while strengthening integrated response that mainstreams protection.- Conduct nutrition surveys and joint assessments with other sectors such as Food Security, Health and WASH; and- CVA for positive nutrition outcomes Expected ResultsThe proposed activities will strengthen the management of SAM in the BAY states by providing the timely screening of children aged 6-59 months, and referrals and management of children with SAM, with and without medical complications in Outpatient Therapeutic Centres (OTPs) and Stabilization Centres (SCs). The project will integrate the management of SAM cases and MIYCN into existing health services in each LGA health system using government staff.
