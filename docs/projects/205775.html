Project Summary

South Sudan remains one of the most food insecure countries globally and in the East Africa region with over 56 percent of the population expected to be acutely food insecure between December 2023 and July 2024 with insufficient means of producing or purchasing food, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes will be widespread at the peak of the lean season. Household stocks depleted earlier than usual in many areas, income-generating opportunities remain limited, and staple food prices are typically high, exacerbated by disrupted trade flows and increased demand related to the climate shocks, economic crisis, conflict, and Sudan and Ethiopia spillover are leading to a persistent food insecurity in South Sudan.
Currently, the humanitarian response plan and FSL cluster target 3.5 million people with the current projection of 5.83 million people (46 percent) of the population facing high levels of acute food insecurity, 5.78 million (46 percent) in the first project between December 2023 - March 2024.
According to the IPC analysis and report there are 7.10 million people in need of urgent humanitarian assistance.
In Jonglei State and Pibor Administrative area, an estimated 632,000 people (61% of the state’s population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 6,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, 269,000 people are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 357,000 people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Of the 6,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, 3,000 people are in Duk County and 3,000 people are in Nyriol County with expected increased in Akobo and Pochalla. Overall, six counties (Canal/Pigi, Duk, Fangak, Nyirol, Pibor, and Uror) are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, 4 counties (Akobo, Ayod, Pochalla, and Twic East) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 1 county (Bor South) is classified in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity.
In the lean season projection period between April 2024 and July 2024, an estimated 696,000 people (68% of the state’s population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in Jonglei State, of which 11,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, 300,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 385,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. All of the 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity are in Pibor County of Pibor Administrative Area. All the counties in the state will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, except for Bor South, Pochalla, and Twic East counties that will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. While recent trends and forecasts suggest the likelihood of escalating conflict or catastrophic flooding is declining and Jonglei has high prevalence of food insecurity with 125,000 population in (IPC Phase 1), 274,000 in (IPC Phase 2), 357,000 in (IPC Phase 3), 269,000 in (IPC Phase IPC 4), and 6,000 IPC Phase 5 (catastrophic level) wit resurgence of conflicts, flooding and returnees still poses a risk of isolating households from already-scarce sources of food. Out of this population in Jonglei and Pibor Administrative Area, 165,445 people are targeted in the three counties, Akobo (43,450), Pochalla (4,885), and Pibor (117,110).

1. Needs

In Jonglei State and Pibor Administrative area, an estimated 632,000 people (61% of the state’s population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity,of which 6,000 people are in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, 269,000 people are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 357,000 people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Of the 6,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, 3,000 people are in Duk County and 3,000 people are in Nyriol County with expected increase in Akobo and Pochalla. Overall, six counties (Canal/Pigi, Duk, Fangak, Nyirol, Pibor, and Uror) are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, 4 counties (Akobo, Ayod, Pochalla, and Twic East) are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 1 county (Bor South) is classified in Stressed (IPC Phase 2) acute food insecurity. Food insecurity is driven by multiple shocks including the continuation of flooding in western Jonglei as well as sub-national and localized insecurity and violence, including incidents of raiding and looting, leading to significant internal displacement, restrictions on market access, and reduced access to livelihoods activities. Unusually high food prices stemming from macro-level currency depreciation as well as supply chain disruptions from the conflict in Sudan have additionally reduced household purchasing power and contributed to significant food access challenges. Irregular rains and prolonged dry spells, flooding of cropland and pastureland, and crop damage from pests (e.g., fall armyworm, African armyworm, quelea birds etc.) and diseases have led to reduced crop production impacting on-farm income and own food consumption. Cattle raiding and livestock diseases have reduced asset holdings and own food consumption. Suspensions of humanitarian assistance resulting from incidents of looting, as well as reductions in overall humanitarian food assistance tied to funding limitations have affected household food access. Recent influxes of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia have additionally stressed local resources with many households hosting the newly arrived. In the lean season projection period between April 2024 and July 2024, an estimated 696,000 people (68% of the state’s population) are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in Jonglei State, of which 11,000 people will likely be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, 300,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 385,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. All of the 11,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity are in Pibor County of Pibor Administrative Area. All the counties in the state will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, except for Bor South, Pochalla, and Twic East counties that will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. While recent trends and forecasts suggest the likelihood of escalating conflict or catastrophic flooding out of this population in Jonglei and Pibor Administrative Area, 165,445 people are targeted in the three counties, Akobo (43,450), Pochalla (4,885), and Pibor (117,110).
