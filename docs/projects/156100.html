Needs

The 2018 Syria HNO reported that “Children continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights on a daily basis. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern………This toxic environment leaves many girls and boys exposed to psychosocial risks (e.g. sense of security, purpose, control and significance, exercising social agency) and experiencing profound distress”. Children continue to endure multiple protection risks and violations of their rights on a daily basis. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern with countless children killed and injured through persistent use of explosive weapons in civilian areas, recruitment and use of children, torture, detention,abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access, particularly to children living in UN-declared besieged areas.108 This toxic environment leaves many girls and boys exposed to psychosocial risks (e.g. sense of security, purpose, control and significance, exercising socialagency) and experiencing profound distress. Concern identified, in a recent assessment in Ar-Raqqa Governorate, a number of alarmingly widespread issues Including, a high number of women leaded households and unaccompanied minors; almost 80% of the households interviewed through the in-take survey for a CFS in Ar-Raqqa Governorate were female headed with several unaccompanied minors sheltered in temporary foster families. These vulnerable categories are exposed to a range of different forms of harm, from forced recruitment into armed groups, to SGBV, from conflict related trauma to early marriage. Women and children have the least access to basic psychosocial support services, psychological counselling and child friendly spaces. Other issues which compound these difficulties are security concerns, distance, and restrictions on the movement of IDPs into areas controlled by the self-administration, which is why a tailored and targeted response directly into the most conflict affected area and recently IS liberated areas is necessary to reach the most vulnerable. Implementation of activities

Concern will working with 600 Children through 8 Child Friendly Spaces across north east Syria. Each space had two sessions per day for three hours, five days per week. For each session, the children will be divided into smaller groups and take turns for the different activities with the 3 teachers (music, art, sport) and the Protection Assistant. These activities will be used by the facilitators as non-formal education tools to enhance the children’s development and learning. The spaces offer protection and empowered so that they become more resilient and build their own positive coping mechanisms to deal with trauma or experiences of violence, abuse, or exploitation. To reinforce child protection and well-being, all children enrolled in the CFS will participate in age appropriate sessions on mine risk awareness, on the importance of education or on basic hygiene and health information sessions to build their life skills. Awareness-raising sessions will also be conducted with 600 caregivers and are aimed at increasing each caregiver’s capacity to protect their child’s rights and physical and psychosocial well-being. In these sessions caregivers will be given an introduction to Concern’s CFS, the services available to them and the complaint mechanisms. Given the serious presence of mines in the project’s districts, the initial session will also focuse on mine risk awareness. Caregivers are taught how to identify different types of unexploded ordnances (UXOs) and improvised explosive devices (IEDs) and advised on different ways to communicate the hazards of these devices to their children. Other sessions given to caregivers are focused on protection issues and children’s rights to minimize exploitation risks, gender-based violence, raising awareness on the consequences of child labour and discussing the importance of education. Children, adolescent girls and boys and caregivers in Raqqa, Tabqa and Der Ez Zor will be provided with targeted psychosocial assistance through the establishment of protection community centres and child friendly spaces. All Concern staff will be trained on protection subjects and best practices, in order to mainstream protection in all programs' stages and ensure access to wider range of specific services for beneficiaries through effective use of referral pathways.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk- There is a broader risk of more widespread resumption of conflict between the SDF, GOS or FSA groups supported by Turkey, especially in border districts such as Manbij and Tabqa. In 2018, peaking at the close of the year, Turkey has taken a much more aggressive stance against areas controlled by the self-administration in North East Syria and is threatening to hold a third military campaign there to establish a buffer zone along the length of the border. There is also the risk that the fragile coalition of parties and groups that form the Syrian Democratic Council could unravel as a result of internal differences and/or external pressure, which would weaken the local authorities and likely spell increased conflict, criminality and insecurity. This situation might prevent Concern working in several areas in NES, could lead to the evacuation of international staff, scaling down to emergency assistance or closing down of some or all operations depending on the severity and scale of the conflict. - Further escalation of the conflict, or deterioriation in the security situation, in Der Ez Zor as a result of any of the following: IS resurgence particularly in the wake of reduction in support from coalition or redirection of military efforts to other conflict areas; tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict; movement of GoS troops into the area. In these instances, partial or complete access to Der Ez Zor - where a significant proportion of this Action's activities are targeted, would be prevented. AssumptionsThe security situation will continue to improve in Der Ez Zor, enabling Concern to establish a field office in Basira in the first quarter of 2019 and to expand activities from the West line to the North Line and East Line. - The self-administration/SDC retains control over key locations west and south of the Euphrates, specifically Manbij and Tabqa. - Concern will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly Mitigating MesuresConcern has developed, in coordination with other NGO actors and the NGO forum, its own scenario analysis and contingency-planning. In the event of wider political instability, insecurity or resumption of conflict - particularly in the wake of the likely US troop withdrawl in early-mid 2019. Concern's response would depend on the severity of the scenario. In the event of localised insecurity/access restrictions/power-changes in one or several locations, Concern would likely suspend operations in these areas and shift its resources to its nearest other functional field offices to assist those newly dispaced and affected by these events. If international staff had to be scaled down or pulled out completely (due to border access issues, loss of emergency evacuation options), Concern would remotely manage staff and the project from its support office in Dohuk and using its remote-management team based in Turkey
