Project Summary

Three district Protection, Gender and Inclusion rapid assessments done by Red Cross in drought affected districts, have clearly shown the need for community engagement in dialogues on GBV risks in their community, mitigation measure that could be implemented, stigmatisation around SGBV victims and lack of appropriate service provisions for GBV victims.
Not only community members and leaders, need a good understanding of the stigmatization around SGBV, but also health service providers and not least Humanitarian practitioners need increased capacity and understanding of the rights, health and wellbeing measures for affected victims.
This project will facilitate community awareness raising, community dialogues and inter-generational dialogues on the risks to GBV in times of emergencies and most importantly, for them to understand their rights in relation to GBV, including reporting, access to services, referral pathways and access to justice. This will be disseminated through community sessions, drama and IEC material, School clubs at high-schools etc.
Humanitarian field practitioners and health service providers in e.g. safe spaces and clinics need increased capacity for onwards training and practising of Psychological First Aid (PFA) for at risk and victims of GBV. There is a need at service provision level to be able to provide support through dignity kits and referral pathway support, something the project will facilitate and support.
Community volunteers and Red Cross youth club members will be trained in PFA, GBV prevention and mitigation measures, and on victim’s reintegration needs - for volunteers’ dissemination to fellow community members and be proactive in providing community services in this respect. Basic trained GBV volunteers can also be availed to other SGBV actors in the relevant communities.
The project expects to reach not less than 27.000 people in age groups specifically from 15 to 18 years, 18 – 25 years and targeted community members in 6 districts in 4 provinces in Zimbabwe.
The project will utilise the Red Cross - Minimum standard commitments to gender and diversity in emergency programming’s as standard for GBV prevention and responce, will utilise standardised messaging, IEC material, information on Referral pathways and support incl. dignity kits provision.

Plan fields

1 - Activities

1. Rapid assessment on capacities and experience of field practitioners with regards to GBV prevention, response and Psychological First Aid (PFA).2. Training of field practitioners on PFA and on providing support through dignity kits and referral pathway support at service provider level (safe houses and clinics)3. Community level GBV training is provided and through CBP approaches, communities develop GBV risk and mitigation strategies and measures. 4. Community level Psychosocial Support (PSS) interventions provided for most at risk groups and survivors of violence, including for reintegration to their communities. 5. Awareness raising sessions on GBV survivor stigmatisation and development of support groups and networks for GBV survivor reintegration in the communities. 6. Support for GBViE considerations into multi-sectoral emergency programming through dissemisation and training on the Minimum Standards for GBViE.7. Availing basic GBV trained Red Cross volunteers to other organisations and networks as The Big Sister and Big Brother Support Network, Friendship bench project, etc.8. Dissemination to the communities of the ZRCS PSEA mechanism and protocols. Provision of awareness raising sessions on SEA and humanitarian aid, how beneficiaries can report exploitation and abuse, protocols, accountability of the Red Cross and humanitarian workers and information provision through material development.

2 - Indicators

1. Under Strategic Objective 1- Number of survivors of violence, including GBV, accessing multi-sectoral services (clinical care, psychosocial support, police and legal assistance, case management, including at mobile OSCs and Community-based shelters) (GBV and CP)Outcomes: Communities, particularly vulnerable groups (women and girls and persons with disabilities) are aware of GBV, the risks to GBV in times of emergencies and most importantly, they understand their rights in relation to GBV, including reporting, access to services, access to justice. Communities, particularly vulnerable groups (women and girls and persons with disabilities) will be aware of the referral pathways and avenues of reporting SGBV to relevant authorities and how to access key services (medical, legal, PSS, shelter)2. Under Strategic Objective 2- Number of vulnerable women and children reached with community-based PSS interventions, including at child and women safe spaces (GBV and CP)- Number of individuals reached with community-based GBViE risk mitigation, PSEA and CP outreach and surveillance (GBV and CP)Outcomes: The project will engage communities and through community-based approaches, the communities will develop mitigation strategies and networks through community sessions, drama, IEC materials and other participatory methodologies. These methodologies will serve as psychosocial support to GBV survivors but also as means of engaging the most excluded groups, such as persons with diabilities, to community SGBV resilience programming. An important part of this capacity building will also be raising awareness to communities, as well as field practitioners on PSEA from humanitarians workers and will inform communities on the existing ZRCS PSEA mechanism. 3. Under Strategic Objective 3- Number of multi-sectoral Humanitarian actors trained to integrate GBViE and CPiE interventions- Number of multi-sectoral humanitarian actors trained on PSEA- Number of specialized GBV and CP service providers and community cadres trained in GBV and CP in emergencies responseOutcomes: These indicators focus on strengthening the capacity, expertise and participation of SGBV field practitioners to serve SGBV survivors, and specifically vulnerable groups, more effectively. Through the development of partnerships that focus on capacity building, the Red Cross project will ensure that community Safe Houses and health clinics have the capacity and ability to offer PFA (Psychological First Aid) services, dignity kits, advice and messaging on services thus strengthening the existing referral pathways and dissemination of information on these pathways.
