Project Summary

During 2021, surveys, market assessment, and needs assessments will be conducted to provide better analysis of the shelter requirements of crisis affected individuals and households. IOM will prioritize rehabilitation of shelters and provision of durable shelters for the most vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees. There are approximately 2.4 million IDPs and 700,000 returnees in Sudan in need of assistance. IOM will expand the provision of shelter assistance and skills building training packages including training on durable and local construction techniques and environmental awareness messaging to reduce hazard mitigation. The provision of durable shelters with resources bought from local markets to support host communities will be encouraged. Shelter support will provide beneficiaries with protection from risks and the elements such as floods, windstorms etc. These shelters will be built on durable brick foundations, with metal structures for the window-frames and door frames to enhance longevity and provide resistance against external elements and natural disaster. This activity will also support capacity development at the local level in construction techniques adopting a Training of Trainers (ToT) approach. By enhancing the skills of the community and enabling the target beneficiaries trained in construction techniques to assist others in constructing shelters, the selected beneficiaries could possibly generate a household income. The training sessions will be conducted with the support of local authorities, targeting beneficiaries, communities, and community leaders. Finally, with regards to both the ToT and shelter assessments, there will be an equal participation of both male and female participants to ensure a greater representation of women in both the operational planning (supporting women to choose whether they wish to be involved in the construction processes) as well as to design mitigation measures against Gender Based Violence (GBV) and other protection measures as coordinated with the protection sector.

1 - Needs

There are approximately 2.4 million IDPs and 700,000 returnees in Sudan in need of assistance as mentioned in the HNO 2021. The greatest proportion of displacement in the country is protracted (ongoing for more than five years). Subsequently, an estimated 1,527,807 current IDPs (64%) were initially displaced between 2003 and 2010 at the height of the Darfur crisis. In comparison, 76,416 IDPs (3%) have been newly displaced in 2018 and 39,379 (2%) in 2019 – demonstrating a decreasing trend in new displacement and potentially reflective of a reduction in insecurities across the region. In terms of population presence, the state with the highest number of IDPs is South Darfur – having recorded an estimated 793,328 individuals (33% of the total IDP count) – followed by North Darfur (21%). Comparatively, West Kordofan is estimated to have the lowest proportion of 73,015 IDPs (3%), followed by East Darfur (4%). North Darfur has the greatest number of IDP locations – consisting of 256 sites (34% of the total), followed by South Kordofan (27%). East Darfur has the fewest number of IDP locations and represents just one per cent of the total location count. It is evident that IDPs are more highly concentrated in the Darfur states than in Kordofan. Central Darfur, for example, has 17 locations (2% of the total) and is hosting on average 22,189 IDPs per location, in contrast to West Kordofan which has a far sparser distribution – averaging 493 individuals per site. This dynamic is likely attributed to the protracted nature of displacement ongoing since 2003, as well as the high presence of densely populated camps in Darfur in comparison to more informal gathering-sites in Kordofan. According to MSNA 65% of refugee, IDP and host community live in substandard shelter so this type of project will be very helpful for these vulnerable populations. IOM will support rehabilitation of existing shelters, provision of durable shelters, and provide skills building training packages including local construction techniques and environmental awareness messaging to reduce hazard mitigation. The provision of durable shelters with resources bought from local markets to support host communities will be encouraged. Shelter support will provide beneficiaries with protection from risks and the elements such as floods, windstorms etc. These shelters will be built on durable brick foundations, with metal structures for the window frames and door frames to enhance longevity and provide resistance against external elements and natural disaster. This activity will also support capacity development at the local level in construction techniques adopting a Training of Trainers (ToT) approach. By enhancing the skills of the community and enabling the target beneficiaries trained in construction techniques to assist others in constructing shelters, the selected beneficiaries could possibly generate a household income. The training sessions will be conducted with the support of local authorities, targeting beneficiaries, communities, and community leaders. Finally, with regards to both the ToT and shelter assessments, there will be an equal participation of both male and female participants to ensure a greater representation of women in both the operational planning (supporting women to choose whether they wish to be involved in the construction processes) as well as to design mitigation measures against Gender Based Violence (GBV) and other protection measures as coordinated with the protection sector.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Outcome 1: Surveys, market assessments and needs assessments provide better analysis of the shelter requirements of individuals (people with special needs (PWSN), elderly, vulnerable individuals, women, men, boys, girls and other vulnerable groups). Outcome 2: Target beneficiaries have the skills and knowledge to construct and rehabilitate durable shelters and mitigate hazards.Outcome 3: Durable shelters are available to the most vulnerable populations. Activities:1.Conduct markets / needs assessments to identify and prioritize the most vulnerable protracted IDPs and returnees within each locality. During the assessment and project design process, consultation with the community including gender sensitive groupings, will be conducted to facilitate input from underrepresented groups where they may otherwise be reluctant to speak by having focus group discussions. It is expected that the shelters will benefit approximately 15,000 individuals. Following assessments, GBV risk mitigation measures will be integrated in the overall shelter design, location, and trainings. 2.Coordinate with local authorities, community leaders and communities to formulate land review committees. Ensure that committees will be composed of at least 55% female and 45% are male participants from IDP, returnee and host communities. The land review committees will conduct risk assessments to ensure that durable shelters are built on risk free zones and ensure the land is free of claim.3.Conduct shelter survey to identify shelter designs which are in line with the sector standards / modules that will be both culturally acceptable and climatically appropriate (with durable structural design to withstand natural hazards such as floods) for each locality with the intention of constructing approximately 2,000 shelters targeting approximately 10,000 beneficiaries.4.Procure and purchase locally sourced and environmentally friendly materials for the construction of durable shelters.5.Provide vouchers to 5,000 beneficiaries to purchase materials for shelters upgrade / rehabilitation to meet their needs with contracted suppliers at the field level.6.Train beneficiaries on durable shelter construction and rehabilitation as an income generating activity targeting protracted IDP and returnee communities, community-based organizations (CBOs) and local partners. Training will include durable construction techniques to ensure that structures will be capable of withstanding natural hazards such as heavy rains, strong winds, dust storms (haboobs) etc. The safe construction training includes compressed stabilized soil blocks (SSB) technique, SSB machine usage, roof fixing through the production of fortified grass mats, proper drainage system around structures, as well as ensuring proper spaces between shelters.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

IOM will integrate environmental considerations into all programmes, and it will follow applicable international requirements. IOM will procure and purchase locally sourced and environmentally friendly materials such as SSB and grass mats for the construction of durable shelters, when materials are available, to ensure that environmental impact is limited. The shelter construction will include proper drainage systems around structures to reduce flood related risks and ensure safe distance between shelter structures to reduce fire related risks and damage to the surrounding environment. Window frames and door frames will be constructed with metal to ensure durability and additional locks fitted on the doors to increase security – these will also provide more privacy to enhance protection for both women and girls.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IOM will ensure to prioritize (1) safety and security issues ensuring that shelters are built in areas where there is access to other basic services and possible land tenure / ownership issues (ensure the land is free of claim) are discussed between local authorities and among the community members before shelters are constructed. (2) Ensure that all community members including boys, girls, men and women and persons with special needs are assessed and those who are found to be most vulnerable are prioritized to receive durable shelters, as well as equitable access to construction trainings and skills development. (3) Through the inclusion of women in the trainings and shelter design, structures will be gender sensitive to accommodate additional gender-based vulnerabilities and protection risks. Locks will be fitted on the doors to increase security.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

All IOM staff are carefully trained in codes of conduct and PSEA, prior to any engagement with affected communities. Trainings will be refreshed on a frequent basis. The project will embrace accountability to affected populations, including host communities in all processes of project design and implementation. Vulnerabilities will be taken into consideration starting from the first phase - registration of returnees, immediate assessment of critical/humanitarian needs of affected migrants/returnees to the implementation of the interventions. IOM will consult with local authorities and community members including vulnerable groups such as persons with disabilities, older persons and women and girls on the design and locations for transitional shelters. People with disabilities and special needs will receive additional support for construction of their shelters and have priority. IOM will ensure that there is a community-based feedback mechanism to take into account community complaints and suggestions and ensure that community members participate in the training and have ownership throughout the process. In addition, IOM has developed a Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) format that will track the implementation of project activities and solicit feedback from beneficiaries face to face through visiting random households. PDM activities will include interviews with beneficiaries to understand their views on quality and appropriateness of support provided. The system has also been developed to assess the short-term effects of transitional shelters in improving living conditions of IDPs/returnees. Information generated will be shared with the ES/NFI working group at the state level while policy or programmatic issues will be taken up in Khartoum during the ES/NFI Advisory Panel Meetings. To achieve this objective, the PDM plan has been developed and outlines data collection methods (household interviews, Focus Group Discussion and observation), data sources, time frame, responsibilities, and handover procedures. Methodologies used will include a desk review before the field exercises are conducted.
