Needs

Camps and sites remain congested seriously impacting physical and psychological well-being, especially of children, women, and persons with disabilities. The round 12 NPM report indicates an average area of 18.76 m2 per person, significantly lower than the sphere standard recommended for site planning of 45 m2 per person. Accordingly, most shelters do not adhere to the covered living space of 3.5 m2 per person. Moreover, the lack of adequate lighting in shelters and public spaces was reported as a top safety concern followed by danger or violence when collecting firewood. Female key informants highlighted that the recent monsoon season had weakened the shelters. Bamboo has also deteriorated due to beetle infestation, resulting in a situation in which shelters can easily be broken into. Several such cases resulting in thefts were reported. Female key informants also emphasized the discomfort in existing shelters due to humidity, especially during the hottest portions of the day. The Shelter Survey was conducted by Shelter/NFI Sector partners in July and August of 2018. Main concerns raised by beneficiaries on their current shelters were related to the strength of shelters, quality of material, lack of private cooking and bathing spaces, and lack of ventilation and privacy. This was also confirmed during the technical visits as it was observed that most ties between structural elements were poorly executed (insufficient rope), 53% HH had foundations that lacked anchoring, and bracing was only found in one shelter visited. Insufficient household level site improvements was also observed. It was observed through HH level assessments that 1/3 of tarpaulin is damaged and that 55% of big bamboo and 50% of small bamboo will need to be replaced in the next 6 months due to infestation of borers. It was observed that 88% of households cook inside of their shelters and 12% of households have their kitchen attached to their shelter. In only four cases was the cooking area protected with non-flammable material. The lack of non-flammable material and shelters constructed in close proximity represents a significant fire hazard that will need to be addressed. Cooking in the shelter has also negative impact on the air quality especially as the materials used do not permit air circulation for walls and roofing. The majority of shelters observed having a height less than 180cm (floor to wall plate) and only 9% of shelters being well ventilated. Ensuring the durable shelter, low smoke cook stoves with LPG and Improved lighting system inside the home is very important. 27,459 households (13% of target) have received alternative cooking fuel (liquid petroleum gas). 5,082 households have benefitted from Transitional and Mid-Term Shelters (as of 15 November 2018) and required more durable shelter to support 180,000 HHs in 2019. According to most recent family counting data, approximately 52% of the Rohingya refugee population are women and girls with the largest gender discrepancy being among the population of working age (age 18-59) where 23% are female. Overall 80 per cent of the Rohingya population are women and children. Key vulnerable groups have been identified as single mothers (16%), person with serious medical condition (5%), older persons at risk (2%), persons with disabilities (4%), older person with child (2%), separated children (2%), unaccompanied children (1%), single male parent with infant (1%)_UNHCR Bangladesh Population Fact Sheet as of 31st October 2018. In the above context, Qatar Charity will implement Midterm Shelter, Improved HH level lighting solution and Alternative Cooking Fuel in Shelter and NFI sector and support other partners to continue services and scale up operations for Rohingya community.

Activities or outputs

Key Activities: • Mid Term Shelter: 1,000 HHs will receive the assistance;• Improved HH level lighting solution: 8,300 HHs will receive the assistance; • Alternative Cooking Fuel: 8,300 HHs will receive LPG including low smoke cook stoves, accessories, cylinder and refill assistance. Sub Activities are following: 1. Coordination with Shelter cluster, Site management and Relevant Stakeholders to avoid overlapping and coordination, 2. Focus Group Discussion with community, 3. Procurement of shelter materials, 4. Provide training to communities on shelter construction and uses of low smoke cook stoves with LPG and improved lighting system 5. Maintain Supply Chain, 6. Staff training on SPHERE, CHS, Protection mainstreaming Principles and CRM; 7. Establish helpdesk and complaint response mechanism, 8. Ensure safety and dignity of beneficiaries and principals of do no harm in all level of project implementation, 9. Equal and impartial access to assistance and service and targeting the vulnerable groups. 10. Maintain database with disaggregated information (Sex, age, disability and context specific vulnerability), 11. Conduct satisfactory survey, 12. Regular report to Shelter cluster and concern departments.
