Project Summary

While building on the achievements and lessons learned from the education in emergencies (EIE) interventions implemented in 2019, 2020, and 2021 under ECW FER, CERF education project, ECW Cameroon COVID FER project, and GPE COVID-19 Emergency Response Project in the Education and Training Sector in Cameroon, and in coordination with Education Cluster, the UNESCO proposes to continue targeting out-of-school children to access relevant, quality and inclusive learning and minimize drop-out for those enrolled in formal and non-formal settings, in particular, girls and Internally Displaced Populations (IDP’s). The project will continue to improve the quality and delivery of inclusive distance learning opportunities in the NWSW, West, and Littoral crisis-affected regions, and improve pupils learning outcomes to promote the transition to formal education.
The EiE projects in 2021 enabled access to the online education platform myschoolonline.cm for over 360,000 learners (170,165 girls and 190,196 boys) while 14,645 out-of-school children (8,457 girls and 6,543 boys) are accessing learning in non-formal community learning spaces operated by the local NGOs in 7 Sub-Regions in NWSW. Additionally, UNESCO has ensured multi-channeled diffusion of learning content national wide through 47 community radios targeting 886,418 learners at the primary level and 425,582 learners at the secondary level. Further 2,093 teachers (909 female and 1,184 male) have been trained in how to provide psychological support and facilitate resilience within learners.
UNESCO will therefore use these achievements and lessons to improve the quality of learning and inform further expansion in NWSW, as well as Littoral and West Regions, to provide access to learning for the IDP families that have settled there from NWSW. Through this action, UNESCO seeks to reach in 2022 at least 154,355 learners in school-age 4-14 years old (86,202 girls and 68,153 boys) by: i) strengthening the quality and delivery of distance learning (online and off-line) to improve pupils learning outcomes and
promote the transition to formal education. This includes building the capacity of learning facilitators and supervisors on learning content, pedagogical skills, learner assessment, and SEL. Further UNESCO will strengthen the E-learning Resource Centers recently established at the University of Buea (UB) to manage distance learning and provide continuous support to teachers and learning facilitators in the Regions. Thirdly, UNESCO proposes to improve the distance-learning infrastructure through the provision of IT Equipment and learning materials (mobile learning units, Tablets, USB sticks, and Elearning portal, printed books) including the production and dissemination of additional prerecorded lessons and internet-based online/offline learning modules, to effectively reach the additional learners. ii) To improve the quality of the learning environment in crisis-affected regions UNESCO will train 105 teachers, supervisors, and other education staff (58 female and 47 male) in Child protection, Protection and Education in Emergencies, Life skills and psychological support, and Coordination and Information management. iii) To further improve access, UNESCO proposes the provision of capitation grants targeting disadvantaged families to reach 8,400 (4,620 girls and 3,780 boys) pupils and ensure that school-aged children do not remain out of school. The grants will be delivered to schools that are operating at a significantly reduced enrollment rate to absorb more learners. iv) To improve access to education for children with disabilities and promote inclusivity, UNESCO will purchase and distribute inclusive & sexo-specific supplies to children living with disabilities.
Protection and Safe COVID-19 messaging will be cross-cutting themes.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The socio-political crisis in NW/SW has led to massive population displacements, with spillover effects in the West and Littoral regions and increased vulnerabilities of people who have often left their villages to live in the bush or as IDPs. Additionally, the number of IDPs from NW/SW in the Littoral, West, and Centre regions has also remained high in 2021. A majority of the displaced are women and children amongst whom are unaccompanied and separated children. The ban on education by non-State armed groups (NSAGs) and attacks on education have forced many families to send their children to the Littoral, West, and Centre regions where they can pursue their education. On 6 September, school doors for the 2021-2022 academic year opened in Cameroon, including in the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions. However, two out of three schools remained closed, keeping over 700,000 children and adolescents out of school. According to statistics from SW education authorities, collected after the first week of schooling, 53 per cent of secondary schools in the region were not functional. At the basic education level, 49 per cent of primary schools and 47 per cent of Nursery schools still have their doors closed. In the NW region, the situation is worse as only 23 per cent of secondary schools were functional as of 15 September. Non-State state armed groups (NSAGs) continue to intimidate parents, children, and teachers with attacks on education. By end of November, NSAGs carried out several attacks from kidnapping eight students in Bamenda town in the NW, attacking a girl and chopped her fingers for going to school during the imposed lockdown in Donga-Mantung division. They kidnapped five public school principals in Ngo-Ketunjia division and killed one of them, six days later. On 24 November, Government Bilingual High School in Ekondo Titi, South-West region was attacked and four students aged 12 to 17 and a teacher were killed, and an unconfirmed number of students and teachers were injured.On 11 September, NSAG announced a general lockdown in the two regions from 15 September to 2 October 2021. The decision banned all movement, work, and social activities. Access to basic services including health, education, and livelihoods was severely affected, and protection activities were severely impacted. During the lockdown, all schools and community learning spaces were closed, except for some schools in a few urban areas which operated at less than 60 per cent of their capacity, compared to the first week of the 2021-2022 academic year.With the above scenario, the needs that gave rise to this response still suffice as the education situation continues to be in distress. Out-of-school adolescents, youth, and particularly girls continue to be at risk of gender-based violence, as they face early marriage, recruitment into armed groups, or adopt other negative coping mechanisms, like child labour to survive. Girls, including primary school students, are reportedly recurring to prostitution to be able to pay for their education. Children with special needs continue to be marginalized and face significant barriers to access education, as they do not have the appropriate support, equipment, or materials to learn at home, while the few open schools are ill-equipped and with low capacity to support them effectively. The strategy proposed by UNESCO will continue targeting out-of-school children to access learning and minimize drop-out for those enrolled in formal and non-formal settings, with a special focus on girls and children with disabilities, and Internally Displaced Populations (IDP’s) while strengthening the quality and delivery of inclusive distance learning in the NWSW, South and Littoral Regions, and improve pupils learning outcomes to promote a transition to formal education.
