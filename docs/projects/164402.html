Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to provide education services for school-age children (5 -17 years) of Aleppo (Jabel Saman and Atareb Sub-districts), Hama (Suran, As-Salamiyeh and Hamra districts), Dar’a (Izra’ and Dar’a Sub-districts) and Rural Damascus (Nashabiyeh and Duma Sub-districts), focusing on areas with a severity scale of 4 to 6 (except for As-Salamiyeh Sub-district which has a Severity Scale score of 3). Locations and target have been identified based on the ongoing areas of intervention (Aleppo, Dar’a, Izra’, Nashabiyeh) and upon assessments and consultation with local authorities and stakeholders about new areas considered potentially accessible (Atareb) or remote areas where education services are needed (Suran, Hamra, As-Salamiye). The total number of beneficiaries to be served is estimated at 22.500 children, of which 10% leaving with disabilities. The access to the areas of intervention is guarantee in collaboration with SARC. Meanwhile, in 2019, COOPI engaged in the signature of MoU with Ministry of Education, that will be finalized in the near future. The project proposal is based on the ongoing experience in the Districts targeted, where COOPI is presently implementing multi-sectoral integrated program. In Aleppo city, Nashabiyeh and Dar’a, were around 13,800 people in need where reached with protection services and 4,000 HHs were supported with livelihood activities, the following proposal intends to complement the ongoing and planned intervention and respond to the needs in terms of education services, by integrating education activities in the existent static and mobile facilities, contributing both to Objective 1, “Increased safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children” and Objective 2 “The quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth enhanced”. In new areas of intervention (Atareb, Hamra, Suran and As-Salamiye), COOPI will focus on Objective 2, providing children and education personnel with school-based psychosocial support programs in formal and non-formal settings. As the impact of nine consecutive years of crisis is generically critical, the overall education system is fragmented and overburdened with multiple curricula taught, having significant repercussions on the provision and certification of learning (HRP 2019). the proposed project will be adapted to the variety of education needs in the different areas of intervention, with different approach regarding urban and rural areas, as well as the identification of static facilities rather than the provision of services through mobile team and community volunteers. COOPI will ensure synergies with other projects submitted to HRP 2020 by complementing protection, food security and livelihood services planned to promote an integrated approach and a multi-sectorial response within the local communities.

4 - Needs

In Syria, more than 6,8 million of school-aged children and almost 228,000 teachers are in need of education assistance, of whom 85% are from severe to catastrophic on the severity scale. The estimated people in need (PiN) of education assistance in the areas targeted by COOPI are 702,549 in Jabel Saman and Atareb, 72,140 in Hamra, Suran and As-Salamiye, 99,077 in Nashabiyeh and Duma, 96,414 in Dar’a and Izra’. As highlighted in HRP 2019, teenagers between 15 and 17 years old are the most affected, as they represent the 40% of out-of-school children (OOSC) in Syria and in need of of different learning, skills development and livelihoods support. Since October 2019 in North East Syria and more recently in the northwestern region, where more than 6,500 children forced to flee every day (Statement by UNICEF Executive Director Henrietta Fore, 1st of February 2020), the escalation of violence has contributed to conflict-related psychological trauma for children, their families and education personnel. The protracted exposure to violence decreases attendance to school, leads to drop out, and affects learning outcomes by limited concentration caused by psychological stress. In addition, harassment, bullying, violence, and poverty push children to drop out from school exposing them to increased protection risks, including child marriage and child labour. A particular effort is needed to assist children with a disability, 50% of whom, in some areas, have an unmet need for education. As highlighted in the IASC Guidelines on the inclusion of persons with disabilities in humanitarian action (2019), learners with disabilities are routinely the most marginalized and excluded group, especially in emergencies context as Syria. Therefore, the project will respond to the need of overcoming the barriers (both in term of physical access and lack of inclusive education systems or special education curricula) keeping learners with disabilities out of the education system in Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

In consultation with stakeholders and in agreement with the Ministry of Education, the proposal is designed to facilitate the enrollment, retention and reinsertion of dropout children at school. The action proposed intends to complement the ongo-ing activities in Livelihood and Protection sectors in Aleppo, Nashabiyeh and Dar’a. The action contributes to Objective 1 in supporting access to, and retention in schools for vulnerable children with the following activities: (1.4) for 2500 beneficiaries in Aleppo, 1500 in Hama, 1200 in Dar’a, 800 in Duma (1.8) for 920 beneficiaries in Aleppo, 500 in Hama, 400 in Dar’a, 250 in Duma; (1.9) for 2 facilities in Aleppo, 3 in Hama, 2 in Dar’a, 2 in Duma; (1.10) for 1500 beneficiaries in Aleppo, 1000 in Hama, 600 in Dar’a, 400 in Duma; (1.11) for 360 beneficiaries in Aleppo, 300 in Hama, 100 in Dar’a, 100 in Duma; (1.13) for 250 benefi-ciaries in Aleppo, 150 in Hama, 50 in Dar’a, 50 in Duma. The action contributes to Objective 2 to provide children with a safe and protected environment with the following activities: (2.4a/2.4b) for 1100 children beneficiaries and 60 teachers in Aleppo, 700 children and 90 teachers in Hama, 400 children and 60 teachers in Dar’a, 300 children and 60 teachers in Duma; (2.5) for 1600 children beneficiaries in Aleppo, 1100 children in Hama, 700 children in Dar’a, 400 children in Duma; (2.8) for 1200 children beneficiaries in Aleppo, 800 children in Hama, 600 children in Dar’a, 400 children in Duma. MEAL Officers will analyse the disaggregated data gathered, monitor the achievements of the two components, and together with Education specialists, will provide corrective measures to adapt the activities to the different environments in which the project will be implemented. Referring to new zones of intervention and in hard-to-reach areas, COOPI intends to deliver school-based psychosocial support programs through mobile units, employing protection staff experienced in the PSS assistance to children.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: a) Ensure Child Safeguarding polices and systems in place or support the learning facilities to develop them; b) All education personnel and COOPI staff are regularly trained on positive discipline and child safeguarding; c) Ensures the safety and confidentiality of the affected person, guaranteeing data protection policy is applied and the referral pathway is followed.Protection Risk 2: Lack of consideration of information about context and social dynamics, hostilities in new accessible areas.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigating measures: a) Ensure the new communities are informed about project activities, services provided, beneficiaries targeted and scope of the intervention; b) Recruit the staff with adequate knowledge of the context and respecting the base and gender balance.Protection Risk 3: Content and materials for BTL campaigns and psychosocial support activities on are not fully adapted to the social and cultural context and create misunderstanding or conflict among community members.Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigating measures: a) Develop feedback mechanism on the quality and usefulness of the services provided; b) Recruit staff who is familiar to the context; c) Tailor the messages content and refer to sector guidelines and SoPs.Protection Risk 4: Exploitation or abuse perpetrated by staff members or service providers when delivering assistance.Likelihood: Low. Impact: High Mitigating measures: a) Establish complaint and feedback mechanism in each catchment area; b) Provide staff with training on Code of Conduct and PSEA; c) Ensure Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies are practiced and monitored. d) Ensure the proper communication to the communities about the gratuity of the services.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

RISK: Delay in the implementation of the activities.ASSUMPTION: Delay in the signature of the MoU with Ministry of Education.MITIGATION MEASURES: COOPI will continue to follow-up the negotiations for the signature of the MoU in collaboration with SARC. COOPI will also approach Education sector to advocate with the Ministry of Education to speed up the signature process. RISK: Direct access to the target areas and HHs is not permitted or becomes unsafe for humanitarian workers. Access to new areas is granted and later withdrawn by the authorities.ASSUMPTION: Escalation of the hostilities in the targeted areas. MITIGATION MEASURES: COOPI will maintain constant communication with agencies, government and in-country security monitoring networks. All staff will receive security on the field trainings in order to gain knowledge how to act if face problems on the field.RISK: Education services provided are might not be in fully coordination with other humanitarian actors/sector of intervention.ASSUMPTION: Other humanitarian actors might not have the capacity to assist additional beneficiaries or the referral pathway or specialized services needed is not fully designed/implemented MITIGATION MEASURES: In this case COOPI will advocate with the sector to fill the gap and finding exit strategies.RISK: Beneficiaries and staff cannot access the most remote area of intervention.ASSUMPTION: The presence of UXOs may hamper access to the villages in new accessible areas.MITIGATION MEASURES: in this case COOPI will organize awareness raising sessions for staff, volunteers and communities in UXOs recognition and will coordinate with SARC for information on progress of UXOs clearance operations.
