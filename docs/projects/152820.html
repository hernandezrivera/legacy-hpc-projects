Project Summary

The aim of this response plan will be increase access, scale-up response and also strengthen the capacities of WASH interventions to provide emergency lifesaving WASHservicesto 12,121 vulnerable IDPs and host populations in Pibor; Twic East; and Bor South in Jonglei, Yirol East; and Yirol West in Lakes, Kapoeta East; and Magwi in Eastern Equatoria, and Juba in Central Equatoria States.
The LiveWell’s HRP budget for 2019 at an overall US$ 400,000 will aim at supporting the following the following level of WASH interventions in 4 former states
• Rehabilitates 15 boreholesto provide safe drinking water and nutritional centers for SAM/MAM.
• Renovates 20 pit latrines and Install new 60 temporary latrines in IDPs settings to provide safe sanitary facilities to women and girls with appropriate GBV risk analysis.
• Renovates 20 bathing shelters in the IDPs setiing
• Install 10 handwashing facilities in IDPs setting
• Distributes 2,000 WASH/Hygiene Kits for admitted SAM at OTP/SC till the household level.
• Distributes 3,000 Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits to girls/women of reproductive age
• Build the capacity of 150 health workers and 100 prefects in schools on handwashing techniques with soap to act as ambassadors of good hygiene practices to prevent WASH related diseases.
• Reached 12,121 people with basic WASH NFIs, and hygiene messages that is geared toward cholera prevention and other acute watery diarrhea.

Needs

• South Sudan’s civil unrest had continued to have profound effect on the existence and functionality of health facilities to provide lifesaving healthcare to vulnerable IDPs/host communities due to looting, destruction and killing of health workers (SSA 2017/18).• Inadequate provision of lifesaving WASH services due to inadequate funding led to inadequate access to improved services leading to increased cases of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWDs). • Recent IPC report that 25,000 people in Yirol East, Yirol West and Pibor counties may go into catastrophe (IPC 5) if there are no measures to improve post-harvest in October and December. Also, a critical GAM rate (15%-29.9%)level of acute malnutrition in Jonglei, and Lakes States while Central Equatoria state at alert rate. All is attributed to poor access to health care, nutrition and WASH services. It was recommended that a multi-sectoral humanitarian intervention be scaled up to meet the pressing needs and improved access to basic services such as health (IPC Report, September 2018).• Increased risks of Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) and cholera outbreaks in Magwi &Kapoeta, Rift Valley Fever (RVF) & measles outbreaks in Yirol East,Yirol East, and malaria endemicity in Twic East, Pibor and Bor South requires stringent health and WASH securities in the hotspots or high risks of transmission areas. (WHO EVD report at week 43, October 2018).• Low level of immunization coverage in Pibor, Twic East, Kapoeta East, Magwi, Yirol East, Yirol West, and Bor South coupled with flooding from heavy rainfall would precipitate cholera, RVF, VHF, EVD,measles, malariaand severe acute malnutrition• Epidemiological studies (2017, UNICEF) put Magwi, Juba, Kapoeta East, Twic East, Yirol East, Yirol West, and Bor South, as the highest hotspot area of cholera outbreaks in South Sudan• OCHA coordinated IRNA assessment in Pibor, Yirol East, Yirol West and Magwi verified that 17,000 IDPs were from Nyang, Lokoromach, Lekuangole, Nimule, Gei and Mapoz bomas (IRNA report, 14 April 2018). There is a deteriorating health situation in Pibor, Twic East, Bor South, Magwi, Kapoeta East, Yirol East, and Yirol West. No safe and clean drinking water, and practices of open defecations, which could worsen the health situation during rainy season,• According to LiveWell assessment (September 2018) in the proposed location,the top causes of morbidity are malaria, respiratory tract infections, Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), and acute bloody diarrhea etc. Heavy rainfall coupled with flooding has been reported in Bor South, Twic East, and Pibor in Jonglei State. It is anticipated that this would led to increase morbidities and mortalities should there be limited health, flood and WASH response in the affected locations. • LiveWell is therefore proposing in this humanitarian response plans for the year 2019 to set-up  Rehabilitates 20 boreholes to provide safe drinking water and nutritional centers for SAM/MAM. Renovates 20 pit latrines and Install new 10 temporary latrines in IDPs settings to provide safe sanitary facilities to women and girls with appropriate GBV risk analysis. Renovates 10 bathing shelters in the IDPs setiing Install 10 handwashingacilities in IDPs setting  Distributes 2,000 WASH/Hygiene Kits for admitted SAM at OTP/SC till the household level. Distributes 3,000 Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits to girls/women of reproductive age  Build the capacity of 150 health workers and 100 prefects in schools on handwashing techniques with soap to act as ambassadors of good hygiene practices to prevent WASH related diseases.  Reached 12,121 people with basic WASH NFIs, and hygiene messages that is geared toward cholera prevention and other acute watery diarrhea. • LiveWell is therefore seeking your approval to recommend our HRP plan for WASH Cluster in the coming Year 2019. LiveWell is actively implementing health and WASH projects in the same locations with support from DFID, SSHF, USAID.

Activities

WASH ACTIVITIES FOR IDPS/Host Populations Rehabilitate and maintain 20 boreholes to provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to 12,121 vulnerable and affected populations in Pibor, Magwi, Kapoeta East, Twic East, Bor South, Yirol East and Yirol West Build the capacity of 150 hygiene promoters to increased the knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among 12,121 vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. Renovates 20 pit latrines, and 10 showers/handwashing) in IDPs setting to reached 12,121 vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe sanitary and hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services. WASH ACTIVITIES TO MITIGATE WASH-related GBV RISK Conduct GBV safety audit in 40 water-points, 20 toilets and 10 showers/handwashing facilities to provide gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of Conduct GBV risk analysis in 20 water-points to provide safe water and access in secure location for women and girls  Distribute 3,000 MHM kit and install 60 new latrines in schools/nutrition centers to improve hygiene and safe sanitation practices with after appropriate risk analysis. WASH ACTIVITIES IN NUTRITION FOR FAMINE PREVENTION INTERVENTIONS Provide safe drinking water and nutritional centers from protected water point for the appetite test for SAM/MAM. Distribute 2,000 WASH/hygiene kits for the admitted SAM at OTP/SC for the household level WASH IN HEALTH FOR OUTBREAK PREPAREDNESS, RESPONSE AND RISK REDUCTION Provide timely WASH minimum package of intervention to 12,121 vulnerable IDPs/host communities to prevent outbreaks and other WASH-related diseases. Training of 150 health workersand over 100 pupils on handwashing-with-soap techniques at health facility and school level.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

 Yes, The project will focus on gender disparity and sensitivity to ensure that all the vulnerable populations enjoy the same positive essential life-saving health services. Women bear the economic responsibilities of their families, are not in a position to make family and personal choices including reproductive issues. The project will ensure that women and girls are empowered proportionally to be given equal opportunity to be employed and serve their community. Women and girls must receive equal treatment in accessing essential lifesaving clinical health and protection services. Special effort will be made to ensure that the needs of adolescent youth (Boys/Girls) will be provided equal opportunities. However; based on the WASH cluster strategies, LiveWell’s activities will be designed to target GBV mitigation in collaboration with the protection cluster and through gendered risk assessments. Staff will be trained on Protection and GBV guidelines and communities consulted (disaggregated by sex/age) before designing a program/project. LiveWell will strengthen advocacy for high protection standards to rollout WASH interventions in the areas of implementation
