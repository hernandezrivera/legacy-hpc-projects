Project Summary

SDI aims, through this project, to contribute to providing safe access to sufficient and affordable safe water and adequate sanitation for 34000 women, men, boys and girls of the most affected population and recent IDPs destinations in NW Syria while focusing on newly displaced populations in IDPs settlements and on WASH facilities in public schools and health facilities in NW Syria, sub-districts of Al-Bab, Salqin, Afrin, Maarat Tamisrin and Daret Azza, where large influx of IDPs have recently arrived taking into consideration protection of beneficiaries specially for women, girls and people with special needs.
In this program, SDI will rehabilitate damaged WASH and modify the existing ones in a way that ensures access for people with disabilities and ensure gender-sensitive hygiene facilities with access for people with special needs in 50 schools and 20 health centres/hospitals in most affected locations in Northeast Syria in addition to thirty latrines (5 cells each) in 3 newly constructed camps in addition to provision of needed water tanks, water trucking, cesspools creation and desludging and solid waste management for 8 months. People of special needs targeted be aimed to be no less than 5% percent of targeted beneficiaries.
All target sub-district are of its severity scale 5 or 4 (based on draft WASH HNO2020). The rehabilitation activities will include repairing damaged WASH facilities; sanitation systems, bathrooms, solid waste disposals facilities, drainage facilities and adequate water for personal hygiene and to clean toilets taking into consideration sphere standards and WASH cluster guidelines and minimum standards.
During the implementation the project we will take into consideration the protection of beneficiaries and gender-sensitive and disability-friendly works that would ensure the protection needs of boys, girls, women and people of special needs as well as the needs of children with disabilities allowing adequate access to the school premises and WASH facilities in schools health centers and camps.
Beneficiaries will include 5000 boys and adolescents, 5000 girls and adolescents, 500 men and 500 women in schools; and 5000 women, 5000 men, 5000 girls and 5000 boys as direct beneficiaries medical centers; in addition to 650 women, 500 men, 900 boys and 900 girls in camps.
Beneficiaries with disabilities will be no less than 60% of beneficiaries, including 20,000 beneficiaries (all direct beneficiaries) in health centers, 300 boys and girls in schools and 300 persons in camps.

4 - Needs

Between 5 to 10 February 2020 SDI conducted WaSH Needs Assessment in (5) sub-districts, (Afrin - AlBab - Daret Azza) in Aleppo governorate and (Ma'arrat Tamasrin- Salqin) in Idleb Governorate.SDI’s MEAL field team consisting of 4 employees (2 Female and 2 Male) conducted face to face interviews with KIs based on assessment form prepared for this purpose.Key findingsAll the KIs in the target communities reported WASH as a priority need. KIs in all assessed communities reported that 86% of households had no access to sufficient water; key barriers were high price of water (including common use and drinking water), and issues with the functionality of the main network. Additionally, 64% of KIs reported that the water networks were not function, and 36% reported that the water from the network was only available 1-5 days per month.That Most commonly reported coping strategies for using water bathes less frequently; spend money usually spent on other things to buy water, do laundry less frequently, and Reduce drinking water consumption. In addition to that, people in the area were suffering from the problem of solid waste disposal, where the most commonly reported ways people dispose of solid waste/trash are free public garbage collection, paid private garbage collection, and Garbage burned.71% of the assessed communities reported that they have problem connected to a sewage system. Where the most commonly reported sanitation issues were No sewage system, Sewage system needs repair, and Sewage system needs cleaningThe KIs reported that random camps do not have drinking water and latrines. Also, there is a shortage of water tanks in schools and hospitals in the area in addition to the lake of gender aggregated WASH facilities in 50% of schools and health centers.The KIs reported that the priorities are the operation of the water networks and supplying the random camps with water tanks and latrines.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented by SDI's experienced field staff in Syria, through its offices in Syria, (Idelb and Aleppo) and administered through its WASH program coordinator in Turkey. The project will begin by ensuring continuous coordination with relevant bodies and stakeholders inside Syria (e.g. camp management and directorates), partner organizations and clusters throughout the project to ensure a coordinated and complementary approach for comprehensive services, avoid duplication and ensure accountability to affected populations. Camp committees, school PTAs, staff of schools and health centers and beneficiaries including men, women, adolescents, elderly and people with special needs and boys and girls in schools (through focus groups, survey, complaint boxes and PSS session for children) will be directly involved in the designing and implementation and monitoring of activities. The project will be handed over to committees in camps and schools upon project closure in addition to prior coordination with WASH cluster to ensure follow-up and enhance the sustainability of the project. All project activities will take into consideration the age and gender differences and the needs of disabled, elderly and people with special needs, will ensure separated facilities for female and male in addition to protection concerns, like ensuring lighting (including providing solar power), locks from inside and privacy...etc. and adequate water; this will ensure better hygiene and a more protective environment based on sphere standards. Materials and services will be procured based on SDI's policies and procedures in a competitive and transparent manner to rule out fraud and ensure cost-effectiveness. SDI has an active monitoring and reporting system with a complaint mechanism that will ensure feedback from beneficiaries, while keeping the confidentiality of sensitive complaints. PDM will conducted at the end of each phase in addition to assessing beneficiary satisfacation.

Effect: medium.Mitigation: SDI will advise staff on alternative routes to mitigate the risk. If the security situation changes in a certain area, we can wait or postpone activities for a few days or relocate certain activities to other accessible locations in need of support.Assumption:2: Skilled and unskilled workers can be hired from the local market to complete the required rehabilitation and WASH activities. Risk: It might be difficult to find enough qualified technicians/engineers/workers to implement the rehabilitation works, Likelihood: low.Effect: low.Mitigation: contract and recruit high-quality workers from SDI roster or hire from a wider geographic range if needed.
