Project Summary

Malaria consortium has been in Northern Bar El Ghazal (NBeGs) for over 14 years implementing health projects in various counties in collaboration with the SMoH and county health department (CHD). For the planned intervention, MC will improve
surveillance and response towards epidemic prone diseases and provide accessible health care services to refugee communities at crisis thus leading to tangible reduction in morbidity and mortality. Services will be extended to 27,225 beneficiaries directly & over 100,000 indirectly within a radius of 10km from the nearest health facility and hard to reach affected population in Aweil Centre, South and West (targeting refugee communities in Wedweil refugee settlement).
Project interventions will prioritize delivery of life saving essential health care services guided by the South Sudan basic essential health care package. The key notable interventions will include prevention ff malnutrition - addressing malnutrition requires a comprehensive approach that includes not only improving access to nutritious food but also promoting education and awareness about healthy eating habits and importantly surveillance and reporting; detection and treatment of malnutrition - screening and treating girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by severe and moderate acute malnutrition; building capacity of health workers to integrate child stimulation and early childhood development services while providing care for children with acute malnutrition [at Aweil state hospital and all high-volume sites]. The nutrition interventions will target Children under five (especially children aged 0-23 months), Pregnant and lactating women [Adolescents], Refugee communities, Women-headed households and Persons with Special Needs (PSNs).
Other interventions will incorporate immunization of children under the age of five against vaccine preventable diseases, treatment of common communicable diseases, Provision of mental health services through diagnosis and referral, improving community health behavior through spreading of behavior change messages at both the static and mobile clinic days. Provision of maternal and adolescent sexual and reproductive health services; provision of BEmONC services by skilled birth attendants. Improving on the nutritional status of the children, pregnant and lactating mothers through screening, referral, and coordination; improving the WASH structures in the non-supported health facilities through renovation and provision of wash supplies; procure, pre-position, and provide essential medicines and supplies to non- supported health facilities. The project will also seek to improve the screening and referral of people with disabilities using the Washington group of questions at every point of contact with health service providers.
For quality assurance of service delivery, malaria consortium will conduct trainings for all the engaged project staff, including trainings on packages such as clinical management of rape, sexual and reproductive health, and safeguarding. Emphasis will be placed on capacity improvement for the county health department to empower them lead effective supportive supervision sessions and mentorship to health care workers to develop skills and knowledge.

1. Needs

South Sudan faced outbreaks of measles, cholera, and other infectious diseases, exacerbated by the impacts of climate change (flooding) and very low government allocation for health interventions (less than 2%) resulting in substantial out-of-pocket expenses [Crown Agents 2022]. Malaria is the top cause of morbidity (64%) and mortality (45%) among outpatients, followed by pneumonia or upper respiratory infections and diarrhoea. More so, 20 several counties report malaria cases above the threshold perennially, especially during the rainy seasons, affecting mainly children under five years. Twenty five percent of women access healthcare facilities or receive care from skilled birth attendants during childbirth, elevating the risk of complications. Most of these deaths are exacerbated by malnutrition especially affecting children and women. The 2023 FSNMS and SMART survey indicate that national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels surpass WHO's 15% emergency threshold thus placing 2.5 million individuals at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024, with 46 out of 79 counties exceeding the threshold. Contributory factors include high food insecurity, inadequate access to safe water and sanitation (50%), poor child feeding and care practices (4% of children receiving the required nutrition). It is also noted that 18 counties (including those in Northern Bahr el Ghazal) grapple with severe deprivation, further exacerbating the malnutrition crisis.The above issues identified are intensified by shortage of healthcare workers, frequent flooding events, inadequate infrastructure, and low service utilization contribute to one of the world's highest rates of maternal and child mortality. Although the Health Sector Transformation Programme (HSTP) is currently supporting 50% of functional health facilities, 18% are non-functional. Only 57% of the population can access the nearest health facility in less than an hour, while 44% cited long distance and poorly facilitated health facilities as a barrier to accessing health care (ISNA 2024). In Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG), a staggering 62% of the population face severe food insecurity, with 66% experiencing extreme levels. In January 2023, NBeG reported the highest cases of Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) in the country, with 17,668 children admitted for treatment. Hepatitis E outbreaks are widespread due to a lack of essential WASH services. According to the NBeG 2021 annual EPI performance bulletin, NBeG state annual Penta 1 coverage was 82%, however the Penta 3 coverage was 68% and the Drop Out Rate (DOR) was 18%, an indication of low use of EPI services. The Aweil region, particularly in Aweil Centre, has been severely affected by devastating floods, resulting in the displacement of over 128,000 individuals and restricting access to essential health facilities. We identified 4 hard to reach Bomas prone to floods in Aweil Centre, 15 hard to reach Bomas in Aweil South and 9 hard to reach Bomas prone to floods in Aweil West. The area also grapples with a scarcity of skilled healthcare workers, high attrition rates, and damage to health infrastructure due to a combination of conflict and foods.Furthermore, the delivery of babies for refugees and returnees predominantly occurs within the communities or camps with the assistance of traditional birth attendants. Each health facility has only one midwife. Aweil South County has 18 health facilities [20 PHCUs & 2 PHCCs]. Five out of 20 HFs, not functional and therefore populations around these facilities are largely reached through Mobile outreach clinics supported South Sudan Humanitarian Fund. Outpatient consultations for children and adults in the last 2 years have increased by 10.6% from 117,260 in the calendar year 2020 to 129,680 in 2021 (HMIS Data) and with trends of children below 5 years sharply influencing these increased morbidity trends with a rapid rise by 28.5% from 39,865 in 2020 to 51,238 in 2021.

2. Project activities

1. Delivering basic lifesaving bio-medical services through static and mobile clinics to reach 186,895 beneficiaries. Nine (09) static health facilities will be supported to offer services to affected communities of Aweil center (Longwurkech, Umora, and Baau PHCUs. Aweil south: Angol Rual, Aladich, Manyiel PHCUs) and Aweil West (Pantit, Achana and Maduany PHCUs), Services will include routine immunization services, treatment of preventable communicable and non-communicable diseases, screening diagnosis and referral of malnutrition, ANC, skill birth attendance, post maternal and child health services, etc. The same HFs will coordinate with BHWs to hold outreaches or Mobile Clinics targeting affected communities in hard to reach and stay locations such as farmlands with the mentioned services above. Notably, procurement of essential medicines and supplies for the treatment of common preventable illnesses will be done to facilitate service provision at static and mobile clinics/outreaches. 2. Improvement of Health Facility structures and general environment. Basic renovations will be done to especially improve access to health facilities – This will include supporting facilities with ramps so that persons with special needs can access them; WASH services such as water tanks; demarcating consultation rooms to improve privacy especially during antenatal days. Health facilities with leaking roofs will also be supported. 3. Strengthening community health department driven surveillance. Capacity of the BHI infrastructure will be improved through routine mentorship and coaching. In turn, these community health workers will apply the knowledge for effective client follow-up. Also, the CHD surveillance offices will be supported for monitoring and reporting of notifiable diseases through EWARS and IDSR for real time reporting and response to any suspected and confirmed outbreak. Data quality sessions will also be held at the health facilities – reviewing performance of health facilities and generating community response to the gaps. 4. Scorecard for community engagement and sensitization for accountability. Malaria consortium will adopt various strategies including community scorecard meetings and dialogues facilitated by the Health Facility Management Committees to lead awareness drives and information sharing sessions as well as cocreation sensemaking sessions. These will help improve awareness and foster community driven accountability. 6. Hold quarterly mentorship and coaching sessions to improve capacity of health workers to integrate child stimulation and early childhood development services while providing care for children. [at Aweil state hospital and all high-volume sites] 7. Facilitate SMoH to lead Quarterly joint-stakeholder program and field monitoring and supportive supervision to strengthen evidence-based decision-making. This will also be used to monitor status of key indicators to strengthen Accountability to Affected Populations 8. Support SMoH to hold quarterly state Health cluster, Reproductive health TWG, to coordinate stakeholder learning meetings. This will help ensure prevention, screening and treatment of malaria and management of diarrhoea and other dangerous diseases. 9. Coordinate biannual client exit interviews (surveys) to inform learning and data driven decision making 10. facilitate sustainable community financing to rehabilitate dilapidated health facilities
