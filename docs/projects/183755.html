Project Summary

“ Improving Wash Services in Vulnerable Communities in Gaza” is a 12-month project designed to address the WASH needs of vulnerable communities in several areas in Deir Al-Balah and Khan Younis, and respond to the objectives of the WASH cluster, specifically Objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians, and Objective 3: Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks.
Vulnerable communities in targeted areas suffer from 1. Inadequate wastewater and HH connections in Khan Younis, 2. Undersized and deteriorated water networks in Deit Al-Balah, 3. Flooding risks at the Shaat Lagoon in Khan Younis, and 4. The lack of hygiene awareness. All these issue represent a physical, environmental, and health risks to the community, including pollution, the spread of diseases and bacterial infections, limitations to movement and conducting daily activities such as work and education, all which add to the enormous challenges faced in these targeted communities that already suffer from poverty and deteriorated living conditions.
The needs of the project were identified in coordination with the Coastal Municipal Water Utility (CMWU), and are planned to improve the lives of over 109,500 beneficiaries, including women, men, boys, girls, people with disabilities, and elders.
Anera will implement this project in coordination with its partner, Bayader for Environment and Development Association (Bayader), to ensure safe and sustainable access to water and sanitation and the reduced risk of flooding and contamination in vulnerable communities in Gaza by improving community-level infrastructure and promoting hygiene practices. The response will be delivered through the following activities:
1. Installation/extension/rehabilitation of sewer collection network and HH connections: Construction of wastewater networks and HH connections at different areas in Khanyounis City to prevent contamination of domestic water and decrease waterborne illnesses by mitigating sewage flooding.
2. Installation/ rehabilitation of water transmission pipelines, networks and HH connections: Rehabilitation and expansion of water networks at different locations in Dair Al Balah to decrease the loss of domestic water and increase the overall efficiency of the water network leading to less degradation of the system and loss of household water over time, whcih will improve access to safe drinking and domestic water to more than 30,000 beneficiaries.
3. Installation of household flood mitigation measures: Rehabilitation of Shaat lagoon including constructing boreholes in Khan Younis to enhance flood mitigation measures and increase the efficiency of the lagoon, to protect more than 35,000 people from flooding risks.
4. Hygiene promotion awareness campaigns: community-level hygiene awareness workshops to create a sustainable positive impact on community health and encourage hygiene practices on the household level to prevent diseases and infections.
The project ensures the inclusion of all community members in targeted area, including all age and gender groups and PWDs, and focuses on enhancing the living conditions, safety and access to WASH services to all groups at the community and household levels. The project, specifically through the hygiene awareness campaigns, will include all household members with focus on women, which will increase the role of household members in supporting women in applying hygiene practices at home. It will also increase the awareness of children, and enhance their safety from water borne diseases or flooding risks.

1 - Needs

The WASH sector in Gaza is one of the most severely damaged and vulnerable sectors; several factors contribute to exacerbating the issues in the sector, including: climate change that results in increased water shortages and flooding, in addition to the continued political and physical isolation of Gaza causing limited resources and regular infrastructural damage. Gaza relies on the Coastal Aquifer as its primary source of water, which depends on replenishment by rainfall. However, due to climate change-related rainfall decrease, the aquifer has seen a deficit in recharge values causing consumption rates to exceed those of replenishment. The permeable, sandy soil combined with the resulting over-extraction has led to the contamination of the aquifer by seawater infiltration and sewage, rendering 90-95% of the water in Gaza unfit for drinking, cooking, or bathing. At the household level, around 72% do not have a safe means of solid waste disposal and 51% do not have access to proper drainage systems, causing an accumulation of polluted storm water surrounding their houses during intense rainfalls. Furthermore, households in vulnerable areas suffer from a shortage in water supplies due to damaged water networks and HH connections; the population growth in these areas was also higher and faster than the capacity of the water systems, which causes limited access to water and increases the risk of waterborne diseases. Populations in targeted areas do not have the financial capacity to repair or improve water connections, and they are also unable to rely on purchasing water due to its significant economic burden. Currently in Gaza, more than 57% of households do not pay for domestic water due to their economic situation and/or not being connected to a municipal network or well. Gaza is also particularly vulnerable to climate impacts, with increases in temperature variability and frequency of extreme weather events reducing groundwater recharge and leading to increased flooding. Floods in particular both flush out domestic water sources and spread wastewater in networks prone to clogging and/or not updated for the increased volume of water. To respond to these issues and prevent long-term damage and additional loss of domestic groundwater, and to decrease health crises associated with these issues, Anera proposes infrastructure improvement activities that include the construction of waste water and HH connections to several neighborhoods in Khan Younis, the rehabilitation and expansion of water networks in different areas in Deir Al-Balah, and the rehabilitation of the Shaat lagoon in Khan Younis including constructing boreholes, in addition to community-level interventions through hygiene promotion awareness campaigns. Anera chose these activities in collaboration with the Coastal Municipal Water Utility (CMWU), which helped Anera identify areas in critical need that will improve the lives of over 109,500 beneficiaries.The targeted areas in Deit Al-Balah and Khan Younis consist of an average of 50.5% men, 49.5% women, 2.65% PWDs, 26.1% boys, 25% girls, and 3% elders. Females in these areas are at a particularly higher risk, especially bacterial infections and risks on pregnant and lactating women and their children. Moreover, female-headed households suffer from increased poverty rates, which limit their access to WASH services and increase the risks associated with water and wastewater management to them and their children. The specific needs for the activities of this project include:- The wastewater and HH connections is several areas in Khan Younis are worn out and inadequate; most neighborhoods have no access to wastewater networks and rely upon cesspits to dispose of household waste. These cesspits contaminate the adjacent aquifer and cause regular sewage flooding along the street. Flooding not only creates an environmental hazard in these communities, it also causes major health risks to families (including all age and gender groups), they also limit daily activities and restricts movement, causing economic challenges to families. Children in specific are at high risk from flooding, which also limit their access to schools during high rainfall seasons. - Due to undersized, insufficient, and deteriorated equipment in Deir Al Balah, residents of many neighborhoods lack proper access to daily pumped water. The overtaxing of these systems is leading to chronic damage, the repairs of which are cost prohibitive to these communities. With the current damaged water networks, families and children are at high risk of water borne diseases and bacterial infections, particularly women who are unable to access proper hygiene and sanitation. - The Shaat Lagoon in Khan Younis represents a physical and environmental risk, during rainfall season, the lagoon is flooded with rain water and not sieged causing drowning risk, especially to children; two years ago, a child drowned in the lagoon. It is also covered with clay, which makes water absorption and filtration impossible. - The lack of hygiene and sanitation awareness in the targeted vulnerable communities on both the household and community levels, which results in increased infection rates, water borne diseases, such as diarrhea and bacterial infections, especially among children.

2 - Activities

“ Improving Wash Services in Vulnerable Communities in Gaza” is a 12-month project designed to address the specific needs mentioned above in three targeted areas in Deir Al-Balah and Khan Younis, and respond to the objectives of the WASH cluster, specifically Objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians, and Objective 3: Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks.Anera chose the activities of the proposed project in collaboration with the Coastal Municipal Water Utility (CMWU), which helped Anera identify areas in critical need of WASH services that will improve the lives of over 109,500 beneficiaries. Throughout the project, Anera will coordinate the implementation and monitoring of activities with the Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) and CMWU to ensure that the activities are responsive, efficient, and compliment other WASH services in the targeted areas.Anera will implement this project in coordination with its partner, Bayader for Environment and Development Association (Bayader), which will be responsible for 1. Collecting the data of household beneficiary in the targeted areas, which will also be used as a baseline survey to measure the results throughout the monitoring and evaluation process, 2. Conducting hygiene promotion awareness campaigns through 180 community workshops and distributing hygiene promotion materials across three targeted areas in Khan Younis and Deir Al-Balah.In the initial phase of the project, 1. Bayader will conduct a baseline survey in targeted communities to identify and select beneficiaries and their needs, and 2. Anera will prepare tender documents and issue tenders and select contractors. Moreover, Anera and Bayader will conduct field/ home visits to targeted populations to provide them with information about the activities, and distribute flyers about the compliant mechanisms and inform beneficiaries of their rights. Anera will be responsible for monitoring and evaluation throughout the entire project period, and it will ensure that participation of all target groups (men, women, boys, girls, elders, children, and PWDs) from design, implementation, and feedback.The goal of the project is to ensure safe and sustainable access to water and sanitation and the reduced risk of flooding and contamination in vulnerable communities in Gaza by improving community-level infrastructure and promoting hygiene practices. This goal will be achieved through a series of activities as follows:1. Installation/extension/rehabilitation of sewer collection network and HH connections: Construction of wastewater networks and HH connections at different areas in Khanyounis City such as Jourt Lout , Al Satar and Al Katiba : Anera will install waste water networks and HH connections to replace the current cesspits that represent an environmental hazard, and flooding risk due to the limited financial capacity of families in targeted areas to pay for their cleaning fees, which poses a danger on the health of the community. The installed connection will connect the waste water directly to the waste water treatment plant of Khan Younis. This will both prevent future contamination of domestic water and will decrease waterborne illnesses by mitigating sewage flooding. This activity will improve access to sanitation, waste and hygiene services to 40,000 beneficiaries.2. Installation/ rehabilitation of water transmission pipelines, networks and HH connections: Rehabilitation and expansion of water networks at different locations in Dair Al Balah: Anera will upgrade and install water networks to replace old and damaged networks that are no longer adequate to serve the community and are not sufficient with the population increase in these areas. This includes the installation and upgrading of HH connection to general water networks. These upgrades, will lead both to a reduction in the loss of domestic water in these neighborhoods and increase the overall efficiency of the water network leading to less degradation of the system and loss of household water over time. This will improve access to safe drinking and domestic water to 30,000 beneficiaries.3. Installation of household flood mitigation measures: Rehabilitation of Shaat lagoon including constructing boreholes in Khan Younis: Anera will enhance flood mitigation measures and increase the efficiency of the lagoon through 1. Sieging the lagoon area to increase safety and protect the community from flooding risks, 2. Removing the clay soil layer covering the lagoon and replacing it with sand to increase the filtration rate, 3. Installing storm water pumps and constructing boreholes to increase efficiency and feed the aquifer. This activity will protect at least 35,000 beneficiaries from seasonal flooding risks in six neighborhoods such as Al Manara , Qa’ Al Qurien , Al Fokhari , Al Nasser, Al Shoka and Al Salam.4. Hygiene promotion awareness campaigns: community-level hygiene awareness workshops: community hygiene promotion events have a high sustainable impact on community health, therefore, Anera and Bayader propose activities to impact positive changes on the community-level hygiene practices in the three targeted areas. These activities will include: 1. 180 awareness sessions at the community level for 4,500 women, men, children, elders, and PWDs (25 beneficiaries per session) to discuss personal and household level hygiene, cleaning, and disease prevention, and 2. Distribution of information education communication materials (IEC) such as Hand washing steps stickers, Masks, (Do’s & Don’ts) stickers, water borne diseases and prevention brochures. These activities will be conducted by the project partner, Bayader, and Anera’s team will oversee the implementation, in addition to monitoring and evaluation.

9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

As a humanitarian organization, Anera is charged with the responsibility of taking care of the most vulnerable beneficiaries served throughout all projects. Complaint mechanisms and accountability measures and policies are put in place to ensure transparency and that beneficiaries are protected, and to give them the chance to express their concerns in different methods that allow the organization and the program staff to take corrective measures and minimize any potential harm in the planning and implementation of activities. Anera’s Whistleblower Policy provides further protections for beneficiaries, staff, and contractors that have concerns in need of addressing.Complaints will be recorded, reviewed and addressed quickly by the responsible staff member at Anera, and proper procedures will be undertaken to address any misconduct and protect beneficiaries from harm in accordance with Anera’s guidelines and work procedures. Complaints concerning the implementation of the project will be addressed by the relevant staff monitored by Anera’s Country Director, whereas complaints regarding misconduct or fraud will be directly addressed by Anera’s Country Director. Anera also has a PSEA focal point who is responsible for receiving and referring any issues, protection as needed, and ensuring a safe environment to beneficiaries. The complaint review committees review cases and come up with solutions that are shared with the project team and partners, and incorporated in M&E tools to ensure that corrective measures are taken. The team then addresses these complaints timely and efficiently as needed, which might include meeting with the community to respond to their concerns and explain the corrective measures that are being put in place to address these complaints, in addition to conducting focus groups with a representative groups of the targeted community to collect their feedback, assess their satisfaction and address any issues or concerns that they might have.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Anera’s “Do No Harm” policies are woven throughout all of its program designs based on its principles of Accountability to Affected Populations document. For this project, Anera ensures that all its activities are guided by protection mainstreaming to protect the safety, dignity, and rights of the communities it serves, and monitor access and accountability from the project planning phase to implementation and monitoring, through regular field visits and assessment to ensure that their dignity is preserved at all stages, this includes ensuring adequate representation of vulnerable groups in the project design and monitoring.Anera is committed to the protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) by its staff and humanitarian workers. We recognize both the particular vulnerability of women, girls, and boys to sexual exploitation and abuse as well as the inherent power dynamics evident within humanitarian and development partnerships. “Do no harm” is a core principle of humanitarian action, and Anera is committed to giving this principle the adequate resources and attention to be implemented at all levels and ensure that this policy is adhered to. All Anera staff, including project staff, have been trained on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) to ensure gender-sensitivity, protection, and detection of abuse or gender violence. There haven’t been any complaints from targeted communities on any misconduct in Anera’s activities, and Anera operates a hotline for complaints and issues from beneficiaries, stakeholders, partner staff, or other concerned parties. These complaints and issues are dealt with as quickly as possible by Anera staff and escalated for further response when necessary. Furthermore, Anera has developed its own PSEA policy that includes code of conduct and procedures for reporting and investigating complaints, and it’s currently creating materials to be made available to beneficiaries to know their rights. Anera ensures that all its activities are guided by protection mainstreaming to protect the safety, dignity, and rights of the communities it serves, and monitor access and accountability from the project planning phase. The principles of Do No Harm are included in the project as follows:1. Anera’s needs assessment during the design phase includes addressing any challenges in the community and planning activities either to address these challenges or to ensure that they do not cause harm on the community level, Anera also conducts a risk analysis prior to the implementation of any project to identify mitigation methods and limit any potential risks on beneficiaries. 2. Anera’s local team and its close coordination of activities with local partners, local authorities, and international bodies and organizations ensures better understanding and consideration of local needs, and increase the depth of interventions’ impact.3. Anera and its partner will provide targeted communities with information about the projects, their impact on the community, and provide them with safety guidelines during the implementation on construction activities. The project partner, Al Bayader, will conduct awareness sessions for households on hygiene and sanitation in addition to technical training on mitigation and safety measures in case of emergencies, this also ensures direct contact with beneficiaries to hear their feedback or concerns.4. The data of all beneficiaries involved in the activities in the targeted communities is protected and kept confidential.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

From the design phase, Anera works with local partners to identify the most vulnerable areas in need of interventions that increase their access to WASH services and prevent them from the risks of seasonal flooding. Based on these assessment, along with the WASH cluster and humanitarian organizations assessment and reports, Anera develops beneficiary criteria that includes the number of household members (above 5 family members), that have children, elderly, or people with disabilities, financially vulnerable households and communities, priority to women-headed households, beneficiaries and communities that were affected by the recent military escalations, and beneficiaries living in inadequate WASH conditions that make them prone to health and environmental risks. Beneficiaries are selected in coordination with local partners, including community-based organizations (CBOs), and the Ministry of Social Development, to ensure that they meet the selection criteria and that they are not receiving similar assistance from other sources.Throughout the design, planning and implementation, Anera conduct assessments and surveys with a representative sample of beneficiaries (inclusive of age, gender, and PWDs) to inform them about the project, hear their feedback and concerns, and ensure that the activities are responsive to their needs. Anera employs a gender-sensitive approach throughout the project cycle, and applies a gender-integrated approach from design to evaluation. At the implementation stage, Anera ensures that its activities are gender responsive and prioritizes the targeting of female-headed households and other vulnerable households, including persons with disabilities.
