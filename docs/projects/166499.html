Project Summary

This project will target IDPs and returnees in three affected states, Yobe, Adamawa and Borno with provision of emergency shelters, Non-food Items (NFIs), transitional and shelter repair kits as protection live-saving assistance aligned with Shelter/ NFI/ CCCM Sector strategic objectives for the HRP 2020. Providing IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities with living spaces represents an essential intervention in reducing the impact of the COVID-19 crisis, and it is a critical first step to reducing vulnerability that preserves the ability of the most vulnerable and affected people to meet their basic needs which are now further exasperated by the pandemic. It is critical to ensure that PoCs are supported to recover their wellbeing and dignity in a protective environment where they can enjoy full exercise of their rights including to adequate shelter.
Provision of shelter assistance will also allow for decongestion of camps which is one of the vehicles of COVID19 facing IDPs living in camps. In highly congested camps and settlement, social distancing is not possible hence putting IDPs at high risk of contracting the disease.

1 - Needs

The Corona Virus and the ensuing COVID-19 pandemic has added a new layer to an already challenging protection environment. COVID-19 is the infectious disease caused by the most recently discovered coronavirus. This new virus and disease were unknown before the outbreak began in Wuhan, China, in December 2019. Coronaviruses are a large family of viruses which may cause illness in animals or humans. In humans, several coronaviruses are known to cause respiratory infections ranging from the common cold to more severe diseases such as Middle East Respiratory Syndrome (MERS) and Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS). The most recently discovered coronavirus causes coronavirus disease COVID-19. Nigeria recorded its first case of Corona virus in Lagos on 27 February 2020. As at 19 March 2020, eight cases were confirmed in the country. As of April 23, 2020, the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control reports a total of 981 lab-confirmed cases, of which 753 are active cases, 197 discharged. The pandemic has caused 31 untimely deaths in the country. The Northeast Nigeria is also particularly affected by the pandemic. As of April 23, one COVID-19 case was confirmed in Adamawa. Borno State totals 12 laboratory confirmed cases, of which 10 are active and 2 tragically passed away. On 19 March 2020, the Federal Government announced measures restricting entry to Nigeria for travellers from 13 worst affected countries in Europe, Asia and the USA. These include China, Italy, Iran, South Korea, Spain, Japan, France, Germany, USA, Norway, the UK, the Netherlands and Switzerland. On the same day, the UN Resident Coordinator announced exceptional measures to address the COVID-19 threat in Nigeria. These included, inter alia, self-isolation for UN staff arriving from R&R, change of R& R station to Abuja for all UN staff in field offices, and the suspension of all non-essential international travel. This also included a restriction on travel within Nigeria for UN staff to only essential travel (for activities rated PC1 or PC2 in the Programme Criticality Plan). While the pandemic disrupts normal life in the country, the restriction of movements, accepted as a lifesaving measure necessary to curb the spread of the infection, further curtailing the population's access to means of livelihood, especially for the most vulnerable among the People of Concern. In an environment already burdened with poverty and limited opportunities to engage in self-reliance activities, the displaced population are further pauperized with the socio-economic impacts of the lockdown measures and global effects of COVID-19 measures. In the ensuing uncertainties, a perceived lack of prospects for the future and inability to influence their own lives can further heighten protection risks. This can lead to adoption of harmful coping strategies such as transactional sex, child marriages, the risk of forced recruitments by Non-State Armed Groups, and other forms of exploitation. Shelter needs are driven by the various waves of displacement and untamed new arrivals in already congested sites and camps. Most displaced populations residing in camps or camp-like settings and new arrivals from inaccessible areas live in emergency or makeshift shelters. Nearly 80% of assessed IDPs live in inadequate shelters, including partially damaged houses. The majority of IDPs families experienced medium to heavy damage to their houses, due, in part to the cyclic harsh climatic fluctuations, punctuated by wild fires in high temperature seasons, and/or rains and floods during rainy seasons. Providing emergency shelter, through either building new ones, or rehabilitating existing ones, is a priority. Through this action, UNHCR intends to improve the protection of People of concern in the North East through the provision of Core relief items and Shelter assistance.

2 - Activities

Activities or outputs: Activity 1: Shelter beneficiary selection to determine vulnerability through registration and vulnerability screening. Activity 2: Undertake proper site selection and settlement planning. Activity 3: Emergency shelters response to respond to congestion problems in formal and informal camps. Activity 4: Rehabilitation and repair of existing shelters. Activity 5: Planning and building of Communal Hangers Activity 6: Procurement and distribution of NFIs displaced and returning IDP households Activity 7: Increased advocacy on access to appropriate land, housing and tenure Activity 8: Monitoring and evaluation. Activity 8: Support to improved reception capacity by providing quarantine spaces in entry points at designated camps. 9: Support to decongestion strategy through innovative shelter solutions as well as NFIs support

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to affected populations will be strengthened through the development of a community-based feedback mechanism; supporting partners to the inclusion of functional and accessible complaints and reporting mechanisms, convening frequent meetings with the community leaders, and beneficiaries and focus group discussions. UNHCR is an active member of the Taskforce on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and has community-based structures known as Protection Action Groups (PAG) within Maiduguri and LGAs where UNHCR is present such as Bama, Banki, Damasak, Damboa, Damaturu, Dikwa, Gudjba, Gwoza, Jere, Gwoza, Madagali, Michika, MMC, Monguno, Ngala, Rann, Yola North, Yola South. Through these structures, UNHCR ensures that issues of concern to the community are brought to the attention of UNHCR and its partners for intervention. UNHCR shall also establish a feedback mechanism through its Protection Desk. In previous projects, some key concerns raised include beneficiary selection, the support provided, community representatives and payment process. All issues reported will be systematically investigated and followed up in a timely manner. Received complaints shall be categorized according to the level of sensitivity and severity and responded in accordance with protection principles.
