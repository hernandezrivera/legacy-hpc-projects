Project Summary

As the livelihood and food security situation in northern Syria is still deteriorating, and the continuous waves of IDPs are creating more social and economic pressures on the host communities. The need for providing livelihood opportunities that will contribute to enhancing the economic conditions of both IDPs and host communities is becoming an increased demand among the crisis-affected populations. Therefore, Rahma Relief Foundation is seeking to implement a livelihood project to increase the resilience of affected communities by offering a series of training on Hydroponic technology and providing the beneficiaries with establishment support and agricultural inputs to ensure sustainable agricultural activities for the long term.

4 - Needs

Through the latest statistics related to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020, the disruption to people’s livelihoods and reduced access to economic opportunities has impacted the resilience capacity of many communities in Syria. As a result, many people are resorting to harmful coping strategies for survival, particularly in overburdened communities with concentrations of IDPs and/or spontaneous returnees, where people face increased competition for scarce livelihood opportunities. Across Syria communities continue to report the spending of savings, running up of debts, buying on credit, depleting household assets and reducing food consumption to achieve a minimum standard of living, amidst a lack of basic relief items and diminished resilience. Recent food security assessments.during 2019 areas across Syria continue to be targeted and prioritized for these activities. Production capacities have been largely disrupted in UN-declared besieged locations, however, where productive inputs were available or provided (such as vegetable gardens), household production has been one of the main sources of food to meet people’s needs in these areas and therefore is fully considered as a part of the humanitarian life-saving response.According to Humanitarian Needs Overview in 2020 the rates of displacement remained very high in Idleb countryside, with more than 650,000 arrivals tracked from locations both inside and outside the governorate from Rural Damascus, Hama, and Aleppo among others.In order to get a better understanding of the situation in this location, Rahma Relief Foundation team tracked the situation of locals and IDPs in terms of lively hood opportunities and conducted a rapid needs assessment in the community in November 2019.A combination of primary and secondary sources and methods were used.• Secondary sources included reports published by a range of humanitarian actors in the governate. • Primary sources, and other methods

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will target an estimated number of 100 households that will be selected on certain vulnerability criteria for the duration of eight months. This project will be divided into two phases:1- Training Phase: The beneficiaries will be divided into five training sessions, where each training session will host 20 participants for the duration of one month. The training will take place at Rahma’s training center in Beksraya community where 10 instructional hydroponic units of nutrient film technique (NFT) to be installed for the sake of the training.2- Establishment support: Rahma Relief Foundation will assist the beneficiaries to install hydroponics units at their houses while providing them with agricultural inputs and consultancy for the next month. In addition, the foundation will link the beneficiaries with the local market in order to sell their products.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Every effort will be made to recruit female staff locally in the project areas. Female staff will be involved in engaging women in project activities at all stages, including assessments, planning, implementation, and monitoring by inviting them to plan and review meetings, project site visits and meet them individually. This approach enables RRF to target the most vulnerable and ensures that RRF is meeting the needs of the female beneficiaries in a manner that is acceptable considered appropriate by them. RRF will ensure that project sites for female beneficiaries are approved by them and that distributions take place in a culturally appropriate manner.- RRF will collect Sex and age-disaggregated demographic information on the affected population (# of households and family members; # of single heads of households who are women, men, boys, or girls.- The needs analysis and assessments carried out by RRF capture the differences between men and women, and both groups are consulted. Through these exercises, a detailed description of beneficiaries for the proposed activities will be set.- During the project implementation process, RRF will hire female staff to ensure female participation.- RRF will conduct singe sex with same-sex facilitators to gain feedback, suggestions from women and men.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The level of insecurity is extremely high in Syria, the growing diversity and complexity of different armed groups both regime and opposition contributed to the instability of the current situation which could impose a direct impact on the feasibility of the project. The main risks identified in the area of intervention are bombing, shelling, kidnapping. The security situation is getting more complex with regards to the number of different local and foreign armed groups that could restrain the area of intervention. This is not expected to change in the coming months. RRF is committed to implementing the project in the targeted location due to the high humanitarian needs and lack of food and livelihood. RRF has set up specific standard operating procedures for identifying risks in order to limit the staff and beneficiaries’ exposure as described.
