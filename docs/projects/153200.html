Project Summary

This project will ensure Camp Management continues to contribute to preventing, reducing and mitigating protection threats for persons affected by crisis in South Sudan through the provision of lifesaving humanitarian assistance. Through the provision of site care and maintenance, community engagement, strengthened communication with communities, site planning and support in technical advice, IOM will further contribute to the capacity of the humanitarian responses and provide timely and targeted humanitarian assistance. In line with cluster target objectives, IOM intends to target 668,543 persons in need across the geographical area of operations, namely Central Equatoria, Unity, Western Bahr el Gazal, Jonglei and Upper Nile states. This project intends to scale up ongoing efforts on purposeful and meaningful participation of women and persons with disabilities in all IOM Camp Management operations. Centrality of protection remains at the core of Camp Management programming, observing conflict sensitivity and considering the specific needs of women, men, girls and boys in project planning and execution.
With the cluster advice and coordination, IOM will ensure Camp Management reaches out to populations in need out of the PoC sites with mobile and remote responses. This will further contribute to continuous efforts to avoid concentration of services in the PoC sites and ensure hard to reach populations have access to life saving support.
In promotion of resilience and support to efforts to decongest PoC sites, IOM will continue to offer information management and data sharing with partners and communities. Through partnerships with specialized agencies, camp management will explore means to facilitate and contribute to multisector efforts to meet the needs of target populations across the country.

Needs

The Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) Cluster has been active in South Sudan since December 2013. In 2018, the parties involved in the conflict took important steps towards the upholding of the Peace Agreement; however, it remains unclear whether this will translate to a sustainable peace and stability conducive to the return and reintegration of the country’s displaced population. Currently, assurances of security have not corresponded with consistent movements of the internally displaced persons (IDP) population back to their areas of origin. In areas where IOM Camp Management maintains operations, patterns of armed conflict are still present: Western Bahr el Gazal and Unity state remain volatile with access challenges. In 2018, the total population of the PoC sites is 216,631, commensurate with the same period in 2017. Nonetheless, opportunities for localized stabilization of the conflict and IDP returns have begun emerging, thus opening the door for targeted CCCM programming. While UN protected sites are not a durable solution, they remain a lifesaving, last resort for many people caught in the conflict. It is therefore critical that core care and maintenance activities are supported that ensure the upkeep of essential humanitarian infrastructure and dignified living conditions in PoC sites. As camp management agency, IOM works with humanitarian partners in order to respond to the needs of the population. This serves to avoid gaps and duplications in the services provided in the PoC and identify synergies between organizations and cluster’s response while ensuring the minimum standards are met within the sites. With some PoCs entering their sixth year of existence, maintaining a healthy, safe, and dignified living environment in the sites remains a challenge, and it is critical to maintain core humanitarian infrastructure in the coming year. IOM will continue to maintain operations to provide basic services, as well as camp management and site care and maintenance. IOM will maintain the existing Bentiu, Malakal and Wau PoCs, with a focus on sustainability and preparedness measures. Where feasible, this will include addressing threats such as fire and flooding, as well as identifying sustainable and environmentally conscious approaches, such as replanting trees to prevent erosion. Resilience measures include efforts towards better energy efficiency, working with WASH actors to support waste collection and safe disposal, water conservation, using energy efficient stoves and solar systems. It is critical to put in place sustainability measures, including increasing beneficiary participation in activities, and investing in national staff capacity. Participation is a key element for allowing a well-functioning camp environment. IOM therefore engages on a regular basis with different camp governance structures, such as the Camp High Committees, Youth Groups and Women’s Groups to obtain feedback on the key issues affecting specific populations within the sites. In parallel to encouraging participation and strengthening communication with communities, there is a need to build the capacity the PoC residents and empower them to play a greater role in the management of the sites. In mobile responses, coordination is critical to avoid duplication and ensure affected population benefits from information, traditional community leadership is supported and is inclusive, avoid further movements into the PoCs where resources are limited and sites are congested. Regarding safety and security in the PoC site, IOM works closely with UNMISS which hold as primary mandate to ensure the physical protection of the IDPs within the PoC site. In order to support UNMISS effort to ensure a safe and peaceful PoC, IOM is working with other partners to map risks and threats that may emerge within the PoC. In addition, fire risk assessments and response were prioritized in the course of 2018 and should be expanded and updated in 2019.

Activities

The CCCM Cluster will target 855,000 of the 1.4 million persons in need. The Cluster will continue to prioritize new and unreached IDPs in camp and camp-like settings while maintaining responsibility to sites of protracted displacement, including PoCs. IOM will focus on providing fire safety in Wau and Bentiu POCs and share results with the Cluster to identify required support in other sites. In Malakal PoC, site works will be completed following space reconfiguration in 2018. IOM will promote meaningful participation focused on women, PWDs, and disenfranchised community members, and respond to new influxes through a mobile approach, supporting NNGOs in hard-to-reach areas with technical support and coordination provided by State Focal Points. Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Beneficiaries are provided with information through regular meetings, service mapping, town hall meetings, and information boards, which are constantly updated in all sites managed by IOM. Information and complaint desks are accessible and the community is increasingly making use of this mechanism to obtain information. In addition, sensitization campaigns and partnerships with specialized organizations on Communication with Communities have improved access and outreach to the beneficiaries, such as a humanitarian radio station in Bentiu and the implementation of a Boda Boda Talk Talk mobile loudspeaker program in Wau. These activities will continue in 2019.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context-specific and tailored for the targeted audience respecting age, gender, and diversity. All messages and meeting minutes are translated and shared via notice boards keeping in mind cultural context and target beneficiaries. Messages to specific groups, such as women and persons with disability, are developed in consultation with specialized partners. In 2019, IOM will ensure the placement of specific messages from Camp Management in women friendly spaces. IOM communication team are experienced in developing context specific messages and coordinate with other clusters for specific sectoral messages.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

IOM Camp Management staff are trained on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse in line with the IOM Code of Conduct. CCCM teams also work closely with IOM’s Protection Officer and IOM’s GVB Specialist to ensure that protection and PSEA is mainstreamed in all activities and that safety and security of targeted population is safeguarded. IOM Camp Management provides information on reporting channels, conducts regular trainings with staff and volunteers, and displays complaint mechanisms throughout the sites, which are regularly updated.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

In addition to the regular consultations with leadership, IOM has been adopting town hall meetings as a way to reach out and consult at the household level. IOM also conducts regular satisfaction surveys to determine gaps concerning information and services in Wau and Bentiu. Focus Groups Discussions are also conducted on an ad hoc basis, which enable consultations concerning the immediate impact of interventions, such as infrastructure improvement for persons with disabilities and utilization of public spaces in the sites.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Camp management maintains a regular complaint and feedback mechanism through which individuals can reach out to camp management who will then channel the complaint to the responsible sector. Extension of the complaint and feedback mechanism has also been piloted in mobile responses such as Agok in Western Bahr el Ghazal.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Elected leadership participates in monitoring of space and service provision in all sites. Camp management, together with community and partners, supports and facilitate community ownership of the care and maintenance of tertiary drainage and support in facilities maintenance.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Conflict sensitivity and observance of do no harm are incorporated in project planning. Camp management is active in participating in safety audits, regular monitoring of security provision and community watch groups, and consults with the community in general matters such as accessibility for persons with disability. Such consultations enable camp management to understand the specific risks associated to women, men, girls and boys in each site.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

IOM program will engage in more consultations and assessments to continue improving infrastructures and, in coordination with partners, address barriers preventing vulnerable groups from fully participating in the camp life. IOM Camp Management has acted upon recommendations on specialized report regarding access to services and facilities for persons with disabilities. Some of the barriers to be addressed include physical barriers, through infrastructure improvement and site planning, and cultural barriers, through meaningful engagement with persons with disabilities and the community in general, as well as undertaking assessments on how to link such groups with livelihood opportunities. In addition, IOM will continue addressing concerns through complaint and feedback mechanisms already in place.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In order to address inequalities, IOM will continue to promote women participation and the meaningful participation of persons with disabilities. This will be addressed by reaching out to governance structures and also reaching out the community in general by improved CwC. On CwC IOM intends to continue partnership with specialized partners to improve campaigns and access to the community. Regarding protection threats, IOM continues to engage UNMISS as the primary stakeholder in security maintenance.
