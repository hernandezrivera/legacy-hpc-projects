Project Summary

based on the HNO 2023, The livestock has not been spared from the weather anomalies, including issues with water scarcity and availability, damaged infrastructure and productive assets, disrupted services and scarcity of energy resources, limited access to livestock production inputs especially feed, and the generally unfavourable weather conditions. The unfavourable 2021 – 2022 rainfall season resulted in a sharp decline in pasture growth and development (critical for honeybees), increased demand for fodder (sugar), limited supply, and high prices, especially imported feed and this trend is likely to continue if the 2022/2023 rainy season is not favourable . The worsening economic crisis affecting Syria and the inability of small livestock keepers (including beekeepers) to provide adequate fodder for their herds (bees) has also resulted in less efficient animal health services. The above-mentioned challenges continue to severely undermine the health and nutrition of livestock and reduced the viability of livestock-based food production and livelihood systems.
Despite that 1,462,056 beneficiaries have been reached with livelihoods interventions during 2022, beekeepers represent very tiny portion of the assisted HHs (the large majority were farmers and livestock breeders / LEGS)
In addition, primary needs assessment was carried out during 2022 for beekeepers in North West Syria (NWS), which revealed that beekeepers are losing their livelihoods gradually year by year, the core reasons are high production costs, loss of productive assets, and limited support provided by humanitarian community and local authorities, Seeds of Hope (SOH) aims to protect and develop the livelihoods of 400 Households working in beekeeping (in Aleppo/Al-Bab & Azaz districts) by providing them with:

1- Vocational Training to enhance productivity (5 Training workshops)
2- Cash Assistant to cover operational expenses, (120$ for 400 beekeepers)
3- Organize the sector by registering beekeepers (100) and tagging beehives (700)
4- Restore assets distroyed by natural disasters if any (100 beehives for 20 beekeepers).
to create feasible income that saves their dignities and sustains their businesses and protect them from negative coping strategies under the stress of conﬂict. The project includes the study of the Honey value chain in the local context and ﬁnding solutions for main barriers hindering this social/economic activity from reaching its maximum limits. The project will provide the following services for the targeted beekeepers: 1- Support food security and operational costs for beekeepers 2- Organize the profession locally.
Targeted groups are "beekeepers" from IDPs, Returnees, and Host Community, from females and males across all age groups.
Selection Criteria are based on elegibility (BNF lives in project areas, and he/she owns at least 5 beehives) and vulnerability (vulnerable people will be given priority e.g. IDPs, HHs headed by women, HHs with above 6 childern, PWDs* ...etc.) other special criteria maybe added based on agreement with LCs.
special efforts will be undertaken during registration phase to involve women and PWDs in the beekeeping sector by holding special FGDs, and interviews with female and PWDs applicants to understand barriers to access this profession and remove them accordingly (to ensure promoting gender equality)
Beekeeping requires physical efforts to lift and transport beehives, PWDs as beekeeping business owners who employ/utilize others to carry out the physical activities of the profession is highly encouraged to apply, MEAL team will conduct FGDs in coordination with LCs to discover the challenges that face PWDs in beekeeping in addition to individual interviews during registration process and PDM.
No fund is secured so far.

3 - Needs

Seeds of Hope based on HNO 2023 and in coordination with donor carried out needs assessment for beekeepers for 6 months (during the last project implementation) in Aleppo Northern areas (Al bab, Azaz, Jerablus, Sharan), the survey covered sample of the beekeepers in the area, who reported the following needs:1- Covering part of the operational expenses (especially after fuel price increase which affected transporting beehives to pastures)2- Restore assets destroyed because of the conflict and other natural crisis (displacement, uncontrolled agricultural pesticides application)3- Provide training on special subjects important for local beekeepers (pest control, queens breeding)4- Organize the general practice in coordination with local councels.5- Provide beekeeping tools and honey marketing solutions for the beekeepers. before the humanitarian crisis in Syria, the Syrian beekeeper had the freedom to move his beehives to the pastures available in the various flowering seasons throughout the Syrian geography, and had the ability to reach stable markets.However, the conditions imposed by the conflict, such as displacement, loss of productive assets, and the inability to access the majority of the pastures, the disturbance of the market system, the high prices of raw materials and the expenses related to operating the apiaries, collectively led to a decline in the profession, and severely affected beekeepers’ livelihoods.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project activities will be implemented by professional agricultural engineers specialized in beekeeping, and according to the FSL Cluster guidelines to standardize the intervention as follows: Expert beekeepers (Consultants) will evaluate the beekeepers businesses/livelihoods from two aspects: 1- professional practices of beekeepers in utilizing existing beekeeping assets in production (to be followed by vocational training whenever necessary). 2- after evaluation, the direction of the assistance will be decided in agreement with the local council.a. Cash assistance (% coverage of operation expenses) will be transferred to beneficiaries Ptt accounts according to the legal procedures imposed by Turksih authorities.b. In-kind assistance (productive assets / Beehives) will be reviewed and approved by our beekeeping experts (specs and design) in negotiation with local council’s agriculture directorate.c. Tender will be announced to ensure competitive bids, and the formal procurement policies and procedures will be followed in bids evaluation and award.d. Our experts will inspect the delivered beehives and ensure they fulfill the quality requirements stated in the tender document, and handover (5 beehives for 20 beekeepers) with orientation session on-site.e. PDM will be conducted by the MEAL team, and post implementation evaluation to capture the sustainable impact of the beneficiary livelihood.

PSEA Risk/Threat 2: beneficiaries sexually harassed or abused in exchange for cash assistance, Likelihood: low, Impact: high, Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the safest mechanism for handing over the cash assistance, Monitoring: Enhance communication:(i) distribute brochures during distribution including hot-line numbers for reporting PSEAProtection Risk/Threat 3: Training will not be utilized by beekeepers to enhance livelihood activities, Likelihood: medium, Impact: medium, Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the reasoning behind the poor practice of training and solicit their feedback with regards to solutions, Monitoring: Enhance data collection: (i) align PDM tool with global standards; where possible, (ii) collect qualitative data through FGDs with different segments of the community; and (iii) consistently review and address contents of feedback mechanisms. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

PSEA Risk/Threat 1: beneficiaries sexually harassed or abused in exchange for project aids, Likelihood: low, Impact: high, Mitigating Measure: Consult community throughout program cycle to determine the safest mechanism for handing over the cash/in-kind assistance, Monitoring: Enhance communication: (i) distribute brochures before/during aids distribution including hot-line numbers for reporting PSEA incidents (ii) avoid 1 to 1 connection with beneficiaries (SOH front-end team will consist of 1 male and 1 female at least), and (iii) provide training for SOH field staff, and ensure they commit to humanitarian principles and code of conduct signed after recruitment.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The project took into consideration the following risks (beyond the project's control): 1- Mass displacement from Idlib to Aleppo based on conflict escalation (SOH contributes actively with local stakeholders during crises and has plan B to shift operation to Turkish/Syrian borderline). 2- Markets may be un-accessible because of insecurity (modalities will be changed if necessary according to local security conditions/challenges). 3- Some humanitarian actors may conduct uncoordinated assistance for beekeepers without informing SOH (coordination with the local council and FSL cluster will take place before implementation). 4- drought and natural disasters impact, and excessive usage of pesticide (our Development unit in SOH will play the role of DDR to support beekeepers before/after any natural/man-made disasters in coordinatin with local councils).
