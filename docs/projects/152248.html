Project Summary

The project will provide immediate and life-saving humanitarian assistance to address the alarming rates of malnutrition experienced by the most vulnerable populations – malnourished girls and boys, pregnant and lactating women and girls (PLW/G)-affected by the conflict in Borno State, Northeast Nigeria. The gender- and age-differentiated nutrition vulnerabilities of the priority groups will be addressed by increasing equitable access to quality, gender-responsive nutrition interventions targeting children under five and PLW/Gs. Equitable access will be achieved by increasing the capacity of health services and nutrition practices of communities to prevent and respond to acute malnutrition among PLW/G and children under five.

Needs

According to the 2017 Multiple Indicator Cluster Survey (MICS), the prevalence of exclusive breastfeeding for children 0-5 months in Northeast Nigeria was 21.3%, which is below the national average of 23.7%. The minimum dietary diversity was 23.8% in Northeast as compared to 35.6% of the national average.According to a SMART survey conducted in Borno (Magumeri, Konduga, Jere, Mafa and Kaga LGAs) by SCI in August 2018, the prevalence of GAM was 15.7% and that of SAM was 4.2%. In the same survey, under 5 mortality was found to be at 1.60 deaths in children under five/ 10,000 children under five / day. This is evidence that the nutrition emergency remains in LGAs, and that there is still need to not only continue, but also expand nutrition interventions. According to MICs the trend of malnutrition was on the increase from 2007 to 2013 (from 10% to 18%. The situation has not improved over time due to insecurity and displacement of populations thus destructing their livelihood. According to the Nutrition in Emergency Sector Working Group, 76,840 new SAM admissions were made in the first quarter of 2018 in Borno against a target of 130,728, which is 41% gap. Further, 270,172 beneficiaries were also reached with infant and young children feeding (IYCF) in the same quarter. In Borno state, as in overall Northern Nigeria, IYCF practices were extremely poor prior to the conflict, and the challenges to exclusive breastfeeding and healthy IYCF-E practices faced due to displacement and strain from the conflict are significantly contributing to malnutrition in children under two years. In addition to making treatment available for children under five with SAM, there is an urgent need to prevent and reduce malnutrition through a multi-sectoral approach. Evidence shows that IYCF practices are a key component of child survival and development and a key determinant of children’s nutritional status. Breastfeeding as a preventive measure may have the single largest impact on reducing child mortality, while complementary feeding has been shown to contribute to reduce stunting.

Activities

The health and nutrition of priority groups will be improved via direct treatment of severely malnourished children under 5 (CU5); information and counselling on Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) for PLW/G, female and male caregivers including those with moderately malnourished children; gender-responsive hygiene promotion activities; and promotion of dietary diversification by setting up kitchen gardens for families with malnourished children.
