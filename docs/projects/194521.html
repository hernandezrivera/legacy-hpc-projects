Project Summary

The 2022 South Sudan IPC report indicated that 6.83 million people were in crisis, 2.37 million in emergency and about 1.34 million children U5 were acutely malnourished. In the Eastern Equatoria State (EES), Kapoeta East County (KEC) and Kapoeta South county (KSC) had 60% and 45% of their population respectively in dire need of food. EES had a GAM prevalence of 9% with KEC in IPC AMN phase 4 (SMART survey 2021). Food Consumption Score (FCS) were poor among 45% HH in KSC while Household Dietary Diversity Scores (HDDS) were sub-optimal among 46% HH in KEC and 27% in KSC (IPC, April, 2022) .Therefore, to complement the on-going interventions, AFSS proposes to implement the “Provision of quality nutrition services for all children aged 6-59months and women and ensuring families have the knowledge, skills, and resources to apply quality infant and young child feeding practices” project in Kapoeta East and Kapoeta South counties. AFSS intends to use a combined nutrition-specific and sensitive approach in a continuum of the current UNICEF-supported nutrition interventions. The nutrition-specific activities will seek to increase access to and coverage of appropriate treatment of SAM and MAM cases as per the national CMAM guidelines. Nutrition-sensitive activities will be geared toward addressing the underlying causes of malnutrition such as; poor child care practices, sub optimal MIYCN and WASH practices through SBCC, supporting households to produce and consume diverse micro nutrient-rich foods using contextualized climate-smart techniques. The nutrition specific activities will include treatment of SAM & MAM using specialized nutritious foods (RUFT & RUSF) while nutrition sensitive activities will consist of; testing of all children under five years for malaria and provision of treatment to the identified malaria positive cases admitted to the programme (n=14,328), distribution of Long Lasting Insecticide Treated Nets (LLITN) to all children under five years (14,328), pregnant and lactating women (n=10038), provision of Oral Re-hydration Solution (ORS), and Zinc sulfate to all children having diarrhea and Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) services to all children under five years, management of pneumonia among children under five years, distribution of WASH kits to all children with SAM (n=3503), conducting Routine follow-ups through home visits of admitted cases and individual and group counseling sessions on the WHO/UNICEF recommended MIYCN practices to mothers/ caregivers of children receiving treatment for SAM (3503) and MAM (11,325) as well as the pregnant and lactating women with MAM (12,538). Additionally, AFSS will use Social Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) approaches, such as; music, drama shows and Information Education Communication (IEC) materials (10,000) to disseminate key messages on optimal MIYCN and WASH; establish model vegetable gardens ( in a selected village); conducting food and cooking demonstrations (276), training of mothers/caregivers on the formulation of Sorghum Peanut blend (SPB) (METU-1) (280) using locally available food ingredients to increase food literacy; empowering of community structures including mother to mother support groups (60) and CNVs (170) to provide CMAM & MIYCN services for sustainability; and community dialogues (48) to encourage male involvement in key family care practices in line with COVID-19 guidelines. Knowledge Attitude and Practice assessments (4) will be conducted to strengthen documentation and evidence generation. Smallholder poultry farming and Guinea pig husbandry will be strengthened to promote nutrition sensitive livelihood. This project is in line with 2023 HRP needs and strategic plan.

1 - Needs

The United Nations Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 2 aims to end all forms of hunger and malnutrition globally by 2030, yet many countries in Africa including South Sudan are not on course (Mohajan, H.K 2022). South Sudan is among the countries that were classified by the World Bank as fragile due to conflicts and violence (Julius Busiri et al. 2021). This coupled with the effects of COVID-19 makes South Sudan uncertain about achieving SDG 2 (Mohajan, H.K. 2022). Instead, there are increased risks of malnutrition and related health challenges among children especially those Under five(U5) years of age (Sarah Karaja et al. 2021). The 2022 South Sudan IPC report indicated that 6.83 million people were in crisis, 2.37 million in emergency and about 1.34 million children U5 were acutely malnourished. In the Eastern Equatoria State (EES), Kapoeta East County (KEC) and Kapoeta South County had 60% and 45% of their population respectively in dire need of food. Nearly half of all deaths in children under 5 years of age are attributable to under-nutrition. Malnutrition heightens the risk of dying from common infections, increases the frequency and severity of such infections, and delays recovery. In this regard, the management and prevention of acute malnutrition among children 6-59 month, pregnant and lactating women become paramount. The government of South Sudan and partners including the UNWFP, UNICEF, and nutrition actors like AFSS are implementing nutrition programs in Kapoeta East County to reduce the prevalence of acute malnutrition and consequently prevent malnutrition-related morbidity and mortality among children under five years of age. These nutrition programs are implemented using protocols stipulated in the national guidelines for Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) which mainly involves early identification and treatment of acute malnutrition using specialized nutritious foods. Although the CMAM program is effective in treatment, it has not been sustainable in addressing the root causes of acute malnutrition in that, the prevalence of acute malnutrition in Kapoeta East has remained above the serious (10-14.9%) levels (FSNMS rounds 25-26) and is expected to remain so for a while as projected by the September 2020 IPC report. Couple with these, AFSS enrolled and treated 3,391 SAM and 12,844 MAM cases (children 6-59months), 12,058 Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) in OTP and TSFP respectively (NIS_2021_AFSS) indicating high prevalence. Jie Payam for example (in 2021), like other parts of the county remained dry without any consistent rains and this prompted the communities to migrate to neighboring communities in search of water, food and pasture. This led to high prevalence of malnutrition among children and the PLW. The community also doesn’t have a functional health facility for the provision of routine primary health care services, education facilities and reliable source of safe drinking water except a dam which is always shared amongst humans and animals. In addition, Jie is prone to Insecurity due to cattle raids from the neighboring tribes. This has continuously left women and children more vulnerable and yearn for sustainable support. The withdrawal of HPF due to funding gaps has negatively affected the health sector; six (6) Nutrition sites in Kapoeta East do not Functional health care services and this led to deteriorating health conditions of the local communities/high morbidity hence worsening the nutritional status of children under 5 and PLW.It is on this background that AFSS proposes a combined integrated approach that tackles both nutrition-specific and nutrition-sensitive components that is sustainable and can reduce morbidity through: “Provision of integrated quality nutrition services for all children aged 6-59months and women and ensuring families have the Knowledge, Skills and Resources to apply quality infant and young child feeding practices in Kapoeta East and South''.

2 - Project activities

Conduct massive screening for all children under five and PLW. Provision of treatment to all identified (3503) under five) SAM cases, MAM cases (11325 under five) & MAM- PLW (12538) using RUFT, RUSF and CSB++ respectively. Conduct MIYCN counselling of primary caregivers of children aged 0-59 months (18323), food and cooking demonstration sessions (280) in all the nutrition sites, vitamin A supplementation to all children 6-59months (60626) and deworming exercise for children of 12-59months (56136). Carryout routine monitoring visits on monthly basis (12) and supportive supervision by the senior management on quarterly basis (4). Conduct on job mentorship for nutrition assistants and community nutrition volunteers (60 N.A &170 CNVs). Conduct home visits to track defaulters by nutrition assistants and community nutrition volunteers. Training mothers/caregivers on the formulation of Sorghum Peanut blend (SPB) (METU-1) (280) using locally available food ingredients to increase food literacy. To ensure project ownership and sustainability, community structures such as; mother support groups (30) and CNVs (170) will be empowered to provide CMAM & MIYCN services and community dialogues (48) will be conducted to encourage male involvement in key family care practices. AFSS will use Social Behavior Change Communication (SBCC) approaches, such as; music, drama shows and Information Education Communication (IEC) materials (10,000) to disseminate key messages on optimal MIYCN and WASH. Knowledge Attitude and Practices assessments (2) to strengthen documentation and evidence generation will be conducted. Conduct Disability and GBV safety Audits (1) in all the nutrition sites. Integration with HEALTH, WASH and FSL. Testing of all children under five years for malaria and provision of treatment to the identified malaria positive cases (n=3503), distribution of Long Lasting Insecticide Treated Nets (LLITN) to all children under five years (11494), pregnant and lactating women (n=12538), provision of Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) and Zinc sulphate to all children having diarrhea, management of pneumonia among children under five years, provision of Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) services to all children under five years and pregnant women. Hygiene promotion education messages in all the nutrition facilities, Aqua education messages and distribution in all nutrition sites, distribution of WASH kits to all children admitted with SAM (n=3503) and installation of hand washing facilities in all the nutrition sites. Training and distribution of vegetables seeds to family with SAM and MAM children under five and PLW. Safety measures will be put in place for both staff and beneficiaries’ and regular safety audits will be conducted every after 3 months to identify areas of non-compliance and assess other opportunities to improve the program. All these will be implemented in line with COVID-19 guidelines.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

A Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) will be in place in which help desks will be established at all service points in which the vulnerable members will report any complaint in relation to the project. The most vulnerable members will be enlightened on the modalities of the project, where to report cases of abuse and how they will get their feedback. These help desks will be headed by one member selected from the community with the endorsement of the vulnerable members. Sensitization on prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Gender based violence will be integrated into all the educative sessions. Toll free numbers will be availed to the vulnerable members to report any cases of discrimination, sexual abuse. For those unable to read or write, posters that carry pictures on PSEA, where and how to report will be displayed at service points and in the communities. Screening for disability will be conducted with the aid of Child Functional Module and Washington Group Disability Questionnaire tools and AFSS will ensure that people with special needs are taken care of despite their disabilities during the implementation period. Safety measures will be in place for all the vulnerable members to ensure they have equal access to services and regular audits (Quarterly) will be done to uncover any issues that may endanger their health and safety.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community mobilization and sensitization about the project will be done through awareness campaigns; nutrition crusades in community centers using public address systems. Announcements during religious gathering, interpersonal communication by CNVs, MTMSG, Nutrition Assistants; Distribution of Information Education communication (IEC) materials about the project to the beneficiaries and community; Orientation of the key stakeholders about the project modalities; during community dialogues and meetings. Use of visibility materials including T/shirts, brochures, leaflets with messages about the project. Complaint and feed desks will be established in all the nutrition sites to get any feedback from the beneficiaries and community

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Gender equality will be considered at all stages of programme implementation following the AFSS gender policy 2017. During staff recruitment, right from top managers to participating beneficiaries’ gender equality will be observed. During trainings, equal participation of both men/boys and women/girls will be given priority. All stakeholder’s/ gender actors will be contacted at all times for proper coordination and to avoid conflict of interests. Community dialogues will be conducted quarterly and any gender related issues raised will be managed by AFSS gender and protection personnel together with County gender personnel in Kapoeta East. Beneficiaries and communities will also be given toll free numbers where they can report any issue of gender inequality at any time without physical contact with AFSS/County personnel in cases of fear. The selection of the service delivery points will be done jointly with the local leaders and community members to ensure that the selected sites are closer to the communities to safeguard the beneficiaries. AFSS will ensure that the beneficiaries do not walk a distance longer than 5kms by taking services closer to them through outreach programs. The selection criteria will ensure that there are no protection risks. The entire implementation team from AFSS staff, community leaders and the beneficiaries will be oriented on Sexual Exploitation & Abuse (SEA) and status discrimination. Clear penalties for culprits will be stipulated. Also, the participants will always be informed about their rights and how to go about it when violated. All cases reported will be treated with integrity and confidentiality. This strategy will be under the mandate of gender and protection officer employed by AFSS. All the community interactions shall be conducted in observance of the Covid-19 guidelines.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

AFSS will develop complaints and feedback journals. These will be used to document and track complaints. Other platforms including community dialogues and home visits will be used to collect feedback and complaints. Joint monitoring exercise will also be used as avenue through which the project beneficiaries can share their feedback regarding the project with their local leaders. Safety Audits will be carried out to assess the project performance in relation to Gender and Disability status of the beneficiaries served. The results of the Safety Audits will be used to designed the project to ensure that all beneficiaries have equal access to the services provided .Data from the complaints and feedback journals as well as routine assessments will be used for improvement of the project deliverables, redesign of the project objectives and indicators. Additionally, all Cluster coordination meetings at both Payam and cluster levels will be used to obtain feedback and recommendations that will be used to redesign the project
