Project Summary

According CPSC strategy for HRP 2021 to this will entail the expansion of Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS), community based child protection (CBCP) approaches; family tracing, reunification and alternative care for unaccompanied and separated children, strengthening case management including targeted assistance to Children with Specific Needs and the extension of services to children in contact with the law; support to child survivors of sexual violence; community-based reintegration services for children separated from armed forces or armed groups; and technical and institutional capacity strengthening of local actors, frontline child protection workers and community volunteers.
The key activities will involve 15 awareness raising campaigns on protecting children from exploitation, abuse, physical injuries, and health and sanitation risks, establishment of 5 community based child protection networks for monitoring, reporting and referral of CP cases. The project will facilitate the training of 5 Community Based Child Protection Network on Child protection and Community Policing (5 Groups, 12 members each) and facilitate 10 Public awareness on Mine Risk Education through CBCPNs including 10 campaigns in CFS, schools and in community’s about gender based violence and Child protection.
The project will also facilitate the establishment of 6 new Child Friendly Spaces and rehabilitate 4 existing Child Friendly Spaces, provide psychosocial support including MHPSS to 17,500 children, train 100 (50F, 50M) Teachers, PTAs, and SMCs members on Child Protection, PFA and PSS. In addition, 30 SSPDF and SSPF Officers will be trained on prevention of child recruitment into military ranks.
For robust community based implementation, 10 FGD sessions will be facilitated to trigger dialogue positive parenting with 200 participants (150F, 50M) so as to improve on positive parenting practices. Due to the huge presence of IDPs in Yei and Lainya Counties, The project will provide life-skills and vocation training to 50 vulnerable children in the IDP collective locations.
Finally, the project will support for 150 unaccompanied and separated children via interim care arrangements and facilitate the identification, registration, documentation and reunification for separated and unaccompanied children targeting 100 children.

1 - Needs

The outbreak of the civil war in South Sudan in December 2013 created a complex humanitarian crisis. South Sudan communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19 including hyperinflation. About 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees within neighboring countries (South Sudan HNO 2021). According to Integrated Phase Classification (IPC) of Food Insecurity in South Sudan (IPC Oct 2020-July 2021) predicted that about 5.8 million people or 48% of the people analyzed will likely face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) between December 2020 and March 2021 and 7.2 million people or 60% of the people analyzed will likely face high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) from April to July 2021. Within the projected period, Yei and Lainya Counties are classified to be in IPC Phase 3 (Crises Level).However, localized conflicts, including inter-communal violence, cattle raiding, revenge killings, child abduction and kidnappings, have been reported across Yei and Lainya counties, impacting households’ ability to access food, and livelihood sources. People’s coping mechanisms weakened as a consequence of the cumulative shocks, leading families to adopt negative practices and children continue to be expose on psycho-social distress, family separation, abuses, neglect, exploitation, sexual exploitation, forced labor, child abduction, child re/recruitment, malnutrition, communicable diseases and physical violence. IPCS teams currently implementing a Child Protection Project in Lainya and Yei Counties found visible cases of people with special needs (PSN) such as extremely vulnerable individual children, orphans, elderly and persons with disabilities. An assessment conduct by SSUHA, in May 2020, suggest that Lainya County host about 6,416 IDPs (3,849 females and 2567 males) among them are 898 children under-5 and 2,695 children 5-17 years including 462 persons above 60 years. On 24 April 2020, IOM DTM report confirmed the displacement of 6,021 individuals (1,261 households) from Lorega in Lainya County who arrived in Jezira displacement site in Yei River County. Nearly two thirds of IDPs were female (64%) of whom 30% were under 18 years old.Yei County has an estimated population of 294,818 people and according the CPSC PIN in Yei County, the CPSC has targeted 93,591 people about 31% of the population in the HRP 2021. Lainya County has an estimated population of 110,939 people ad according the PIN in Lainya County, the CPSC has targeted 30,135 people about 27.1% of the population in the HRP 2021. This project is aligned to South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan 2021 Strategic Objective 2 which is to ”Ensure safe, equitable and dignified access to critical cross-sectoral basic services to enable populations meet their basic needs in locations of severity level and in accordance with Specific Objective 2.2.4 which is to ”Reduce the suffering of girls, boys, women, men, older persons, persons with disabilities, and other persons with specific needs at risk of, or who experienced violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect, including gender-based violence, through the provision of specialized protection and multi-sectoral services”.Proposed activities are in line with the Child Protection Sub-Cluster (CPSC) and Protection Cluster priorities, which include identification, reunification and provision of interim cares to separated and unaccompanied children; reintegration of children associated with armed forces and groups as well as prevention of family separation, children recruitment, as well as land mines and sexual violence and abuses preventions. In addition Institute for Promotion of Civil Society (IPCS) will establish and strengthen community based child protection networks in order to monitor, report and referee protection concerns.
