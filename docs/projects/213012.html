Project Summary

In 2024, over 14.9 million people in Syria need life-saving primary and secondary health care, this includes 5.1 million displaced persons, 2.3 million children under five years – including 532,595 live births expected, 4.1 million women of reproductive age (15-49 years), more than 700,000 older persons, and 2.4 million persons with disabilities. Categories 3 and 4 health severity districts have increased from 89 and 113 in 2023 to 122 and 118 in 2024. 152 out of 270 sub-districts, home to 11.76 million people, lack minimum standards for hospital beds, healthcare workers, and functional primary healthcare centres (HNO 2024).
The project aims to address the severe healthcare challenges in North-West Syria, where compounded emergencies and socio-economic decline have resulted in widespread mental distress and significant health system strains. With many health facilities non-operational, the project focuses on improving access to primary and secondary healthcare services, including specialized oncology, for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host communities. Expected outputs include comprehensive primary healthcare services, impactful secondary healthcare, and improved access to oncology services. Specifically, the project establishes five primary healthcare centers and operates one general hospital, and one maternity. Through these facilities, services ranging from communicable and non-communicable diseases to emergency care, primary and specialized treatments are provided. Moreover, the project ensures equal access to quality healthcare services for all genders and age groups, directly addressing the diverse needs of women, men, boys, girls, and vulnerable individuals.
Key Project Risks:
- Natural Phenomenon/Earthquake/Aftershock (Medium):
Sheltering during earthquakes and avoiding aftershock-prone areas.
- General Protests & Demonstrations (Medium):
Avoidance of protest areas and maintaining a neutral stance.
- Aerial Bombardment/Shelling/Mortar (Medium):
Various security measures include following SOPs, fortification, and early warning systems.
- Opposition armed group internal clashes (Medium):
Provision of situation reports, adherence to security protocols, halting activities if necessary, and SOPs.
- Lack of Skilled Staff and Resource Access (Medium):
Recruitment strategies, staff care, training programs, and advocacy.
- Delay in Procurement of Medical Supplies (Medium):
Early procurement approval and good planning.
- Continuous Fluctuation in Exchange Rate (High):
Advance funding, monthly budget review, and early fund amendment requests.
- Temporary closure of border crossing between Türkiye and Syria for humanitarian aid (Medium):
A reserve was allocated for contingency cases and close contacts with local Turkish authorities to ease the border crossing of goods and personnel.
Protection/gender/GBV strategy
MI prioritizes gender equality across all its programs, mandating the disaggregation of data by gender and age. Compliance with this standard is obligatory for MI's partners. Ensuring equitable access to medical supplies, MI actively fosters fair healthcare delivery.
Field staff undergo comprehensive training on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) principles and utilize gender markers to address healthcare accessibility. The project staunchly advocates for the equitable access to humanitarian services, seamlessly integrating Gender-Based Violence (GBV) policies and PSEA principles through staff, activities, and information dissemination. All MI and partner staff receive orientation on no-harm principles and the protection of beneficiaries' rights. The project seamlessly integrates GBV awareness and PSEA measures, providing confidential complaint mechanisms and hotlines for reporting misconduct. Risk mitigation strategies include comprehensive service mapping, safety audits, and the provision gender and age sensitive facilities and services.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project aims to improve access to primary and secondary health services, and specialized oncology in NW Syria for IDPs and host communities. The project participants will receive health services with the following expected outputs: • Provision, access, and availability of comprehensive primary health services to the planned population. • Impactful, lifesaving, and life-sustaining secondary health care services provided to the planned population.• Provision of improved access and availability to oncology services such as diagnostics, early detection procedures, and financial support. The project aimed to improve the population's health care in the Northern Aleppo "Azaz district" and Idlib "Harem district" by providing primary and secondary healthcare services. The specific objective will be achieved regarding primary health care through 5 primary healthcare centres. The package of provided services includes communicable diseases (CD), non-communicable diseases (NCD), minor injuries management, and reproductive health services (RH) (excluding family planning services). However, referral will be provided for patients who need those family planning services. The Secondary health services are provided through 1 general hospital, and 1 maternity. The services include emergency care (ER), internal medicine, general surgery, orthopaedics, Ear, Nose and Throat (ENT), urology, neurology, radiology, ophthalmology, nephrology, gynaecology, and intensive care unit (ICU) and laboratory services. This project directly addresses the different needs of women, men, boys, girls, and vulnerable people in the health and nutritional sectors, and other needs indirectly through either referring, consultation or empowering (recruiting and capacity building). As a health and nutrition project, the project will deliver equal qualitative services for all genders and age groups at several service levels. Bab Al-Salam Hospital MI will support all departments except the pediatric which will be provide through IDA using another funding sources. While for Hope Hospital MI will support only the maternal services, and the pediatric will be provided through IDA using other funding sources.
