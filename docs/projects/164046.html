Project Summary

Returnees are assisted in compliance with the Sector Minimum Standards. Installation of durable material at the place of origin by selected local construction companies in the governorates of DRC intervention. This is a needs-based intervention taking into account vulnerability criteria in the selection of people beneﬁting from this intervention. DRC target number for 2020: 36.000 individuals
IDP assistance follows Shelter Sector Standards and is executed in the governorates of DRC intervention. Emergency IDP assistance may be provided in camp settings, collective shelters, and private unﬁnished/demolished buildings ( where the needs do arise ) in both, urban and rural areas. Newly displaced populations get prioritized as emergency response, according to the Sector’s directive. DRC target number for 2020: 17.500

4 - Needs

As outlined in the SNFI HNO 2020 the Analyses of Humanitarian Needs is as follow:MSNA data demonstrates the cumulative impact of the crisis. More than three-quarters of those paying rents are unable to afford it. Shelter adequacy is critical in 238 out of 272 sub-districts. This translates into families not having the security of tenancy while others living in an inadequate shelter may be left unprotected from the elements, in insecure situations or overcrowded housing, raising possible risks to personal safety and protection. IDPs are particularly affected by shelter damage and deterioration - there has been a 30 percent reduction in the number of IDPs with access to undamaged shelter. However, the overall IDP population has not decreased, while return movements to some areas have increased over the last three years. In the north-west, there are critical levels of overcrowding putting significant pressure on overburdened communities, with few if any remaining shelter options available. In north-east Syria, ongoing displacement coupled with a lack of safe or adequate shelter, and explosive contamination is a significant obstacle. Across Syria, levels of damage and destruction to shelters have not improved over the last year with 76% of all households listing at least one issue as compared to 2019 where 47% cited adequacy issues, thereby reducing the potential for sustainable return and undermining attempts to restore basic services. This lack of access to basic services including safe and adequate shelter increases the vulnerability of households and forces the adoption of harmful coping mechanisms such as selling assets or keeping children out of education, especially among female-headed households, households including youth, and those with functional difficulties

5 - Implementation of activities

Activities related to both, durable solution and IDP response, are carried out by local construction companies. DRC team is conducting the technical assessments to identify the needs which form the relevant BoQ and drawings of the specific scope of work. In compliance with DRC procurement guidelines, in a comprehensive and transparent process, the company submitting the best offer for the execution of the said work is contracted. In each governorate of intervention, the DRC technical unit comprises a responsible team leader and field staff in number to monitor and oversee the contractor’s performance with regard to quality, progress and other contract relevant processes (material delivery, etc.). The evaluation of the outcome of the final executed work is taken over by the DRC internal MEAL department in conducting a satisfactory survey and other monitoring processes involving different stakeholders.

Advocate with local authorities to facilitate approval for the spontaneous settlement.2. Increased risk of inter or intra-community tension due to varied assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria or poorly timed distributions leading to a feeling of discrimination. Tension can also arise between the host community and IDPs, due to a lack of assistance from the previous. LH: HIGH, IMPACT: MIDUM, Mitigating Measure: Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Ensure beneficiary selection criteria is uniform/agreed among sector members and with the government and are clearly communicated widely within a community and amongst different groups. Engage with community leaders, to raise awareness in the community. Establish a complaint mechanisms at the community level.3. LH: HIGH, IMPACT: MIDUM, Mitigating Measure: Distribution methodology with clear protection risks analyzed and mitigated.Verified beneficiary list to prevent cash or voucher value being misused for other non-humanitarian purposes.4. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Due to international bank sanctions, the cash flow from the HQ office to the country mission’s bank account might be affected resulting in a shortage of funds for operations and covering fix costs. Mitigation measure: exploring in several cash flow routes: from HQ to other banks in the neighboring country to DRC Syria bank account 2. Local authorities are not providing required approvals for the intervention and/or for the beneficiaries selected. Mitigation measure: maintaining a good relationship with local authorities, advocation towards the authorities for the benefit of the addressed population, active participation in the Sector Cluster3. The crises in Syria are expanding to governorates of implementation and thus no access to DRC is granted. Mitigation measure: re-allocating the funds to areas of access, exploring the possibility to execute work through local partners having granted access4. Unavailability of material at the local market in quantity and quality required. Mitigation measure: conducting a market survey in case of expected shortage, selection of contractors having a reliable logistics department and/or required material on stock and/or agreements with suppliers.
