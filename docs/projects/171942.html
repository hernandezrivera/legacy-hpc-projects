Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis, as a consequence of the prolonged military conflict in eastern Ukraine, is aggravated by the growing COVID-19 pandemic and its negative impact on civil communities. Conflict-affected populations at NGCA face additional threats and a higher risk of death due to COVID-19.
The population in rural areas has limited access to sources of livelihood, more dependent on additional funds. They are more likely to suffer from high unemployment and poor transport infrastructure connecting them to nearby villages and towns. Farming and cattle breeding are common to meet basic needs and create conditions for further survival but the necessary equipment is not always sufficient for this.
And if the population of working age can independently obtain the means of subsistence, the elderly are forced to rely only on social or humanitarian assistance. About 500-600 thousand people are deprived of the opportunity to leave for the GCA and receive a pension or benefit due to the impossibility of crossing the contact line due to quarantine. These factors have led to an increase in morbidity and mortality among the elderly living on the NGCA.
Pensioners, disabled people and socially unprotected citizens are forced to live on the minimum allowance, which is provided by social institutions of the NGCA. From 01/01/2020 the minimum allowance is 4800 RUB (UAH 1760 at NBU rate). Payment for housing and communal services is at least 800 RUB (294 UAH), the cost of the necessary food set (5 kg of meat and fish, 10 kg of cereals, 10 kg of vegetables, 10 loaves of bread, 4 l of milk, 2 packs of butter, 1 l of vegetable oil) in the NGCA will be at least 2350 RUB (862 UAH).
The overwhelming majority of elderly people feel the need for regular intake of essential medicines, their purchase is a mandatory item of expenditure, and is at least 1,500 RUB (550 UAH). Thus, the minimum expenses of a socially vulnerable person per month will amount to 4,650 RUB (UAH 1705) for self-satisfaction of basic needs (excluding expenses for hygiene products, disinfection, clothing and footwear, purchase of solid household fuel). Thus, the amount of compulsory costs is 97% of the amount of the benefit received.
Women living in rural areas have significantly less access to livelihoods. They often suffer from a lack of service delivery, high unemployment rates and very poor road infrastructure connecting them to nearby villages and towns. To meet basic needs and create conditions for further survival, farming and raising livestock are common, but the necessary equipment is not always sufficient for this.
Disease outbreaks affect women and men differently, and the COVID-19 epidemic is exacerbating existing inequalities. Women may be at increased risk of domestic violence due to economic insecurity and heightened household tensions, while limited access to justice in areas along the contact line and at the NGCA makes it difficult to respond to cases of gender-based violence. At the same time, the amount of care women need is decreasing as resources are channelled into responding to the general health crisis.
The implementation of the first project activity is designed to improve welfare and provide 250 households that have resources (land, premises for livestock or crop production) for agricultural activities, the necessary equipment and tools based on their needs and requests.
Supporting at least 50 of the most vulnerable female-headed households that have agricultural resources will create additional opportunities for them to earn independent income, increase the long-term sustainability of beneficiaries, and reduce women's dependence on external factors and traditional humanitarian assistance.
The second project activity is designed to help 1,500 elderly people in the form of food packages for three months of residence. This period will be sufficient for the beneficiaries to be able to spend the savings on the purchase of expensive drugs or essential goods.

1 - Needs

Socio-economic conditions on the NGCA continue to deteriorate, which threatens the life and well-being of the population. There is a clear relation-ship between food insecurity, vulnerability and so-cio-economic trends.In the context of insufficient access to financial re-sources, high unemployment rate, up to 60% of families have low income. Many families are made up of retirees and elderly people. According to a February 2020 REACH survey, at least 50% of as-sessed urban households in Donetsk stated that pensions are their main source of income. With the closure of the CPR, vulnerable persons with NGCA are denied free access to their pensions and GCA benefits (for 36% of assessed households in Do-netsk depend on GCA pensions), which has creat-ed an additional burden and worsened living condi-tions. According to the same survey, in the twelve months prior to data collection, only a small per-centage of respondents (6-15%, depending on lo-cation) received assistance, which indicates the difficulties that humanitarian organizations face in accessing beneficiaries and delivering assistance in non-controlled areas.A vulnerable part of the population requires addi-tional support from humanitarian organizations (45% of all surveyed households reported that they need additional assistance). These categories of those in need include retirees, low-income and large families, single parents, families with disabled children, guardians of minor children or disabled children. People with the above vulnerability crite-ria are often forced to make difficult purchasing choices between essential commodities (such as food, medicine or hygiene products). Basically, old-er people are forced to live without family support and are subject to any serious health problems. Many older people have mobility problems and need constant use of additional hygiene products. According to the Humanitarian Needs Survey 2020, about 1.9 million people in need of assistance live in NGCA. The elderly make up almost a third (32%) of those in need of assistance - the highest rate in the world. Women make up more than half of people in need of help (56%), and many of them take care of their families themselves. The affected areas are also home to a large number of people with disabilities, accounting for 12% of those in need of assistance.According to official data from local social institu-tions, at the beginning of 2020, the rural population is about 107 thousand people. (excluding the townspeople living in the private sector) and runs a personal subsidiary farm in order to provide their own households with food. Women-headed households and large households experience the greatest difficulties in cultivating household plots and in managing livestock.The conflict in eastern Ukraine has a particular im-pact on older people. These people face serious difficulties in accessing basic services due to their disabilities and limited mobility, and are often sepa-rated from their families. They are also more vul-nerable to abuse and neglect, have special health and nutritional needs, and are the most economi-cally vulnerable. Available data indicate that more than half of the people who are food insecure are older people, and the pension is the main source of income for 9 out of 10 older people in NGCA,. At the same time, this income is insufficient to meet basic needs, since a quarter of the pension is spent on medicines and more than half on food.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

As a result of the project activities, it is planned to implement the following activities and achieve the following results:1. Implementation of a set of measures to improve living standards by providing agricultural equipment and tools for 250 most vulnerable households, which will help individual households to receive assistance at a new level of quality. The decision to design measures and the means of intervention at all stages of the activity will be planned based on the needs and requests of the beneficiaries, which will ensure that the appropriate form of interven-tion is implemented.2. Purchase and distribution of food packages for the elderly. This form of intervention is provided for 1,500 people with a frequency of 1 time in three months throughout the year. Priority - lonely, peo-ple with limited mobility and people with disabili-ties.The food set will be completed in accordance with the recommendations of the Cluster.During the implementation of the project activities, all activities will be carried out in compliance with basic humanitarian quality standards and accountability to the affected population. Actions and approaches will enable local communities to participate in key decision-making at all stages of program implementation: evaluation, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and revision. Communicative approaches will be used for this, assuming a transparent and constant two-way communication, through which information about the risks, needs and opportunities of communities will be collected.

3 - Indicators & targets

During the implementation of the project, it is planned to achieve the following goals:1. 250 of the most vulnerable households located along the contact line will receive assistance in the form of agricultural equipment, tools, which will improve the living standards of the vulnerable population in the target agricultural sector, create conditions to help reduce dependence on humani-tarian support, as well as additional conditions for diversifying sources of income2. 6000 food packages were purchased and distrib-uted, calculated to cover the need for food within 3 months. It is expected that the number of elderly people who will receive food packages will be 1,500. Frequency – once every 3 months during the year. As a result of this activity, the saved funds can actually be used to purchase medicines, hygiene products and necessary goods, turn into additional rescue interventions and improve the life of the affected population.The total number of direct beneficiaries is 1750 people.
