Project Summary

Under this project, ALIMA aims to reduce the morbidity and mortality among vulnerable populations (displaced and host populations) affected by humanitarian and health crises in conflict zones in Borno (Jere and Maiduguri LGAs) and Yobe (Karasuwa LGA) States, through access to quality health and nutrition care, while taking into account security and COVID-19 context. ALIMA will work closely with health authorities and communities to ensure sustainability and ownership. Our operations will focus on primary and secondary health care, nutrition, SRH, MHPSS and WASH.

1 - Needs

In Maiduguri, since July 2017, a network of Community Health Workers and Traditional Medical Attendants (TBA) is deployed in the camps and within the communities and regular visits/assessment in Muna and Teachers Village IDP Camp (TVC) allow constant monitoring of health and nutrition situation among the population. In November 2019, ALIMA conducted a Mass MUAC Screening campaign in TVC in Maiduguri, the Campaign aimed at ascertaining the prevalence of malnutrition among children under 6-59 months so as to better prevent morbidity and mortality due to malnutrition. All children between 65 and 110 cm, selected according to their height were screened by Community Health Workers, as they are considered as children from 6 to 59 month. Bilateral pitting oedema was then checked before measuring the child’s MUAC. In case of oedema +, MUAC was not measured. The results (oedema +/or colour of MUAC) was recorded on the tally sheet. Parents of children with oedema or MUAC Red are asked whether they are already in the Nutrition Program (OTP). If there is a case which is not already in a programme, they are referred to the OTP. Due to Covid pandemic in 2020, ALIMA couldn’t conduct such mass screening and relies on IOM figures to evaluate the number of IDPs in camp. In June 2020, a rapid needs assessment was conducted in 11 IDPs Camps and 3 Primary health centers (PHCs) in MMC/Jere LGAs. IDP Camps selected for assessment were listed as having “no health support” in the DTM R31. ALIMA met with community members and leaders to discuss their needs, and assessed the status of shelters, WASH, accessibility, and security at the sites. ALIMA also met with local health authorities to identify PHCs in need of partner support. Three PHCs were selected for assessment and ALIMA met with health workers onsite to review patient registries, pharmacy stocks, observe and identify areas for improvement in the facilities. In Yobe State, a needs assessment was conducted in January 2021 to assess the needs and gaps and determine the intervention area. In order to conduct its evaluation, ALIMA met with the various health and administrative authorities. Meetings with partners were also held (OCHA, PLAN, UNICEF) to define precisely the LGAs to be evaluated. Finally, this assessment was concluded with a field assessment in order to visit the different health centers and meet with health workers in these structures. ALIMA relied on direct observation as well as data from the different health facilities visited during this assessment.

2 - Activities

The intervention aims to respond to the needs identified with the result of ensuring access to free and quality health care to displaced populations and host communities in Borno and Yobe states (Jere and Karasuwa LGAs.As part of this project, ALIMA proposes to act in these 6 activities:1. Emergency primary health care including OPD and references2. Support to emergency secondary healthcare for paediatric cases for Children under 5 (CU5)3. SAM management including screening and treatment4. SRH care including Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEMoNC) and management of sexual assault survivor (GBV)5. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS)6. Rehabilitation and maintenance of WaSH infrastructure in health facilities These activities are divided into 3 sectors and implemented according to the area of intervention Health: ALIMA will focus its intervention on the provision of primary health care consultations as well as mental health, Sexual and Reproductive Health Services (SRH). ALIMA will provide Primary Health care consultations to children under 5 in all targeted areas. ALIMA will provide emergency hospitalization care to CU5 and will reinforce the referencing system in Karasuwa (Yobe) LGA. Drugs, HR and medical equipment will be provided to ensure the proper management of complicated diseases (such as malaria, diarrheal, respiratory diseases) that require intensive care. ALIMA skilled birth attendants will continue attending deliveries in Jere/Maiduguri (Borno) as well as in Karasuwa, ALIMA will provide BEmONC services as a response to the gaps observed in terms of provision of obstetric care. In all areas of intervention in NE Nigeria, ALIMA will rely on a network of community mobilizers (CHIPS) to strengthen the acceptance and participation of the population as well as to reinforce the referral of women requiring obstetric care to ALIMA-supported facilities. ALIMA will support referral for cases requiring referral to higher level health facilities. In Muna/TVC (Borno) as well as in Karasuwa, ALIMA will ensure the clinical and psychosocial management of SGBV cases. The establishment of a strong referral pathway will allow ALIMA to provide comprehensive care to victims of sexual and Gender-Based Violence. A strong partnership will be created with protection actors for whom the case will be referred. Nutrition : ALIMA will continue its emergency response in the ATFC for malnourished CU5. Referrals and counter referrals will be ensured by ALIMA to guarantee a wider coverage of the population and a better access to secondary nutritional care. The State Primary Health Care Development Agency (SPHCDA) will be more involved for ownership and sustainability. WaSH: ALIMA aims at providing health services in safer health structures with adequate clean water and sanitation facilities to control infections. In line with this commitment and the willingness to develop an integrated approach in this project, ALIMA dedicated a specific budget to ensure covering the gaps for WASH in health facilities. The budget of this activity is based on previous needs assessments. Activities will mainly focus on the maintenance and rehabilitation of existing WASH facilities in intervention areas.
