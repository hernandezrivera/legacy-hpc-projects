Project Summary

This project integrates a multi-sector approach, linking Education in Emergencies (EiE) with long-term development. It will be implemented in locations rated 4 on the education sector severity scale based on the HNO 2024: Al Thawara-Raqqa, Lattakia city, and Jabal Saman-Aleppo. Identified locations and schools were selected in collaboration with local authorities (Department of Education) and have been confirmed as areas of high need and potential for return. The main activities of the project include school rehabilitation,
provision of furniture to those schools, organizing NFE classes for out-of-school children and children at risk of dropping out, winterization distribution for all children, increasing the quality of education through teacher training, providing teaching and learning
materials to schools, and improving protection and psychosocial well-being of children in and outside the schools. At the same time, building on previous experience and following Education sector suggestions, the project will support graduating students enrolling in
national exams with transport support to reach the exam locations from students’homes. School rehabilitation works will include strong WASH elements by rehabilitating segregated and dignified WASH facilities in the schools, accessible for children with disabilities, as well as rehabilitating/installing water points that will be accessible not only for the students and staff of the schools, but also to the surrounding communities, thus expanding the number of people benefiting from the project. Hygiene materials will be distributed to schools and students, and students will receive COVID-19 awareness sessions. All schools will receive solar panels that will power the water pumps as well as provide lights in the schools that operate in two shifts, especially during the darker winter season. This project will support 4 NFE cycles within a 2-year project duration. In total, 6000 children will be positively affected by this project. The needs assessment for the proposed proposal was conducted in Aleppo, Latakia, and Raqqa governorates through field visits that took place in December 2023. During these visits, FCA's team assessed the situation of education services and facilities, as well as
the needs of students living in the communities targeted by the assessment, Three schools/areas were selected for project interventions after thorough consultation with the DoEs, the Education sector, and coordination with other INGOs:
In Aleppo:
- Rahmo Hattab School in Jabal Saman subdistrict, Jabal Saman district.
In Ar-Raqqa:
- Mesharefet Assab (in Debsi Afnan), Mansura subdistrict, Ath-Thawrah district.
In Latakia:
- Fares Saubaih (Al Jamhoureh street), Latakia subdistrict, Latakia District.
In the areas surveyed in Aleppo, Latakia, and Raqqa, an estimated 5300 children are outof school. The main reasons given by key informants were poverty, earthquakes, child labor, and child early marriage. The number of Out of School Children (OOSC) in the communities surrounding the three schools is 1000.
While Rahmo Hattab School and Meshrefet Essab are functioning, Fares Subaih School in Latakia is not operating due to significant damage caused by the February earthquake. Allthree schools are in dire need of rehabilitation due to their poor condition. The assessed schools present critical gaps not only in basic facilities, such as classrooms and bathrooms, but also in their overall structure. Most of the schools lack doors, windows, electrical cables, pipes, and tiles, making them unsuitable for immediate use. The remaining doors and windows require maintenance work as most are non-functional. One out of three schools are completely empty of furniture, while the other two have partially available furniture - noting that the average class occupancy in the three schools is around 55 students.

The project addresses the following needs: 1. Addressing Psychosocial Needs, The M&E framework is integral, ensuring stakeholder engagement, quality assessment, and responsive decision-making. Incorporating gender, disability, and protection aspects, the plan uses surveys, interviews, and discussions for impact assessment and indicator measurement. FCA field staff will directly monitor activities, with corrective measures for issues. The M&E officer, supported by field staff, will collect documents, monitor results, and collaborate with the project manager. Templates, frameworks, and indicators prioritize age, gender, and diversity. Data collection tools include surveys, interviews, and checklists, with a baseline established as needed. The M&E officer plays a crucial role in coordinating efforts, ensuring a robust system for enhanced project effectiveness, FCA has a zero-tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse. Complaints mechanisms and whistle-blowing policies and guidelines are already in place in FCA Syria. Relevant complaints will be followed up confidentially at the FCA Syria director level with the management team, together with the Complaints Response Management Committee. If the violation was carried out at the level of CD or SMT, the complaints mechanism focal point has the authority to communicate directly with the FCA HO to inform and initiate a quick investigation. Pro-active measures and protection sessions for such incidents will take place through presenting relevant policies and procedures to beneficiaries and local stakeholders (schools’ teachers, admins, principals) before the kickoff of activities.
