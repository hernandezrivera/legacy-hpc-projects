Project Summary

The goal of this intervention is to strengthen the protective environment for conflict-affected communities through responding to the urgent Protection emergency needs and therefore enhancing resilience and psychosocial wellbeing. NRC will provide frontline protection services in Gaza to prevent and respond to emergency needs including comprehensive protection case management for vulnerable and at-risk individuals, legal aid services and community-based interventions to improve resilience and community self-protective capacity and overcome barriers to access services. This will be achieved through the delivery of a comprehensive package of integrated protection services that provide a continuum of care. NRC’s Protection package will consist of three core programmatic pillars. These pillars include legal aid services, protection case management and referrals, and community-based protection.
NRC will target Palestinians in Gaza affected by the protracted protection crisis and conflict, including vulnerable groups. Legal support will target families at risk of eviction or lacking legal civil documentation; Protection case management will target people with specific needs and at risk of harm, including people with disabilities, elderly people at risk, people with chronic illness, youth at risk, people survivor of physical attack, deprivation of basic services, recent displacement, female headed household. General protection Case Management is provided in recognition that protection risks, and threats go beyond Child Protection and GBV. Cases identified and requiring child protection and GBV support will be promptly referred to specialized CP and GBV agency as per internal Referral SOPs and GBV and CP inter-agency referral pathways Protection case management target vulnerable adult male and female at risk of protection threats compounded by protection vulnerabilities.

1 - Needs

Protection needs analysis HNO 2022 has stressed the urgency to provide critical humanitarian assistance to 5.3 million in the occupied Palestinian territories (oPt), among which 1.2 million are children and 49% women1. The 10-21 of May 2021 conflict escalations, and the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic event have deteriorated the already severe humanitarian crisis, and the outlook of people in need for the year 2022 comparing to 2021 has increased. The context in the oPt has been defined by many analysists and humanitarian actor as a “chronic protection crisis”. Additional key drivers of this protracted protection crisis are the COVID-19 pandemic crisis, the resulting restrictions of movement and limited access to basic and specialized services and the socio-economic distress. In Gaza, the situation is even more acute due to 14 years of military blockade. A recent assessment conducted by NRC has found that fifty-three percent of Palestinians in Gaza are living below the poverty threshold, more than three times the number in the West Bank. The purchasing power of the Gaza population has constantly deteriorated and so has the resilience capacity of communities. Gaza ranks now as the worst employment rate in all Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region and as one of the worst in the overall world. Individuals and families are increasingly resorting to negative mechanisms in their effort to cope with shocks and crisis. The most common negative coping mechanism is making debt and purchase food and goods on credit, followed by reducing non-food expenditure to meet food needs, sending family members elsewhere to eat including begging, selling assets and spend savings. The assessment calculated that it would take an average family six years to recover from debt. The socio-economic vulnerabilities above must be seen as compounding and catalyst to increasing protection risks. The Protection Cluster has identified 1.2 million in oPt in need of protection specialized services, 66% of them reside in the Gaza strip. While the number of Palestinians exposed to violations of IHL and IHRL, including killing, maiming, injuries, forced evictions, restrictions of movement and access to critical services, has increased over the past years, the COVID-19 outbreak and resulting lockdown have shrunken down the preventive and responsive capacity of Protection actors, authorities, and communities. Individuals and families face protection risks including physical assault and violence, ERW contamination, displacement due to insufficient or destroyed shelter, prolonged exposure to severe psychosocial distress, violation of their socio-economic and legal rights, deprivation of access to critical and basic services, child protection and GBV threats, house, land, and properties (HLP) issues and lack of legal civil documentation (LCD) to secure access and movement. Individuals at heightened risks, including persons with disabilities and with chronic illness, older people, women, youth, children, and displaced families, are falling through the cracks due to lack of capacity and resources of the authorities and absence of social welfare services. While multi-sectorial response has been provided to vulnerable people in Gaza, Specialized case management support for people with specific needs is limited and need further strengthening. Following the 10-21 of May 2021 conflict escalation, there are still 8,230 internal displaced people in Gaza, primary because of destroyed or severely damaged shelters and houses. During the conflict, 256 Palestinians were killed in Gaza, including 66 children and 40 women. Among those at least 128 were civilians, 62 members of armed groups and 66 undetermined2. Approximately 1938 people were injured, of whom 610 were children and 400 women3. The Israeli military bombing has also affected critical services infrastructures, including WASH facilities, living approximately 2000 people with no access to safe water, Education facilities, 179 partially or severely damaged, and 33 Health facilities partially or severely damaged, including hospitals and primary health centres. A Rapid Needs Assessment (RNA) conducted by CARE in the aftermath of the escalation, shows that 87% of the interviewees stress the urgency for psychosocial support to mitigate the effects of anxiety and severe distress caused by the military occupation, blockade, latest escalation, and COVID-19 pandemic event4. The population in Gaza reports increasing signs of psychosocial distress including nightmares, disturbed sleeping patterns, prolonged fatigue, hypervigilance, self-harming and suicidal thoughts, lack of concentration and increased tension with communities and within the households. In 2021, the number of Palestinians killed or injured by Israeli forces has disproportionately increased comparing to 2020. As per the recent REACH MSNA, elderly people and people with disabilities accounts for 3% and 2% of total population in need, respectively. People with disabilities remain mostly unidentified and marginalized due to barriers and lack of appropriate services, resulting in unemployment, low education rates, and higher exposure to violence and discrimination5. Lack of services include psychosocial support, rehabilitation, and medical assistive devices. Elderly people remain also a group disproportionately affected by conflict, socio-economic crisis, and pandemic event. During a recent survey conducted by HelpAge, the top perceived risks for older men and women were neglect, isolation, denial of resources and emotional abuse. 86% of elderly people are living with at least one type of disability, the rate increases to 96% per persons over 80 years old.

2 - Activities

1. Individual Protection Case Management, Referrals, and Individual Protection Assistance (Cash for Protection) In 2022 NRC will establish Individual Protection Case Management intervention to support the most vulnerable and at-risk individuals affected by conflict, chronic poverty, violations of human rights and humanitarian law. NRC will provide Case Management services to vulnerable beneficiaries facing protection threats and layering of risks. The eligibility criteria established in 2021 will be constantly updated and to date include: dependants' households, people with disability, people with chronic diseases, recent displacement, youth at risk, elderly persons at risk, individual with severe and prolonged signs of distress. In October 2021, NRC has conducted trainings on Protection in humanitarian action, protection case management and psychosocial support. NRC Case Management and Referral system relies on a robust set of tools, guidance, and capacity building opportunities. The Protection approach is built in line with the Global Protection Cluster guidance and standards, and the UNHCR/IRC Case Management manual for people with specific needs. NRC Palestine can also count with the support of a global specialist who is currently revising the NRC Package for Protection Case Management. In August 2021, NRC has conducted a service mapping exercise and the related document is constantly updated. Cases requiring child protection, GBV and MH specialized support will be promptly referred to specialized actors, following the AoRs guidance on referrals. As part of the case management process, NRC will provide IPA to emergency Protection cases through a cash-for-protection modality. IPA is used as a last resort and is a one-off action aimed at reducing exposure to or limit the effects of a protection risk, which are likely to be exasperated by increased reliability on negative coping mechanisms due to the effects of restrictions, and loss of income related to the outbreak of COVID-19, blockade, siege and socio-economic crisis. IPA is complementary to other NRC protection interventions and as a response can be one of the outcomes of a case management process, based on the individual case. IPA, which follows strict vulnerability criteria assessments. The provision of IPA is not a stand-alone activity, and the provision of cash will be accompanied by a protection action plan where individuals and/or households are supported to identify long-term strategies to address their protection concerns beyond the short-term cash intervention. The delivery of cash should further be coupled with individual counselling on safety and security measures in accessing and managing cash, how the assistance received can support the individual and/or household to address their protection need, and how to access services through referrals and follow up to other services providers if applicable. Individual Protection Assistance will be provided through cash or in kind. Following an assessment conducted by case worker of the specific protection needs and rationale for assistance, a list of the items requested with the estimated value. The IPA disbursement is discussed and approved by a panel which includes the Case Worker, Protection Technical Officer, and Protection Coordinator. A post-distribution monitoring visit is also conducted after the disbursement. 2. Legal Protection Aid and Protection Monitoring In 2022 NRC Lawyers will continue to provide a set of legal aid services to the population in Gaza including HLP services for people at risk of eviction, support to obtain legal documentation, information, and counselling sessions on HLP, LCD, IHL and IHRL. Protection monitoring activities will also be established. 3. Protection Community Based Mechanisms and Psychosocial Support. NRC will address key PSS needs of conflict and displacement affected communities in Gaza with the aim to improve the psychosocial and general wellbeing of affected individuals and their capacity to cope with the shocks and stressors in both protracted conflict and displacement and acute emergencies. Psychosocial support activities will focus on the level 3 of the MHPSS pyramid (non-focused community and family support). The activities will be provided by the local partner, AISHA. Aisha’s has staff with a wide range of expertise such as social and psychological support and GBV issues including psychotherapist, psychiatrist, social workers and psychologists, case managers, Legal advisors, vocational trainers, projects coordinators, financial experts, and admin support staff, etc. Additionally, NRC has recently hired Protection Staff with a solid background in PSS, social-work, and Protection in Gaza and trainings have been conducted on Protection in humanitarian action, Case Management and PSS. AISHA and NRC PSS programming will rely on a robust set of tools and guidance, in line with the IASC Guidelines on Mental Health and PSS in Emergency settings. The PSS curriculum will focus on signs and impact of emergencies on well-being, stress management, positive coping mechanisms and self-care. Unstructured PSS will focus on community events and recreational activities. During the group-based PSS, individuals identified in need of more specialized support will be referred to mental health actor, according to NRC internal SOPs for referral and MoU with partners NGOs such Gaza Centre for mental health program. 4. Protection Capacity Building: NRC will conduct capacity building and training opportunities as considered instrumental to strengthen the social protection system and enhance the protection environment of conflict-affected communities. Capacity building activities on Protection related topics will be conducted for CSOs and CBOs, partner NGOs and authorities. NRC Protection Team will organize trainings on: Protection in Humanitarian action, Protection mainstreaming, Protection Case Management, identification etc.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

In 2021, NRC has started to review its Protection approach in Gaza to make sure its adapted and relevant to the changes of the context. An assessment of Protection internal capacity beyond Legal Aid has been conducted alongside a secondary data review of existing assessments, evaluations, community consultations, and data to understand gaps in service provision. A comprehensive service mapping exercises has also been conducted, and new Protection specialized Staff with solid background in social-work, psychosocial support and community-based activities have been hired. A Protection risks analysis matrix have been developed to assess risks and mitigation measures developed including: a solid capacity building plan in place on Protection in humanitarian action, referrals, case management and psychosocial support and on NRC internal Protection SOPs and Global Protection standards and guidance; establishment of supervision mechanisms; update of the existing referral pathways, SOPs and relevant MoUs. A range of meetings with key stakeholders have been conducted including Protection Cluster, UNICEF and MoSD. NRC Staff can also access to a tailored psychosocial counselling provided by a specialized external contractor, to mitigate the impact of stress and exposure to grief, loss, and trauma, promoting self-care and avoid burn-out. NRC Protection Team will also receive trainings on GBV and Child Protection to be able to identify and refer cases requiring GBV and CP support according to AoRs guidance and pathways. IPA (or Cash for Protection) is provided to address protection needs when the persons are being exposed to specific protection risks or have been subjected to rights-violations. IPA is embedded within the broader Protection Case Management support and used to address protection concerns related to a person’s life, physical safety, psychological well-being, liberty, dignity and other fundamental human rights. These include situations where the concerned individuals or HHs are resorting to negative coping mechanisms generate protection issues, such as accepting exploitative job practices, self-restricting from accessing essential services, using child labor or child marriage, resorting to survival sex etc. due to a lack of resources and alternatives. The provision of IPA is not a stand-alone activity, and the provision of cash will be accompanied by a protection action plan where individuals and/or households are supported to identify long-term strategies to address their protection concerns beyond the short-term cash intervention. The delivery of cash should further be coupled with individual counselling on safety and security measures in accessing and managing cash, how the assistance received can support the individual and/or household to address their protection need, and how to access services through referrals and follow up to other services providers if applicable. Being part of a broader Case Management support, there will be a regular follow-up of the case provided by the case worker and supervision on the use of cash and a post distributing monitoring process. NRC’s Protection Case Management SOPs, IPA SOPs, Community-based and PSS SOPs, will guide Staff and partners in the provision of protection support. Additionally, Staff will be trained on NRC Gender Policy, Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy and PSEA policy and reporting mechanism. NRC’s Code of Conduct holds staff, partners, and associates (e.g., contractors) accountable, while projecting a common set of standards to the beneficiaries being served. PSEA policies and related mechanisms are included in the project design. PSEA prevention and response training is part of the induction for all staff.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

MEANINGFUL ACCESS: eligibility criteria for Protection intervention have been carefully established and adapted to the Palestinian contexts, ensuring the most vulnerable people and hardest to reach communities will be identified and supported in proportion to their need and without any barriers (e.g., discrimination). Special attention will be paid to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services (e.g., elderly people, people with disability, youth at risk, etc.). Protection programme is designed to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestinians in hard-to- reach areas have access to basic rights and humanitarian assistance, reduce vulnerability and build resilience. Information dissemination and awareness: Protection main priorities will be to empower women, men, girls, and boys to have equal access to information and services. Protection will develop information and communication products to support IDPs/refugees and host communities’ access to basic services, rights, and entitlements. The project will also use a community-based and participatory approach to identify and train members of the communities to disseminate information, recognise protection concerns and refer cases to specialised service providers. Emphasis will be placed on the rights and issues of women and other vulnerable groups such as people with disability and elderly people. Subject to needs assessment, the project may also provide financial and organisational support to implementing partner to strengthen their capacity and ensure sustainability of the intervention. Disability mainstreaming NRC Protection Team has received training on inclusion and age, gender, and diversity approach and principles of disability inclusion will be mainstreamed across the project: awareness raising will be conducted on PwDs rights, barriers PwDs face in the society, and inclusion strategies; Participation: PwDs will be included across the all program cycle and the project will target PwDs; Comprehensive accessibility; and twin track approach, contributing to include PwDs in the project activities, while referring PwDs to specialized actors. In the past months, NRC has also built through the Gaza Protection Consortium a partnership with Humanity and Inclusion on provision of multi-rehabilitation therapy, psychosocial support, and medical assistive devices. Cases in need of these service will be referred to HI. Data will be disaggregate for gender, age, refugee status and disability. NRC’s intervention seeks, to directly address gender disparity and promote gender equality to ensure the do no harm principle is upheld. Through a gender mainstreaming approach, NRC promotes a concerted effort to ensure that gender equality is promoted in all stage of the programme cycle. NRC will be engaging directly with girls, boys, women, and men on identifying gender specific needs and considerations across all programming. Specific focus will be invested in deliberately targeting female beneficiaries, female-headed households, and women at risk. Gender sensitivity of this action is visible through: • Designing activities to equally benefit girls, boys, women, and men. A capacity building approach which seeks to equalize the power dynamics between genders and promotes gender equality safely without placing girls and women at higher risk of violence because of benefiting from these services. • Ensuring that we include mobile as well as static services to reach individuals, often women with caregiving responsibilities or people living with disabilities. • Hiring, minimum standard 50 per cent female staff, and ensure female staff presence at all project sites. • Staff training on PSEA mechanism. • Ensure staff are trained on GBV and identification of GBV cases and know how to conduct a referral to specialized GBV agencies.
