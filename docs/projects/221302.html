Project Summary

Between 0ctober 2019 May 2024, 11 children died in Njikwa Sub Division in the bushes as they went hunting. All these happened between 9am and 1pm – School Hours. These children should have been in school by the hour they died. Seven Njikwa teenages have been killed in Bamenda because they dropped out from school and were struggling to survive in town. This project proposal targets 2500 children who are currently in some form of community led learning centers handed over by IVSPD Organization that created them and continue funding them, 1500 children have not gone to school this academic year in Njikwa of age 2-14 including boys and girls for quality formal learning. This entails creating new accessible centers in neighbourhoods communities. Two Secondary School level centers created with 260 students need urgent support. The Project lays emphasis on 1000 adolescents and young boys/girls of 14-24 years training in vocational skills in quick income generating programs in Vocational Training Centers hosted in Community halls and public spaces. It considers 400 teenage/adolescent mothers as victims of the crisis to be trained and empowered in livelihood activities including market gardening and livestock rearing. 22 Child Protection and Social/Education Learning Centers improved upon and supplied with Learning kits/didactic materials and 150 Volunteers teachers/trainers recruited and trained in child protection in emergency tips. 9 Community Education Advisory Committees in emergency created in the villages and trained on community emergency response, child protection, safety and security tips. They will formulate cultural child protection policies, ensure quality and formal learning programme at various levels is implemented. Radio Education is considered with creation of listening groups, gargets and didactic materials distributed. Volunteer trainers motivated and children refreshed for 2 months with a school meal to boost enrolment. Giving vulnerability rate of young girls and boys, this proposal will consider GBV and SEA education and sensitize general public, WASH lessons, civic education and morality taught. Special centers created for the Muslim Fulani children to meet their specificity in 3 zones with needs supplied accordingly.
Given the importance of good hygiene practices in schools and community and the need for good drinking water in learning centers, the project will sensitize on hand wash, supply of 22 Learning Centers with WASH kits including 154 tapped drinking buckets/4150 drinking cups, 154 cartons of soap, provide 44 water taps, 22 latrines with 132 cubicles, 1000 young girls and women targeted for dignitary kits. As innovation, 1000 young boys are captured for dignity kits (including pants).
Given that education in emergency is at the heart of humanitarian action as a basic need of children caught in emergencies, it remains the fundamental right of children. The project will be of great relief to communities in Njikwa for obvious reasons; First, education provides immediate physical, psychosocial and cognitive protection in protracted crisis. Out-of-school children are more vulnerable and exposed to protection risks, eg. about 400 teenage mothers in 7 years, GBV, child labour and estimated 300 children connected to NSAG in Njikwa. Second, education is a major contributor to individual, community and societal resilience.
In our need assessment, education was prioritized by the communities, even in challenging humanitarian situations. In line with the principle of accountability, humanitarian responders should listen to the self-identified needs of the beneficiaries. Humanitarian responders are uniquely placed to meet education needs in crisis situations, with the agility and capacity to reach children that development actors cannot provide. Consideration to this project as top need will be appreciated. Lastly much ground work is done for the communities to accept the project by IVSPD

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The protracted crisis in NW-SW Regions of Cameroon has plagued the country with thousands of school dropout with rural communities most affected as no formal/regular education going on. Schools institutions and premises are either destroyed or deserted, and some hosting armed groups. Teachers and children remain targets for kidnapping for ransom while others are tortured and killed for going to school. As a consequence, rural communities have missed 7 school cycles with devastating impact on the children especially adolescents and young adults. Njikwa Sub Division is highly hit in all 9 villages where public and social services are shut down. Njikwa had been calm and at peace until the killing of the Police Commander at Police Post in Oshie on a matter that was totally unconnected with the crisis (eye witness confession) which provoked the burning down of Oshie and Ngwo Markets and other private homes on the May 4, 2018. Now in Njikwa there are no formal schools, limited healthcare services, no administrative services and timid markets occasionally shut down. The main and biggest market in the sub division which serves not just the sub division but also parts of Ngie, Widikum-Menka, and Akwaya Sub Divisions was burnt down. The biggest district hospital in Momo Division in Ngwo - Njikwa, 8 Health Centres including the District Hospital vandalized timidly functional with inadequate staffs and no Doctors. All the schools in Njikwa - 7 secondary/technical/SAR-SM and 28 primary are closed and teachers fled affecting over 7 000 children who dropped out of school and over 3000 deprived from the opportunity to start schooling. 2 secondary schools burnt down and one badly damaged and looted. 70 homes burnt down affecting 80 families directly who lost basic needs - shelters, clothing and bedding. 51 shops were burnt affecting 51 direct families as the only source of livelihood. About 1000 houses damaged and looted causing over 5000 people to desert them. The central administrative area is abandoned to the military with no other inhabitant. From our preliminary inquiries, about 400 school dropped out girls have been impregnated as teenage mothers excluding the over 500 cases of abortions. Some are now unwillingly married while a good number remain single teenage mothers. KIs information from Health Services reveals that HIV/AIDS is most prevalent in Njikwa than any other sub division in Momo. Involuntary promiscuity is at its peak with high rates of GBV and SEA. There is great need for serious sensitizations and advocacy on GBV and SEA in all 9 villages. Njikwa has no electricity, no communication media, irregular telephone network, and broken water systems with no repairs since all technicians fled. The common playing grounds for children had been the many large school fields which are all deserted and no go zones. There are no community recreational or friendly spaces and no social services for the children. The Fulani Muslim community in Njikwa is highly affected and threatened for their purported involvement in the crisis supporting the government that caused much pains and loses for the people. As a consequence, trust was lost and the Fulani are being chased away and have suffered a great loss of their livelihood - cattle and sheep and some have been killed in the course of the crisis. Their children timidly attend the few learning centres we established. The need to create theirs is an urgent necessity. From our findings, apart from Indigenous Volunteers for Sustainable Peace and Development that is more regular with humanitarian work in Njikwa, especially for school children other organizations activities have very temporal and mostly material supplies and data collections. Given these varying problems in the sub division, we hope that this project proposal if funded will bring some relief to the traumatizing population especially the children who are out of school for seven years.
