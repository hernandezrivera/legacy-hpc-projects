Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis, as a consequence of the prolonged military conflict in eastern Ukraine, is aggravated by the growing COVID-19 pandemic and its negative impact on civilian communities. Conflict-affected populations at NGCA face additional threats and a higher risk of death due to COVID-19.
Every day people face violations of human rights in the field of protection, and above all, the right to life. Residents of remote locations live in difficult conditions because they have limited access to health care and education, and also experience additional economic hardship due to the loss of jobs due to the closure of enterprises. This translates into further reductions in food, medicine and heating costs. A significant proportion of people in these settlements (about 30%, according to HADC's own monitoring) are over 60, and the majority are women. Also, the large number of households with the most vulnerable members (including 12% of households with at least one disabled member) increases the burden on social service providers. According to statistics, the vast majority of households with disabled members in the NGCA do not receive any kind of care (95%). Many of the most vulnerable older women and men affected by conflict are at risk of being excluded from the normal distribution of aid. They may face movement restrictions, disabilities or chronic illnesses that prevent them from receiving assistance in urban or rural centers. Older men and women often need individualized assistance and require different approaches, such as medical examinations and home care, the delivery of social benefits, food and medicine. As a result, the degree of protection at the individual and community levels is insufficient to cope with the risks posed by constantly emerging threats, and a significant proportion of the people on the NGCA have to rely on the support of humanitarian organizations.
The first project activity aims to help meet the health and social needs of the vulnerable population by increasing the mobility of rural health and social workers, which will be achieved by providing rural communities with electric bicycles.
The second project activity is aimed at improving the living standards of the vulnerable population through the provision of in-kind grants based on the individual needs of the beneficiaries. The composition of such grants may include: medical equipment, technical means of rehabilitation, basic necessities, etc. Providing one-off support in the form of such in-kind grants will reduce the long-term vulnerability of beneficiaries.

1 - Needs

About 1.9 million people in need of assistance live on NGCA, according to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Survey. The elderly make up almost a third (32%) of those in need of assistance - the highest rate in the world. Women make up more than half of people in need of help (56%), and many of them take care of their families themselves. The affected areas are also home to a large number of people with disabilities, accounting for 12% of those in need of assistance.According to official data from local social institutions, at the beginning of 2020, the rural population is about 107 thousand people. In order to receive qualified medical care, residents of remote and rural locations are forced to travel considerable distances to visit city and regional medical institutions. The pandemic has significantly increased the burden on health and social workers. The increased self-isolation regime has significantly increased the need for physical examinations and home care.The conflict in eastern Ukraine has a particular impact on older people. These people face serious difficulties in accessing basic services due to their disabilities and limited mobility, and are often separated from their families. They are also more vulnerable to abuse and neglect, have special health and nutritional needs, and are the most economically vulnerable. Available data indicate that more than half of the people who are food insecure are older people, and for 9 out of 10 older people on NGCA, pension is the main source of income. At the same time, this income is insufficient to meet basic needs, as a quarter of the pension is spent on medicines and more than half on food.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

As a result of the project activities, it is planned to implement the following activities and achieve the following results:1. Individual targeted non-monetary assistance (priority - persons with special needs)2. Increasing the mobility of medical and social workers in rural and remote locations.Through the implementation of the project activities, it is planned to increase the economic, physical and psychological well-being of both individuals and their families and communities, reduce their long-term vulnerability and develop long-term solutions. Targeting the most vulnerable people in hard-to-reach communities affected by conflict will have a major impact in terms of reducing negative coping mechanisms and supporting beneficiaries.Planned results of project activities:- the most vulnerable people affected by the conflict in remote and rural areas will get access to health and social services-provision of non-cash grants based on the individual needs of the beneficiaries will reduce their long-term vulnerability.During the implementation of the project activities, all activities will be carried out in compliance with the basic humanitarian standards of quality and accountability to the affected population. Actions and approaches will enable local communities to participate in key decision-making at all stages of program implementation: evaluation, analysis, planning, implementation, monitoring and revision through communicative approaches that involve transparent and ongoing two-way communication, through which information about risks, community needs and opportunities will be collected.

3 - Indicators & targets

As a result of project activities:1. 300 beneficiaries (180 women, 120 men) with special needs will receive targeted non-monetary assistance2. 45 rural administrations will receive 90 electric bicycles for use to increase the mobility of medical, social workers, as well as employees of post officesThe potential number of project beneficiaries will be 37,500 people.
