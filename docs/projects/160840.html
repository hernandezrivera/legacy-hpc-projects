Project Summary

IOM will provide Co-Coordination of Communication and Community Engagement Working Group, together with ACTED and INTERNEWS to support the humanitarian agenda by ensuring transparency in humanitarian decision-making towards a participation revolution; involving people receiving aid in decision-making. Communication and Community Engagement (CCE) will contribute to enhancing accountability to affected population (AAP) by ensuring that affected communities are adequately engaged and provided with timely and accurate information about how and why humanitarian aid is available to them, know and demand their rights from the humanitarian responders. This promotes transparency in the humanitarian environment by considering and involving the views of the affected people at every step of project development and implementation.
CCE activities serve to strengthen mainstreaming and inclusion of persons with disabilities; ensure men, women, boys and girls are fully informed of their rights and entitlements; enhance complaints, accountability, and feedback mechanisms; facilitate communications campaigns, mass communication systems, and widespread awareness raising sensitization including prevention of SEA; and enhance two-way communication systems through the promotion of a standardized platform where partners can obtain meaningful analysis on complaint and feedback systems across the humanitarian response.

1 - Needs

Community Engagement is a two-way dialogue between crisis-affected communities, humanitarian organizations, and within and between communities. It should enable affected people to meet their different needs, address their vulnerabilities and build on their pre -existing capacities. Community Engagement serves to improve the effective delivery of humanitarian assistance through improved two-way communication with disaster-affected populations. Engaging with affected communities ensures that accurate and timely information is shared with them, and that their feedback and participation are integral parts of humanitarian response. This is based on the principle that communities’ meaningful participation in humanitarian processes is essential and that information is a critical form of assistance. It is critical to respond to the need for both better access to information and for communities to have a stronger voice and better accountability in humanitarian response through mainstreaming community communications and engagement in humanitarian activities. These activities ensure that the most vulnerable communities (particularly women, children, elderly and socially excluded) have better access to reliable, accurate, timely and effective information so that they can:- make informed decisions about their own lives; - have accountability to services delivered by humanitarian actors; - be fully engaged in the decision-making process about the humanitarian response and service delivery; and- be engaged by the humanitarian agencies so that their concerns and issues are understood and, if needed, plans can be adapted by the implementing organizations.This is particularly pertinent in a context such as South Sudan, in which the media infrastructure is still in a nascent stage of development; a limited proportion of households have televisions, computers, or internet access, and print media has extremely limited distribution, whilst low literacy rates renders print media inaccessible to much of the population. This has led to radio constituting a key source of information in the country. However, localized conflict can still limit the capacity of humanitarian actors to communicate key messages to communities, and can also interfere with phone and radio networks. Though there are a number of CCE interventions ongoing in South Sudan, there is inadequate information sharing, which precludes the systematic identification of mutually reinforcing synergies among clusters and service providers in general, and sharing of best practices and lessons learned through a common platform. The expansion of the Communication and Community Engagement Working Group is therefore a critical intervention, for which support is required for strong coordination by agencies with significant experience and operational presence within this sphere.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

As a platform for information sharing and analysis, the CCE working group will work on the development of a standardized complaint and feedback mechanisms platform. Though meaningful access to feedback mechanisms communities and service providers will benefit from targeted programme adjustment, as well as ensuring higher standards of AAP and meaningful participation. Through technical support to all clusters, the CCE working group can promote and mainstream inclusive communication strategies. As a common platform for information sharing and standardized approaches to community engagement, the CCE working group can play a central role in ensuring a humanitarian response that is accountable and transparent. The CCE WG will work under the best practices in data protection and will ensure that members are compliant.
