Project Summary

WaSH project is in continuation to ensure inclusive and equitable provision of life saving water and sanitation services building on infrastructure already constructed in the previous phase in 2019 ( Camp no. 13, 14 and 15 and its nearby host community) and to strengthen Behaviour Change Communication (BCC) by encouraging community-based collective measures.
The provision of safe water, sanitation, hygienic living and environmental conditions are crucial especially during infectious disease outbreaks, like COVID-19. Personal hygiene such as hand hygiene is a key element in preventing such disease to spread. As of date, a number of aspects of COVID-19 prevention and control are still not clear, including implications related to WASH interventions (like virus persistence on surfaces or sewage).
The previous phase of the intervention has contributed to an increase in the appropriate usage of handwashing. Almost 79% of the respondents from the end project evaluation reported that handwashing points installed under the project were utilized for hygiene maintenance. In terms of safe and inclusive access to water, the water point accessibility for PWD, pregnant women, adolescent girls and aged persons has been enhanced. The increase in ease of accessibility for pregnant women adolescent girls, it improved from 24% to 83% (MSNA Report 2020). So, maintenance of these essential WaSH facilities and services are vital to reduce the risk of disease outbreak.
Bearing in mind the gravity of the issue, CAID will continue to ensure lifesaving water and sanitation services to the Rohingya and host communities in 2021. Focus will be provided to address the key challenges facing WaSH service such as congestion at water points, unequal distribution, inclusive access and protection concerns in bathing cubicles and latrines.

1 - Identified Needs

Dirty and dysfunctional latrines as well as insufficient sanitation facilities has been a common concern across all the camps. It was reported that dysfunctional sanitation facilities and an inability to repair those due to a lack of funds, resulted in open defecation practices (MSNA Report 2020). On the other hand, hygiene items had been delivered across all the camps, but not in enough quantities and sometimes only limited to in areas adjacent to the camps (Ibid). Data from the camps suggest lack of water was a general concern across all the camps as mentioned in MSNA report 2020. Only 54% of the Rohingya refugees have access to chlorinated water supply in the camp whereas, 63% of people reported having enough water in the host community (COVID 19 Response Plan, 2020). Meanwhile, COVID 19 Response Plan suggested to distribute soap and installation of additional water points in the camps to enable handwashing. At the same time, hygiene promotion activities focused on COVID-19 activities to strengthen awareness are being continued as a necessity. Only about 27% of Rohingya households reported often or always finding visible waste near their house, whereas 11% of households reported the same in host community (MSNA Report, 2020). Access to bathing facilities on the other hand as also been challenging. 61% of households reporting accessing bathing facilities at their home and the rest have access either to community-based bathing facility or in open tube well platform (MSNA Report, 2020).
