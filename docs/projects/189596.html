Project Summary

Initiative for Community Prosperity (ICP) will provide integrated life-saving Food Security and Livelihood assistance to the most vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and vulnerable host communities in Juba, Terekeka and Kajokeji counties in Central Equatoria State through emergency in-kind distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits, establishment of farmers groups and training on integrated farming practices, and equipping fisher forks groups with skills and knowledge on sustainable fishing practices. By providing crop, vegetable and fishing kits and training on improved farming and fishing methods, food insecure households are expected to produce and access food for home use and for sale, and diversify their diet. To ensure complementarity, project staff will participate in FSL assessments initiated by the FSL cluster and Ministry of Agriculture and Fisheries. Also, the staff will monitor crop disease and pest outbreaks and make referrals to FSL cluster and MAF in target locations. The project will mainstream protection in order to reduce risks and exposure to Gender Based Violence. Most importantly, the project will support the existing coping mechanisms of the local community and will build resilience towards other shocks that have direct effect (such as the inflation and flooding) through integrating the FSL response to NFIs, Wash, Nutrition, Education, and Health responses in target locations. Beneficiaries in this project will be linked to other Wash, NFIs, Shelter, Health, Protection and Education interventions currently happening in proposed location to holistically and comprehensively address most pressing needs facing them. The intervention will also be characterized by a focus on community engagement activities such as monitoring, awareness-raising and risk reduction achieved through a strong system of community leaders and chiefs whereby ICP will provide basic training and engage community members in specific techniques for contextualized approaches to strengthen an enabling and protective environment for beneficiaries. ICP will ensure activities are tailored to the specific gender and age of target population as detailed in the needs analysis section. ICP will also strengthen the capacity of farmers and fisher forks through thematic workshops and trainings as per the needs identified in each micro-community. Farmers groups (Trainer of Trainers) will be trained on integrated farming, disease and pest control methods in their demonstration gardens and community outreach. Topics on nutrition, food wastage, harvest management and storage will also feature prominently during the training. Fisher Fork Groups will be trained on sustainable fishing methods, preservation techniques, and hygienic ways to handle and package fishing, value addition and fish marketing. The project will directly support 79725 (48608 female) individuals in the three counties.

1 - Needs

According to South Sudan’s Humanitarian Needs Overview Report (2022), an estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity at the peak of the 2022 lean season (May – July). This represents a 7 per cent increase from the 7.7 million people in need in 2021. In 2022, an estimated 2 million people in South Sudan will be at risk of acute malnutrition, including 302,078 children (148,018 boys and 154,060 girls) with SAM, 1,039,214 children (509,215 boys and 529,999 girls) with MAM, and 675,548 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition (AM-PLW). In addition, 50,638 (15.4 per cent) out of a total of 328,610 refugees in South Sudan will need treatment for acute malnutrition, including 8,388 SAM children, 26,092 MAM children and 16,158 AM-PLW Food insecurity remained a major concern in Juba, Kajokeji and Terekeka Counties in the last half of 2021. According to Integrated Phase Classification Report (May-July 2021), Juba was classified as crisis (IPC3) while Kajokeji and Terekeka were classified as Emergency (IPC4) and Crisis (IPC3) food insecure or worse respectively. This meant that majority of households in crisis and emergency phases relied on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic needs. In the first six months of 2022, Juba and Kajokeji are expected to remain in crisis (IPC3) food insecure or worse while Terekeka will slide to emergency (IPC4) food insecure or worse. According to Humanitarian Needs Overview Report ( HNO,2022), Juba will have 234,900 individuals in great need of humanitarian assistance out of which 78,400 and 52300 persons respectively are expected to face extreme and catastrophic needs. In the same period, Kajokeji will have 171,600 persons in need of humanitarian assistance, of which 34,900 and 23,300 will face extreme and catastrophic needs. On the other hand, Terekeka County which is expected to slide to emergency (IPC4) will have 175,800 persons in dire need, out of which 38,800 and 25800 are expected to face extreme and catastrophic needs. Drivers of food insecurity in these counties include conflict that has disrupted farming and trading activities. During the latest conflict in Terekeka County, food crops were burnt down, livestock stolen or killed and households lost basic assets. Massive flooding that occurred in the third quarter of 2021 only escalated the level of needs affected population in Terekeka and Juba were already experiencing. Beyond violence and flooding, other drivers of food insecurity include economic shocks associated with COVID-19, lower oil revenue, deficit crop production, persistently poor macroeconomic conditions, and the protracted political conflict. Due to Covid19 restrictions, domestic trade flows and business activities have not recovered since the first case of Covid 19 was reported in Juba and neighbouring countries. Depressed casual labour and petty trade has had negative impact on household income and by extension the ability of the household to access food. Covid-19 preventative measures have continued to slow down the ability of traders and humanitarian agencies to deliver much needed food and non-food items in markets and to affected persons. Flooding and conflict has also affected distribution of in-kind food, livestock vaccination, livestock treatment and agricultural and fishing inputs thus negatively impacting household’s capacity to produce food. Taken together, these factors have escalated food insecurity and worsened nutrition situation of families forcing households to experience either large food consumption gaps or engage in severe coping strategies in an attempt to mitigate food consumption gaps.

2 - Project activities

OUTPUT 1.1: 79,725 beneficiaries comprised of 15000 Host Community, 49725 Internally Displaced and 15000 Returnees Households provided with emergency vegetable and crop kits and training. ACTIVITY 1.1.1: Conduct community communication and sensitization meetings on the FSL project with local authorities, community leaders and members to provide project information and the role of the community in the implementation process. ACTIVITY 1.1.2: Community Mobilization and selection of beneficiaries with the participation of community members, leaders, and local authorities, and beneficiary’s representatives. ACTIVITY 1.1.3: Prepositioning of vegetable, crop and fishing kits items from UNFAO core pipeline .ACTIVITY 1.1.4: Conduct distribution of vegetable and crop to 79725 (48608 female) using UNFAO criteria in target counties .ACTIVITY 1.1.5: Formation and training of 55 vegetable and crop groups, each composed of 25 individuals. 25 Demonstration sites to be established in schools and nutrition sites. Topics to include; establishing sunken and raised beds, nursery management, planting methods (row planting and number of seedlings per row), seedling care management, thin/transplanting, mulching, soil and water conservation, integrated pest and disease management, and harvesting and harvest preservation. ACTIVITY 1.1.6: Conduct joint field monitoring visits to farmers and farmers groups with project staffs, community members, local authorities and officials from MAF. Joint monitoring meant to obtain information on project progress, challenges faced and to seek continued participation and support from target communities. ACTIVITY 1.1.7: Conduct farming stakeholder’s forum to obtain information on challenges faced by farmers. ACTIVITY 1.1.8: Conduct PDMs on crop and vegetable kits distribution with 15% beneficiaries randomly selected to ascertain use, appropriateness and benefits of items distributed. ACTIVITY 1.1.9: Conduct a joint Post Harvest Survey with farmers, Officials from MAF and local authorities to obtain data on post-harvest food harvest, losses and waste. Report to be shared with farmers, local authorities, and officials from MAF during stakeholder’s forum in target locations. ACTIVITY 1.1.10: Conduct environment education and awareness targeting farmers and fisher fork groups to mitigate effects of Global Warming. OUTPUT 2.1: 33000 beneficiaries comprised of 6000 Host Community, 20000 Internally Displaced and 7000 Returnees Households provided with emergency fishing Kits and Training. ACTIVITY 2.1.1: Community Mobilization, Registration, verification and selection of beneficiaries with the participation of existing fisher forks, community leaders, local authorities, and beneficiary’s representatives. ACTIVITY 2.1.2: Conduct distribution of fishing kits to 33000 beneficiaries in target counties using UNFAO criteria. ACTIVITY 2.1.3 Formation and training of 33 Fisher Folks Groups each composed of 15 members. Training topics to include benefits of fish diet, fish preservation, sustainable fishing, net making, fish packaging, fish handling and marketing. A total of 6600 individuals comprising 4200 males and 2400 females to be trained through the groups: ACTIVITY 2.1.4: Conduct PDM on fishing kits with 15% of fisher forks randomly selected among beneficiaries to ascertain use, appropriateness and benefits of items distributed. ACTIVITY 2.1.5: Conduct joint Post-Harvest Assessment to obtain data on post-harvest losses and waste. Report to be shared with fishermen, MAF, local authorities during stakeholder’s forum. ACTIVITY 2.1.7: Conduct joint field monitoring visits for accountability purposes to fishermen and fisher fork groups with project staffs, community members, local authorities and officials from the ministry of agriculture and fisheries. Joint monitoring meant to obtain information on project progress, challenges faced and to seek continued participation and support from target communities.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

ICP is committed to the protection of human rights, therefore it has established mechanisms to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). ICP is dedicated to upholding IASC 6 core principles on PSEA and will integrate PSEA sessions in all its activities, ICP will have a full time Gender Officer tasked with creating awareness on PSEA, training staff and beneficiaries on PSEA during project implementation. The Gender officer will provide information about PSEA during inception meetings with community leaders, IDPs and host community representative, during focus group discussions and meetings with women and girls, and during selection of beneficiaries and on Women Girl Friendly Spaces. Also, the Gender Officer will ensure PSEA messaging through banners, T-shirts and brochures during project activities. The Gender Officer will hold biweekly meetings with women and girls representatives to gauge their perception on protection concerns as well as seek their views on how best to safeguard their protection. ICP gender officer will also play a prominent role through existing referral pathways in Aweil South and North where she will liaise with partners to make appropriate referrals of SGBV cases.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Information about the project (aim of the project, its objectives, targeted beneficiaries, project duration, project donors, and the kind of assistance and beneficiaries selection criteria) will be provided to community members, leaders and local authorities during inception meetings, review meetings and end of project stakeholders meeting. Project reports including progress reports and end of project reports will be shared with community representatives, FSL cluster at State and National level and with the community leaders in target Counties. Information on feedback mechanism will be provided by ICP’s Gender Officer during meetings, training, and selection of beneficiaries. The Gender Officer will conduct sensitization and awareness about the type of feedback that beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries can submit and avenues of receiving feedback. As far as complaint mechanism, ICP will distribute a toll free number which will be under custody of Gender Officer. The number will also be displayed prominently at meeting and training venues as well as at distribution points. Complaint and Feedback desks will also be availed during selection of beneficiaries.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

ICP will create forums where project staff, its Gender and Protection Officer will conduct Focus Group Discussions, individuals and group meetings to obtain information from beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries on their perceptions regarding project implementation. In instances where a member of a family is disabled, ICP will conduct personalized meetings so that the individual or their caregivers can be able to communicate their preferences, submit complaints and received feedback. Also, ICP will seek to engage women in leadership positions so that their voices, concerns and preferences can be taken into account right from project planning, project implementation, project planning and evaluation. Further, Complaint and Feedback Data will be disaggregated by gender and age in order to measure the level of participation of females and those living in disability in the mechanism.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

ICP employs evidence-based programming in all its projects which will apply to this project through constant information sharing and feedback from the community. Project feedback and suggestions from the community will be gathered through safety audits, focus group discussions, meetings, suggestion boxes and face to face meetings with beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries. The information gathered will be used to improve project implementation. The feedback/information gathered will also be used in advocacy for further actions from relevant parties in situation where ICP is unable to respond to the needs. Positive feedback will form part of documentation of the project success stories.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Majority of vulnerable people in South Sudan are women and children. More so, women face more risks than men and boys in the current emergency setting. Negative gendered norms, safety concerns and domestic chores can exclude women and girls from accessing humanitarian assistance. To ensure equal participation of women and girls in the project, project consultations and planning meetings will be conducted in locations that women and girls can easily and safely access and during time of day they can attend. Women and Girls will be engaged in project monitoring to embed their views and concerns in project activities. To mitigate occurrence of GBV, ICP will deploy a protection officer who will provide guidance to project staffs on existing and potential GBV risks to ensure activities do not pose additional risks to women and girls. Women and Girls will be provided with information on how to access and utilize the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism in each of the targeted project sites. All complaints received on the project will be safely, impartially and swiftly acted on with complainants being provided with feedback within the shortest time possible. Prior to commencing project activities, a safety audit will be conducted in each target location to map existing or potential protection concerns posed to beneficiaries, project staff and project supplies. The Safety Audit report will be utilized by project staff, community leaders and members in locations where women and girl friendly spaces will be established to ensure safety and access for girls and women.
