Project Summary

The project seek to provide emergency education support to children in Banadir, Hiran and Lower Shabelle region. The project proposes to support 15 schools (8 IDPs schools in Mogadishu Banadir and 3 schools in Lower Shabelle and 4 schools in Beledweyne Hiran Region) that will actualize the provision of basic education to 2,100 children (1,235 girls and 865 boys) in a conducive, protective learning environment.
The proposed project will target IDPs in Kadha District in Banadir region. This emergency support project to education through interventions in improved school WASH, learning space, education materials and motivated staff is focused on creating a conducive, safe learning environment especially for the girl child. Water tanks will be constructed to reserve clean water for drinking and washing hands. School Education Committee members and teachers will be trained on psychological support and positive discipline and how to be sensitive and supportive to Gender specific needs.

Needs

Conflict and collapse of education system, a high number of children have been left with no access to education across Somalia and for those who do access it, retention and continuation is a challenge as institutional education capacity remains weak with no trained teachers in service and a severe lack of learning facilities (HNO, 2019). In a recent assessment report indicate the dropout rate of host community is around 30% and IDPs around 17%. These national figures hide significant worse regional level variations (HNO, 2019). The recent flood in Beletweyne and Lower Shabelle region destroyed infrastructures such as schools leaving vulnerable children without safe and protected learning spaces (OCHA, May 2018). The attendance rate of girls is consistently lower across age-groups and types of school. Household inability to pay school fees has been identified as the reason for non-attendance at school, for both girls (72%) and boys (69%) in Banadir. According to the joint Multi-Cluster assessment report 2017, 9% of the school-aged children in the assessed households were reportedly attending school at the time of assessment in most regions such as Banadir, Hiran and Lower Shabelle. The attendance rate of girls is consistently lower across age-groups and types of school. Household inability to pay school fees has been identified as the reason for non-attendance at school, for both girls-72% and boys-69% in Banadir. According to Education cluster HNO 2019, those suffering the greatest educational inequities are IDPs, females, nomadic/pastoralist and rural communities. WARDI will give priorities to children from IDPs and food insecure households as geared towards access to learning and retention in a safe and protected environment. The response will meet the needs of vulnerable girls, women, boys and men IDPs and host communities. The project proposes to support 15 schools (8 IDPs schools in Banadir and 3 schools in Lower Shabelle and 4 schools in Hiran Region).

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Target locations of the project is safe and no major security concerns has been reported in the last one year though security dynamics of Somalia especially on clan conflict and attack from militia is not predictable. The project is aligned with National Government strategic objectives with a lasting solution in the education development. The education system to be used in the schools will be in line with the Ministry of Education strategic plan. The project proposes to work with the community in identifying lasting solution and management of the schools. Training of Community Education Committees will enable the communities manage the schools. Temporary learning facilities will be handed over to the community at the end of the project with all the infrastructure used during the project. The community will be mobilized before the project and all the beneficiaries will be informed of their entitlement and services they will receive. Contract agreement with the donor as well as local administration engagement will outline all the services and entitlement the target beneficiaries will receive. WARDI will hold at the inception of the project, community mobilization, and sensitization meetings with all stakeholders including the local authorities to officially launch the project. During the sensitization meetings, the project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement and roles and duties of each stakeholder including M&E roles will be discussed and agreed. WARDI will develop elaborate beneficiary complain and feedback tool, project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be given 1 day workshop on how to use the complaint /feedback system to enhance transparency and accountability. Community ownership of the project and keeping the sustainability of the Education infrastruture after the face out of the project will be done through formation of structures such as Beneficiary Representatives, CEC Committees, and Community Hygiene Promoters with equal share of men and women. In order to meet the Education needs of the affected community, WARDI will involve the target community through its leaders and engendered committees in the first stage of planning of the project to ensure community participation. The organization will establish engendered committees (women participation will be strictly adhered) that will have hand-in-hand working relations with the project team and beneficiary communities; both sides will exchange any information related to the management and implementation of the project activities. Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will also be used to make sure that the beneficiaries are satisfied with services provided and complaints can be channeled to the right direction. WARDI will adhere the principles of "do no Harm" through not creating any partial, nepotism, conflict oriented and sensitive issues within the project beneficiaries.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

IDPs are more vulnerable to education inclusion since they are mostly affected by the environment they live in. Insecurity and conflict due to insurgency and clan conflict is now one of the main causes for displacement. Drought, floods and cyclone are also the root cause of the displacement in Somalia. Somalia faces a dire humanitarian situation, having endured drought and famine as well as continued fighting and heavy rains, all of which have aggravated the conditions of IDPs. People denied the rights of the IDPs as human being and they are facing evictions, rape and looting due to the fragility of the central government who was under their responsibility to protect them. Most of the IDPs left behind their livelihood assets and displaced to the big towns with empty hands. Thus, the IDPs have the right to receive necessary service, such as protection, food security, shelter, education, WASH, health and nutrition services in order to save their lives. Unfortunately, conflict sometimes is preventing humanitarian agencies from delivering assistance to the IDPs. In this project, WARDI will address protection concerns and supporting education retention activities for children already in schools e.g. through provision of safe drinking water in schools, provision of emergency school feeding, hygiene promotion in schools, school supplies and construction of temporary learning spaces among other related interventions is thus vital in ensuring retention of learners. WARDI will also promote inclusiveness in project management where 50% of the project committees will be from IDPs more so women IDPs. Construction of temporary learning spaces will target IDPs camps in the three regions.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The overall goal of the proposed project is to enable vulnerable schools aged children and youth with immediate access of learning opportunities, support learners retention, improvement of learning environment and strengthening and improvement of quality of effective service delivery. Negative attitudes of parents, especially mothers, toward girls’ education is one of the barriers to girl child education in South-Central Somalia that negatively impacts on access and retention of the girls in school. WARDI will conduct awareness sensitization of parents especially mothers through mother forums or school mother clubs where they can be engaged on ways to retain girls in schools and how to reach more mothers and the general public on the importance of girl child education, and the use of mass media for awareness creation on the significance of girls’ education as well as activities that promote awareness creation on the importance of girl child education. WARDI will promote gender sensitive learning environment in school will be promoted to support education needs for both boys and girls such as separate gender sensitive toilets from boys and girls. This project is totally addresses the needs of water and latrines to the affected men, women, girls and boys in the three target regions. Provision of safe drinking water in schools will reduce the girls walk long distance for searching safe and reliable water (women and girls are responsible for fetching water to their households) and for searching place for their latrine needs; this will reduce the exposure of women and girls to SGBV, while providing more time for livelihood activities for women and give spaces for girls education. In this project women, men, girls and children will give equal opportunity to take part the project design before its implementation and also to be equally heard their voices. Women, men and marginalized communities will equally share the members of the CEC committees.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

As far as protection issues are concerned, the schools serve as protective spaces for both girls and boys, hence with the school dropout mainly affecting girls also means that they are more exposed to risk of attacks as they move from one place to another in search for water and food for themselves and their families. Interventions that focus on retention of both girls and boys will not only ensure continued learning but also their safety and security within the school environment. The target location is within IDP centers thus it faces armed conflict and violence. Temporary learning spaces will be set up in an area where the land has been provided by the community to avoid destruction. The community will be mobilized to safe guard the school learning environment from external attack by reporting security incidences to the security personnel within the area. The community will also be mobilized to safe guard the girl child from violence. Effective school committees composed of parents, teachers, school managers and religious leaders to be established. The committee should lead in administration of schools, discipline of students and prevention of violence in schools. This will ensure that schools are violence-free and that they provide a safe learning environment for girls. WARDI will identify the risk of conflict in the project area and immediately avert this risk. WARDI will closely engage the local leaders, government officials and the community to sort out any possible threat and the community prominent members as cross-cutting will be trained on Disaster Risk Reduction and conflict resolution mechanism measures. WARDI is currently implementing WASH, food security, Education, Nutrition and Health projects in Hiran, Banadir and L/Shabelle regions without any security problem, although sometimes there is security issues in L/Shabelle region. Safety and security for the project staff and beneficiaries are our top priority.
