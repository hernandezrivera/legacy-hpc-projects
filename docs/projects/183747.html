Project Summary

Through this proposed action, REACH (a joint initiative of ACTED, IMPACT Initiatives and UNOSAT) will support the coordination and technical implementation of two initiatives: a Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA) and a Joint Market Monitoring initiative, to provide evidence on vulnerabilities and market dynamics throughout the West Bank and Gaza.
The MSNA will consist of a multi-sectoral household survey employed among a representative sample of the affected population in both the West Bank and Gaza. The MSNA will be conducted in partnership with OCHA oPt and within the framework of the wider development and humanitarian architecture. As in 2021, household data for the MSNA will be collected in partnership with the Palestinian Central Bureau for Statistics (PCBS). Central to this collaborative data partnership are strategies and protection measures, including specific channels to ensure accountability to affected populations, the most vital of which is a dedicated strategy and platform to ensure complaint mechanisms are accessible, properly communicated, and relevant to all population groups according to CBCM SOPs.
The proposed MSNA and JMMI will aim to achieve the following objectives:
1. Directly inform joint humanitarian-development response planning based on the nexus approach that the United Nations Country Team (UNCT) has adopted beginning in 2022, through the provision of comprehensive, multi-sectoral household data as well as monitoring and reporting of market prices and availability dynamics. Data from both the MSNA and JMMI will therefore aim to inform the development of both the 2023 HNO/HRP and further support the UN Sustainable Development Cooperation Framework planned for 2023-27;
2. Provide a detailed inter-sectoral analysis of the magnitude and severity of humanitarian needs among crisis-affected populations in oPt, to support the calculation of sectoral and overall People in Need (PiN) and severity calculations.
3. Provide a detailed vulnerability analysis to identify at-risk and ‘furthest left behind’ population groups, as well as geographic areas with the most acute needs and vulnerabilities, to subsequently inform assistance targeting and response planning efforts;
4. Support inclusive and people-centred assistance provision based on the principles of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP), through the collection of information regarding preferred communication, information sharing, and assistance modalities.
5. To improve access to timely and accurate regional and nationwide data through the harmonized joint market monitoring framework that will provide evolutions in prices, market functionality/access, and household expenditure to inform cash transfer programming, development programmes and identify contextual factors (both historically and on a monthly basis) that might affect market dynamics to support cash-based interventions for programmatic actors.
REACH will produce the following key outputs:
1 clean, anonymized multi-sectoral needs household dataset;
1 static summary findings dashboard; series of presentations to key stakeholders;
1 package of six sectoral factsheets;
1 final analytical report (MSNA Bulletin)
1 Interactive/dynamic Market Monitoring dashboard
2 Static infographics on Survival Minimum Expenditure Basket (SMEB)/Cash Transfer Value (CTV) and price indexes
2 Static infographics on market functionality and goods availability.
1 final JMMI report/findings summary

1 - Needs

The situation in occupied Palestinian territory (oPt) is characterized as a protracted protection crisis. According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 2.45 million Palestinians out of a total population of 5.2 million were estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance, 60% of whom were classified as being in severe need; both figures that have increased dramatically since the 2020 HNO by approximately 250,000 individuals and 17%, respectively. The population faces multi-faceted and compounding humanitarian and protection challenges, resulting from “more than 50 years of Israeli occupation, insufficient respect for international humanitarian and human rights law, internal Palestinian political divisions, and recurrent escalations of hostilities between Israel Security Forces and Palestinian armed groups.” These factors put vulnerable Palestinian households at continual risk of conflict and violence, displacement, food insecurity, and poverty, requiring a robust humanitarian response to aid those in need. However, continued low levels of funding for humanitarian action continues to strain the availability and provision of critical services and assistance. In addition to the humanitarian context, Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza are also facing a number of socioeconomic and environmental challenges, as well as a marked escalation of violence beginning in April 2021. The Government of Palestine, Palestinian civil society, and international organisations are thus working towards achieving sustainable development targets in the midst of ongoing insecurity and occupation. As such, and as outlined in the 2020 Atlas of Sustainable Development, addressing immediate humanitarian needs in oPt must also be complemented by efforts to build resilient communities through bolstering social justice and rule of law, tackling climate change, and eradicating poverty. Furthermore, the global COVID-19 pandemic has continued to exacerbate humanitarian needs and pre-existing vulnerabilities among the Palestinian population and poses an additional risk to sustainable development. Finally, the aforementioned increase in widespread violence beginning in April 2021 across oPt – most acutely in the Gaza strip due to an escalation in – has further increased the risk placed on Palestinian households in terms of vulnerabilities. Although the presence of acute humanitarian needs is evident, significant information gaps exist due to the lack of a comprehensive inter-sectoral needs analysis to inform the oPt response. This is particularly vital as various response actors seek to identify cash-based programmatic opportunities for more targeted, flexible, and impactful programming, indicated by a recently completed nine-month multi-purpose cash assistance pilot in Gaza. While oPt is not a context with significant monetary instability, monitoring the availability of and access to markets as well as household expenditure (and associated constraints) is necessary as the response considers cash and voucher assistance as a primary modality for a holistic approach to both SMEB and CTV calculations. Furthermore, granular household data to inform development assistance planning and to support the monitoring of progress against global SDG indicators is also needed. To address these gaps, REACH proposes to conduct an MSNA and JMMI within the framework of the humanitarian-development nexus. The two complimentary assessments will be conducted in close partnership throughout 2022 with UN OCHA, the Cash Working Group (CWG), and other relevant humanitarian-development actors through the mandate of the UNCT/Humanitarian Country Team (HCT). The REACH Initiative was formed in 2010 as a joint initiative of ACTED, its sister organization IMPACT Initiatives (Geneva-based think-and-do-tank), and UNOSAT in order to promote and facilitate the development of information products that enhance the humanitarian community’s decision-making and planning capacity. IMPACT is programmatic lead of the initiative, in charge of all strategic and programmatic oversight, the HR management of REACH international staff as well as of dissemination and external relations at the global level for REACH. As sister organizations and global partners, IMPACT and ACTED coordinate on field implementation. In all field operations of IMPACT/REACH programs, ACTED provides operational support (country registration, office, accommodation, national HR recruitment, security, and complaint response mechanisms). This global partnership is formalized in a global MoU and project specific agreements. The protracted protection crisis in oPt resulting from decades of occupation, combined with traditional patriarchal norms and legal framework, have led to gender inequality. It is estimated that at least 37% of women are or have been victims of GBV, with particularly high occurrences among vulnerable communities such as internally displaced persons (IDPs) (Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020). Moreover, the COVID-19 crisis is disproportionately affecting vulnerable groups and deepening pre-existing vulnerabilities, especially for women, the disabled and the elderly. As such, the proposed MSNA will ensure to focus on these differing needs as part of the assessment. Furthermore, MSNA vulnerability analysis will be disaggregated by gender and age of the head of household. Finally, based on the contextual realities outline above and to inform the wider response community, these gender-sensitivities are deeply considered during the design and operationalization of the proposed activities below, particularly as related to sensitive information.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 – Assessment Coordination: REACH will seek to conduct a second MSNA in 2022 under the mandate of the UNCT, the HCT and further endorsement of the MSNA TORs through the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG) and the UN Data Group (where decision-making on technical matters will take place). An MSNA Technical Working Group will also be established to support coordination with MSNA partners, to include the PCBS, who will be contracted for the data collection exercise, as well as Clusters, UN agencies, and international/local NGOs, and agencies. REACH will engage closely with partners to develop key MSNA sectoral indicators and ensure that gender, age, and disability-specific considerations are integrated in the design and planning of the MSNA, including complaint response mechanisms. This process will begin with the review of established indicator frameworks, forming the basis of the MSNA analysis plan and the household survey tool. Data collection will be coordinated by IMPACT with PCBS; this includes ensuring PSEA standards and procedures are mainstreamed. REACH will also work closely with the Gaza CWG, clusters, donors, and implementers to ensure close alignment of the design and production of all JMMI deliverables. Considering existing data sets and REACH’s existing partnership for the MSNA, REACH will engage closely with PCBS to ensure datasets are integrated into JMMI deliverables; PCBS’ baseline economic data will be the primary data source (historical, current, and future) for market monitoring. Activity 2 – Technical Implementation: For the MSNA, REACH will lead the research design, including the sampling strategy and data collection methodology, as well as development of the assessment indicators, household survey tool, and analysis plan. REACH will also lead the collaborative design of a harmonized JMMI dashboard with actors with cash-based interventions as a priority, including the development of an JMMI-specific tool/framework to analyse, visualize, and support cash-based strategy, programmes, and operations The JMMI outputs will leverage market pricing, availability, access, and supply as well as household expenditure, to support coherent, evidence-based calculations for SMEB and standard CTV. For both the MSNA and JMMI, REACH will partner with PCBS to conduct the household data collection for the MSNA as well as provide relevant economic data as a part of their current data collection portfolio. For the MSNA, a random sample of households will be drawn using probability-based techniques, based on the sample frame from the 2021 MSNA. The sample will be stratified at the governorate level, with the aim of producing findings that are statistically generalizable with a 95% confidence level and 5% margin of error per strata. Additional stratifications and/or an additional sample of households will then be drawn in priority communities or areas where more granular findings are of interest. Approximately 7,800 households are anticipated to be interviewed, only after expressed informed consent, right to refuse participation, and channels to submit complaints are explicitly clarified to each participant (for which IMPACT and PCBS will dedicate staff trained in PSEA to take responsibility for). MSNA findings will be aggregated and presented at both the areas demarcated by the Oslo Accords and the crisis level. Data collection will take the form of one of three scenarios – face-to-face, phone/remote, or a mixture of the two –depending on security, access, and public health considerations (particularly related to COVID-19 for which REACH has global SoPs). Joint analysis of the MSNA data is anticipated to be collaborative, bringing together key humanitarian and development stakeholders to analyse urgent humanitarian needs alongside vulnerability, risk, and resilience with long-term, developmental priorities as primary consideration. REACH intends to integrate JMMI analysis in support of SMEB and CTV to better analyse and understand MSNA data and identify opportunities for the response to scale-up and/or adjust cash-based programming. Furthermore, the holistic process will support the nexus approach to response planning to provide data analysis for actors outside of the traditional humanitarian sphere; as of late 2021 REACH is currently in an inception phase with the UNSCO to develop a framework for reanalysing MSNA data from 2021 to support nexus strategy and programming in oPt whilst also ensuring that humanitarian programming under a nexus framework remains guided by needs-based approaches. Activity 3 – Output production: REACH will support OCHA in conducting joint inter-sectoral analysis specifically for the HNO, using the MSNA data to inform calculation of the overall inter-sectoral PiN, in line with the Joint Intersectoral Analysis Framework (JIAF) or other calculation models that may be utilised. The MSNA data will be used to produce the following information products: an anonymized household dataset; a static dashboard of Cluster-selected; presentations to key response stakeholders; sectoral factsheets with key MSNA findings, including composite indicators such as Living Standards Gap and household Capacity Gap; Final report providing detailed analysis of needs and themes relevant to oPt. For the JMMI, an initial inception phase will be required to understand how best to collate and analyse PCBS economic data for the purposes of cash-based programmatic strategy, as well as the development of a dashboard with monthly data updates. Four static infographics will also be produced, providing six-month benchmarks on relevant market trends in oPt; two infographics will provide insight on SMEB/CTV size and price indexes, while two will visualize market functionality, access, and the availability of staple goods/commodities. Finally, IMPACT will produce a final JMMI findings report, reflecting on and analysing the data throughout the year.

Accountability to Affected Populations
9. - Feedback and complaints mechanisms

Yes

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be firstly ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), implemented in all ACTED countries of intervention, including Palestine, as well as PCBS’ complaint hotline/platform. CRM for the MSNA will be coordinated between ACTED’s AME Unit and PCBS, the former of which is independent from the delivery of the activities. Partner entities, such as PCBS, supporting data collection will be consulted with on CRM SoPs in order to ensure ACTED’s CRM standards are integrated and that during data collection, information is shared with respondents and encourage respondents to report any issues or concerns during the MSNA’s data collection process. Several channels will be available to respondents to file a complaint or raises concerns: throughthe data collection team (who will then refer respondents to ACTED) and ACTED’s dedicated phone line open 24/7 (incl. via SMS/WhatsApp), dedicated email address, complaints box (within ACTED offices). All MSNA respondents will be explained their right to provide complaints in confidentiality. It is worth stressing that the CRM relies on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, complaint tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanism, as well as mandatory timely feedback (within 15 days) to the complainants. This CRM will enable ACTED and PCBS to identify and solve any issues reported by beneficiaries in a reasonably rapid period of time.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

Several procedures and guidelines for CRM are in place to ensure accessibility for all population groups. Informed consent will be a central principle guiding the data collection process, which specifies that participation may be withdrawn at any time; at this stage, an overview of access to CRM channels will be articulated. Furthermore, data collection teams (including REACH and partner enumerators) will participate in CRM awareness modules as part of the MSNA data collection training. Finally, as indicated above, the CRM approach includes multiple modalities for complaints to be submitted based on respondents’ ability and access to these modalities. Finally, CRM information will be provided to the HH again at the conclusion of the survey, including all relevant telephone/WhatsApp numbers and addresses to submit written complaints.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

1. Informed consent will be a central principle guiding the data collection process – enumerators will inform survey respondents of the purpose and objectives of the assessment and confirm their willingness to participate before commencing the interview. Enumerators will also inform respondents that they can retract their consent to participate at any time during the interview. 2. Phone numbers of respondents will only be collected if the respondent consents to providing it; phone numbers will only be kept on file (for participation in future assessments) if the respondent consents to this.3. Enumerators will only interview adult respondents (18+ years in age). 4. Data collection teams (including REACH and partner enumerators) will participate in PSEA and AAP awareness modules as part of the MSNA data collection training prior to commencement of data collection. 5. Mixed sex data collection teams will ensure that female respondents may be interviewed by female enumerators, as requested or required. 6. Assessment indicators and the corresponding household survey questionnaire will be designed to be culturally appropriate and sensitive to social norms and values as well as context, particularly related to protection (SGBV, child protection), mine action, and WASH practices. 7. All personal identifiers (e.g. village name, GPS coordinates, phone numbers, etc.) will be removed prior to sharing/publishing data sets with any external individual or entity.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Data and analysis from the MSNA is intended to provide humanitarian actors with the necessary evidence base to perform impartial, needs-based targeting, and in in turn ensure that the most vulnerable groups and/or groups with the most acute needs are prioritized for assistance. In terms of MSNA design, this includes collaborating with specific clusters to ensure indicators, questions, and responses are not only aligned with specific vulnerabilities but may also allow for disaggregated findings based on gender, persons with disabilities, and age.
