Project Summary

Provision of full WASH services in Hassansham U2 IDP camp. Camp capacity is 1560 tents and 9360 individuals. Camp is currently holding 4,250 individuals that are expected to remain in the camp for at least 4 months. Most of them mainly displacements for from Ninewa governorate: The IDPs are in need of essential WASH services that include provision of an average of 35 liters of safe water per person per day, currently done through water trucking; daily solid waste removal from the camp; facilitation of safe access to sanitation through regular monitoring and maintenance of 390 latrines and 390 showers; and 58 of these latrines are designed for and accessible to persons with disability (29 for males and 29 for females) and routine hygiene promotion through sharing key awareness messages, provision of hygiene kits for the most vulnerable households and maintaining the functionality of 400 hand washing facilities.

Needs

Due to conflict in Iraq and neighboring countries, there are 13 million crisis affected people within Iraq, 10 million of which are in need of assistance. There is an estimated 250000 refugees and About 3.4 million people have been displaced in Iraq. An estimated 350000 of the internally displaced persons (IDPs) were hosted in several camps throughout Iraq. Most of the IDs were fleeing form the fighting in Mosul and the surrounding districts in Ninewa GovernorateHassansham U2 Camp is one of the Camps that is hosting IDPs. Hassansham Village is located in Hamdaniya district in Ninewa governorate. The Hassansham Village was ravaged by the war and is completed deserted. There are three other camps are located in the same area. Two of the camps were closed in December 2017 and the population were consolidated into the remaining camps. It is operated under the overall coordination of UNHCR. The other camp are under the authority of MODM World Vision, in collaboration with the Iraq WASH Cluster and UNICEF, is the WASH service provider for Hassansham U2 Camp. World Vision installed some water and sanitation facilities during the initial phases of Hassansham U2 camp Using internal private funding resources, (January through to March, 2017). The camp has WASH facilities installed for an expected full capacity 1,560 households (9,360 individuals). As from May 2017 to September 2018 WV has been providing WASH services in the camp through support from OFDA. The funding agreement with OFDA ended in September and WV went on to provide support using private funding resources. The resources are limited and WV is looking for additional support to continue providing services in the camp. In January and February of 2017, World Vision conducted a multi-sector assessment of needs of IDPs living in the camp. The assessment found most IDPs, who come primarily from West Mosul have no financial resources and are completely dependent on humanitarian assistance. They came to the camp with little or nothing in their possession and are in need of full shelter and WASH services. Lack of financial resources and poor access to markets means that these IDPs have little capacity to purchase assets, neither can they pay for services such as clean water, and garbage collection services some families even struggle to get core relief items such as hygiene kits. This poses particular challenges for vulnerable groups, such as women, children and disabled, as it also increases the protection issues, as they have to walk long distance to collect water, or having issues accessing safe latrine and bathing spaces that can provide privacy, etc. In some cases, female has to bath inside shelter during dark or wait until other family members are away. Also, unclean water also poses challenges for health of vulnerable groups, especially young infants. Overall, Hassan sham U2 camp operated at less than full capacity of 9360 individuals, in the last 12 months the population in the camp fluctuated continuously because there were departures and new arrivals of IDPs throughout the implementation period. As coalition Iraqi forces regain control over Mosul city and government went on to gain control over parts of northern Iraq and, some IDPs were leaving the camp and returning to their places of origin. However, in some sub-districts around Ninewa Governorate most infrastructure were damaged / destroyed, leaving the returnees without access to WASH and other services. As a result, some of these families moved back to the camps. On the other hand, there were some families that did not want to go back to their places of origin because they were afraid that there might be further violence and or retribution. In light of this WV seeking additional resources to continue support for another six months into 2019. Due to the non-permanent setup of the camps, the IDPs will continue to need essential WASH services to promote and maintain adequate hygiene standards. It is envisaged that IDPs will remain in Hassansham U2 camp for at least six months after the current private funding ends in December, and will continue to need essential WASH services to promote and maintain adequate hygiene standards in the camp. WV is currently providing a full WASH service package for the camp that is currently holding 4, 750 individuals: The services include routine hygiene promotion through sharing key awareness messages, provision of hygiene kits for the most vulnerable households and maintaining the functionality of 400 hand washing facilities; provision of an average of 35 liters of safe water per person per day, through water trucking; daily solid waste removal from the camp daily; facilitation of safe access to sanitation through regular monitoring and maintenance of 390 latrines and 390 showers; and 29 pairs of latrines accessible to persons with disability (each pair has two latrines, one for males and one for females).

Activities

Activity 1.1.2 Clean Water Trucking: WV will provide water via water trucking with support from UNICEF. The primary water sources are Hassan sham water treatment plant and a borehole (BH2) near camp. All water is chlorinated. The water is delivered into 112 x 5000 liter storage tanks installed in camp. Each tank connects to two tap stands and is shared by 16 families. Activity 1.1.3 Tap and Tank stand Maintenance: Periodically WV mobilizes families to clean their tanks with guidance from hygiene promoters via tank cleaning campaigns. To ensure water is uncontaminated, WV will conduct random tap stand, communal water tank and jerry can sampling, once every two weeks. WV will also check pH values and turbidity if the FRC levels are below the minimum.Activity 1.1.1 Chlorination and water quality monitoring: WV will distribute water after quality testing. Water quality testing is also done on all water trucks before filling tanks to ensure water chlorine level is suitable for drinking, in accordance to WHO standards. Water chlorination and pH checks will be performed by trained hygiene promoters, monitored by WV. Bacteriological water analysis will be performed monthly or where contamination is suspected, so fecal contamination remains zero per 100 mm. Chlorine levels will be checked daily to ensure minimum levels of 0.5 mg/L at distribution points. Activity 2.1.1Operation/ cleaning of latrine and Showers the project will ensure safe access to sanitation via regular operations and maintenance for all (361) latrines and shower units constructed in camp, including 58 disability-sensitive units of latrines/showers (29 male 29 female). A block of “2 latrines and 2 showers” is shared by 8 families.Families will clean their latrines, monitored by hygiene promotion teams with support from community leaders. Latrines cleaning kits are distributed monthly, one set per block. WV installs privacy screen on all latrines for females and will continuously monitor if intact. Activity 2.1.2 Desludging Cesspools: All latrines use a pour flush system and waste/black water is collected in cesspools in each latrine block. Sludge removal from cesspools is done when full via a service provider. Grey water from Showers is connected to side drains, which families are responsible for ensuring that all drains near their tents are clear/clean. Activity 2.1.3 Distributing Cleaning Kits: WV will conduct awareness raising for families sharing the latrines and showers on cleaning. Families will organize and prepare weekly schedule for daily cleaning. WV will distribute latrine cleaning kits for each block, to ensure toilets are cleaned with min health hazard. Activity 3.1.1 Hygiene kit distribution: WV will distribute hygiene kit every month. Most IDPs have been in the camp for period of time and could use their own resources to get some basic items. Hence, hygiene kits distribution will focus on 10% of families, who are the most vulnerable in the camp. Activity 3.1.2 Peer group sessions project staff and hygiene promoters will conduct focus groups sessions and household visits. To ensure gender sensitivity, topics such as menstrual hygiene will be included, and household visit teams will have both male & female. Separate sessions will be conducted for specials groups, especially women group and children group, to promote open discussions, with lowered cultural barriers or power dynamics that occur during mix of different age & gender. Activity 3.1.3 Hygiene promotion campaign: WV will conduct hygiene promotion campaigns during WASH special events, e.g., world water day, via community participatory approach. WV will use these platforms to reinforce key WASH messages, and promote social harmony. Activity 3.1.4 IEC Materials: Due to diversity in camp, the project will adopt different media, visual aids and written messages to convey messages to community, including illiterate, disabled and elderly. WV will design banners and stickers for CFSs, schools, latrines, showers, water points, and gathering places for IDPs. For materials targeting children, WV will adopt more animation and fun initiative for messaging. 3.2.1 Solid waste removal: Families will remove trash from their community block and deposit into liter garbage bins distributed. A few casuals will be engaged to collect garbage from streets and public places. Families also have to regularly clean their own garbage bins. This is monitored by hygiene promoters and WASH committees. 3.2.2 Distribution of trash bins: Each community block has 2 120L garbage bins for collecting trash. WV will replace 120L garbage bin when damaged or pose health risk. Each household will receive 20L garbage bins, with no replacement plan, but families will receive trash bags.3.2.3 Clearing side drains: WV will hire casuals in the camp for care and maintenance, such as cleaning open channels, Simple tools will be provided as needed. Casuals will also remove any trash from public places.

Protection Risk Analysis

1.0 Outbreak of infectious disease: (diarrheal diseases, shigellosis, measles, meningitis, hepatitis). Could result in a spike in morbidity and mortality due to infectious disease. This is manageable but might pose a risk due to the dense settlement and limited services for the population. 2.0 Delayed contracts or insufficient funding would pose a serious risk because it might place the continuation of O&M WASH services in Hassansham U2 camp in jeopardy.3.0 Humanitarian access limited or restricted, serious risk could result in slowing implementation (critical for water supply which relies on water trucking)4.0 Security situation deteriorates: serious impact, affects implementation and access to the camp, Delays or vehicles turned around at checkpoints; difficulty in hiring staff from area5.0 Forced displacement from camps: resulting in people leaving the camp shelters only partially filled. Influx of returnees and higher needs in areas of return 6.0 Increase in sectarian or political based clashes in contested areas: The relationship between military actors (Kurdish, Shia, Sunni, Christian groups including civilians, militias and formal military) deteriorates.

Mitigation Measures

1.0 Outbreak of infectious disease: (diarrheal diseases, shigellosis, measles, meningitis, hepatitis). WV will conduct ongoing hygiene and health promotion campaigns. It will also participate in the camp coordination and surveillance system of the EWARN. In case of an epidemic disease outbreak, WV will comply with local contingency plan applied at the national level. 2.0 Delayed contracts or insufficient funding: Another actor takes over or UNICEF is forced to step in as a provider of last resort and fund the WASH services in the camp.3.0 Humanitarian access limited or restricted, WV is already working in the Hassansham area with multiple projects and enjoys positive relationships with local security actors. Contractors will be encouraged to employ locals from the area where possible. Hygiene promoters will be selected from within the camp residents. This will promote temporary continuation of services 4.0 Security situation deteriorates: WV has international and local security staff monitoring security issues. The camp is in a stable area and WV continues to receive access permission from Asayish, Peshmerga, and ISF authorities. Evacuation and contingency plans will be in place for all staff in the field. WV maintains coordination with the UN and other agencies.5.0 Forced displacement from camps: Services will continue unabated for remaining camp residents. The water tanks could potentially be repurposed for new uses including reconstruction elsewhere. Consolidation of camps may be done The O&M services can be re-allocated to areas of return in consultation with the WASH cluster.6.0 Increase in sectarian or political based clashes in contested areas including Hamdaniya: WV will apply the Do No Harm Principle and include all possible population groups. Furthermore, staff originating from the target populations will be hired, which has proved to have positive impact on WV’s programming throughout KRI.

How is your project accountable to affected population

WV’s accountability framework includes beneficiaries from the early stages. Communities are consulted through direct visits, focus group discussions and are provided regularly with relevant progress reports/ updates and evaluation reports communicated in appropriate ways so that they can advise on project implementation and give feedback on service levels. The project will also ensure good “information provision” throughout project for beneficiaries, so communities will have information of interventions, selection criteria, PESA, rights and reporting mechanism. A fully functional complaints mechanism will be established to receive feedback from beneficiaries on the intervention: the beneficiaries have the choice of 1) calling a helpline, 2) writing feedback (feedback box), 3) presenting a memorandum, or 4) talking to WV staff about their problems and find solutions, which will allow feedback and communications from different beneficiaries of varying background and literacy rateThe MEAL team will receive monthly feedback from the project beneficiaries categorized by a) inquires b) sensitive complaints c) non-sensitive complaints d) suggestions and e) feedback. These inputs will be compiled using a cloud based web portal that is centrally monitored by the Accountability Coordinator from WV KRI. The inputs received are referred to the project team for redress and swift action for resolution.
