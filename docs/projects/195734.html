Project Summary

Since December 2020, the WASH gaps identified in Awerial especially in Mingkaman and the surrounding villages of Padaar, Dounygok, Ahuo, Marik, and Kaltok by the WASH partners are numerous as a result of the influx of IDPs from Jonglei that affected the already strained services delivery in WASH, with no funding by the WASH partners to cover the old caseload of IDPs of 68,000 individuals, and the currently verified new influx of IDPs from Jonglei is 52,015 individuals by the WFP. In September 2022 (IRNA report, 11.10.2022 Awerial County in Lakes State), the flood overpour from Gel river to the nearest low land settlements in Awerial county forcefully displaced 4,841 HHs with 24,205 individuals in 7 payams which include Dor, Bunagok, Abuyung, Magok, Nile, Alel and Puluk and 17 Bomas of Awerial County. This increased the needs of humanitarian assistance and require urgent consideration of the affected population for humanitarian’s intervention. The flooding affected schools, food security and livelihood, health & nutrition, shelters and road access.Most of the boreholes in Awerial Center have been submerged by floodwaters and some have collapsed- meaning that water in the boreholes have been contaminated. The displaced populations in Dor and Watnhom drink water from seasonal water ponds, 100% of the displaced persons are practicing open defecations and they have no knowledge of hygiene and sanitation practices. As the result, majority of children are suffering from diarrheal and other waterborne diseases. There are numerous WASH gaps identified during the IRNA assessments report ranges from the shortage of water for drinking and domestic use that requires drilling of new wells in the up/dryland settlement areas far from the water sources, rehabilitation of the non-functional motorized systems, rehabilitation of broken-down wells, and shock chlorination of wells submerged by the floodwater, construction of shared household latrines, de-commissioning of latrines, distribution of latrine construction materials to family/households willing to construct their pits, hygiene awareness sessions and WASH NFI’s distributions. According to the IRNA report (04-06 December 2021), the main triggers and drivers of the situation in the Duk Islands settlements were in two folds: the inter-communal violence due cattle raids and abductions that occurred in July 2020, and this forced the people to relocate from the Duk mainland to the islands for safety and security. The displacement was followed by devastating floods in 2020, 2021 and 2022. According to the estimate, the number of people affected both IDPs and the host are 27,006 individuals confined in deferent islands within Dongchak Payam of Duk County during the visit, the team found that the most affected people are female and children including youths in the islands. The immediate priority needs identified by the assessment team include, WASH for hygiene promotion, noting open defecation in the river is being practiced, the affected community relies on river water for their daily uses without treating or boiling and the available water points are submersed in water. The IRNA report (21.10.2021 Twic East County) indicate that devastating flood from 2020, TwIc East as one of the Jonglei state became one of the most affected regions with thousands of people displaced to different locations. Some people flee to Mingka,man (Awerial County), Mangala (Rajab County) and Bor County Headquarter. IRNA report of 21.10.2021, an estimated 58,069 individuals (20,324 host community, 31,938 IDPs and 5,807 returnees are affected, and the figure by now has increased with the more devastating floods of 2022. Out 249 boreholes in Twic East, over 200 of them have submerged, Approx.65% of HH assessed use surface water for drinking and other household uses, Limited or no latrines on islands and IDPs camps, Poor personal hygiene due to lack of basic hygiene items such as soaps and MHM kits for women and girls.

1 - Needs

The living standards and their wellbeing of an estimated 6 million people will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) conditions. The long rainy season replenished major waterways, and in these Counties only 39 per cent of the population reported having enough water to meet their household needs countrywide, dropping to 34 per cent in rural areas. Lack of enough water for drinking, personal hygiene and domestic with extremely low coverage of water infrastructure explains that 49 per cent of households reported relying on surface or unimproved water as their main water source. In 2022, the long rainy season from 2020 continues to devastate most part of the country. In Twic East, the affected number of people affected by the floods are estimated number (58,069 individuals) with 20,324 individuals from host community, 31,938 IDPs and 5,805 returnees per IRNA report October 2021. According to IRNA report in December 2021, the estimated number of people affected both IDPs and the host are 27,006 individuals (4,501 households) confined in deferent islands within Dongchak Payam of Duk County with the majority from Duk Mainland, and the most affected people are female and children including youths. Some people fled to Mingkaman in Awerial county, Mangala in Rajap County and Bor County. In Mingkaman per the registration done by WFP SCOPE BMR a total of 50,376 individuals (7,287 households) displaced from Twic East (26,792 individuals/3,817 households), Duk (5,409 individuals/ 770 households ) and South Bor County (17,631 individuals/2,700 households). IRNA conducted in Awerial County in September 2022 indicates, the flood overpour from Gel river to the nearest low land settlements in Awerial county forcefully displaced 4,841 HHs with 24,205 individuals in 7 payams which include Dor, Bunagok, Abuyung, Magok, Nile, Alel and Puluk and 17 Bomas of Awerial County. This increased the needs of humanitarian assistance and require urgent consideration of the affected population for humanitarian’s intervention. The flooding affected schools, health & nutrition facilities and road access. All work will be done inline with the WaSH Cluster strategy in close coordination with line ministries’ guidelines for implementing WaSH during the COVID-19 Pandemic. The project has 4 Main objectives; CLWSH/CO1) Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition through provision of 5 nutrition facilities with improved access to sanitation and 5 nutrition facilities with improved access to safe water by construction of flood-resilient institutional latrine stances and water points. CLWSH/CO2) Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties through reaching household with water treatment, water carrying containers and improvement of water source to benefit 15,000 persons, hygiene promotion and soap in health facilities, schools, IDPs sites and host communities, including those that help to protect them from Covid-19 through training of 30 community hygiene promoters,distribution of WASH NFI’s and Conducting hygiene & sanitation awareness massages to 15,000 persons, Construction of emergency latrines to improve access to sanitation to 1,000 persons. CLWSH/CO3) WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation through reaching women and girls with improved access to water and/or sanitation after a safety audit to 1,000 persons with the distribution of WASH NFI kits, Distribution of water treatment product to 1000 persons. CLWSH/CO4) Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of inclusive gender sensitive disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure by construction/rehabilitation and shock chlorination of 30 wells and 10 latrines with flood resilient design.

2 - Project activities

The project has 4 Main objectives with the activities are; CLWSH/CO1) Contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through Construction/rehabilitation and shock chlorination of flood resilient Boreholes, Refresher/Training of water Management Committees, Construction/rehabilitation of flood-resilient institutional latrines. CLWSH/CO2) Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties. The activities include; Water - distribution of water purification tablets, water quality analysis, shock chlorination of water points, FRC measurements, distribution of Water containers, rehabilitation/construction of water points. Sanitation – distribution of latrine digging tools, construction of household latrines by the community. Installation of handwashing points. Rehabilitation of latrines in health facilities/schools, Hygiene promotion – hygiene awareness in health facilities/schools/community on use of soap and good hygiene practices, distribution of soap, hygiene kits and MHM. CLWSH/CO3) WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation; Construction of bathing/washing facilities, construction of communal latrines, Construction of communal latrines after GBV risk analysis with women & girls, Construction of new borehole (hand pump), Construction of new hand dug well (protected) after GBV risk analysis with women & girls, Sensitizing communities on emergency sanitation (CATS, OD fields, trenches, etc.)Solid waste, Distribution of sanitary cloth (reusable). CLWSH/CO4) Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster-resistant structures and infrastructure by Construction/rehabilitation of disaster-resistant latrines, construction/rehabilitation and shock chlorination of water points, Hygiene promotion sessions (participatory interaction), Latrines Desludging, Rain Water harvesting, Construction of latrines through CLTS , Operation of SWAT system, Training of community hygiene promoters, Training of School hygiene clubs, Training of water (WASH) user/management committee, Water quality testing & treatment.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Registration of the beneficiaries will be done through the community leaders like the chiefs and the office of the RRC, Help-Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe normally does its distribution of the WASH items at the site to minimize distance of the travel and any associated risk, equally, the community leaders have to be present during the distribution to avoid any violent that may arise during distribution.All the WASH infrastructures will be designed that provide minimum risk to the beneficiaries (women, girls and boys without and with disabilities) are not exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance.- The sanitation facilities will be designed such that it is gender segregated, safe, secured doors and minimum walking distance to the beneficiaries to access them. are the major protection concerns among the affected population. The persons with disability will have design and construction of all facilities incorporated with ramps and hand rails to support them.- Water point infrastructures will have a minimum walking distance to the beneficiaries including the vulnerable individuals. - Community engagement on GBV will be part of the intervention to aware them on the danger of accessing the WASH facilities at night and most importantly alone by the women, boys and girls.- Help – Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe e.V, will at all-time be in collaboration with protection cluster partners for all GBV related case for information and referral of the cases. Help –Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe in collaboration with the stakeholders, community leaders GBV and child protection cluster partners, will help in the mitigation of such act. Help-Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe will not work in isolation but instead in coordination with relevant stakeholders on matters relating to child safeguarding and PSEAHelp –Hilfe zur Selbsthilfe Integration of GBV, and child protection streamline activities like awareness creation will help sensitized the community on where to report such case or where to get the protection assistance
