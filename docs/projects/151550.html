Project Summary

This project will focus on provision of access to safe learning spaces for internally displaced populations. The project will facilitate access to education for school going children from the most vulnerable IDP households. The project has three objectives:
Objective 1: Ensure access to safe and protective learning environments for displacement affected children in Banadir, Hiraan, Galgaduud and South-Mudug, as well as in Lower Jubba, Lower Shabelle, and Bay regions.
Objective 2: Ensure vulnerable children are engaged in life-saving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion
Objective 3: Strengthened capacity to deliver effective and coordinated education in emergencies preparedness and response within the education system
DKH will mainstream protection in all the interventions by using a beneficiary selection criteria guiding on vulnerable groups to be selected for support, gender-sensitivity programming, and reducing risks through sensitization in school and communities on protection issues. Disabled, pregnant and lactating mothers will also be considered among the vulnerabilities for support.
The project is based on an existing approach that DKH and its partner DBG implemented to increase the enrolment rate amongst the most vulnerable IDP households (hh). 30 TLS with 4 classrooms each have been build inside the camps in Banadir between K7 and K20, operated by 30 teacher associations (TA) with 5 teachers each with a gender rate above 50% female teachers, and 2880 learners from most vulnerable households have benefitted from vouchers for the payment of school fees and learning materials so far. An evaluation in Jan 2018 shows that teachers are in need to be trained on national curricula, testing and referring methods, life skills, and beneficiaries need support in food security to reduce risk of child labour. The MoE released national curriculum that needs to be applied in the TLS to comply with the national EiE strategy and they shall register as private learning institutions for more durable solutions. While the food security issue shall be addressed through other sectors, DKH plans to to address the lessons learned and to expand the approach to more IDP boys and girls in Banadir and to IDP camps in Beletweyn and Galmudug state.

Needs

Somalia has 2.6 million IDPs and 1.8 m children in need for education. During the drought 2016/18 the IDP camps along Afgooye road/Banadir saw high influx and a high rate of evictions. The camps grew into Lower Shabelle and expanded far away from the tarmac road without schools in those new camps. While the IDP enrolment rate in Kaxda was at 11% in Oct 2016 (own assessment), today it is expected to be even lower.Most often only boys and girls from better-off IDP households (hh) access permanent schools in host communities. Only they can afford to pay school fees (lesson learned 2015). 94% of IDP respondents (own assessment Dec 2016) could not pay school fees and their children were out of school. About 31% of IDP households in Banadir live on less than 65 USD per months (own assessment Dec 2016). Reasons for poverty are high levels of vulnerabilities like female or minor headed hh, pregnant or lactating women and adolescent girls, and elderly and/or disabled persons in the hh. Such hh are also food insecure and child labour is used to contribute to the income (own evaluation Jan 2018). Meanwhile DKH and its partner DBG implemented an approach to target the most vulnerable IDP hh and to increase the enrolment rate: 30 TLS with 4 classrooms each have been build inside the camps, operated by 30 teacher associations (TA) with 5 teachers each from the IDP community with a gender rate above 50% female teachers, and 2880 learners from most vulnerable households have benefitted from vouchers for the payment of school fees and learning materials so far. An evaluation in Jan 2018 shows that teachers are in need to be trained on national curricula, testing and referring methods. Beneficiaries need support in food security to reduce the risk of child labour. The MoE released national curriculum needs to be applied in the TLS and they need to register as private learning institutions.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

One purpose of the project is to enable the TAs and CECs to better manage the TLS and to integrate the TLS better into the education system. In this context, training in management and maintenance, teaching skills, application of curricula, testing and referring methods, lifesaving skills, psychological support, school safety, and hygiene promotion shall be given to the teachers and, where applicable, to the CEC. The TA shall be supported to register their TLS as learning institutions with the MoE. Capacity in EiE shall be improved through capacity building for local partnering NGOs in the education sector. With the application of the national curriculum referring of learners is possible when they either return to previous schools or change schools to other areas, provided there are other learning institutions that apply the national curriculum, too.Condition to achieve this is the enhancement of a higher teaching qualification of most of the teachers in Somalia and especially within DKH’s existing TLS. Therefore, manifold teachers’ training and formalization of the TLS in the education system is a necessary step that points the way of durable solutions.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Many IDP households’ income is below the limit of absolute poverty. Due to conflict and natural disasters households may get displaced and members may get separated, killed or disabled, and children may get orphaned, separated or abandoned. When economic active members in households are absent due to displacement the coping capacity of a household is drastically reduced. According to an own assessment, there are 31% of IDP households that show vulnerability criteria like female headed hh, pregnant or lactating women and girls as members of the hh, hh with many children and/or elderly people and disabled persons. Such households cannot gain sufficient income to afford the payment of school fees. At the other hand side, under the condition of the absence of a national budget for education, all operational schools and learning spaces in Somali depend on the income from school fees. These constraints create an automatism that excludes children of the most vulnerable displaced households from education.The approach of “voucher4education”, which is applied by DKH, allows to target specifically children of the most vulnerable households. Especially vulnerable children that otherwise would be excluded from education through economic pattern can gain access to education with equal chances.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Children and adolescents of both sexes are vulnerable to forceful recruitment in armed groups and to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) as two of the most prominent risks. Training on various protection issues of teachers and CECs as well as regular monitoring of the conduct of teachers and the behavior of learners, girls and boys, will help to mitigate the risk of SEA. A reporting system of attacks against education that includes also a complaint mechanism that allows the reporting of sexual exploitation committed by humanitarian aid workers will also contribute to avoid SEA cases. Reported cases shall be examined and onwards reported to the competent governmental juridical authorities. Attempts of recruitment of learners for armed groups shall also be reported to the competent law enforcement authorities.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Forceful evictions have impacted learning spaces constructed for IDPs by DKH and destroyed 2 TLS.OCHA (August, 2018) stated that the risk of eviction continues to rise for IDPs, with 204,000 IDPs having been forcefully evicted in the year as at the time of the update, equating to the total number evicted in 2017. Addressing the causes and consequences of evictions, DKH will train the CECs to engage the land owners in negotiation for the space where the TLS are constructed and also for the land owners to issue a prior notice period sufficient for relocation when needed.To mitigate attempts of armed forces to enclose schools for the purpose of forced recruitment, the TLS are be designed to allow for the learners to easily escape in case of an attack by jumping out of the large windows or to break through the plastic sheeting, and then to escape in all directions. This is based on a lay-out of schools developed by UNICEF in the North-East of the DR Congo, when schools have been raided by militias of the Ugandan Lord’s Resistance Army (LRA). Though this didn’t happen in Somalia yet, reports of physical attacks against schools or forceful recruitment of learners, e.g. in Gedo and in the Al-Shabaab controlled areas in Galgaduud, become more frequent.DKH will report and respond when learning spaces are affected by armed forces or other groups.
