Project Summary

The project has four main components focusing on 1. Capacity building of local partner, civil society organizations and local authorities with regards to protection and social cohesion; 2. Building social cohesion between Rohingya and Host Communities; 3. Improve awareness for protection risks and meaningful access to protection services; and 4. Increase safety in the immediate physical surroundings of the community members. 50% of all project participants will be female and particular attention will be payed to vulnerable groups, such as elderly, persons with disabilities, youth and girls and women in general. The project will set up different dialog fora for conflict resolution and several community-based protection groups, which will raise awareness for protection risks and will facilitate access to protection services. Rohingya and Host Community members were consulted for their needs during a feasibility study conducted during the project design stage. All core activities will be implemented in a participatory manner where group members identify protection risks themselves and will decide about actions to be taken. Two Community-based Monitoring Committees will be set up (one for RRs and respective authorities; one for HCs and respective authorities), which each will meet regularly to assess the project process and to propose adaptations where needed. Representatives from all beneficiary groups within the project and local authority representatives will be chosen in a participatory manner to sit in the Monitoring Committees. In the selection of direct beneficiaries, especially those who receive financial support, the Do-No-Harm approach will be ensured through a participative and transparent selection process. Furthermore, the project will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM). The AAP principles will thus be adhered to throughout the project period. WHH and FIVDB will cooperate closely with other actors and local authorities in the area and participate in relevant sector/Protection Working Group meetings. FIVDB and WHH have a PSEA policy with zero tolerance for any misconduct. Any incidents reported through the CRM will be followed up upon in a due process and all staff involved in project implementation will be trained on PSEA. The project will be implemented in conformity with Covid-19 prevention measures and with consideration to DRR and environmental aspects.

1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya Refugees who sought refuge in camps of Cox’s Bazar and are highly dependent on the support of humanitarian actors continue to face several severe protection risks, including human trafficking, child labor, sexual and gender-based violence, and exploitation. Especially people with special needs, such as elderly, people with disabilities, gender diverse populations and women and children are at risk and often lack knowledge about and/or access to protection services, including health, livelihood, legal, education and welfare services. Only 17% of Rohingya households were able to name at least one of the four most important services for SGBV cases (health, psychosocial services, legal aid or policy and safety) in the J-MSNA-2019 and 58% of persons with disabilities do not know where to seek assistance. Protection is also an issue among Host Community households and according to the Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment, overall awareness of protection services among Host Community members is low, with 57% reporting not being aware of community protection measures. Furthermore, according to Innovations for Poverty Action, 46% of Host Community members are aware of gender-based violence in their community. In addition, the J-MSNA-2020 reports that 49% of children are engaged in income-generating activities. 20% girls under the age of 18 are married off, while 7% of children experienced psychosocial distress. Moreover, 2% children are exposed with violence and are reported to be missing. According to Mid Term review of JRP 2020, the socio-economic impact of COVID-19 exacerbated pre-existing protection risks. The provision of support to adolescents through life skills, pre-vocational, resilience and peace building skills due to closure of facilities and social distancing measures also presented challenges. Children are increasingly exposed to child marriage, forced labor, trafficking, and exposure to criminal elements. With the decrease in livelihoods opportunities for both refugees and host communities, refugees reported an increase in theft and violence when accessing resources such as water and food. This has exacerbated tensions with host communities with reports of violent conflict between local Bangladeshis and refugees. Intra-refugee conflicts have also increased, as have incidents of intimate partner violence and threats of eviction, all of which have contributed to a deteriorating protection environment and negative discourse on refugees and humanitarian workers. Furthermore, the Protection Working Group has identified that tensions between host and refugee communities are continued to be reported and that social cohesion measures are urgently needed. According to the ISCG Social Cohesion Bulletin (June 2019), the attitude of Host Community members towards Rohingya is characterized by 59% of respondents reporting no interaction with them at all and 45% stating they were unhappy or very unhappy with their presence. It is problematic that RRs and HCs don’t have a common platform where conflicts between the groups can be addressed and that qualitative structured dialogue between the two groups is not given any space. Moreover, local government representatives find it difficult to resolve those conflicts because of lack of resources and lack of awareness among the communities.

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Advocacy with duty bearers and civil society organisations will be undertaken for timely delivery of qualitative, non-discriminatory assistance for Rohingya and Host Communities. They will be sensitized towards the needs of community members and their protection risks, incl. human trafficking, SGBV and needs of particularly vulnerable groups, such as elderly and persons with disabilities. They will also be sensitized towards integrating age, gender and diversity principles, to implement a rights-based community approach and to adhering to PSEA standards. In addition, duty bearers and civil society organisations will receive capacity building to facilitate conflict resolution between and within Rohingya and Host Community members. 2. Social cohesion will be built through mobilizing Rohingya and Host Communities for conflict resolution in line with protection principles. Dialogue and conflicts will be structured and ideally integrated into existing structures. Members from both communities will furthermore be invited to join inter-community events including sports games and theatre performances to foster exchange between them. These events will be organised by Community Protection Groups together with established adolescent clubs, which will also learn about peace-keeping and gender equality. 3. A community-based protection mechanism will be implemented and awareness of protection risks related to WASH, Nutrition (Maternal and Child Health and Nutrition), Psychosocial Support, Safety and Empowerment, incl. human trafficking and smuggling will be strengthened among community members. Causes and consequences of protection risks will be explored and prevention/response strategies will be developed, so that community members can increase their safety by being empowered to respond to e.g. SGBV. SGBV survivors, persons needing psychosocial support, psychological first aid, medical support, incl. SRH, or legal support will not only be informed about available services, but will be directly enabled to physically access them. During the first half of the project, there will be separate CPGs for women, men, boys and girls to make sure that each group is able to address their specific needs and to create a safe space in which problems can be shared and discussed. In order to create awareness for the other groups' needs, the CPGs will be mixed later on in the project. 4. Teams with Risk and Vulnerability Mapping Volunteers (RVMVs) will be formed to map risks and vulnerabilities focusing on DRR, infrastructure and the physical environment, and to draw up plans to reduce them. This shall result in safer and more dignified living conditions and increase their resilience. RVMVs are also trained as rescue volunteers and can thus quickly support their communities in the event of a disaster. Planning and implementation will be coordinated with all relevant (protection) actors and duty bearers and only agreed-upon instruments to respond to natural disasters will be implemented. Through the Community-based Monitoring Committees a solid feedback mechanism is ensured and accountability will be ensured through the transparent and inclusively communicated Complaint and Response Mechanism, incl. help desks and a special complaint number for PSEA cases. Meaningful access will be ensured through a participatory beneficiary selection process, fair and inclusive implementation and through strict follow-up of issued complaints. Age, Gender and Diversity of the communities will be represented in the different activities and project staff will be trained on PSEA and Do No Harm. Sustainable outcomes will be achieved through the participatory nature of the project, facilitated through the Community-based Monitoring Committees. Community members and local authorities are involved in the design of the new structures, which will increase their ownership to maintain these after the project end. DRR, environmental and Covid-19 aspects are considered throughout.
