Project Summary

The War Child (WC) programme, with a budget of 2,415,300 USD, is dedicated to increasing resilience and wellbeing among vulnerable children and youth in Northeast (NE) and Northwest (NW) Syria Hubs. This one-year project aims to respond to urgent Child Protection needs in Idleb and Aleppo and expand services in Raqqa, Al Hasakeh, and Deir ez-Zor. The project targets 9,939 people in need, focusing on Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) and Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG). Under this project, WC will utilize Child-Friendly Spaces (CFS), structured Psychosocial Support (PSS), and awareness-raising activities. A key component is developing specialized service capacity within local partner organizations to deliver quality case management service. This approach addresses immediate child protection risks and contributes to long-term change by supporting parents, caregivers, local leaders, and communities in building a more protective environment for children affected by conflict. The project also involves collaboration with youth aged between 14-17 and their caregivers, focusing on enhancing life skills and strengthening protective factors at individual, family, and community levels. Structured workshops will address cognitive, emotional, and social functioning, supplemented by meetings with parents and caregivers. By aligning with the overarching objectives of the WC program and the North Syria hubs, the project not only tackles immediate needs but also fosters sustainable technical Child Protection capacity within local organizations, thereby enhancing the quality of the overall response and contributing to the mainstreaming of Protection outcomes. The War Child program diligently navigates various protection risks encountered in its operational areas. These include security incidents affecting staff, facilities, and beneficiaries. In response to these challenges, War Child adopts a vigilant and proactive stance. This involves a keen observation of security trends and developing contingency plans. Moreover, the organization is committed to providing thorough training to the management of partner organizations, equipping them with the necessary skills to manage these risks effectively. Furthermore, incidents related to child safeguarding are met with immediate and focused action. This includes the implementation of training initiatives and ongoing support to local organizations. The foundation of this approach lies in establishing clear policies and appointing dedicated focal points for these issues. Additionally, War Child enhances its adherence to humanitarian standards, creating a barrier against increased local authority interference. Efforts to counteract burnout and psychological distress among staff and caregivers are also a key focus. This is achieved through staff care programs and capacity-building initiatives. These measures are integral to War Child's strategy of collaborating with communities and caregivers. Special attention is paid to fostering social cohesion and easing tensions in areas with high concentrations of internally displaced persons (IDPs). The emphasis on these last two points is crucial, as they underline the organization's comprehensive approach to Protection risk analysis and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, thereby maintaining the consistency and integrity of the program's objectives. To enhance the project's effectiveness and ensure a targeted response to the unique challenges in Northeast and Northwest Syria, WC conducted a comprehensive internal needs assessment. This assessment focused on Child Protection in general with more focus in understanding the specific requirements of Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) and Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed Groups (CAAFAG).The assessment utilized key insights from the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for years 2023 &2024, integrating this information to align the project needs
