Project Summary

The project intends to provide a holistic, comprehensive and inter-sectoral response to the problems of the Education System in both CES and Unity States, and with assumption that schools represents platforms in which also Protection, Nutrition, Hygiene, Health and Psychosocial needs of vulnerable children can be addressed. With this in mind, POH response plans to support the education system, starting from nursery and primary level and including the already existing structures of alternative programs offered by the Accelerating Learning Program (ALP), and strengthening of Adult Literacy centers especially in Unity State. As an initiative that has worked, POH has refined an approach that has proved to reduced dropout, improve cognitive results, and promoted female education. And on this therefore, learners and teachers from all the different steps of education system will be supported in an integrated way, by providing
scholastic materials, training and sensitization sessions on subjects such as education, child protection, SGBV, health, psychological well-being including early-entrance-into school awareness campaigns. And in order to avoid the absenteeism of teachers in schools,
the project intends to provide an integrated and comprehensive support to the teachers through the provision of food incentives, teacher mentoring in the schools, support to county education departments and payams, support the re-service trainings for female teachers in order to increase the enrolment of female teachers. Structural interventions will be done in schools to improve the quality and safety of the learning environments, i.e. Temporarily Learning Spaces, renovation of classrooms, construction of latrines and where there is need for school kitchens, erection or rehabilitation of fences, drilling or maintenance of boreholes to ensure schools having water point for clean drinking water, for safe re-opening after Covid-19 pandemic and as an entry point to promote good hygiene and health practices through hygiene sessions, HIV/AIDS prevention and reproductive health sessions including Covid-19 prevention campaigns. A part from communities, learners and teachers will also be supported in improving their food security status, by receiving training and agricultural inputs for school gardens for their daily consumption. On the basis of a holistic concept of education, the project aims to involve also the other relevant actors in this process, first of all the parents and the communities at large. For these reason, parents, community members, PTAs & SMCs will be involved in
campaigns and meetings to promote the importance of education and discuss about sensitive topics such as education for girls, violence and SGBV, child protection, among other. Since COVID-19 pandemic still represents a menace to the already fragile education system, the project will have a cross cutting component in the whole intervention to ensure all COVID-19 mitigation measures are in place. The objectives of the project are: To improve the quality of education and to ensure access to safe learning spaces for vulnerable children, adolescents, and youth; To provide access for children, adolescents and youth to integrated social protection services; To promote resilience and capacity building of teachers, parents, and community accordingly.

1 - Needs

Based on Feb 2022 HNO, there are 3.6 million people estimated to be in need of education services in 2022 in the Republic of South Sudan. Of the estimated population, 97% are children, 50% are female and 15% are those with disabilities. The interplay of underlying forces such as COVID 19, broader socio-economic challenges, destruction of some schools in prone-flooded parts of the country talk of Unity State and insecurity in volatile areas talk of CES has overwhelmed the education system and these impacted on school functionality. Worse on these, the prolonged closure of schools in the period between March 2020 and April 2021) for all grades except the candidate classes (Grade 8 & S4) as a result of COVID 19 exacerbated existing education challenges and inequities. Recurrent communal violence through 2021 in Unity, Central Equatoria, Lakes, Jonglei, the Greater Pibor Administrative Area, Upper Nile, Warrap, and Western Equatoria led to school closures. The displacement of teachers and children and revenged killings limited children's access to schools. Learners in the 2021 Education Need Assessment (ENA) cited insecurity in and around schools as a major reason for learner absenteeism and drop-out in 2021. There was a significant learning loss due to these factors. The assessment findings cited the delays in the payment of teachers’ salaries and incentives across South Sudan as the main reason for the high teachers’ attrition and absenteeism rates and poor quality of teaching in schools and thus poor performance. In the POH’s 2021 multi-sector survey findings reveal the same trend in Central Equatoria and Unity for high attrition and absenteeism rates due to delays in payments of teachers’ salaries. The survey revealed that a good number of pupils dropped out of school for lack of teachers or truancy on the part of the teachers. Direct and indirect costs (SDF), volunteer teacher payments, uniform, and examination fees requirement were cited as the un-solved barriers to irregular attendance and drop out in the two States of Central Equatoria and Unity. In Unity state, the most stressing factors for not attending school by both children below 10 years and up to 15 years is lack of money to pay for scholastic materials & parent contribution fees. Other barriers to children/adolescents accessing education are inadequate learning spaces, untrained teachers, hunger, distance to school and lack of school supplies. A good number of schools assessed do not provide food for learners; thus, the reason why hunger is a key barrier to education and one of the determining factor for regular attendance, performance and retention. Early and forced marriages and teenage pregnancies are widely reported and are among the reasons for drop-outs. In the same survey, it is noticed that, in lower classes of P.1 to P4 girls’ enrolment is promising but it keeps on reducing in the upper classes (P.5-P.8). Over-age girls who enter into the education system late are vulnerable to early and forced marriages and pregnancies before completing their primary level education simply because of some harmful cultural perceptions inherent in the communities especially in Unity State. During the prolonged closure of schools, a good number of girls fell victims of forced marriages or teenage pregnancies and the two States were not left behind. Children with Disabilities (CwDs) also face numerous challenges in accessing quality education because of lack of mobility or assistive devices and so it was also noticed that, most of the schools in the two States do not have latrines accessible for CwDs. And on top of these, teachers are not trained to achieve or deliver inclusive education and school communities are not sensitized on the need to create enabling environments for CwDs. In the survey, a good number of schools are reported to have no access to safe clean water and lack menstrual health management to improve female retention and progression.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

POH has in place a tested and working system of four years multi-sector surveys that through the employment of more than 300 questions (including question on FSL, nutrition, GBV, hazards, protection, disabilities, education, gender inclusion, etc.), a good number of interviews more than 300 HHs that are capable of providing detailed reference at the Counties and Payam levels, an analysis centered on specific groups (single mothers, families with members with disabilities, poor farmers, etc) and the participatory discussion of such findings with the staff and representatives of the communities ensures not only that projects are designed on the base of the specific needs of the different groups of the population, but also that such knowledge becomes the internal foundations by POH staff and thus the base of an informed conversation and a common understanding with local communities, ensuring feedback from the most vulnerable within the community. Finally, the coordination bases that POH has across the targeted communities are equipped with phones of hot-lines, suggestion boxes; focal point protection help desks for unanimous and isolated reporting of abuses or complaints whenever it occurs.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concerning protection, POH will work collaboratively with other actors (UN agencies, NGOs, FBOs and CBOs) involved in child protection issues to safeguard the delivery of protection services in the schools and communities. It will work directly with the project schools’ teachers and the PTAs to promote zero-tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse in the schools including all forms of discrimination against girls and CWDs. POH will be accountable to the beneficiaries by enabling them to participate in the project at the planning, implementation, and evaluation phases. Finally, appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints will be set up. All POH South Sudan staff members have been trained on child protection, disability and gender mainstreaming, has own policies in place on PSEA, Child Protection, Child Safeguarding, Code of Conducts that are explained and countersigned by each staff and key supplier and have capacity to share the importance of this policies with any stakeholder they interact with. On regularly basis, POH conducts community engagement meetings such as awareness campaigns, innovative youth events that allows open participation/discussion over the quality of progress of the activity conducted. POH embrace a last-mile approach through field-based personal and daily visits to the field and conducts impact analysis and post monitoring reviews, which are analyzed distinguishing relevant categories of people with the objective of providing detailed information about the targeting of the intervention and beneficiaries barriers to access services. Subsequent projects are designed to address such barrier and put in place a system of monitoring that puts special emphasis on monitoring the targeting of those groups of beneficiaries found to face particular barriers.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Pilgrims of Hope has time and time placed protection and gender issues at the center of every intervention by engaging directly with the host communities’ children, IDPs, returnees and CWDs so that their needs, their priorities and their rights are addressed within the scope of the project. On this therefore, POH will always create conducive and interactive environments that that not only allows them but also encourage them to express their needs, rights and priorities. Apart from the active involvement of the rights holders in the project, POH will ensure that the duty bearers (Government, PTAs/SMCS, institutions and non-state actors) play an effective role to promote the rights of the CWDs and girls. Activities will be developed with the active participation of women and parents in all stages of the implementation process, to ensure that girls’ priority needs are taken into consideration and that WASH infrastructures meet the right gender standards (IASC, 2015, Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action: Reducing risk, promoting resilience and also aiding recovery accordingly. More importantly, prevention of GBV (Sexual Exploitation) is pursued by displaying SEA code of conducts at service access points (schools, health facilitates, distribution points and at the community. But also ensuring that, safety audits are conducted and multiple locations selected to avoid far travel of beneficiaries and reducing risks. Community members, staff and stakeholder’s capacity will be strengthened through specific PSEA trainings accordingly.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The capillary and continuous presence that POH has in the communities targeted by the intervention POH has more than 75% of its staff, including senior positions, and the extensive use of staff recruited locally among people recognized by the communities, ensures that such fruitful cooperation with project beneficiaries continues throughout the implementation of the project. All project’s beneficiaries (female and male IDPs and returnee’s children, CWDs and host communities’ children) will be considered as capable and active resources rather than as passive recipients who do not have a say in the project. To include the perspectives of the very pupils and teachers, moreover, POH conducts in the schools that supports surveys that provide a reading of people and students wellbeing, expectations, and opinion about the schools. For this goal POH has prepared specific tools adapted to the context and of proven efficiency. By running this survey, POH found that in the schools of Unity-(Panyijjiar, Mayendit and Leer), where more pronounced had been the flood and community conflict sort of displacements, and nearly 60% of pupils/students reported to have lived in refugee camps over the course of the previous two years. Thanks to a very localized approach with POH staff regularly present and usually coming from the same community of project locations, accountability is pursued also directly through its staff that can act as bridge between top management and beneficiaries. Furthermore, considering the purposely field-oriented management team (officers and also project managers are field based), beneficiaries and affected population at large as constant possibility to interact and file complain to staff at different level. Furthermore, POH periodically shares finding of monitoring and evaluation activities with SMoE, PTAs and communities at large, ensuring that project decisions are justified, shared and finally owned by the affected population. Yearly, POH conducts surveys which results are discussed through open meetings with the community where we work, the county and payam officials and traditional leaders (Boma Chiefs). During these events, POH explains to the community its plans for the incoming years as a means to an informed discussion among community members and receive solid suggestions. POH extension officers, AAP committees before selecting a person/group/community as beneficiaries, establish a case specific dialog where POHs expectations are compared with the one from the beneficiaries in order to evaluate possible challenges/ intervention failure chance. Beside the surveys, POH’s beneficiaries have access to information during participatory meetings which provided platform to address issues linked to power-relations whereby some strata of the population culturally marginalized can find the opportunity to express themselves, include the view of beneficiaries in programming and allow intervention in case of issues reported thus increase transparency and accountability through awareness of beneficiaries on protection-related issues as well as project-design accordingly.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

POH will ensure initial involvement of teachers, women, and men and local women groups representatives in AAP committees and during the planning activities implementation; at project implementation they will be provided with information about available GBV services and collecting feedback from women,, men,, boys and girls about their needs/safety concerns; at the time where SMcs and PTAs are meeting their responsibilities especially during the covid -19 pandemic, the project will ensure the above are trained/oriented on how to safely respond to a disclosure and that they have the most up-to-date referral pathways. POH will meet with the AAP committee to discuss on the complaints and feedback from time to time preferably twice in a Month, on termly basis and there will be a help desk at field sites attended by one personnel from POH at a higher position to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries accordingly.

Finally, POH will have a gender & protection specialist, a gender officer and a protection officer dedicated to the analysis, supervision and adjustment of activities from the gender perspective.
