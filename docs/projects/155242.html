Needs

Violence in Rakhine State, Myanmar, has driven an estimated 706,000 Rohingya across the border into Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh, since 25 August 2017. A significant proportion of the new arrivals have settled in the Kutupalong-Balukhali Expansion Site in Cox’s Bazar district, now hosting more than 631,000 refugees. The speed and scale of displacement has resulted in a critical humanitarian emergency, both for refugee and host communities. As the camps are the most densely populated in the world, with poor hygiene conditions and limited sector capacity in hygiene promotion, the refugee population is exposed to a high risk of disease outbreak. Overall, WASH facilities that were set up in the first phase of the emergency a year ago have been of low quality. Many latrines across the camps were built in close proximity to the water source, without adherence to SPHERE standards, and the lack of space across all camps has hampered the development of an efficient waste management system.The fact that camps are overcrowded, lack appropriate WASH facilities and sufficient lighting is putting women and girls at a high risk of GBV. Furthermore, the high illiteracy rate of the Rohingya population disproportionately affects women and girls who are less likely to receive information relevant to their lives in the camp. Female leadership is widely absent as military-appointed block leaders, Majhis, within the current governance structures are almost exclusively male. As a consequence, women’s and girls’ voices are often not heard and addressed.
