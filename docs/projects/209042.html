Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Sudan, exacerbated by fighting that began in April 2023, has led to significant increases in humanitarian needs, particularly for the displaced population. Over 7.4 million people have been displaced, both within and outside Sudan, severely straining the already fragile health system. Many health facilities in conflict areas are non-functional, leaving two-thirds of the population without healthcare access. The prevalence of malnutrition is high and expected to worsen due to the compromised health system, poor Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services, and displacement.
The River Nile and Northern States, hosting significant numbers of Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), are struggling with weakened basic services and increased demand from IDPs.In response to the critical situation, MedGlobal, alongside its health-focused partner, the Sudanese American Physicians’ Association (SAPA), is initiating a wide-ranging health, nutrition, and WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) program. This program aims to swiftly address and fulfill the immediate humanitarian needs of the communities in question, fostering rapid improvements in their living conditions. This strategic intervention is consistent with the goals and priorities of the State Ministry of Health, the Humanitarian Aid Commission, and various involved clusters.
The intervention includes the following activities:
**Wadi Halfa**:
- Operational support for Halfa Hospital and a mobile team in Argeen, offering integrated health, nutrition, and WASH services.
- Comprehensive healthcare services, including maternal and child health, newborn and childhood illness management, reproductive health, chronic disease management, and support for non-communicable diseases.
- WASH support with clean water trucking, construction of latrines, distribution of hygiene kits, and promotion activities.
- Mass screening for malnutrition in under-fives and pregnant and lactating women (PLW), with treatment programs for severe and moderate acute malnutrition.
- Community-based activities for malnutrition case finding and referrals, and support for SAM (Severe Acute Malnutrition) cases requiring medical attention.
- Enhancement of IYCF activities.

**River Nile State**: (In partnership with SAPA)
- Support for two primary healthcare centers (PHCs) in Atbara and two mobile clinics.
- Provision of medicines, medical equipment, staff incentives, and operational support.
- Capacity building for the Ministry of Health (MoH) to respond to medical emergencies.
- WASH initiatives including water trucking, equipment for water supply improvement, latrine rehabilitation, and hygiene awareness.
- Nutrition support through MUAC screening, Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) counseling, and referral to feeding centers.
This project aims to alleviate the humanitarian crisis in Sudan by providing essential health, nutrition, and WASH services to the affected communities, with a focus on the most vulnerable populations.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Med global is committed to practicing the highest standards of behavior, accountability, and integrity with everyone we encounter regardless of age, ability or disability, gender identity, race, religion or beliefs. Med global recognizes that many project participants fall under most marginalized categories, and we accept responsibility to incorporate protection principles into the aid delivery cycle to ensure safety, dignity and ‘do no harm’. Med global `s approach to provision of health, nutrition and WASH will enhance protection mainstreaming and to ensure the inclusion of vulnerable groups in the proposed activities. MG will conduct protection risks to be identified and addressed through the consultation and coordination mechanisms in the camps. MG will ensure the protection of beneficiaries by incorporating protection principles and promoting meaningful access, safety and dignity in humanitarian aid. This will be done through systematically taking into consideration protection risks and potential violation in the projects’ design and implementation through improved access to quality psychosocial and inclusive services such as peer to peer counseling and psychosocial support for the vulnerable population at the facility. Systematic dialogue engagement with beneficiaries, training both MG staff and partners on internal protection policies (PSEA, Child Protection, CoC) and strengthening feedback mechanisms will form part of this strategy

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

MG has a hotline, complaints forms, and a help desk to enable program participants to speak to staff in person. SMS messages (WHATSAPP), community meetings, and emails are used mainly by partners, particularly during project implementation and coordination between the clusters. MedGlobal also utilizes Suggestion boxes to enable anonymous complaints. Where informed consent is given, disclosure of information is restricted to those who need to know. Unauthorized disclosure of allegations constitutes misconduct. Staff are advised to refrain from making public statements on any proceedings. The complaint and feedback database can only be accessed by the accountability officer. In Closing the feedback loop the reported cases are discussed with a multidisciplinary team who act on the complaints and reach back to beneficiaries within two days. However, some complaints and feedback are acted upon immediately, including those associated with violations of the code of conduct, PSEA, and Child protection. MG is also a signatory and a member of the Joint Fed back Complaint and Response Mechanism Technical Working Group (FCRMs-TWG) in the camp, where consortia members and peer organizations share roles and responsibilities for feedback and complaints. MG will champion ongoing resource investment and management, which need to be articulated in budgets and work plans, and emphasize clear data management and data‑sharing protocols to ensure timely referrals of all relevant feedback and complaints to each organization and assurances of confidentiality in handling sensitive complaints.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

At the design stage, beneficiaries' needs were identified through participatory need assessments which formed the basis of this proposal. At the implementation stage, the project will be launched and implemented in a collaborative way with the beneficiaries to ensure feedback access and mutual understanding. All information and communication will be provided in accessible forms in line with the ‘furthest behind first’ principle. all patients seeking health they should be informed of type of services provided and which are for free - billboard should install at the site.. All feedback and reporting channels will be child and disability-friendly access to all. At the monitoring and evaluation stage, MG will interact with beneficiaries and seek their feedback on the provision of services and information, gaps, and needs, as part of accountability mechanisms and will use improved services. Reporting mechanisms include in-person reporting, inter-agency staff, phone hotlines, SMS, email, and secure suggestion/complaint boxes guided by four core principles of 1) confidentiality where MG limits access to the reported information; use code names for those involved and omitting identifiers from the incident reports and anonymous reporting, 2)Transparency achieved through obtaining prior informed consent of the complainant. MG shares confidentiality procedures with all complainants, including notice of MG’s obligations for mandatory reporting, 3)Accessibility achieved through making reporting mechanisms easy to use and 4) Security achieved through avoiding creating or exacerbating risks for those reporting allegations or concerns, as well as other parties involved. This involves training and strengthening of the referral of patient from our facility to the protection sites where they can be assisted
