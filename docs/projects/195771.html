Project Summary

This program is an ongoing intervention by SDH and its partners to contribute (securing operational costs for the centers and increasing coordination through the referral system) in reducing morbidity and mortality rates, improving comprehensive health services for conflict-affected Syrians, and providing equitable access to health care in order to support the care system. health in the targeted areas, as well as providing life-saving and sustainable humanitarian assistance.
The intervention will primarily focus on providing and improving primary health care and following up on malnutrition cases.
The program will provide basic health care services to a number of targeted districts for beneficiaries through supported health facilities in northern Syria.
This program consists of these projects:
1- Supporting Primary Health Care Centers (PHC):
- Afrin Primary Health Care Center B0203081 (the population of the region is 15,000).
- Primary Health Care Center in Bab Al-Hawa Camps G0703148 (a random gathering area for the camp with a population of 28,682 people) is large.
- Primary Care Center in Mhambel G0705035 (housing 37,000 people, including 18,000 displaced persons), it is the only medical center in Mhambel, and it provides primary care services to the residents of Mhambel and 11 villages in the vicinity of Mhambel.
- Ain Sheeb Health Center G0700175 (21,860 people, including 6,660 displaced persons). It should be noted that the nearest other medical center is 12 km away from Ain Sheeb.
(The plan is to fully support 4 primary health care centers so far in line with the basic health care package. These centers are distributed in Aleppo and Idlib, targeting: 102542 thousand Belgian francs / for men: 13900, for females: 35600, compared to males: 24300, for females: 28742).
2- Supporting field hospitals for families:
- Aphamea FH G0703170 in a harem (with the possibility of moving the site to cover the needs in the gap of the other site).
(The plan is to fully support the hospital at a fair operating cost to be able to provide an essential healthcare package, targeting: 60,000 BNFs / Men: 11,100, Females: 23,700, Boys: 7,300, Girls: 17,900) .
3- Supporting physiotherapy centers:
B020206 - Batbo Physical Rehabilitation Center.
- Martyr Mustafa Abed Center for Physical Rehabilitation B02207.
- Youth Center B0200112.
(The plan is to fully subsidize PCs at a fair operating cost to be able to provide a basic healthcare package, target: 13,000 BNFs / Men: 9,500, Females: 1,700, Boys: 1,000, Girls: 800).
The projects aim to maintain the continuity of work of the targeted health centers, by supporting the continuation of primary care services (children's clinic, inpatient clinic, and women's clinic) in the aforementioned areas, in addition to investigating and treating cases. For malnourished children (from 6 months to 59 months), breastfeeding and pregnant women. The project also aims to revitalize the Apamea Hospital, which provides natural and cesarean delivery services (two delivery rooms, two operating rooms, and 10 beds), a department that includes 4 incubators for premature babies, and a department that includes 10 beds for inpatients, in addition to outpatient clinics that provide primary care (medicine). pediatric, gynecological and inpatient),
In addition to supporting the continuation of physiotherapy and rehabilitation centers to follow up on providing rehabilitation sessions for cases of war injuries and rehabilitation after surgical operations (joint fitting, neurosurgery, and childbirth dislocation...), which effectively contribute to the reintegration of cases disability in society.
The project also aims to activate an awareness program to enhance awareness of the prevention of communicable, epidemic and contagious diseases, and how to increase pathological resistance to enhance community health and mitigate the effects of successive epidemics, and thus reduce diseases and deaths.

3 - Needs

According to 2022 Syria (HNO), there are 15.3 million people inside Syria in need of humanitarian assistance including 2.1 million displaced people living in IDPs of last resort, with needs growing across all sectors.The need for life-saving health assistance in 2023, an increase of 3.2 million from 2022 The protracted crisis in Syria has resulted in 203 sub-districts out of 270 sub-districts now classified as under severe, severe or catastrophic conditions. According to WoS HeRAMS 2022, 41% of hospitals are either non-functional or partially functioning, while 43% of primary healthcare (PHCs) are either partially or not functioning at all. A crippling economy, characterized by high inflation, currency depreciation and commodity price increases, remains one of the biggest drivers of need. In addition to the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic and frequent infections, the Syrian Ministry of Health declared a cholera outbreak in September 2022. The outbreak is spreading rapidly, and as of November 5, 2022, there were 33,567 suspected cases and 2,002 confirmed cases of cholera. In addition to high levels of chronic malnutrition, which is evident in the high prevalence of stunting; increasing levels of acute malnutrition; and emergency levels of anemia among children and women of childbearing age. This need is evident in the targeted areas, especially since the percentage of the displaced in them exceeds 70%, and the targeted centers are the only reliable ones in the targeted areas.

4 - Implementation of activities

In order to implement the proposed activity, SDH plans to support the existing staff of the proposed facility with SDH Syria Payroll.The line staff that will be supported are the health facility staff as all the mentioned health facilities are already operational. The field staff will be the SDH field team. The support team will be the health management team. The project activities will cover: 1. Improving access to primary, secondary and trauma health care through full support covering operating costs, rehabilitation, medicines and consumables: Activities: 1.1: Provide primary health care consultations to the IDPs and the host community. 1.2: Providing trauma services, including minor and major surgeries (especially obstetric cases) to all groups of society. 1.3: Providing medicines, supplies and equipment for hospital maintenance. (Free) 1.4: Follow up on cases of moderate and severe malnutrition without complications (children > 5 years, P & L women), providing them with nutritional supplements, and referring mixed cases to specialized hospitals. 1.2: Provide mental health and psychosocial support services to IDP camps (counselling, medication and other services). in the target areas. Monitoring and evaluation will be provided by the M&E department, through routine data collection, spot checks, KII & PDM, focusing on beneficiary satisfaction, areas for improvement, potential challenges, and access to services. Monthly 4W reports are shared for the respective sectors.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Violence against health facilities and health workers: Potential: High impact: High mitigation measures: - Applying a dispute resolution mechanism to health facilities. - Healthcare Attacks Tracking System (SSA), - Provide training on evacuation plan in case of accidents. 2- Risk-based violence, gender and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding and shared toilets as well as exploitation of patients by staff in exchange for service provision: Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigation measures: Establish separate toilets for men and women in the health facility - Ensure that waiting areas are sufficient for all patients - Allocate a dismissed person to regulate the flow of patients - Create an accessible complaints mechanism (complaints box, WhatsApp number, interviews) - Inform all employees of the workplace harassment prevention policy and include this policy in their contracts. 3- The contribution of medical centers to the spread of the cholera epidemic through the mixing of cholera patients with beneficiaries inside the medical center: Likelihood: high, effect: high mitigation measures: medical services for crises in isolation from the rest of the beneficiaries.4- Increased cases of malnutrition among children and the inability to secure nutritional supplements: possible: significant impact: high mitigation measures: - continuous coordination with the nutrition sector and implementing partners to meet the need for nutritional supplements and track referrals.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SDH is committed to protecting beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse, in compliance with PSEA standard policy and procedures. In the SDH new staff recruited will have to sign the CoC released by the PSEA network recently and will be fully oriented about PSEA and the available complaint mechanism. Awareness raising of the community about SEA will be a part of activities with full illustration of available compliant mechanism. PSEA focal points are responsible for mainstreaming PSEA into all departments and projects, through developing the measures and practices including feedback and compliant mechanism along with concerned departments. Posters and Banners raising awareness about PSAE will be used in all facilities.New modalities of PSEA raising awareness - due to COVID-19 crisis are followed, such as sending text messages to the beneficiaries, online training, using Banners in the distribution locations. The project ensures the community and beneficiaries' engagement in project designing, and planning since the beginning through the need’s assessments and so on during the next phases, and through targeting young girls and women with socially accepted activities including life skills. There will be multiple channels to report feedback and complaints privately and confidentially, including hotline number.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Assumptions: SDH will coordinate closely with the health clusters, as well as local authorities (including health directorate) and local NGOs operating inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, sending feedback to the cluster when interventions are needed to be addressed. Risk: an emergency status occur (increase the #COVID-19 cases "the second wave"). Mitigation: SDH will use its coordination channels for developing a coordinated response plan with the partners. Risk: The close of borders which prevent the shipments of supplies and medications to access to facilities. mitigation: Develop a strong sustainable strategy for accessing and deliver goods through local actors. •Assumptions: health facilities remains accessible for the SDH team and for the beneficiaries. Risk: The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities. mitigation: SDH through the S&S team has developed a strong risk management strategy for emergency situations. •Assumptions: currency exchange rates remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned activities. Risk: high volatility of exchange currency. mitigation: fixing the operational cost inside Syria in USD. •Assumptions: the medical supplies & medicines & PEE are provided to the supported HFs. Risk: UNSCR wasn't renewed, So, interruption of WHO supply line as well as the supply chain from Turkey to Syria. mitigation: SDH has a strong supply chain based on Turkish & Syrian companies that move the commodities.
