Project Summary

Through this project, WFP will provide cash and food support to 51,000 vulnerable people (mostly refugees) in the East, Adamaoua and North regions, in line with its Country Strategic Plan for Cameroon (CSP 2022-2026). Targeting will be refined in parallel with physical verifications to ensure that only extremely food-insecure households are assisted. The project proposes to continue providing vital support to extremely vulnerable Central African refugee households to ensure that they are able to meet their immediate food security and nutrition needs, as well as their basic needs.
The modality for transferring food aid to beneficiaries will be mostly cash (CBT) through unconditional monthly cash distributions to support vulnerable households' access to adequate and nutritious food, thus preventing them from adopting negative coping practices. The monthly cash transfer ration is 9,000 FCFA per person to beneficiaries who are entirely dependent on humanitarian food aid. A further 40% of beneficiaries will be assisted in-kind via monthly food distributions. The full ration of the in-kind distributions will provide 2,100 kcal energy per day per person for the beneficiaries who entirely rely on humanitarian food assistance The daily food rations will comprise 370g cereals, 120g pulses 40g vegetable, and 5g iodized salt. Mechanisms will be put in place to assess the evolution of household needs to ensure a gradual approach from one form of assistance to another.
During project implementation, regular monitoring activities will be carried out during food distribution, and monthly data will be collected against performance indicators for reporting through WFP's COMET system. In line with WFP's responsibility, affected populations, targeted vulnerable IDPs and local populations will be closely involved in the various stages of the project, including the targeting process. The people concerned will be regularly informed of project activities and plans. Through complaints and feedback mechanisms, they will freely express their opinions and concerns about project implementation. In line with WFP's responsibility to the affected populations, beneficiaries' complaints are treated with respect, and their opinions are considered in project adjustments. The WFP, in collaboration with its partners, will carry out assessments to identify and update the list of the most vulnerable IDPs and local populations to be considered in projects, based on vulnerability criteria.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The socio-political crisis that has been raging in CAR since March 2003 has forced many Central Africans to seek refuge in Cameroon. The country currently hosts around 354,523 Central African refugees (OCHA and UNHCR report, January 2024). In 2024, there were an estimated 260,392 refugees in the East (58%), Adamaoua (23%) and North (19%) regions. Since 2005, the East has regularly seen large influxes of refugees following major political events in the Central African Republic, with the largest waves in 2008 and 2014 (over 107,000 people). This situation has continued to exacerbate pressure on natural resources, particularly land and foodstuffs, stimulating conflicts between herders and farmers. Insecurity and crime have also increased, with more regular cases of kidnapping and theft. Despite the results achieved on the ground, the multifaceted assistance provided by the Cameroonian government and humanitarian actors is still far from optimal for ensuring sustainable food security and meeting the basic needs of this population. This is due to a number of factors, including persistent insecurity in CAR (resulting in a steady influx of new refugees) and multiple global crises that have displaced donors to other parts of the world. This situation has led to a decrease in resources for Central African refugees in Cameroon. According to the March 2024 “Harmonized Framework” exercise produced by the government and its partners in the 10 regions, some 487,349 people are in phase 3 of the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) in the East (14%), Adamaoua (85%) and North (68%) regions.The World Food Programme (WFP) and its cooperating partners will support the Government of Cameroon in strengthening food security in the Far North region.Main activities include:- Food distribution: WFP will distribute food to vulnerable populations affected by food insecurity.- Cash transfer: Cash transfers will be made to enable beneficiaries to purchase food and other essential goods.These activities will be implemented over a 12-month period and will cover around 51,000 people from vulnerable households in the East (31500), Adamawa (12500), and North (7000) regions. The WFP already has a strong presence in Cameroon, with initiatives such as cash transfer programs for vulnerable people.In addition, the WFP is working closely with the government of Cameroon to strengthen food security and community resilience. These efforts are crucial to meeting the needs of vulnerable populations in the East, Adamawa, and North regions.
