Project Summary

This project aiming to contribute to achieve main objective that are:
Support livelihoods of affected communities and households by increasing agricultural production, protection of productive assets, and restoring or creating income generating activities to prevent negative or irreversible coping mechanisms for 900HHs in Aleppo and Idleb governorates.
Main targets:
1- Improved the socio-economic status in targeted areas for 900 HHs in 2020 season.
2- Increased sustainable livelihood opportunities in Aleppo and Idleb areas for 900 HHs during 2020 season.
This project by the two implementation methodologies will contribute in create work opportunities, Ahl Horan Association (AHA) can summary the opportunities as following:
1-The first methodology depends on cultivate one dunum with summer vegetables so each farmer will get profits by selling their products in the market at the end of the harvest season which allowed them to have a source of income to plant their lands for the next season and AHA will select 600 local farmer whom their lands are near their houses with no affordability to agricultural supplies, financial resources or guidance in cultivating their lands. 30% of beneficiaries will be from the families who headed by women, additional that AHA will depend on the specific criteria in selection the BNFs, the prioritized will be for the families that have children, pregnant, disabled, and the families that have elderlies.
2- The other methodology is to distribute 4 sheep with fodder enough for seven months, also veterinarian’s support will be available along seven months. So, in regards of the beneficiaries for this methodology, and depending on the criteria and prioritized that mentioned above. AHA will select 300 internal displaced people who fled previously form their houses towards the northern Syria., AHA will increase skills and knowledges by conduct practical and theory trainings.

4 - Needs

Based on ACAPS project, The conflict had killed more than 475,000 people, including 106,000 civilians, and caused waves of displacement and wide-ranging concerns about the protection of the population is the highest on a large scale within Syrian territory. The estimated number of people living below the poverty line with 85% of the total population, have passed more than six years of war, and GDP shrank more than two-thirds. The unemployment rate of 57%. More than 50% of households are buying food, and at a higher rate for the displaced and internally displaced persons. Besieged areas have the highest percentage of households that are used to buy food and other negative coping strategies. Livestock numbers declined sharply since 2011: the number of cattle has dropped by 30% and of sheep by 40% and poultry by 50%. Animal feed prices have risen by 168%. based on UN official statistics and statistics from ACAPS project. The proposed locations in Afrin and Jandairis subdistricts are one of the locations considered as level 4 of severity as by HNO severity scale. As per our need assessment currently being conducted in which showed that more than 34% of the population are IDPs with more than 50% of people in need for food and livelihood assistance. General overview of Food Security An estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity based on HNO 2019. One out of every three Syrians in debt as a result of their inability to buy food. Of the 8.7 million people in need, in less than 6.3 million people suffer from the loss of livelihood assets, and resorting to negative adjustment or coping strategies (such as the sale of assets). The remaining 2.4 million people are able to maintain a minimum of adequate food consumption only by engaging in negative coping strategies, and if there is no help, they will drop down to the most dangerous class in terms of living conditions.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption the security situation remains steady the the time of impelementing th project. risk the situation deteriorate in the area mitigation: since targeted location is in Afrin which is controled by the Turksih government it is unlikely for the security to deteriorate in this area. Assumption 2 – Access to Syria remains possible and safe from the neighboring countries. Risks: A change of government policy/attitude in Turkey could lead to a closure of the borders or and/or negatively impact on humanitarian access to Syria Mitigation strategy: As a registered NGO in Turkey, AHA is expecting that this legal framework will, at best, give the leeway to operate. If the general attitude towards cross-border humanitarian assistance should change. Assumption 3 – The activities can be completed without delay Risk: As the activities will be implemented during the rainy season, AHA might face difficulties accessing the area. Mitigation strategy: AHA has anticipated such situation could happen and as a consequence, included the risk of not being able to access the communities due to heavy raining or flooding by designing the work plan of the activities. Assumption 4 – Markets are functioning properly in the area identified Risk: Due to the unpredictable situation, there is a risk markets are not functioning properly due to lack of access and/ or supplies. Mitigation strategy: AHA teams have confirmed the market is functioning and the security situation allows the implementation of the project. The situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis by the program team. Assumption 5: The project does not create distortions in the local economy Risk: As consequence of the project, there is a shortage of materials in the area and the prices increase. Mitigation strategy: contractors will be identified at the initial stage of the project and provided with necessary information.
