Project Summary

UN Women, will work together with other UN Agencies to contribute to long-term changes in relation to protection, with a focus on protection from Gender Based Violence, including : (i) support and engage the communities in the response to support access to lifesaving information and services, (ii) provision and expansion of quality of services with a focus on essential health services, mental health and psychosocial care for persons in heightened risks, in particular women and adolescent girls (iii) social cohesion prioritized and strengthened between refuges and affected host communities.
A major goal of UN Women's contribution to the joint intervention will be to address targeted life-saving and survival needs specific to women and adolescent girls, particularly female headed households, elderly women, single mother, widow, and adolescent girls and families without adult males. One strategy to address these needs is the creation of more demand for services through the setup and running of Multi-Purpose Women's Centres (MPWCs). The MPWCs provide integrated services to women and adolescent girls from life-saving information (WASH, nutrition, health, GBV risks) and services (psycho-social and health counselling). To reach out to women who are restricted from coming to the centres (e.g. elderly, disabled and otherwise socially/culturally restricted in mobility), the centres provide orientation through community outreach members. Beside services through MPWCs UN Women will support to secondary health care facilities/referral services that provide mental health and psychosocial care to the women and adolescent girls from both refugee and host communities. In order to address the impact of the influx on host communities and governance institutions, the intervention also aimed at women and girls among the Rohingya refugees and Bangladeshi host communities, this includes women and men serving in Bangladeshi police, and individuals and organizations providing services to survivors and those at risk of GBV.

Needs

An estimated 725,000 Rohingya, approximately 56% of which are women and girls, have fled from Myanmar to Cox’s Bazar, the popullation, while the total population of Cox's Bazar district increased by 50% in less than 3 months. A significant number of the women and girls have either personally survived or witnessed incidences of sexual assault, rape or murder of a close family or neighbours in Myanmar and live with the trauma. The camps in Cox’s Bazar are not safe for them either. The Gender Based Violence (GBV) Sub Sector reports that hundreds of GBV incidents are reported every week. Practices such as purdah , is restricting the mobility of women and their access to life-saving assistance, services and information. Women and girls and especially the most vulnerable and marginalized among them, from female headed households, elderly, disabled, adolescent girls or GBV survivors, are experiencing additional access barriers to overstretched relief services. The lack of life saving skills, income generating and portable skills development opportunities has catalysed the exploitation of adolescent girls and women in the form of trafficking for commercial sexual exploitation, forced marriage, survival sex, drug smuggling and forced labour. Fear of abduction, harassment and sexual violence severely leads to limited movement of women and girls, which in turn hampers their access to essential services, information, and support networks. However, Rohingya women have little confidence in the ability of police and camp authorities to respond to their needs, and many women fear they will be arrested (or deported to Myanmar), if they report a crime in Bangladesh.Resentment of host community towards refugees is rising linked to competition for resources, services and opportunities. Domestic violence is a challenge for host community women also, and police and legal-aid agencies report that incidents have recently increased. This is due to heightened frustrations within households, including from husbands taking vulnerable Rohingya women as second wives. Local leaders and the police often seek to resolve cases of domestic violence through mediation, in many cases without training on GBV and domestic abuse. There is urgent need for robust interventions, a gender-sensitive, protective, environmentally sustainable, and comprehensive response must take into account all refugees in Cox’s Bazar and their host communities, by addressing the gaps on services provided by all sectors, especially by Camp in Charges (CiCs), Police, mental health, health service providers etc. and enhance the access and improve the quality of responses and prevention services for individuals, with focus on those at heightened risk of GBV. Such a response is key to saving lives and mitigating tensions both within Rohingya communities as well as between refugees and host communities. The participatory elections that UNHCR piloted in June 2018 resulted in women being elected for half of the leadership positions and there is a plan to roll out these elections across all camps. According to reports, the elected women are facing intimidation from the Madjis, and the community . This demonstrates the need to prepare women to seize the opportunity, since they have not received any training to effectively carry out their leadership responsibilities. The strong collaboration between the Protection Sector (including the GBV and CP Sub-sectors), PSEA Task Force and GIHA WG need to be esablished in order to mainstream gender to this response, that will be solved by establishment of Gender Hub.

Activities or outputs

Through eight Multi purpose Women's Centres -two existing, one that will be built in 2018 in camp 5 and five to be built in 2019 ( the locations will be determinded in close consultation with the CiCs, and Site Manegment) the information on health, sanitation, nutrition, personal hygiene, disaster preparations, trafficking, child marriage, domestic violence and other forms of violence against women, livelihood and learning activities in collaboration with Livelihood sector; also information on relief and services provided by government and non-government agencies. In addition the basic first aid psycho-social counseling and referral to GBV services offered by GBV sub-sector partners for furtherer clinical management. MPWCs will be used as a shelter for rohingya refugees in case of natural disaster. Help desk to collect complains of women including SEA cases will be established, and regulral meetings between CiCs and women will be established. the SEA cases will be reported to PSEA network. Enhanced capacities of CiCs through trainings and equipped at least 8 CiCs to address women’s needs by placing Bangladeshi and Rohingya women as their community advocacy officers, in order to outreach Rohingya refugee women and adolescent girls. Law enforcement officers assigned to work in camps, the community advocacy officers will be trained in sensitively handling cases violence against women involving vulnerable women, including referral to legal aid providers, health workers and local authority. Police, Health workers, Women's Development Forum members, and community leaders to support the women at risk Host community and Rohingya and victims of violence will be supported through training, outreach, and other forms of awareness­ raising interventions. Implementation locations for activities will be in the five most affected Unions Raja Palong, Palong Khali, Nhilla, Whykong, and Baherchara. Joint awareness raising for host community and Rohingya refugee women and girls on protection from GBV, PSEA, self- management and participation and leadership as well as the Joint activites will be supported. A task force/ working group on women’s empowerment with the key responders working with women and girls in the camps including CiCs, Site managment sector, UNHCR and IOM will be established under the leadership of UN Women to develop an action plan for support and capacity development of women potential leaders in the camps. A leadership and governance training module will be developed by UN Women and will be finalize jointly with the Task Force and delivered in all camps in a period of 6 months. Consultations with men and community mobilization activities will be organized to build male and community support for women’s participation in decision making and activities outside of their shelters. The project activites will be constantly consulted with GIHA, GBV, PSEA and Protection and other sectors through the Gender Hub that at the time of writing the JRP, it was not established, but it will play a key role in coordinating issues on gender across the response.
