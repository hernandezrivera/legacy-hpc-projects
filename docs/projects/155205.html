Project Summary

This project aims to address the health needs of Rohingya refugees and host community and will be implementing following activities reaching 405,068 patients.
5 Comprehensive Primary Health Care Centers providing 24/7 health services
● 9 Health Posts providing services during day time and 30 community health outlets providing referral and basic health services
● In addition, 30 community health outlet will provide basic health services ( counseling, referral, and , ORS)
The facilities will provide primary health care service according to the essential primary health care services packages (ESP)with special focus on sexual reproductive health, maternal, neonatal, child health, and adolescent health, non-communicable diseases and Mental health psychosocial services in addition to the regular and routine services such as Outpatient departments, Inpatient departments, Child health, EPI, Nutrition, non-communicable diseases, Referral services, Information Management, Waste management and infection control.
5 Primary health care center and 9 Health posts are functional to a more durable, permanent and sustainable structures operational.
● 1,200,000 Medical Consultation provided in a year desegregated by sex, age provided to Rohingya (60 % are female and 40% males)
● 3,600 deliveries safely conducted by skilled birth attendant
● 36,000 family planning services provided
● 14 health facilities have at least one staff trained on Mental Health Gap Action Program
● 14 health facilities have at least one staff trained on clinical management of rape.
● 14 health facilities providing care ( screening, diagnosing, labs, treatment) of non communicable diseases such as diabetes, hypertension, and heart diseases.
● 14 health facilities providing a comprehensive mixture of family planning methods at least four short methods(pills, injectable, condom) and one Long Acting Method (Implant, IUD).
● 300 Community health worker and volunteers trained (75 % are female)
● 2000 group awareness session conducted focusing especially on women, reaching 36,000 participants (60% of the participants are women).
● 5 Ambulances functional and available 24/7
● All health professionals (Doc,Para,MW,HW) will be trained on Basic First Aid and Public Health in Emergencies.

Objectives

• Improve equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for crisis-affected populations with special focus on sexual reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support; and non-communicable diseases. • Ensure the prevention and timely response to outbreaks of diseases with epidemic potential and prepare for other health emergencies including monsoon and cyclone.• Encourage healthy living, improve health seeking behaviour and utilisation of essential service package among refugees and host populations through community engagement, with special attention to gender considerations and vulnerable groups.• Strengthen health sector coordination and information management with a focus on rational and accountable health service delivery systems, including monitoring of minimum standards and the quality of the services provided.

Needs

"The needs of the population are changing from acute emergency to more regular health needs the situation remains very critical and major gaps remain evident and unaddressed. The majority of health facilities are temporary structures (bamboo and tents), which is not ideal as they are at a risk of flooding and landslide and require continues maintenance and not sustainable. The availability and accessibility of 24/7 health services remains a challenge as less than 10% of the health facilities offer around the clock essential services with a ratio of 1:50,000 beneficiary per Primary health care centre compared to the standard ratio 1:25,000 beneficiary per primary health care center. Furthermore, health services across the camps and NGOs are not standardized and fail to adhere to the essential primary health care services packages (ESP), such as sexual reproductive services, mental health and non-communicable disease services which results in patients receiving varying degree of quality health assistance. The access to services is overall more restricted for women, one of the reasons may include limited number of female staff and that services are not segregated. Women’s mobility is generally controlled by men and they require a man’s permission to visit a health center. It is therefore likely that access to family planning is still controlled by men. low utilization of sexual and reproductive health services is observed and is reflective of challenges in access and availability of services, Women continue to deliver at home instead of facility based deliveries, it is estimated that only 32% of total deliveries take place in a health facility and are attended by a skilled birth attendant. Additionally, only 28% of health facilities provide appropriate short/long acting family planning methods. Also, needs for high-risk and special need populations including sex workers, survivors of gender based violence and adolescents are yet to be addressed. The violence experienced by the refugees prior and during the exodus and the emotional distress of being forcefully uprooted from their home and separated from family has resulted in the need mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS). However, such services are still limited with only 13% had access to mental health care. Non-communicable disease care such as hypertension and diabetes, including diagnostics, treatments and continuum of care. Community engagement activities faces many challenges due to language and culture barriers unclear messaging and overlapping in health services and activities Although, much progress has been made in increasing the immunization coverage (89% have been immunized), the program needs to be strengthened and expanded to protect the population from potential outbreaks. Disaster preparedness and Response need to be addressed by trained health professionals."
