Project Summary

The project will be directly implemented by German NGO Welthungerhilfe (WHH). Welthungerhilfe is implementing 2019 1st standard allocation TUR-19/3559/SA1/FS-WASH/INGO/12813 coded and 2019 2nd allocation TUR-19/3559/SA2/FS/INGO/14507 coded projects in the camps of A'zaz. In addition to that, WHH implemented a FSL project in the same locations. This project shall be complementing the ongoing project by filling the livelihood needs of camps.
The need to incorporate the support of sustainable livelihoods, while at the same time increasing food production to decrease dependency on emergency response activities, is important for a number of reasons. First, to empower local communities and to foster a sense of self-reliance among population during conflict time. Second, which is to guarantee access of food in these areas. As local communities grow, sell and purchase local food, local economies will be stimulated, creating a multiplier effect to expand number of beneficiaries. Further, local populations will have better access to nutritious foods, as part of the proposed project involves providing seeds for vegetables in A'zaz. These areas are in particular need for FSL activities. WHH will be able to implement these activities as a result of gained experience in the same field. After consultations with relevant camp managers and other stakeholders, it is understood that the importance of shifting away from emergency response interventions and toward sustainable food security and livelihood activities shall be better for the people living in A'zaz camps. At the initial phase 500 potential farmers shall be assisted but 5% of them shall be trained at the Farmer Field School. Each selected trainee shall receive 100$ allowance. Afterwards, these 25 people shall act as focal point and expert for the rest of the beneficiaries. Each one shall be responsible for monitoring and training of the 20 farmers. People with previous home-gardening activity shall be prioritized. Afterward, 500 male/female farmers shall receive agricultural inputs, agricultural arable lands and technical support in order to produce summer vegetables for house consumption. The land shall be rented in the neighborhood of camps by WHH. Yet, the assistance shall be in two phases. In the first phase 60$ worth agriculture voucher shall be provided to men and women farmers based on criteria. In that phase, targeted people shall have the option to buy agricultural inputs, seeds or fertilizers. There will be a flexibility to choose for the people. In order to get the second tranche of the assistance, they have to fulfill their tasks. The first phase shall be monitored by a committee of field staff and trainees. Afterwards, the tranche of the voucher shall be released. 500 households are expected to benefit from this activity.

4 - Needs

According to WHH's assessments from July 2019, about 450,000 people reside in the districts of Northern Aleppo (Azzaz, Al Bab, Jarablus), 150,000 of them are IDPs from other regions in conflict-torn Syria, about one-third (50,000) reside in camps. Azzaz is the district with the highest number of IDPs. The Turkish authorities directly addressed WHH with regard to the high needs in the region. Three rapid assessments have been conducted by WHH in A’zaz, Aleppo governorate, between the dates 19 December 2018 – 01 November 2019. The main objective of the assessments was to gain an overview of the humanitarian situation in the informal camps in the targeted areas. The assessments focused on specific sectors which are food security, NFI and WASH based on the scope of WHH activities. Key Informant Interviews have been conducted in 23 informal tented settlements in A’zaz, and in 6 central retailer-shops in A’zaz.According to the recently conducted rapid needs assessment on the 1st of November 2019, people living in camps namely Al-Ryan, Al-Resalah and Tal-Jbeine mainly prone to unemployment problems. The majority of the people are working as daily workers and earning generally less than 3 $ per day.

5 - Implementation of activities

Initially, 25 Male/female farmers are trained practically through Field Farmers School on Good Agricultural practices for winter vegetables crop. The selection of these people will be depending on eagerness and previous knowledge about agriculture practices. After being trained, they will be disseminating their knowledge to the rest of the farmers.At the second phase of the project implementation; 500 male/female A'zaz camp residents receive agricultural vouchers and technical support in order to produce winter vegetables for house consumption. WHH is also providing lease support for the arable land as well. In order to control the suggested activities, support shall be provided in two tranches.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

WHH has a program participant protection policy (P4) and requires all project participants including partners, suppliers and staff to understand and comply with this policy. The policy ensures that all project participants understand the power imbalances and the potential protection risk from implementing projects to help people affected by crisis and conflict. The P4 policy also clearly states the expected behavior of staff, the staff of partner organizations and suppliers in relation to the people affected by crisis and conflict.WHH is committed to mainstream protection into all of the projects and does so through the use several mechanisms. These mechanisms include the creation of feedback and complaint mechanisms for projects, capacity building of all staff in relation to protection and mainstreaming protection throughout all phases of implementation and creating a work environment that encourages the consultation and active participation of women and children throughout programming. Collection of beneficiary information is kept internal and only shared at the request of beneficiaries. Furthermore, WHH's policy of documentation, including photography and videography, strongly considers protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity.The project is designed to contribute significantly to gender equality. Improving the resilience of female-headed households and IDP households is one of the major objectives of this project. Protection needs to be seen as a cross-cutting issue and protection outcomes are also considered in this project. Home gardening activity can have not only positive protection outcomes, but also for cross-cutting issues such as gender, age and disability. Within the vulnerability criteria, households having women headed households, PLW, disabled people shall be able to benefit from the assistance. Since Welthungerhilfe has a wide range of NGO network, it can do either referral or send referral to other peer partners.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: People received agricultural vouchers shall be using for summer vegetables productionRisk 1:Vouchers may be exchanged for cash at a lower valueMitigation factor 1: Agricultural support shall be divided into two tranches. Those people who do not comply with the planned activities shall not be provided with second tranche.Field team and the trained farmers shall be closely monitoring the activities.Assumption 2: People can reach arable land with no difficulty at all. Risk 2:Rented lands could be very far away from the campsMitigation factor 2: If the rented land is far away from the camps, transportation support shall be provided to the targeted people.Assumption 3: People can exchange agricultural inputs from the contracted vendors at an agreed pricesRisk 3:Vendors may try to charge more than the agreed list of pricesMitigation factor 3: Different monitoring mechanisms shall be pursued like ghost buyers.Field team shall be closely monitoring the prices and the redemption process.Assumption 4: The goods shall be received by the targeted groups as planned.Risk 4: Delays in delivering the goods from the contracted vendor.Mitigation factor 4: All agreements with the vendors will be implemented in frame of Welthungerhilfe's policy and procedures. Welthungerhilfe will take every precautionary measure in order to avoid any delays in receiving the items.Assumption 5: Welthungerhilfe staff work honestly during agriculture voucher distribution/redemption activity.Risk 5: Staff shall be CorruptedMitigation Factors 5:- We will be open with the community about the project.- Use clearly verifiable beneficiary targeting criteria and check beneficiary list.- Work closely with the camp managers and sector managers.- Segregation of duties.
