Project Summary

The revision of the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2019 sees addressing the needs related to displacement as a priority alongside food security and health. IOM participates actively in the Camp Coordination and Camp Management/Non-Food Items/Shelter (CCCM/NFI/Shelter) Cluster and coordinate activities closely.
Chad’s Lac province has been directly affected by the ongoing conflict with Boko Haram. As of October 2019, 169 003 internally displaced persons (IDPs), 47,364 returnees (either former IDPs or Chadians who returned from Nigeria and Niger) and 5,652 third-country nationals (from Cameroon, Niger, Mali and Nigeria) lived in the three most affected departments of the Lac province either in camps and informal settlements, or in host communities. People are forced to flee their homes very quickly during periods of more intense fighting, leaving behind assets, food and other belongings. There is a consensus among humanitarian agencies involved in the cluster that emergency shelter and NFI assistance is a high priority Humanitarian needs among these populations remain high and not all of the sites have so far been reached with humanitarian assistance due to security issues and related access problems. On October 2019, IOM Emergency Tracking Tool estimated 47,000 new displaced people in the Lake region because of the intensification of Boko Haram activities in the first 6 months of 2019.
Southern provinces of Chad have been affected by heavy rains and consequent flood during the months of August and September 2019. IOM Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT), as product of the wider DTM department, tracked the movement of around 2700 individuals who are now displaced in host villages or displacement sites (ETT #24, #25 and #27). At same time, in August 2019, IOM Goré received the request of assistance from leaders of Kobiteye villages (Nya Pendé department) as floods destroyed an important number of houses. These displaced people are in need of Shelter/NFI and other kind of assistance.
During the same period of 2019, IOM ETTs also tracked the movement of around 6000 individuals caused by intercommunal conflict between farmer and herders. The ETT #21 reported 5200 people displaced in the Ouaddai province (Ouara department). The INGO Premiere Urgence Internationale, following IOM ETT alert, conducted a partial rapid evaluation on the affected sites (limited access on all displaced sites because of ongoing rainy season), retrieving 2000 displaced people in need of Shelter/NFI and other humanitarian assistance. Few days after this movement, another group of 1000 people were intercepted by IOM #23 ETT as fleeing intercommunal conflicts in the Moyen Chari province (Bahr-Köh department). To date, no intervention has been reported.
Humanitarian needs also exist among stranded migrants in the north-northwest of the country after military operations that started in early 2018 and continue on 2019 with the aim of evacuate goldmines areas on the Tibesti, who find themselves without any assistance and are extremely vulnerable to become victims of traffickers or smugglers. IOM already assisted around 3000 people in 2019 (600 only between September and november 2019) when more waves of movements are expected to reach Faya in the next months.
IOM suggests the following priority measures:
Lac and Soutern provinces: provision of 3000 emergency and 150 semi-permanent shelter and distribution of 3500 NFIs for the most vulnerable persons among the above mentioned target groups and host communities.
Northen provinces: Assistance to 3000 stranded migrants coming from Libya and goldmine areas as emergency food, NFI and emergency transportation.

Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

Shelter & NFI (Outcome 1)To improve the housing situation of displaced populations, IOM Chad suggests the following activities:• Delivery of 3000 shelter kits for displaced populations and inhabitants of host communities;• Construction of 150 semi-permanent shelters for displaced populations and inhabitants of host communities with labor from these communities;• Sensitization on home economics for beneficiaries involved in shelter construction.• Coordination at the level of the CCCM/Shelter/NFI Cluster. Needs in terms of NFIs among the displaced populations are related to any sort of household items which may have been left behind when they left their homes. Therefore, household kits, kitchen kits and hygiene kits will be distributed. Depending on the development of the security situation IOM will include sites that have so far not been reached with NFIs. NFIs will also be distributed to vulnerable households in host communities.• IOM, thus, suggests distributing 3500 NFIs to households of displaced persons and host communities. Emergency assistance to stranded migrants (Outcome 3)IOM seeks to provide the following assistance to 3,000 stranded Chadian migrants:• Registration will start upon referral from local authorities that usually reach IOM with formal requests. Stranded people in Faya and/or Zouarké will be registered by the registration team. The registration will be done together with CNARR and Chadian Red Cross. The CNARR will identify at first stage refugees among the target and refer directly to HCR while TCNs seeking return assistance will be refereed to IOM’s Assisted Voluntary Return and Reintegration (AVRR) AVR program. • In second place, a visual “fit to travel” verification prior departure will be done by the IOM nurse. It will be needed in order to ensure de health status of travelers to undertake the journey. If a migrant is identified as needing medical attention, the person will be referred to the local hospital through the Red Cross.• During the registration, protection aspects will be duly considered. The project team will be including one Social Action Ministry official that will follow up on any case that should arise.• Once the person has been registered the stranded migrant will receive the appropriate response, which can include the provision of emergency food and NFIs (blankets, cloths, hygiene kit, collapsible water container, foldable mattress, flash lamp among other survival items);• Onward transportation to the hub closest to beneficiaries’ destination and cash assistance for onward journey.
