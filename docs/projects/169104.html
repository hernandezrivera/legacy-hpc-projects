Project Summary

In line with the FS sector-specific objectives, ACTED designed programming which aims to provide life-saving food security as well resilience livelihoods support to the individuals and entities most affected by the port-explosion to complement the ongoing/planned food security assistance across the most affected locations with pre-existing vulnerabilities. Tapping into the overall coordination mechanisms (WFP coverage of food security needs / UNDP led cash for work programming) this programme proposes area-based and punctual assistance to the estimated 21,375 beneficiaries (estimated 10,140 women and 11,235 men) most in need via:
a) cash-based assistance to an estimated 3,650 target HH beneficiaries;
b) cash for work intervention to support HH income and subsistence in the early recovery stage for an estimated 545 individuals (50% adult women and men participation);
c) in-kind/cash provision for the 80 businesses in the food sector directly impacted by explosions.
This program aims to tackle immediate income gaps as a result of the explosion, such as basic income support for highly vulnerable affected HHs to replaced disrupted livelihood activities and cover punctual needs resulting from the explosion that are not covered by other response activities (emergency cash approach), as well as substituting lost livelihoods for affected individuals through supporting ongoing local initiatives with subsidized labour, namely focusing on solid waste management/recycling, front line psychological first aid/PSS support and reconstruction efforts, and finally immediate cash support to affected businesses to enable the following types of businesses to recover and resume activity: i)businesses providing essential goods and services, including those necessary to respond to the disaster ii) subsistence micro-businesses with replacing the need for direct HH cash support and iii) business providing employment for a number of vulnerable individuals to allow them to regain their livelihoods. As FSS co-chair and previous Basic Assistance co-chair, ACTED will ensure each form of cash programming will align with sector harmonized packages and adhere to sector guidance to prevent generating conflict due to lack of harmonization of assistance. Building on an extensive environmental portfolio and history of experience working on solid waste management, including supporting equipment and technical support to sorting facilities, conducting municipal training and peer to peer exchange for improved waste systems (including secondment of SWM technical staff to Beirut Municipality following the explosion) and operationalizing decentralized waste schemes such as the Live Love Recycle program, ACTED will also ensure that programs serve to reinforce environmental management efforts in partnership or collaboration with key actors. Given that ACTED has a long-standing history of providing cash and livelihoods assistance in the Greater Beirut area, ACTED will identify beneficiaries of cash grants through ongoing and complementary programming which engages vulnerable HHs, notably in shelter, protection and women’s empowerment and livelihoods. ACTED will apply a protection lens to the selection of beneficiaries to further prioritize socio-economic needs.
ACTED has been closely coordinating with all sectors in response to the Beirut explosion and has been on the ground responding to critical needs since August 5th. To date, ACTED distributed 1064 core relief items (including sealing off kits, dignity and baby kits) and removing over 1232 tons of debris from the streets and HHs of Beirut. Since 2013 ACTED assisted in a total of 14,137 households (88,922 individuals) in 14 neighbourhoods of Beirut with activities including Emergency wash and shelter rehabilitation, provision of unconditional and conditional multi-purpose cash assistance, technical and financial assistance to MSMEs, CSOs, and institutions, providing vocational training to more than 500 individuals.

1 - Needs

The destruction of Beirut Port and accompanying structures have had a direct effect on the country’s food availability. Lebanon’s grain silos, holding up to 120,000 metric tonnes, were destroyed. Lebanon depends heavily on imports, including food supply. Before the explosions, the country imported 80% of its wheat, most through Beirut Port. Estimates suggest that four to six weeks of grain supply reserves remain. Food security was a cause of serious concern before the crisis. Since October 2019, hyper price inflation has adversely affected access to food for many households. The World Bank estimates that up to one million people are living below the poverty line and 45% of the Lebanese population at risk of sliding into poverty. The risk for refugees, migrants and other non-Lebanese communities is even higher. The explosions affected many neighbourhoods across Greater Beirut, including several with poor, migrant workers and refugee communities. The area surrounding the port is densely populated, with a high concentration of Lebanese, Syrian and Palestinian households living in some of the most vulnerable urban neighbourhoods of Lebanon’s capital, just a few kilometres away from the blast site. Some of the most heavily impacted areas include the municipality of Bourj Hammoud, and in particular the highly vulnerable neighbourhoods of Nabaa, Arax, Maraache and Adana, located north-east of Beirut in el Metn district characterized by a chaotic urban structure, with shelters built haphazardly and not connected to the government’s services where a high concentration of Syrian refugees have settled; Karantina, a low-income commercial and semi-industrial neighbourhood lying east of the Port of Beirut, adjacent to the North-South highway; and Basta el Tahta, a traditional working-class neighbourhood close to the centre of Beirut which hosts most of the city’s second-hand markets, and where most residents have been living for generations in colonial buildings. Many of these communities have limited resources to reconstruct damaged shelters or purchase food and non-food items. According to ACTED’s Rapid Needs Assessment (https://reliefweb.int/sites/reliefweb.int/files/resources/Lebanon_Beirut%20Blast_Rapid%20Needs%20Assessment%20Report_August%202020_final.pdf), the amount of food intake since the disaster is reportedly decreasing, with the majority of all surveyed area’s populations in need of food assistance (70% in Bourj Hammoud, 70-90% in Karm el Zaytoun and 80% in Karantine). This finding is echoed by HelpAge International , according to which 49% of the 802 surveyed people in the affected areas reported the need for food assistance (with 71% Syrian refugees, and 43% elderly expressing this need). Similarly, according to WVI, the majority (59.3%) of respondents expressed the need for food. This compounded with the disruptions in the core supply chains will have a long term effect on the livelihoods and subsistence of the local populations and markets. To gain an understanding of the impact of the explosion on the local markets and businesses, ACTED in coordination with 7 Livelihoods actors conducted a rapid assessment with 190 local businesses in Karantina, Basta el Tahta and Bourj Hammoud (data assessment finalised and triangulation ongoing). While no assumption can be drawn before finalisation of the report, preliminarly findings show that ex. out of 16 businesses assessed in Karantina only, one was operational. Main damages include wall/roof damages, loss of stock, lack of water/electricity. As expected these damages have either completely stopped or seriously impaired operations for these businesses, majority of which directly employees staff. Most of the assessed entities were already struggling before the explosion due to Lebanon’s multiple political, economic, and COVID 19 crises. These MSMEs need urgent assistance to resume their operations and regain their livelihoods, to continue delivery of the services and sustain the jobs

2 - Activities

1: Cash-based food security assistance to 3,650 target HHs (estimates as 18,250 individuals; 8578 women and 9672 men) Based on ongoing HH level assessment which ACTED is undertaking under the coordinated efforts of LRC, referrals from local authorities/CBOs where ACTED and partners are currently implementing the rapid emergency response, as well as through the referrals from WFP (and the joint in-kind intervention that ACTED and WFP are designing) and FSS partners, ACTED will select the HHs that require cash assistance in the 4 selected neighbourhoods. Assistance will be granted to the selected family units that fall out of the scope of WFP assistance. HHs that have one or more of the following vulnerability criteria will be prioritized: a) Low or No Income HHs; b) Single-Parent HHs; c) HHs with members experiencing limited mobility due to self-isolation (due to the COVID suspicion); d) HHs with one or more elderly dependents; e) HHs with members who are disabled and/or are suffering serious medical conditions. The cash transfer value will be an average of 250 USD per HH (based on a range of 50-300 USD to be set according to the specific needs) however this amount may be revised to reflect ongoing cash harmonization discussions. The support will be distributed through one of the Financial Service Providers (FSP) with which ACTED has an existing agreement. Cash or check for critical cases with restricted mobility will be considered in the absence of alternatives. 2: Cash for work intervention to support HH income and subsistence in the early recovery stage for an estimated 545 individuals (50/50% women and men participation)Based on referrals from municipality/CBOs where ACTED and partners are currently implementing rapid emergency response, ACTED in coordination with local partners (ex. Arc en Ciel, Cedar Environmental, LRC etc.) will identify the skilled and unskilled workers to take part in cash for work schemes across different domains. These can include minor shelter repairs and cleaning (painting, minor repair works) ; community-based (parental/peer) PSS; glass sorting/cleaning for recycling etc. Workers will be selected based on information sourced from ongoing assessments (where data is available) or through a rapid assessment undertaken by ACTED to determine the current HH income and composition, age, gender and nationality. The individuals directly impacted by the lost sources of income as a consequence of the explosion will be prioritized, with an emphasis on ensuring opportunities for women. 3: Cash provision and business support for the 80 businesses in the food sector directly impacted by the Beirut explosion (targeting estimated 400 individuals, 5 employees per entityACTED will provide a cash grant for businesses based on the assessed need to relaunch their activity. Each business will receive one cash grant for up to 3500USD to be used for rehabilitation/stock replenishment/assets/staff salaries whereas 10 to 15 of the MSMEs will be eligible to receive a second grant of up to 3500USD, to be decided based on ACTED’s field team assessments and specific criteria, primarily intended for larger businesses with estimated more damage, and employ 5 or more people to ensure multiplication of livelihood outcomes from the assistance. Businesses ensuring the provision of essential goods and services for the response will be prioritized, including food production, grocery stores, health care and pharmaceuticals etc. Business owners will be obliged to submit BOQs outlining their needs and what the grant will be spent on, and the decision on how to spend the cash for business will then be done with the support of ACTED’s technical Coordinator and engineers. ACTED will provide information and support on how to access online platforms such as ‘Digimart’, a b2b service that helps connect retailers to local wholesales, importers and producers, to ensure businesses get most competitive price for restocking their inventory.
