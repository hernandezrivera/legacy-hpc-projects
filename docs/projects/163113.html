Project Summary

Over the years, much of food insecurity prevalence was pronounced in rural areas. However, within the current context in Zimbabwe of rapid urbanisation, high demographic growth and rising urban poverty, urban food insecurity has risen and has become a topical issue. The recent urban food security assessment conducted by the Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) in 2019 revealed critical food shortages and the consumption of poor diets among poor households in urban and peri-urban areas. Household vulnerability to food insecurity are arising from a range of factors, including: high levels of unemployment and poverty; high dependency ratios; low levels of house ownership; hyperinflation; skyrocketing food prices; and the general collapse of the formal food system. The vulnerability to food insecurity is further exacerbated by climatic variabilities, a prolonged adverse socio-political climate that undermined national economic recovery and reduced the livelihood opportunities available to the urban poor. FEWSNET’s food security outlook report of November 2019 revealed that the foreign currency parallel market exchange rates are impacting severely to a large proportion of people in urban areas who are earning the weaker Zimbabwean Dollar.
It is estimated by Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZimVAC) report for urban food security in 2019 that 2.2 million people in urban areas will be food insecure during the peak hunger period up to March 2020. Vulnerable households in urban area are founding it difficult to provide for their families, especially during the peak hunger period from January to March 2020 due to high poverty (estimated by World Bank to be at 72.3%) and unemployment rates. According to ZimVAC urban assessment report of 2019, Epworth is ranked 2nd with an estimated proportion of 48.3% food insecure populations. According to the Census report of 2012, Epworth had a population of 161,840 people and this population has increased over the years. Findings from the urban food security assessment by ZimVAC in 2019 implies that over 78 169 individuals are in need of emergent food assistance in Epworth. The peri-urban of Epworth is adjacent to Ruwa which according to ZimVAC urban assessment of 2019 has a proportion of 40.1% food insecure (over 22,589 individuals).
It is against this background that the organisation is proposing to implement an urban food security project over 4 months from January to April 2020 in Epworth and Ruwa. The main purpose of the project is to improve the availability of food through scaling up emergency cash assistance to support 5,000 acute food insecure women, men, girls and boys in need including people living with disabilities, illnesses, child headed and the widowed in Epworth and Ruwa by April 2020.
The project will target beneficiaries in IPC 3 and 4 through community-based selection process that will be agreed between all the stakeholders and the community. The community will define food insecurity indicators of own choice that matches with IPC 3 and 4 characteristics. Special considerations will be given to marginalised groups such as people living with disabilities, chronical illness, the elderly, widowed, orphans and child headed. Verifications of beneficiaries will be conducted through purposive and random sampling to reduce inclusion and exclusion errors.
Since its establishment in 1964, the organisation has implemented emergency projects in urban areas across the whole nation and it has created good reputation with local authorities and the government departments including those in Epworth and Ruwa.

1 - Activities

The major activity of the project is provision of monthly food baskets through mobile cash based transfers. The project is targeting 5,000 beneficiary and each beneficiary will receive Zimbabwean dollar via ecocash equivalent to US$13.20. A household of 5 will receive a monthly ration of US$66. Weekly market assessments will be undertaken to inform programming about changes in prices and availability of cereals and basic commodities on the local market in response to the cash transfer program. Monthly post distribution monitoring will also be conducted 2 weeks after the cash disbursements to determine the outcomes of the food distributions. The project will be implemented in coordination with line government departments, local authority, food security humanitarian organisations and local leadership. ZCC will attend all the coordination meetings that will be organised by government departments, other humanitarian partners and the Food Security Cluster.

2 - Indicators

The success of the project will be measured through determining the percentage of women, men, girls and boys in need including people living with disabilities, illnesses, child headed and the widowed who received emergency food assistance through mobile cash-based transfers. Baseline and endline data comparisons will be conducted to gather information about the main indicator (percentage of households) as well as other outcome indicators of the projects. The monthly post distributions monitoring will inform the success of the project on monthly bases by tracking on the outcomes of the project such as food consumption scores, coping strategy index, food consumption patterns, food expenditure shares and food utilization trends.
