Project Summary

According to the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 12.2 million people in Syria are in need of WASH assistance, with the most severe needs concentrated in areas of ongoing conflict or areas with large numbers of IDPs. As a result, to address the needs of the most vulnerable households, this intervention will focus on providing targeted and effective WASH support in Northwest and Northeast Syria, combining elements of emergency response with the establishment of durable solutions.
In-camp, where residents are heavily reliant on humanitarian assistance for their water needs, ACTED’s intervention will focus on life-saving WASH support and delivery of basic services for IDPs residing in camps across Aleppo, Idleb, and Al-Hasakeh governorates. This will include hygiene kits distribution, as well as emergency water trucking for camps and communities deprived of access to water networks. In addition, ACTED will continue its provision of in-camp sanitation services and solid waste management services, through the operation and maintenance of wastewater systems, the installation of latrine blocks, the collection and disposal of waste, and the deployment of communal bins.
Off-camp, ACTED’s intervention will integrate a more sustainable component, with a focus on long-term improvement of access to safe water sources, through the rehabilitation of WASH assets, such as water stations and boreholes, across NES and NWS. ACTED will work in close collaboration with local stakeholder, including technical department, to build their capacity to operate and maintained rehabilitated infrastructure.
To complement WASH services delivery, ACTED will organize hygiene promotion sessions, both in and out of camp, including sessions specifically dedicated to Covid-19. This intervention will benefit a total of approximately 389,580 individuals across Northern Syria.

4 - Needs

In Northern Syria, years of conflict have negatively impacted particularly the ability of the local leaderships and authorities to deliver basic public services to residents, with public water services and facilities disrupted and damaged. Fuel shortages and shifts in territorial control are further affecting access to water. 77% of surveyed communities connected to the water network in NES suffer regular piped water shortages. In NWS, an alarming 88% of IDPs in NWS lack access to clean and sufficient water and 27% lack access to adequate sanitation facilities. Destroyed, damaged or otherwise dysfunctional water networks push residents and IDPs toward unaffordable alternative water sources.In camp and informal settlements, access to sufficient water, adequate sanitation and solid waste management and hygiene supplies is a challenge for IDPs, as displaced populations are highly reliant on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic WASH needs. According to the 2021 HNO, in 2020, 69% of assessed households in IDP sites were exclusively reliant on water trucking services, and among those, 20% reported insufficient support to cover their daily water needs. 45% of the assessed households also reported not being able to access hygiene items, 54% faced barriers to effective handwashing, 26% reported challenges with toilets functionality or wastewater disposal, and 75% of female-headed households reported lack of access to bathing facilities. The Covid-19 crisis further impacts the ability for conflict-affected households to meet their basic WASH needs, particularly with regards to hygiene. Household cleaning items and adult hygiene items remain largely unaffordable for many displaced or resident households.

5 - Implementation of activities

In order to fill critical WASH needs gaps among vulnerable conflict-affected populations in Northern Syria, ACTED will continue its extensive in-camp WASH response, benefiting camp residents in Areesheh and Washokani camps in Al-Hasakeh governorate, A’zaz camps in Northern Aleppo governorate, and the northern and western camp clusters in Idleb governorate. people across the targeted areas. Additionally, ACTED will continue rehabilitating and maintaining sewage systems, and providing solid waste management services to 227,850 camp residents. ACTED will facilitate access to WASH NFI for roughly 81,000 residents, including 72,000 through in-kind distribution and another 9,000 through a cash modality. Some 236,850 camp residents will be targeted with hygiene promotional activities, with a focus on Covid-19. ACTED will also continue to provide life-saving water assistance, mainly through emergency water trucking in NES and NWS, reaching 114,000 beneficiaries. ACTED’s intervention will also reach out-of-camp communities, with water trucking in Al-Bab and Jarablus districts (Aleppo gov.) and Hasakeh city, as well as hygiene kits distribution in NWS. Lastly, ACTED will continue repairing and rehabilitating public WASH assets across NES and NWS, including communal water stations, water network systems, and treatment stations, improving water access for some 330,000 persons in and out of camp.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of highly vulnerable groups and minorities needs during project design/implementation. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitization, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuse. Likelihood: Low – Impact: High. Mitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of ongoing efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issues.

7 - Does the project include a PSEA strategy ?

Yes

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

All ACTED staff receive induction and regular refresher trainings on the Code of conduct, PSEA, and Child Safeguarding policies. Contractors have the obligation to sign an ethical declaration and respect ACTED Code of Conduct. Contractors are briefed on PSEA before the implementation of the project. ACTED staff will be present to monitor daily the work of the contractors but also the behavior of the contractor employees towards camp residents. Information on the CRM will be communicated to camp manager and community leaders and as much as possible directly to camp residents, especially those who may be directly impacted by the road rehabilitation. Information will also be displayed on the channels available to raise complaints. Mitigation measures are monitored through regular on-site monitoring at the rehabilitation sites. The presence of women staff, in the programme and monitoring teams, will be guaranteed particularly during monitoring and evaluation activities conducted at household-level. ACTED staff, programme and monitors, are trained on protection and gender mainstreaming and on how to refer SEA incidents and/or suspicion internally. All SEA incidents are investigated by ACTED SEA Committee and communicated to the PSEA Network, as part of ACTED responsibility as member of the network.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Security and Political: Expansion to new areas: in-depth access mission from the ACTED Security team, formal approval required for daily movements and daily sharing of security updates. Conflict escalation: movement suspension in specific areas, relocation of staff to other offices to continue activities Community acceptance: monitored daily by liaison assistants, regular meetings with local community leaders and Key Informant network in order to prevent any tensions. Regular dialogue ensured with all relevant authorities, with support from various coordination mechanisms at area level. Risk of interference: policy of low visibility, recruitment and procurement processes, information sharing sensitivity, and clear communication with local actors regarding expectations and responsibilities.- Resource availability: Road closures: ACTED diversified its network of suppliers through tender processes and reinforced local suppliers’ capacities to ensure that items are procured regularly and prepositioned. Procurement related risks: 2-week local advertisement in multiple locations and subsequent transparency control and safeguards: analysis of all offers at capital office level, evaluation of technical and financial offers conducted separately between field team and capital. Recruitment related risks: process entails 2-week advertisement with inputs from field, regional office, area coordinators. - Community and cohesion dynamics: CRM inside Syria: stakeholders can safely provide feedback and report any project issues or concerns.
