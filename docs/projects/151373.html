Project Summary

The Humanitarian Response Plan identified WASH red sport districts which are at high risk to disease outbreak, water scarcity, high latrine safety index owing to high mobility associated with disaster. WV Somalia will target 3 regions and having worked in Lughaya, Zeylac, Burtinle, Garoowe Dollow and Luuq districts would like to build on its foot prints with regards to WASH service delivery. WASH programming will be undertaken at community, school and health levels. The program aims to achieve two results/outcomes, that is, firstly increased access to sustainable and safe water supply for poor, vulnerable communities, children in school and in health facilities and secondly target population has access to reliable and sustainable environmental sanitation. WV has the commitment to supporting capacity-building efforts for local people and government line ministries. World Vision currently works with the Ministry of Energy and Water Resources and the Ministry of Health in the implementation of its WASH activities in South West State. This deliberate effort at partnering provides a platform for capacity building for the Ministry personnel, and at the same time strengthens collaboration and ensures World Vision strategy is in sync with the mandate and strategy of the local government. WV is mindful of local custom and culture. It is also committed to participatory and sustainable development and to providing assistance impartially. WV acknowledge their accountabilities to those whom they assist and those who provide support to their work and also committed to ensuring that it does not expose intended beneficiaries, including children, to any form of discrimination, abuse or exploitation. Inorder to ensure sustainable delivery, WV seek to encourage participation in decision making processes and skills training so as to ensure that the beneficiaries will act as an active component in the implementation of the program. The WASH program has been deliberate in ensuring its facilities are inclusive and can be used by people with physical disabilities. One of the key areas has been ensuring that latrines are accessible to wheel chair bound persons by incorporating ramps as standard at doorways and having wide fully opening doors. For public sanitation facilities, ramps and rails have also been installed as standard for the sanitation facilities. Sector integration will also be fostered. The WASH program will work closely with the Education and Protection and Health and Nutrition sectors. The WASH programme in Health Facilities will focus on ensuring all consultations and admissions including children under the age of 5 have access to clean water and sanitation within the target locations. At each of the health facilities World Vision will provide an elaborate water and sanitation management programme which will include incinerators, autoclaves, clean water, and institutional latrines for proper infection prevention and control practices. The schools will be connected to the main water supply and receive occasional water trucking at times when the main water supply is erratic. Children will also be provided with WASH NFIs, including MHM kits that will be given to adolescent girls at the schools to help reduce on the number of girls who drop out of school during their menstrual cycle.DRR will include developing capacity of local government to understand, own and trigger demand for WASH through advocacy to take policy decisions to develop political will that enable a WASH resilience building environment.

Needs

Health, nutrition and resilience outcome are closely related to positive WASH outcome. However, lacking access to sufficient and clean water, inadequate sanitation facilities and commodities and poor hygiene practise have put 4.4 million of people in Somalia in need of proper WASH aid (OCHA HNP 2018). A forecast issued by the Greater Horn of Africa Climate Outlook Forum (GHACOF50), in August 2018, indicates a high probability of normal to above-average Deyr rains across Somalia. Specifically, the forecast indicates a 35 per cent chance of above-average rainfall in the north-western areas. This probability is higher (40 per cent) in the rest of the country. The anticipated rains are expected to further enhance pasture and water availability; however except for the improved rainfall can bring more flood risk to the community, it also increase the risk of people rely on contaminated, open source of water. Continued displacement further exacerbated the humanitarian crisis in Somalia. In IDP settlement, open defecation is common due to the absence of proper sanitation facilities. Host communities will also be overburdened by increased incomer. The preliminary finding released for the Joint Multi cluster needs assessment conducted by REACH October 2018 reported the reduction in household consumption due to lack of water at the source was the most commonly cited reason for inadequate water access for both IDP and host community. Lack of storage capacity for water was the second most common –particularly for IDP households, suggesting a WASH NFI gap. For sanitation, the REACH report finds that over 65% of IDP and host community report being able to access a latrine but 50% of which rely on unimproved latrines which cannot yield the sanitation outcome. Hygiene promotion will be crucial to complement other infrastructural interventions to yield the intended outcome through behaviour change given yearly AWD outbreaks in target districts and the desire for behaviour change.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

WV successfully conducted a hydrological survey where 3 drilling sites were identified. One borehole is already drilled and is in the process of being connected to the beneficiaries.According to the hydrological survey, the borehole was drilled to a depth of 130m, with a borehole diameter of 10 inches and a casing diameter of 8 inches for both steel (top 10m of borehole) and PVC (rest of the borehole) and this will ensure sufficiency of good quality water connected to people.Construction of earth dams and mini-water systems (comprising of rehabilitated boreholes, elevated water tanks, solar pumping systems, pipe connections, water kiosks and animal troughs). WV Somalia is working on ensuring that inline chlorination mechanism are installed on the system. The dams will be lined with 2mm plastic lining to reduce loss of harvested water to infiltration. During the upgrading of boreholes to solar power; solar panels that generate the desired KW power, pump, inverter, riser pipes, Dry Run Switch - Well probe sensor, Level Switch, control boxes and other miscellaneous components will be delivered and installed. Finally on site extensive training was provided to the borehole operators. Sanitation provision will mainly be the construction of upgradable family shared VIP latrines. An upgradable latrine consists of a lined latrine pit designed to last a minimum of 5 years and much longer if desludging facilities are available. The superstructure is made of cheaper materials, but with emphasis on providing privacy, and satisfying the GBV checklist. The latrine design will be as per latrine design standards set out by the Somalia WASH Cluster. The design feature a superstructure with an offset pit (pit behind latrine, not below latrine). This allows pit to be easily desludged. The pan is joined to the pit by an angled PVC pipe (no S-bend), 160mm in diameter, at a slope of 2:1. The pit will be covered with a reinforced concrete slab which can be removed for desludging purposes. The slab has a good seal, to prevent the pit from becoming a mosquito breeding site. A vent pipe will be installed for each pit, with wire mesh over the top to prevent flies and mosquitos entering pit. Provision of water facilitated the education programme two fold, first ensuring children have water for rehydration, sanitation and hygiene and secondly water for ongoing school feeding. At each school, one male and one female teacher will be trained as a hygiene master at the respective schools. The hygiene masters will further trained school clubs with support from the WASH team, Ministry of Health and Ministry of Education staff.. A range of exercises and educational games were used to teach pupils about the links between personal hygiene and health. The hand dug wells will be deepened to a minimum depth of 10 m to ensure yearly supply, and then well rings were installed to protect the sides of the wells from collapse. Grouting will be 3m depth and 0.5 high head wall added to.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The selection and prioritization of support will be totally need based regardless of communities’ status and identify. Project areas, for instance, will be prioritized base on its population against water sources and/or hygiene facilities ratio. Recognizing displaced population, who usually have less access to economic and social resource, often live in over-crowed area with minimal WASH infrastructure, the project will consider displacement as one of the prioritization criteria.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The project will ensure water source and hygiene facilities are accessible and appropriate for all in the communities. This will take into gender specific protection needs into account. This will include but not limited to making sure latrines will be equipped with locks and have clearly indication and proper separation of male and female. Hygiene kits will also be distributed to women and girls to address their menstruation need. While gender disparity in Somalia often exclude women from accessing information and making decision, the project will not only ensure hygiene promotion will include both men and women in the event, it will also intentionally include both male and female in the WASH committee so that they can have equality opportunity to take part in decision making on WASH issue in the community.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

Competing over access to basic commodities and services are often one of the major causes of community conflict. To avoid provoking fraction among communities, the project will closely engage community throughout the project cycle, ensuring they are involved in site and community water committee selection, fully informed of project details, and making sure accountability system is in place to allow them voice their concerns and complaint. WV, throughout the project cycle, will ensure the safety of beneficiaries and staff during program. This will ensure timely provision of services to prevent late travels by the staff and beneficiaries which might expose them to security risks. Throughout the project duration, world vision will monitor the security situation and also collaborate with the local administration and the community to ensure that the beneficiaries and the staff are well aware. WV has the security team that closely monitors the security situation within the target areas, hence able to advice and prevent staff and also beneficiaries from being affected.
