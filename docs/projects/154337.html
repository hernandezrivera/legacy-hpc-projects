Project Summary

Through this request WFP, and FAO as the co-lead of the Food Security Cluster, seek to support relief efforts in Mali by expanding the FSC coordination team and dedicating adequate financial resources to cover FSC operational costs. This will allow the FSC to strengthen coordination at the regional level and provide technical support and capacity building to FSC partners to improve the quality and accountability of operations.
1) The recruitment of a national information manager will aim at:
• Scaling-up information management capacity of the cluster through enhanced data collection, management and information sharing;
• Strengthening coordination at regional level through regular field missions to support sub-national clusters and capacity building of FSC teams and partners at decentralized level;
• Ensuring continuity of this position through capacity building with a national staff.
2) The allocation of financial resources dedicated to the FSC to cover operational costs will aim at:
• Providing technical support and ensure capacity building with food security partners both at national and sub-national level through trainings and workshops to improve the quality and accountability of their operations;
• Improving food security and nutrition situation, response and gap monitoring and analysis through facilitation of ad hoc (joint) evaluations and monitoring missions in the field in case of information gaps.
3) The overall FSC staffing (1 Coordinator, 2 IMs and 1 NGO co-chair) will aim at:
• Ensuring stronger and more effective coordination with other clusters (nutrition, WASH, Health), State and development actors to create synergies between humanitarian and development interventions and support the operationalization of the Humanitarian-Development Nexus;
• Empower the food security government leadership in Mali for the emergency response through stronger coordination with and capacity building of national and state services (CSA and SAP) to prepare for a gradual handover of responsibilities.
The direct beneficiaries are the partners of the Food Security Cluster, co-lead by WFP and FAO, meaning all stakeholders including the Malian Government, international, regional and sub-national organizations, national and international humanitarian actors and donors.

Needs

Since 2015, food security across Mali has continuously deteriorated reaching an estimated 1.6 million people in need of emergency assistance during the 2018 lean season (June - September) including some 70,000 displaced persons. With support from the United Nations and NGO partners, national authorities have developed prioritized response plans that focus on food security and resilience, and humanitarian actors have scaled-up their response plan by mid-2018 to reach 1.4 million people with food security and livelihoods interventions. However, the scale and complexity of humanitarian needs coupled with the extremely difficult operating environment in Mali, a combination of chronic and cyclical vulnerabilities and humanitarian access constraints in the North and Center, emphasize the need for strong coordination mechanisms and joint planning of humanitarian interventions. Although seasonal rainfall forecasts for the 2018/2019 season show conditions likely to remain near or above average, several areas of the country have been affected by irregular rainfall distribution marked by heavy rains, dry and wet spells which could have a significant impact on the food security of vulnerable populations in addition to insecurity and populations displacement which have prevented many families in rural areas from cultivating their fields. In response to increasing needs and coordination challenges, the Food Security Cluster (FSC), co-chaired by WFP and FAO, has undertaken important efforts in 2018 to strengthen coordination among humanitarian partners and national authorities as well as resource mobilization to scale up operations following early warning signs. However, challenges remain numerous considering the continuing deterioration of the food security situation and the capacity gaps of the competent Government entities (Commissariat à la Sécurité Alimentaire – CSA and of the Système d’Alerte Précoce – SAP) to better prepare and respond to mounting needs. At cluster level, there is also an important need to strengthen sub-national clusters (in Mopti, Gao and Timbuktu) and the links between emergency response field operators and actors involved in rehabilitation and development activities (Humanitarian – Development Nexus). In this challenging context, the Mali Food Security Cluster (currently composed of a Coordinator, an Information Manager and a part-time NGO co-chair) requires additional human and financial resources, to efficiently perform all its core functions at all stages of the humanitarian programme cycle as defined by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). This includes enhancing coordination efforts and allocating scarce resources to ensure the delivery of coherent, coordinated and comprehensive assistance to the most vulnerable communities while avoiding duplication and gaps.
