Project Summary

The community mobilization project aims to support grassroots initiatives of vulnerable communities to increase community-based protection (with immediate impact on civilian protection) and meet their social cohesion needs. The project is directly aiming at supporting organic efforts within the communities, and increasing dialogue with key authorities and stakeholders, to increase their resilience and find sustainable solutions to their issues.
All project activities are based on the analysis of protection issues and needs (based on age, gender and diversity approach), and prioritization of protection risks and solutions in every particular community. Community mobilizing seeks to empower communities to develop their own community-based coping mechanisms, to exercise their rights, and to find solutions for the most pressing issues of protection mainstreaming in their communities. Communities will mobilize themselves when they have trust and confidence in the process and when they see benefits emerging from their own efforts. Through local ownership, inclusive participation and community-based approach the community engaged projects are to create social cohesion in fragile communities and have more sustainable effect.
Activities will include facilitation of community coming together to prioritise their needs and realise their community-based protection and social cohesion projects. Activities will also include trainings and support to local activists, so they can continue community mobilisation in the longer-run, and engage in dialogue with authorities and other key stakeholders. Activities will also include, from individual empowerment perspective, working with local partner(s) to help with legal assistance and similar support to the most vulnerable individuals in the communities: this is to ensure, not only basic legal protection, but also to foster a rights-based consciousness that will enable inclusive participation in community life and further advocacy. Activities will finally include evidence-based advocacy: raising the voices of the communities, up to national level advocacy, on the main issues they face and to promote proper protection mainstreaming in humanitarian response.
Throughout its action, PIN will pay special attention to Accountability to the affected population. PIN will do so by actively engaging women, girls, boys and men, individuals in their diversity without discrimination (with due regard to underlying inequalities) in the identification and prioritization of their needs through extensive consultations and facilitation of community actions. PIN will also strengthen its established Community Feedback and Response Mechanism, including with strengthened response to complaints as well as referrals, as well as internal learning cycles to ensure that affected populations’ feedback is integrated into project proposals, strategies, monitoring and evaluations, trainings and performance tracking management, partnership agreements etc. PIN will continue to provide accessible and timely information to beneficiaries on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between an organization and its beneficiaries over information provision.

1 - Needs

As the protracted conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues, the humanitarian needs remain significant in the Protection and social cohesion areas. Based on PIN’s recent community-based needs assessments, basic social, economic, and administrative infrastructures and services are either difficult or impossible to access. Or there is lack of communication, coordination, transparency or accountability. There is severe lack of trust within communities and between communities and civil-military administration or local authority. It is unknown how long this crisis will continue and the local governmental and municipal systems are not capable of responding to the protracted emergency and humanitarian needs of conflict affected populations in both GCA and NGCA. They not only lack resources, but also operational and technical capacities to take the lead in mobilizing, coordinating and carrying out the response. Most importantly, to practice basic rights and in application of humanitarian core principles, such as participating and holding decision-makers accountable, remains a challenge, translating into risks to stability, social cohesion and protection. Although some social assistance in GCA is operational through municipal structures or local non-governmental service providers, still they are not accessible for many vulnerable people, mainly in the hard-to-reach, isolated areas near the LoC. In other areas mistrust between communities and civilian protection stakeholders is often a challenge. PIN’s assessments show that the social cohesion needs of the most vulnerable communities remain the same e.g. lack of communication means, compromised access to education and cultural life, lack of means or knowledge to up-hold basic rights or hold duty-bearers accountable on services for daily life needs, but also damaged infrastructure system, absence of socially vital services such as medical treatment. Therefore, residents of these areas rarely travel outside of the village and live under constant potential threat to their lives and health . The limited transportation and the lower mobility increase the isolation and vulnerability of elderly residents, especially those with disabilities, chronical diseases and other serious health concerns. The children (both boys and girls) lack spaces for socialization and out-of-school activities for self-development and life within their communities. Adolescent girls and boys lack such spaces also to understand their rights and be able to participate in decision making, and are in constant influence of information war. Lack of involvement in community life can in turn translate into harmful coping mechanisms. Women face exhaustion and time poverty in handling unpaid care work made more difficult in the absence of adequate support services. The communities in general lack voice, mechanisms and media to raise everyday protection concerns, and tools to advocate those protection and claims their rights at various levels. This includes coordination with civil-military administration on basic rights, such as voting, communication and other aspects. According to PIN PDM report and SWOT-analysis, Community Mobilization Projects allow communities in their diversity to analyze, address and mobilize their own resources to tackle/solve their own social cohesion needs, allow to encourage further development of community and civil society as well as community-based advocacy (May, 2020). Engagement of girls and boys, women and men of different ages and with diversity factors into the discussion, design and implementation of the projects contributes to the gender equality, including across age groups within the communities. All the community members age (with due regard to underlying inequalities affecting access to opportunities for a truly inclusive approach) can benefit from the implemented project initiated and designed themselves.

2 - Activities & Outcomes

PCO1. Ensure conflict-affected people, including IDPs, benefit from full and non-discriminatory access to quality essential services and enjoyment of their rights, with particular attention to people with specific needs.Activity: Provision of legal assistance and protection information/counselling. To make sure that the most vulnerable have access to basic entitlements and to foster the recognition and claiming of their rights, PIN will support local partners to do legal empowerment (legal assistance etc), with gender equality/inclusion lenses.PCO2. Strengthen protection and social cohesion through prevention and mitigation of rights’ violations and community based protection for conflict-affected people; support IDPs in identifying durable solutions.Activity: Advocacy interventions. Advocacy for participatory solutions in humanitarian assistance (protection mainstreaming gaps). Activity: Community-based protection, Support to Peaceful Coexistence & Durable Solutions Building on the above, PIN will empower communities to develop their own coping mechanisms, to exercise their rights, build their capacity and to find solutions for the most pressing needs/issues in their communities. Targeted communities will be selected on the basis of localised context analysis, integrating contextual vulnerabilities from protection and social cohesion perspectives. The communities will mobilize themselves when they have trust and confidence in the process and when they see benefits emerging from their own efforts. PIN will provide grants to at least 20 communities for community-based projects, with their own design, meeting needs identified by community members in their diversity. This will follow PIN’s tested community-based approach to social cohesion in 25 communities along the contact line. An obligatory condition for receiving the grant is the community's own contribution to the project, including documented needs-based assessment and all direct activities for the realisation of the community project. Such community engagement projects will be seen in a community-based protection frame and may for instance include: awareness campaign to stop domestic violence, creating a volunteer youth house to meet, share information and have access to internet and learn media literacy, safe public lighting, girls’ advocacy club etc.PCO3. Improve resilience of conflict-affected people and system of service provision.Activity: Capacity building for activists of communityPIN will build capacity of local communities as a means to ensure meaningful transition and exit strategies. Identified community groups and activists (possibly including informal grassroot groups) in the Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts, especially along the line of contact in GCA, will receive trainings on Community engagement and community-based protection. Initiatives (CSI) include a range of activities designed to mobilize and empower conflict-affected and displaced communities, provide social services, build / strengthen their own (intangible) resources to analyse and organize emergency response, protect themselves and identify solutions, even where areas are restricted for service providers due to conflict or COVID-19.Capacity building will cover skills and gaining experience on e.g.: community mobilization and empowerment; on (self) management, building inclusive leadership structure, gender equality and social inclusion; complaint mechanisms, referrals, platforms for ideas exchange, advocacy and dialogue with authorities etc; project realisation; community participation in governance, basic protection principles (as they are often first responders. Local community-based community support) etc. PIN also proposes to bring all participants from different communities together (in workshops and other events) for solidarity, and sharing experience. This will allow local activists gain adequate knowledge and expertise to continue this activity independently after the intervention.
