Project Summary

Building on the humanitarian work delivered by Street Child already under Early Recovery in the North East, Street Child will provide crucial livelihoods support for 5,000 vulnerable families to ensure that they can cope with the socio-economically consequences of the Covid19 Pandemic, across Borno & Adamawa. Street Child will prioritize households where extremely vulnerable categories of children have been identified and their families have been hit hard from the Covid19 – 3,000 Families will be provided with cash grants to ensure that they can recover from the consequences from the lock down. Furthermore, 2,000 HH will be supported to rebuild their financial capacity to meet their long-term needs. Furthermore, the 2,000 beneficiaries will be support with business training and mentoring, and supported savings schemes. 70% of the target beneficiaries that receive the family business grant will be women and female headed households.

1 - Needs

The long-term effects of COVID-19 crisis will be felt by most those who are most vulnerable in North East Nigeria, as petty trading will unable to attract many customers and may even struggle to buy new items to sell. There is an urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods, especially for female HH, especially IDS in Host Communities, along with people from the Host Communities. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and especially in restoring social cohesion amongst these groups, that are already. Without addressing this urgent need for livelihood restoration, a premature return of IDPs (or refugees) could lead to secondary displacement & deepen IDPs’ vulnerability to any even greater extend. Already now, the deprivation from Covdi19 and the lockdown has led to societal tensions between IDPs, returnees and host communities, especially in Maiduguri and resulted in increased hardships for displaced populations. Targeting recovery or establishment of small enterprises for vulnerable HH, especially female headed HH is essential to address negative coping strategies which many households have already resorted to. These include buying food on credit, selling of productive assets, borrowing cash from others and more importantly there is severe protection concerns including early marriage, child recruitment, child labor, trafficking, and sexual exploitation and abuse, which Street Child is working hard to mitigate through other projects. Cash- based programming has been described as a fast, efficient and dignified way of delivering humanitarian and development assistance in protracted conflict situation and as an alternative source of immediate income or complementary in-kind assistance where it’s feasible. To ensure that the most vulnerable groups are reached, Sector partners will adhere to following principles: Ensure that physically and socially vulnerable groups including female-headed households, elderly and people with disabilities are provided with alternative means of assistance through cash grants.

2 - Activities

In line with the ERL Strategic Priorities of 1) Contain the spread of the COVID-19 epidemic and decrease morbidity and mortality & 2) Decrease the deterioration of human assets and rights, social cohesion, food security and livelihoods, Street Child will equip public facilities, led by community committee’s with Hand Washing stations to promote good hygiene practices. This will consist of Handwashing stations, with locally produced hand soap, at a unit cost of NGN 15,000 accompanied with posters and on best practices and key messages, as created by the Protection Sub Sector. It will be monitored by Street Child’s implementing partners and will also be there to share information and message on social distances. Street Child will provide immediate livelihood support in forms of immediate cash transfers valued at NGN 40,000 to 3,000 vulnerable households that need immediate support to recover from the severe consequences of the lockdown. Furthermore, 2,000 HH, with 70% of them being female HH will received a cash grant to start a micro business with a value of NGN 20,000 to enable them to recover from COVID-19 pandemic. Tailored business assessment & plan creation: Following selection and registration onto the programme, the livelihood officers will carry out an assessment of household income and caregivers’ capacity to undertake business. On this basis, a trained livelihoods officer will provide varying levels of tailored support to enable each beneficiary developing a business plan for the use of the cash transfer. Vocational and business management training: Two cycles of four-day training courses will be carried out, following Street Child’s proven model. Afterwards the livelihoods officers will train each beneficiary in basic business skills, including bookkeeping, pot saving, and basic business strategy. The market analysis will also identify key areas for vocational training relevant to selected business types. Street Child & Implementing Partners facilitators will lead on sessions, together with community committee, on awareness-raising of COVID-19 preventions measures and stigmatization of aid-workers/xenophobia. These sessions will ensure that community members’ knowledge and awareness of key of COVID-19 preventions measures is improved. Building on lessons learned from previous and current projects, Street Child will set up clear and effective Complain & Respond Mechanism in each community in order to deliver clear with COVID-19 messaging in order to reduce the spread of the disease, fight stigma, detect community tensions and resolve related local dispute.
