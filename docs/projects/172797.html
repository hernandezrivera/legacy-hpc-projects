Project Summary

IMC will continue implementing its integrated health, nutrition and WaSH interventions in ﬁve states, West, Central and South Darfur, South Kordofan and the Blue Nile covering around 1,250,645 people. The proposed intervention will address gaps in the provision of primary health care and nutrition services. Under the health intervention, International Medical Corps will be providing basic primary and nutrition services, including outpatient consultations, reproductive health services (antenatal care, delivery service, post-natal care, and family planning), and immunization through 6 health care facilities across the targeted 5 states. In addition to facility care, community-based health interventions including; health and hygiene education, referral of cases, and campaigns will also be provided. In collaboration with SMOH, WHO, and HAC, IMC will respond to seasonal outbreaks such as AWD, measles, and malaria in all targeted areas. Health facilities will be prepared with necessary human resources and equipment (including laboratory equipment and materials) to enable them to respond to outbreaks and related emergencies. IMC will be implementing community-based interventions such as the promotion of child and maternal health, health education, community mobilization for EPI, mass MUAC screening and other activities through local partners.Under the nutrition intervention, IMC will support SCs, OTPS, and TSFPs, IMC (in conjunction with the Ministry of Health) will provide nutrition services to the most vulnerable groups- children under 5 and PLWs- among the IDPs, returnees and host population and refugees in the targeted localities. The active and passive screening will be done to determine the children that should be enrolled for treatment. Treatment will be provided based on the national guidelines for the management of acute malnutrition

(MAM). Children 6-59 months found to have SAM without complications will be treated in the OTPs and those with medical complications or children below 6 months will be transferred to SC where they will receive medical and therapeutic nutrition.
To promote optimal IYCF practices, including exclusive breastfeeding, timely complementary feeding, and dietary diversity, individual counseling and group sessions for caretakers of children 0 to 23 months will be provided. Nutrition, health and hygiene education will also be provided for caregivers of all children admitted in the CMAM program, women attending antenatal and post-natal services, and when immunization services are provided. Key messages will be on recommended feeding and child care practices, improving dietary diversity using locally available foods, and improved water, sanitation, and hygiene practices. Community outreach activities will result in improved timely access to treatment, program coverage, and community engagement and contribute to the prevention of malnutrition.The health facilities will be made fully functional by the rehabilitation of the structure, provision of drugs, nutritional supplies, medical equipment and capacity building of State Ministry of Health (SMoH) staff on the provision of nutrition-speciﬁc and health care services. The aim is to facilitate access to quality health and nutrition services for all, with a focus on the most vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

The health systems in Darfur, South Kordofan, and the Blue Nile remain weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic life-saving health services and responds to outbreaks. In Central Darfur, it is reported that 26% of the health facilities are not functional. Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) coverage in health facilities is only 30% in Central Darfur. Only 40% of Clinics in Central Darfur States provide Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI) Services. The coverage of measles vaccination is only 55.9% while only 38.3% of children are fully vaccinated. Only 54% of the clinical OPD cases are managed according to the protocol despite training and supportive supervisions, which signals the need for strengthening support and follow up to ensure adherence to protocols. Detection and referral of pregnant mothers is weak because of low coverage of community outreach activities. The clinics are not yet fully staffed and still lack the necessary equipment and physical structure to provide comprehensive health services. Water supply, medical waste management and infection control practices are identiﬁed as a gap in the clinics. The community outreach activities need expansion to more villages. Staff need to be trained as they are relatively new to the areas for intervention. Dispensaries do not have enough shelves and storage capacity. Health workers in targeted clinics need training on IMCI which is an important skill for better treatment of childhood diseases. According to the Ministry of Health, in Sudan, some 2.2 million children suffer from malnutrition annually (Global Acute Malnutrition) out of which over 573,000 suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition.. South Kordofan and Blue Nile States have been affected by persistent conﬂict between government forces and rebel groups since 2011. Two million people are affected by the conﬂict since then and more than 250,000 refugees ﬂed to Ethiopia and South Sudan. The United Nations estimates that armed movements in South Kordofan and Blue Nile resulted in additional displacement of 545,000 people in areas under their control. The conﬂict has disrupted traditional livelihood activities and eroded community resilience to withstand shocks though the areas were known to have good agricultural productions before the conﬂict. Among displaced people, women and children are the most vulnerable and at the greatest risk of being exposed to violence. Healthcare facilities were destroyed in both states as a result of the ﬁghting. Lack of basic services and food scarcity perpetuates displacement and migration of people out of the country, mainly to the camps in South Sudan. The humanitarian context in South Kordofan is further complicated by arrival of South Sudanese refugees in the state increasing the humanitarian need and straining the already compromised basic services infrastructure, including health services. There are new arrivals of around 5000 refugees in South Kordofan in December 2019.According to S3M II 2018 survey result, seven out of the 18 states have malnutrition prevalence rates of above the critical threshold as per the WHO standards (15%). Nationally, GAM prevalence is at 14.1%, whereas some states have higher GAM prevalence rates than the national such as GAM prevalence rates of acute malnutrition in U5 children in South and Central Darfur States have remained above the emergency threshold (15%), which is recorded at 16.43% and 15.28%, respectively, High rates of acute malnutrition is recoded in South Kordofan (8.13%) and the magnitude of aggravating factors such as poor feeding practices (low rate of exclusive breastfeeding (61.12%), continued breastfeeding (77.04), dietary diversity (23.58)) (S3M II 2018, Similarly, prevalence of stunting in South Darfur, South Kordofan and Central Darfur States is as high as 23.85%, 36.80% and 43.67%, respectively. 2 - Activities/Outputs

Health: Deliver minimum basic package of primary health care services (including maternal and child health) and support referral to secondary health care. Support and conduct routine or acceleration interventions for immunization. Strengthening Early Warning, Early detection and reporting of AWD(EWARS). Procurement, storage, and distribution of drugs and medical supplies. Conduct health education training for health staff. Conduct awareness/orientation sessions at the health facility for the community. Nutrition: Provision of CMAM services for SAM and MAM cases of U5 children and PLW (incl. refurbishment of OTP/TSFP, provision of RUTF/SUTF, MUAC screening, referral service for SAM with complication case etc.). Conduct training for nutrition workers, community volunteer on CMAM, IYCF etc. Conduct community awareness campaigns on CMAM, IYCF etc. Establish a mother support group for the promotion of IYCF. Through a tailored approach to service delivery based on the needs of the different vulnerable groups, the proposed action speciﬁcally attempts to address disparities and differences in access to health, nutrition services among vulnerable populations, including women, children and the elderly. Children and women's health and nutritional needs are a speciﬁc focus of this Action and women, in particular, are solicited as catalysts for behavior change in the communities through their participation in various community committees. IMC's ongoing collection and analysis of health and nutrition data are disaggregated by age and sex. IMC will continue to ensure the impartiality of program services while enabling the identiﬁcation of individuals and families with speciﬁc vulnerabilities related to age, sex, disability, family size, health status, socioeconomic status, or other factors. International Medical Corps will continue to work with federal and state-level MOH and actively participate in federal and state-level relevant sector clusters (Health, Nutrition) meetings. WaSH: Provision of safe and suﬃcient amount of water, medical waste management, provision of sanitation facilities and hygiene promotion in the health facilities and catchment population
