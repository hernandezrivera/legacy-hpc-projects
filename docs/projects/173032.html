Project Summary

The crisis in NE Nigeria continues to disrupt and limit access to livelihoods of millions of people, undermining opportunities for durable solutions. As a result of the continuous conflict, which generated a situation of widespread unemployment that has been further aggravated by the COVID-19 pandemic, many households lack sufficient income to meet their basic needs, and livelihood opportunities remain scarce. Although the need for essential life-saving assistance is still widespread, IOM recognizes the urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods & access to essential services, including access to justice, to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations, especially girls, youth, women, & the elderly, against future vulnerabilities & shocks. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs & restoring social cohesion. Without those pre-conditions, a premature return of displaced populations may lead to secondary displacement, deepen IDPs’ vulnerability, & exacerbate tension & social polarization between IDPs, host comms. & returnees. The inadequate capacity in areas experiencing return is by IOM considered a direct risk to the overall progressive resolution of displacement in the north-east. This project seeks to contribute to the recovery & resilience building of conflict-affected populations through a series of community support interventions. As people continue to return to their places of habitual residence from before the insurgency, there is urgent and significant need to restore infrastructure, promote early recovery, including through economic & livelihoods restoration, & facilitate a robust resilience building process. Livelihood interventions under this project will include Cash for Work (CFW). Cash-based interventions are a more dignified way of assisting affected populations, as they empower people to determine their own needs and the best way of meeting them. They can also promote peaceful co-existence between IDP, returnee & host communities, as well-designed & run cash-based interventions have a multiplier effect, jointly engaging all members of the community & directly benefiting the local economy. In addition, vocational skills training & skills enhancement activities for youth will be organized as per outcome of a labor market assessment to create human capital as necessary for the early recovery phase, with a special focus on youth. Each targeted beneficiary will also receive business start-up capital to establish small scale business. CFW & skilled labor schemes will support activities such as comprehensive neighborhood upgrades & light community infrastructure rehabilitation to directly contribute to recovery in conflict-affected areas. In addition, this project will work towards enhanced coordination & complementarity between humanitarian, stabilization & development partners, through a people-centered approach. To supplement economic recovery interventions, the project will additionally support restoration of social cohesion & local governance mechanisms in the areas of intervention. To this end, the project aims to provide technical & logistical support to community peace committees to address social cohesion concerns. Furthermore, at the ensure protection is ensured in recovery efforts, the project will build the capacity of law enforcement bodies, including traditional/religious leaders, to prevent and respond to SGBV crimes in compliance with human rights standards & through a victim-centered approach. IOM will align its response plan to the 4 strategic objectives of Early Recovery and Livelihoods Sector as follows:
• Support Livelihood Stabilization
• Promote inclusiveness and peaceful co-existence among IDPs in Host Comms. & Returnees.
• Support Restoration of Local Governance Administration and Services
• Improve Access to Infrastructure & Basic Social Services through Rehabilitation and Reconstruction of Public Service

1 - Needs

According to 2021 HNO, out of 61 LGAs in the BAY states, 39 are in severe need concerning the lack of a sufficient and reliable income. The critical needs for crisis-affected groups remain livelihood support and job creation, access to credit and vocational training, support to productive assets and the private sector for value chain strengthening and development, restoration of markets and critical infrastructures, such as hospitals, schools, roads and waste management systems. Severe damage to or destruction of community infrastructure, as well as the lack of access to adequate basic services, ultimately affect the sustainability of return and reintegration. There is an urgent need for early recovery interventions, including restoring livelihoods and access to essential services, including access to justice, to strengthen the self-reliance of affected populations. These elements are also crucial for creating conditions for durable solutions for IDPs and restoring social cohesion. Specific measures to restore livelihoods include short-term employment creation, providing market-based skills, including vocational training, and micro and small enterprise recovery, targeting the recapitalization of small enterprises for youth and women, derived from community consultation. In addition, areas of return and host communities must be conducive to receive returnees in terms of security (including freedom of movement; safe routes; removal of UXOs/IEDs including from housing, agricultural land and market/trading routes) and the re-establishment of civil governance including the presence of police and judiciary.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

IOM seeks to contribute to the recovery and resilience-building of conflict affected populations through community stabilization interventions in order to enhance stability and security, restore trust among community members, vulnerable populations and local authorities and lay the foundations for durable solutions, lasting peace and sustainable development. To promote positive coping mechanisms, IOM provides psycho-social support to re-establish family and community support structures disrupted by the conflict. IOM also provides technical and logistical support to community peace committees’ operations, and supports capacity building of local governance mechanisms to promote local recovery, restore social cohesion, and prevent and respond to acts of gender-based violence in compliance with human rights standards and through a survivor-centered approach. IOM will work towards enhanced coordination and complementarity between humanitarian, stabilization and development partners, through a people-centered approach spanning across the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus (HDPN).

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

IOM plans to guarantee the participation of Women, Men, Girls and Boys asequals to ensure that the special needs of each group are taken into consideration. The design of activities and services will address specific needs of each gender and different age groups, and address women's, girls' and boys’ specific needs, especially in terms of protection and access concerns. All activities related to this project will equally target the different age and gender groups and will be delivered on a sustainable and equitable basis. Regarding assessment, monitoring and evaluation of gender specific needs and the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations during missions and set-up communication channels with them where possible, and all activities will be conducted based on Do No Harm principles.IOM also provides continuous PSEA trainings for all IOM staff hired.IOM has due to its longstanding presence in north-east Nigeria developed contextualized capacity in the area of protection mainstreaming and supported the establishment of agency as well as inter-agency referral mechanisms to support protection outcomes throughout programming.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

IOM will ensure accountability to the affected population and most vulnerablecommunity throughout all stages of the project life cycle. During the design of theproject and throughout the planning stages, IOM will promote active participation andfeedback from the targeted population in assessing their needs and specific vulnerabilities, including those of women, youth, and persons with disabilities, among others. This will ensure, for example, that the physical abilities of the elderly and people with disabilitiesare factored into any forward planning in terms of accessibility to services provided. IOM will also seek regular feedback from the targeted population during the implementation of theproject, most notably through the complaint and feed-back mechanism team and tools. Affected populations may also provide feedback or submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. This mechanism is managed by the IOM Camp Coordination and Camp Management Unit. After the project has been completed, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure furtheraccountability to affected populations and promote learning.
