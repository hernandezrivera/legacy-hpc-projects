Project Summary

Borno State is one of the three major states affected by the activities of the insurgents that has spanned over nine years in Northeast Nigeria. The state is the most affected state by the crisis which has affected over 14.8 million people making it one of the worst global humanitarian crises according to Relief web. As it is in most places hit by crises, women, and children were the most affected.
Originally Borno State before the insurgency was a very conservative and patriarchal society and anything that could affect the power relation can lead to conflict and GBV but with the introduction of the insurgency, the situation of women and girls has worsened and pitiable. Women and girls are forced into early marriage on daily basis; many women and girls are raped and stigmatized if they report it, denied access to livelihood and educational opportunities, and forced to accept the ugly treatment as normal.
It is against the aforementioned reasons that YAI and SNAZZY HOPE FOUNDATION decided to collaborate together to address some of these concerns which align with the mission and vision of both organizations. The project shall contribute to ending all forms of violence against women and girls and enhance the safety and security of all women and girls within the proposed project locations. It will support community structures and groups within the communities with training and provide them with advocacy materials. The organizations shall partner with the paralegal groups to address issues of GBV promptly and train the media houses on the reporting of GBV related issues. It shall empower schools with GBV sensitization and campaign materials and help them to conduct risk assessments. The action shall also promote immediate response to the prosecution of the perpetrators of Gender-based violence and other domestic abuses. It shall also support the schools with psychosocial support services (PSS) to survivors of gender-based violence.
The project shall:
(i) Women's rights groups and relevant civil society organizations have increased opportunities and support to share knowledge, network, and a partner jointly to advocate for GEWE and ending VAWG, including SGBV/HP, and promoting SRHR, with relevant stakeholders at sub-national, national, regional and global levels
(ii) Enhance the security and safety of women and girls and strengthen the public systems to address issues of GBV
The project will run from January 2020 to December 2020 in the two LGAs (Borno State).
Project Sites: The project will be located in Magumeri and Konduga LGAs, Borno state.
Target Audience: There will be a total of 25,000 beneficiaries in the target areas. About 80% will be females made up of Younger Women and Older Women (20,000) who will benefit from the services; and 20% males Men (5000) and boys

1 - Needs

A decade of conflict and insecurity, hunger and massive displacement in Northeast Nigeria has made women and girls in extremely vulnerable and at high risk of gender-based violence (GBV). While the humanitarian response in the region has stabilized the condition of a small number of the previously affected populations, perpetual external shocks limit the population’s access to basic services causing fluctuating levels of vulnerability. As a result women and children are forced to adopt risky and sometimes harmful coping mechanisms, including child labor, payment of a debt through forced/early marriage and prostitution in order to provide food for the family. Pressure for survival intensifies the potential for intimate partner violence. Furthermore, the modalities of aid assistance have sometimes exacerbated vulnerability and risk of exploitation. High levels of GBV occur on a daily basis and the institution is weak and lacks the capacity to prevent and respond to most of these issues. Although, the facility to address these concerns might be there the personnel that will perform the responsibility lack the knowledge and sometimes the willpower to do that. The project focuses on community-based interventions for the prevention of GBV by strengthening critical sectors in the state and in the communities to provide quality response services to survivors of GBV.The members of the action (YAI and SNAZZY HOPE FOUNDATION) propose to support this commitment by improving the capacity of female-focused organizations like the CBOs, CSOs, etc. and the public institutions like the Nigeria Police, paralegal institutions and the media to combat Gender-based violence. Impact and outcome: the overall objective of the action is to contribute to ending all forms of violence against women and girls and enhance the safety and security of all women and girls in Conflict-Affected Borno state. Prior to the insurgency, women, and girls have suffered various degrees of GBV in the form of rape, sexual exploitation, denial of access to own properties, denial of education, and other related human rights abuses. Although, Nigeria was a signatory to VAPP act which was a product of the UNSCR1325 which prohibits and advocates for the ending of all forms of violence against women and girls (VAWGs) but the act have been domesticated at the Federal level but it still requires to be domesticated at the state level which most of the states have failed to do.
