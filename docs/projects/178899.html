Project Summary

Syria is one of the 92 countries eligible for Advanced Market Distribution (AMD) of COVID-19 vaccines under the COVAX facility, a partnership between the World Health Organization (WHO), the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI), and the Global Alliance for Vaccines and Immunization (GAVI). In coordination with GAVI, WHO and UNICEF continue providing detailed technical assistance to and sharing guidelines with the national health authority and established committees on how to proceed with vaccination roll out focusing on target population and front-line workers
The target groups for NWS were prioritized based on series of discussions among the parties involved and include health care workers (3%), the elderly aged 60 and above (7.5%) and people in the age group 20-59 with special conditions such as immune-compromised persons and persons with chronic illnesses (9.5%). The GAVI letter received on 3 February 2021 expresses the intent to allocate vaccines to cover the initial 3% of the population with AZ SII vaccines (indicative, 336,000 doses). On the moment of HRP submission the northwest of Syria is allocated 53,800 doses of the AstraZeneca AZD1222 vaccine and initiated vaccination campaign on the May 1, 2021; the rest of the amount previously communicated before (336,000) is expected to arrive through June 2021.
The aim of this project is to provide financial support to cover the 20% of the population during 2021 with 2 doses (AstraZeneca) of COVID 19 vaccines that been committed / secured through COVAX and GAVI. The priority categories identified in the National Deployment Vacciantion Plan (NDVP are based on the CTAG’s recommendations, the Strategic Advisory Group of Experts on Immunization (SAGE) values framework as well as the COVAX facility fair allocation prioritization roadmap.
The following high-risk groups were agreed upon as targets under COVAX: health workforce (including frontline social workers and teachers); 3% of the population; older adults (>55 years); approximately 12% of the population; and people with chronic diseases; 5% of the population.
This project will support the operational cost of vaccine roll out in 14 Syrian governorates, including Northeast Syria and part of Northwest Syria GOS controlled areas. Under the current project, 76 hospitals will be used as service delivery points to provide vaccinations, along with 101 primary health care facilities all over the country. Services will be provided by trained hospital teams and routine immunization personnel in mobile teams. This number of facilities and associated teams is preliminary and subject to change based on ongoing microplanning. Each hospital will have three or more teams assigned to microplanning for each phase of the campaign.
Implementation across Northeast Syria will follow the current experiences of the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) microplanning through 17 fixed facilities (hospitals and primary healthcare centers) and 105 mobile teams. Formal and informal settlements will be targeted in the same way. Microplanning is covering camps across Northeast Syria. The first batch of vaccines will target eligible high-risk members of the health workforce and frontline humanitarian workers regardless of location.

4 - Needs

In Syria, the first case of COVID-19 was reported on 22 March 2020, as of 2 May 2021 a total number of 61,115 laboratory-confirmed cases have been reported in WoS. COVID-19 cases were reported as follows: 22,898 (37.47%) of cases by MoH/GoS, 16,184 (26.48%) cases by SA in NES, and 22,033 (36.05%). The CFR was highest in GoS areas 6.8% (about 45% of total deaths). The total recovered cases are 19,979 and the total confirmed death cases associates with COVID-19 in NWS is 655. Given the limited testing across Syria, it is likely the actual number of cases far exceeds official figures, with significant numbers of asymptomatic and mild cases in particular going undetected. The number of health workers tested positive for COVID-19 was 4,545 healthcare workers of them 879 health workers reported by MoH/ GoS including 31 deceased cases. The overall attack rate (AR) in Syria is 300 per 100,000 and all of governorates have reported confirmed COVID-19 cases. AR was highest in Al-Hasakeh (965), Ar-Raqqa (519), Idleb (455), Qunietera (434), and Aleppo (374). The lowest incidence rates were in Hama, Rural Damascus, and Dier Ezzor. As well the number of new COVID cases among healthcare provider is high – 7,1% (1573 physicians) among healthcare workers and 5,18% (1149 other personnel) among allied health workers. Epidemiological analysis indicates to three waves of COVID-19 in Syria. the first peak was on 19 August with (111) 7-days rolling average cases, the second peak was in December 2020 with 781 7-days rolling average, and the third wave peak was on 17 April 2021 with 457 average cases. COVID 19 vaccination rollout is conducted for the 1st time in Syria, the needs are to cover 70% to reach population herd immunity.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

There is a protection risk for health workers and beneficiaries who may be exposed to transmission of covid19 infection, this will me mitigated by using PPEs by vaccination teams during trainings and vaccine administration. The pre-registration system will help in avoiding mass gathering at health facility level. Hostilities in Syria continue to take a heavy toll on the lives and livelihoods of civilians who have already endured a decade of violence, multiple displacements, and a major economic downturn. The COVID-19 pandemic has only worsened the situation, adding new layers of complexity and hardship. Therefore, the availability, access and sustainability of essential health services and medical supplies was and continues to be always in risk due to the security situation, internal displacement and donors’ support.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Low vaccine acceptance might be the main risk beyond project capacity. WHO and UNICEF developed special communication strategy for demand generation as mitigation measures. Access in some areas may not be possible due security problems. Therefore, the design of the service delivery mechanism under this project using different approaches, including fixed facility base teams (Hospitals and health centres in each district) and mobile teams to cover hard-to-reach areas and camps. Deploying health workers for the same areas could reduce the security risk in addition to coordination with all parties in the ground is being addressed and used in routine immunization and mass vaccination campaigns. Regarding the COVID-19 vaccination roll out there are several major risks: • Manufacturing and global vaccine availability – the arrival of the first batch of vaccine allocated to Syria is still not defined.• COVAX commitment beyond the initial 3% is not ensured. • Options to secure the vaccines, in the long run, may be limited resulting in increased humanitarian needs.• Availability of funding for COVAX Vaccine to support the roll out of COVID-19 vaccination
