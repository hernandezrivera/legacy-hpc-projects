Project Summary

This project will include a network of 32 (21 female and 11 male) Community Health Workers (CHWs) to provide nutrition services focusing on IYCF counseling and screening of children under 5 and referral for treatment of malnutrition at the community level. The project will also focus on building the capacity of the CHW on IYCF. The health facility staff and CHWs will be trained in aspects related to child health care and nutritional screening and referral, they will also be provided with sufficient education to help raising the awareness of the related communities. Relying on CHWs will cover a big gap resulting from poor coverage of nutritional services in health facilities.
The motivation of this project is to provide child-friendly nutrition services in the locations to the population in northern Syria. Needs

4.7 Million girls and boys under the age of five years and pregnant and lactating women are at risk of undernutrition and in need of comprehensive curative and preventive nutrition services to continue during 2019. Around 92,000 children under the age of five years are expected to suffer from acute malnutrition during 2019, out of which 19,000 girls and boys are at risk of death due to severe malnutrition if not treated appropriately. Without appropriate care, acute malnourished children are eleven times more likely to die than well-nourished children. 865,295 girls and boys under the age of five years suffer from micronutrient deficiencies. It is estimated that 1.6 Million mothers will require maternal nutrition services and skilled support for optimal infant feeding and caring practices. Global Acute Malnutrition among boys and girls under the age of 5 years remains within acceptable international benchmarks in most of the assessed areas during 2018. However, there are some pockets where SMART surveys, rapid assessments, and nutrition surveillance information show an increase in chronic malnutrition rates such as Eastern Ghouta (36%) and Tel Abyed (32%) which exceed the very high stunting rate classification according to WHO 2018 thresholds. Chronic malnutrition was a problem in Syria even before the crisis and increased rates have been observed recently. In addition to data that indicates ‘High’ stunting levels in Lajat, Dar’a in 2017 (27.5%) and in the sub-district of Jarablus, Aleppo in 2018 (25%), stunting rates confirmed to be ‘Very High’ in Tel Abyed (32%) and Eastern Ghouta (36%) in 2018, as indicated by SMART surveys and using the 2018 WHO-UNICEF stunting thresholds. All the above rates are considered high as per the new WHO/UNICEF classification of stunting 2018. The increase in chronic malnutrition potentially reflects long term inadequate dietary intake, including inadequate micronutrient intake, as well as repeated infections in younger children, poor feeding practices due to low rates of exclusive breastfeeding in the first 6 months of life, and inadequate complementary feeding in terms of diversity and frequency. Maternal and child malnutrition is impacted by deterioration in security in some areas such as Idlib and rural Aleppo, increase in needs among returnees and internally displaced people, as well as coverage of nutrition programs such as in underserved North-East Syria and northern Rural Aleppo, including Afrin. Implementation of activities

The project provides humanitarian nutrition assistance, actively protecting and supporting infant and young child feeding with the active engagement of the community through the eight nutrition centers. Each center is equipped with mother-baby area (MBA) to provide a safe and quiet space for mothers and infants to receive feeding support from skilled IYCF-E staff, and a place for mothers to meet and bond and discuss facing similar issues to infant feeding. The MBAs will offer 1-1 counseling on maternal nutrition and child feeding. In addition, the IYCF-E staff will be trained in PSS skills to support mothers who are in distress or unable to engage with their infants, and to recognize when a mother needs further specialized mental health support. Children 0-24 months, their mothers/caregivers and pregnant and lactating women are most at risk of malnutrition. The program focuses on infant feeding and complementary feeding activities to promote, support and protect optimal breastfeeding, and ensuring children receive complimentary foods from their parents to grow and develop to their full potential; and address psycho-social aspects related to the crisis that interfere with feeding and care practices. Group counseling sessions will also take place in the centre and at household level for a maximum of 5 women per session, on appropriate maternal and child nutrition. Consolers and outreach workers will address common feeding challenges, misconceptions, foster mother-baby bonding and promote how IYCF-E practices are life-saving. As complementary feeding will be focused on and linked with KAP survey to explain the need of the child and the different food groups, 1to 1 and group sessions will also include demonstrations on how to improve complementary feeding practices and will be supported by information, education and communication materials (IEC).Integration of fixed nutrition center services through outreach activities to provide a link between the communities and the facilities, and help to promote and refer people to our services through improved health awareness. Community outreach workers are an integral part of the project and are responsible for health, nutrition, and hygiene promotion messages. They conduct house to house visits, and visits to community centers for messaging and promotion, as well as health and nutrition screening, as well as contribute to needs assessments and other health and nutrition data collection for our increased awareness of the health and nutrition context of the areas where we operate our facilities. This project will include a network of 32 Community Health Workers (CHWs, to provide integrated health and nutrition services focusing on IYCF counselling , outreach activities that include nutrition message delivery, as well as screening for acute malnutrition using Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) and provide referrals to the nutrition centre for treatment services referral. These CHWs will be linked with 8 nutrition centres, MUAC screening is conducted as routine and any maternal and child feeding difficulties will be identified by health staff in PHCs near the facilities and referred for immediate nutrition counselling.Re-lactation activity is provided in the centers in addition to Orientations with health workers and community to advocate for the adherence to the BMS SOP, highlight the dangers of uncontrolled infant formula, and underscore the life-saving practices of breastfeeding and complementary feeding.

Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers:Data on attacks against healthcare collected through SSA during the period of January - September 2018 documented 134 attacks across Syria, exceeding the incidents reported throughout the year 2017 (122 incidents).Although the rate of attacks against healthcare declined significantly over the course of 2018, violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers and affecting their ability to respond in contested areas, with a long-term effect hampering the scale up effort in newly accessible areas that witnessed recent change of control.Mitigating measures:- Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL).- Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare to the health cluster to highlight protections challenges facing health workers across Syria.Gender Based Violence GBV:Health services are often the first, and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBV related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate the required care, survivors must have safe access to health facilities, and it is critical that health providers are equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services.Mitigating measures:-Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package.- Specialized health staff are trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals to GBV centres- Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health servicesProtection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA:Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerable members of the community. The health sector and healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels.Mitigating measures:- A PSEA policy describes standard of behaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures.- Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance.- Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic.- Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff.Duty of care:In the Syria context health care providers were always amongst the first responders and the last to move out of areas witnessing increased conflict activities or change in control, something made them subject to physical harm and mental stress exacerbated by the protracted nature of the crisis and years of operating under continuous emergency conditions. Furthermore, similar to the rest of affected population, they were subject to displacement, insecurity, reprisals, and loss of livelihood. It has been estimated that Syria has lost up to 50% of its pre-crisis qualified medical personnel capacity due to immigration, displacement, violence, and criminal activities. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: the security situation is stable and helps to deliver the project services. Risks: Security situation deteriorates (airstrikes, shelling, inter-fighting between NSAGs) and control over the area of operation changes which might cause a delay in implementing the activities: Mitigation: SR may suspend some activities till the situation is stable. The staff is trained in and adhere to security protocols. SR employs program and monitoring stafffrom the area so they can move more freely. Strong community support will be maintained. If the control changes, Syria Relief will negotiate access for other areas.Considering the need for health services for men, women and children across northern Syria, the service provided can be targeted to another location and SR may move to mobile units instead of static HFs temporarily. All Syria Relief’s offices and field personnel implementing the activities have emergency contacts and receive updates on the security situation. SR informs donors and partners of relevant activities and security incidents. Risk: Staff and beneficiaries might be attacked or injured causing attrition or limits to the implementation of activities. Mitigation: All the activities that Syria Relief implements are based on the skills and competencies of the field staff inside Syria along with strengthened support from Senior staff based in Turkey. Access: Syria Relief maintains a good relationship with the communities it operates in allowing access and movement of staff from and to project location easily. Coordination: Syria Relief coordinates its project activities with NGOs, through UN-led clusters as well as through direct coordination with NGOs and local authorities. The unavailability of a local expert to provide technical trainings:Mitigation:-SR conducts training to the staff in Turkey and distribute the tools.-Turkey staff will roll out the training to the field staff -Conducting training of trainers.-Providing relevant training to key staff.
