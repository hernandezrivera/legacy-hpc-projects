Project Summary

CARE’s approach responds to identified needs through own assessments and other agencies approved by WASH sector. The database of the situation in the camps will be the reference point to all infrastructure improvement works. In HOST community people have less access of safe drinking water. Also to collect safe drinking water female are mainly responsible to collect it which is far distance from household level. For people with disability the water points are not accessible and this a challenge of daily life. For more accessible water source water distribution network will be established for HOST community people.CARE will put particular attention to sanitation by providing technical support to the partner staff to engage sub-block level desludging teams for a more decentralized service delivery due to the high population in the targeted camps versus the existing latrines. Mechanized extraction of sludge will promote high public health standards to prevent potential cross contamination as well as ensure environmental safety and protection. Based on need, sludge transfer stations will be used as holding chambers to fasten the desludging process and safer transportation to existing FSM’s. New latrines and bathing cubicles will be constructed and existing ones will be assessed for quality, safety and security. In HOST community safe sanitation is not available which CARE will ensure safe sanitation which will be accessible for all. Also for the lack of bathing facility gender based violence can occur in HOST community, to reduce GBV women friendly space will be constructed. More awareness session will be conducted by CARE to make community learn more. Gender markers will be assigned to ensure privacy and safety especially for women and girls. CARE will prioritize menstrual hygiene management for women and adolescent girls by upgrading existing facilities and or constructing new facilities with exclusive MHM provisions (I.e enhanced privacy, water supply, washing areas, hanging lines, disposal chambers, etc).
CARE will conduct capacity building to WASH committees (including partner staff and volunteers, etc.) to strengthen their skills and practical knowledge on simple maintenance procedures, water safety and effective management of WASH facilities within the sub-block levels. The training will also consider/involve community leaders (Imams, Manjis, etc.) to be able to convey accurate messages to their respective audiences and contribute in influencing positive behaviour change. CARE proposes a multi-sector response to prevent the spread and respond to the corona virus in the targeted camps 11, 12, 14, 15 and 16, leveraging current networks and programming to ensure effective and timely intervention. The project will put special focus on the inclusion of most vulnerable groups, such as the elderly, unwell, caregivers and people living with disabilities to ensure appropriate messages/services and shared inline/with the approval of the Ministry of health/Civil Surgeon and WASH sector. Approaches will enhance community resilience through capacity building to minimize the spread of Covid-19 within the community, households and individual levels to prevent the re-emergence or spread of the disease including acute watery diarrhea (AWD’s). CARE has a strong emphasis on community engagement and will ensure block/sub-block level sessions are relevant to the WASH sector strategy and are actively coordinated at the sub-block level to sensitize women, girls, men and boys as well as the general community level actors to create awareness across-section of key health exposure risks thus mobilize participatory actions such as mass clean camp campaigns (in coordination with respective CiC’s, other implementing partners), awareness on proper use/maintenance of WASH facilities.

1 - Identified Needs

Access to adequate sanitation facilities is also still limited to significant number of households with majority who have to share communal facilities. One of the main factors contributing to low latrine coverage if the lack of land space in the camps as well as no-functionality of most latrines that were constructed during the onset of the influx and require periodic repairs/maintenance. According to WASH sector gap analysis, latrine coverage at a camp level provides access at a ratio of 1:20 in line with sphere standards, but block level data shows that at least 13 of the 34 camps (including camp 15 targeted by CARE with future funding) are unable to facilitate the necessary minimum ratio due to the high population density with a camp wide estimated at 8,500 latrines. The sector acknowledge additional latrine instillation during 2021, but improvements in existing facilities with inclusion and privacy features and regular facility monitoring by the Area Focal Agencies and partners show the number of latrines has stayed constant with 2020 figures, highlighting the continued lack of operational space for WASH infrastructure. The sector needs analysis show that the gaps in bathing spaces has reduced, and again note that there has been significant increase in private spaces, with 86% (NPM report, Dec 2020 - Aug 2021) of households reporting private bathing space inside or attached to their shelter for use but those bathing places sometimes associated with laundry spaces (79%) and dish washing places (77%), very often those spaces are unhygienic and poorly maintained or used as alternatives for urination and defecation, with 11% not connected to any form of drainage for safe disposal thus posing Public Health risks.Overall, a number of households use public facilities for bathing, but still women and girls face issues of overcrowding and lack of privacy when using latrines and bathing spaces during the day, while at night they risk harassment and violence with 95% preferring private bathing spaces/makeshift within their shelters. WASH sector has encouraged construction of women friendly spaces and WASH blocks but lack of adequate land space is a major bottleneck. These broad challenges highlights the huge need for new latrines and sustained maintenance of the existing ones including the fecal sludge management units. Solid Waste produce daily in the targeted area, remains another key challenge as only 51% of the refugee households are disposing waste in designated area (WASH sector SWM guidelines, 2019) and 16% reported disposing of waste direct to open space. Generation of solid waste in the camps is high with crude dumping being rampant in many public drains and hidden sections of the camps. Good hygiene is critical towards the prevention of the spread of infectious diseases and helping children to live long and healthy lives. For families especially living in crowded environments, good hygiene means avoiding illness and spending less on healthcare. According to WASH sector needs analysis, hygiene knowledge and access to soap remains high although behavior change remains an issue that requires further research to assess the impact of knowledge on hygiene habits. Access to MHM remains a barrier for many females through stigma, taboo and restrictive practices but also from a lack of MHM kit individuality due to common practices of one size fits all or variations of inter sector kit and insufficient coordination. WASH sector (2020, JRP) also identified that uptake of appropriate hygiene practices for specific risk behaviours is still low with only 32% of households reported washing their hands after handling feaces and in respect of menstrual hygiene management (MHM), 24% of women and girls reported not receiving MHM materials within the last 3 months. It is therefore evident that inadequate, dilapidated water and sanitation infrastructures coupled with low hygiene practices elevate the levels of vulnerability persons settled
