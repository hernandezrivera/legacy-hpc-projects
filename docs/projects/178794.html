Project Summary

This project aims at improving NES EiE response capacity to offer safe and accessible learning environments to conflict affected school communities both in non-formal and formal Education setting.
All of the sub-districts targeted have a severity of 4 with the exception of Manbij which has a severity of 5. It is estimated that 15% of people targeted in this project are persons with disabilities.
This Education in Emergencies Humanitarian response in NES will create opportunities to ensure the reintegration of Out-Of-School-Children (OOSC) in NFE settings and by supporting their transition to the formal education system while promoting the retention of students attending formal education but at risk of dropping out.
Additionally, due to gaps in school infrastructure and the safety risks posed by damaged, non climate-proofed buildings, limited access to sanitation facilities and clean water the project will provide public school rehabilitation (windows, doors, holes) including the fence. Existing WASH facilities will also be fixed and additional facilities will be added. WASH rehabilitation will adhere to SPHERE standards regarding the number of latrines as well as a gender separation of latrines, latrines for teachers and at least one accessible latrine in each of the schools.
The project will promote quality teaching and learning by, in coordination with the Education Committees, teachers and students provide basic material for teachers to support teaching and learning including basic School Furniture, Hygiene Supplies and a limited amount of Recreational and Learning Material.
Complementary, EiEHR in NES will contribute to protective educational services by providing Teacher in Conflict Context training to teachers and other education personnel to foster a safe and positive learning environment and offering caregivers the opportunity to increase engagement in children’s' academic and socio-emotional learning by attending parenting sessions.

4 - Needs

According to recent HNO and REACH reports, the cumulative effect of the conflict, displacement rates and trends, the closure of learning facilities due to the covid-19, the worsening of the economic situation, changes in the population and other factors are impacting Syrian children right to education, where it is estimated that more than 2.45 million 3-17 years old children are out-of-school, and more than 1.6 million at risk of dropping out. The national enrolment rate average only reaches 60% of the students, where in NES governorates the average is even lower, with Hassakeh and Raqqa at 53% and 44% respectively, being Deir Ez Zour the exception at 74%. NES school attendance rates are also low with Raqqa at 64%, Hasakeh at 62% and Deir Ez Zour at 57%. Lack off resources and capacity from the Self Administration in NES to support OOSC or for school retention programmes, as education is one of the most underfunded sectors in NES.Findings by the NGO in its own Education Assessment reveal 3,080 HH in Raqqa city with 44% of the children (1,931) OOS; 19% of the HH had children aged 6-12 not attending school due to poor condition of school buildings (24%), school is not safe (21%), school is too far (20%) child is too old to go back to school (11%).Additionally, a significant number of schools are in urgent need of rehabilitation, with high risk to the safety and health of about 8,800 children. WASH facilities in 15 schools (8,163 students) without any functioning latrines.A study by Integrity, 2020 has found that 32% children reporting physical punishment and verbal abuse, which highlights the preponderance of poor practices that fail to alleviate and worsen issues around children’s wellbeing and learning challenges.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

As mentioned above under needs, many schools in NES are in dire need of intervention, since they were not rehabilitated after the conflict and, therefore, they represent a tremendous risk to children´s safety and health. Insufficiently safe learning facilities - lacking protection from elements, increased risk of COVID, and contribute to children dropping out or not attending school e.g the lack of windows and heating in the classrooms exposes children to temperatures below zero degrees Celsius during the winter. Rehabilitation and improvement of learning facilities so they are safe and conducive for learning. Child safeguarding measures to be put in place. Teachers or other education personnel in formal schools participate in physical punishment. To counter this, Education in Emergencies training will be provided to teachers on positive discipline methods; prevention and reporting of violence; sensitisation to code of conduct; basic self-care and PSS. In the NGO classes the project will ensure that all staff , facilitators and contractors are abiding and signing on the child safeguarding policy and will ensure all classes are free from any kind of risk including physical, sexual and psycho-social risks. the project has Community Response Mechanism in place. Participants in our activities can directly provide feedback or complaints about programmes, staff and systems via WhatsApp, email, telephone and complaints boxes.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks- Further escalation of the conﬂict, or deterioration in the security situation, in NES as a result of any of the following: - Escalation in the ongoing conflict between the SDF and Government of Turkey, extending beyond the current areas of operation. -IS resurgence in Deir ez Zour with extension of attacks and activities to surrounding areas -Tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conﬂict, jeopardising access to affected project areas. - Markets affected by continued currency devaluation, border closures or escalation of conflict, leading to shortage of supplies and market price fluctuations - COVID-19 pandemic escalates, new lockdowns restrict access Assumptions- This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly. There will be no signiﬁcant shifts in control over key locations in NES. Markets continue to function, internal and regional borders remain open and goods are available locally/regionally Mitigating Measures- Strengthen operational resilience. In the event of increased insecurity in an area, shift operations to nearest unaffected base of operations. Flexibly shifting modalities as required between in-kind, voucher and cash depending on cross-border access, the state of markets and availability of key goods. Full engagement in, and the development of robust, continuity of services plans to minimise the potential humanitarian consequences of any disruption in services to target area COVID-19 SOPs for general IPC in place as well as activity level SOPs.
