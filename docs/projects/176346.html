Project Summary

Mercy Corps has established long term static presence and excellent reputation in the above proposed locations on long term basis. Presently, Mercy Corps is undertaking implementation of emergency lifesaving activities using grants from OFDA, UNICEF, RSRTF and SSHF in the above mentioned locations. In order to move forward and build on the ongoing activities, Mercy Corps proposes a 12 months Water Supply, Sanitation and Hygiene Promotion services to the returnees, host and IDPs communities in Unity State (Panyijar, Rubkona and Koch) and Western Equatoria (Mundri West and East).
The Program goal is to reduce and/or mitigate potential further escalation of WASH-borne or WASH related suffering of already much affected and vulnerable population (including but not limited to malnutrition, diseases, suffering, morbidity, mortality and violence), to provide integration between nutrition and WASH activities, WASH and Gender Based Violence (GBV) to drastically reduce risks relating to GBV of populations being affected by triple shocks of flooding, conflict and COVID-19 through timely, sustainable and equitable access to clean and safe water, sanitation and hygiene services including distribution of WASH NFIs and Menstrual hygiene management for IDPs, host communities and returnees. This is in line with the South Sudan’s HRP objectives and more specifically the objectives being outlined by the 2021 South Sudan WASH Cluster. The program will be targeting about 45,000 individuals in Unity State (25,000) and Mundri West and East (20,000).
Below are the main components of the WASH program:
• Timely provision of adequate clean, safe and equitable water supply to the affected population including households, health facilities, nutrition centers and protection structures by ensuring access to water infrastructures through rehabilitation of broken water points, regular water quality monitoring and capacity building water technicians and WASH-Protection Committees.
• Provision of dignified sanitation facilities in a participatory manner to IDPS, host communities and returnees and construction of semi-permanent sanitation facilities with careful consideration of appropriate designs for persons with disabilities, elderly and children.
• Promotion of participatory and inclusive hygiene services with much emphasis on behavioral change interventions, menstrual hygiene management and incontinence as well as NFIs distributions that meets lifesaving needs and facilitate individual ownership of personal and community health and well-being.
• Health, nutrition, food security and livelihood, and WASH integration is crucial to mitigate malnutrition, gender based risks, WASH-related illness and morbidity through referral systems and integrated outbreak response planning. Mercy Corps will coordinate with Health, Nutrition and Protection clusters to ensure an integrated timely response to any need. Mainstreaming of Menstrual Hygiene Management among school going children with an aim to increase school enrolment among girls.
It should however be noted that, the project will also aim at developing a unique strategy termed as ‘user fee model’ within this project period to ensure future sustainability of WASH activities through placing much emphasis on strengthening the capacity of the affected populations especially in host communities hosting returnees and in some IDP sites (Non-POC and refugees settings) by forming female dominant WASH-Protection management committees since women and girls are the major users of WASH facilities, fencing of water points and setting time table for usage even for boreholes.

1 - Needs

Unity State is one of the state worst hit by the triple shocks being experienced by South Sudanese that include conflicts (national and sub-national conflicts, cattle raiding and revenge attacks), severe flooding and the COVID-19 pandemic. Severe floods displaced 10,060 households (48,235 individuals) in Panyijiar County and 2,264 households (13,581 individuals in Koch County) as per rapid needs assessment conducted by Mercy Corps on 18/8/2020. The displaced population mostly re-relocated to schools, health and nutrition facilities, and many were hosted by already vulnerable communities which caused a lot of strain and stress on existing WASH facilities. Due to resources constraints, Mercy Corps managed to distribute WASH NFIs to only 4,905(49%) of flood affected households in Nyal and Ganyiel but these NFI kits that were requested from the core pipeline were not complete and in adequate since other vital items such as soap, female dignitary kits, among others were not included. On the other side, Mundri East and West are part of the counties in Western Equatoria State that have been worst hit by the Conflicts in South Sudan since 2015. The recurrent insecurity in Mundri West and East has very much hindered efforts to provide humanitarian assistance not only lifesaving and but also developmental. The inter-agency assessment conducted in June 2019 in which Mercy Corps was actively involved indicated that, 4,878 individuals who fled the 2016 conflict from Kediba area were IDPs living in Mundri town and improvement in the security situations due to progress though quite slow of implementation of the revitalized peace agreement signed in September 2018, many of the IDPs have returned to their original land. Based on flood assessment report in September 2020, floods affected settlements along the rivers/streams that displaced 2,086 households (12,516 individuals) in Mundri West and 3699 households (22,196 individuals) in Mundri East totaling to 5,785 households (34,712 individuals). Areas most affected by the floods included, Lunjini, Kasiko and Madungama, Tegye in Mundri East County and Hai Tiriri & MRDA, Matara B & C, Hai Tarawa, Hai Payi, Hai Bandala, Landrewa & Jarangala, Amadi center/Bitti, Nyau1&2 in Mundri West County. Continuous intercommunal conflict occurred in April 2020 between Mundari of Tali and Dinka of Awerial followed by another fight in December 2020 between Mundari of Tali and Tindilo where livestock were looted, houses burnt and 29 people killed, 8 people injured and 2 male children went missing, displacing 6,973 people from 1,618 households who are now hosted in Kediba Payam of Mundri East (According to interagency assessment conducted in January 2021). This is in addition to anticipated increase in the number of returnees mainly from neighboring Countries of Uganda and Democratic Republic of the Congo which would increase stress and strain on already limited WASH services. Mercy Corps’ efforts to further strengthen sustainability in provision of WASH services would be restricted due to funding constraints as the current WASH project ends early 2021 but given that the community was responding very well to the user fee model of maintenance of WASH facilities (water points), there is need for further funding to not only strengthen this unique model but also to be able to cope with the ongoing and anticipated hike in influx of returnees since it would also require acute lifesaving support at the initial stages of their return. 2 - Project activities

The major activities include: • Strengthening the user fee model of water point maintenance in response locations where it was previous launched while launching and implementing it in response locations where it’s possible for markets to support this initiative.• Provision of safe, adequate and equitable water supply to the vulnerable population through rehabilitation/repairs of the non-functional water supply facilities, regular water quality monitoring, distribution of point-of use household water treatment chemicals with water containers, training of pump mechanics and water management committees (at least 50% females) on operation and maintenance of community water supply facilities and promotion of safe water chain. Provision of appropriate and safe sanitation services considering people with special needs including people with disabilities, women and girls through construction of communal latrines at the IDP sites, supporting and promoting household latrine constructions.• Hygiene and Sanitation promotion intervention to enable the affected population to prevent themselves form WASH related communicable diseases though intensified and targeted awareness raising activities. In the hygiene and Sanitation promotion intervention all segments of the population particularly people with special needs will be involved. Mercy Corps will conduct hygiene and Sanitation promotion activities using different approaches, including: Selection, trainings, support and follow up of Volunteer Community Hygiene Promoters (VCHPs), at least 50% females, house-to-house visits, focus group discussions, and mass campaigns at water collection sites and various public gathering areas focusing on safe human excreta disposal including children’s feces, proper hand washing, household safe water handling, food hygiene, the use of WASH facilities and solid waste management, design/adaption, production and distribution of IEC materials, establishing, training and support school WASH/Hygiene clubs and Comfee clubs for Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), distribution of WASH NFIs including dignity/MHM kits to adolescent girls and trainings of different stakeholders including community leaders and food vendors on basic hygiene. • Provision of water, sanitation and hygiene services target communities, rehabilitation/protection of water sources, construction of semi-permanent latrines and solid waste disposal facilities, and using Mercy Corps referral forms, trained VCHPs will refer identified cases with danger signs of diarrhea and malnutrition to health and nutrition facilities respectively.• Integrating COVID-19 awareness into all WASH response works to ensure comprehensive approach to achieving better public health.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

In all of its programming, MC works to promote gender equality, safety and protection of vulnerable community groups and mitigate the risk of GBV and PSEA. In this project, along with Mercy Corps’ Gender Minimum Standards, the team will take into consideration key protection principles that includes meaningful access, prioritizing safety, dignity and avoiding causing harm, and accountability, empowerment and participation of project participants. All of MC's work incorporates the crucial dynamic that gender brings into programming, and programming is developed with an analytical “Do No Harm” perspective to ensure that the safety and protection of women and girls is enhanced. As part of our staff capacity building, all MC team members will be provided with a training on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) as well as gender and protection mainstreaming. Information on PSEA will also be shared and discussed during meetings with beneficiaries. Messages on PSEA will be posted in offices and public spaces to ensure that communities receive the information. Community members will also be informed about the agency policy on PSEA as well as the reporting mechanism. Additionally, the CARM mechanisms and community leaders will be avenues by which the community can express its protection concerns and through which MC will be able to address and respond to them in timely basis.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Handling complaints from vulnerable members, MC organization will enable them to raise complaints and receive a response through an effective, accessible and safe processes the below systems are in place to ensure feedback or complaints are received • A secure box is installed in accessible locations (e.g. outside toilets) in each field location if suitable/possible and complainants can provide their feedback or lodge their complaints. • Mercy Corps field staff visiting the communities may hold informal gatherings alongside their field activities where community members can confidentially communicate a complaint • Anyone who wishes to make a complaint can directly visit the Mercy Corps office • Mercy Corps may also periodically reach out to communities and administer a questionnaire that captures feedback and complaints.

Community and Accountability Reporting Mechanism (CARM) system is in place with MC organization. The system was established from HAP benchmark that says: • handling complaints, requires that MC organization enables the people it aims to assist and other stakeholders to raise complaints and receive a response through an effective, accessible and safe processes the below systems are in place to ensure feedback or complaints are received • A secure box is installed in accessible locations (e.g. outside toilets) in each field location if suitable/possible and complainants can provide their feedback or lodge their complaints. • Mercy Corps field staff visiting the communities may hold informal gatherings alongside their field activities where community members can confidentially communicate a complaint • Anyone who wishes to make a complaint can directly visit the Mercy Corps office • Mercy Corps may also periodically reach out to communities and administer a questionnaire that captures feedback and complaints • Call or SMS Mercy Corps where feasible

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Mercy Corps will treat all feedback with confidentiality and will use beneficiaries’ feedback to inform management and programming. This will include redesigning the project or program activities to ensure our beneficiaries are protected against PSEA.
