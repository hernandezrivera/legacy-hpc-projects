Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in northeast Nigeria, particularly in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY) states, remains dire after fourteen years of conflict. Currently, over 7.9 million people face severe protection concerns and extreme deprivation, compounded by daily threats to their well-being. Vulnerabilities are highest among women and children, with approximately 83% of those most affected. The situation is complex and volatile, characterized by ongoing conflict, climate change, disease outbreaks, food shortages, economic hardship, and limited humanitarian access.
The 2024 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) indicates that around 3.8 million people in Borno, 2.1 million in Adamawa, and 1.9 million in Yobe are in urgent need. Food insecurity is a critical issue, with a June 2024 report revealing that 17.9% of households face inadequate food intake, and 74% experience severe hunger. Malnutrition rates are alarming, with 20.6% of children suffering from Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and significant mortality rates exceeding emergency thresholds.
The ongoing crisis has led to overcrowding in safer areas, stressing existing infrastructure in host communities and IDP camps and increasing the risk of disease outbreaks and sexual and gender-based violence (GBV). Deteriorating WASH facilities due to constant displacement have resulted in intensified open defecation and reduced hygiene practices.
In response to these pressing challenges, Lindii Peace Foundation (LPF) aims to leverage its presence and expertise to deliver life-saving assistance, particularly focusing on hard-to-reach areas. LPF seeks to enhance targeting of the most vulnerable populations and improve accessibility to humanitarian aid.
To address the high levels of malnutrition, mortality, and unsustainable coping strategies, LPF is submitting its Humanitarian Response Plan for the year 2025 to implement Mainstream Protection, Nutrition, and Livelihood integrated WASH emergency interventions. This initiative will provide safeguarding for the affected population and deliver essential nutrition services, prevent malnutrition, and provide comprehensive emergency responses in WASH, particularly for vulnerable groups such as children and women amid the ongoing crises in the BAY states.

1 - Needs

The project aims to address the assessed needs of vulnerable populations in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States by providing life-saving WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) support. These needs have been identified through various assessments and surveys conducted in the region. Some of the assessed needs that the project will address include: 1. Lack of access to safe water: Many communities in these states lack access to safe drinking water, which has led to the spread of waterborne diseases such as cholera and diarrhea. The project will provide access to clean water sources through the construction of boreholes, wells, and handpumps. 2. Poor sanitation: The lack of proper sanitation facilities has contributed to the spread of diseases such as typhoid and hepatitis A. The project will construct latrines, urinals, and handwashing stations in schools, health centers, and other public places. including provision of desludging services using the LPF Direct Sanitation Service Unit (DSSU) 3. Inadequate hygiene practices: Many people in these states do not have access to basic hygiene education, which has led to poor hygiene practices. The project will provide hygiene education through community sensitization programs and school-based hygiene promotion activities. 4. Displacement and displacement-induced WASH needs: The ongoing conflict in these states has led to the displacement of many people, who are now living in overcrowded camps with limited access to WASH facilities. The project will provide emergency WASH support to displaced populations through the construction of temporary latrines, water tanks, and handwashing stations. 5. Lack of maintenance and operation: Many WASH facilities constructed in these states are not being maintained or operated properly, which has led to their deterioration over time. The project will provide training on maintenance and operation to community members and local authorities to ensure the sustainability of WASH facilities.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1.1:Hydrogeological Survey, Drilling of Solar Motorised Borehole and Equipping with Infrastructure and Water Point Construction: The activity will be preceded by a Hydrogeological survey to identify a site with good groundwater potential and quality. Attention will be paid to monitoring groundwater quality through continual monitoring. Water quality will be tested upon completion of work to ensure physical, biological and chemical parameters are suitable for human consumption. Activity 1.1.2 Operation and Maintenance of Solar Motorised Boreholes (SMBH) and Hand Pump Boreholes (HPBH). Provision of supplies, materials and labour and all that is required to make the job complete in every part and detail to completely operate and maintain existing Solar water systems in a sustainable manner. The systems should operate for as long as the pump is operational (enough solar energy) and should be leak free. Water should be chlorinated at all time. Water collection points should be fully functional, with no structural defects and no broken taps. No flooding should be seen around collection points and each point should be attended by 2 people to organize spillage-free and organized water collection. Sustained O&M should include the availability of spare parts within the camps and HTH (Chlorine provided by Common Pipeline) at all times. Activity 1.1.3 Provision of Fuel and overall running cost for Generators installed on ( dualise (Hybrid) solar boreholes for extra 6 hours) in Camps. LPF will operate the provision of supplies, materials and labour and all that is required to make the job complete in every part and detail to completely operate and maintain generators on existing solar motorized water systems in a sustainable manner for additional hours. The generators will operate for a minimum of 4 hours in addition to 8 hours of solar-powered water system daily. Motorized BHs will run for 12 to 16 hours per day depending on the water demand Activity 1.1.4 Capacity building & training of Borehole chlorinators/caretakers (SMBH and Hand Pumps) and FRC Monitors, hygiene promotors, CRM Desk Attendants and latrine Cleaning Community Mobilisers ) including training on PSEA (one-off). Activity 1.1.5 Rehabilitation of SMBH with Stanchion and Reservoir; and Reticulation of Water from Borehole with adequate capacity to additional Fetching Activity 1.1.6LPF will conduct construction and rehabilitation of sanitation facilities, including minor repairs. This activity will be supported with Daily cleaning, disinfection, desludging, provision of sanitation kits, etcActivity 1.1.7LPF will support the SBC objective, by ensuring hygiene promotion are conducted in line with behavioral change, WASH Kit will be distributed to support the hygiene practices in the community.Activity 1.1.8 LPF will response to any anticipatory Action for all emergencies Including Cholera and Flooding in partnership with the sectorActivity 1.1.9LPF as part of the CVA TWG, we integrate CVA into its programming, to support the vulnerable population with Multipurpose cash to improve good hygiene.

7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

The Nexus activities for this project are taking place in Borno Adamawa and Yobe State. These areas were specifically selected due to their high levels of vulnerability to WASH Services for women and girls, and ongoing challenges related to conflict, displacement, and socio-economic instability. The intervention integrates humanitarian, development, and peacebuilding (HDP) components to address immediate needs while fostering long-term resilience and peaceful coexistence. It targets diverse groups, including women and girls, individuals with disabilities, returnees, and host community members, ensuring a holistic and inclusive approach to building safer and more equitable communities. The intervention addresses the socio-economic, cultural, and environmental challenges in the LGAs with a focus on empowering women and girls while fostering community-wide resilience. The integrated approach aims to improved WASH services, and mainstream process for mitigating gender-based violence (GBV), and promote economic inclusion.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

To ensure protection mainstreaming in our WASH project in Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe States, we will adopt the following modalities: 1. Inclusion of Protection Principles: We will integrate protection principles into all aspects of our project design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. This will involve ensuring that our activities are sensitive to the protection needs of vulnerable populations, particularly women, children, and people with disabilities. 2. Capacity Building: We will provide capacity building to our staff and partners on protection mainstreaming, including child protection, gender-based violence prevention, and disability inclusion. This will ensure that everyone involved in the project understands their roles and responsibilities in protecting beneficiaries. 3. Community Engagement: We will engage communities in all stages of the project cycle to ensure that their protection needs are addressed. This will involve consulting with community leaders, women's groups, and people with disabilities to understand their specific protection concerns and develop appropriate responses. 4. Collaboration: We will collaborate with other actors working on protection issues in the region to ensure a coordinated response to protection needs. This will involve sharing information, resources, and best practices to maximize the impact of our collective efforts. 5. Monitoring and Evaluation: We will incorporate protection indicators into our monitoring and evaluation framework to track progress in addressing protection concerns. This will enable us to identify areas where further action is needed and adjust our strategies accordingly. 6. Reporting: We will report regularly on our progress in addressing protection concerns as part of our overall project reporting. This will ensure that donors and other stakeholders are aware of our efforts in this area and can provide feedback and support as needed.
