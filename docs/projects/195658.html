Project Summary

The project aims to provide WASH services to 11,000 HHs of approximately 55,000 individuals (11,950 men, 13,250 women, 14,900 boys, 14,900 girls), most vulnerable IDPs, and host communities in Idleb and Aleppo governorates in communities and IDP informal camps.
The main intervention would be concentrating on the following:
20,000 individuals will have access to safe drinking water through the rehabilitation and maintenance of Sab Wiran water station, which feeds the Kundurli community and part of the Qabasin community, in addition to the camps affiliated with the Kundurli community, including operational support in the communities/camps, installing solar power feasible, and ensuring that all community utilities are connected to the system (schools, health facilities, etc.) in addition to developing the cost recovery system and forming the relative water committee in the targeted areas.
20,000 individuals will access safe drinking water in the (Al Omareen, Al Taawun(Kelly), Aladala Wa Altanmia, Ataa Alrahman, Khair, Al_Diaa 8, Bayrakdar(kelly) camps (Maaret Tamsrin subdistrict) by Providing 40/capita/day chlorinated drinking via water trucking to IDPs and providing the needed galvanized water tank.
Support systematic and relevant water quality indicator monitoring and reporting in high-risk areas. Chlorination and sterilization of water, daily FRC tests, and water distribution and quality monitoring. Conduct periodic water tests to ensure the validation of water for drinking and checking the sources and testing it, where Provided water will go under quality test. Periodically for the water tanks to ensure their safety.
20,000 individuals will have safe access to a clean environment and vector control through Solid waste management, including rehabilitating communal landfills and providing/maintaining the garbage containers / 100 L size garbage containers for every 50 individuals / in communities in addition to solid waste removal in camps.
20,000 individuals will have safe access to proper sanitation through the Construction and rehabilitation of latrines, and showers, including construction, plumbing, and electrical works, with all that is necessary and cleaning the latrine daily by recruiting volunteers from the camp residents. In addition, to ensure safe excreta disposal in at-risk IDP camps and other high-risk areas by dislodging septic tanks and treatment septic tanks with concentrated doses of chlorine. Septic tanks will be treated daily with two doses of concentrated Chlorine within the camps where cholera cases occur. All septic tanks dislodging tanks will be sterilized with a total amount of chlorine after each shift.
40,000 individuals within camps and communities (15% host community and 85 IDPs) will have Hygiene promotion awareness sessions during the distribution time for the targeted beneficiaries. This session will be through a trained team of males and females to organize the HHs sessions in each targeted location and provide printed awareness brochures about hygiene and how to use hygiene materials, water conservation, and rational consumption. The family will be trained during each session. We will use the IEC materials issued by the WASH and Health clusters and RCCE; the Cholera prevention procedures IEC will be explained, printed, and posted.
15000 individuals will have good hygiene practices by receiving hygiene kits dedicated to the cholera response, including each basket (soap, aqua-tab, and a 10-liter jerrycan).
Conduct capacity-building activities with WASH focal points and staff working at treatment centers and healthcare facilities on WASH interventions during cholera outbreaks.

3 - Needs

According to WASH HNO 2022, IDPs living in displacement sites are often entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance, and those in informal areas often experience worse WASH conditions making them particularly vulnerable. Also, newly displaced populations face specific WASH needs and require emergency WASH.Humanitarian needs remain immense, with an estimated 4.4 million people needing assistance in 2022, including 3.1 million food insecure and 2.8 million internally displaced. By the end of the year, 1,9 million people are projected to be living across 1.401 IDP sites (up from 1.7 million in summer).The overall operating environment in northwest Syria remains complex, with the COVID‐19 pandemic showing signs of resurgence after a phase of decline (WHO) that will result in additional needs and compound the struggles of an already vulnerable population.Inadequate WASH services contribute not only to disease outbreaks but also to increased malnutrition. Lack of improved sanitation and limited knowledge ofhygiene practices specifically for those under two years of age contribute to stunting, with long-term, and frequently permanent, effects on children’s long-termcognitive development. The lack of safe drinking water, inadequate excreta disposal, and poor hygiene practices leave many of the population at persistentrisk of preventable water related diseases. The first cholera case was confirmed in NWS on 19 September in Jarablus district in the Aleppo governorate.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project will begin by ensuring coordination with all stakeholders inside Syria to ensure a coordinated and complementary approach for comprehensive services, avoid duplication and ensure accountability to affected populations. The staff of SDI and contractors will receive induction about PSEA and GBV. SDI will create IDP representative committees, if not existing, and ensure they have female and PWD members, and coordinate project activities with them to ensure participation of Men, women, boys, girls, adolescents, and elderly in the designing, implementation, and monitoring of activities through focus groups, surveys, and complaint mechanism. A team of engineers will conduct the technical study. Materials and services will be procured based on SDI's policies and procedures competitively and transparently to rule out fraud and ensure cost-effectiveness. The project will be handed over to committees/ camp management in camps after project closure in addition to prior coordination with the WASH cluster to make sure it's followed up and enhance the sustainability of the project. Project activities will take into consideration the age and gender differences and the needs of disabled, elderly, and PWDs in addition to protection concerns, accessibility of PWDs to WASH facilities and to provide lighting, locks for privacy, and ensuring sphere standards, WASH and Health clusters and RCCEs in term of sufficient water and soup, especially under covid19 and Cholera.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: violation of property rights, hindrance to activities or beneficiary eviction. Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high; Mitigation: Conduct HLP due diligence according to clusters guidelines. Risk: Child labor, risks on workers' safety and health. Likelihood: high; Impact: high. Mitigation: zero tolerance to child labor and requirements to provide safety equipment to laborers will be included in the code of conduct and contracts signed by contractors and followed up. Risk: Explosive remnants of war and mines. Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high; Mitigation: selected sites are declared as clear. Awareness banners and awareness-raising sessions will be included. Risk: The spread of Covid19 across NW Syria. Likelihood: high, Effect: high. Risk mitigation3: SDI will take Covid19 prevention measures according to Clusters and SCHF guidelines such as social distancing, using PPE and proper hygiene for all staff and workers...etc in addition to awareness-raising of IDPs in sites. Risk: Lack of participation and accountability to beneficiaries; Likelihood: moderate, Effect: high. Involving the local authorities, camp management and IDPs representative committees including women and children (and forming committee if they are absent) in all stages of the projects, including needs assessment, designing the project activities, and HLP verification in addition to active information sharing through meetings and focus group discussions.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Beneficiaries are at risk on being exposed to any sexual misconduct by the humanitarian workers and they might not report the misconduct due to the following reason: 1. lack of knowledge of reporting mechanisms, especially for people with disabilities and women living in far away areas. 2. feeling afraid of retaliation. 3. fear of stigma. 4. fear of deprivation of assistance. 5. being unable to understand written banners for being illiterate and/or for having a visual disability. 6. for children, language might be complicated To mitigate these risks, SDI will : 1. aim at clearly announcing the hotline number and email specialized for PSEA of SDI and UN numbers. through many means, like, banners, social media, and messages to beneficiaries phones through telegram channel to ensure reaching to the biggest number of beneficiaries including women living in far away areas and people with disabilities. 2. Conduct PSEA training for all staff members and contractors/suppliers dealing with beneficiaries and make them read and sign CoC that state Zero Tolerance towards SEA violations. 3. Conduct awareness raising sessions on PSEA for beneficiaries to encourage them to report any violations and stress on the confidentiality and on eliminating any other fears that they might have. 4. Conduct awareness raising sessions for children in an appropriate language. 5. complaint boxes 6. Using PSEA field and organizational checklists tools.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption1: Access to all project locations is secured; Risk1: Access issues due to security concerns or local actors. Likelihood: low; Impact: high. Mitigation: Security assessment to targeted sites, coordination with local authorities, staff training, provision of safety tools and equipment, identification of appropriate services and medical facilities, evacuation plan, relocation (plan B). Assumption2: the borders will keep open for humanitarian response. Risk2: Closure of Border Crossing Point, Likelihood:High; Impact: Low. Mitigation: using locally available materials while advocating through advocacy channels. Assumption3: Needed material, workshops, and skilled and non-skilled workers are available in the project location. Risk3: Some material, workshop, or skilled and non-skilled workers become unavailable. Likelihood: very low; Impact: high. Mitigation: RNA and market assessment. SDI already has experienced staff and engineers in the area, and has the capacity to procure items from a wider geographic range. Risk4: Danger on children or residents due to construction or digging works; Mitigation: Awareness raising, clear warning signs, safety tapes..etc. to keep civilians out of danger zones. Risk5: Fire risk in camp; Likelihood: moderate; Impact: high. Mitigation: Fire mitigation, awareness, training and equipment is one of the main components of this project.
