Project Summary

The outbreak of fighting in Sudan on April 15, 2023, has triggered a devastating humanitarian crisis in neighboring South Sudan, particularly in the country's northern states. Since the onset of the conflict, a massive influx of people fleeing Sudan has overwhelmed the border points between the two countries, primarily in Upper Nile, Northern, and Western Bahr el Ghazal States. As of 28 November, 2024, over 882,693 individuals have crossed the border through more than 14 monitored official Points of Entry, with the majority entering through the Wunthow border point in Upper Nile, seeking refuge from the ongoing violence.
Since the start of the Sudan crisis, IOM has facilitated over 507,793 returnees with Onward Transportation Assistance from Point Entry to Transit Centre in Renk; and among them 226,503 further assisted by IOM from Transit Center to final destinations of their choice.
To promote safe and dignified movement of returnees, IOM also provides protection, health, WASH, site management and maintenance services to vulnerable returnees, working in coordination with the broader humanitarian and development community in both transit centres and areas of return, incorporating referral pathways to and from Government and partners.
Onward transportation assistance is a critical component of the Emergency Response Plan (ERP) and will be facilitated by IOM via river, air, and road to ensure that returnees reach their final destinations safely and securely. A cessation of OTA services would have a significant impact, leaving Renk county with a population that far exceeds available economic opportunities and services. In addition, many returnees do not share a linguistic, historical or cultural background with Renk county, making local integration and land allocation extremely challenging. Overcrowding at border points, host communities, and transit centers, will lead to increased tensions and conflict over scarce resources and restricted access to essential services. Vulnerable groups, including women, children, and the elderly, will be further exposed to protection and health risks, such as gender-based violence, sexual exploitation, and disease outbreaks (i.e. Cholera), OTA remains the most impactful solution to the faced challenges of Renk county.

1. Needs

South Sudan continue to receive influx of returnees from Sudan due to the ongoing conflict in that country. Between October - November 2024, IOM DTM tracked an average of 1200 individuals arriving at Points of Entry daily, with the majority being returnees; in recent months, however, the arrival of refugees had increased. Estimates from the HCT and data based on the arrivals, the projection is that over 250,000 individuals will cross the border into South Sudan in 2025, among them, 85,000 people will be in need of OTA support and services to reach final destination of their choice.Protection monitoring conducted at PoEs indicates a high number of vulnerable arrivals, including unaccompanied children, PwDs, and those with urgent medical needs. According to the last head count conducted by IOM DTM , an estimated 13,784 returnees are in Transit and collective centers and 45,114 returnees have been integrated into host communities. Furthermore, on average there are 2500 returnees in Malakal waiting for their onward flights and boats every week. According Joint Intention Survey conducted in last quarter of 2024, in Renk transit over 80% of returnees indicated that they wish to go to their areas of origin or relocation.Many of these returnees are arriving in South Sudan with limited resources and no means of transportation to their final destinations. As a result, there is a critical need for OTA to support these returnees in reaching their homes and communities As of 28 November 2024, more than 612,966 individuals have arrived through Renk. 76% of the arrivals are South Sudanese, 23% are Sudanese, less than 1% are from other nationalities. These figures represent only those identified by humanitarian actors at borders, thus actual figures might be higher than reported. Among the arrivals, 51% are female, more than 46% are below 18, while 4% are older than 60 years. The people reaching the border are exhausted and without resources to proceed to their destination. South Sudan has a limited transportation infrastructure, making it difficult for returnees to travel to their homes and communities. This is especially true for returnees who are located in remote areas.

2. Project activities

Registration and vulnerability screening: IOM will continue to conduct vulnerability screenings to identify, register and prioritize the most vulnerable individuals at PoE and ascertain their voluntariness to return to their areas of origin or relocation. Registration and vulnerability screening also allows the provision of documentation, humanitarian services, and referrals to other partners for the returnees. This activity will be implemented at the point of entries (PoE), transit centers, docking station and airport. Bio-metric registration was introduced in 2024 in Renk by IOM DTM. Data sharing was subsequently done in Malakal for those arrived and then departed from Malakal transit center. Chartered flights: To facilitate the return of displaced people to their areas of origin or relocation, IOM will arrange charter flights from various locations (Malakal) to the nearest accessible points (Wau, Bentiu/Rubkona, and Juba). IOM flights also pick up returnees from Yida, who intends to travel to Malakal/Wau and Juba. IOM has already contracted several air transport service providers for this purpose. The charter flights will be offered to those returnees who face challenges in travelling by road or boats due to lack of infrastructure and security risks. The cost of air transportation is high, as the available aircraft are small and can only land at limited airports. Voucher for boat movement and fuel supply: Transportation Token/Tickets: IOM provides restricted transportation tokens/tickets to returnees who want to travel from Renk to Malakal and then to other places by boat, such as Unity, Fangak and so on. To facilitate the movement of returnees from Renk, Malakal and other places IOM will collaborate with the Government of South Sudan and other private entities/NGOs that run boats/vehicles by supplying them with fuel. Cash assistance for last-mile movement: IOM will provide one-time cash assistance equivalent to USD 20 for last-mile movement to the final destination. This cash assistance is conditional, upon their boarding of the chartered flights. The cash amount is not determined by the household size, but by the individual needs of men, women, boys and girls. Improve conditions of Transit site: The transit site in Malakal on average can host 3800 returnees, which needs continuous repair and maintenance. As the transit site is part of OTA, so IOM will continue to maintain and operate the transit site Health screening: IOM will continue to support returnees with health screening while on transit
