Project Summary

Bonyan Organization designed its CCCM intervention based on MSNA analysis of affected population in NW Syrian, and HNO 2021 key messages, as well as, in line with SOs of HRP and CCCM Cluster. The project aims to support provision of humanitarian assistance to IDPs in NW Syria, targeting 14,900 INDV (7,950 M, 6,950 F), through maintaining the camps facilities, coordinate multi-sectoral assistance, improve the management quality and accountability, and enhance\create fire prevention system. The project activities fall into two main components:
*Running the established planned IDP sites
Bonyan established 2 camps in Salqen and Al-bab district, all camps were established in coordination with all relevant stakeholders and registered in CCCM database. The planned camps have all the needed infrastructures. To ensure the continuance of the camp's activities, Bonyan is planning to conduct camp operational and Regular maintenance activities.
BO will coordinate with humanitarian actors and develop MoUs to provide multi-sector assistance to ensure the sustainability of services in the planned IDP sites which is including Shelter & NFI, WASH, food, health services, protection, and education, with applying protection mainstreaming activities such as lighting the key facilities, provide access for PwD to wash facilities ... etc. As needed, identification in gaps in services, and advocacy for improved service delivery, especially on their adherence to sphere standards and equal access for all, particularly for vulnerable groups (i.e., PwD, female-headed HHs, elderly, women and children).
*Improve the management quality and accountability, BO has previous experience in enhancing the management skills for both (camp management, community committees) therefore as a first step BO will conduct wide consultation with IDPs living in the site to form the camp management committees, besides helping in developing their ToRs. The committees will receive capacity building training to be responsible for carrying out site management activities.
*Formation of Safety Volunteer Units (SVUs) in 20 camps in Kelly,Atarb and Maaret Tamsrin, the SVUs will be selected based on certain criteria to ensure their ability and readiness towards their responsibilities as safety volunteers. Bonyan will provide them with, IFAK training, Fire safety training.Moreover, BO will establish small blocks to store fire equipment. Conduct fire safety awareness sessions, provide fire blanket and train IDPs on how to use them, especially women as they are the ones who stay in tents the most.Establishing personal cooking areas for each HH to be used as a kitchen, to prevent one of the essential fire causes. Ensure the redness of all fire prevention distributed equipment. Throughout the project, BO will select the target IDP community in coordination with CCCM Cluster and will ensure the engagement of the selected community, do no harm principles, targeting the most vulnerable people and applying AAP mechanisms.

4 - Needs

Based on CCCM cluster the number of IDPs residing in IDP sites is 1.6 million as of Dec 2020. the households in IDP sites are entirely dependent on humanitarian assistance while services and infrastructure in these sites are overstretched and in need of repair. At the start of 2021, the CCCM Cluster reported that fire incidents affected 34 IDP sites and led to 18 injuries and the destruction of 55 sheltersBy mid-February, 900,000 people had been displaced due to hostilities in Idlib governorate, according to UNOCHA. Given the increase in the number of people seeking shelter in IDP sites in NW Syria, severe overcrowding was reported as a growing concern. As of Oct2020, there are 1,172 IDP sites listed in Cluster’s database hosting 1.6M IDPs, the vast majority of which are self-settled camps. The issues of over-populated sites and lack of camp management continue to be prevalent concerns, especially in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic where the application of preventive, response and mitigation guidance is essential. Moreover, overcrowding in these sites has resulted in many protection risks, lack of privacy, and increased risk of GBV. Over concentration of people has put essential services and infrastructure under strain leading to a lack of or limited access to latrines, bathing spaces, and other common facilities’ usage. IDP sites along the Turkish-Syria border in Idleb are prone to flooding. Inadequate shelter and essential services in these sites may lead to increasing the risks of COVID-19. This requires timely response to address immediate needs for IDPBO consider the fire mitigation and training activities as a priority because of the lack of awareness and equipment’s in camps

5 - Implementation of activities

BO will coordinate with relevant stakeholders to conduct a need assessment survey in order to collect up to date data about CCCM needs of IDPs (keeping in mind the specific needs of different age and gender groups), in addition, to collect any other necessary data (i.e., market assessment or security assessment). Then, BO will select beneficiaries based on clear vulnerability criteria to ensure provision of support to the most vulnerable IDPs. Along with the implementation, MEAL team will carry out ongoing M&E activities to verify project indicators. CLCCM/CA1: BO will coordinate with humanitarian actors and develop MoUs to provide multi-sector assistance including Shelter & NFI, WASH, food, health services, protection, and education, with applying protection mainstreaming activities.CLCCM/CA2: BO will identify gaps in services, and advocate for improved service delivery, especially on their adherence to sphere standards and equal access for all, particularly for the vulnerable groups. CLCCM/CA3: BO will conduct Camp operational and Regular maintenance activities including repair of lighting, fence and other camp facilities, and graveling of communal areas and roads, especially after heavy rains,cleaning of sanitation and communal facilities, provision of fire extinguishers and fuel for running generators and other maintenance costs. CLCCM/CA4: BO will conduct wideconsultation with IDPs living in the site to establish the camp management committees, and will assist in developing their ToRs. CLCCM/CA7: BO will assist in formation of Rescue Teams and provide them with training on Firefighting, First Aid, and Rescue Principles in case of emergency.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The main protection risks can be listed as follows:risk: The danger of the current global pandemic. spreading of COVID-19 between the camp population and administrative.Possibility: HighMitigation Measures:- PPE kits will be distributed to all camps staff.- Screening all IDPs temperature on the sites entrance gate.- following WHO\CCCM guidance in case of emergency.2- risk: Sexual and physical violence is one of the main risks associated with the use of latrines in the sites.Possibility: mediumMitigation Measures:-Latrines\showers for men and women will be separated.-Latrines\showers will be well-lighted.- site security guards will do night shift. 3- Risk: lack of education and long distance to the closest school.Possibility: mediumMitigation Measures:- BO education team has the capacity to cover some of the targeted camps under its running projects.-coordination with the education cluster and other partners to provide their services in the camps.-all education facilities (tents) will be installed inside the camps.4- Risk: the risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (for women and girls)Possibility: highMitigation Measures:forming a confidential complaint mechanism.Training employee on the code of conduct, PSEA, raises awareness of beneficiaries and informs on available complaints mechanism.The M&E team will include post-monitoring and PSEA topics in supervision sessions to identify any misconduct.7- Risk: The firefighters' teams will contain males only.Possibility: highMitigation Measures:BO will conduct awareness sessions to show the importance of women participation in the teams as in the majority of fire cases women were exposed to the danger of fire.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: the availability of certified firefighting materials for the emergency points in the Syrian market especially the firefighting blankets.Procedures: -BO will conduct a market assessment to get better understanding of the availability of all requested materials in the Turkey\Syria market.Risk: Exposure of materials from which firefighting points consist to theft and messing around.Possibility: HighEffect: Moderate.Mitigation Measures: Small cement rooms will be built to keep these materials and providing rooms by doors with a big number of keys, the keys will be distributed to firefighting committee members, in-charge person for the camp sector and camp manager where we guarantee the availability of one person at least out of these ones if the fire starts.Risk: Unavailability of compensation or incentives for firefighting committees, consequently they will not commit to their tasks sufficiently.Possibility: Moderate Effect: Low.Mitigation Measures:-Coordination will be done with camp managers and sectors managers inside camps to select persons of good manners and enthusiastic on whom SCA in firefighting response.The priority will be given to them for CFW plans and/ or other livelihood opportunities in which SCA or other agencies may control and manage.Risk: Fire emerges in the site and fire volunteering units don't know about it due to lack of communication. Mitigation: - WhatsApp groups will be used to report fire units, conducting awareness sessions and emergency alarm bill will be used.Assumption: the spread of COVID-19.Procedures: All Protection Recommendations and preventions will be applied according to COVID-19 Protection Guidelines during the fire prevention training.
