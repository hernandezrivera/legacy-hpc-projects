Project Summary

In a context characterized by prolonged strain and economic isolation-de-development-, vulnerable families, including those with a large number of children, female-headed households and families with people with disabilities, have adopted different coping mechanisms to deal with the high level of poverty and vulnerabilities. According to OCHA (2019), the deteriorating socio-economic condition in Gaza has a negative consequence on children’s rights and their ability to access education and protection services as manifested in an increase of school drop-outs and child labour (OCHA, 2019, UNICEF 2018, and Tdh 2019). This Action involves multi-sectoral interventions that are consistent with the strategic objectives of Protection, Education, and Food Security Sector. The project aims to eliminate child labour in the Gaza Strip and to increase children's access to formal education system and other vocational training opportunities through provision of child protection services, psychosocial services, educational reintegration, family support and increased community mobilization toward the issue of child Labour and dropout of schools. Along with protecting children's rights to access to education, this project focuses on the right of the child to be protected from economic exploitation and from performing any work that is likely to be hazardous and could negatively affect their overall wellbeing and development. The project will target 8985 beneficiaries, including 700 working and or dropped out of school children (450 boys and 250 girls).
The proposed activities will include responsive and remedial protection activities. The responsive activities will include: (1) provision of structured PSS for working and or dropped out of school children that aim to increase their feeling of safety, connectedness, to be emotionally assured, and to assess and identify the main psychological and mental health needs. (2) provision of child protection case management, including provision of remedial classes, provision of individual and group counseling sessions, joint child-parent interaction activities, life skills, integration into schools and vocational training centers, referral to specialized mental health services. (3) Provision of economic support to 50 families of working children through food voucher conditional restricted cash transfer as in-kind modality (285 persons). Finally, training sessions to 7925 community members, mainly caregivers, on the importance of eliminating child labour (3963 men and 3962 women). The project team will provide services to the identified cases through the child protection centers, distributed across the Gaza Strip, one in each governorate. A high level of coordination with relevant ministries and relevant clusters, including Protection and Education. Finally, Tdh will report its intervention to Protection cluster, UNRWA, Child Protection Area of Responsibilities Working Group, and FSS. Tdh will be actively involved in cash-programming working group and will closely coordinate with FSS team, relevant ministries, and all other relevant actors. The total project budget is USD 432,000.

1 - Needs

The ongoing Israeli blockade has devastating consequences on all aspects of life. Gaza Strip has one of the highest unemployment rates in the world (52%), and more than half of Gaza's population live in poverty (PCBS, 2018). According to 2020 HNO, the deteriorated humanitarian situation has forced and trapped poor families into poverty, undermining and jeopardizing their abilities to afford costs associated with school enrolments such as education supplies and transportation costs. According to UNICEF (2019), among 6-9-year-old children in the Gaza Strip, about 1.4% are out-of-school, among 10-15-year-old children in the Gaza Strip, about 4.3% are out-of-school. Also, from Tdh database on out of school children targeted over the 7 past years, 11% of out of school aged 6-12 years, 42% aged 13-15 years, and 47% aged 16-17+). Being out of schools expose children to multiple vulnerabilities and risks, including being engaged in child labour, subjected to early marriage, and participating in Great March of Return Demonstrations that exposes children to the risk of being killed and or injured, such risk is, unfortunately, becoming a serious child protection concern in the Gaza Strip. The deteriorating socio-economic conditions in Gaza have also negative consequences on children’s rights and their ability to access education and protection services as manifested in school drop-outs and child labour (OCHA, 2019, UNICEF 2018, and Tdh 2019). According to 2020 HNO, about 20,000 children are in need of MHPSS responses, along with 250,689 children already acutely vulnerable suffering from severe or moderate mental health disorders (Protection Cluster 2019). Thus, within such a volatile context, the need for child protection services is incredibly important. Against this backdrop, Tdh and its partner organizations have been offering child protection services for working and/or dropped out of schools. Up to date, Tdh jointly with its implementing partners across the Gaza Strip have managed to identify 3453 dropped out and/or working children (2910 boys and 543 girls), of them, 1308 were working (1086 boys and 222 girls). The reintegration of working and recently dropped out of school children into schools and vocational centers is a core activity of Tdh child protection services. So far, a total of 1162 children (238 girls and 924 boys) were reintegrated into vocational training centers and schools. The number of boys receiving services reflects the higher number of out of school working boys compared to girls. A product of the previous phase of the project, SPSS dataset was developed. The SPSS dataset includes detailed information on all identified cases of working and/or dropped out of school children; currently, there are tens of children have not yet received any services, due to financial limitations. From Tdh 5 years of experience in working with child labourers and out of school children, it is noticeable that child labour negatively impacts the physical, cognitive, behavioral growth and development. Additionally, working children were exposed to physical, emotional, and sexual exploitation from their employers, peers, and community members (Tdh, 2018). This project focuses on the right of the child to be protected from economic exploitation and from performing any work that is likely to be hazardous or to interfere with his or her education, or to be harmful to health or physical, mental, spiritual, moral or social development. Under this Project, Tdh will continue to provide child protection services to vulnerable children, including working and out of school children. Beneficiaries of the project, both children and adult were consulted during the project design phase. Boys, girls, men, and women from different age group were involved in the analysis of beneficiaries' needs, identification of project activities, and designing the project .

2 - Activities

Identification of vulnerable working and/or dropped out children will be a priority for the project team, the aim is to identify about new 700 working and out of school children distributed as 450 boys and 250 girls. The process of identifying children will involve conducting field visits to working areas and to the most deprived areas in Gaza. It will also involve visiting governmental schools and obtaining a list of dropped out of school children. Data obtained from the governmental schools will be validated by home visits, case by case, as the reliability and validity of MoEHE data on drop out of school children is not high. With regard to working girls, special attention will be dedicated to identifying them through home visits. Activities proposed under this action are consistent with the Education Cluster objective (relates to the second Strategic Objective), as proposed activities, include the provision of education services to out of school children, such as reintegration drop out of school children into the official education system and vocational training centers and providing them with PSS support, provision of catch up. The project team will provide services to the identified cases through the five child protection centers across the Gaza Strip. Under this Action, both boys and girls will receive protection services according to their assessed needs. The provided responsive activities will include PSS and individual counseling to the identified cases of working and or dropped out of school children, with particular focus on children victims of worst forms of child labour. New identified cases of working and/ or dropped out of school children will be provided with structured PSS individual counseling in order to increase their feeling of safety, connectedness, to be emotionally assured, and to assess and identify the main psychological and mental health needs. Additionally, under this Action and to prevent further harm, the activities will include provision of child protection case management, such as the provision of remedial classes, provision of individual and group counseling sessions, joint child-parent interaction activities, life skills, integration into schools and vocational training centers, referral to specialized mental health services. Additionally, the Project will involve the provision of economic support to 50 families of working children and or school dropped out (a total of 285 members). The economic support would motivate families to reintegrate their children into schools or vocational training centers and not to send their children to work. Within the scope of this project, Tdh will utilize the mixed method modality that involves (1). Food voucher of 6 months (USD 100 per month, biweekly distributed) in parallel with cash support to establish small projects, the modality that will be used is in-kind modality, each family will receive in-kind goods that equal of a value of around $ 2500 to establish their own micro-project. The selection will be based on a vulnerability gird with a scoring system. Tdh has a proven track record of supporting poor families with income-generating activity. Although beneficiaries prefer a cash transfer as compared with an equal-valued in-kind transfer, Tdh will utilize the in-kind modality as this option is economically viable, especially for the most deprived people as it reduces the risk of spending the assistance to cover daily living expenses. Beneficiaries will be encouraging to select projects that have short-life cycles such as mini-markets in order to quickly generate income. Families with the highest scores will receive in-kind assistance. Tdh will make sure that families will identify their projects based on the experience they have; families will have unrestricted flexibility for purchases as they will identify the items they need. Children coming from families that will not receive cash assistance will be provided by school stationery and uniform.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Tdh will fully respect, protect, and fulfil beneficiaries’ safety and fully respect their dignity. Tdh will achieve this by protecting beneficiaries’ personal and sensitive data such as names and Id numbers. As a good practice for Tdh, no action would be planned and/or implemented without the child’s agreement, under the supervision of her/his legal gradient. Additionally, Tdh will not publish or report any data could reveal any identification data, all publications/report will include only aggregated data. Tdh and partner organizations will also involve beneficiaries in as many decisions as possible, including monitoring the project implementation. All interventions will seek to avoid exacerbating the situation for children by ensuring properly planned and implemented interventions that take into account children’s and their family's needs to access to humanitarian services. All services will be provided in a safe protection environment that maintains and respect beneficiaries' rights, including respecting and maintaining their privacy and not demeaning them. Tdh has zero-tolerance to exposing beneficiaries to violence and or harmful practices, all measure will be taken into account to grate this, including full compliance to Tdh Child Safeguarding Policy (CSP), PSEA Policy, Code of Conduct and confidential mechanisms. All staff members are trained on the CSP, PSEA and the Code of Conduct and have signed the policies.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

In respect of equality and impartiality, all identified cases, regardless of their gender, age groups, and ability status will receive protection services according to their assessed and identified needs. All Project beneficiaries will have equal access to services, including beneficiaries with disabilities and beneficiaries living in the Access Restricted Areas. Tdh will also target equally men and women without any regard to any discriminatory factors such as age. The proposed activities are based on the different needs' assessments done by the Tdh team in which beneficiaries engaged heavily and all their needs were included in the project activities. During the project design, beneficiaries were also consulted through conducting a series of FGDs and interviews. Beneficiaries will also be part of the project monitoring. Children were also involved in the assessment and design of the project and will be actively engaged in monitoring the project activities. Finally, as any advocacy product of this project, children will propose, design, and implement such products.
