Project Summary

This project will support critical public facilities in maintaining safe and sufficient drinking water and adequate WASH services to reduce public health risks (WASH Cluster SO 1). Interventions will focus on improved water quality and basic sanitation in the communities, schools, and health institutions.
The proposed intervention builds on the detailed assessment completed by Norwegian Refugee Council (NRC) and Action Against Hunger (AAH) end of 2017 and updated by AAH in 2018 of WASH needs/gaps in public desalination plants, schools and health institutions operated by CMWU, MoEHE, and MoH. Based on the known needs, critical interventions will be made through a hardware approach (construction, minor rehabilitation/maintenance, and procurements related to WASH services and water quality) and software solutions (capacity building of technical staff in O+M of small-scale desalination plants/RO-units, Hygiene ToT for school health coordinators, and awareness raising for truckers/consumers of publicly supplied drinking water about safe water handling).
Based on the evidence from assessments, both AAH’s 2018 assessment of public desalination plants and other work completed in the WASH Cluster’s Water Quality Working Group (WQWG), tailored advocacy and awareness messaging will be conducted to address duty bearers of the right to safe water. AAH as the new lead of the WQWG and NRC as the previous lead, and with strong advocacy capacity within each organisation will maximise in partnership the outcomes of advocacy campaigns and water quality interventions. The proposed intervention ensures protection mainstreaming as it is guided by protection principles and is taking into account safety and dignity; meaningful access; accountability as well as participation and empowerment.

Needs

In 2018, Gaza has moved rapidly closer to a potential collapse of public service provision due to an inability to conduct maintenance owing to a lack of funding for parts & salaries reaching the public sector; and an inability to maintain operations owing to a lack of electrical power & spare parts. The protracted crisis seen in Gaza has been exacerbated in 2018 & urgent response is required to enable basic access to water and sanitation. According to PWA, public health related to water quality & access to adequate WASH facilities is a major concern in public institutions. Infrastructure in schools has deteriorated significantly over the past 11 years with negative impact on quality of education. Lack of regular budget for running costs, overcrowding, and the electricity crisis have seriously affected WASH conditions in 283 public schools (UN 2017: Gaza – 10 years later). Toilet blocks are badly maintained, insufficient to meet needs, and inaccessible for students with disabilities. Almost half of Gaza's students, with higher proportion of females, refuse to use toilets in school stating lack of cleanliness/toilet paper/soap/waste bins, lack of lockable doors, overcrowding, and lack of safety/privacy as reasons (UNICEF 2016: WASH KAP survey). Brackish or seawater desalination RO units lack repair/maintenance, are insufficient to meet daily drinking water needs, and stop functioning when electricity cuts. Insufficient water availability & lack of basic cleaning & hygiene materials negatively affect a hygienic school environment & personal hygiene. Support in meeting general needs for minor rehabilitation/regular maintenance of existing WASH facilities was requested for toilets, drinking water points, drinking/domestic water storage tanks, and 91 desalination plants operated by MoEHE. Construction of new toilet blocks, installation of new drinking water points, solar panels, & equipping 5 MoEHE laboratories (1 per governorate) for water testing was considered a priority need to ensure safe WASH services, accompanied by capacity building measures for technical staff responsible for O+M of desalination plants/RO units & water quality testing (AAH 2018: Public Desalination Plants Assessment). AAH assessment for public desalination plants demonstrates that MoH, health service providers in Gaza, including 13 public hospitals & 29 primary health centers, face challenges in their drinking water supply. 13 hospitals are supplied by 16 desalination plants, which provide water for drinking, sterilization of medical instruments, and washing of clothes. All 16 plants require maintenance & spare parts. In Shifa hospital, the largest in Gaza providing health services to 40% of Gaza’s population, 8 desalination plants are in need for urgent maintenance & rehabilitation to meet critical needs, as currently only 50% of needs are met. 32 primary health centers are equipped with RO units for drinking water supply & meet additional needs by purchasing privately trucked drinking water. Although WASH facilities in hospitals suffer from similar conditions as public schools, there is no detailed data available yet, as assessment conducted by UNICEF/GVC is not finalized yet to identify repairs needs. According to AAH assessment for public desalination plants, CMWU operates 45 small scale desalination plants that need rehabilitation, regular maintenance & spare parts, and solar panels to be operational during emergencies. Lack of O+M & lack of technical capacity of responsible staff affects access to sufficient & safe drinking water, that lead to produce only around 12% of the plants’ capacities.68% of desalinated water (from small scale public/private providers) being consumed in Gaza is susceptible to biological contamination. Lack of awareness about water handling during distribution, storage, and consumption adds to contamination (PWA/NRC & WASH Cluster Partners 2016: Assessment Report; AAH 2017: Water Truckers Survey Report).

Activities

The activities are proposed to ensure critical access to WASH services, prioritising safety & dignity taking into account gender, age & diversity: Activity 1: Provision of material & technical support to public desalination plants/RO units operated by MoEHE, MoH, CMWU & local charitable facilities1.1 For Public schools(1) AAH: minor rehabilitation, routine maintenance & spare parts for unoperated 23 desalination units operated by MoEHE including installation of PV solar energy systems.(2) NRC: Equipment of 2 (out of 5) MoEHE laboratories to allow for biological & chemical drinking water testing (3) AAH: capacity building for 20 technical staff of MoEHE responsible for water quality testing, O+M of desalination plants in public schools1.2 For Public hospitals and primary health centers to ensure women, children and men, incl. people with disability and elderly, have safe access to drinking water. (1) AAH: minor rehabilitation, routine maintenance & spare parts for 13 desalination RO units in 13 hospitals operated by MoH(2) AAH: minor rehabilitation, routine maintenance & spare parts for 26 desalination RO units in health centers(3) AAH: chemicals for desalination plant in Shifa hospital/Gaza City(4) AAH: capacity building for 20 technical staff of MoH responsible for water quality testing & O+M of desalination plants 1.3 For CMWUAAH: minor rehabilitation, regular maintenance & spare parts for 23 desalination plants operated by CMWU incl. installation of PV solar energy systems to overcome electricity crisis, to secure safe access to drinking water for women, children & men. Capacity building for 20 technical staff of CMWU responsible for water quality testing and O+M of desalination plants.1.4 For local Charitable Facilities(1) NRC: detailed assessment of existing desalination plants.(2) NRC: minor rehabilitation, regular maintenance, and spare parts for 10 plants & installation of PV solar panels. Activity 2: Provision of adequate WASH facilities in public schools in Gaza by NRC(1) Detailed WASH needs assessment in 283 public schools of boys & girls(2) Construction of 5 new toilet blocks for girls and rehabilitation/maintenance of 78 existing toilet blocks for boys & girls(3) Installation of new & rehabilitation/maintenance of existing drinking water points in 20 schools(4) Provision of additional & rehabilitation/maintenance of existing drinking/domestic water storage tanksActivity 3: Provision of awareness, trainings and hygiene materials, to enhance safe hygiene & water management practices (AAH and NRC).Local IPs will support in ensuring community engagement, conducting assessments and post-implementation surveys, and implementing soft components of the project (capacity building, hygiene promotion). The local IPs have sufficient partnership experience with AAH & NRC and successfully proved their capacity in implementing suggested components of the project.(1) Hygiene ToT in 26 public schools for boys & girls engaged with the school health coordinators to train school health committees & cleaning staff delivering key messages on safe water handling and hygiene(2) Provision of hygiene cleaning items in 26 schools incl. special girl’s needs(3) Training about safe water handling practices for 140 consumers (community focal points) of drinking water produced by CMWU operated desalination plants(4) Training of 70 water truckers selling drinking water from public/private desalination plants to improve water quality standards & best practices Activity 4: Design of advocacy materials in view of enhancing accountability of duty bearers & Third States in the context of the right to safe, sufficient and affordable water (AAH and NRC).(1) Private presentations of water quality-related research and findings(2) Consumer awareness raising events at community level to strengthen consumers as self-advocates for the right to safe drinking water (3) Strategic media outreach

Indicators

AAH and NRC proposed interventions will support public service providers to cover part of the gaps and needs in WASH facilities to maintain their critical services. AAH and NRC will maintain close coordination with public service providers, WASH cluster and other organizations working in the targeted areas to avoid duplication and ensure complementarity. AAH and NRC will contribute to exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community, and to be used for advocacy of purposes.AAH and NRC adopted M&E frameworks to meet planned targets with quality. Project staff, in collaboration with M&E unit, ensures that a tracking and reporting system is in place to document project progress toward desired outcomes. An indicator matrix records data from the field on a monthly basis to measure progress and provide corrective measures where needed. AAH and NRC support quality control measures, accountability and transparency towards beneficiaries through establishment of a beneficiary feedback mechanism with well-known access by targeted beneficiaries. In addition, the M&E unit conducts regular field visits to monitor project progress and feedback key findings to project teams. On a technical level, AAH and NRC staff monitor tightly the progress in daily implementation.AAH and NRC with the support of the local partners will ensure communities’ engagement for the interventions in public schools, female and male students & teaching staff and parents will be informed in meetings about the project objective and main activities. In FGDs with male and female student representatives including CwDs, parents’ clubs, and school health coordinators, specific hygiene-related concerns and appropriate activities will be discussed and agreed considering students with special needs, age and gender specific concerns and educational approaches. Within the target institutions and within each community where WASH activities will take place in schools and hospitals, the respective community including women’s committees will be informed in meetings and leaflets about the core project information including the feedback and complaints mechanism. This will be coupled with outreach messaging related to safe water handling and water quality. For the overall project activities, male and female beneficiaries and members of community representative structures are consulted in FGDs as part of a post-project review to provide feedback on the efficiency and effectiveness of the project for continuous improvement.The following are the expected results and indicators:- Improve drinking water quality and access to safe drinking water by rehabilitating and resuming operation of 69 public desalination plants and connect them to PV solar energy systems. Indicator D.1 # of essential maintenance and operational items supplied to WASH facilities (target 73 facilities, to support 54,429 people, 27,214 female students and 27,213 male students)- Improve hygiene knowledge and practice for male and female students and teachers in schools as well as training on safe water handling for consumers. Indicator B.4 # of people received hygiene materials/kits (targets 20,800 people, 10,400 female students and 10,400 male students)- Capacity building for 60 operators of public desalination plants representing CMWU, MoEHE and MoH and 70 water truckers. Indicator B.5 # of key institutions with improved access to WASH services (targets 3 )- 5 Advocacy events organised with/for duty bearers and Third States representatives and 3 products focusing on water quality.
