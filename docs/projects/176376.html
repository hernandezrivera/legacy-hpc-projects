Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to provide displaced and affected vulnerable host communities with essential Non-Food item and shelter materials to ensure their protection and life with dignity through distribution of basic household and shelter materials as well as build the resilience and preparedness of communities to crisis and shocks. It will be implemented in Jongele, Jur River , Wau and Tonj North . This main objective is consistent with Sectoral objective 5 which aims to ensure participation of displaced persons, to enhance the community’s ability to respond to new shocks, build resilience and support existing skills.
The project will target vulnerable displaced, returnees and affected vulnerable host communities and will involve both support with in-kind as well as cash and vouchers to support to targeted and vulnerable households. The main cluster objectives that this project support include Sectoral objective 3 which aims to support the most vulnerable returnees and host communities with durable solutions that rebuild lives through shelter and NFI solutions.
NCA will also support with distribution of in-kind Shelter and NFI materials and also carry out training to communities and local partners to impart skills on improving accountability, improved feedback systems and also preparedness and response initiatives, we will train community members and committees on assessments and post distribution monitoring and reporting. The project is 9 months and will be delivered through local partners working closely with local government departments, UN and other international organizations. NCA will ensure coordination and that quality of the response and to ensure that it delivered within agreed timelines.
Cash vouchers are also included as a conditional cash distribution where assessments carried out by NCA have shown strong markets, and access to markets to provide right holders a more flexible access to much needed NFI and shelter materials. NFI GIK commodities will be distributed where markets are non-functional such as in Baggari Corridor.

1 - Needs

Assessments carried out in Pibor, Tonj North, Warrap, Jur River and Baggari Corridor of Wau County between December 2020 and February 2021 indicate high levels of needs for Emergency Shelter and NFI support needs among displaced and returnee communities. In Tonj North, Warrap , and Jur River , the shelter and NFI needs relate to newly displaced populations needing support for shelter while in Wau and Baggari River , the need is for rehabilitation and improvement of shelters for transitional as well as vulnerable host communities . In Pibor, communities affected by floods require emergency Shelter and NFI materials to re-establish after the devastation of floods and from intercommunal violence sweeping the area resulting in loss of household goods and destruction of property and homes. All these locations also have intersecting multisectoral needs due to a combination of factors and will need support with WASH and Food assistance as well as protection. These areas also correspond with some of the counties on the high priority list as outlined in the 2021 HRP and prioritised by the NFI, GBV, food security and WASH clusters. In Baggari Corridor, for instance, assessment findings carried out by NCA in January and February 2021, shows that many villages are experiencing large numbers of returnees which also consequently places NFIS and Shelter services under severe strain. Many shelters have become dilapidated and need to be improved. The cessation of hostilities and prevailing calm that has enabled many families that had been IDPS in Wau town to return thus need to support with transitional shelter materials as well.Many households do not have proper shelters and need improving them to more permanent structures but need support to help them access local materials to retrofit transitional shelters. With return, families that had been scattered are in need of more dignified shelter assistance and NCA proposes to carry out a market analysis of local shelter availability and market and support transitional shelter construction through cash and vouchers schemes.Jur River and Warrap North are receiving large numbers of internally displaced people fleeing the conflict in neighbouring Tonj North and Tonj East. Many communities fleeing conflict in Wardalelo have sought refuge in a number of locations in Jur River where local host communities have welcomed and sheltered them, informal shelters have been put up in places such as in Barwol , Kuajieno , RocRoch due to new arrivals is causing a strain on the host communities that are not accommodating the newly arrived. There is need to provide decent emergency Shelter support. due to lack of markets in the area as well as lack of local building materials. NCA proposes to provide in Kind shelter as well as basic household goods to these populations.In Pibor and much of Jongelei, the flooding in mid to late 2020 has affected large tracts of land and displaced communities destroyed shelters and damaged farms water points and farms. Many families have lost basic household materials as a result but also lost their shelters. Lack of shelter means that families are crowding in the few available places or sleeping in the open. With imminent rainy season on the horizon, it would be key to support families with emergency Shelter material and basic household materials but also provide training to community members on developing resilient livelihoods and shelters, set up Complaints systems and train committees to help with post distribution monitoring. The distribution of household materials as well as hand washing facilities through NCA WASH interventions and soap will also help to prevent the spread of COVID 19. We will also support the GBV sub cluster with the construction of women and girls friendly spaces and also ensure that the facilities have adequate Shelter, basic household materials and WASH support to ensure that vulnerable women, girl and boys have a space to recover.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

At the design stage, we considered issues of access particularly in wet season where costs of transportation of humanitarian materials becomes difficult due to bad roads. We considered the challenges related to costs for transport as well as costs and risks associated with in kind donations and also its effects and impact on local businesses. We considered the existing markets and their ability to supply some of the goods that we transport from big town e.g., Juba and Wau. We considered the importance of promoting local bussineses, the importance of not disrupting the function of markets by introducing external goods which are already available from the markets, We considered the need for sustainable access to WASH services as well as the need to build resilience of communities and markets as well as issues and factors for safety, market supplies and costs. We considered that markets get disrupted by road access constraint but also considered that local businesses are more resilient and more adaptive to shocks. We considered the impact of the economic crisis and how humanitarian responses can also play a role in supporting markets for delivering goods.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In Gumuruk one of the locations where we are planning to implement our WASH programme, has a vibrant local market which has a variety of products including WASH supplies. We have carried out a preliminary cash and markets survey and it has shown a viability of using vouchers for targeted beneficiaries to receive WASH goods through vouchers. We have established a safe mechanism for payment of traders through the existing local Money transfer agents which would enable us to transfer cash safely after verifications. Discussions with Women and Girls has shown that there are no barriers to receiving hygiene materials from shops and that cash vouchers are generally safe. The distances and routes for right holders’ access to markets has undergone and In order to ensure a more resilient way to access sanitation materials as well as access to soap and other sanitary products, we have determined that a cash and voucher system is most ideal in this area. Traders are happy to assure of a steady resupply of goods based on market demand and have a price range that does not fluctuate beyond a certain threshold. In Jur River, we have similarly carried out an assessment and analysis of needs, including function of markets survey as well as a protection survey with right holders and we have determined that it is possible to provide soap, sanitary materials, hand washing facilities from local traders. The existence of money transfer services and accessibility of the area for monitoring shows that we would provide better services and ensure sustainability of interventions by using cash and vouchers. Overall, we have determined that using cash and vouchers will give right holders better value and choices and that we will be able to deliver our responses more quickly, with better value and more appropriately compared to in-kind assistance

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Staff will be made aware about their expected conduct while working at the community level. All NCA and partners staff will be trained in different polices on code of conduct and safeguarding children, CHS and humanitarian principles. Any breaches to the code of conduct will be addressed in the manner prescribed in the HR and employment terms. Community members and their leaders will be informed about their right to complain if any member of the staff or of partner staffs does not uphold their values, breach any policies according to the expectations of the community and according to the employment guidelines and policies. Community members and structures within the communities we serve will be provided with avenues for reporting any breaches and complaints of misconduct. NCA recognizes our responsibility to create a safe and abuse-free environment and in doing so, will treat anonymous complaints seriously because of the potential for future abuse and harm. Therefore, every possible effort will be made to investigate the allegation and satisfactorily address any evidence of abuse, misconduct or malpractice. All NCA employees are requested to report allegations, concerns and suspicion of breaches related to abuse, exploitation and fraud and corruption. Deliberate non-disclosure will lead to disciplinary action. Mechanisms have been established for whistle blowing as well as for Complaints response mechanism which will help us to monitor and act on any breaches.Our monitoring processes are undertaken regularly with monthly and quarterly field monitoring visits while our evaluation programmes are undertaken annually. We use participatory approaches and processes to ensure that the data collected has input from all sectors of the community we serve and also ensure that every individual opinion counts. 7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

NCA has a very deliberate policy of community engagement at every stage of our programme cycle and this includes at various stages. For instance, at the inception stage, we will hold community sensitization sessions with community members through a variety of approaches including focussed group discussions with key informants, leaders, religious leaders, representatives of minorities and people living with disabilities, women, youth and other gender groups. This approach will help us to understand the issues and needs and gaps within the community and among the various gender groups. We will use this information to carry out a participatory and focused assessment of needs and to map vulnerability which will help us to design our programme. The information from this process will be fed back to the community and their views incorporated. We also involve and consult community members. When we need volunteers or local staff to work on projects at the community, we as the community members to identify and provider names for volunteers to work on programmes. At the monitoring and evaluation stage, we try to ensure that in all these stages of the programme that all members of the community are consulted to provide input and feedback on how the project components has affected them positively or negatively. Our accountability to affected populations strategy is based on NCAs Complaints response management strategy. NCA system at the community level involves programme staffs and community members having dialogue at the initial stages to identify how community would like to provide feedback or receive it. Community members discuss a range of options that may include email & telephone, complaints register, suggestion box, face to face or community meetings or a mix of these options. We then set up meeting with community members to discuss ways of setting up the system at the community level once the most preferred system is outlined and discussing on how it will operate. In many of the community settings, electronic systems are not possible, and communities often choose face to face meetings and complaints boxes and complaints registers. The practicalities of the system in relation to local set up is discussed. The key issues discussed include what is a valid complaint and how to handle sensitive complaints, what are sensitive and non-sensitive complaints and how they are handled. The complaints and feedback mechanism is incorporated into the NCA monitoring framework. To receive complaints, the community will appoint a focal person to register the complaint. We use these engagements to also provide feedback and receive back from community and we use this information to address concerns but also to ensure that the community is at the centre of our response actions. Normal community meetings are also undertaken to engage community members during normal course of programme implementation. This helps to improve our engagement with community. Data collected during monitoring and evaluation sessions is also shared with community members, leaders and stakeholders as part of feedback and to also see how their input was reflected. 8.11. Mitigation Measures

In our GBV programming, NCA has established and mainstreamed referral pathways with its partners and sub cluster members and enacted mechanisms to creates awareness to beneficiaries about the available referral services. Through safe, confidential, and accessible referral systems, NCA has been able to refer survivors of GBV and other vulnerable population groups to service providers for life-saving services like clinical management of rape and safe maternal care services. NCA has trained its staff on data safety and security as well as confidentiality. All survivor’s information that is in hard copy is kept in files and locked up in a cabinet. Survivor’s personal information is entered using codes with no disclosure of names. Additionally, all staff including implementing partners’ case workers and community mobilizers are trained on case management and GBV case management operating procedures, including the GBV guidelines for case management adopted from IASC and UNFPA GBV guidelines for case management tool kits.8.5.
