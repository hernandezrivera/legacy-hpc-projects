Project Summary

This project will focus only on Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) activities undertaken in planned or unplanned settlements across Somalia. It will be designed to mitigate the physical and protection consequences of drought, conflict and any other natural or man-made disasters on populations and communities directly affected. This overarching objective will be achieved by enhancing the overall protective environment for displacement affected population, increasing access to basic services linked to different sectors, reducing the risk thereof, and availing specialized support assistance to affected population to help them address other interconnected protection concerns. Through this project, NRC will reach a total of 10,000 HHs (60,000 individuals - 50% females) with necessary services through; setting up of CCCM coordination structures in collaboration with appropriate government counterparts, participation in relevant coordination forums on behalf of CCCM, ensuring the relevant Protection and Shelter respondents are aware and take action with regards to HLP issues, conducting Detailed Site Assessments, establishing/updating master list of sites at appropriate geographic level, providing capacity building to existing governance structures/focal points and construction of communication/ information centers in coordination with local authorities. NRC will also conduct mapping exercises on existing community structures. Settlement planning training will be conducted to improve accessibility in site and
community led site maintenance activities will be supported to ensure upkeep of sites (cash for work, site maintenance committees) through cash for work to improve emergency sites to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites (flood mitigation, fire prevention). NRC will also provide platforms for displacement affected communities in sites for access to vocational training and skill diversification opportunities. Settlement mapping exercises will be conducted to get the information of all targeted settlements for necessary services in place and gap analysis. The settlement mapping exercise data will
be conducted identify facilities gaps in the settlements.
This project is designed to meet the needs of 80% IDPs and 20% host communities of the total beneficiaries targeted. Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data. CCCM activities will also improve the coordination and monitoring of service provision at site level and intra-site to ensure efficiency in service delivery and avoid duplication or gaps in services through capacity building for the existing community mechanisms to ensure access to information and services and improve accountability to affected populations.

Project Activities

- Maintain CCCM coordination structures in collaboration with appropriate government counterpart - Represent CCCM in relevant coordination forums - Ensure through CCCM coordination that relevant respondents are mobilized towards providing sectorial assistance to those identified as very vulnerable - Ensure the relevant Protection and Shelter responders are aware and take action with regards to HLP issues in sites, specifically regarding evictions. - Conduct site verification on 3 month basis - Establish/update service mapping of partners operating in sites - Monitor service delivery at site level to ensure that there are no gaps or duplication of activities and that minimum standards are being met. - Provide capacity building to existing governance structures/focal points - Provide training on site maintenance and site planning to camp committees - Support community led site maintenance activities to ensure upkeep of sites (cash for work, site maintenance committees, distribution of tools) - Implement emergency sites improvement projects to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites (flood mitigation, fire prevention) - Advocate with local authorities for public land for displaced people - Support relocation to public land when appropriate - Mapping of existing community structures - Provide community spaces are available for management committees to meet - Establish/reinforce mechanisms for communication of multi-sector services available per geographical area or in IDPs sites in the appropriate language.- Implement community based activities to promote community cohesion and recovery.

Mitigation Measures

1b. Protection of civilians: In a rural context, communities or groups are either forced into temporarydisplacement because of climatic shocks or conflict, or might join urban centers in search of better access to humanitarian aid (pull factor), access to services and economic opportunities. During slow onset and long-tail crisis such as the recurrent drought and conflict, the same communities may partially displace to neighboring towns. Since all the CCCM interventions will be channeled through cluster approach and involvement of relevant local authorities, thus the impact of armed conflict andviolence will be mitigated through proper coordination, advocacy at national and international level, media and referral mechanisms. 2a. Exclusion and marginalization: TThe Federal Government of Somalia has committed to women’s empowerment and eradicating violence against women, recognizing that without institutionalizing the equal rights of women, the country will never be able to stabilize and develop in a sustainable way. Somali women and girls continue to bear an unequal burden of the hardships occasioned by poverty, conflict and clan-based culture, which promotes strict male hierarchy and authority. This is further exacerbated by cultural limitations on the role and status of women in Somali society. Women with disabilities are often marginalized on account of their disability and their gender. They may be more vulnerable to poverty and social exclusion, and often have limited social, political and economic opportunities and lack of access to basic services. Women with disabilities may also be at greater risk of sexual and physical violence and abuse. Girls with disabilities often experience discrimination, for example in education and family life. This project will ensure engagement of people, especially women with disabilities, and the elderly at the co-management level and will ensure that programs interventions do not generate negative side-effects.. The beneficiary selection will be conducted in a participatory approach with the community leaders and the local authorities that will identify the most vulnerable people. Women and Child headed households will be given special priority. 2b. Exclusion and marginalization: Targeted assistance will be provided to the most vulnerable groups including women, girls and boy. Beneficiaries will be selected through a community-based approach, which will identify the most vulnerable based on certain criteria as female or child-headed households, elderly, disabled, large families and the chronically ill. The beneficiary selection will be conducted in a participatory approach with the community leaders and the local authorities that will identify the most vulnerable people. Women, girls, boys and Child headed households will be given special priority.
