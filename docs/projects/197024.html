Project Summary

Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protection crisis compounded by over ten years of hostilities, protracted displacements, and prolonged consequences, including destruction of civilian infrastructure and violation of international humanitarian and human rights law. Humanitarian needs in NWS remain immense, with an estimated 4.1 million people (out of 4.5 million) in need of assistance at the end of 2022. 2.8 million people in NWS are internally displaced, of with 1.8 million are living in over 1.400 IDP sites (up from 1.7 million in summer) , many of them still in tents and makeshift shelters.
Macro‐economic conditions continue to worsen, evident in the currency devaluation, soaring prices, widespread losses in livelihoods, and increase in poverty. According to MSNA data for WoS, by August 2022, the average household expenses exceeded the average household income by 60 per cent, compared to 49 per cent in 2021. The increase in the income‐expenditure gap has resulted in widespread utilization of increasingly desperate coping mechanisms. The reduced access to electricity and fuel shortages are having a crippling effect on the population, eroding livelihoods opportunities, and severely limiting access to essential services. 47 per cent of NWS households report livelihoods as a need, and 25 percent report needing electricity. The water crisis affects access to sufficient and safe drinking water, irrigation, food production and power generation. Critically bad sanitation conditions and the deterioration of water quality have a direct impact on increasing public health risks, including the new rise of AWD/Cholera, malnutrition, and C‐19.
As the Syrian conflict enters its twelfth year, the population in NWS face mounting challenges due to protracted displacement, poor living conditions, restricted economic opportunities, and limited humanitarian access. These conditions exacerbating the existing vulnerabilities of affected populations who are in high need of necessities including food and livelihoods.
The overall objective of the proposed project is to increase the ability of vulnerable conflict-affected populations in NWS through addressing the most acute humanitarian needs and enhance their capacity for socio-economic resilience. Over 1,400 HHs (50% Female headed HHs, 8,000 individuals) to be supported. This 12-month project focuses to enhance the availability and promote access for food sources through a multidimensional approach for poverty reduction through adopt a hybrid modality (value-based e-vouchers and Commodity transfer ration) to provide regular food assistance over an eight-month period to 1,000 households (6,000 individuals). In addition, provision of structured support to 400 (from the 1,000 targeted) small businesses of the ultra-poor beneficiaries engaged in the “Monthly Food Assistance” focusing on nutrition-sensitive agriculture activities to enhance Agro-food products availability in local markets and promote access of the local population to stable food sources, enabling participants to re-store, increase Agro-based livelihoods and become more food secure. So, enhance self-sufficiency and improve nutrition status at the household level, as well as create income generation by selling the surplus.
Moreover, the planned intervention aims to alleviate the negative impacts of severe economic deterioration by supporting the provision of economic and livelihood opportunities which will be critical to building and strengthening both HH and community resilience through the rehabilitation of public infrastructure affected by years-long conflict, following cash injections through CFW activities. This will be done through rehabilitating/cleaning/maintenance the Agri-irrigation canals and related pumping stations. So, maximize 400 farmers' irrigated planted areas and Agri-production through enhance their access to a viable source of irrigation water.

3 - Needs

RNA carried out in Azaz & Afrin SDs. The key findings linked with Food security and ability to meet immediate needs were: 50% of the population are PIN of food and do not have cash to buy food items, and due to continuous shortage of food in the district, the HHs would be unable to absorb economic shocks, such as loss of a livelihood source. Break out of C19 and Cholera further worsened the situation and there is a need to provide distribution of food vouchers/ food assistance for vulnerable HHs especially for HHs who have lost their source of income due to pandemic. Prior to the conflict, majority of the population relied upon agriculture sector, where the wheat and vegetables, along with livestock breeding were widespread. Similarly, the Syria regime government was the leading supervisor in all agri-activities providing subsidies, thus supporting their production and income generation. Later, this support was interrupted due to the conflict.The basic agri-related infra. such as irrigation canals and pumping stations, has been disrupted and in urgent need to be rehabilitated to enhance the agri-production aspects and promote the availability of food in local markets. The problem affects different groups. Women are critical actors in promoting Hhs food security and diversified LHs. Men have the access to land but lack agri-inputs and technical skills awareness. PwDs are especially vulnerable to food and LH insecurity, mainly IGAs, may be impeded by their disability.

So, enhance self-sufficiency and improve nutrition status at the household level, as well as create income generation by selling the surplus.4-Support the agriculture sector through market infrastructure rehabilitation. 8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Clear standards of conduct, technical and behavioral competencies for all staff, consultants: Bahar will ensure that staff have the technical knowledge and skills, and behavioral competencies to deliver commitments to affected population, and understand what accountability looks like in practice, and to abide by Bahar’s Code of Conduct CoC which includes: Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Prevention of Conflicts of Interest and Fraud, and Child Protection. All staff members are obligedto follow Bahar’s CoC as outlined in their job contracts and measures will be taken should they be found in breach of it. However, malicious complaints will be identified as such and our staff protected from false allegations. Bahar will consulate a PSEA field officer who will make appropriate recommendations to management on enhancing prevention strategies. This could include collecting and analyzing information on actual/potential risk factors for vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse andelaborating measures to address them. Make sure that PSEA materials and posters are visible and in place in all locations. Receive complaints, reports, and questions about alleged acts of SEA committed by the organization’s personnel. And track all PSEA-related activities and ensure that all information on PSEA-related activities is shared with relevant persons in the organization
