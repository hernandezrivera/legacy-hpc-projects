Project Summary

This project is aimed at providing quality lifesaving child protection services to 13,404 children. Narrative Hub will increase its static response to hard to reach areas with protection monitoring and assessment, strengthening the Psycho-social Support Services and Protection awareness-raising and prevention messaging to the affected communities within the selected counties, increased capacity building program for staff and communities to support the children after the exit of NH and to continue improving the well-being of children that have gone through difficulties. Narrative Hub will adopt and multi-sectoral approach promoting a combination of delivery modalities and, mainstream CP and GBV into Education, WASH, Nutrition, Health, and FSL. Referral pathways will also be established to strengthen and support the implementation of case management and link children to other services and address the gaps in coordination within the targeted counties were Narrative Hub operates. Community engagement and accountability to affected population will be at the centre of the project activities. In all activities, Ebola and COVID-19 Protocols and SOPs will be observed to avoid infections and its spread to both staff members and beneficiaries.
This project is linked to the allocation strategy and HNO 2022/23 in view of the timely and tailored response to target populations facing the greatest needs from the compound effects of armed conflict, displacement, hunger, disease and natural disasters such as floods. This project is aligned within the cluster strategy focusing on protection as the top need among the targeted counties. Especially on the provision of structured psychosocial support and life-skills interventions to conflict-affected children; provision of comprehensive case management; strengthen community-based networks including families to prevent and respond to violence against children, supporting vulnerable children through Case Management and supporting community-based mechanisms in Canal/Pigi, Twic East, Nyirol of Jonglei State and Yei in Central Equatoria State. Affected communities liaise with the area civil leaders to select, build and empower the Community-based Child Protection committee/Network or Parents Teachers Association (CB_CPN, PTA) and Caregivers, rehabilitation of established CFSs and support to vulnerable children. These interventions match NH’s organizational objectives and work that has been carried out for the last 3 years in child protection intervention.

1 - Needs

The South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview 2022 and 23, attacks on civilians by armed cattle keepers, and cattle raids continued to displace people. Cattle raids were characterized by multiple forms of violence against civilians, including killing, injury, rape, abduction, looting and destruction of housing and property. Incidents have occurred in remote areas where victims of violence or survivors of sexual assault had little to no access to health services. Children were at the risk of recruitment into local defense groups and other forms of abuses, including abduction and possibly trafficking. In 2021, 5,862 schools were operating across the country, of which 560 were damaged due to flooding and 535 due to conflict. Safety and security incidents in and around education facilities as well as the destruction or occupation of facilities during conflict were reported to limit access for children to schools. These included displacement of teachers and children, killings of teachers and the risk of revenge killings of boys in schools. Other barriers include distance of schools from settlements and limited financial resources within communities to send their children to school. Girls face additional challenges, including cultural perceptions within communities that being educated would lead them to engage in criminal activities or risk becoming pregnant, which would in turn result in them fetching low dowries when they are married. Between January and December 2021, UNMISS Human Rights Division (HRD) documented more than 982 violent incidents affecting at least 3,414 civilians, subjected to one of the four major types of individual harm (killing, injury, abduction and conflict-related sexual violence). IRNA conducted in Yei in February 2022 indicate that the armed conflict between the SPLA and SPLA-IO entered Yei County in 2017 and forced most of the inhabitants to flee to DRC as refugees, while several others went into hideouts within Yei Center and other areas leaving destruction of livelihoods. REACH Humanitarian Situation Monitoring of April - August 2021 shows that humanitarian needs remained high across Jonglei State. The needs are high in Canal-Pigi which was severely affected by flooding that resulted in widespread displacement and food insecurity. In FGDs participants in Nyirol expressed that those that returned to their settlements. Flood-driven displacement, was likely strongly connected with livelihood-driven displacement as livelihood collapse was presumably a direct consequence of flooding. In August, reporting of people leaving their settlement as a consequence of flooding was particularly high in Nyirol (100%), Canal-Pigi (100%), and Twic East (97%) Counties. Across South Sudan, including in Jonglei State, all educational facilities reopened in May 2021, after a 14-month period of closure due to the COVID-19 and now Ebola pandemic. Likely due to flooding, reporting of no available educational facilities within walking distance remained particularly high in assessed settlements in Twic East County (86% of assessed settlements) and and Canal/Pigi (52%) Counties. Conflict emerged as another driver of displacement; it was reported across several counties in Jonglei Sate, including Nyirol (40%) County. In addition, findings from an IRNA conducted in Twic East County, in August, indicate that over 1,500 individuals were displaced by insecurity. An IRNA conducted in Pigi Canal County between 3rd – 5th December 2021 to determine the effect of flooding on the population reveals an estimated 35,462 has been affected by the flooding. This population, particularly children is in urgent need of education and protection services. A number of protection concerns were noted in the settlement areas. Most of the families came without any household items. Many are sharing spaces without any privacy to women and girls. In both Diel and Canal, there were reported cases of unaccompanied children, GBV and sexual exploitation.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

While Narrative Hub sets up a feedback and complains mechanism at the project location among the beneficiaries, at Juba level, a committee composed of Juba staff and led by the M&E will be in-charge of receiving those feedbacks directly from the field locations and conducting the feedback analysis. Each feedback will be collated and grouped accordingly into areas that will ensure they are better addressed. Those that are related to programming will be addressed from the perspective of the beneficiaries advise and lenses and they are at the centre of the project. Whether it is changing the meeting or distribution location or time, that will be changed, and beneficiaries will be informed of the changes through downward feedback from Juba office. The M&E staff will travel to the field locations to communicate directly with beneficiaries and also notify the field staff of the changes that has been made.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Narrative Hub has presence in the locations and hence there is already community engagement and trust with beneficiaries as well as the local authorities, who already had given some of the needs to help design this project. Narrative Hub will spend some time with beneficiaries at the inception of the project creating an awareness of the project and its goal including how to report complains and feedback and how they will be addressed. Narrative Hub will then set up an implementation committee composed of the same beneficiaries. Beneficiaries and other agencies on ground will be involved in monitoring to continuously review and gaps will be identified and discussed jointly and addressed in order to meet the main goal of the project. Key community members including individuals living with disabilities, women and girls will be involved during joint monitoring of the project and from the different constituency groups. A complains and feedback committee will be selected by beneficiaries to engage beneficiaries, listen to their stories and give upward feedback. For those who can write, a complains box will be put around distribution locations and CFS for upward complains and feedback. Where necessary, focused group discussions will be held at project site with beneficiaries and community leaders to provide feedback. Sampling for FGDs will be representative for all affected groups of people. Where distribution is involved, a PDM will be conducted to harvest beneficiary feedback.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Narrative Hub will ensure that training is conducted to all the staff members both at Juba office and field staff on PSEA before the beginning of the project implementation. During signing of contracts, all staff will sign a PSEA code of conduct. The training will then be extended to beneficiaries during project inception. This training will to beneficiaries will be done by Juba based staff members and not by field teams to avoid biases, victimization or any conflict of interest. The beneficiaries will be trained on communication channels available to them to report PSEA and contact persons.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

A complains and feedback committee will be selected by beneficiaries to engage every beneficiary (PWDs, individuals who are not literate, women and girls), listen to their stories and give upward feedback. Where necessary, focused group discussions of each constituency (PWDs, individuals who are not literate, women and girls), will be held at project site with them to provide feedback. Where distribution is involved, a PDM (which will involve individual interviews, FGDs and observations) will be conducted to harvest beneficiary feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

This project is conceptualized based on protection risk analysis and will mean-stream protection at every level. NH is mainstreaming protection in all its programming by ensuring that the most vulnerable groups such as women and girls and others such as PWDs, elderly, receive protection when they are providing information at any stage of the project cycle and that the whole process is confidential. NH is a very active national NGO on child protection in Jonglei State and will employ a protection advisor for this project to work closely with established committees to guide the process with a serious protection lens. Information provided by survivors, victims of sexual exploitation and abuse or beneficiaries will be safeguarded not to be used to expose them to harm and referred to a level that they can seek help. NH with other agencies will develop/strengthen referral pathways in the targeted locations, and will ensure that the meeting sites or locations are safe and closer to beneficiaries. Barriers to access particularly the meeting locations will be discussed with all the beneficiaries in separate groups (constituencies) before it will be agreed on where everyone is comfortable to access. Monitoring tools NH will use have a section of monitoring safety of beneficiaries, and other barriers to access as protection is mean-streamed in the tool and also have a section that asks questions as regards the use of the items support and whether the beneficiaries feels comfortable using them. The project activities will address inequalities and protection threats by analyzing power dynamics in the community. Local leaders will not be in charge of determining who will benefit but beneficiaries with support from our staff. NH staff will sign PSEA before engagement in the project. Our actions will address both basic needs that improve protection of those at risk of violations. NH is also a very active member of Protection Cluster.
