Project Summary

A total of 422,594 (Male: 210,581,Female: 212,013) people are in need of health services in Fangak, canal pigi, Koch, and Rubkona counties out of which 69,377(Male 30,045,Female 39,332) are children under five years of age. A third consecutive year of flooding has negatively impacted the health status of the populations living in these counties as many of the social facilities; health and nutrition facilities, boreholes, latrines have been destroyed. Many families have been displaced to higher dry areas and are living without access to safe drinking water and sanitation facilities. They are being exposed to waterborne and vector-borne diseases. Increased snake bites due to an increase in swampy environment. Lack of essential medicines and medical supplies are the many challenges facing the flood affected populations. Recent incidences of inter-communal fights and cattle raiding in Koch have worsened the humanitarian situation causing displacements and subsequent loss of livelihood and properties, an estimated 12,198 individuals in (2.033HHs) are said to be in critical need of humanitarian assistance according to the December 2021 IRNA report. Bentiu IDP Camp is currently surrounded by floodwaters and a large scale Malaria outbreak among this population is imminent. HEV transmission continue to persist within the IDP Camp with a surge in the number of cases since the beginning of 2021. In responding to the immediate health needs, this project will ensure the scaling of life-saving essential quality health care services to the population in Fangak, Canal Pigi, Koch, and Rubkona counties through the creation of mobile health outreach units in areas of flood-displaced persons in Fangak and Koch counties. Creation of two PHCUs in Canal Pigi County. Upgrading of Petpet PHCU into a PHCC in Koch county. Likewise health services in Phom will be re-stated and its PHCU will be upgraded to a PHCC in Fangak County. Activities will also include post-flooding rehabilitation of water damaged facilities, formation of dykes around health facilities in Koch and Fangak counties and the continued provision of healthcare services through static health facilities. One malaria point will be established in the Bentiu IDP Camp to serve cases during the peak of malaria season to decongest the large numbers of malaria patients going into the health facilities. This will help to enhance social distancing of patients, a pre-requisite to COVID-19 prevention. The project will target a total of 100,000 (Male:50,155, Female:49,845) people as direct beneficiaries, particularly women, children U5(17,000-7000 M, 10,000 F) and people with disabilities( 15,000) . Primary services at WRSS-run health facilities will particularly focus on addressing the top three morbidities of malaria, acute watery diarrhea (AWD), and acute respiratory infection (ARI), as well as prevention and control of epidemic-prone diseases like Cholera, measles, EVD, HEV, and other outbreaks. MCH services, EPI, disease surveillance, and infection prevention and control. The response will strengthen local capacity to prevent, detect and respond timely to outbreaks; It will Finally integrate activities with WASH, Nutrition, FSL and Protection sectors to provide timely and promptly lifesaving interventions to vulnerable population in the prioritized locations. Referral mechanisms will be established or enhanced towards the care of vulnerable people with specific needs like SGBV survivors and SAM children with medical complications. The project will deliver health response to meet health cluster objectives of improving equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations; Activities will be monitored through well-coordinated supervision strategies together with the CHD, MOH and UN agencies.

1 - Needs

The 2022 HNO indicated 8.9M people need humanitarian assistance compared to 8.3M in 2021. The total number of people in need of health service in Fangak county is estimated to be 116,062 (Male:61,536 and Female: 54,526) out of which 24,362 (Male:11,479 and Female: 12,883) are under 5 children and 17,409 are people with disabilities. An IRNA conducted in early August 2021 in Manajang and Phom Payams of Northern Fangak were WRSS operations are located indicated that an estimated population of 52,827 individuals (8,805 House Holds) were reported to be directly affected by flooding. Displaced families are congested in the small available drylands and living with no access to safe drinking water, adequate sanitation facilities. Waterborne and vector-borne diseases have been increasing. Access to basic life-saving health services became extremely limited for the community members in these affected areas as a result cases of community deaths were reported in some of the villages during the assessment. the affected population is in critical need of health support. The major driver of vulnerability in Koch is the Unprecedented flooding that affected most areas in the Peripheral parts of the county, recent increase in incidences of inter-communal fights and cattle raiding has worsened the situation causing subsequent loss of livelihoods and properties. Koch county is categorized as IPC 4 with a total population in need of health services estimated to be 56,055(Male: 25,786 and Female 30,557) out of which 8,890 (Male: 4877 and Female:4013) are under 5 children and 8,408 people with disabilities . An IRNA done in December 2021 indicated an estimated 12,198 individuals in (2.033HHs) were in critical need of humanitarian assistance as a result of the flooding that affected areas of Ngony, Kuachlual, Gany, Jaak and Baow Payams. According to the report there were increased cases of malaria and diarrheal diseases, Children 0-11 months not receiving routine EPI services, increase cases of snake bites, pregnant mothers have to walk a longer distance through the water to access MCH services in Koch PHCC. An IRNA conducted in early November 2021 in Rubkona county indicated 8,757 individuals (1,751 HHs) have been affected by the floods and were displaced to higher areas in Bieh, Thoan, Dingding, and Makhazin. High numbers of malaria cases, increase cases of snakes’ bites, lack of essential medicines and medical supplies, limited access to health services, lack of referral mechanism were also reported. Bentiu IDP camp has been facing the problem of malaria outbreak for the past 4 years, with the floodwaters surrounding the IDP camp, a large-scale malaria outbreak is imminent in 2022.Hepatitis E cases significantly rose to over 1800 cases with 11 deaths and still remain above the epidemic threshold in 2021. Other areas that needs strengthening include, lack of well-integrated strategies, weak supply chain mechanisms, community approaches. Laboratory settings are also challenged by a lack of supplies and a limited technical capacity for the staff to diagnose and handle laboratory wastes. WASH in health facilities is a neglected area where a strong and well-structured mechanism to handle all biohazards and clean water in the facilities is lacking. According to the August 2021 IRNA done in Pigi Canal County 35,462 individuals have been affected by flooding in Kaldak, Canal, Diel, Khorfulus, Korwai Mat/Alam villages. Limited access to health services, cases of snake bites, increased malaria and bloody diarrhea cases were the major health related challenges reported in most of the villages however some areas reported community deaths of mostly children and the elderly due to malaria and bloody diarrhea infection which wasn’t treated due to lack of functioning health unit.

2 - Project activities

Objective1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations;This project will consider the creation of mobile outreach units in Fangak and Koch counties. One malaria point in Bentiu IDP Camp during peak malaria season.It will also include Upgrading PetPet PHCU in to PHCC in Koch county. Like wise, health services in Phom will be re-instated and its PHCU will be upgraded to a PHCC in Fangak County.There will be two PHCUs created in Canal Pigi county. Activities will also include,Post flooding, rehabilitation of water damaged health facilities in Koch and Fangak Counties. Primary healthcare services will be through the six PHCUs and four PHCCs including outpatient consultations, reproductive health services (ANC, Normal deliveries, PNC), EPI, and health education. Health facility staff will be trained on, IMCI, BEmONC, CMAM, CMR, PAC, RUM, clean delivery and use of misoprostol. Community Health Volunteers will be mobilized to provide health education in communities and refer people who are ill to the health facilities for treatment. Health promotion messages will be generated from the weekly disease epidemiological trends of health facilities integrated with COVID-19 massages. Integrated community case management of Malaria, AWD, and Pneumonia will be implemented in Fangak County and Koch County. Other activities include; Referral of complicated cases to the nearest available secondary level of care.Provision of routine EPI services through static HFs and mobile outreach sites.Procurement of supplementary drugs, obtain EHK, RH kits from core pipeline and maintain the uninterrupted pharmaceutical supply chain. Identification and referral of patients with mental health problems for treatment and psychosocial support.Clinical management of rape cases and referral for psychosocial support. Identification and referral of patients with disabilities to the nearest available service point.Community mobilization and Sensitization on SGBV.Training staff on infection prevention, control, and waste management.provision of Hand-washing stations in the facility, consultation rooms, Maternity rooms and waiting areas.Conduct an awareness campaign for community leaders and other stakeholders on family planning. Objective 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality through early detection and response to epidemic-prone diseases.This will be achieved through training of mobile outreach teams, health and nutrition staff on epidemic-prone diseases and outbreak surveillance.Case definitions for earmarked communicable diseases will be provided to all health care workers for early identification and treatment. All data will be compiled and submitted into EWARS/DHIS 2 for analysis and decision making through the Health Cluster. Aberrations and risks of epidemics will be identified and controlled at the very lowest level through health promotion efforts.WRSS will participate in any outbreak response in coordination with stakeholders, other partners, and Health Cluster.The establishment of a waste management area at the facilities and implementation of standard precaution procedures for infection prevention and control.Objective 3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms.All project activities will be implemented in collaboration and coordination with the existing Health Pool Fund or World Bank supported health activities in these locations.Coordination with CHD and other partners on the ground to avoid duplication and increase coverage within these locations.Quarterly Joint-monitoring and supportive supervision with the CHD to the health facilities will be conducted as well monthly community consultative meetings to identify needs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The feedback received from beneficiaries will be use in designing or re-designing project interventions. WRSS has been operational in these project locations for a long time. WRSS has had a chance to engage the beneficiaries at various stages and at different levels and at different times and all the feedback have been used in designing new projects.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The various partners and stakeholders, as well as the beneficiaries themselves, will be asked to par-ticipate throughout all stages of the process from needs assessment and inputs on project design, beneficiary selection to building the work plan, to scheduling and designing trainings, to monitoring & evaluation. Affected populations will be involved in the project in a few main ways.The project will target to include 52% of the beneficiaries as women and girls. this is so because women are have a high health seeking behaviors than their men and boys. People leaving with disability will be included during community feedback meetings to give their feedbacks.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

WRSS has in place a PSEA policy. All staffs are trained on PSEA and are required to sign on it as a prove of having gone through and understood the policy. During project implementation, all staffs are not only expected to adhere to PSEA policy but also to sensitize beneficiaries on this policy. The project will employ one protection officer and two assistants who are part of the beneficiary communities. their role will be mainly to ensure inequalities are minimized, inclusion in project activities is enhanced and all population groups are protected from exploitation and abuse. they will also be responsible for training new project staffs on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, they will also establish and ensure the functionality of a reporting mechanism of all cases of sexual exploitation and abuse and raising it to the attention of WR senior management for immediate action.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Project objectives will be discussed and agreed upon by community leaders at county, payam and Boma levels. targeted beneficiaries and geographical location will be discussed at the project inception. Monthly community feedback meetings will be done with beneficiaries and the information obtained will be used to align project with the context and the needs of the beneficiaries further more, complaints registration mechanism forms will be availed in all health facilities for beneficiaries to register their complaint and a feedback will be provided promptly and documented to be in future project planning.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Women and young children particularly are at risk as a result of implementation of project activities. Women could fall victims of rape and sexual abuse when walking to health facilities. The project team will therefore discuss with the Boma Health Teams (BHT) members to ensure and encourage men to escort their women or girls whenever they coming to health facilities. Especially when ever they are to walk through forest to the health facilities. Vulnerable individuals, including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities are likely to be discriminated against in accessing health services. Distance is a possible barrier to inclusion. The project team will ensure that these individuals are particularly targeted during registration. The project will employ one protection officer and two assistants. their role will be mainly to ensure inequalities are minimized, inclusion in project activities is enhanced and all population groups are protected from exploitation and abuse. This will be achieved by ensuring equitable gender inclusion, targeted inclusion of people with disabilities and other vulnerable groups including children and elderly individuals.People leaving with disability and the elderly who will not be able to access the health facilities shall be reached by the mobile outreach team members in their shelters. The risk of explosive hazard contamination will be mitigated through community awareness and working closely with the protection partner to ensure all identified explosive remnants of war are reported timely to the demining partner so that they are removed. Safety of beneficiaries is of paramount importance to project implementation. World Relief has a security focal point. Working together with other protection players, a security risk analysis will be conducted to ensure safety of staff and beneficiaries. This will be particularity so for distributions which act as pool factors and have a potential of exposing beneficiaries to attacks and injury.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

This project will provide clinical management to all rape cases that will be reported to the health facilities and appropriately refer them for psychosocial support. WRSS will also work with protection partners to provide minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV. These services will include; counseling with emphatic listening, inquiring about needs and concern, validating and ensuring safety of the victim. through existing referral pathways, children, women at risk and GBV survivors will be able to access information on prevention and management of GBV.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

WRSS will work with communities to institute disaster risk mitigation measures across the target locations. the project will build the capacity of the community to institute early warning signs that will trigger response before the flooding occurs in this way communities are able to prepare early enough to socks caused by flooding. This project also plans to build dykes around health facilities to ensure that they are not submerge in case of future flooding this will enable facilities to remain in place and continue to providing basic life-saving services.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Poor health status of individuals prevents them from getting involved in farming activities reducing their house hold food levels, this project will contribute towards reduction in morbidity thus improving the health status of the populations within these locations leading to improve farming activities as thus improving house hold food levels this will in turn lead to reduction in the number people in IPC 4 and IPC 5 severity levels.Health promotion activities will prevent people especially the children from becoming ill hence reducing the risk of malnutrition among the children under 5 years of age.
