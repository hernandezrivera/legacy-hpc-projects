Project Summary

Over 2022, this project aims to enhance the living conditions of vulnerable groups thorough providing a comprehensive lifesaving SNFI and Reinforcement and enabling of protection environment and community cohesion for 29,000 HHs (145,000 individuals) of vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities through the following:
- Distribution of 8,000 new arrival kits/NFIs for 8,000 IDPs households (40,000 individuals disaggregated by gender: 7,920 men 10,080 women, 12,320 girls, and 9,680 boys) in Afrin, Jandaris, Raju, Albab, A'zaz, Jarablus, Ghandoura, Suran, Aghtarin, Armanaz - Idlib - Sarmin - Bennesh - Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts.
- Distribution of 7600 winterization kits/vouchers to 7600 HHs (60% IDPs, 20% returnees and 20% host)/ 38,000 individuals disaggregated by gender (7,524 men, 9,576 women, 11,704 girls and 9,196 boys) in Afrin, Jandaris, Raju, Albab, A'zaz, Jarablus, Ghandoura, Suran, Aghtarin, Armanaz - Idlib - Sarmin - Bennesh - Maaret Tamsirin sub-districts.
- Rehabilitation of emergency shelter spaces in collective centers, unfinished buildings, and damaged houses reaching 750 households, where 550 unfinished houses will be upgraded (medium and light) disaggregated by gender (743 men, 945 women, 1155 girls and 908 boys) in Dana, Maret Tamsrin, A'zaz, Albab, Aghtarin, Bennsh,Idleb and Harim sub district and 200 HHs living in 5 collective centers in Albab, Jarablus, Suran, Aghtarin and Maret Tamsrin, Dana and Jisr Ash-shogor sub-districts in addition to conudcting cash for shelter activity for 70 HHs disaggregated by gender (69 men, 88 women, 108 girls and 85 boys) in Idleb, A’zaz and Albab sub-districts. .
- Construction\Installation of 350 temporary dignified shelters for IDPs disaggregated by gender (347 men, 441 women, 539 girls and 424 boys) in A’zaz, Aghtarin, Suran and A'rima sub-districts.
- Implementation of site levelling, gravelling, installation rain drains, and tents ground isolation for 40 sites reaching out for 18,000 IDP HHs (45,000 individuals) in Afrin, Jandaris, Raju, Albab, A'zaz, Jarablus, Ghandoura, Suran, Aghtarin, Armanaz - Idlib - Sarmin - Bennesh - Maaret Tamsirin sub-districts.
-Installation of solar light panels for site and IDPs units in Zoghara and A’rima sub-districts reaching to 850 IDP HHs (4250 Individuals) disaggregated by gender (842 men, 1,071 women, 1309 girls and 1029 boys)
-Build the capacity of the IDPs and host HHs through provision of shelter mentoring sessions for HHs with skilled workers (piloting the approach) for 50 HHs disaggregated by gender (47 men, 51 women, 82 girls and 70 boys) in Idleb, A’zaz and Albab sub-districts.
The project will be aligned with the cluster guidelines. Shafak intends to increase its input to the cluster and ensure new experiences like solar panel installation and cash for shelters are shared. The project has been partially secured in funding for 2022 while the remaining amount will be advocated for to be funded by other donors for 2023.
As for 2023, according to Shafak's strategy and fund availability, it is expected to implement similar activities reaching approx. 130,000 individuals. During Activities implementation, Shafak will maintain good coordination with the SNFI cluster through monthly submission of 4ws, contribution to the winter planning and floods response actions.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, number of PiN for shelter has reached 5,916,585, highest percent of them are in Aleppo and Idleb while number of PiN for NFI has reached into 4,909,468, with Idleb and Aleppo scoring highest 17% and 14% respectively. 88% of IDP sites in Aleppo and 82% of IDP sites in Idleb are self-settled camps (HNO 2022). Inadequate shelter percent is 61% in Idleb while the number decreased to 32% in Aleppo Governorate. Needs of winterization and shelter ranked as fourth need (scored 61%) and shelter as fifth need (scored 49%). During March 2021, CCCM reported' many people in northwest Syria also rely on burning unsafe materials for heat, which can cause fires or be unsafe for breathing.' Last report of CCCM in December 2021 mentioned during that month only around 300 IDPs sites were flooded. According to the assessment conducted by Shafak in October 2021 covering 24 sub-districts, winterization responses have been priorities for the HHs in NWS. Needs of the sub-district were ranked as needs for food, cash and heating materials respectively in the assessment. Number of IDPs estimated to be received nearly in case of attacks on Manbej or M4 has been 36,200 HHs and major sub-districts are Taftanaz, Sarmine, Suran, Ghandoura, Jarablus and Ar’raee. In last quarter 2021, the SNFI winterization sheet mentioned huge gaps in need for covering in Dana, Salqin, A’zaz and Ghandourah sub-districts and number of PiN for winterization support has reached 4,502,722 (HNO 2022).

5 - Implementation of activities

Shafak will implement the proposed activities according to its SOPs and guidelines based on the SNFI guidelines and best practices. Shafak will do -Needs assessment to determine the targeted sites and communities for the planned activities,–Community mobilization sessions for the IDPs and host communities explaining the criteria for selection of camps and communities and explain the modality of intervention. Site analysis and MO, technical assessment and preparing BoQs and site analysis. – Market assessment for winterization, NFI, rehabilitation and infrastructure items- Contracting with service providers to implement the maintenance activities -Conduct upgrading activities in camps locations (45 K individuals) through maintenance and rehabilitation of rain-drain systems, gravelling of main and sub-roads in the sites to improve IDPs access to health and WASH services and provide the at the targeted camps with ground insulation, tent replacement and installation where needed, construction of dignified transitional shelter units and installation of solar panels for roads lighting and housing units lighting (4250 ind). In off-camps locations, the rehabilitation activities will include rehabilitation of 5 collective centers (900 ind), Upgrading of unfinished houses & rehabilitation of damaged houses (3100 individuals). Shared activities will take place between Off-camps and camps like distribution of NFI (40 K ind) core relief items kits, distribution of winterization kits(38 K).

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk (1): Sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), including harassment, against women and girls by cash for workers, male beneficiaries while registering for the services or attending the training. RM Strategy: - recruit male and female volunteers who are respected in the community and independent of armed groups. No children to be employed (under 18 years old) - make sure staff are already trained on Do No Harm, protection from SEA (PSEA), and child safeguarding; and sign and adhere to code of conduct that respects and uphold the rights of beneficiaries and PSEA - set up communication and complaints mechanisms or desks that are clearly marked; face interviews with beneficiaries, including women and children Risk (2): Marginalization of most vulnerable groups like disable and women during project design and implementation. RM Strategy: Set a fixed quota for these groups to be involved in the camps committees, ensure that field teams have female staff- ensure that mobilization sessions for the project include all categories. Risk (3):Difficult access for women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities(PWD) RM Strategy: - Register all adult women, unaccompanied Register all adult women, unaccompanied children, elderly, people with disabilities as main recipients of services- During post-distribution follow-up. materials Risk (4): Resistance to gender mainstreaming from male leadership structures. RM: : Involve the key leaders in the community to endorse women role


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk of PSEA and CSG. Likelihood: High Mitigation: a) Clear complaint mechanism in place b) Train staff on code of conduct and PSEA and CSG policies c) field staff should be accompanied with female staff d) Incorporate strong consideration for protecting the rights of the beneficiaries and maintaining their dignity, e) Safe recruitment of staff f)Safe response standards.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1 Eviction from shelters units, camps, transit sites, spontaneous settlement due to lack of approval from property owner/conflict in property ownership. Likelihood: medium. Mitigation: Ensure that no launch of site improvement activities takes place without obtaining the approval from the land owner or the stakeholders, Include HLP documentation into the MoUs or agreements signed, Work closely with population at risk of eviction together with other sectors and local authorities to find solutions and clearly communicate the options available. Risk2: Increase of infrastructure materials prices, Likelihood: high Mitigation: Sign a fixed framework agreement with the contractors, conduct market assessment of regular basis for monitoring the prices. Risk 3: Increased risk of inter or intra-community tension due to varied assistance packages, unclear beneficiary selection criteria leading to a feeling of discrimination. Likelihood: High. Mitigation: Standardize assistance package with guidelines followed by all sector partners. Conduct mobilization sessions about the project scope with clear unformed criteria of selection, coordinate with community key representatives and leader. Risk4: Misuse of distributed cash for rent by the IDP HHs due to other priority needs like food. Likelihood: High Mitigation: Ensure that selection of IDPs is based on verification of their needs and vulnerability, coordinate with other humanitarian actors to provide needed support for the targeted IDPs
