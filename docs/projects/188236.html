Project Summary

Protection is an integral component of CARE’s multiple sectoral interventions linking beneficiaries to essential, needs based protection services and providing stand-alone services as needed and permitted by the context. CARE will integrate a menu of critical protection services, including structured and unstructured PSS, monitored regularly to measure change in people’s ability to cope with stress. The focus will be on ensuring vulnerable men, women and children are reached with protection related information, are linked to available services, have space to voice protection concerns, share information, and build trust. Consideration is being given to providing specialized services particularly GBV prevention and mitigation where gaps exist and community sensitivities allow. GBV components will account for up to 40% of the budget, with the remainder for Gender Protection interventions. Key services will include:
1. General Awareness raising, information sharing sessions, conducting roleplays and facilitating community-based discussions covering sensitive protection issues including GBV as a prevention approach for all the community members (no GBV Survivors to be included) and other related topics based on the consultation with the community also sharing info about the available service after updating the service mapping and after CARE MSNA is completed. For non GBV sessions, the awareness raising session are intended to enhance beneficiaries access to critical protection information including information about the available services and how to access them. This information is updated regularly through a service mapping exercise and also continuous consultation with the community members. COVID-19 is also part of the non-GBV awareness raising.
2. Community volunteers, community members and other service providers will be trained on diverse protection related themes that will enable them to detect, identify and mitigate protection related risks including GBV.
3. Gender sensitive mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services offering non-specialized sessions to enhance beneficiaries’ psychosocial wellbeing using a “light approach”. Focused psychosocial (PSS) sessions will target vulnerable women and children.
4. Provision of “safe spaces” activities including recreational activities and community events to enhance the beneficiaries psychological coping mechanism.
5. Establishing multipurpose community centers providing a safe space for conducting awareness sessions, psychosocial support and training with a particular focus on GBV.
6. Provision of holistic case management (as described under Outcome #1).
7. Provision of psycho-social support (PSS), possibly in tandem with VT.
8. Information and/or referrals for marginalized individuals on/to needed services (where they exist) to increase safety within the home and community (i.e. GBV, physical rehabilitation, PSS, prosthetic services, etc.).
9. Training, awareness raising, and /or the dissemination of information on accessing legal identity and civil documentation.
10. Distributing female dignity kits to vulnerable women and girls.
Our activities will focus on men and women, children and adolescents’ girls and boys. We will also work inside the camps, in host communities and those who have returned.

4 - Needs

The follow needs where identified primarily during an MSNA conducted by CARE in Deir e Zor in addition to those highlighted through the Protection WG:Case management: Increased need for GBV case management especially in response to COVID19 and the ongoing economic deterioration across Syria, including targeted communities. Psychosocial support services: Increasing need for Mental Health and Psychosocial support services due high levels of stress, trauma and exposure to violence. Increase awareness around critical protection issues and services: related to GBV, child labour and child marriage to reduce and refer cases. Referrals and in-kind assistance: Increased need for referral support for medical care, legal documentation etc. Dignity kits, New born kits for women and recreational kits for children: Deteriorating economic conditions had reduced people’s spending power, especially for women to buy essential personal, childcare materials and educational and recreational materials for children.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries or other community members by staff and partnersPrevention/ Mitigation: CARE Syria developed a standalone PSEA strategy that aims to improve the effectiveness of its programming. The PSEA strategy is designed create a more protective and empowering environment for all program participants, with a specific focus on women and girls. The strategy focuses on enhancing safety and dignity of programme participants, whilst also promoting and protecting human rights without contributing to or perpetuating discrimination, abuse, violence, neglect and exploitation. In line with the strategy, CARE will ensure the following measures are in place:· PSEA mandatory training to be conducting for all staff covering core definitions related to sexual exploitation and abuse and how to report through CARE reporting channels. · PSEA messaging will be part of the Protection awareness raising and MEAL leaflets.· All partners will be aware and accountable for PSEA by signing the CARE PSEA policy and CoC. · PSEA Refresher is provided on a bi-annual basis for all CARE and partner staff in the local language.· PSEA focal point at the hub level is assigned to follow up on any PSEA reports and lead on investigations. · PSEA resources and lesson learned to be shared with the INGO’s through the NES PSEA network.CARE will follow up with the implementing partner to assess their internal capacities and support them enhancing their PSEA mechanisms.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Political developments and/or security incidents leading to temporary or permanent loss of access. Mitigation: - CARE has developed contingency plans inclusive of evacuation plan for the community centers & WGSS to ensure staff and beneficiaries safety. CPs also include detailed procedures on asset and information/records disposal as required.-CPs include necessary procedures if access to project sites is compromised, including adaptation measures in place to allow the Protection team to shift from a static programming to mobile. - Regular monitoring of security and geo-political dynamics and indicators. Coordination between the safety and security unit with the program teams. Risk: Financial mismanagement among Partners. - CARE uses a well-developed Organizational Capacity Assessment tool to assess all potential partners during the Due Diligence. - Monitoring visits from MEAL and Protection Teams - Established financial reporting mechanisms. Partner required to submit supporting documentation to the project activities. Verified before releasing the partner financial payment. - Clear messaging on CARE has a zero tolerance fraud policy and fraud investigation processes. CARE is obliged to launch an investigation if there is a suspected cause of fraud. Risk: COVID19-CARE has been able to provide Protection services through several adaptations, including shifting to online platforms. - All staff are trained on COVID19 SoP to reduce the risk of further exposure.
