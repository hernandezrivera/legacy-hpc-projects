Project Summary

26 field assessments were conducted by the FSL and MEAL teams through KIs or FGDs, in Deir ez Zor, Al-Hassakeh, Ar-Raqqa, Idleb, Aleppo, Rural Damascus, Dar’a and Hama for 16 districts at Admin Level 2; field visits were made to 26 sub-districts at Admin Level 3 with an area specific needs assessment and analysis report published. The information collected concerns context, livelihood activities, food security and production status. The responses were analyzed and programmatic recommendations formulated. AAH developed a mission strategy identifying priority locations based on confirmed needs and vulnerabilities, historic areas of intervention, and AAH access capacity and resources. An area based integrated approach will be promoted. Beneficiaries are selected by Beneficiary Selection Committees according to AAH selection criteria. A validation survey will be conducted. The Action focuses on women headed households. Adhering to SO 3.1, the project will distribute 10,000 food parcels to address the immediate needs of vulnerable households. The composition of the food parcels follows the recommendation from the Food Security Cluster (1500 kcal/day <70% of 2100 kcal/day>) for short term food assistance for a family of 5-6/30 days. The food ration will be distributed in kind adhering to the sector sample food basket components. Adhering to SO 3.2, the Action will distribute agricultural inputs to home-gardeners, crop farmers and herders, through direct provision or a conditional voucher. The voucher will be for a one-off payment with a value up to the value of the in-kind inputs distributed to beneficiary categories according to CWG recommendations. Coordination with agencies implementing similar interventions in the area, and with the CWG, is essential. 1000 basic vegetable production kits for the winter and spring production seasons will be distributed. 2,500 crop farmers will implement a crop rotation system. The lands will be divided into two 5 donum sections. Seeding rate will be as per the sector recommendations (seed wheat max 250kg/ha

4 - Needs

There are 13.4M people in Syria in need of humanitarian assistance. 12.4M people are estimated as food insecure (1.3M considered severely food insecure). The WFP’s national standard reference food basket price in February 2021 was SYP 136,398. The protracted crisis strained the ability of the population to cope; negative coping strategies are on the rise. Households responded by reducing the number of meals per day and not consuming expensive food categories (e.g., meat). As hostilities decreased, the demands from the population relating to livelihood opportunities and basic services have increased as they seek to rebuild their lives. AAH conducted 26 needs assessments in 8 governorates at sub-district level during 2020-21. The assessment involves collection of information from Key Informants (sections on context, livelihood activities, food security and production status); the assessment undertaken through a gender perspective. Households have indicated there is limited access to agricultural inputs (seeds, fertilizers, tools, irrigation equipment) and market support to establish or maintain sustainable agricultural livelihoods. Access to fuel for tractors or irrigation pumps is either not available or unaffordable. The Ministry of Agriculture and Agrarian Reform (MAAR) has established a support scheme to provide subsidized fuel and fertilizer, but the quantities distributed are insufficient to meet the needs of farmers. Livestock farmers with limited financial resources are unable to provide optimal rations to their animals. Extension services are limited.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented by the AAH FSL team composed of international and national staff based in the Damascus, with regional offices in Aleppo and Hassakeh. AAH has partnered with ACSAD, which is responsible for ensuring access to project locations, implementation of activities, and developing and presenting agricultural extension material to farmers in cooperation with MAAR. AAH is responsible for project design, development of technical specifications, monitoring and reporting to the donor. A tripartite agreement has been signed between AAH, ACSAD and MAAR. AAH has a dedicated MEAL department with staff in the hub and regional offices. A comprehensive Monitoring & Evaluation plan will be designed to support collection and tracking of sex and age disaggregated data in line with the proposed Monitoring Framework. Technical and MEAL staff conduct regular monitoring field visits at all stages of the project cycle to confirm beneficiary selection criteria have been maintained, aid distribution has proceeded correctly (a Post-Distribution Monitoring report prepared) and at project completion, outputs are measured against defined indicators to measure the impact of the intervention through baseline/endline surveys. All monitoring information is used to capture good practices and inform future project implementation monitoring procedures. The MEAL team collect quantitative testimonies from the perspective of beneficiaries; this material can be used evidence-based advocacy messaging. A feed-back mechanism is already in place where recipients of aid or other stakeholders of the Action can voice their complaints or other comments. A telephone number and contact information is included.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Prioritize safety and dignity, and principle of “Do No Harm”: AAH implements a transparent beneficiary selection procedure to target households with high levels of vulnerability. AAH negotiates with ACSAD and MAAR to locate the point of aid delivery close to beneficiaries’ home. The time and location of aid distribution is widely publicized to allow beneficiaries time to beneficiaries arrange for transportation of the aid. Distribution points are managed to ensure effective crowd control with gender considerations. No location is selected at risk of UXO Distributed aid is sourced from reputable suppliers with government approved quality controls. Meaningful access: AAH ensures special arrangements for people with protection concerns (e.g., PWDs, the elderly) for a representative of the beneficiary to collect the aid on their behalf. AAH ensures protection of the rights of the marginalized or those facing discrimination by identification of these groups during assessment and beneficiary selection. Accountability, Participation and Empowerment: AAH ensures key informants represent all groups in the community during the assessment phase to understand their needs and methodology of assistance; identifies measures to help people avoid resorting to negative coping strategies; establishes a complaints feedback mechanism with a widely available hotline accessible for all stakeholders. PSAE: Ensure procedures are put in place to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH follows internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power and whistle-blower mechanisms. The Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. Employees sign a Code of Conduct. AAH has a PSEA policy with an implementation plan.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

AAH recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect, and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. Implementation of humanitarian aid activities can sometimes have the unforeseen impact of creating unequal power balances within the AAH team and for recipients of aid; the organization is committed to mitigating against such risks. Sexual exploitation and abuse can do great harm to people affecting integrity and trust; AAH prohibits such exploitation by affirming a zero-tolerance approach. Disregard of the PSEA polices is regarded as gross misconduct, which could result in summary dismissal. AAH reserves the right to refer such cases to the relevant legal authorities in the abuser’s country of origin and in the host country. All AAH staff will always treat people with dignity and respect; undertake awareness raising of the PSEA policy and report any instances of a breach of this policy to the prescribed channels. All employees and representatives of AAH will not engage in sexually exploitative behavior or linking sexual favor with the receipt of aid; engage in any sexual activity with a child under 18 years of age; or being involved with any forms of prostitution or payment for sex. AAH will investigate allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in a timely, professional, and confidential manner.

Mitigation: situation closely monitored with communication to donors, monthly input price monitoring conducted, possible increase in number of beneficiaries. Risk of aid diversion. Risk of data collection hampered. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: transparency, trust-building, developing relationships with municipalities, district authorities and facilities managers. Ethical risk. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: trainings to staff members on organizational ethical policies. Follow the internal policies relating to fraud, anti-corruption and abuse of power, and whistle-blower mechanisms. The Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. Employees and contractors sign a Code of Conduct. AAH has a PSEA Policy. Risk of voucher fraud: Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: apply security control measures to printing vouchers, monitor use of vouchers at vendor locations, confirm vouchers only used by selected beneficiaries.
