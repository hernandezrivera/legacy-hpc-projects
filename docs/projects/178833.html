Project Summary

The purpose of the Caravan for a Better Childhood project is: To help maintain the children's right to survival and development in the Syrian conflict, by protecting children from harm, abuse, neglect, exploitation and violence - through strengthened community involvement. Violet will work towards achieving the overall goal through two project goals: 1) to increase access and attention for 4,500 internally displaced children between 0 and 18 years with their parents living in informal internal refugee camps for existing relief, protection services. 2) To support age-appropriate development of 10,780 internally displaced children between the ages of 0 and 18 living in informal internal refugee camps by providing community-based psychological first aid and psychosocial support through the active participation of children and parents/carers. Violet is establishing three mobile community-based child protection units to reach the vulnerable children in the three areas of Idleb, and Aleppo in northwestern Syria. These so-called "magic" caravans will be equipped with toys and materials for child-friendly activities. Through these activities, the children will be given psychological first aid and psychosocial support. The planned activities are aimed at two main categories of children: (1) those who have lived in the camp for two days or longer, (2) those who have just arrived (before 72 hours) in the informal camps, (3) children who affected by floods, and (4) children who at-risk or have any child protection concerns. As the main problem in the selected areas is not necessarily a lack of services, but more detailed knowledge of and access to them, there is a strong focus on local referral mechanisms. Violet expects to be able to connect 250 children reached with other emergency services through these referral mechanisms. Conduct structured PSS activities; Child Resilience and Parenting Skills for 200 children and 50 parents in Violet existing health facilities, RCs, camps, schools, and CFS in the targeted locations. Violet believe that positive defence mechanisms can be activated by providing more access to life-saving basic services for children in need and by encouraging positive self-development through child-friendly activities; In the long run, the built capacity of those children and their parents will result in a collective resilience that supports not only the individual but also society as a whole in the humanitarian crisis in Syria.
Violet has previous experience and solid intervention through the targeted locations, using community acceptance of its health and emergency services. Violet is still providing many ongoing projects in both Idleb and Aleppo governorates, by multi-sectoral approach, through existing hospitals and PHCs, in addition to more than 70 schools and CFS, RCs and camps. Violet will build on its experience in evacuation and relocation response within the emergencies after clashes or floods, especially on its Ambulance and Transit units in Idleb and Aleppo.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO 2021, CP People in need is 5.913M; 51.6% Boys, 48.4% Girls, 24.6% CwDs. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, including in areas where hostilities have declined, with children at risk of being killed or injured, recruited and used in hostilities, tortured, detained, abducted and sexually abused. In 2020, the UN verified 2,388 of such violations against children, illustrating persistent trends of violence.Deepening poverty continues to fuel harmful coping mechanisms and strain the capacities of families and communities to protect children, particularly adolescent girls and boys. Child labour, including its worst forms, is reported and in 22% of assessed communities as frequently occurring. Separation from caregivers is a persistent issue across Syria. Death of a caregiver (15% for boys and girls), economic need (25% for boys, 13% for girls), child marriage (11% for boys, 44% for girls) and child recruitment (10% for boys) are among the top-cited reasons for family separations. The absence of a continuum of alternative care options, including formal options, is a concern.Recruitment and use of children by parties to the conflict have become a normalized practise in some parts of Syria. In 2020, the UN verified 813 incidents of recruitment and use of children (96% boys), with 99% used in combat roles.Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 23% of surveyed households demonstrating the cumulative toll on mental well-being with immediate and if not addressed lifelong consequences.COVID-19 has increased risk factors that drive the intensity and frequency of the aforementioned CP risks and vulnerabilities, whilst also undermining partners’ detection mechanisms.

Direct communication with focal point and rapid evacuation as per Violet’s training with highest priority on safety of team and target group. Access via remote methods. Shift of activities to accessible areas with a large IDP populationAssumption: Increased in the number of displacement Risk: The current staff and activities will not be able to overstretched capacityMitigation: Readjustment of activities for a larger audience of children. Allocation of additional volunteers to the activities. Using additional funding resources from the main donor and other projects too.Assumption: The displacement movement is continuous to other locationsRisk: Inability to follow children’s cases Mitigation: Updated the displacement movements on daily basis.
