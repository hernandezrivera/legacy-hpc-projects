Project Summary

South Sudan has been engulfed by conflict from 2013 to 2016, where armed conflict caused unbearing suffering of many people. Millions have been displaced internally and externally. In 2020, the country received large amounts of rainfall that caused severe flooding in many parts of the country leading to the destruction of farmland and crops, increased risk of water-borne diseases, flooding, and destruction of schools and other public utilities. Sub national conflicts continue to displace more people causing death, looting, and destruction of properties. The COVID-19 pandemic that hit the world in early March 2020 led to school closures, the collapse of the economy making life unbearable to IDPs and the host communities. The combined effects of flooding, inter-ethnic conflicts, and the COVID-19 pandemic have hit the education sector hardest, destroying schooling infrastructure, school closures, and the collapse of livelihoods increasing the vulnerability of parents, teachers, and children.
This project will support the resumption of learning, provide safe protective learning spaces, improve learning conditions by supporting the learners and teachers with education materials, build the capacity of teachers and the school community to support safe learning, and improve coping mechanisms and resilience. The project directly targets 3,918 learners in the 4 most-affected counties of Ayod, Fangak, Pochalla, and Akobo west counties of Jonglei State. The establishment of classrooms will provide a safe and protective environment for learners. This will launch resumption of learning in the project targeted areas and protect children from a harmful associations like drug abuse, early marriages, and recruitment to armed groups. The establishment of WASH facilities including hand washing will provide a safe environment from water-borne diseases and improve COVID-19 compliance. Integrated capacity building of teachers in pedagogy, PSS, protection/GBV would help them to give lifesaving skills support to learners so that they can be able to protect themselves and improve resilience by the youths. The SMC/PTAs would be trained in the management of schools in emergencies to support education. Education supplies provision will support a faster resumption of learning in schools.
This intervention intends to cover 4 primary schools and 4 ALP centers in Ayod, Fangak, Pochalla, and Akobo west state to provide a quality emergency learning environment to the affected IDPs, Returnees, and host community children.
CAO proposes to target 1750 Boys, 1850 Girls, 158 Men, and 160 Women as direct beneficiaries among the affected communities of IDPs and host communities’ children, youth, and adults including people with disabilities who have limited or no access to education services.
Inline EC strategy, the project will establish temporary learning spaces(TLS) and classrooms, establish gender-segregated latrines with water points and hygiene promotion, support school feeding programs, schools supplies, and hygiene kits, recreational materials, train teachers on EiE pedagogical skills, life skills to improve teaching-learning, train teachers and learners on PSS and the impact of COVID-19 and its protection mechanisms, Train teachers and SMC/PTA on protection/GBV, COVID-19, health, nutrition, and governance. CAO will maintain as strong coordination mechanisms with the other sectoral clusters at national and sub national levels and partners to ensure information and regular updates on beneficiaries reached and referred for complete integration.
CAO enjoys a static presence with very good experience and good relations with local education officials and other education stakeholders as well as having implemented multisectoral programs integrated within the education institutions in Fangak, Akobo West, Pochalla, and Ayod counties. Further, CAO has technical educationists who are experienced in implementing education in emergency programs in all our target locations.

1 - Needs

The South Sudan humanitarian overview (HNO) Report 2021, estimated 3.4million (51 percent girls) school-aged children have lack of or inadequate access to education services in 2021. This was a slight increase from the 3.1 million girls and boys in need in 2020. Exacerbated the COVIFD-19 effect, the HNO further estimated about 18,000 children are out of school. The negative impact of COVID-19 and subsequent school closures have resulted in an increased number of children in need of education support according to the humanitarian needs overview (HNO) report released this year Children and youth, aged between 3 and 17, represent 44 percent of South Sudan’s population. Education in the country has been severely impacted by successive years of conflict, economic crisis instability, displacement, and widespread flooding. Unfortunately, the capacity of the Ministry of General Education and Instruction (MoGEI) to respond to the huge needs of children remains limited. Displaced people, refugees, and returnees are identified as vulnerable groups and the education needs of children in these groups are particularly dire in Upper Nile and Warrap where a high percentage of children do not have access to education. The closure of schools and all learning institutions followed by COVID-19 as well as the severe floods continues to impart devastating effects on the education of very many children including IDPs, returnees, and hos community in the counties of Ayod, Fangak, Pochalla, and Akobo West has jeopardized critical gains made over the years. This was followed by the subsequent exposure to forms of gender-based violence including forced marriage and teenage pregnancies. Education needs remain very high in Jonglei states to spontaneous returnees and IDPs. The 3rd phase of severe flooding in 2021 significantly impacted education infrastructure, particularly in Fangak, Ayod, Akobo West, and Pochalla counties which are among the priority location targeted by this intervention. Many flood-affected people took shelter in schools. This could have affected the first phase of reopening of schools as announced by the MoGEI.According to the south Sudan IPC report published in November 2020 on the food insecure counties, the most population in Jonglei state are in IPC phase 4 and 5 (catastrophe) and this was projected to worsen in the first and second IPC projections through 2021. According to the same report, the drivers of this crisis is food insecurity due to destruction of farmland and harvests, death of livestock and lack of access to markets leading to increased food prices, communal conflicts that have led to displacements, majorly Fangak and Ayod with poor access to food and COVID-19 pandemic which led to increased travel restrictions, transportation of food commodities to the markets hence increased food prices and scaled-down education and food security interventions and poor WASH facilities hence unsafe water consumption by the households and inadequate water storage containers that lead to inadequate water supply and this exposes children to frequent diarrhea diseases. According to the FSL cluster report Jan 2022 which CAO actively participates, Ayod, Fangak Counties are in IPC phase 4 with huge flooding precipitating into a 3rd phase of floods resulting in the displacement of 25,626 Individuals and 23,190 People by the end of 2021. CAO's abstract observation in Ayod and Fangak indicates that children (both boys and girls) and Women are in dire need of basic education and health services. CAO will make use of its long-term operational and static presence in Jonglei state with a field office in Ayod, Fangak, Pochalla, Akobo West where the organization is implementing the 2021 SSHF SA1 to support this project. In line with Education cluster objectives and strategies, CAO will provide access to safe and protective education services, inclusive education services and enhance teachers' capacity to deliver EiE services in project locations.

Yes

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The Lessons learned by CAO from the 2020 and 2021 responses indicate that engagement of communities and relevant stakeholders is important to deliver education response which enhances ownership and accountability to the affected population. CAO regularly shares timely information, consultation, and dialogue between service providers and affected populations to improve the quality and efficacy of services to humanitarian response. CAO aims to ensure, the appropriate inclusion of expressed priorities, needs, and views of the affected population, particularly children, concerning protection and solutions. The project will encourage stringent accountability to the affected population and adapt the coherent approach to AAP through existing community structures. In this project, CAO will regularly undertake consultation with particularly high-risk groups of the affected population such as women, girls, and children with disabilities and priorities them while planning education interventions. SMCs, PTAs, and Community leaders will also continue to be involved in community sensitization efforts focusing on access to education services, school WASH and protection messages, and prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) to increase access to education for boys and girls as well as preventing and reducing protection risks for children on their way to and from school. Community members will be involved in the design of TLS, identification of a safe location for the construction of TLS, and monitoring teachers' and pupils’ attendance through the PTA. Setting up a simple, safe and accessible Community-Based Complaints Mechanism (CBCM) for SEA and protection of school children will be enhanced and strengthened. A community-based approach for the protection of school children will also add efficiency to strengthen reporting mechanisms by incorporating local solutions into the overall SEA reporting system. A successful complaints mechanism will ensure the full participation of the community, through integrating formal and/or informal community structures.

12 - Contribution to Collective Outcomes
12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

CAO is highly specialized in the provision of emergency protection interventions with a minimum package of prevention and risk mitigation of GBV in all the target locations for this project. Therefore, CAO will contribute to the collective outcome 2 by ensuring that GBV interventions especially related to GBV awareness through sensitization events and mobilizations will be conducted in all targeted education facilities that this project will establish. CAO will train teachers on GBV awareness, risks, and how to mitigate them. Further, school going children enrolled in this program will be educated on GBV taking into consideration of their age. In this case, it is anticipated that by 2023, CAO will have contributed to the prevention and risk mitigation of GBV in all its operational areas by at least 80% coverage of the GBV risk communication and mitigation interventions in Ayod, Fangak, Akobo west, Pochalla counties.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

Throughout implementation of this project, CAO will use the education platforms to carry out whistle blowing and early warning communications that are geared toward conservation of sustainable environment not only for the current generation but also for the future including establishing tree planting clubs in schools, and also promoting climate sensitive interventions like afforestation awareness among all learning centers in Ayod, Fangak, Pochalla, and Akobo West. This way, it is anticipated that CAO will contribute to lowering of flood related displacements by less than 30% by the year 2025.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

During the implementation of the project, CAO intends to use a multi-sector approach to integrate food security and livelihood activities in schools. For instance, CAO will establish school gardens in schools in Ayod, Akobo West, Fangak, and Pochalla and ensure that teachers and school-going children are trained on the best agronomic practices like vegetable production in school gardens. This way, it's anticipated that CAO will contribute to the reduction of people in IPC phase 4 to below by at least less than 20% by the year 2023. Further, CAO will ensure that school-going children and teachers are trained on maternal-infant and young child nutrition practices like ensuring minimum dietary diversity across the school meals. This will be provided through our integrated nutrition programs on nutrition in early childhood development programs in Akobo West, Fangak, Ayod, and Pochalla counties of Jonglei state.
