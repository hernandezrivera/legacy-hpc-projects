Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to reduce excess child morbidity and mortality through delivery of lifesaving curative and preventive nutrition services to IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Khartoum, Gedaref, Blue Nile, Medani, White Nile and South Kordofan states. This action will specifically target boys, girls and PLW with essential nutrition services. The services to be implemented will include lifesaving treatment, prevention activities, capacity building for healthcare providers, health infrastructure improvements, referral support to stabilization facilities. This plan has been revised to incorporate anticipated increased malnutrition caseloads in prioritized locations following the escalation of armed conflicts in Sudan since April 15, 2023. The IRC will support screening for malnutrition at the service delivery points and, in the community, deliver treatment for severe and moderately acute malnutrition, infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counseling to pregnant and lactating women (PLW). The IRC will deliver services directly through deployment of trained staff, support existing MoH efforts to deliver services and utilize existing local capacity by engaging with local CSO to deliver and scale up services. The IRC will build MoH capacity to improve skills in nutrition screening, growth monitoring, SAM and MAM treatment as well as follow up services. In addition, the IRC will provide infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counseling to pregnant and lactating women (PLW) to reduce incidence of malnutrition in the communities. This will be closely integrated with water and sanitation (WASH) activities to ensure good hygiene and reduction in WASH related diseases. The IRC will work closely with protection colleagues and local partners including community health volunteers (CHVs) to identify communities and children at risk of malnutrition and scale up screening services.
The IRC is a global leader in the delivery of quality nutrition services and innovations in humanitarian settings to improve nutrition outcomes including the development of the combined protocol to scale up lifesaving nutrition treatment. The IRC has the requisite staff capacity in country to lead nutrition activities, and already involved in providing nutrition services in Khartoum, Gedaref and Blue Nile states. The IRC technical excellence unit provides technical support to country teams to ensure quality delivery, with regional Technical Advisors for nutrition based in Nairobi in regular contact with the country teams. Even during the ongoing crisis in Sudan, the IRC maintains adequate capacity in country and within the region to deliver quality nutrition services and expand to new states with identified needs.

1 - Needs

The ongoing armed conflict between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) since April 15 has worsened the already precarious humanitarian situation in Sudan, already facing influx of refugees from neighboring countries, putting additional pressure on the limited resources for social services already strained by a worsening economic situation and the COVID-19 pandemic. According to revised 2023 HRP, deterioration of nutrition situation particularly in the hotspot areas and IDP receiving localities leading to estimated increase in the number of children with acute malnutrition of up to 30% in the hotspot areas, 15% in the IDPs receiving states and 10% in the rest of the localities. Interruption of lifesaving health and nutrition services in the hotspot areas leading to thousands of severe with and without medical complications and moderate malnourished under-five children, pregnant and lactating women missing lifesaving nutrition interventions. Thousands could not continue treatment due to closure of nutrition facilities, insecurity and interruption of supplies and absence of nutrition service providers. Increased partners operational costs due to transportation costs, inflation on commodities, hiring extra staff, warehousing including looted office equipment and assets resulting in increased unit costs for implementation of nutrition responses across the country ranging from 50 to 100% for some of the interventions. Due to the ongoing crisis, the PIN of nutrition services has a revised figure of 4.5 million and the nutrition cluster target revised from 2.4 million to 2.2 million in the May 20323 revised HRP. Deteriorating WASH services further compound the nutrition outlook with increased incidences of WASH related diseases that directly contribute to acute malnutrition. According to 2023 updated nutrition cluster estimates, treatment of severe acute of severe acute malnutrition with medical complication has increased by over 69% due to increased target and unit costs; treatment of severe acute malnutrition without medical complications by about 123% due to increased target, unit costs, operational costs; treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among under-five children by about 43% due increased targets and unit costs, and Maternal and Young Child Nutrition- feeding in emergency has increased by about 28% due increased unit costs, this is expected to have deteriorated further given the current economic situation and sky rocketing food inflation. The IRC has trained over 80 healthcare providers in Gedaref, Blue Nile and Khartoum on IYCF and CMAM, however more efforts required for White Nile, Medani and South Kordofan, and additional refresher trainings for Khartoum, Blue Nile and Gedaref. In Gedaref and Blue Nile the partners supporting MoH efforts to deliver nutrition services include ALIGHT, MSF, IRC, ZOA, MTI and IOM. Most of the nutrition centers require infrastructure upgrades to expand storage facilities for nutrition supplies and waiting shades, and human resource capacity enhancement in terms of training. Currently nutrition activities in Khartoum temporarily suspended due to access challenges and will resume as soon as the security situation in Khartoum improves.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The IRC will ensure safe disposal of non-medical wastes like plastic package, papers, and medical wastes like soiled swabs and organic materials using the colored bin systems to ensure proper waste segregation before final disposal and sharp containers for disposal of all needles, sutures, and blades. Medical wastes will be stored away from reach of children in secured or fenced area and incinerated, and remnants disposed safely. The project will prioritize installation of solar systems for health facility lighting to promote sustainable renewable energy sources and minimize emissions. The IRC health team will closely work with WASH colleagues to ensure appropriate IPC measures instituted at all service delivery points.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In designing its programs, the IRC makes every effort to address protection mainstreaming in the technical design. In all aspects of the project, from design to implementation and monitoring, the core protection principles of ‘Do No Harm’, meaningful access, safety and dignity, and participation will be mainstreamed. The IRC health team works closely with protection colleagues to identify protection risks, vulnerabilities and referral pathways for services. The action will ensure availability of essential nutrition services through training of health facility staff and preposition of nutrition supplies. Providers will be trained on ensuring privacy and confidentiality including in the health information system (HIS).

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The health team will conduct regular client interviews to collect feedback from caretakers on their experience during the visit to the health service point. This will cover waiting time, quality of services, medications received, their perception on how well they were treated, and what can be improved at the facility, among others. The IRC also generates additional feedback through the protection work in the community with the feedback shared with the health team for action. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its MEAL team and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action. The IRC Protection team will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the IRC Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, child safeguarding policies, process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). Overall, the IRC has zero tolerance for bullying, harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC has the following policies in place: Child Safeguarding Policy; Beneficiary Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) Policy; Harassment Free Workplace Policy. All allegations and investigation information received is maintained in a confidential centralized case management system. Cases are evaluated and prioritized when received taking into account, among other things, whether the alleged conduct is ongoing or historical. The IRC will ensure that all new staff hired for IRC Sudan are trained on and understand the seriousness with which the IRC takes such issues. The IRC will also participate in the UN-led PSEA network, ensuring communities and staff understand PSEA and how to access reporting channels. The IRC also has a neutral department called the global Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, who is responsible for all major complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
