Project Summary

Many parts of Somalia are experiencing prolonged drought conditions due to poor 2021 Gu rains. According to HNO 2022, approximately 6.6 million individuals (around 42% of the Somali population) are expected to need life-saving WASH assistance, of whom 4.2 million will require safe drinking water, 3.2 million sanitation services, and 5.2 million hygiene kits. 16% of these are females, 64% are children while the elderly form 4%. This presents an increase of 43% from 2021, in which 4.6 million individuals needed WASH responses across Somalia. The occurrence of drought conditions has led to significant water stress, particularly in the south. In addition, flood cycles, disease outbreaks and ongoing conflict-related displacements continue to contribute to WASH needs.
The JMCNA 2020 noted that >50% IDPs reportedly faced challenges in terms of access to soap and handwashing facilities while JMCNA 2021 reports that only 53% of households across Somalia practices proper handwashing with soap and water while 39% reported that they wash their hands but without soap and only 45% of households have access to Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) material at home or can access it through their local market.
In September 2021, Medair conducted a WASH Needs Assessment in the IDP settlements in the district of Daynile, Mogadishu which indicated that there is significant water shortage in the area. Many IDP families reported collecting only 10 litres of water per day for all uses. In addition, all Medair project locations in rural areas of Cadale, Badhaadhe, and Kismayo reported inadequate supply of water. Limited access to sanitation and poor hygiene practices especially in IDP settlements and vulnerable host communities often result in recurrent disease outbreaks such as AWD/Cholera.
The proposed action aims to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality attributable to the consumption and use of unsafe water, the lack of or inadequate sanitation facilities during emergency situations and inadequate hygiene practices through provision of an integrated WASH response. The proposed action includes intensifying hygiene promotion at community and household level to be conducted by an established network of behaviour change communication (BCC) groups. The project will also increase access to reliable safe water sources, provide emergency water supply and essential WASH NFIs to IDPs and vulnerable host population. Medair will also focus on improving sanitation facilities at the supported health facilities to ensure standard WASH facilities are met.
The proposed actions will be integrated with Health and Nutrition services. The interventions will be implemented in partnership with four local partner organizations namely; DAWA, SAACID, Somali Aid and ZAMZAM in Mogadishu - Karan and Daynile districts; Shabelle region - Cadale and Balcad districts; South West State - Afgooye district, and in Jubaland State - Kismayo and Badhaadhe districts targeting 68,730 beneficiaries. Medair will ensure close coordination with other local WASH actors and the cluster system to ensure better coordination as well as to avoid duplication of efforts.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

Minority clans/Ethnic groups are not included in consultations and or are excluded from the humanitarian services. Medair will embark on identifying and addressing differential risks of exclusion and discrimination from humanitarian services, including those based on societal, power structures, vulnerability, age and gender; the aim is to ensure all eligible beneficiaries are included. Medair will ensure that minority ethnic groups including internally displaced persons are fully involved in the entire project cycle. A consultative committee will be formed comprising host and displaced population representatives as well all minority ethnic groups. Exposure of women and girls to sexual evidence including sexual exploitation and abuse. All Medair and partner staff will receive training on prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse. They will also be expected to sign a code of conduct which forbids them from sexually exploiting beneficiaries. The humanitarian principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality, and independence will be observed. Individuals who are known to have committed acts of sexual exploitation and abuse will not be hired anywhere in the Medair Somalia system. Screening of candidates will be done for every Medair post prior to hiring. Once employed, Medair will continue with organization wide awareness-raising about what constitutes acceptable behavior by staff and personnel and how to report wrongdoing through the existing mechanisms.The BCC groups include an informal reporting mechanism and also provide messages on sexual violence and exploitation. Psychosocial support, counselling and first aid will be provided to the victims and staff trained on clear referral pathways in coordination with the gender-based violence actors in the villages. Medair recognizes women and children are among the most vulnerable groups during conflicts and ensure they are aware of their rights for seeking treatment whilst continuing to seek to address stigma. Risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance to and location from humanitarian assistance and services or indiscriminate attack from armed actors Medair performs security assessment where information is gathered from the local communities as well as international security watch groups, in particular the International NGO Safety Organization (INSO) This information is carefully analyzed to identify potential threats and risks, prior project set up. Secure locations, which don’t put staff at risk of accidental and incidental insecurity incidences are identified for project implementation. Areas near military installations and hotspot roads with frequent landmines and IEDs or violence re avoided. Services will be provided as close as possible to the beneficiaries, which reduces the risks to beneficiaries being harmed as they seek services
