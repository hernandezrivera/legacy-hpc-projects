Project Summary

The project meets strategic and cluster objectives:
Strategic objective 02: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of essential services, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL.
HEALTH AND NUTRITION CLUSTER OBJECTIVE 1: Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt.
In general, this project will contribute to addressing the lifesaving needs for high risk babies at neonatal units in the Gaza and improving neonatal care management in order to contribute to reducing mortality, morbidity and long-term disability among newborns.
The project’s main two objectives are:
1- Saving neonatal lives by ensuring the availability of life saving items and essential infection control supplies at the neonatal units. MAP aims to achieve the project’s objectives in coordination with the Ministry of Health, UNICEF, WHO, UNFPA and other stakeholders.
2- Improving the skills and competencies of staff at the neonatal units on neonatal life support, neonatal safe transport, and infection control.

1 - Needs

Root causes of babies being born prematurely or with any birth condition that would make a baby visit a NICU are the harsh socio-economic status that the population face in Gaza. The MSNA of 2022 indicated that 63% of the population in Gaza are in need of humanitarian assistance. To add, the MSNA also indicated that 65% of the households surveyed had moderate to severe shortages in food. The HNO confirms that 40% of pregnant women are anaemic and that malnourished pregnant women constitute 18% in Gaza. Whenever MAP follows up with parents at neonatal unit, it usually is a trend (still needs confirmation through a research) that we find parents are of low socio-economic status which negatively affects the health of parents, but also their children. Digging deeper, the head of the Gaza Neonatal Network confirmed to MAP that majority of parents of neonatal babies at ICUs in Gaza have their babies with IVF operations. “The cost per IVF cycle is double the annual GDP per capita in Palestine. IVF services play a significant role in increasing premature births in Palestine and contributing to an increase in the rate of Cesarean Section as a result of multiple-pregnancy and the possibility of developing life-threatening complications for women.” (UNFPA, 2019). The 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for the oPt indicates that there are 20,900 neonates in total with several health conditions. Of those it is estimated that 7,600 would need transfer to neonatal intensive care units for specialised life-saving treatment. Almost half of the babies are boys and the other half is girls.Even after they get born, neonates are still one of the most vulnerable groups in Gaza. The ongoing and protracted shortages of staff and their skills, shortages in drugs, disposables, and infection control supplies are all factors affecting the care of neonates in Gaza. According to MAP’s neonatal monthly monitoring data, neonatal case fatality in 2021 reached 12.84, compared to 11.3 in 2020 (in Israel, the rate is 1.9). So far, the neonatal case fatality in 2022 reached 12.7, hitting the peak in January 2022 with an alarming high rate of 15.25. Incidence of neonatal sepsis deaths reached 11.88 in 2021 in comparison to 10.68 over the first three quarters of 2022. All in which indicates the importance to continue supporting the NICUs in Gaza. As for staff and their skills, MoH have been suffering from high staff turnover and a rotational policy to fill the huge gaps of manpower; neonatal units are no exception. Therefore, it is important to continue training on Neonatal Life Support (NLS), neonatal safe transfer (NST) and NLS in-service to raise the skills of staff rotated through these units. In addition, it is very important that neonatal babies go through a systematic RoP screening process to prevent blindness. While started the process of RoP screening and recently the treatment, still, an investment in human resources is the most important. Currently, there is only one doctor at MoH who is specialised in treatment of RoP cases, who was supported by MAP to get a training in Egypt, but the load needs at least one more doctor and an increase in the capacity building and staff development. Not to forget awareness for the parents. To add, MAP is leading in the field of developing neurodevelopmental clinics for neonatal follow-up but more training and awareness are still needed. As for supplies, the MoH, GNN with the International Cooperation Department’s (ICD) collaborate with stakeholders to continuously assess the needs. However, gaps still exist, and the needs are huge. The estimated budget to cover the neonatal needs for 2022 in total is over 5 million USD and this is expected to be almost the same in 2023, according to the head of GNN. Recent MoH zero stock levels of drugs and disposables indicated that in September 2022 there was a shortage of 43% in maternity and pediatric drugs specifically. For disposables, there are shortages of about 8% in ICU disposables and 45% in ophthalmic disposables, both which are used for neonatal care.

2 - Activities

• Procurement of life saving neonatal drugs and consumables to be used for 7600 at high-risk newborn babies/year including female and male babies admitted to neonatal ICU to benefit from the neonatal care project’s interventions. This will happen in coordination with MoH, UNICEF, WHO, UNFPA through the GNN and all related parties. • Procurement of infection control supplies and printing educational materials (infection control booklets) to be used for 7600 high risk newborn babies/year including female and male babies admitted to NICU and who will benefit from the neonatal care project’s interventions. This will happen coordination with MoH, UNFPA, WHO and UNICEF. • Procure necessary equipment that is urgently needed to address the over-crowding in NICUs. This will happen in coordination with MoH and NGOs. • Implement three Neonatal Life Support Trainings NLS and three Infection Control training workshops for neonatal and obstetric department for 200 health professionals. 120 female and 80 male health professionals working at the neonatal and obstetric units of MoH will be targeted. These training will be coordinated with MOH different departments. It is worth mentioning that MAP has trained 11 male and female health practitioners (doctors and nurses) to provide support to the UK consultant when the training is done. Also the availability of male and female trainers ensures that we are addressing any gender and power dynamics.• Implement on-job NLS training courses and procure the needed equipment for these courses. These trainings will be also coordinated with the above-mentioned parties. • Procure drugs related to RoP screening and treatment in coordination with GNN members. • Train ophthalmologists on RoP treatment via local expertise and via fellowships in the ME area.• Provide RoP IEC materials to the parents of neonatal children.• Increase capacity of neonatologists in Gaza via the provision of two new online master’s degrees an in a joint programme between the Neonatal Online Training and Education (NOTE) Faculty of the European Society of Pediatric Research (ESPR), and the University of Southampton. Currently MAP is sponsoring two pediatrics from Gaza (a man and a woman) and they are about to finish their first year in the programme.• Continue to support MoH to collect data on sepsis and hand hygiene practices to inform the IPC unit at MoH and GNN of the statistics that will lead interventions of MAP and other entities. All the above activities are a combination of procurement, capacity building, awareness raising, and medical missions which are all part of the proposed activities under the HNO 2023. All the emergency services that MAP will support will contribute to the reduction of long-term disabilities in neonates as these babies are endangered and subject to death or develop long-term disabilities. With regards to gender, MAP has designed the project activities to meet the different needs of women, men, boys and girls. The project will target boy and girl neonates but for the training, more emphasis will be given to women health practitioners as they form the majority of the workers at NICUs, plus, the local trainers’ cadre that MAP has invested in, who help the consultant in provision of all courses, are a mixture of both genders to address cultural sensitivities. As for partnership with local NGOs, while we do not have direct links under the neonatal project with any community partner; MAP has other partners involved in its MCH projects (with CFTA and ACHA) focusing on women at reproductive age, MAP is working with these women to support prenatal and antenatal care which will have direct impact on the neonatal morbidity and mortality and referral links with partners could be easily established. Please note that the focus of this project is about provision of life-saving items and equipment to the NICUs that are solely by MoH. There is only one neonatal unit in the NGO community which is considered as an intermediate unit. The babies received at this NGO unit, if extra care is needed, they will be transferred to one of the MoH NICUs eventually. 3 - Indicators

The project will be monitored thoroughly via MAP’s staff including the Senior Programme Manager, the Emergency and Complex Hospital Care Manager, the programme officer in charge, the programme assistant, the procurement department, and the finance department under the auspices of the Director of Gaza. PMEAL is a crucial part of our work and all our projects have logframes, M&E plans, implementation plans and detailed budgets. MAP conducts field visits to the NICUs, conducts interviews with relevant MoH staff, collects data and official records from CDS and also distribution lists to MoH, and has its own internal quarterly reports, financial reports, and end of phase reports all on which focus on results-based management. In addition, please note for this project in specific MAP collects monthly data from the 6 NICUs about neonatal mortality rates in general and those related to sepsis in specific to inform our decision making but also the MoH’s and the Gaza Neonatal Network (GNN). To add, MAP collects monthly data on Infection Control Prevention (IPC) compliance inside the NICUs which also helps us in planning proper interventions and workshops related to IPC. The last special M&E tool we have under this project is the fact that collect data on Retinopathy of prematurity (RoP) related cases. During the years RoP data has helped us indicate the levels of procurement needed to address the issue but also decide on new interventions related to treatment and training needs for MoH staff. It should be mentioned that for all training courses, we conduct pre and post tests to measure knowledge but also have satisfaction surveys. Although we do not provide services directly to the parents of the neonatal babies, we will also employ phone calls that will enable us to reach out to them and seek their feedback about anything that we can enhance or communicate with the MoH about. The indicators for this project are: • # of boy and girl neonates receiving healthcare services in NICUs• # of boy and girl neonates benefited from procured medical supplies• # of male and female healthcare workers trained on Sexual Reproductive and Maternal Child• Health and Nutrition (SRMCHN)• % of knowledge enhancement in NLS, NIST and RoP trainings• % of increased good practices in IPC and Hand Hygiene methods• % of neonatal Mortality rate before and after the intervention (this is a goal indicator and MAP will contribute towards it as other HC partners will do, this is not ours ultimately to claim or achieve but MAP was able to contribute to reducing the NMR from 30-40% before 2008 to almost 9-10% in the last couple of years)• Staff at NICUs report better attitudes towards NICU work (qualitative through interviews, FGDs, and phone calls) Target: 7600 newborn Male: 3800 Female: 3800. Target: 30% of NICUs’ health staff members and professionals participating in the NLS and infection control training courses. Target: 200 (120 F, 80 M). The means of verification will include:• Field visits by MAP staff to NICUs and neonatal clinics• CDS entry records• Photography and Videography• Official distribution lists from CDS to MoH facilities receiving the items• Interviews with NICU staff• Collection of stories form parents on neonatal babies• Donation certificates by MAP detailing the donation and signed by MoH officials confirming the receipt• Sepsis surveillance monthly data• IPC and Hand Hygiene monthly data• RoP screening and treatment data

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

MAP has designed the project activities to meet the different needs of women, men, boys and girls. The project will target male and female neonates but for the training, more emphasis will be given to female health practitioners as they form the majority of the workers at NICUs, plus, the local trainers’ cadre that MAP has invested in, who help the consultant in provision of all courses, are a mixture of both genders to address cultural sensitivities. MAP applies strict rules to ensure that the items procured are what is needed by MoH. We provide testing as part of the contracts, and we reject any items that do not match the technical criteria ensuring that the medical items are of the most benefit to the beneficiaries.The provision of the items will make sure that availability (free donated items with no charge) and accessibility are guaranteed for the targeted population. In addition, we make sure that the quality of the items procured is top notch as we have many clauses in our contracts defending this right. To add, the items procured are items that reduce invasive interventions with children, such as nasopharyngeal tubes instead of using endotracheal tubes. As for acceptability, MAP always seeks feedback from the beneficiaries and the partner and will make sure that the intervention is tailored to the need and fills a critical gap. MAP will also make sure to coordinate the response with the MoH, health cluster, GNN, UNICEF, UNFPA, and other stakeholders in order to avoid duplication of services which in a way is considered as a harm being brought on the health system in general and the beneficiaries in specific.Safeguarding of children and vulnerable adults. MAP has developed an organisational algorithm for responding to Safeguarding concerns and region-specific algorithms for the initial investigation of such concerns. These documents were developed in conjunction with safeguarding experts and are considered fit for purpose and consistent with current sector guidance and the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS). They contain clear lines of responsibility and reporting and, where appropriate, confirm the timelines to be followed when responding to safeguarding concerns. All these documents are reviewed at least every two years. An incident report template is used to report any suspected breaches in MAP’s Code of Conduct or Safeguarding policy to MAP’s Safeguarding focal point.

Failure to answer this part properly results in exclusion of the candidate from the interview list.
