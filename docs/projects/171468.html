Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Somalia is continuing to remain one of the most difficult and tragic scenario worldwide. Political instability, conflicts and natural disasters are still the main causes of social unrest, displacement, lack of health care and nutrition, violence and extreme poverty. In this context, the most vulnerable groups are women and girls, disabled, people with mental health issues, who are constantly marginalized, abused and deprived of their rights and resources. In addition, the Covid-19 pandemic contributed to a general worsening of the situation in the country, and a constant rise of protection issues registered in many regions.
Boys and girls in Somalia represent the 66% of the population in need (HNO,2020) and continue to be exposed to several types of violations, such as physical and sexual violence, abduction, child labor, separation from their families. Psychological distress is an overwhelming consequence (71%, ref. perception survey by the CP AoR -2020). The project aims at activating a protection intervention in Puntland (Bosaso, Garowe and Galkayo) in order to respond to the needs of boys and girls victims of violence.
The intervention will tackle both prevention and response mechanisms through a combination of activities, which will rely on the consolidated experience of the organization in the sector and long-term presence in the geographical area.
In particular, project activities will focus on the establishment of an IDTR system for the prompt identification and case management of children in need. Community base mechanisms will be activated in order to set up individualized plans, trace their families and reintegrate the children in a safe family environment. For all cases that cannot be easily and readily reintegrated, a foster care system will be organized by identification and training of foster families, as an emergency measure to guarantee a safe environment for children in need.
Children will benefit from Psychosocial support provided by project protection staff, as well as referral medical services when needed.
Training on Child Protection to local authorities, community and traditional leaders, child protection actors, will be provided to strengthen community capacity to respond to child protection needs.
Lastly, awareness campaigns will be organized concurrently to relevant calendar events.
The organization prioritizes by statute people with disabilities and with Mental Health problems by addressing the access to services-issue, developing tailored intervention based on individual assessment and documentation. Focal points are trained to identify and facilitate assess of persons with disability, training on disability inclusion is part of capacity building.
GRT uses internally developed tools to monitor and plan activities, which are developed based on the agreed project log frame.
The project/area coordinator is responsible for updating the monitoring tool on a monthly basis from information received by field staff involved in direct implementation. The tool is shared with senior management for monitoring of progresses. This ensures that adherence to targets ad indicators is monitored in a timely manner, that relevant staff is involved with clear roles and responsibilities and that proper planning is revised periodically on a result-based manner.
In addition to the above, monitoring of activities is done on-site by area coordinator through field missions with the purpose of meeting the beneficiaries (on-site visit, interviews), stakeholder and authorities.
A review monitoring mission is also conducted jointly/by MOWDAFA on project implementation progress, particularly on the alternative care, psychosocial support provided to children and adolescents.
Information are collected according to the international standards respecting the principle of confidentiality and shared for coordination purposes (including GBVIMS /CPIMS and info to the clusters) anonymously.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents: the project will ensure proper dissemination of information is done within the communities about availability of services, locations and contacts. Emergency Support telephone line will be available 24h, to enable anybody reach project protection staff and request assistance. Meeting with communities will be periodically organized in order to spread information and ensure target groups are reached and involved in all project phases. 2. Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries: project staff will be trained on confidential collection and management of personal data. All personal records and case management documentation will be stored in dedicated safe lockers, with access limited to project staff only. Compliance with confidentiality and privacy protocols will be assessed periodically by field mission of senior management staff, as provided by organization's internal policies. 3. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: the organization will rely on long-term experience it the geographical area implementing similar projects, using a community-based approach with attention at involving all communities. Project objectives and activities will be disclosed, beneficiaries' selection criteria discussed openly and transparently, complaint and feedback mechanisms availed in case of any issue. Meetings with communities will be held periodically to review implementation progresses and address concerns. Moreover, periodic monitoring from senior staff will address possible demands or requests by meeting with local leaders and authorities and ensuring the project implementation will not be affected by external pressures.
