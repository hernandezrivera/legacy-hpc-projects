Project Summary

BRAC's protection programme eliminates and reduces abuse, exploitation, violence, and neglect of Rohingya refugees and the host community members. Various protection-centred activities are being conducted to ensure the provision of basic needs, restore dignity and ensure wellbeing and recovery while protecting the vulnerable members of the communities from abuse and violence.
In 2023, BRAC's Community Based Protection (CBP) and legal protection will reach the host Communities (Rajapalang, Palangkhali, Whykong, Nhila)to support communities with specific needs prioritising the elderly, people with disabilities, single women, and LGBTQ. BRAC will provide information on access to the available protection and other services through its programme. In addition, BRAC will address protection concerns in host communities based on protection monitoring information and enable the communities to lead in the provision of protection services via Information Service Centers, Community Groups and Legal Services.
BRAC will monitor the protection environment in the proposed unions through mixed monitoring methods, using tools and engaging relevant stakeholders. BRAC will identify Persons with Specific Needs (PSN) and People at heightened risk and will provide referral and case management support as required. In host community, BRAC will ensure that programme beneficiaries have better self-management and empowerment within their communities. The community outreach members will respond to the protection and assistance needs of members of their communities, arrange awareness sessions and social dialogue on various protection issues to promote Community Based Protection (CBP) and focus on broader challenges which often undermine social cohesion.
BRAC's protection programme will work with its DRR team and partners to integrate protection during DRR programming and response. BRAC's protection programme will also build the capacity of the host community to strengthen emergency preparedness. BRAC recognises the important role the volunteer plays for programme implementation, advocacy and quality assurance. Therefore, 10% of the total project amount is planned towards volunteer remuneration who will be engaged to play a key role in message dissemination, volunteer lead project initiatives and outreach activities. Community and information centres will facilitate various activities to unite people for creative and constructive individual expression and social dialogue. It will, in turn, enable the safety, dignity, meaningful access, participation and empowerment of affected people. Feedback mechanisms such as complaint boxes in service points, hotline numbers for PSEA focal, and sensitisation on right-based assistance in all existing community centres to resolve intercommunity disinformation will be implemented.
BRAC will provide Legal Aid through integrated protection, assist in gender-based violence (GBV) cases, and mediation and counselling on gender-based protection risks in host communities. Thus, the legal aid programme will focus on strengthening access to legal assistance for host communities through legal representation, legal counselling, mediation, and facilitation of family visits to refugees in prison. BRAC will further provide legal awareness sessions on legal rights, services, applicable laws, and legal procedures. Specific topics related to the legalisation of marriage, divorce registration, birth registration, divorce, child marriage, domestic violence, SGBV and human trafficking.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The district of Cox’s Bazar, located in southeastern Bangladesh, faces some of poorest living conditions in the country in part due to higher poverty rate. The Bangladeshi Bureau of Statistics identified Cox’s Bazar as one of the country’s “lagging districts” in 2017 and estimates the district’s headcount poverty rate at 17% [1]. The aforementioned developmental issues have been compounded by the most recent influx of refugees. For decades, Rohingya refugees have been fleeing to Bangladesh from Rakhine State, Myanmar due to periodic outbreaks in violence. As of 31 August 2022, 943,529 Rohingya refugees are now residing in 34 camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas in Cox’s Bazar District, Bangladesh[2], roughly five years after the recent influx. It is in these two Upazilas where refugee populations represent the majority of the population, estimated to outnumber the host community by three-to-one, and where host communities are assumed to have been most affected by the crisisIn 2022, the overall protection environment in host communities has not improved significantly due to multiple factors. The host communities are still recovering from the pandemic's effects. The situation is the same in the host communities, as there are cultural similarities between the two groups. There is an increase in female-headed households in the host community either due to abandonment or main earners temporarily migrating off the island for economic reasons. As a result, girls and young women from host are particularly vulnerable to child marriage, sexual abuse, and trafficking. Women and girls in polygamous marital situations, those living with disabilities, and transgender people are more exposed to physical, psychological, and sexual harassment and abuse. There is strong needs of reinforcement community outreach on issues including domestic violence, trafficking, right to safety and dignity of diverse gender groups and person with disabilities, sexual and reproductive health issues, harmful social/cultural practices (e.g- dowry, polygamy, child marriage) through active engagement of different community people including the gate-keepers in culturally sensitive approaches [3]. In addition, due to the political status of the Rohingya community, their safety and security in terms of protection against crime is significantly affecting them. As a result, limited access to justice and legal remedies remains one of the major challenges faced by Rohingya refugees. The main impediments to justice include restrictive administrative policies, slowness of the judicial system to resolve disputes, corruption, ambiguous laws, low legal literacy, and lack of access to information. Having limited mobility of women and other vulnerable people in the Rohingya community, has restricted their regular access to get information on legal support or counselling. Therefore, to effectively prevent community protection, there is a need to promote a community-based approach and provide legal aids to address unequal social practices, gender stereotypes and support survivors' access to quality services. Reference- [1] CARE. Host Communities Situational Analysis, 2021. Available here (accessed 20 August 2022). [2] UNHCR Population INFO, August, 2022[3] UNDP, Impacts of the Rohingya Refugee Influx on Host Communities (Cox’s Bazar, 2018). Available here (accessed 15 August, 2022).

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Following the JRP protection rationalisation strategy, BRAC will follow an integrated approach to ensure effective and impactful outputs during project design and implementation. BRAC has been responding to the crisis since 2017. The last five years of learning and experience show that an integrated approach incorporating other relevant sectors like Shelter, WASH, health, DRR, GBV (referral, case management) and child protection will have a more holistic and greater impact on the response for host communities. This project will focus on Brac’s expertise in delivering specialised protection services at vulnerbale host locations. Therefore, the overall objective of this project will be to provide services to people most at risk of protection. This project will adopt an integrated approach to ensure cost-effectiveness and set a clear vision that thrives on broader objectives of a longer period to achieve a significant impact. On different components, this project will be integrated with BRAC’s other sectors, including WASH, health and DRR, as well as with GBV (referral, case management) and child protection to develop a holistic service mechanism in the proposed host community. Preventing and mitigating the risk of security needs wider attention with longer-term planning to analyse the root cause and architecting an intervention through tailored activities to transform the gender impurities in communities and strengthen resilience among vulnerable people, including women, girls, People with Disabilities and LGBTQ. With this objective to develop an inclusive protection service structure, BRAC will focus on a longer-term approach to ensure sustainability and accountability to the affected communities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

To mainstream protection, this project is designed to address four protection principles including safety, dignity and do not harm; meaningful access; accountability; participation and empowerment. As the project will focus on improving overall protection environment through community engagement and legal supports, , from project participants selection to implementation, need based tailored activities (Information service center, vocational training, community outreach, imam engagement, legal case management/mediation/counseling) will be implemented. Project participants will be actively involved in operationalizing the overall activities in community centers, legal service centers and all facilities will be well equipped to ensure equal participation of PWD, elderly and LGBTQ . Furthermore, a well-trained project staffs will be deployed to ensure safety and do not harm principles. In large, this project will aid to the overall project environment in its operational host community.

3.2 - Gender

Ensuring Gender equality is the core focus of BRAC's strategy. BRAC will emphasise empowerment and inclusivity of vulnerable genders, mainly women and girls, older people, LGBTIQ and persons with disabilities. BRAC will set different indicators to measure the integration of age-gender segregated data. BRAC will ensure all its staff are trained on gender mainstreaming. In addition, BRAC will addresses gender needs and gaps and ensure meaningful access(Clear and informed information dissemination process, accessible location, timing, need-based activities, volunteer supports for EVI ETC) to services and facilities for vulnerable people, including women, girls, men, boys, PWD and LGBTIQ. Furthermore, to ensure the 'do not harm' approach and our accountability towards the vulnerable community, men and boys will also be an integrated part of this project to prevent violence against women by transforming power imbalances.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

BRAC will set specific indicators to measure the integration of age inclusion in its programme. BRAC has a strong child segregated policy and shares child safeguarding data with UNICEF. In addition, BRAC will ensure all its staff are trained on age inclusion in its programme. BRAC has a child safeguarding policy aligned with the global child safeguarding policy. All BRAC staff and volunteers are trained and monitor the safeguarding status in its implementation areas, and data is collected to monitor the trend to ensure all children under the programme are safe and have access to services. BRAC also provides action, including legal measures taken quickly to protect children when and where required. The programme will enhance meaningful participation and strengthen resilience among different ages through community and rights-based approaches. Furthermore, to ensure the 'do not harm' approach and our accountability towards the host communities.

3.4 - Diversity

BRAC believes in ensuring diversity in its programmes or workplace. All age, gender, ethnicity, race, and religion-diversified people will receive services though the most vulnerable will be prioritised, including disabilities. A broader spectrum of diversity will be promoted through activities and policy. Through its programmes,BRAC will encourage different sexual orientated people including transgender survivors to report any protection or GBV incidents. BRAC will promote the rights and safety of diverse sexual oriented individuals to advance gender equity and the elimination of all types of GBV and try to eliminate stigma and taboos surrounding transgender people. Along with the Rohingya community, this project will also address the need of women, girls, men and boys inclusive of PWD and LGBTQ in the host community based on a contextual analysis of their situation.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to affected populations (AAP) is an active commitment to use power responsibly by taking into account giving account to and being held to account by the people humanitarian organisations seek to assist. BRAC will give beneficiaries a meaningful influence over decision-making in inclusive, non-discriminatory accounts for the diversity of communities. Besides this, BRAC will also put individual feedback mechanisms and innovative approaches that can reinforce transparency and mutual accountability and have a positive impact. Complaint boxes will be installed in service points, and hotline numbers for PSEA focal will be shared in the service points and even in the community. Project information will be shared with the beneficiaries effectively and transparently.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

BRAC emphasises building resilience to disasters and emergencies.This programme will raise awareness on disaster risk reduction in the community including slope degradation, landslides, deforestation, water logging, water scarcity and contamination, solid waste management and ways to place measures that will reduce negative impact during natural disasters and accidents. This programme will build the capacity of the host volunteers thus they can act as fast responders during emergencies. Therefore a contingency plan will be developed thus protection services can continue during emergencies. In addition, BRAC has an Environmental and Social Safeguard Framework (ESSF). As per ESSF, BRAC will not directly or indirectly support any activity that will significantly convert or degrade critical natural habitats for environmental sustainability. BRAC will ensure that the operation does not have potentially adverse severe impacts on biodiversity and ecosystem services.
