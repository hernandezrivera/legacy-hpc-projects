Project Summary

"The overarching goal of this project is to significantly enhance the living conditions and accessibility of Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) services for both the Rohingya Refugee population and the vulnerable Host Communities residing in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh. This multifaceted initiative encompasses a spectrum of activities designed to address the critical needs of these communities and contribute to their overall well-being.
The cornerstone of the project involves the construction and rehabilitation of essential WASH infrastructure, including latrines, bathing cubicles, hand washing points, and water tap stands. This infrastructure is meticulously planned to not only meet minimum safety standards but also to incorporate gender considerations and ensure accessibility for individuals with disabilities. In parallel, existing WASH facilities undergo meticulous maintenance, operation, and upgrades, aligning with protection standards. This includes the installation of solar lights, secure door locks, and the implementation of gender-segregated facilities.
Environmental hygiene is a focal point, with a focus on the operation and maintenance of waste management systems. This encompasses the establishment and upkeep of waste collection points, provision of bins at household and community levels, and the overall enhancement of waste disposal practices. The project also addresses the crucial aspect of faecal sludge management, involving plant maintenance, desludging, and transportation.
Vector control is prioritized through targeted training sessions, equipping the communities with the knowledge and tools to mitigate the transmission of arthropod-borne diseases. Additionally, measures are implemented to safeguard WASH facilities from environmental hazards such as floods and landslides. This includes the construction of retention walls, drainage systems, and ditches to enhance the resilience of the infrastructure.
The operation and maintenance of the water distribution system are integral components of the project, involving chlorination and water trucking to ensure a safe and reliable water supply. Rigorous water quality testing, extending to the household level, is conducted to monitor and maintain high standards of water safety.
At the household level, the project emphasizes the improvement of water storage and treatment practices. Concurrently, personalized hygiene promotion sessions are conducted with specific focus groups, including those with special needs. These sessions cover a spectrum of topics, ranging from environmentally friendly practices to age- and gender-sensitive WASH behaviors, COVID awareness, menstrual hygiene management (MHM), and family planning promotion.
To further support hygiene practices, the project includes the distribution of hygiene kits, extending crucial assistance to the affected communities. Regular coordination mechanisms are established with key stakeholders, including the RRRC office, CiC Office, DPHE, Wash Sector, and other relevant actors. This ensures a collaborative and integrated approach, optimizing the impact of the project and fostering sustained improvements in the WASH conditions of the targeted populations."

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"The Joint Response Plan 2023 estimates that 1.52 million people (978,000 RRs and 538,000 HCs) affected by the crisis are in urgent need of WASH support. The needs range from access to safe drinking water and water for domestic needs, adequate sanitation facilities including solid waste management and access to hygiene services need to improve. The proposed project focuses on Camp-27 (Block A & C), Camp-25 (Sub-Block-A4, A5, B6 & B7) and Camp-24 (Block-A, B, C, D & E), where many HHs have high needs in all these areas simultaneously. Project will support around 41,104 Rohingya refugees and adjunct host communities directly (8523 HHs) in the targeted camps and locations. Indirectly around 100,000 inhabitants of camps 24, 25, 27 and Nhila Union will benefit from the project. Twenty-five percent of the project provisions will be allocated for the host community (as specified by the government). Project will give priority to the extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs), persons with disabilities (PWD), girls, women and children. Adolescents and women will be specifically targeted for the hygiene behavioral change, promotion and capacity building activities. At the beginning of the Rohingya response, many NGOs constructed WASH facilities that did not apply the sector standard which created health risks, also many NGOs left due to funding cuts. In the existing project locations, more than 48% of latrines need to be replaced or upgraded in camps 24, 25 and 27. Consequently, people with disabilities have no or very limited access to the WASH facilities. According to the UNHCR camp profile in camps 24 & 27, 20 to 25 individuals share one latrine. In all three targeted camps, care and maintenance & upgrading of the existing WASH infrastructures, solid & fecal waste management, and hygiene promotion are the priorities at this moment.Teknaf is an area of water scarcity and in the dry season there is always water crisis. In this project we supply safe drinking water from borehole & surface water to the Rohingya refugee and adjacent host community. Also ensure the access to the wash facility with certain gender segregation at wash intervention. Besides, regular maintenance & operation of FSM, SWM & Plastic Recycle Plant to ensure safe & sustainable environment. Change of behavior towards hygiene through awareness raising. Formation of user group & wash management committee at sub-block level to improve their capacity and develop ownership.Project will also identify needs/gaps from multi sector through coordination meeting in camp level, sectoral meeting in camp level and cox’s bazar level, Wash sector portal, AFA findings etc. Besides, joint physical assessment is also fruitful to find the actual gaps in the field level where CiC Officials, wash actors, site management support, others relevant actors, beneficiaries etc. will be involved. In assessment, consideration of protection issues, gender sensibility and environmental factors and make project successful.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will be implemented in an integrated approach where an implementation plan will be developed and followed coordinating with camp level WASH sector, relevant agencies, CICs and other govt agencies and stakeholders. For service delivery to the refugees and host community participation of the project's participants will be ensured. In the project, there's an opportunity to promote hygiene for the beneficiaries and raise awareness to maintain proper hygiene practices. If the community is maintained properly, it simultaneously reduces health risks and decreases diseases. To ensure safe drinking water for the target beneficiaries, use rental boreholes from the host community and borehole owners to benefit from the project and develop their socio-economic condition. Also, we have an existing solid waste management plant that will have produced the compost which will be used in the agricultural land we may link with the agricultural development project or agricultural department which will also benefit from the project.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection strategies in WASH will ensure that all groups of people facing additional vulnerabilities including children, adolescence boys & girls, women, older people, and people with disabilities, have adequate and safe access to water collection and use; functional latrines and bathing units with safe and accessible pathways, solid doors, locks and other inclusive features such as screening and lighting; and safe spaces for practicing Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM). Latrine or bathing cubicles that are used by the females are distinguished by fencing around the facilities. Two separate queues are made for male & female while collecting water. The WASH Sector coordinates with the Protection Sector, the GBV Sub-Sector to improve the responsiveness of WASH actors in relation to protection-related issues. The WASH partners pursue full implementation of the Global WASH Cluster’s five minimum commitments for safety and dignity.

3.3 - Age & Disability

"Child safeguarding & people with disability are considered as a cross-cutting issue, in most organizations do not consider them as such, and without institutional recognition, it is likely that responses aimed at them will remain inconsistent. During monthly distribution of NFI kits volunteers will deliver in their (child, vulnerable people, pregnant women etc.) house. Ensure sanitation for children and vulnerable groups while construction of any intervention. Anando obtained funding from WHH to research how the WASH sector is currently responding to the needs of children & vulnerable groups, in order to identify best practices for their behavioral change by forming different child groups to increase their promotion. Anando includes gender, ethnic origin, age, disability, language, religious beliefs, social background, physical appearance and colour. This project will support the community people considering all this. Here all kinds of people including transgender, vulnerable people, older & younger generation and people with disability will be ensured of sanitation, water supply & hygiene promotion.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

For the sake of transparency and accountability, the necessary project information will be shared with the community members to raise their awareness, community consultation sessions need to be conducted to ensure the participation of the community and to collect their opinions and feedback. Under the project, community complaints will be received through a number of channels: complaint boxes, use managed hotline, FGDs, community meetings and routine monitoring, field staff, observation distribution monitoring (ODM) and post-distribution monitoring (PDM). All complaints will be recorded in the CRM register book with a unique number in order to easily identify each feedback and complaint. After sorting and classification, complaints will be forwarded to the relevant technical person to be addressed and resolved. Generally, complaints referred to the need to repair the roof and change the door lock of a latrine or bathing cubicle, to non-functional lights, theft of handwashing taps, latrine desludging needed, distribution of hygiene kits and waste bins.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

ANANDO and WHH have PSEA policies which are strictly following the sector standards. While implementing the project ANANDO with technical support from WHH will arrange training for the project staff and volunteers specifically focusing on those who are directly involved with the beneficiaries. Additionally, beneficiaries will be made aware of their rights and availability of feedback and complaint mechanisms. Also, the organization has a centrally focal person who will take care of PSEA cases and regularly follow-up the CFRM (Community Feedback response Mechanism) pathways and practices. All the received cases will be handled with confidentiality and as per the sector-recommended standards and the organization’s policies. The project will also have a referral pathway where cases will be referred to appropriate agencies/actors if required.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

In times of disaster, the normal systems of protection may break down; certain groups may be affected disproportionately and may have to adopt coping mechanisms that increase vulnerability. Age is a major factor influencing vulnerability to disasters. The effective participation of older people and children can ensure that life-saving actions are designed and implemented to include the specific needs of the different age groups. A number of initiatives from a rights-based approach to Disaster Risk Reduction have highlighted the role of children as agents of change, including disaster preparedness interventions led by and for children. Under this project, funds for flood mitigation, emergency response and capacity building are available.
