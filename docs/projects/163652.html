Project Summary

Waves of conflict have caused population displacement and severe damages to housing and infrastructures across Libya, essentially along the coastal areas, impacting the living conditions of the affected populations, Libyans and non-Libyans. The lack of adequate shelter options have led to housing shortages and an increase in rental prices, affecting the most vulnerable population.
While most IDP families are staying in private accommodation, an estimated 4,000 IDPs are currently hosted in collective shelters. For IDPs residing in informal settlements, essentially in urban areas, humanitarian assistance continues to be central to maintain a basic standard of living. Among the migrant and refugee populations, approximately 5,000 individuals remain in detention centres across Libya.
An estimated 447,025 Returnees (DTM N° 26, June - July 2019) continue to form a complex caseload for humanitarians, development actors, relevant government agencies and donors. Of the total returnees in need, an estimated 32,126 Returnees are considered in acute resilience and recovery needs ranging from lack of proper shelter support, to livelihoods and lack of social cohesion.
According to the Shelter and NFI Sector analysis, there is presently in Libya an estimated 668,875 people of concern regarding their living conditions – and among this population, 296,938 people are considered as in need of Shelter and/or NFI assistance by the Sector.
UNHCR aims to provide tailored assistance in-kind essentially but also in the form of cash grants wherever feasible and appropriate, to ensure most vulnerable people can meet their needs in a manner that allows choice and promotes dignity.
UNHCR, in 2020, is targeting 21,000 IDPs, 20,00 Refugees and asylum seekers and 15, 000 returnees.
In order to prevent further deterioration of the shelter situation of the most vulnerable populations of concern, the sector’s response will be delivered in accordance with the following objectives which resembles the agreed on with the SNFI sector too:
• Objective 1: Provide humanitarian life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support
• Objective 02: Contribute towards the resilience of communities and households by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure.
• Objective 03: Provision of capacity building and community strengthening.
UNHCR budget a total of 2,150,000 for its intervention in the area of SNFI in order to ensure that cost of vetted activities are aligned with objectives and outcomes for the 2020 year.

1 - Needs

The need for housing continues to stretch, especially into urban areas and around the major cities, exacerbating the difficulties in securing affordable housing and putting an increased number of families at risk of eviction. IDPs in informal settings are among the most vulnerable in need of shelter and basic infrastructure support.The 2019 year has been marked by new waves of conflict in Coastal areas and Southern areas as well as natural disaster such as in in Southwestern areas; increasing numbers of migrants, refugees and internally displaced people causing population displacements and damages to housing and infrastructure in Libya and severely impacting the living conditions of the affected populations, Libyans and non-Libyans.On top of this housing situation the current economic decline continues to impact the livelihoods opportunities of Libyans and non-Libyans. Displaced populations encounter greater difficulties to afford rental costs and can’t afford the costs of repairs while constraints to return their damaged dwelling of origin. Adequate shelter options were already severe before 2019 and now rental prices have risen and most vulnerable population have also headed to collective centres due to a lack of affordable solutions.The impact of the conflict continues to affect the living standards, and capacity for resilience and recovery of Libyans and Non-Libyans. Many people, especially the most vulnerable, are unable to independently meet their basic needs such as affording shelter. The most vulnerable people in Libya and those in acute need of humanitarian assistance are those directly affected by the last waves of conflict, particularly those who were displaced and whose lives and livelihoods were uprooted and destroyed.While most IDP families are staying in private accommodation, an estimated 4,000 IDPs are currently hosted in collective shelters. The majority of displaced households were identified in different neighborhoods of Tripoli, the Nafusa Mountains and various locations along the coastal line of Western Libya. While the main frontlines are situated in the greater Tripoli region, the consequences of the conflict are felt heavily in the Southern protracted areas.For IDPs residing in informal settlements, essentially in urban areas, humanitarian assistance continues to be central to maintain a basic standard of living.Among the migrant and refugee populations, approximately 5,000 individuals remain in detention centres across Libya.An estimated 447,025 Returnees (DTM N° 26, June - July 2019) continue to form a complex caseload for humanitarians, development actors, relevant government agencies and donors. Many of the returnees are grappling with critical problems related to resilience and recovery. 2 - Activities

UNHCR will continue both with emergency response as well as more durable and tailored support depending on the specific needs of the population targeted.The first key objective for the Shelter and NFI response will be to save and sustain lives through the provision of timely, targeted and appropriate shelter assistance and relief items. This will include the provision, distribution of core relief items or installation of emergency shelter kits or materials to displaced people in temporary sites. It will also include the rehabilitation, repair or upgrade of existing shelters that are below minimum standards in collective centres, unfinished buildings or any other type of emergency shelter space. This will ensure such shelters meet a minimum standard and provide a higher quality of living conditions and better protection for those who are expected to remain in these substandard shelters for an extended period of time.In-kind assistance will be prioritized in the forms of family NFI packages during emergency responses and also for long-term displaced populations in need of support. Seasonal items (such as warm clothing and thermal blankets in winter, summer clothing in summer), will also be distributed to sustain lives and reduce the impact of exposure to extreme conditions and will be one of the main focuses of the NFI response. Migrants and refugee populations will also receive assistance in-kind through tailored NFI packages according to their specific needs, especially in detention centres. Urban migrants, refugees and asylum seekers, require particular attention, especially in areas such as Tripoli, due to the expected closure of detention centres along the 2020 year but also in areas such as Murzuq and Sebha where shelter solution will be expected in urban areas. 4 - Protection risk analysis

The hostile working environment in Libya due to the continuous security risks might jeopardize the lives of those most in need and seek to benefit from SNFI assistance provided by UNHCR and its partners. The same applies for the humanitarian workers. Movement between sites can be unsafe for both and can expose them to safety and security risks. Additionally, distribution of in-kind and shelter assistance can sometimes be associated with violent acts to which humanitarian actors can be exposed to. Especially when the distributed items cannot cover all the population in need. This might create a sense of frustration and develop lack of trust among the two communities: population in need and the humanitarian actors.

5 - Mitigation measures

Regarding risks associated with safety and security, UNHCR, normally relies on its partners to implement projects in hard-to- reach areas where its humanitarian workers reside in those affected areas. This risk mitigation can lower the safety and security risks and grants safer working environment given the limited movement required to implement the project activities. The Field Coordination unit in UNHCR strives to work in a cross-sectoral approach to ensure that protection considerations are mainstreamed and incorporated at all stages of interventions. Therefore, it ensures that protection teams are included in all the implementation of its projects and they are part of any prioritization solution resorted to in the case of limited assistance provided. Additionally, the Field and Protection units in UNHCR engage continuously with the affected communities through its protection activities where they ensure that dialogue is taking place to grant transparency to its project implementation.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

UNHCR monitors and evaluates the impact of its intervention through a number of mechanisms namely:1- Monthly reports submitted by partners on project implementation against project performance indicators agreed upon with partners at the time of signing its agreements with UNHCR. 2- UNHCR through its Field staff members conducts field visits to monitor the implementation of its programmes whenever the security situation allows. 3- UNHCR ensures the receiving of Post-Distribution Monitoring Reports (PDM) from partners. These reports include feedback received from the population on the type and quality of assistance provided.
