Project Summary

The project will contribute to the sixth strategic objective of the health cluster and aligns with the second objective of the HRP.
Health Cluster SO 6: Establish MHPSS services, ensuring inclusion of marginalized people, provide psychological first aid to those exposed to traumatic events, integrate MH into all health facilities through provision of MHPSS training for healthcare worker essential psychotropic medicines and ensuring clinical case management is evidence based.
Save the Children (SCI) will aim to reach around 10,000 children and adolescents and about 2,000 of their caregivers with a specific focus on children at risk due to physical disabilities related to conflict and their mental health needs, which is one of the project aims to mainstream proper inclusion of CWDs in MHPSS interventions with a gender lens. Thus, the following priority groups will be reached and supported through the scope of interventions in West Bank and Gaza:
1. Children and adolescents facing moderate to severe mental health challenges.
2. Ex-detainees children.
3. Children and adolescents with mental health needs due to conflict related physical disabilities.
4. Children and adolescents with mental health needs due to GBV.
5. Adolescents at risk of suicide.
The project will build the capacity of 500 MHPSS and health service providers (200 Gaza, 300 WB) to improve the provision and access of MHPSS services to children and adolescents. This is through enhancing the capacity of the service providers on providing evidence based approaches and protocols such as trauma-focused therapy, cognitive behavior therapy (CBT), MhGAP and detection. Introducing those protocols will facilitate and increase the access of around 10,000 children with moderate to severe mental health needs to more responsive services. SCI will hold capacity and needs assessment for potential target organizations in collaboration with exiting partners who are acting as MHPSS and healthcare service providers such as (PMRS, Aisha, PCC, MAAN and Haidar Abd AL Shafi "Formerly PRCS". SC will follow a direct implementation approach for this project.

1 - Needs

oPt has an estimated population of 5.10 million as of 2020, including 2.59 million males (51%) and 2.51 million females (49%) (PCBS report on population (2020). The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) of 2021 shows that 62% of the families in the Gaza strip are food insecure compared to 9.2% in the WB. Around 2.45 million of Palestinians are in need for humanitarian health assistance and protection, of which 64% are in the Gaza strip with an estimate of 1.57 million, and 36% in the WB, including around 312K in Area C, 86K in east Jerusalem, and 37K in H2 zone (HNO 2021 Key Findings OCHA). Poor mental and psychosocial health constitutes one of the largest, least acknowledged, humanitarian problems in the oPt. Chronic recurrent exposure to trauma and violence, including conflict-related and gender-based violence, has increased the vulnerability of Palestinians to a wide range of emotional, cognitive, behavioral and psychosomatic reactions. While extended family has traditionally been an important coping mechanism, increasing poverty has strained this and eroded family security, while there is still a lack of professional mental health services. The COVID-19 pandemic has further eroded physical and mental health through loss of livelihoods, increased domestic violence, and economic vulnerability and uncertainty. Fear, depression, and anxiety/worry are common reactions in most affected communities. For some these reactions are prolonged, severe and disabling, and can trigger mental disorders and increase the severity of those already with pre-existing disorders. Mental health service providers report spikes in calls to hotlines and for phone counselling from people stressed and anxious and threatening self-harm. A disturbing number of suicides has highlighted a growing public health issue in the occupied Palestine territory. A 2019 study by MDM-CH on the risk factors of attempted suicide patients and prevention strategies in Palestine, it was found that 52% out of the total attempted suicide patients were youth amidst 16-25 years old. However, these figures probably do not convey the full extent of the problem in the oPt, where suicide is shrouded in social stigma, sinfulness (Haram), weak early detection and early interventions for vulnerable groups specifically adolescents, and shortages of mental health services. Against the conditions of increasing need, the mental health system is in urgent need of reform. The system is under-funded, under-developed, frequently inaccessible to those requiring care, and lacks proper resources, including human resources. In this context, cross-sectoral humanitarian interventions are urgently needed to plug structural gaps and support urgently needed community mental health services, including psychosocial rehabilitation for vulnerable groups such as GBV survivors, traumatized children, people with substance use problems, those with severe mental illnesses and physical disabilities and adolescents with risky behaviors. Furthermore, frontline health workers are facing tremendous stress during their work in isolation and quarantine centers. Shortages of specialized personnel and drugs add to the challenges. There are 0.41 psychiatrists per 100,000 populations currently active in the oPt, well below the global median of 1.3 per 100 000, and shortages in medication further limit treatment for severe neuropsychiatric cases. In addition, while COVID-19 has led to a significant increase in GBV across the oPt as mentioned, there is still a significant capacity gap in the clinical management of rape and insufficient integration of GBV detection and referral in PHC, including in mobile clinics. Health services remain the most appropriate and used entry point for GBV survivors, especially during lockdowns. The health cluster estimate 148,061 people in need of MHPSS in WB and 196,000 in Gaza. Priorities:1. Building the capacity of MHPSS and health service providers. 2. Public education and raising the awareness of people on mental wellbeing and mental disorders. 3. MHPSS referral system among relevant service providers is reinforced. Accordingly, SCI aims to address the critical gap in mental health services in the West Bank (Area A, B, C and East Jerusalem) and the Gaza Strip. It will address increasing access to services through building capacities of service providers, advocating and developing policies’ and utilizing community based awareness methodologies. All activities will be implemented in coordination and collaboration with MoH/ Mental Health Department and relevant organizations in Gaza.

2 - Activities

The following are the main activities which come in line with MoH reported needs1. Build and improve the capacity of 500 service providers. 1.1 MHPSS service providers are to be trained in MHPSS assessment and support interventions that are evidence based, able to meet good practice standards and relevant to the needs of children and their care givers, through organized trainings per specific approach like CBT and PM Plus. 1.2 MHPSS service providers are to be trained in crisis intervention for example eg. PFA and risk assessment including self-harm behaviors1.3 MHPSS service providers and interns will be routinely supervised in provision of MHPSS support to beneficiaries, using individual and group technical supervision and helping the helpers approach during crisis. 2. Provisions of supportive working environment for service providers and staff in MHPSS programs through self-care trainings, helping the helper’s supervision and improved work conditions. 2.1 Managers, supervisors and service staff will be trained in developing and implementing staff MHPSS care policies, procedures and support at targeted NGOs. 2.2 Policies and procedures are to be updated/ developed, and implemented at targeted NGOs. 2.3 Service providers will be empowered with self-care trainings and cared for with helping the helping the helper’s supervision during emergencies. 3. Provision of direct MHPSS awareness activities at the community level, while ensuring safety and protection of children, adolescents including children with disability and their care givers.3.1 Reinforce the efficient utilization of the Referral system.3.2 Children and adolescents, their care givers and communities will be provided with psycho-education/ MHPSS awareness and life skills relevant to their needs and backgrounds.3.3 Children and adolescents, and their care givers survivors of crisis events will be provided with psychological first aid (PFA) according) according to their needs and in a timely consideration.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to theservices provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will beconducted at all project stages with girls, boys, women, men, elders and people withdisabilities. This will enable communities to raise their concerns and together come upwith solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create newrisks. SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living withphysical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services outreach of bothmale and female people with disabilities tends to be limited. The project will prioritizefor example PwDs with means to enhance their access to project services. The projectgender sensitivity will be integrated and considered as a minimum standard. Theproject will be open to boys, girls, women and men according to the vulnerabilitycriteria, with the option to create women only working groups to facilitate theirparticipation. SCI, together with project partners, will check during the project visits theproblems that women are facing and that the project activities are designed throughgender and child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activitiesand/or abuse by the family members. Both Men and women will be included insessions with different interests and fields related mainly to enhancing their protectionand building positive resilience and coping strategies. The Gender Advisor will ensuregender mainstreaming in the interventions. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise theimplementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to the projectservices. Inclusion and equity are dealt with by SCI at two levels; the first throughstreamlining in existing activities and indicators as part of SCI policy to reach the mostmarginalized, and the other through promoting specific interventions to serve thechildren with disabilities in communities
