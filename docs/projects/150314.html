Needs

Nearly 63% of farming land is in area C of the West Bank restricting Palestinians from accessing their land, social services and economic opportunities, hindering farmers ability to sustain their livelihood and hence increases their vulnerability. Continued Israeli restrictions to land, water, and increased settler attacks on farmers, their land and other livelihood assets puts them at risk of loss of livelihoods and hence, forced displacement. The main aim of this project is to support the livelihood of vulnerable farming families. The impact of settler violence on communities, families and individuals include physical injuries, insecurity; psychosocial distress; iimpedes access to education and services. It is estimated that more than 100K HHs rely on agriculture as a source of income. The destruction & damage to productive assets by settlers, have a cumulative & detrimental impact on the livelihoods of farming families and carries significant economic loss for HHs. This economic loss forces the increasingly vulnerable populations to resort to negative coping strategies of selling personal assets, consuming less and poorer quality food, fogoing education and health services, etc. to maintain their family’s livelihood. Palestine remains a patriarchal society where the burden of child care & HH duties lay primarily on women and it is the women who have to make do with what is available, often sacrificing her own well-being. During the project formulation, an extensive needs assessment was conducted by the project partners in coordination with the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA). The assessment focused on farmers affected and prone to violence, demolitions and confiscations by Israeli military and settlers. Vulnerable and high risk farming areas were selected for the needs assessment according to the farmers reported damages to the MoA and was used as a major tool for identifying the priority locations. The needs of women and men farmers in the targeted communities were analyzed and women were interviewed separately as part of the needs assessment. When identifying the needs of agriculture livelihood, the decision maker of the HH-male and female farmers represent the family members-as they are work related initiatives for which under 18 would be consider child work. This project that is targeting poor & marginalized HHs will improve food security and supply, therefore, increasing self reliance, and strengthening the ability of communities and farming households to cope with protracted crisis. This project will ensure better & equal access of women, girls, boys, disabled, old people & men to the enhanced or created access for food security for the targeted families. Furthermore, women involvement in the project activities will be ensured through the formulation of the committees and through the implementation on the family levels. Equal access to the project of women, girls, boys elders and men for enhanced food security. For these activities, the main interlocutors at any level are and should be their parents/adults, as this is most likely not about providing general services, but agreeing about work-related initiatives, from which under 18 shouldn’t be involved (child work).
