Project Summary

FAO/WFP Joint Rapid Food Security Assessment, The overall objective of the joint
FAO/WFP mission is to conduct an independent assessment of the 2020 production
shortfall and the food security situation in the country. Provision of accurate, timely and
credible information through this exercise is critical to inform appropriate interventions
by the government, the international community, and others to mitigate the impact of the
reported drops in harvest output

1 - Needs

Continuous fighting and instability across the country, along with the COVID-19 restrictions to curb the spread of the virus – such as closing air, land and sea borders, imposing curfews and social distancing measures have led to the disruption in the access of basic services, weakening livelihoods and leading to increased protection risks. With the conflict also leading to continued displacement and disruption of available infrastructure have had a significant negative impact on all components of food security. An estimated 604,307 Libyans (332,453 undisplaced Libyans, 70,264 IDPs, 71,287 returnees, 123,090 Migrants and 7,213 Refugees) were found to be food insecure in the multisectoral needs assessment. COVID-19 and insecurity have affected the availability of food, as well as it cost. Due to high reliance on imports for food (90 percent for cereals) and yet a reduced capacity to import due to fluctuation of exchange rates and liquidity crisis, food prices were forced to increase due to a shortage in supply. The minimum expenditure basket is currently reported to be higher by 14.3 percent compared to pre-covid levels in March 2020. This led to an increase in the cost of living, and compounded by increased unemployment and under employment especially for the migrants and refugees that are employed in the informal sector. For the Libyans, delayed salaries for government employees who constitute about 85 percent of the population affected their ability to access food and other basic services. Due to the current insecurity leading to increased displacement and restriction measures that have greatly impacted the livelihoods of the population in Libya, there is a high frequency in the adoption of negative coping strategies in order to adapt to the shortage of food. Including for those engaged in the agricultural sector, especially in the southern and western regions. Nearly twice as many households engaged in food production have abandoned these activities due to the deteriorating situation COVID-19. Farmers report that "power cuts, insecurity as well as expensive inputs, including seeds, water, fuel, tools and machinery, continue to constrain their ability to produce". Along with movement restrictions, these challenges have likely led to a decrease in farmer income, and may cause additional price spikes for locally produced products. JMMI October 2020. This has also disrupted livelihoods and led to food shortages for many households, resulting in families adopting negative coping mechanisms. Although agriculture contributed less than 3 percent to GDP in 2011 (last information available), over one-fifth of the population is engaged in agricultural activities, often producing crops only for household consumption. Those households engaged in food production and the agriculture sector increasingly abandoned agricultural activities due to the deteriorating situation. Agricultural productivity in the last 12 months has been affected by insecurity, scarcity of water and fuel, as well as higher-priced agricultural inputs and constraints on labour due to COVID-19. Among households that were engaged in the sector, around 45,000 households have abandoned agricultural activity in the last 12 months, compared to 15,000 households that were estimated to have abandoned agricultural activities in 2019. This also impacts on food production in Libya, particularly in the southern region, which remains extremely important for the country’s agricultural production and, due to its geographical position, hosts many migrants and displaced people, many of whom are employed in this sector. There is a need to conduct an assessment on the food security situation in Libya to have reliable data to inform needs-based planning.

2 - Activities

The assessment aims to provide an update of the current food security situation ofdisplaced population in Libya, its scale, and severity. The information generated throughthe assessment will feed into future programming and inform the Humanitarian NeedsOverview. The main activities are : - Data collection ( surveys and investigation on thefood security situation in Libya - Data completion and Analysis - Recommendation onthe food security situation in Libya. The assessment will be conducted face-to-face and/or through mobile survey, and all the data analysis will be gender-disaggregated.
