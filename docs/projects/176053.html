Project Summary

The Emergency nutrition program is designed with integration to address the immediate needs and empower the affected communities to recover and build resilience against disasters. However, it’s noted that all states and Counties in South Sudan are affected by the impact of protracted conflict and regular flooding affecting 8.3 million people nationwide. 7.3 million people are severely food insecure and are in agent need of food assistance, 5.2 million people in need of health services and 1.9 needs treatment for malnutrition in 2021 (HNO 2021). GAM rate is predicted to be 15%, above the emergency threshold by WHO definition. COVID 19 imposed implementation challenges in nutrition program hence limited comprehensive data collection for evidence based response planning as movements and MUAC taking are restricted. Kapoeta East, Tonj North and Wau are IPC 4 and 3 priority locations targeted for this intervention. Kapoeta East has bad nutrition indicators with GAM of 23.30%, SAM burden 3298, MAM 8980, PLW 9888 and projected 165814 people in need of health services. The County has an immense service gaps for all social services as is expected to deteriorate by the onset of lean and rainy season. Tonj North is severely affected by the impact flood and intercommunal conflict, currently in IPC 4, GAM 15.30%, SAM case load is 2324, MAM 6039 and PLW 9452. WASH needs is 166809 while health need is 81614, affecting nutrition HNO 2021. Wau is in IPC 3, with SAM burden of 4938, MAM 20360, PLW 8703 while health needs is 62467 and WASH need is 197809, affecting nutrition indicators HNO 2021.
IRW intends to scale up nutrition response in high priority locations including Kapoeta East (Lopua, Nakabongole and Kokoro), Tonj North (Athieng Poul, Kuanyiel, Rorkou, Pachaweng and Achiirchok) and Wau (Gaitan, Bazia, Akuyo, Biringi and Bagari). The project will contribute to reduction of malnutrition prevalence and associated mortality among children under 5 years and pregnant and lactating women through provision of quality integrated and dignified lifesaving nutrition services to the vulnerable people.
Malnutrition prevention will be approached through behavior change communication using MIYCCN program, nutrition events and campaigns such as world breastfeeding week, cooking demonstration, food shows and nutrition counselling on optimal feeding practices. IRW through other partners will support provision of specialized nutrition food at BSFP for children under 5 and PLW. Vitamin A supplement and deworming tablets will be received by children under 5 to prevent micro nutrition deficiencies and helminthes which are in line with the sector and HRP objectives. This project will increase equitable access and utilization of lifesaving nutrition specific through early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for boys and girls 6-59 from months and PLW respectively. Girls and boys 6-59 months will treated and rehabilitated for SAM and MAM as well as pregnant and lactating women through OTP (Out-patient therapeutic program) and TSFP (Targeted supplementary feeding program). Project staffs will provide monitoring and supervision support to measure performance and direction of implementation. IRW will work with the cluster to support KAP survey for MIYCN and SMART survey to measure nutrition program performance and identifying new needs. Malaria testing and treatment for children under receiving nutrition assistance will be ensured. Preventive package including mosquito nets and WASH NFIs will distributed to children enrolled on program for malaria and diarrheal disease prevention. Integration activities such as kitchen garden, child friendly space and referral GBV cases will be supported to enhance comprehensive solution to the vertical underlying causes of Malnutrition. The project result will be reduced malnutrition prevalence in worst affected areas of Kapoeta East, Tonj North and Wau in IPC 4 and 3 which are targeted priority locations.

1 - Needs

Food security and nutrition situation in South Sudan continue to deteriorate mainly driven by severe food insecurity, insecurity attributed to conflict, economic crisis, impact of climate change (floods), disease outbreak, weak and fragile health system pillars, effects of COVID-19, poor water and sanitation infrastructures, poor health awareness and health seeking behavior, sub-optimal feeding practices and wide spread GBV and protection incidences leaving 8.3 million people in dire humanitarian need (HNO 2021). 7.3 million people are severely food insecure and GAM rate is predicted to be 15%, surpassing the emergency threshold by WHO definition yet, expected to deteriorate at the onset of lean season when harvest stock are depleted. Under 5 mortality remain at 90.7/1000 live birth moreover, an estimate of 1.9 million people will need treatment for acute malnutrition in 2021(HNNO 2021). All states and Counties are affected, off 78 counties, 72 are in severe need while 5 are in extreme need and 1 in catastrophic level (HNO 2021). IRW targets high priority locations including Tonj North and Kapoeta East in IPC 4 while Wau in IPC 3. The severity of nutritional needs in Wau tends to be high despite the IPC level; attributed to the presence of IDPs and displacement to remote locations where social services have never existed, poor road access, high food prices, limited sources of livelihoods, inadequate water and sanitation infrastructures, un equal access and distribution of health services are the affected nutrition determinants despite reduced insecurity and humanitarian access challenges. GAM is 10%, nutrition burden for SAM is 6597, MAM 21405, PLW MAM 12681 (HNO 2012) and yet will deteriorate with onset of lean and rainy season when people deplete their harvest stocks, drink unsafe water from the surface. IRW nutrition centers in Kapoeta East and Wau registers high malaria and watery diarrhea, cases with a proportionate increase in admission trends for malnourished children. Kapoeta East is the largest County in Eastern Equatoria but severely underserved and a hot spot for disease outbreak such as malaria, measles and cholera. Regular incidences of climate change (drought), cattle rustling, inter-communal violence and wide spread GBV incidences perpetrated mainly on women and girls, poor hygiene, sanitation and water sources and chronic food insecurity are the main driving factors of need. In the HRP analysis of 2021, Kapoeta East is in IPC 4, GAM is 23.30% and has the highest health need in 2021 in Equatoria region and will likely impact on nutrition coupled with the general poor living conditions of the population. SAM burden is 3904, MAM 11,333 PLW MAM 9888 and BSFP 14,782 PLWs and children under 5. This will further deteriorate during onset of lean and rainy season as per the outlined determinants above. Tonj North County is in IPC 4, GAM is 15.30 % and has an estimated SAM burden of 7011, MAM 23585, PLW MAM 11015 and an estimated health need of 81614 by 2021. (HNO 2021). Moreover 16480 are affected by flood from 7 of the 9 Payams in the County. Food crops are equally destroyed by floods. Humanitarian situation in Tonj North is driven by inter-communal violence, Fighting resulting from rival over natural resources such as water sources, and grazing land for cattle, climate change (flood), poor access to health services. IRW in the October 2020 assessment noted inadequate WASH infrastructure and chronic food insecurity eroded the coping mechanisms of the affected population. Poor health indicators contribute to the nutrition situation where as 14 out of 27 health facilities are minimally functional with very basic health care packages affecting access and utilization of health care services by women and children. IRW has presence in Kapoeta East, Tonj North and Wau which are in dire humanitarian situation.

2 - Project activities

This project intends to support the most vulnerable Boys, Girls, women and people with debilitating illnesses to have access to and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific and dignified lifesaving nutrition services in the priority locations. In line with sector objectives and strategic objectives of the HRP 2021, IRW will carry out the following activities: (i) hire and train technical nutrition staffs on the national nutrition guidelines to deliver quality services and enhance a sustained local capacity. (ii) Establish nutrition community structures will support behavior change communication activities through MIYCN events such as cooking demonstrations, food shows and world breastfeeding week campaigns and nutrition counselling at house hold, community and facility levels. (iii) Malnutrition will be prevented through distribution of specialized foods with BSFP program hence, IRW will work with the established community nutrition structures and link up with UNICEF, WFP and other nutrition implementing partners to ensure the services are delivered. (iv) The project will provide Vitamin A supplement to 13105 children 6-59 months and deworming tabs to 7335 children 12-59 months for prevention of micro-nutrient deficiencies and helminthes. (v) Early detection and treatment of SAM and MAM for children 6-59 months and PLW will be ensured. 1055 Boys and Girls will be screened, treated and rehabilitated for SAM at out-patient therapeutic program. 1814 Boys and girls 6-59 months and 1302 PLWs will be treated at Targeted supplementary feeding program. In line with nutrition guideline for COVID 19 context, care takers/mothers and fathers will be trained on family MUAC and referral of children to nutrition centers, CNVs and mother support groups will conduct household visits to address family challenges with MUAC taking and referrals and adherence to treatment protocols to enhance treatment outcome. (vi) Regular program monitoring and joint supportive supervision will be ensured to measure program performance at facility level. (vii)Complain and feedback mechanism will be strengthened through use of the existing health facility management committees. Inclusive representation and engagement shall be ensured for people of all categories in the process of receiving and addressing complaints as they are affected differently and have varied priority needs. (viii) With the support of the cluster and other stakeholders, IRW will conduct KAP survey for MIYCN and SMART survey to measure program impact and identify new evolving needs to enhance response planning. IRW will participate in cluster activities such as FSNMS surveys, joint response mission, and cluster coordination at national, state, County and community levels. (ix) 1055 Boys and Girls with SAM will be tested and treated for malaria at all nutrition sites. (x) Similarly, mosquito nets will be distributed to 2869 children 6-59 months with acute malnutrition and1055 Boys and Girls 6-59 month with SAM will receive WASH NFIs. Nutrition program will be integrated with other sectors including WASH, Health, FLS, GBV and Education to improve nutrition sensitive response; water infrastructures and hand washing facilities will be provided at all nutrition sites to increase access to safe water and hygiene promotion services. Growth monitoring and immunization sessions will be integrated with nutrition services. (xi) Kitchen gardens and 8 nutrition sites will equipped with child play materials to support child stimulation and improve cognitive recovery from the impact of malnutrition. (xii) 8 safety audits shall be conducted to assess the operational environment and conditions of nutrition sites. (xiii) support 58 GBV cases reported or identified at nutrition centers with referrals for further treatment and support. IRW will ensure equitable and inclusive access to quality and dignified integrated nutrition specific and sensitive services to children 6-59 months and PLW.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All IRW staffs are trained on PSEA and have signed to the policy. Additionally, all field locations have PSEA focal persons to monitor and observe staff behaviors and advise accordingly. The complaint and feedback response committees are trained on PSEA and the beneficiaries will be given awareness to enhance knowledge, coordination and establishment of reporting using the complaint and feedback channels.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The complaint and feedback committees are representative of all categories of people and ethnic groups from the affected population. Feedback will be received directly from project staffs or committee members using local language that is easily understood by the affected people. Physical and language barriers to access information will be addressed using various community structured platforms including complaint and feedback response committees, Health facility management committees, community owned resource persons such as community nutrition volunteers, community health workers, maternal and child health workers and project staffs to collect and address or forward feedbacks.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

IRW has formed complaint and feedback response committees at all the locations of intervention to link projects with the community and the entire beneficiaries. The committees are trained on their roles to coordinate complaints and feedback between the community and the organization and are given leverage space to participate in all the project cycles. The organization will use this platform and directly interact with affected population to transparently provide information on the project right at the planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages. IRW has a well-coordinated complaint and feedback system established at all levels of offices and intervention locations to receive and address complaints using various tools including email addresses, phone calls, complaint box and direct interaction where other tools are not applicable while maintaining confidentiality/anonymity to sensitive information.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

IRW staffs will work with the committee platform to engage with the beneficiaries in designing modalities appropriate for delivering assistance to the affected population. People of all categories will participate in the design to make it more inclusive and appropriate to all age groups, gender and ability challenged people.
