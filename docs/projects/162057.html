Project Summary

Christian Aid (CAID) Shelter intervention target in Camps 14 and neighbouring host community covering 2,000 Rohingya HHs for Transitional Shelter Assistance (TSA-I), provision of NFI (HH level Solar light) for 6,582 Rohingya HHs; and Shelter re-construction, repair support for 100 Host community HHs, provision of NFI (HHs level Solar light) for 500 Host community HHs in Palongkhali Union.
Christian Aid will strengthen and reaffirm its community-based participatory approach in assessment, design, implementation, monitoring to Transitional Shelter Assistance (TSA I) programme underpinned by rights-based and inclusive programming principles, by mainstreaming protection and strong accountability as cross-cutting themes and prioritizing the needs of the most vulnerable and hard to reach. The inclusive humanitarian programming approach will prioritize its objective and activities to strengthen governance, access to information, resilience, protection, accountability and communication with communities. Community consultation meetings, assessment of individual shelter, KAP survey, Complaints & Feedback and Response mechanism will be the key factor ensuring influence the project by affected people.
TSA-I will enhance the durability of shelters and improved living condition by using treated bamboo on structures. Shelter will be upgraded based on revised Key Messages particularly raising bamboo structure using footings. Special attention needs to be given to the DRR features such bracing, tie down, internal partition wall, floor with cement finished and raise plinth height, locks for door, fire protection (especially cooking areas wall plaster), Site protection/Bansa-Berra, and HHs level drainage. TSA-I shelter/NFI will be sourced from IOM Pipeline, CAID is IOM partner for Shelter/NFI Pipeline. This will start with assessment of individual shelter ensuring a gender balance in assessment to identify the component needed to upgrade, followed by training of community skilled, unskilled persons, all individual beneficiaries; distribution of essential and voucher based SNFI. Upkeep and maintenance of shelter will be part of the training as it goes a long way in ensuring durability of shelter. Diversity determinants (i.e. include older and disable persons) need to be considered while providing training, as all members of the community can play essential role in shelter maintenance.
Additional training will be provided to each group specially to Women, boys and girls and focus on shelter strengthening, maintenance and improvement, fire safety, and site improvement.
Rohingya and Host Community Extremely vulnerable individuals (EVI) / PSNs benefiting from potter service and technical support in shelter construction/upgrade. Shelter access will be designed to the need of HHs with people with specific need.
CAID Shelter intervention will contribute in meeting all three Strategic Objective of Shelter Sector. Project also aim to contribute in meeting major indicator i.e. 2,000 Rohingya HHs with shelters meeting at least 50% of Minimum Performance Standards, 2000 Rohingya HHs benefitting from treated bamboo, 6582 Rohingya HHs with access to at least one functioning HHs-level solar light, and 100 Host Community HHs benefiting from shelter support and 500 HHs benefit from Solar light support.
CAID Transitional Shelter Assistance intervention will address the shelter improvement needs that benefit to 2389 Female above age 18 years, 2022 Male above age 18 years, 2574 Child females above age below 17 years and 2710 Child male age below 17 years. However, NFI will address the need that benefit to 9273 Female above age 18 years, 8086 Male above age 18 years, 9973 Child females above age below 17 years and 10469 Child male age below 17 years. Needs

Needs of Rohingya communities: Despite the positive impact of shelter interventions on living conditions, shelter condition is a concern because the main materials used, untreated bamboo and tarpaulin, are not long-lasting. In Camp 14 - 74% of Rohingya HHs report roofs leaking, 53% of HHs report leaking walls, 63% report rotten or damaged bamboo. Solutions are needed that provide enhanced structural strength, protection from the elements and resistance to insect attack. The Rohingya refugee’s main safety concerns are a) unstable structure, b) shelter deterioration, and c) no adequate internal lighting (NPM R15). Insufficient supply of all the required NFIs has also been observed.Slope failures during the 2019 monsoon rains highlighted a need for shelter-level site improvement works not covered by the SMSD Sector remit. Such work includes slope protection, raising plinths, HHs drainage and access route improvement (51% of HHs (MSNA 2019) note access difficulties). During 2019 monsoon camp 14 witness more than 80 HHs level slope protection issues and due this HHs internally displaced and 19 HHs relocated. Despite the widespread distribution of portable solar lights, 40% of HHs (MSNA 2019) say they have no functioning solar light. These data highlight a need for high quality, durable portable lighting. 52% of the total refugee population are women and girls, while 85% are women and children and 16% of HHs are female headed. Girls, who represent a larger proportion (57%) of the vulnerable group. (Ref: ISCG Gender Profile_2). Camp 14 having 118 HHs with separated children, 46 HHs with unaccompanied children, 184 HHs with a person with disability, 314 HHs with older person at risk, 137 HHs with older person at risk with children, 305 HHs with people with serious medical condition, 42 Single male parents with infants, 966 Single female parent and 1847 HHs with people with specific needs. (Ref: UNHCR Population data and key demographical indicators July 2018). The specific need of men, women, boys, girls and people having disability includes:• Shelter and NFI materials are often too heavy to carry for women, older persons and persons with disabilities, and they need the support of others to carry their loads. • Female head of HHs rely on the kindness of male relatives or neighbours to build/upgrade shelters for them, and they need support of skilled, unskilled manpower to construct/repair/upgrade their shelter.• Inadequate lighting and lack of locks on doors, aggravate sexual violence risks for women and girls. They need door lock, HHs level light.• Need for durable shelter that meets minimum standards of covered living space as well as being resistant to heavy winds and rains.• Need for training focus on shelter strengthening, maintenance and improvement, safe cooking space and practices, and site improvement.• A partition wall which allows for privacy of women and girls and/ or couple.• Disability friendly access Needs of host communities:In the five Unions that include camps, 12% of host community HHs (MSNA 2019) (recorded as 13% in the 2011 Census) live in Jhupri shelters (a rudimentary shack), compared with a national average of 3%. 64% of these HHs reported dirt floors. 56% said they had no money for improvements. 26% are not connected to the electricity grid. 40% who are connected report intermittent supply.Host community in and around camps are hardly supported with shelter intervention. In Palong Khali Union Host Community HHs (MSNA 2019) report having 64% of Dirt floor, 56% of Mud walls, 14% of Tarpaulin roof, 6% of Tarpaulin walls, 5% of Hay roof. Similarly, 50% of HHs reported that they are connected with electricity grid, 37% of HHs reported that they have access to electricity at some nights. Shelter and HHs level solar light system are needed that provide enhanced structural strength, protection from the elements and resistance to natural disaster.
