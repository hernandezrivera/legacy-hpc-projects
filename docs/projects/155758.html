Project Summary

According to UNHCR fact sheet, October 2018, so far 623 families returned to their areas of origin in Orakzai Agency. The remaining 12,198 IDP families are expected to return later in 2019.according to reports the overall security situation is conducive for returns with the whole area under the full control of the military. The community emphasized on the importance of education and informed that one of the biggest challenges faced by the community is the continuation of their children’s education in the return areas. It is also observed by the mission that filling female teachers’ positions remains a challenge which will be vital in education revival in the areas of return. Education sector analysis in Orakzai, most of the education facilities are damaged and the government is in the process of making an estimate for their reconstruction and rehabilitation.
In response to Education cluster Plan 2019, Sanjh Preet proposes a project to implement in 50 schools of areas of tehsil Lower, Central and Upper of Orakzai Agency. The Current information taken from the Agency Education Officer Orakzai Agency, Hangu, there are 115 schools which are fully or partially damaged needs rehabilitation. Sanjh Preet will implement the project in 50 schools (33 schools (16 fully damaged and 17 partially damaged schools of Lower Orakzai and17 schools (9 fully damaged and 8 partially damaged) schools of Centre Orakzai.
The project will help in bringing the vulnerable returnee children towards normalcy and reconnecting with education which most them missed since years. The target 50 schools will be finalized in consultation and agreement with education cluster coordinator and local education authorities. The project interventions are designed in aligned with Education Cluster Plan 2018 that include: on need basis the project will establish 25 temporary learning centers where schools have been fully damaged, carrying out minor repairs in 25 schools structures to make them functional, provide 50 assistant teachers in public schools to address the teaching needs, provide teaching and learning materials like, school bags for children, mats, School in a Box and necessary stationary for 5,000 children(50% girls) to make the schools child friendly, establish/activate the 50 TIJs(50% female) and build their capacity on their roles, capacity building of 50 teachers(50% women) of government schools and 50 assistant teachers(50% women) on psycho social support, child friendly school approaches, use of the provided material/kits; use sports as a means to promote peace, school renovation using low cost material. The project will also establish school level 50 children clubs (50% girls) containing 500 members (50% girls) to extend assistance to teachers in bringing more children in schools, organize school assembly and various events/co-curricular activities, supervise the health hygiene condition of the schools. The organization in collaboration with education department will also launch a campaign in spreading awareness among st the local community on importance of education, Provision of mine risk education and also to bring the out of school children in schools. For over age dropout children need base accelerated learning centers will be established in target localities (it may be in public schools, donated buildings and rented premises)
The project will benefit directly to 5000 children (50% girls) and 500 members of TIJs (50% women) and 100 teachers (50% women) in making the schools environment child friendly, reconnecting the communities with schools and bring them towards normalcy.

Needs

As per UNHCR fact Sheet, Oct. 2018, it is reported that between 2017 & 2018, some 60,121 families have returned to their areas of origin in five tribal districts of KP. This translates into a population of 162,326 children in age-bracket of 3-18. The erstwhile FATA EMIS report for 2016-17 shows that more than one million (1,038,933) children in the age group of 04-14 are out of school in the tribal areas. The overall drop-out rate in Government Primary Schools over a cycle of 6 years is 76% (74% Boys, 80% Girls). Per the FATA Vulnerability Assessment (August-September 2017) report around half of the respondents expressed dissatisfaction with the quality of education due to lack of basic facilities, lack of teachers, teaching materials and damage to school infrastructure. In terms of damaged infrastructure, the RRU data shows that 943 educational institutions were partially and fully damaged in five tribal districts. The Education Cluster in its meeting on 20 Nov. 2018, has agreed on a case-load of 100,000 children (50% girls) to be supported in 2019 for continuation of education. Instead of focusing on recent return areas, it was recommended to expand geographical scope to all seven tribal districts given the alarming figure of one million children being out of school. It was highlighted in the meeting that beside providing a safe and secured environment by rehabilitating damaged institutions, there is a need to initiate conditional cash transfer schemes to enroll and retain children, particularly girl students in schools. The experiences shared from field underlined the need for ensuring provision of water, and sanitation facilities in schools. It was noted that there is a need for initiating a fast track or second-chance condensed education programme to support and mainstream over-age children, particularly girls, complete their primary/secondary education. The reported landmines/UXOs in and around schools in some areas prompt the need for a specific mine-risk education programme to protect children and communities from any harm. As the Orakzai Agency data shared by the Agency Education Officer Orakzai, Hangu, there are 115 schools which are fully or partially damaged needs rehabilitation. The increased number of returnee children and presence of less functional schools with inadequate staff, scarce teaching and learning material is really a big challenge to accommodate sufficient number of children in schools. Low enrollment before the crises, less value of education, cultural norms for girls education, absence/damages of education institutes, nonexistence of teaching staff and learning material, less involvement of TIJs/communities in schools matters are the underlying factors that impede the access to education of vulnerable returnee children. To keep in view the education cluster plan 2019 following interventions would assist the children and communities in restoring with education.• Provision of safe and child-and adolescent-friendly temporary school facilities/rehabilitation of damaged institutions with latrines and water facilities that are gender and age sensitive;• Provision of recreation materials for psycho social • Cash stipend/incentive to encourage enrollment and retention of children at primary level, and for girls at secondary level.• Coordination with government for provision of textbooks.• Provision of school supplies (school-in-a-box), recreation kits, school bags and stationery.• Hiring of teachers on contract basis to reopen closed schools, if needed• Formation of school management committees for improved school-community linkages.• Accelerated Learning Programme for over-age dropped out children.• DRR programme for improved school safety and mine-risk education

Activities

• Provision of safe and child-and adolescent-friendly temporary school facilities.The project will provide access to 5,000(50% girls) children with education through establishment of 25 TLCs and make functional 25 partially damaged schools by doing minor repair including provision of WASH facilities. The establishment of TLCs and minor repair work will be done by engaging the TIJs, education department officials and local community to make the process transparent and to build the community ownership. Provision of school supplies (school-in-a-box), recreation kits, school bags and stationery.As the reports indicate 80% of the schools are partially or fully damaged along with all teaching aids hence project will provide all essential supplies in the schools that include:• School in a box• Text BooksHiring of teachers on contract basis to reopen closed schools.In order to rationalize students-teachers ratio and reopening of closed schools the organization in consultation with Education Department will hire 50 (50% women) teachers in the schools. Formation of school management committees for improved school-community linkages.The project will reactive the 50 TIJs (50% female) through involvement of education department and changes will be made where needed in membership. These TIJs will be trained through two days training on their roles and responsibilities like school management and development plans, record keeping, social mobilization and local resource mobilization, enrolment campaigns, how to monitor the repair work etc• Accelerated Learning Programme for over-age dropped out children.In order to Provide alternative learning pathways to the out of school children 10-18 years who have missed out the chance of getting education through accelerating learning programs following steps will be taken. 1. Identify the target children of age 10-18 years from the base line data2. Establish the alternative learning centers in target localities 3. Hire one teacher per center to educate the learners and build their capacities on accelerate learnings, class room management, multi grade teachings4. Provide educational, learning and recreational material in the centers5. The enrolled students would be encouraged to mainstream in the nearby formal schools• DRR programme for improved school safety and mine-risk education.Project will be carried out following activities in order to improve safety and mine-risk education:1. Inter and intra schools competitions organized at school level to increase children knowledge on mine risk education & school safety2. Developed and implemented school safety action plans (SSAPs) at school level by following NDMA standard guidelines and template3. As part of the mobilization strategy local festivals, national and international days would also be celebrated with key messages on education and mine risk education.• Provision of recreation materials for psychosocial support For provision of psychosocial support to raise children’s self-esteem, reinforce resilience and provide an opportunity for self-expression, the project will provide all essential supplies in the schools that include early child development materials (Sport and recreational kits, Stories books and Life skills bases education (LSBE)• Cash stipend/incentive to encourage enrolment and retention of childrenChildren whose are at risk to dropout will be identified with the support of teachers and need base Cash stipend or incentives will be arranged to encourage enrolment and retention of these children. Moreover, in order to create regularity competition and to increase daily attendance different incentive will be provided to students having 80% monthly attendance ratio. • Coordination with government for provision of textbooks.During enrollment Phase coordination meetings conducted with respective AEO,s and required books demand will be provided to concern department for provision of books.
