Project Summary

In 2019, UNRWA will continue to sustain the delivery of quality primary health services through 17 Health Clinics offering the full range of Agency primary medical services, five Health Points (HPs) operating in areas of displacement of Palestine refugees and two mobile health clinics which are able to deploy according to needs. UNRWA health facilities provide a range of primary medical services, including out-patient consultations, vaccinations, growth monitoring, provision of medicine, gynecology consultations, laboratory services, non-communicable disease care, maternal and child health care and dental services.
Furthermore, the Agency will continue to provide support to Palestine refugees seeking secondary and tertiary healthcare through a system of referrals to public hospitals. UNRWA subsidizes the costs of treatment of referred patients by 75-95 per cent, depending on the socio-economic status of the patient. UNRWA will continue to ensure that patients have adequate access to essential medical supplies and drugs. The Agency will also strive to maintain contingency stocks of medical supplies and drugs to address any emerging situation.
UNRWA will endeavor to resume healthcare services in newly accessible areas and to rehabilitate and maintain damaged facilities. The re-institution of the Agency health personnel in areas of spontaneous return will also be a priority. Those areas will include the South of Syria (Dera’a camp, Muzeirib, Jillin), southern Damascus area where accessible (Yarmouk/Yalda), and Ein El-Tal camp in Aleppo, as well as other gathering areas of Palestine refugees depending on access and based on needs. This component is largely responsible for the increase in the budget requested by UNRWA under the Health sector in HRP 2019. If conditions allow (access, safety and security, rehabilitation of basic infrastructure by authorities, sufficient funding ), the rehabilitation/reconstruction of health clinics in Dera’a camp, Yarmouk camp, Ein El-Tal among other locations will require additional financial resources this year in comparison to 2018.

Needs

Civilians in Syria continue to face the consequences of eight years of conflict, mass destruction and suffering. Over 5.6 million individuals from Syria are now registered as refugees with the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), and over six million are displaced within the country. Whilst violence has reduced and conditions have improved in some parts of the country over the past 12 months, hostilities continue in other areas, resulting in further loss of life, injury and displacement. Palestine refugees are amongst those worst affected by the conflict. Of the 560,000 refugees registered with UNRWA in Syria, over 120,000 have fled to Lebanon, Jordan and beyond. Inside Syria, 254,000 Palestine refugees have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict, while 13,500 are trapped in hard-to-reach areas as of November 2018. Yarmouk, Ein el Tal and Dera’a camps – previously home to around a third of the Palestine refugee population in Syria – have been almost completely destroyed. While the general cost of living, including of health services, has dramatically increased since 2011, the ability of public and private hospitals and clinics to respond to the needs of the population has decreased. As a result, the vast majority of Palestine refugees have become increasingly dependent on UNRWA for the delivery of healthcare services.Access to UNRWA health services has become challenging in many areas, where UNRWA health clinics were destroyed or became inaccessible (as in Yarmouk, Dera’a, and Ein El-Tal camps) as a result of the conflict. In order to respond to the healthcare needs of displaced Palestine refugees, UNRWA has established health points and deployed mobile clinics in areas with high concentration of displaced Palestine refugees, such as in Yalda and Dera’a. Although Palestine refugees in Syria benefit from many of the same rights as Syrian citizens, UNRWA became their sole health care provider as government health institutions became overwhelmed with increased demand and struggled with loss of qualified personnel and the destruction of many hospitals and other healthcare facilities. The ongoing conflict has presented a new challenge to both the Agency and the community. UNRWA is working to protect the health of this vulnerable population by reimbursing non-contracted hospital bills; cooperating with partners, including the World Health Organization, to provide vaccinations against infectious diseases; and maintaining access to the vital services, medicines and treatments that Palestine refugees require. In 2019, the Agency remains the main provider of healthcare services to Palestine refugees, offering a range of primary medical services, including out-patient consultations, vaccinations, growth monitoring, provision of medicine, gynecology consultations, laboratory services, non-communicable disease care, maternal and child health care and dental services, in addition to subsidizing secondary and tertiary treatment. In the past couple of years, UNRWA witnessed the voluntary return of Palestine refugees to newly accessible areas as in Sbeineh camp, and Khain Eshieh camp. UNRWA responded by rehabilitating clinics and resuming healthcare services to respond to the basic needs of Palestine refugees. In Yalda, where many Palestine refugees had been displaced as a consequence of the hostilities and destruction that took place in neighboring Yarmouk camp, UNRWA deployed a mobile health clinic and established a health point with a temporary lab and pharmacy. In 2019, UNRWA will continue to respond to the needs of vulnerable Palestine refugees wherever and whenever they arise, particularly in newly accessible areas and areas that witnessed major destruction (Yarmouk, Dera’a, and Ein El-Tal camps). If conditions allow (access, safety and security, rehabilitation of basic infrastructure by authorities, sufficient financial resources), UNRWA will strive to resume services in those areas.

Implementation of activities

UNRWA will provide lifesaving humanitarian health assistance to up to 445,000 Palestine refugees in Syria with an emphasis on those most at risk through the provision of primary health services, subsidized referrals to secondary and tertiary treatment, rehabilitation and maintenance of health facilities with particular attention to newly accessible areas and areas that were severely affected by conflict, and sustained access to medical supplies and drugs. For this aim, the Agency will undertake the following activities in 2019: • Delivery of primary healthcare services by maintaining operations and supporting staffing costs• Provision of secondary and tertiary healthcare through subsidized referrals; • Operating of mobile health clinic(s) in newly accessible areas and areas with restricted movement/access such as Yalda; • Conducting of Immunization campaigns; • Rehabilitation and maintenance health centers; • Procurement of essential drugs and medical supplies;• Reconstruction/rehabilitation of health clinics in newly accessible areas and areas that were severely affected by conflict (mostly notably Yarmouk, Dera’a and Ein El-Tal camps);

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Improved access with localized conflict in certain areas of the country: Overall, humanitarian access is expected to improve with fewer Palestine refugees in hard-to-reach areas (13,500 as of end October 2018). At the same time, hostilities may continue in certain areas of the country. Should safe access resume for civilians in areas like Yarmouk and Dera’a camps, the Agency will focus on resuming its basic healthcare services in these areas. Most Palestine Refugees in Syria will remain displaced: It is anticipated that the levels of displacement will remain high among Palestine refugees, as the housing stock in Yarmouk unofficial camp suffered significant destruction during the conflict, most recently in May 2018. The majority of the 160,000 former residents of Yarmouk are therefore expected to remain in displacement, often paying high rents, which further increases their vulnerability. A similar situation is expected in Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps which have sustained major destruction; these three camps used to provide housing for more than 30 per cent of Palestine refugees in Syria. Increase in spontaneous returns as more areas become accessible: As new areas become accessible to civilians, as observed for Sbeineh and Khan Eshieh camps in 2018, additional small scale spontaneous returns are anticipated, provided that safe access is granted for civilians and basic public services (water, electricity) are restored by the authorities. UNRWA will therefore seek to resume its basic healthcare services in defined locations where feasible, to ensure that Palestine refugees are served. While a small number of spontaneous returns of PRS (Palestine refugees from Syria) from outside Syria were observed in 2017 and 2018 (about 2,000) and are expected to continue, most returns are expected to involve Palestine refugees displaced inside Syria. UNRWA continue to raise sufficient funds to sustain its operations in Syria: UNRWA will continue to mobilize resources and to advocate for the needs of Palestine refugees among donors, partners, and other relevant stakeholders. UNRWA will need an estimated US$ 220 million to respond to the emergency needs (health, education, protection, humanitarian assistance, WASH) of 445,000 Palestine refugees in Syria in 2019. UNRWA will mitigate the risk of limited funding by prioritizing key projects and interventions, and focusing on the needs of the most vulnerable.
