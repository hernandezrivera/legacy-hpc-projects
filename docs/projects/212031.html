Project Summary

Across NW Syria, 42 percent of the population relies on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs, and at least 70 percent of the discharged sewage is untreated and charged to open areas. An estimated statistic: two-thirds of water systems, half of pumping stations, one-third of water towers, one-quarter of sewage systems, and one-sixth of boreholes have been damaged, mainly after the Feb 2022 earthquake. The situation in IDP camps is particularly critical, requiring the provision of a full package of emergency WASH services. This proposal aims to incorporate climate resilience into the WASH program. This will involve understanding and managing risks resulting in the protection of water resources, adaptation to increasing water scarcity and deteriorating water quality, and climate-resilient water and sanitation technologies.
IYD conducted a comprehensive need assessment to identify the gaps and needs; the priorities are summarized:
•Focusing on resilience programming, with limited life-saving interventions in IDP camps far away from the water systems and water sources.
•Emphasis on rehabilitation/repairs of WASH infrastructure, emphasizing camps with Safe and dignified Shelter and wide restoration of WASH service provision systems.
•The lack of electricity is a main bottleneck for the WASH sector, limiting the existing network from effectively distributing existing water quantities.
•There is a continuous need for rehabilitation of the water stations and water network infrastructure in communities to ensure safe water for drinking, including support for the operation and maintenance process.
•The water safety plan is still critical with cholera spreading.
•NFI kits and CVA assistants are still an urgent need in camps.
•The need for hygiene promotion.
This proposal is designed to fill out the gap in the WASH sector after the fund cut. Focusing on the areas that most need and suffer from a lack of water sources in Idleb and Aleppo, targeting around (1,828,796 Beneficiaries) through providing the following activities in communities and IDP sites:
•Provide lifesaving WASH activities and emergency response in IDP sites to meet the immediate needs of IDPs in parallel with the distribution of HKs
•Rehabilitate and expand existing water systems to increase access to IDP sites and communities
•Construct new water sources and distribution networks to reach underserved areas in IDP sites and communities
•Provide innovative water treatment technologies to address water scarcity and contamination
•Improve sanitation facilities in camps and informal settlements, including constructing latrines and showers
•Implement sewage network upgrades and provide greywater treatment solutions to reduce environmental pollution
•Rehabilitate dump sites and implement waste segregation and recycling programs to improve waste management practices
•Promote the use of environmentally friendly materials and technologies in WASH interventions
•Integrate solar and wind energy systems into WASH infrastructure to reduce operating costs and ensure sustainable operation
•Prioritize the needs of persons with disabilities (PWDs), women, children, and other vulnerable groups in all project activities
•Address gender-based violence (GBV) concerns through awareness raising, training, and the provision of safe spaces
•Distribute hygiene kits containing soap, hand sanitizer, and other essential hygiene items
•Support the operation process of water stations to provide access to clean drinking water
•Upgrade sewage networks to reduce environmental pollution and improve public health and implement wetlands and ABR systems
•Capacity building for the communities through Train local staff on the operation and maintenance of systems
•Conduct vulnerability assessments to identify the specific needs of PWDs, women, children, and other vulnerable groups
•Raise awareness about GBV
•Establish safe spaces for women and girls in WASH facilities

0 - Project Summary (extended)

This project is proposed to contribute to filling part of the gap in WASH sector as a result of the significant decrease in the fund allocated to the water sector and as a result of the steady increase in need, especially in the overcrowded sites of the displaced, which have recently been affected by waves of displacement due to the increasing escalation on the contact lines. This proposal also supports building the capacity of communities to manage the rehabilitation and operation of water systems effectively and sustainably. The proposal focused on meeting the needs of the most vulnerable groups of adults and Women and people with special needs. Involving the community in provide the solutions and the participation on decision making for the implementation.
