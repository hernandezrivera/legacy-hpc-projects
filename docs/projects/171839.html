Project Summary

The Project Objective is to reduce child protection risks faced by conflict-affected children & adolescents through the provision of well-coordinated, gender-sensitive appropriate & quality child protection services. The key focus will be to prevent and respond to separation of children from their families by strengthening community based child protection mechanism for prevention of separation and build capacity of protection service providers and stakeholders to facilitate appropriate referrals and strengthen coordination in service delivery to ensure quality comprehensive services to unaccompanied and separated children (UASC).
The project will support 3500 Unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) through comprehensive in family tracing and reunification in line with minimum standards and inter-agency (IA) information and management systems (IMS), initiate appropriate alternative care arrangements & train 500 foster families (All women) to provide interim care & temporary foster care support targeting 80% of UASC identified children. 500 foster families will be provided with food and Nutritional support to ease the economic burden on the families. Community sensitization /outreach services will be conducted targeting caregivers on prevention of family separation, child recruitment, risk education, targeting education and community members. In addition, the project will seek to increase the capacity of target communities to identify, report, and refer children with urgent child protection concerns as well as to provide psychosocial support services in the long term. Activities will focus on the establishing community-based child protection mechanisms training and mentoring them and raising awareness about existing referral pathways.
The implementation of the above-mentioned activities is expected to reduce the risks which are currently faced by the targeted children boys and girls in these areas. This will further enhance and improve the coordination and support of the existing services which are being provided by other local and international NGOs working in the area. With the above mentioned needs, the proposed project is designed to improve the living conditions of boys and girls living in conflict affected population by providing child protection response targeting 24,000 vulnerable children in Banaadir, Lower Shebelle, Lower Juba and Gedo Regions.
The proposed intervention has been designed in a way to enable children to fully access and enjoy their rights, safety and dignity, while at the same time avoiding harm. All project activities will be carried out in accordance with AGDM principles: equal participation of girls, boys will be integral part of the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the overall project. NoFYL will ensure that key cross-cutting issues are mainstreamed into the proposed intervention including age, gender, diversity, environment and human rights. Gender and disability inclusion will remain a key focus, guaranteeing consultation with children when undertaking protection assessments through staffs accessing them and identifying their needs. 
The safety and dignity of children will be prioritized by ensuring confidentiality, prioritizing the best interests of the child and respecting the wishes of parents/caregivers, where they do not endanger the child further. All measures to respect and adhere to the Do No Harm approach will be taken during the process of beneficiary selection, service provision and data collection and management. Specialized services and useful information about service providers will be provided to all beneficiaries that meet the vulnerability criteria, including both IDP and host communities.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1.1: Provisional of mental health and psycho social support/ basic emotional support. This includes provision of Psychological First Aid (PFA)/PSS/counseling and emergency referrals to external services for newly displaced boys and girls, through mobile teams of GBV/CP Outreach Workers. MHPSS will also be provided through mobile community based approach (CFS/YFS) providing psychosocial support, recreational activities and non-formal education activities to the affected children and their families. Activity 1.1.2: Case management of Unaccompanied Minors (UAM) through Identification, documentation, these will facilitate family tracing and ensure that children receive appropriate care and assistance until reunification or until alternative solutions are arranged. This will target of 3500 UAM. The Unaccompanied Minors (UAM) will be Identified and documented through mobile teams of Child Protection Workers and Managers, and identify emergency alternative care arrangements, and provide tracing and reunifying services through best interest determination. Through the case management NoFYL will establish referral mechanisms and ensure all children affected by any form of violence are referred to appropriate medical care and other services. Activity 1.1.3: Provide family tracing and reunification, alternative family care and arrangements including community-based support to identified UASC. UASC will be referred to NoFYL’s CP Case Workers through inter-agency emergency response teams, in which NoFYL will participate, alongside other agencies. 25% of the identified Unaccompanied Minors (UAM) are expected to be reunified with their families. Activity 1.1.4: Facilitation of caregivers support sessions for 500 parents on how to deal with children in distress and how to manage their own stress. The sessions will also be on toxic stress, which has been customized for children facing separation by a caseworker who is embedded into the mobile response team. These sessions will be organized through the community outreaches by engaging parents who are seeking services with their children and by working with mother-to-mothers’ groups. Activity 1.1.5: Food and nutritional support to the child being placed under a foster family, this goes in to reducing economic burden to the foster family for absorbing extra person to feed during the family tracing process. Targeting 1200 families, 100 families per month for 12 months’ project period. Activity 1.1.6: Capacity building of working group members and other NGO staff, service providers (e.g. social workers), community focal points and local authorities particularly those supporting emergency response teams on CPiE, child safeguarding, IDTR, Case management, SCA and PFA trainings. Activity 1.1.7: Develop, obtain, print and distribute through mobile teams of Outreach Workers, IEC materials on grave violations against children/CPiE, mine/IED risk, IDTR for UASC, referral pathways and available services in the local language.

Mitigation Measures

1- Preventing risks of beneficiaries not being informed. Develop and widely disseminate the referral networks and ensuring service mapping are regularly conducted and referral networks updated. Enhanced community outreach and awareness campaigns around access and availability of services. Continuously assess and closely monitor the evolvement of the security and accessibility situation in the target areas, and have the flexibility to quickly adapt the implementation methodology as needed in order to guarantee the achievement of the project objective and the most effective impact. In this regard, NoFYL will maintain regular information flow and constantly consult with the cluster leads and donor. On the staff skills continuously train, coach and follow up the frontline workers to ensure timely and quality services is provided to beneficiaries. 2- Preventing risks of exclusion based on disability, gender and age. The proposed intervention has been designed in a way to enable beneficiaries to fully access and enjoy their rights, safety and dignity, while at the same time avoiding harm. All project activities will be carried out in accordance with AGDM principles: equal participation of women, girls, boys and men will be integral part of the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the overall project. NoFYL will ensure that key cross-cutting issues are mainstreamed into the proposed intervention including age, gender, diversity, environment and human rights. Gender will remain a key focus, guaranteeing consultation with female beneficiaries when undertaking protection assessments through NoFYL female staff accessing them and identifying their needs. 3- Preventing risks of community not using available complaints and feedback mechanisms. Ensure all beneficiaries are informed of the available complaints and feedback channels. Ensure CFM mechanisms are easy to use and accessible. Ensure complaints and feedback provided are acted upon and feedback provided to the community.

Please provide further explanation

Sustainability of the project will be ensured through community ownership of the project. For all activities reaching the communities, IDP community leaders and host community leaders, including women and youth leaders will be consulted in order to adapt the activity to the context and ensured community participation, Where possible community members will be encourage to volunteer and participate in the project. Collaboration with both host community and IDPs will ensure the project adhere to do no harm principles. Using community participation leverage will ensure a longer term impact of the project. Establishment of community structures and its capacity building aims at ensuring the community focal persons have capacity to continue support referrals from the communities to other service providers beyond the project durations, established service mapping will also provide a long term network between the communities and service providers thus ensuring sustainability.
