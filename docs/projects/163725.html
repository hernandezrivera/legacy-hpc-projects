Project Summary

The primary goal of this project is to reduce avoidable morbidity, mortality and disability as a result of NCDs complications and mental health disorders through supporting existing health facilities. The project establishes an improved access of vulnerable people including migrants, displaced families, returnees and people with special needs to quality health service that will include treatment of NCDs. In this project, WHO will strengthen the capacities of the health facilities with the provision of basic and supplementary medical equipment for the treatment of NCDs. In addition to strengthening MoH capacity on managing NCDs during emergencies as well as pre and post emergencies.

1 - Needs

Noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) are the leading cause of death in Libya and account for 77% of the burden of disease and with 20% of premature death. Of the total deaths in Libya, 35% are due to cardiovascular diseases (CVDs). Hypertension and diabetes are the main causes for CVDs if remain untreated. Despite the importance of timely treatment regarding primary and secondary prevention of CVDs, a large number of the population remain untreated. However, while the impact of NCDs on the health of population, health system, and socioeconomic development is well known, their importance in humanitarian emergencies has not yet been fully recognized. During an emergency or at any given conflict, people with NCDs are more vulnerable to the health impact of emergencies. Emergencies appear to increase the risk of NCD related complications; events such as heart attacks and strokes may be up to 2 -3 times more common than in normal pre-emergency circumstances. Emergencies can lead to acute exacerbation or a life –threatening deterioration in the health of people with NCDs. Elderly people are particularly vulnerable. These complication can be the result of physical injuries, direct traumatic injuries can exacerbate NCDs, a worsening of chronic respiratory disease and a worsening of diabetic control. Forced displacement with loss of access to existing medication or assistive devices, loss of prescription, lack of access to health care services due to displacement leading to prolongation of disruption of treatment. Degradation of living conditions, loss of shelter, shortage of water and regular food supplies, and lack of income adding to physical and psychological strain. Interruption of care through the destruction of health infrastructure, disruption of medical supplies and inability to access health care provider who been killed, injured, displaced or are unable to return to work. This can also include interruption of power or safe water which may have life threatening consequence, especially for people with end-stage renal failure requiring dialysis. With given the current emergencies in Libya, leading to the increasing numbers of IDPs, difficulties to access health facilities either damaged or deserted, sever lack of essential medicines and absence of professional health staff providers. Many people lack sustained health access related to NCDs either to primary health care facility or even to secondary and tertiary centres. Professional health staff and medicines are the main factors to ensure minimum quality of essential services to NCD population.

2 - Activities

1- Strengthen MoH capacity in providing essential NCD services at primary health care (PHC); A- Distribute NCD kits with essential primary diagnostic material and equipment with preliminary focus on area with higher vulnerable groups such as IDPs and migrants B- Advocacy activities approaching senior officials to endorse the national procurement of NCD kits.C- Reactivate NCD units in post-conflict areas D- Printing and distribution of Libyan national NCD guidelines E- Reactivate referral mechanism in cooperation with municipality ambulance health services.F- Conduct screening campaigns for diabetes and hypertension in IDPs camps.2- Improving capacity of government PHC providers to prevent and manage NCDs at PHC level. A- Train senior level MoH staff at central and district level.B- Review the current guidelines and update if needed based on WHO PEN/HEART technical packages C- Train facility health workers on NCD guidelines.D-Train relevant health professionals on NCD kits contents and how to maximise benefits of kits content. E- Train a cohort of community health workers based on NCD training module of the regional office F-Train staff from ambulance health services, paramedics and allied health services. 3- Strengthen MoH capacity to prevent and manage NCDs at the level of PHC during pre and post phases of emergencies. A- Develop preparedness plan for the management of NCDs during emergency. B- Develop outreach mobile NCD medical teamsC- Conduct Supervisory visit to the reactivated health facilities with NCD units by NCD experts.D- Build capacity of PHC directorate and NCDC department on implementation, supervision and monitoring of NCDs. E- Review pilot integration sites and plan expansion of facilities providing NCD services.

4 - Protection risk analysis

• Challenges in the implementation of NCD guidelines due to the presence of foreign guidelines promoted by different universities and medical schools. • Emergencies can directly compromise the health of people with NCDs• No information is available to population about functioning and supported health services. • Very limited availability of essential medicine at the level of primary health care facilities. • Health care staff, patients, health facilities are subject of violence with no protection mechanisms in place.

5 - Mitigation measures

• Libyan national NCD guidelines will ensure most of health providers use same updated reference as a guidance for NCD management. • Continuous provision of treatment for life threatening or severely symptomatic conditions.• Ensure identification of the subgroup of NCD patients with special needs for which interruption of treatment could be fatal or critical. • Avoid sudden discontinuation of treatment and prioritize patient needs and management by the equitable distribution of NCD kits. • Rapid response mechanism should be more effectively continued via different modalities, including replenishment of health supplies, support to the referral hospitals, and deployment of mobile health teams.
