Project Summary

This project aims to support IDPs in the camps in the Northwest Syria and the longer term objective of this project is to contribute to making the 32 camps at (Janudiyeh, Dana, Atareb, Maaret Tamsrin, Badama) sub districts reasonably resistance to the winter conditions during protection of 3930 HHs (21586 Individuals) from the negative effect of the winter season, protect the 3930 thousand inhabitants of the camps from the negative effect of the winter season during (Ground insulation, thermal insulation and external insulation of tents)
Through this project a group of activities will be executed which include:
1- Preventing the leaking of rain water into tents by raising them 15 cm above the ground a bricks barrier around the tent in addition to the space will be filled by stones and sand, and
will be compacted manually with providing a smooth livable surface for the families. (2396 tents need to ground insulation)
2- The thermal insulation for the interior of the tent to protect camp inhabitant from the cold weather, increase the efficiency of heating, and reduce the consumption of fuel by preparing the insulation rolls in the site measuring the dimensions needed vertically and start cutting the rolls to fit the internal part of the tent. (3057 tents needs to thermal insulation)
3- Preventing the leakage of rain water into the tents through the walls and ceiling, by the installation of plastic sheets cover. (3466 tents need to external insulation)
4- Rehabilitation of some main roads and footpaths and graveling to ensure a safe and healthy living environment in the IDPs Camps.
ATAA will conduct FGDs with targeted community to inform them about goal, outcome, and, activities of the project. Brochures and banners will be used to share information with the community.
ATAA will set a community – based mechanism to make sure that community members can submit their complaints and feedback. feedback tracker will be prepared on monthly basis.
ATAA Accountability team will set the appropriate complaint channels as (Hotline, WhatsApp, Viber, Email, Observation, Meetings, and Field visits)

Needs

According to CCCM1 SECTOR INPUTS 2019 HNO, during sudden displacements, IDPs have often no choice but to seek shelter in planned camps, informal sites, transit/reception centres and collective centres. Of the estimated 6.2 million internally displaced persons in Syria5, an estimated 871,150, or 14 per cent of all IDPs, are living in IDP sites where they receive humanitarian assistance and services. Of these, almost half (420,103 IDPs) are in the northwest of the country, the majority of whom are located in Idleb Governorate. Basic multi-sectoral life-saving assistance is provided to IDPs living in these sites through humanitarian actors. However, the cluster continues to monitor significant gaps in the response across all sites in northwest Syria. An estimated 34 per cent of the sites’ population, for example, cannot receive minimum food assistance on a monthly basis. Approximately 25 and 26 per cent of the population in sites, do not received adequate shelter and NFI assistance, respectively. Challenges also persist in sanitation, waste removal and water (15% of the population).A large proportion of IDP sites in Syria are informal settlements, also known as “spontaneous” or “self-established” camps. These usually consist of groups of tents or other shelters that were set up by IDPs themselves or other actors. Crucially, these sites are not managed by anyone and often have no legal claim to the land on which they were established. In these sites, the delivery of dignified protection and multi-sectoral assistance cannot be guaranteed by humanitarian actors, a critical issue for vulnerable groups, and particularly for women- and child-headed households. Planned camps, on the other hand, are managed by accountable humanitarian organizations that are responsible for these services. The same applies to transit and reception centres, which are temporary structures established for very short-term displacements.ATAA MEAL team during 23th and 26th of January/2018 conducted a Rapid need assessment in both Idleb and Aleppo Governorates Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Ma'arrat An Nu'man and Harim sub-district in Idleb and Jebel Saman in Aleppo across 16 villages. The used tool development and coding was done by ATAA MEAL department. The assessment was piloted with ATAA field team. This provided appropriate terminology and phrasing to allow a natural delivery of questions using wording common to the populations being assessed, thereby minimizing discomfort of the assessment. The Assessment was administered by 12 assessors working in pairs (one female/one male). This also allowed one person to address the questions while the other acted as reporter feeding the given answers to the KOBO application on the smart phone. The pairs were arranged into 6 teams of two people. Both of training the teams and providing guidance on daily basis were conducted.Totally 50 camps had been assessed some of these camps were established since 2013, 18 out of them are registered in CCCM cluster the other are not.In total, 6549 HHs which is equal to 33301 individuals are living within the targeted camps distributed in terms of age and gender as followed: 16722 less than 18 years old (8061 males and 8661 females), 14519 between 18 and 59 years old (6934 males and 7585 females) and 2060 above 60 years old.Based on the conducted assessment, the following findings can be identified: there are 5801 tents in the 50 camps 4058 out them are in need for internal insulation, 4467 out of them are in need for external insulation and 3397 out of them need isolated floors. Most of these camps have slope ranged between 5% till 40%.12 camps out of 50 camps are managed by ATAA association. In 25 camps 1980 tents floors had been isolated.

Implementation of activities

1.3.1.2 Upgrading infrastructure in IDP sites to meet the minimum international standards. The mail activities for the project will be as following: 1- Preventing the leaking of rain water into tents by raising them 15 cm above the ground, a bricks barrier around the tent, blocks size for the project is 15*20*40 cms (width, hight, length) by Dig a tunnel (3 to 5 cm height, 15 cm width) on the boarder of the tent to be able to place the cement blocks on and cleaning the surface of the tunnel from any small rocks or items, in order to have all the blocks on the same level after that placing a layer of fine gravel (3 to 5 cm) on the tunnel and compact it and starting placing the cement blocks on the gravel with adding cement paste between them, then filling the gaps between each block and the other with cement paste. In addition to the space will be filled by gravel, the size of the coarse gravel used is 1.6 – 3.2 cm and the fine gravel is 0.4 – 0.8 cm, and will be compacted manually, before placing the gravel on the soil surface of the tent, ground will be cleaned from any rocks or unwanted items to be able to place the gravel and the cement blocks layer on it. Placing 12 cm of coarse gravel with a proper compaction, and then 5 cm of fine gravel with proper compaction, with providing a smooth livable surface for the families. (2396 tents need to ground insulation) 2- The thermal insulation for the interior of the tent to protect camp inhabitant from the cold weather, increase the efficiency of heating, and reduce the consumption of fuel by preparing the insulation rolls in the site measuring the dimensions needed vertically and start cutting the rolls to fit the internal part of the tent with making sure not to leave spaces between one roll and the other and install a thin aluminum wire between the tents columns and the tent fabric, with the insulation rolls in the middle to provide full benefit for the families.(3057 tents needs to thermal insulation) ground insulation4*4 m tents size will require 40 m2 thermal insulators, 4*5 m tents size will require 55m2 and 4*6 m tents size will require 70 m2 3- Preventing the leakage of rain water into the tents through the walls and ceiling, by the installation of plastic sheets cover, (3466 tents need to external insulation) 4- Rehabilitation of some main roads and footpaths and graveling to ensure a safe and healthy living environment in the IDPs Camps. In general, each camp will be divided into sections, residents of tents whom will execute the activity on their tents will be asked to reside for one day at other tents that we are not doing any work at the time. Keeping in mind to allocate separate women’s and men’s tents to accommodate the residents. Normally, the residents of any camps are usually are relatives, this will be a helpful point. This will be done to all sectors of the camps until all work of this activity is done. In case of a gap occurring during the implementation of this activity, the team will install a number of temporary tents to be used as a temporarily shelter for IDP’s until the work of this activity is done. The selection any camp and all the activities will be according to HLP guidelines, ensure that the site where tents will be installed are properly graveled, and the CCCM Cluster guidelines on safety and security measures and camp management are applied. ATAA will conduct FGDs with targeted community to inform them about goal, outcome, and, activities of the project. Brochures and banners will be used to share information with the community. ATAA will set a community – based mechanism to make sure that community members can submit their complaints and feedback. feedback tracker will be prepared on monthly basis.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: It is possible that land owners for these camps objecting any construction of cement infrastructure on their land that is rented by the IDP's beneficiaries. Mitigation 1: ATAA will execute this activity of isolation of tents by an environmentally friendlier approach to raising has been adopted, involving use of small bricks and gravel/soil compaction. This will avoid long-term damage to the soil on the property, particularly in agricultural areas. Coordination with camp's managers and some private land owners will be to do these activities. Risk 2: Lack of having a temporary and alternative shelter for the selected camps residence during waiting for the next day until the cement paste between the blocks dry. Mitigation 2: Normally, the residents of any camps are usually relatives, this will be a helpful point. This will be done to all sectors of the camps until all work of this activity is done. In case of a gap occurring during the implementation of this activity, the team will install a number of temporary tents to be used as a temporary shelter for IDP's until the work of this activity is done, keeping in mind to allocate separate women's and men's tents to accommodate the residents.ATAA team will consult with benefices and local community's leaders on camps, and from several focus group discussions with all community sections, a clear strategic plan will be to implement this activity without any problem.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Staff mobility and access restrictions to affected population, with active fighting, shifting of the conflict lines and potential closure of the border might prevent delivery of humanitarian assistance,Mitigation 1: ATAA will elaborate different plans when certain areas are inaccessible Contingency areas in other locations have been pre-identified with the CCCM cluster, Risk 2: IDPs remove insulation for possible resale in local markets in order to gain cash liquidity.Mitigation 2: Although the removal and sale of insulation material will potentially have a high impact, the risk is relatively low based on past experience.However, in order to mitigate the risk, the installation teams will directly fasten insulation within the tent using the toolkits (best practice that is now programmed), but also extol the benefits of the insulation in reducing fuel costs, fire risks and also uses during summer months to help IDPs maintain a longer term view of keeping the asset. Risk 3: Some sites are on land privately owned, and private owners may refuse physical works for raising the tents.Mitigation 3: ATAA will utilize HLP methodologies to investigate ownership of land, and discuss with owners and available site management. Sites in which the land owner refuses the activity of raising tents, will only focus on internal insulation and weatherproofing. - An environmentally friendlier approach to raising has been adopted, involving use of small bricks and gravel/soil compaction. This will avoid long-term damage to the soil on the property, particularly in agricultural areas. Risk 4: Rainfall is likely during the implementation process and thus impede the progress of project implementation.Mitigation 4: Isolation of tents will begin during the period from May until the end of the year.
