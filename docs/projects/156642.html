Project Summary

The projects aims to provide support to water systems to ensure regular services for affected people in Syria through support to water quality assurance and operation and maintenance of water supply systems, and to deliver humanitarian WASH supplies, services and improve hygienic behaviour and practices of most vulnerable people through provision of water, distribution of essential WASH NFIs & Hygiene promotion, and improve access to lifesaving/ emergency WASH facilities and services.

Needs

Based on WASH assessments there are an estimated 15.6 million people who require WASH assistance (people who are at elevated risk of consumption of unsafe water, including people vulnerable due to displacement), 6.2 million of whom are considered in acute need. Due to changes in control of many areas across Syria, the sector has observed discontinuation of WASH service provision to extremely vulnerable populations in areas including East Ghouta, Afrin, southern Syria, and northwest Syria. During hostilities and in the months following changes in control, access has remained restricted for partners operating from inside Syria. The delay in re-establishing assistance and services to these areas, limited funding and numerous (additional) conditionalities applied by donors has been a significant driver of needs. Humanitarian needs stay humanitarian needs and have to be alleviated even in case of change of control, therefore more flexibility is imperatively needed. Despite some investment in water treatment systems and provision of water disinfectants the main WASH need across Syria remains access to sufficient quantities of safe water. Up to 55 percent of surveyed households are relying on alternative and often unsafe water sources to meet or complement their water needs. Results of free chlorine (FRC) testing are even more concerning and indicates that countrywide only 40 percent of water received from networks have a sufficient level of FRC at the point of use (below both national drinking water and emergency standards of min. 0.2 mg/l). The worst situation is observed in Dara’a and Idleb where only around 13 percent of the population is served exclusively via piped water, with this figure only slightly higher in Aleppo (16 percent) and Deir-Ez-Zor (22 percent). In Dara’a, Quneitra, Idleb, and Deir-Ez-Zor water trucking is the only source of water for 35-45 percent of the population and accounts for as much as 63 percent of the overall household-level water supply in Idleb. Results of FRC testing for water supplied by water tankers indicates that water safety is much lower than water from networks, with only 13 percent of samples taken from across Syria indicating a sufficient level of chlorine at the point of use. Average water consumption during 2018 amounted to an estimated 71 liters per capita per day. However, communities purchasing water exclusively from commercial water trucks received on average only 57 liters per capita per day, with at least 10 percent of them receiving less than 30 liters per capita per day. Areas well served by free water from networks use far more than the average of 71 liters per day. Families who have no option but to purchase water from commercial water trucks spend on average 10 percent of their income on water, with 3 percent of the population spending more than 20 percent of their income to purchase water and in extreme cases as high as 40 percent (Areesha). The situation is even more dire for people living in informal settlements, where families can spend more than 50 percent of their incomes on water. Reduced incomes and purchasing power of families in Syria could lead to the adoption of harmful coping strategies, with potentially significant implications on health and hygiene behavior/practices.According to the NPM Collective Center assessment report (2017), 45% of collective centers have no access to tap water, lacking the necessary infrastructure. Less than 20% of collective centers have proper water storage facilities. (HNO 2019)

Implementation of activities

Beyaz Eller will start a rapid assessment in target sites, this assessment will help BE to inspect the following key issues:1. Number of IDPs in targeted sits in sex and age disaggregated figures.2. Prevalence of diarrhea cases and skin infections.3. Availability and affordability of WASH related NFIs and hygiene detergents.4. Availability and number of large water containers to compare storage capacity with the required water quantity.5. Main water sources in sites and identify the quantity and quality of existing water supply sources.6. Water treatment methods and average water consumption per household.7. Coping strategies when water is not sufficient.8. Garbage disposal mechanism and whether garbage is safely collected and disposed.• Coordinate with the local authorities to facilitate the implementation of the project activities, water containers, hygiene kits, and logistics related to drilling of wells and other logistics will be prepared, a detailed implementation/distribution plan will be set, and the project will be advertised through the first week of the project after the confirmation of it.• Beyaz Eller will provide water (25L/Person/Day) monthly to vulnerable beneficiaries in the target areas of operation.• Beyaz Eller will provide hygiene kits to vulnerable beneficiaries in the target areas of operation.• Beyaz Eller will coordinate this distribution/implementation with other humanitarian actors who are also drilling wells/distributing water in the same regions. Efforts will be made to ensure there are no duplications or gaps. Cooperation with local councils, community leaders and the WHASH cluster, will take place via field meetings, e-mails, Skype.• Beyaz Eller (BE) will distribute water from pre-agreed distribution points within communities. Distribution points will be chosen in coordination with the site managers or local authority and will be walking distance from the homes of beneficiaries, in case of vehicles cannot access houses. For beneficiaries who are not able to access the distribution points, BE will provide home delivery.• Before each round of water distribution a water testing will be conducted to verify the water is free of contamination and is potable.• Based on the lessons learned in previous interventions, BE has developed a more detailed distribution checklist that will be used when planning and holding distributions. Program staff will fill out the checklist prior to the distribution, and monitoring staff will check it has been complied with during the distribution. The Syria WASH Field Coordinator has the responsibility for the process, and to ensure the checklist is fulfilled, a process that will be backstopped by the Turkey team.• The checklist includes more detailed information to guide project staff on distribution times, crowd control, information management, special measures for vulnerable beneficiaries, the organization of the distribution site (clear signs, barriers, protection from the weather). The checklist includes ways to explain to beneficiaries how they can complain – both about the assistance itself and about the behaviour of staff or the distribution management, which are anonymous and free. BE takes seriously feedback from beneficiaries with regards to access for people with disabilities/ elderly/ pregnant, gender considerations in crowd control, access to drinking water and waiting times. It is expected that the introduction of a more detailed distribution checklist, with responsibilities to implement good practices outlined, will result in more dignified distributions.

Protection Risk Analysis

•There is a risk of Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. So, the establishment of an adequate management plan is required; Promote involvement communities in distribution planning and feedback on package for improvement.• Gender insensitive contents verities of kits with inconsistent items, therefore it is important to harmonies and standardize gender friendly hygiene kits among the sector members and indicate quantity and instruction for use.• Not enough water distribution points available so people have to wait for extended periods to get water, fights, and harassment could occur; inequality in distribution, in this case, ensure context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, during the design and implementation phases.• Available means of transporting water not supporting equality and accessibility of water distribution for vulnerable groups, to avoid that BE must ensure context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design and implementation phases; Distribute smaller water containers more convenient for women and elderly people; Consider specific support to people with disabilities to ensure they access a sufficient quantity of water; {protection risk analysis}.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

-Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards or improper level of chlorine dose; , Beyaz Eller will hiring experts to supervise the process and make sure that water is free of contamination and is potable, and ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities or local councils. -Understanding that Syria remains a war prone zone, Beyaz Eller is not able to mitigate risks around shelling, airstrikes, etc, and also potential exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/ rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure (water stations, towers, distribution networks), BE may Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared; Conduct safety training with stuff and contractors; Add explosive hazard awareness material to the programming. -In case there is a military operation and a resultant escalation of conflict, there may be large displacement of people needing WASH assistance, Beyaz Eller will ensure relevant local host authorities are aware of response and commit to ensuring basic safety/access, and Consult community (separately women and girls) regarding location of distribution points, delivery routes and its schedule; Avoid location of water points in narrow streets, congested areas; Establish clear routes and schedule for water trucking that use wider access routes, avoiding school areas during the time children travel to/from school; Conduct safety training with truck drivers/operators; -Water distribution not safe or accessible, especially for women, girls (exposure to GBV risk) and children elderly and people with disabilities; inequality in distribution, Ensure context/SPHERE standards and closely monitor these aspects during implementation; Consult separately girls, boys, women and men, including older people and those with disabilities in the assessment, design and implementation phases; Make sure that distance of water collection points remains at minimum distance as outlined in-country policy document to ensure that this does not overburden women and adolescent girls with extra workload in particular; -There are possibility of beneficiaries selling of some items from hygiene kits to secure cash, so Beyaz Eller will closely monitor utilization of supplies and keep a track of what is being sold out in local market. An analysis will be carried out that will advise future procurement and/or selection of beneficiaries.
