Project Summary

Emergencies typically exacerbate existing conditions that contribute to food and nutrition
insecurity. The nutritional status of other vulnerable groups, beyond young children, can
deteriorate rapidly in an emergency setting. Pregnant and lactating women (PLW) and
people living with HIV (PLHIV) are disproportionately affected by emergencies. The
response in Zimbabwe requires comprehensive support to restore food and nutrition
security. While scaling up general food assistance is essential, it is equally critical to
scale up WFP’s emergency nutrition response. The intervention will complement existing
UNICEF-supported acute malnutrition treatment services that support children under-five.
WFP's nutrition component will target malnourished PLW, pellagra and PLHIV/TB as well as those with chronic conditions such as cancer with treatment
support to promote recovery, reduce acute malnutrition rates and long-term
consequences of malnutrition include increased risk for mortality. Targeting PLW is in line
with the first 1,000 days, ensuring that the intergenerational cycle of malnutrition is
broken.

1 - Activities

Malnourished PLW, pellagra, chronic conditions and PLHIV/TB will be identified and treated through health facilities.Screening will be done using mid-upper circumference (MUAC) and weight for height and all individuals identified with acute malnutrition will be enrolled for treatment. The acute malnutrition treatmentintervention includes a specialised nutritious food (Super Cereal) and will becomplemented with nutrition education/counselling as part of a comprehensive packageof care integrated within the routine system for management of PLW (antenatal careservices) as well as treatment for PLHIV/TB and those with chronic conditions. The treatment duration is a minimum of 60days. WFP will also strengthen community outreach through its existing platforms atcommunity-level for example, the general food assistance programmes, to raiseawareness and promote health seeking behaviour among PLW and PLHIV/TB.
