Project Summary

This project will focus only on Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM) activities undertaken in planned or unplanned settlements across Somalia. It will be designed to mitigate the physical and protection consequences of drought, conflict and any other natural or man-made disasters on populations and communities directly affected. This overarching objective will be achieved by enhancing the overall protective environment for displacement affected population, increasing access to basic services linked to different sectors, reducing the risk thereof, and availing specialized support assistance to affected population to help them address other interconnected protection concerns.
Through this project, NRC will reach a total of 12,000 HHs (72,000 individuals - 50% females) with necessary services through; setting up of CCCM coordination structures in collaboration with appropriate government counterparts, participation in relevant coordination forums on behalf of CCCM, ensuring the relevant Protection and Shelter respondents are aware and take action with regards to HLP issues, conducting Detailed Site Assessments, establishing/updating master list of sites at appropriate geographic level, providing capacity building to existing governance structures/focal points and construction of communication/ information centers in coordination with local authorities. NRC will also conduct mapping exercises on existing community structures. Settlement planning training will be conducted to improve accessibility in site and community led site maintenance activities will be supported to ensure upkeep of sites (cash for work, site maintenance committees) through cash for work to improve emergency sites to minimize protection risks and ensure safety in sites (flood mitigation, fire prevention). NRC will also provide platforms for displacement affected communities in sites for access to vocational training and skill diversification opportunities. Settlement mapping exercises will be conducted to get the information of all targeted settlements for necessary services in place and gap analysis. The settlement mapping exercise data will be conducted identify facilities gaps in the settlements.
This project is designed to meet the needs of 80% IDPs and 20% host communities of the total beneficiaries targeted. Gender will be mainstreamed into the project through the specific consideration of the different needs of women, men, boys and girls during the design of activities. The project will ensure that all participants have equal access to resources and training. Monitoring and evaluation will be based on gender, age and diversity dis-segregated data.
CCCM activities will also improve the coordination and monitoring of service provision at site level and intra-site to ensure efficiency in service delivery and avoid duplication or gaps in services through capacity building for the existing community mechanisms to ensure access to information and services and improve accountability to affected populations.

Needs

There are almost 2,000 recorded IDP sites across Somalia, the majority of which are informal settlements on private land in urban areas. Evictions have increased significantly, putting further stress on displaced families who can be forced to move multiple times. Displaced people in informal sites are in greatest need of CCCM services in order to reduce barriers to assistance, ensure access to quality basic services, enhance the accountability of humanitarian actors to affected populations, and strengthen community participation and empowerment through consistent engagement. Following are Key CCCM needs in Somalia. 1) Coordination and information management at site level continues to be a challenge in urban areas with large numbers of IDPs. Inadequate site level information on humanitarian services, protection needs, and poor beneficiary selection curtail effective service delivery to the IDPs, making them more vulnerable, and increasing protection concerns and risks.2) The majority of IDPs settle in informal and unplanned settlements where living conditions are poor and forced eviction is a common threat. Sites are overcrowded and unplanned which increases risks of flood, fire and disease outbreak.3)IDPs in informal sites lack access to information about their rights and available services. Displaced communities, especially new arrivals, need access to information and representation in community governance structure in order to make an informed decision about how to receive services and potential solutions to displacement.4) IDPs have limited options for durable solutions to their displacement. The JMCNA findings indicate that the majority of IDPs prefer local integration, however, this has remained a challenge due to lack of land, repeated evictions and minimal livelihoods options. Returns to the area of origin are extremely limited, as most IDPs have lost livelihoods and are reliant on humanitarian services.

Communication/ information centers (in coordination with local authorities and protection actors)- Implement community based activities to promote community cohesion and recovery.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Through site/settlement planning training coupled with provision of settlement tools, the project will focus proper planning of the sites to ensure long lasting affects while building towards the achievement of durable solutions for displacement affected population. Similarly, NRC's CCCM projects will be integrated with NRC's other sectors (Shelter, WASH, LFS, Education and protection) and will adopt area based integrated programming to have maximum impact and have more sustainability, resilience and durability. NRC will also advocate through CCCM cluster, other coordination mechanisms and local authorities to mobilize different organizations to work in the same area to achieve the goal of integration, resilience and durable solutions.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The principle to be adopted for this project will address critical protection concerns with increasing displacement towards IDP sites and collective-centers, including heightened protection risks/threats that have emerged in the failure to end displacement through appropriate solutions (local integration, return, and settlement elsewhere). The targeting criteria will include areas that experience high levels of migration due to recurrent droughts as per PRNM displacement data. This will be used to determine district vulnerability aligned with the objective to target rural locations to increase resilience and prevent future displacement. This will be balanced out with criteria relating to good programming opportunities (potential for return, relatively continuous access)

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The Federal Government of Somalia has committed to women’s empowerment, eradicating violence against women, and changing sexist societal mind-sets, recognizing that without institutionalizing the equal rights of women, the country will never be able to stabilize and develop in a sustainable way. Somali women and girls continue to bear an unequal burden of the hardships occasioned by poverty, conflict and clan-based culture, which promotes strict male hierarchy and authority. This is further exacerbated by religious and cultural limitations on the role and status of women in Somali society. As a result, deeply rooted gender inequality prevails; Somali women are either excluded from formal decision-making and asset ownership or operate through a patriarchal filter. Gender equity means equal opportunity for women and men to realize their full human rights and potential to contribute to national, political, economic, social and cultural development, and to benefit from the results. The concept of equity acknowledges that women and men may sometimes require different treatment to achieve similar results, due to different life conditions or to compensate for historical and social disadvantages that prevent women and men from otherwise operating as equals. This project will ensure engagement of people with disabilities and the elderly at the co-management level will ensure that programs interventions do not generate negative side-effects. These populations will be involved in project management at community level to ensure that their resilience is built in ways adapted to their specific needs and capacities and that their access to basic services is prioritized. Women will be give special preference in site/settlement planning training, settlement tools distribution processes to enhance their skills, where needed, referral mechanism will be used at different forums and at cluster level so that principles of Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) are ensured.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

In a rural context, communities or groups are either forced into temporary displacement because of climatic shocks or conflict, or might join urban centers in search of better access to humanitarian aid (pull factor), access to services economic opportunities. During slow onset and long-tail crisis such as the current drought, the same communities can partially displace to neighboring towns, while also hosting displaced household coming from hard to reach areas. Since all the CCCM interventions will be channelized through cluster approach and involvement of relevant local authorities, thus the impact of armed conflict and violence will be mitigated through proper coordination, advocacy at national and international level, media and referral mechanisms.
