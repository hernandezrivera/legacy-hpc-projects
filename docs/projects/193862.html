Project Summary

Healing & Hope 4 is the 4th phase of the education program- to institutionalize the Mind-Body Medicine approach in the school system. The Center for Mind-Body Medicine (CMBM) proposes a 1-year project working with 10 schools. The project is a continuation of the previous project activities, and will provide trauma-relief and resilience-building support through staff training, Mind-Body Skills Groups (MBSG), Workshops, and classroom integration of Mind Body Skills.

1 - Needs

In February 2022, CMBM conducted a screening at 10 schools at Khan Younis to assess the level of need for MHPSS support, among students and parents. The total number of respondents was 4,487. 41.1% of respondents reported symptoms of PTSD. Save the Children International (SCI), did another assessment in July 2021 targeting 403 caregivers after the escalation in May 2021. Based on the survey for caregivers, 78.2% of the respondents stated they experienced signs of distress and observable changes in their mood or behavior. This includes sadness, struggling with relationships, agitation, social withdrawal, anxiety, fear, panic attacks, inability to relax and being worried. In the same study, around 79% of respondents’ children showed signs of distress and observable changes in behavior. Interestingly, 87% of the respondents whose children show signs of distress were themselves showing signs of distress and changes in mood or behavior. This reflects the strong correlation between caregivers’ distress and children’s psychosocial difficulties.Based on the results of the needs assessments and the results demonstrating that MBSGs can significantly reduce trauma (in studies conducted by CMBM and SCI), CMBM proposes an urgent psychosocial intervention for school staff, children and their parents. According to the baseline study which was done by CMBM in February 2022, children of different age groups experienced a range of emotions in reaction to their trauma. It was observed that young children suffer from more severe symptoms in comparison to older children. The researchers recommended a comprehensive program that can address the diverse experiences of school children ages 6-17.Referring to Terre des hommes Foundation, 2021 Rapid Needs Assessment in the Gaza Strip and West Bank including East Jerusalem, which indicated that children in armed conflict areas experience high rates of mental disorders, including post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), depression, anxiety, behavioral problems, and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, as well as functional impairment. Children in conflict areas are also at increased risk of suicide ideation, enuresis, nightmares, hypervigilance, grief, separation anxiety disorder, phobia, stuttering, stereotypic movements, refusal to attend school, learning disabilities, conduct disorders. Children present symptoms of psychological distress including upset, a lack of focus, disrupted sleep patterns, mood and emotional swings, a lack of self-control and aggression. The study recommended providing mental health and psychosocial support to children, families, teachers, frontline workers. UNICEF points to the need for child protection / safeguarding interventions to include: MHPSS services to children and caregivers. It's also emphasized that Mental health needs of children must be addressed, both in schools and via community based psychosocial health programs.In this project, Children, school staff and parents will experience trauma centered interventions, learn problem solving techniques and positive coping mechanisms. The CMBM model has been overwhelmingly successful in our previous experiences with schools and our partnership with the Ministry of Education- Gaza. The CMBM model integrates well into the school system and can be implemented by teachers, school staff or parents.According to the baseline study conducted as part of phase III of this intervention, the majority of school children reported experiencing and signs of trauma immediately following the shelling. Younger children were more likely to report high levels of anxiety due to the separation from caregivers. Based on CMBM’s extensive field experience it can be reasonably assumed that the impact of the shelling on different children may be categorized as either low, moderate or severe. All the beneficiary children will receive appropriate interventions that are responsive to their needs and will support them in healing trauma and building resilience. The children with severe and very severe PTSD will join the groups facilitated by teachers and school counselors. Children with mild PTSD will join the classroom interventions, led by other teachers who participated in a three day training workshop. To ensure the integration of mind-body skills with children and parents, 500 parents will participate in Mind Body Skills Groups facilitated by teachers under the supervision of CMBM supervisors. To ensure sustainability of the program, 50 school employees will be trained to facilitate CMBM’s Mind Body Skills Groups. Those trainees will then be equipped to lead additional groups with children and adults beyond the 12-month scope of this project.

2 - Activities

1. 3 days MB workshop for (300) teachers: from 10 schools, (30) teachers from each school.Workshops will be implemented in (5) three rounds, each round will serve (60) teachers.6 CMBM trainers will conduct the training.2. PTP: Professional Training Program: Initial Training in Mind-Body Medicine. A 5-days training for (50) teachers, from the 10 schools, 5 teachers from each school. 3 CMBM trainers will conduct the training. This Initial training offers an introduction to mind-body skills within the group context and focuses on teaching participants to use these skills in their own self-care. It includes extensive scientific material on the biology and physiology of stress and trauma and on the specific techniques used, as well as experiential work in an intensive, supportive small group. 3. ATP: Advanced Training Program, a 5-days training, for the same (50) teachers. 3 CMBM trainers will conduct the training. The Advanced Training Program will teach participants to use mind body skills with others the approach and tools learned in the Initial Training. Participants will have the opportunity to lead a small group with supervision and mentoring from CMBM supervisors. During this training, they also attend interactive panels on issues related to teaching mind-body skills, and receive individual and group support.4. 8-Sessions Mind Body Skills Groups for 800 students, CMBM supervisors will support trainee teachers who attended PTP & ATP to implement 45-90 minutes weekly sessions for (800) of the most traumatized students at the 10 schools (CMBM will conduct a screening study to determine prevalence of PTSD among students, and to identify the most traumatized students to bring them to MBGs. Every group serves (8 students). The small group model includes includes: Drawings, Autogenic training & biofeedback, Body scan, Mindful eating, Breathing & movement, Genogram 1, Genogram 2, Dialogue with a symptom, Wise guide and Drawings 2 & closing rituals. 5. 8-Sessions Mind Body Groups for 500 parents and staff of 10 schools’ students. CMBM supervisors will support trainee teachers who attended PTP & ATP to implement 2 hour weekly sessions for (500) parents and school staff. Every group serves (10 parents). The small group model includes: Drawings, Autogenic training & biofeedback, Body scan, Mindful eating, Breathing & movement, Genogram 1, Genogram 2, Dialogue with a symptom, Wise guide and Drawings 2 & closing rituals. It will be conducted in two batches.6. Integration of Mind-Body skills in the classroom for (6000) students, (600) students in each school.CMBM supervisors will support schools' teachers and conduct Mind-Body based workshops and support (300) teachers in integration of Mind-Body skills in the classroom for the first 5 minutes, or as needed for (6000) students. Teachers will learn to use skills such as Soft-Belly Meditation, Shaking exercise & Imagery. Teachers will conduct integration activities sessions in 2 batches (semesters). 7. Supervision: Three CMBM supervisors will supervise (50) teachers who run Mind-Body Skills Groups, and support (180) teachers who run MB classroom-based sessions.

3 - Indicators

CMBM is responsible for planning and overseeing the project timeline, activities, data collection, data analysis, and reporting. Monitoring activities may include but are not limited to on-site visits to project activities, in-depth interviews with key project partners and participants, and beneficiaries.CMBM will evaluate the impact of the program on teachers, students and parents participating in Mind-Body Skills Groups and training. CMBM will use pre- and post-surveys with a convenience sample of each group. All evaluation will be done using paper-based questionnaires. CMBM proposes to use the following evaluation instruments, and projects seeing statistically significant improvements for both populations. Quantitative evaluation will be conducted to measure the effectiveness of Mind-Body activities comparing pre and post readings, using:for students (6-18 years): CPSS-V scale & Pediatric Symptom Checklist (PSC)For parents (Adults): - PCL-5 -- Dass-21- Brief resilience scaleFor teachers: After the training will apply the following tools on teachers PCL-5 - Dass-21 - Brief resilience scaleThe Impact scale (Improvement in personal, social & professional dimensions) will be implemented during the project implementation to measure the scale dimensions to ensure sustainability as well. CMBM expects statistically measurable improvement in all domains except the environment. CMBM has used this successfully in our programs in Gaza.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

CMBM will minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the project intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. Beneficiaries' safety & dignity is our top priority. If hostilities escalate during the project, CMBM will mitigate safety and security risks by waiting until fighting stops to continue service, or offer services online or via telephone, or moving intervention to an emergency focus. CMBM will mitigate health risks from COVID-19 by following WHO & MoH guidance. CMBM has a clear plan to conduct project activities virtually via phone or internet if necessary
