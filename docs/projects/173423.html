Project Summary

Continuing to address complex, acute, and multilayered needs, HI proposes to foster a safe and protective environment with the provision of specialized and community-based services to Rohingya refugees and host community through a twin-track approach:
- Accessible community static points established and operated, where community members, including persons with disabilities and adolescents, access group PSS/Protection, rehabilitation, SRHR, and COVID-19 awareness sessions.
- Mobile units composed of qualified Social Workers, nurses and midwives, physiotherapists, psychologists and protection officers deployed to identify and provide tailored individual assistance, and community based sessions, to the most vulnerable individuals and their caregivers.
Both tracks are supported by community based volunteers. In addition, to strengthen community based protection of persons with specific needs, the project will mobilize peer to peer support groups of women and men with disabilities. The project will provide technical support to ensure they are sustainable, and empowered to claim their rights, which will strengthen community structures, and foster their resilience.
This approach integrates safe and timely referrals to both internal and external services. It has been developed, reviewed and consulted with beneficiaries including persons with disabilities. It has proven effective to reduce barriers and empower communities to achieve their rights with safety and dignity.
To strengthen disaster risk reduction, HI mobilizes seasonal Protection Emergency Response Unit in several camps, participating in an effective early action system to any sudden-onset disaster.
COVID-19 protection measures will continue to be adopted and its awareness will be prioritized especially for people with limited access to information.
HI will continue its active participation in Protection Working Group, Task Force, MHPSS Working Group, and Age Disability Working Group. With HI’s standardized and accessible Complaints Feedback and Response Mechanism, it ensures promotion of its PSEA policy.

1 - Identified Needs

Over three years after the influx from Myanmar, around one million Rohingya refugees live in extremely precarious conditions in 34 camps spread between Teknaf and Ukhiya Upazilas in Cox’s Bazar district. Shortly before the COVID-19 onset, women and girls were reporting increased security concerns, at 71% and 59% for Rohingya and host community respondents respectively (UNHCR, CARE and ActionAid, 2020). Reports included high prevalence of SGBV, intimate partner violence, child early forced marriage (CEFM) – a risk for adolescent girls, survival sex, trafficking, and other protection concerns. High mobility restrictions for women and girls, normalization of SGBV, coupled with stigma and shame associated with it, contribute to low reporting and help seeking behaviors. Psychosocial distress resulting from the persecutions, violence, and forced displacement is common; exacerbated by unsafe living conditions and secondary stressors.For persons with disabilities, these risks are even higher. Some of the challenges faced by them include lack of specialized services to address their specific needs; physical barriers; few accessible services; and social and attitudinal barriers including discrimination, bullying and abuse. In 2019, HI assessed that 78% of persons with disabilities face major difficulties in accessing or have no access to community spaces, and up to 100% of respondents reported facing barriers to accessing healthcare. During focus group discussions, only 10% of women with disabilities reported ever having accessed services outside of home.While levels of insecurity and rates of SGBV were high prior to the pandemic, the onset of COVID-19 crisis exacerbated the risks for many. Loss of limited livelihoods opportunities, containment measures, withdrawal of non-essential services (including protection) from the camps, all contributed to increased levels of SGBV. As many as 44% of Rohingya women respondents report increased tensions in their household as a result of COVID-19 restrictions (RGA, ISCG GH, October 2020).As a consequence, many women, men, adolescents, and older people experience mental health issues, stress, and anxiety, and are unable to access adequate services (RGA, ISCG GH, May 2020). Protection was identified as a priority need by 38% and 34% women and girls from Rohingya and host communities respectively (RGA, IASG GH, October 2020), and people with disabilities were identified as having protection related vulnerabilities (J-MSNA August 2020). CEFM is reported to have increased, as are other protection concerns. Additionally, social cohesion – already precarious – has deteriorated as tensions rose between the camp and host communities, owing to fears and misconceptions surrounding COVID-19 risks. Containment measures have hampered access to services; safety, mobility restrictions, limited transportation options, and limited access to information presented additional barriers for persons with disabilities and women and girls. In July 2020, as many as 84% of persons with disabilities surveyed did not know what COVID-19 was (RGA, IASG GH, October 2020). In addition, these individuals face attitudinal barriers, such as discrimination during service provision. In the host community, households with a person with a disability are found to resort to negative coping strategies at higher rates (J-MSNA August 2020). Due to these risks and barriers, the mental health impacts, distress, and anxiety levels are even higher among persons with disabilities, and access to services lower, than they are for the rest of the population.In addition to these multi layered protection needs, both refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar are at risk of sudden onset extreme weather events, such as floods, cyclones, and landslides. This year’s floods, combined with COVID-19 pandemic, exacerbated risks for many individuals already living in precarious conditions inside and outside camps, especially for those with specific needs.
