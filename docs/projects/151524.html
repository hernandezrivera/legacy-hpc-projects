Needs

While the conflict in Ukraine enters its fifth year and is considered as protracted by many observers the violations of the ceasefire happen regularly resulting in newly damaged or destroyed private dwellings on a monthly basis. This, in combination with the fact that neither the Ukrainian Government, Donors, International Organisations nor INGOs have been able to meet the complete shelter needs generated from the start of the armed conflict and, consequently, has resulted in unmet shelter needs in eastern Ukraine. At the end of 2017 the combined data from the local authorities of Popasnyanskyi, Novoaydarskyi and Stanichno-Luhanskyi rayons indicated that there remained approximately 3,700 damaged shelters in Luhansk Oblast (GCA). Over the first eight months of 2018, NRC conducted a house by house, technical survey to cross-check this information and to compile a relevant and updated damage assessment of the remaining shelter gap in Luhansk Oblast. The main gaps identified in the assessment were that, once all planned programming for 2018 is completed, there will be a total of 1,289 households still in need and are eligible for Shelter interventions in 2019 in Luhansk oblast. Of these, 1,289 damaged shelters deemed eligible for further intervention, 865 have received no support as of yet. The 424 which have received past assistance were included in the total number of 1,289 due to the fact that their assistance was deemed as insufficient for various reasons, and further assistance would help to overcome barriers to occupancy. In addition to the direct damage of shelters as an impact of the conflict, eastern Ukraine experiences long and harsh winters, below 0°C from end November to mid March, with an average low of 10°C and down to 20°C in the colder areas. It is recognized within the humanitarian community that winter represents a major concern for IDPs and conflict affected populations, especially in the most exposed communities along the contact line. Most houses along the contact line do not have full thermal insulation, and with increased costs for fuel and electricity, this leaves families at risk of exposure, or having to choose between food security or heating. The Shelter programme provides assistance to conflict affected, vulnerable groups of all ages and gender. In our area of operation, the ratio is approximately 60% Female and 60% Elderly. This is not by design, but rather the census of the population who are unable to move away from the line of contact.

Activities & outcomes

Within the frame of its response plan for 2019, and based on a 2018 damage assessment, NRC will aim to provide shelter assistance to the remaining gap of 1,289 households or 3,480 persons residing in conflict affected area of Luhansk oblast (GCA), up to 500 households in Donetsk Oblast, and provide life-saving heating and insulation solutions to an additional 200 households.In order to provide further data assessment in Donetsk Oblast, NRC will support the shelter cluster and relevant actors to conduct a Humanitarian Shelter Damage Assessment in Donetsk as was conducted in Luhansk during 2018.In order to respond to new damages in the Luhansk Oblast, NRC expects to respond to 50 new shelter cases during 2019 and 2020.NRC will implement a project focused on five different categories of responses, (i) Light & Medium repair works, (ii) Heavy Repair works, (iii) Reconstruction of Core Houses, (iv) Shelter winterization and (v) connection of essential utilities(i) Light & Medium Rehabilitation work to conflict affected households whose houses have been partially damaged; this will target communities along the contact line. (ii) Heavy Rehabilitation work to conflict affected households whose houses have been heavily damaged and require significant engineering. This component will target communities along the contact line. (iii) Reconstruction of a permanent core house to the absolute most vulnerable conflict affected households whose houses have been totally destroyed, and need a complete rebuild. This component will target communities along the contact line(iv) Providing thermal enhancement (insulation material) of houses for residual populations in order to ensure protection from the winter cold, reducing costs, and energy demand. (v) Where possible, and identified as a barrier to occupancy, assistance in connection to sustainable utilities, specifically targeting Heavy Repair and Core shelter assistance recipients from 2016-2019In an effort to contribute to families’ ability to live in safety and dignity, NRC will use a beneficiary-driven approach for all repair and construction activities with technical assistance provided throughout the entire process from assessment and design through construction. Furthermore, the team will work proactively to positively impact labour and price inflation. The beneficiary-driven approach is effectively the equivalent of the self-managed construction of homes, the common practice before the conflict. Community involvement in the programme through integrations with livelihoods opportunities will serve to inject capital into the micro-economy and increase overall community ownership of their own recovery. Due to its strong multi sectoral presence in Luhansk oblast, NRC will strive to undertake integrated programming, creating synergies between its shelter, legal aid (ICLA) and food security/livelihood programmes. Specifically, potential beneficiaries for shelter assistance who have lost their ownership or tenancy documentation, will receive support from NRC’s legal aid programme in order to apply for extracts from the official administration records.Where feasible, the above project activities will be aligned with relevant GoU strategies (e.g. IDP Integration Strategy, State Targeted Recovery/Peacebuilding Programme, Regional Development Strategies, etc.) and will create synergies/linkages with the activities of recovery/development actors (HDN) operating in eastern UkraineGender analysis demonstrates that needs for both gender groups are the same, but due to higher percentage of women, especially among the elderly, the assistance will be provided accordingly, and beneficiaries will be selected in close consultation with relevant stakeholders and municipal authorities and committees.

Indicators & targets

Light and medium repair in Luhansk Oblast: 1,160 affected households supported with shelter solutions; in Donetsk Oblast: 450 households. Heavy repair in Luhansk Oblast: 109 affected households supported with shelter solutions; in Donetsk Oblast: 50 households. Reconstruction of a core house in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts: up to 20 affected households supported with shelter solutions; Winterization (thermal insulation) in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblast: 100 affected households supported with winterization solutions; Connection to essential utilities in Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts: 100 affected households supported with utility connections. The geographic focus for NRC shelter interventions will be in the front line districts of Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts (GCA), within 20 km from the contact line. All components will be planned in close coordination with other operational actors in order to avoid overlap and to ensure gaps are addressed. Targeting will focus on a range of vulnerable household categories: elderly, disabled people, single woman headed household, pregnant and lactating women, large number families.
