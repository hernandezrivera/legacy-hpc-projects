Project Summary

To enable the targeted categories of beneficiaries to cover their basic needs in food and non-food items, including shelter and utilities cost thus contributing to their access to decent living.
PIN activities in eastern Ukraine are designed and implemented based on needs assessments, vulnerability assessments and gender analyses in order to provide comprehensive assistance and relief to the most vulnerable populations in the region. In this project, PIN proposes a Community-Based Approach to the provision of humanitarian assistance. The project is intended to support activities of eligible communities in eastern Ukraine affected by the complex humanitarian crisis. In particular, the project will aim at ensuring access of the conflict-affected population to health care, education, water and sanitation, food security and basic livelihoods, including overall stabilization, recovery, vulnerability reduction and resilience. For this purpose, PIN will target one community/settlement (or specific areas cluster of a few neighboring villages) and provide an integrated solution to their humanitarian needs. The community will be mobilized and together identify their overall community goals/needs that can be addressed within the priorities. As a result – PIN will design the project accordingly to assist the community priorities within the budget limitations.

1 - Needs

Areas near the contact line in Government Controlled Area are characterized by high vulnerability, the principal driver of which is the continuation of fighting concentrated in densely populated areas and remote villages. Access to basic public services, including healthcare, and lack of access to medical supplies are of particular concern. The current crisis has also eroded the residents’ purchasing power. Thus, their food insecurity has doubled since 2016, with 1.6 million people considered as food insecure. Access to food is mostly hampered by the increase of prices, rather than access to markets, and lack of financial means. Cold winter periods pose additional challenges to food security requiring higher caloric intake. According to the recently released statistical data, in the first quarter of 2019, the unemployment rate among the economically active population in Donetsk region (GCA) remains critical. In addition, during this period, population in GCA has experienced a reduction of salaries and income generating opportunities. These findings were confirmed through Focus Group Discussions where respondents emphasized the loss of permanent jobs or pensions/other social benefits as well as a sharp reduction in income or the problem of salary arrears. Such developments usually force vulnerable residents to resort to negative coping usually be deprived of out-of-school activities like art and sports. Besides that, such dire economic situation of vulnerable HHs has negative effect on the psycho-social condition of their residents, especially children, and remain one of the reasons of their decreased The single-headed households led by females are considered among the most vulnerable ones with respect to the lower job opportunities for women at the current job market in the area close to the LoC. The elderly people (both men and women) often have special health needs (chronic diseases etc.), which are not covered by the state health initiatives and require additional personal expenditures. The overall worsening of socio-economic conditions of the vulnerable population, especially women, girls, elderly and disabled persons, often become a reason for domestic and gender-based violence. Based on the strategic priorities, protection mainstreaming recommendations and PIN’s generated experience, cash-based interventions and cash-based programs in particular continue to be among the most appropriate and timely ways to assist people in meeting of their basic needs, thus stabilizing their food and NFI security and reducing their negative coping strategies to survive the crisis. Furthermore, the cash-based intervention is flexible enough to consider specific needs of each beneficiary. In addition, by applying protection mainstreaming standards through all humanitarian activities, PIN will strive to provide equal access to all, with special emphasis on women, children, elderly, people with alternative gender identity/orientation and disabled persons. Furthermore, women, elderly people, people of minority groups will be fully included in all stages of project design and implementation. Also, based on the assessment field visits and PIN’s generated experience, cash-based interventions not only assist people in meeting of their basic needs but also stimulate local economic growth through humanitarian assistance. With regards to vulnerability criteria and beneficiary selection, generally, PIN will follow recommendations of the Cash Working Group. PIN will continue to prioritize individuals in vulnerable categories which have limited access to income opportunities.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Objective 1 Activity 1: PIN will provide multi-purpose cash transfers to the targeted population of the front-line locations of Donetsk region characterized by very high vulnerability. These geographic areas were selected within 0-5 km from the contact line following the important criteria of adherence to “do no harm” principle and necessity to provide essential services in ‘safe’ locations, avoiding unintended pull factors or returns to unsafe locations. Furthermore, the selection process followed strategic priorities to ensure the intervention targets both critical areas and vulnerability groups of population. The population of selected locations has access to local markets and thus is able to purchase basic food and non-food items, therefore the proposed cash modality is both timely and justified. Under this activity, PIN will provide MPC to 4,000 beneficiaries transferred via a remittance system. The amount of cash assistance is calculated per each beneficiary, thus all beneficiaries living in a HH will be covered. The standard financial allocation per beneficiary is 970 UAH (33USD) per month during 3 months, following the Cash Working Group recommendations. Based on current experience of MPC implementation projects, PIN will select the beneficiaries based on previous assessment data and verification process, which includes community awareness sessions and information sharing by posters, leaflets, notices, etc., and provision of preliminary lists of beneficiaries by local communities. During the final selection stage, the household composition, status, income, health and ability status, shelter and food needs will be considered. A special attention will be paid to beneficiaries with disabilities to ensure their full access to the benefits of the MPC project. For this purpose, PIN will facilitate help to this category of beneficiaries whereas a group of local active community members will be involved to provide such beneficiaries with food and non-food items procured at their request and render other necessary assistance. Monitoring and evaluation: Of all the activities PIN undertakes, community monitoring and evaluation are the most essential, since they identify shortcomings and provide for transparency and accountability. Monitoring and evaluation also refer back to participatory assessments for determining what is or is not working. Community-based monitoring focuses on the implementation of activities to ensure that they are running smoothly and helping to achieve immediate objectives. The PIN’s Assessment, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit will execute monitoring and evaluation to ensure the applicability, accuracy and efficiency of the proposed activity over the course of the project and in the post intervention period, utilizing strong and harmonized processes in terms of outputs, methods, outcomes and impact assessments. PIN and other NGO active in the area will divide the intervention zone to provide meaningful assistance. PIN will build a database of selected beneficiaries to cross-reference them with other humanitarian actors to avoid any risk of overlap. PIN will conduct regular monitoring activities through both the management and the M&E functions. Field checks will be made, randomly interviewing beneficiary who have received assistance through the project. Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted via structured phone interviews to collect qualitative feedback on the relevance of aid and beneficiaries’ experience of receiving it. Furthermore, PIN M&E Unit will carry out PDM surveys reviewing beneficiary experiences on the whole aid process (from beneficiary identification to aid delivery). Findings of the monitoring reports will be disseminated to relevant clusters and other stakeholders.
