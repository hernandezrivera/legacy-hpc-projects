Project Summary

Zimbabwe has been affected by two consecutive droughts (2018/2019 and 2019/2020) receiving below normal rainfall. This has resulted in low surface water levels with the national dam average as at June 2020 stood at 49.4% against a national average of 70.5% for the month of June . Groundwater levels have receeded significantly with 29.08% of water points becoming seasonal . Metereological data has predicted floods for the 2020/21 rainfall season.
The project seeks to restore access to safe water of appropriate quantity and quality and improve hygiene practices to flood, drought and Covid 19 affected populations in 4 districts. The project will rehabilitate 110 boreholes, 3 piped water schemes and equip 10 boreholes with solar powered pumps. Community based management of water points will be promoted through capacity building of water point management committees and pump mechanics. Access to water benefits the entire community, however restoring access lessens the burden of fetching water on women and girls as well as reduce the risk of sexual exploitation and abuse.
Hygiene promotion will be conducted through EHTs and VHWs who will be capacitated to cascade life saving hygiene messages to the community. Key target groups are health clubs at community and school levels. The project will create age appropriate enabling environments for WASH in schools. In addition, hygiene kits will be distributed to vulnerable households such as female headed, the elderly, diasbled and households with malnourished children.

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Throughout the project AAP will be mainstreamed into the activities in a manner that ensures the affected people are at the centre of our work, and this will be achieved by ensuring the following:1. Communities participate in the project in a safe and appropriate manner ensuring equal opportunities for all including the vulnerable. 2. Communication and information with the affected populations will be shared in a two-way communication channel that ensures the targeted communities are aware of their rights in the project and the obligations of the ADRA while working in their community. Community dialogues and inception meetings from District level down to ward level and even village level as the project progresses will be tools utilised. 3. Feedback and complaint mechanisms will be established and utilised throughout the project from needs assessment to closure, to get feedback on experiences and perspectives of the project implementation. Mechanisms such as help desks, suggestion boxes and feedback sessions will be implemented at a frequency to be determined by communities. Feedback received will be recorded and adequately addressed in a manner that safeguards communities. 4. The Partner will also work with other existing WASH partners to ensure communities are not overburdened, and efforts are also not duplicated.

9 - Environmental considerations
9.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The project will trigger communities through the SaFPHHE model to realise the impacts of open defacation on the environment. Reduction and eventual non-existence of open defacation improves water quality for both surface and groundwater. The SaFPHHE model allows for environmental assessment by communities before and after implementation. The project will also promote environmental protection through tree planting and basic gully reclamation works by the community.
