Project Summary

The project aims to provide improved quality and protective learning environment for Syrian boys and girls in 10 schools in Idleb and Aleppo Governorate. These schools are located in the areas where there is high proportion of out of school children. As per the non-structured interviews with school management, 30% of primary school age children (5-17 years) are out of school. Non-availability of education supplies (school bags, stationary, uniform), child labor, increase trend of early marriages and inappropriate learning facilities are common factors contributing to high percentage of out of school children. A complete Back to School campaign will be run in the targeted communities. Community volunteers (teachers) will be recruited and will receive monthly stipend to facilitate this campaign. This campaign will sensitize the parents and caregivers about the importance of education and will bring back 2000 children into targeted formal schools. All the children in the targeted schools will be provided with student kit. The sensitization campaign will continue throughout the project period to mitigate the risks of school drop outs. The targeted schools will be provided with student kit and education supplies (text books) and furniture as per the need. 120 teachers and school management staff will be identiﬁed for monthly stipend. These teachers will also include psycho social trainers. The teachers and administrative staff will also be trained on child protection and psychosocial support. The teaching staff will conduct regular classes in the targeted schools and individual/group psychosocial sessions and recreational activities will be conducted. In additional minor school repair and rehabilitation will be conducted specially to improve WASH facilities in schools. Provision of prefabricated classrooms to ensure that education space is suitable for learning. The provision of conducive learning environment, improved enrolment rates, psychosocial support, education supplies, student kit, school rehabilitation and regular classes are expected to contribute positively in the overall child development as well as attendance and school performance. The project is aligned with the objectives and priorities as set by Humanitarian Response plan. This project will also contribute to the No lost generation initiative by enrolling the out of school children and providing them with protective learning environment
A Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) Framework will be developed to enable continual improvement during implementation and decision making with an emphasis on accountability systems for information sharing and handling feedback/complaints. An indicator tracking tool will be developed, along with a detailed plan for data collection, entry, analysis and reporting on the indicators. Regular monitoring of a core set of activities will be carried out to ensure the quality of implementation, thereby contributing to the achievement of the overall impact of the project. Tools/checklists will be developed with a specific section to enable follow up on key issues arising from the activity monitoring process.
Islamic Relief has an accountability system that will focus on information sharing, participation, and complaint and feedback mechanisms. A system will be in place to share information and gather/respond to feedback from beneficiaries to effectively improve the quality of the action's design and implementation, and strengthen the relationship and acceptance by stakeholders.
A learning and knowledge management system will be established to ensure the generation, capture, dissemination, and application of the action's learning. Ongoing monitoring findings, best practices, and lesson learned will be captured to generate knowledge. A database will be in place to capture these for further dissemination among project stakeholders.

4 - Needs

As per Education cluster HNO summary, There is a total of 7-million people in need of education support. 86% of these people in need have acute and immediate need of humanitarian education assistance (severity of 4-5-6). Aleppo and Idleb remain top two governorates for the people in need. Household-reported attendance is lower than the national average in: Damascus at 69%, Aleppo at 66%, Raqqa at 64%, Idleb at 63%, Hasakeh at 62% and Deir ez Zor at 57%. Household-reported school attendance is not consistent across population groups. At the national level 76% of attendance is reported by residents, 68% by returnees and 65% by IDPs. The disparity between total household-reported attendance and the attendance rates of residents, IDPs and returnees become greater in upper secondary school. enrollment data indicates that there is gender party at the primary level and a widening gap between boys and girls at the secondary level where there are more girls enrolled than boys. There is a wide range in enrollment rates across geographic areas for both boys and girls. he estimated rates of female enrollment are near or below 50% in Hasakeh, Aleppo, Raqqa and Idleb. Estimated rates of boys enrollment are near or below 50% in Raqqa, Idleb, Aleppo and Deir ez Zor. It should be noted that Idleb, Aleppo are Raqqa are the governorates with the lowest rates for both boys and girls. The proportion of over-aged learning is converse to the trend in household-reported school attendance. While household-reported attendance decreases from primary to secondary, the proportion of overaged learners increases from primary to secondary. The national average proportion of overaged learners in primary schools is about 9%. The national average proportion of overaged learners in secondary schools is about 24%. The children are exposed to multiple child protection issues including early marriage, child labour, family separation etc. Source: Education HNO 2020 Summary

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented in strong coordination with the local authorities, the education directorate involving all relevant stakeholders. The project is designed to contribute to the overall objective of contributing to the improvement of the education and individual development of the Syrian children affected by the on-going crises and is set jointly by the humanitarian community under the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Detailed meetings will be conducted with the school management to design back to school campaign. This campaign will sensitize the parents and caregivers about the importance of education and will bring back 2000 children into targeted formal schools. All the children in the targeted schools will be provided with student kit. IR will also work with the school management to list down the furniture and education supplies /running cost for each school. Based on which procurement of furniture, education supplies will be conducted. To ensure teachers and the school management staff remains committed for their regular attendance, monthly stipend will be provided to 120 teacher and school management staff. IR in coordination with the local authorities will identify training institute to provide TOT trainings on INEE minimum standards for education. 120 teachers and school management staff will be trained as master trainers. These trained group of teachers will conduct monthly workshops in their respective schools. These workshops will provide an opportunity to disseminate their learning to PEER and colleagues. Also through these workshops plans will be developed to improve the teaching quality and conduct Psychosocial support activities. This platform will be used to share the ideas among the teachers and school management staff to further improve the learning environment for children. Prefabricated class rooms will be provided in the areas having large number of IDP children as well as rehabilitation of schools will be conducted.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include: airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff / students or military use of the school. Mitigation measure: Learning facilities can be de-conflicted using the de-confliction mechanism administered by UN-OCHA and IR will report any such attacks to the cluster. 2. Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, organization staff or related entities. Mitigation measure: IR will put child safeguarding policies and procedures in place and will ensure that anyone interacting with the children abide by those policies. Students, teachers and other education personnel will also be provided regular psychosocial support services. 3. Teachers or other education personnel participate in physical punishment or bullying of students or Students participate in bullying of students. Mitigation measure: Teachers and school personnel will be trained on prevention and reporting of, and issues related to, physical punishment and bullying. Sufficient safe and clean bathroom facilities will be provided. These will be sex and age segregated. 4. Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving. Mitigation measure: Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies will be in place, practiced and monitored. The targeted communities and learning facilities will be aware of the organization’s policies and procedures
