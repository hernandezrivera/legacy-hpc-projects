Project Summary

Chronic food insecurity and malnutrition in parts of Western Bahr El Ghazel is attributed to lack of livelihood opportunities for mainly women, coupled with recurrent shocks such as conflict, erratic weather patterns that led to flash floods, and high livestock disease burdens.
Severe food insecurity is expected at the height of the lean season of 2022 due to the impact of conflict, climatic and economic shocks. Local currency depreciation, inflation, high import and fuel costs, and other factors, are sustaining high staple food prices and limiting economic activity, which in turn restricts household purchasing power.
Food Agriculture and Disaster Management (FADM) is a national NGO working in Warrap, Northern Bahr El Ghazel and Western Bahr El Ghazel States and is currently implementing WASH, Nutrition and livelihood programs funded by UNDP, OCHA and USAID/RRF.
Food Insecurity persists in the locations targeted, with the majority of those affected females from especially female headed HHs. Children, persons with disability, older persons and women- headed households are identified as the most vulnerable
to be affected by increased food insecurity.
Food security and livelihoods were identified as the most needed forms of assistance for men in 55 per cent of assessed settlements under the latest round of the FSNMS+; similarly, food and livelihoods were recognized as the number one priority need for women, in 52 per cent of assessed settlements and in the case of children, food and livelihoods were the second most frequently identified pressing need (as reported in 29 per cent of assessed settlements), behind education in 48 per cent.
The project will target persons through cluster objectives CO2 and CO3. FADM plans a 12 month response targeting 12,000 people with food security and livelihood support inline with cluster frameworks and plans, with a focus on reaching women, the elderly and people with disability. 58% of the beneficiaries will be female; 4% PWDs.

1 - Needs

Food insecurity continues to be a challenge in the locations of Western Bahr El Ghazel States in the counties of Wau, Raja and Jur River. High levels of food insecurity drive humanitarian need in most parts of Western Bahr El Ghazel. An estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May – July) 2022 in South Sudan, an increase of 7 per cent from 2021. The main drivers are floods and dry spells, inflation and high food price rises, and the continued disruption of livelihoods due to violence or the fear of violence; and household stressors, such as death of bread winner, gender-based violence, and morbidity and mortality due to poor hygiene and lack of access to essential basic services, which are greatly amplified by the continuous effect of shocks. High levels of acute malnutrition are attributed to severe food insecurity, poor access to health and nutrition services, high morbidity, extremely poor diets, poor sanitation and hygiene services. Food insecurity negatively impacts people’s health and nutritional wellbeing, and their ability to carry out livelihood activities. Those identified as most affected are children, people with disabilities, older persons and women-headed households. These people are the most vulnerable to be affected by increases in food insecurity. Food security and livelihoods were identified as the most needed forms of assistance for men in 55 per cent of assessed households; similarly, food and livelihoods were recognized as the primary priority need for women, in 52 per cent of assessed households and in the case of children, food and livelihoods was the second most frequently identified pressing need, as reported in 29 per cent of assessed households, following education in 48 per cent. World Food Programme in April 2021 announced ration cuts from 70 to 50 per cent and the reduction of the duration of support in IDP camps in Juba, Wau and Bor from 12 to 9 months. Multiply displaced IDPs have significantly higher levels of food insecure households (over 53%) than host communities (35%). Female-headed households also have higher prevalence of food insecurity (45% versus 33% for male headed households), a possible indication of gender-based vulnerabilities. At the household level, food availability is now expected to be lower than previously projected due to crop damage from Fall Armyworm which has spread among nearly all the counties as well as damage of seedlings by floods that have affected vast locations in Western Bahr El Ghazel States. Loss of livelihoods, economic challenges, lack of tools and inputs, post harvest losses, livestock diseases have all contributed to the dire food security situation. Households in both urban and rural areas remain unable to afford the minimum food basket. The latest economic update indicates a significant decrease in the number of households that receive income from casual (skilled and unskilled) labor in Western Bahr El Ghazel States according the latest FSNMS+. Disease outbreaks such as AWDs have had significant effects of food security. Livestock conditions are also impacted. Changed migration patterns due to conflict, insecurity, and concentration of animals in small areas have raised concerns about disease outbreaks, reduced access to resources and veterinary extension services, as well as increased tensions between herders and farmers. Importantly, the terms of trade between livestock and cereals have drastically declined across all markets, undermining purchasing power of agro pastoralists in Western Bahr El Ghazel States. Food consumption scores as well as household hunger indices indicate variances from accepted thresholds immensely manifested in the lack of dietary diversification.

2 - Project activities

• Carry out, participate and contribute to food security and other multi sectoral assessments including IPC assessments, FSNMS and other WFP, FAO, led FSL fact finding missions. • Enlist and enroll 12,000 persons in the locations of Wau, Jur River and Raja Counties as direct beneficiaries of the project including 60% of these female, 40% IDPs, 5% elderly and PWDs. • In partnership with FAO and WFP, distribute inputs including seeds, tools livelihood kits and vouchers to vulnerable communities in Wau, Jur River and Raja Counties. • In partnership with FAO, carry out emergency livestock initiatives amongst vulnerable communities involving vaccinations, case identification, surveillance, treatment and training of CAHWs amongst other initiatives. • Carry out plant and animal disease surveillance and early warning systems in collaboration with state line ministries and lead agencies. • Conduct seed fairs in locations with functional markets and apparent surplus produce to support the trading farmers and local economy while supplying locally adapted seeds to the beneficiaries. • Establish, rehabilitate and revive community owned post-harvest handling facilities. • Rehabilitate and revive markets through Emergency Market Initiatives aimed at strengthening livelihoods, directly targeting IDPs and vulnerable host communities. • Carry out trainings of agriculture workers, community animal health structures, county FSL structures, as well as integration of ongoing WASH and Nutrition programming in Wau, Jur River and Raja Counties funded by UNDP, OCHA and USAID/RRF.
