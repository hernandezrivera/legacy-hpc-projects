Project Summary

Based on HNO, out of the 16.7 PIN, 14.9 million are in need for health services and 7.9 million are in critical diverse need: children, elderly, disability, pregnant and lactating women in different settings requiring integrated programming, intra-sectoral and intersectoral coordination. Observed by UNHCR field offices, reported in HeRAMS, access to and functionality of basic health services continue to be challenged by 40% of idle facilities aggravated by the earthquake 2023. 56% of sub-districts being underserved, additional shortage in human resources, increased medicine prices hindering access to treatment of patients with NCDs, accounting for 49% of mortality in Syria. Increased transportation cost, fuel cost and power supply impeded access of children, pregnant women, and persons with disabilities in rural and remote areas. UNHCR database of over 5 Million beneficiaries in 100 community centers in 14 governorates showing medical conditions first ranked in identified vulnerabilities forming 15% of total beneficiaries, persons with disabilities 7%, and persons with MHPSS needs 11% out of whom 71% were not able to receive specialized MHPSS services. Poor environmental health including drought, living conditions, reduced coverage with potable water and poor sewage networks increase the risks of outbreaks.
Community health will be implemented through NGO partners. Static and mobile modality by 150 Community health workers targeting 650,000 PoC in static health points embedded in the CCs and mobile units. 1300-1600 trained community health outreach volunteers including women thematic volunteers trained on neonatal home care. Joint work with UNICEF, WHO and UNFPA in capacity building, identification and referrals. 25 physicians provide disease management including MHPSS and GBV medical response services. Engagement and participation of the community in identifying, early detection and addressing health risks. Selection of outreach representative volunteers, FGDs, committees and nearly 190 community led health initiatives further engage the community throughout the program cycle and support equal access to services. Linkages with levels of care, local mapping, coordination, and cooperation with health actors to address health needs and optimize community resources. Support to reviving primary health care system, improving quality of services, and ensuring equal access to essential package of PHC through direct or partner rehabilitation of 8 PHCs, sector coordinated using MoH local standards. Supporting 7 selected public PHC to pilot equality in access to quality delivery of PHC package using family medicine approach. Weekly monitoring by partners, monthly by UNHCR offices and third party when need be. UNHCR information system will report to the sector lead.
Protection risks: gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and abuse, discrimination against vulnerable groups, and inequitable access to health services particularly for persons with disabilities. Mitigation measures: mainstreaming protection including using customized tools to examine physical environment, processes, policies, SOPs in place and upscale partner capacity to identify and address protection risks. Community-based approach is the core of the project and conflict sensitive programing is integrated in the project design and implementation including community committees, outreach volunteers and community feedback mechanisms.
PSEA Zero-tolerance policy, PSEA focal point for coaching, information sharing, reporting and mainstreaming, Code of conduct signed by each worker/contractor.
High staff turnover especially in rural/remote areas, fuel and electricity shortages, depreciation of currency, increased costs of services and monitoring challenges in remote areas are identified risks. Backup staff and recruitment policies prioritizing residents in remote areas, resident outreach volunteers and additional transportation budget are mitigation measures.
