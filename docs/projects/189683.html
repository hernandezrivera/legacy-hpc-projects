Project Summary

Conflict, sub-national violence and heavy seasonal flooding continues to cause internal displacement in South Sudan leaving 2.4 million of its population impacted through destruction of housing and loss of essential household goods. The complex operating environment combined with accessibility challenges indicates that in-kind provision of lifesaving supplies continues to be the most significant modality for emergency responses in the country.
Since 2011, IOM has been successfully managing the shelter and non-food items (S-NFI) Core Pipeline, which is an essential support to the S-NFI Cluster in providing critical services to Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) partners.
Under the HRP 2022, IOM will maintain its commitment to support the S-NFI Cluster Pipeline through the provision of key shelter and NFI supplies to benefit a total of 886,254 people in need in South Sudan. Procured supplies will be transported for prepositioning in strategic logistic hubs throughout the country, namely Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Rumbek, and Wau to ensure response efficiency by contributing to the reduction of response time through moving the supplies closer to final distribution sites. The Pipeline system optimizes economies of scale, reduction of cost, harmonized quality of items, non-duplication and supply chain efficiencies. The distribution will be prioritized to directly benefit vulnerable people affected by displacement, conflict, natural disasters (such as the heavy seasonal rains and flooding), disease outbreak, famine/food insecurity, and GBV risks.

1 - Needs

In 2022, despite the prospect of peace and development, approximately 2.4 million people in South Sudan continue to struggle with shelters and basic necessities as they experience destruction of their housing and essential household goods brought about by the compounded effect of the complex protracted humanitarian crisis; intermittent sub-national, endemic violence; displacement; and recurrent long and heavy seasonal rains (Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2022). According to the HNO 2022, the number of people in need increased this year due to the increased displacement of approximately 300,000 people in 2021 and the returns of nearly 270,000 from neighboring countries. This has resulted to nearly 1.9 million vulnerable population with inadequate shelters and about 2.3 million requiring essential non-food items (NFI). The three consecutive years of long rainy seasons causing devastating floods that seem to increase in scale and magnitude every year has impacted even non-displaced communities grappling to cope with damages to loss their shelter/properties and yet have to cope with hosting and absorbing the demands of newly displaced people. IOM, as the main agency managing 100% of the SNFI core pipeline, will maintain its supports to the SNFI Cluster in providing partner organizations with rapid access to lifesaving materials to meet the population's emergency needs under the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) of 2022. The most vulnerable and underserved populations will be targeted, specifically, those displaced (including protracted and new displacements, inside and outside of IDP camps), at risk of GBV, and those affected by environmental disasters. Depending on the local context and severity of needs, the Shelter-NFI Cluster and pipeline teams will maintain a flexible approach to responses in order to swiftly respond to emergencies. The contents of packages provided to beneficiaries are determined through assessments of the needs of target communities and will include prioritized key lifesaving items. IOM's extensive experience in pipeline management has built its capacity and expertise to meet partner needs in a complex environment characterized by major logistical and accessibility challenges, including surges of insecurity and a lack of basic infrastructure. South Sudan has a very poor road network with less than 100 kilometers of paved road. Most markets have collapsed as a result of the protracted conflict, and the affected population cannot access the most basic of needs. About 70 percent of the country's road network becomes inaccessible during the rainy season, which has increasingly lengthened from May to December for the past three years. Due to the magnitude of flooding in Unity State in 2021, one of the major logistics hubs in Bentiu, is still inaccessible more than halfway through the dry season, which is having an impact on prepositioning for the incoming rainy season, which is forecasted to come earlier in 2022. In general, airstrips within South Sudan are of poor quality, with only a few airstrips used continuously in the wet and dry seasons. During the dry season, insecurity along the main supply routes is a significant challenge restricting movement and increasing transportation costs due to a high-risk operational environment for transporters. Transportation by road where/when possible remains the most cost-effective means of delivery. The preposition of supplies in key-hubs locations across the country, as well as transportation of emergency S-NFIs to response locations are important part of SNFI Cluster's crucial strategy to ensure swift access to emergency relief items in a coordinated approach.

2 - Project activities

IOM core pipeline will provide critical S-NFI supplies to implementing partners so that the most vulnerable have improved access to emergency shelters and lifesaving non-food items. The following activities are proposed: - Procure emergency S-NFI supplies for the humanitarian response following assessed needs, targeting 886,254 individuals- Administer and process partners' approved request for distribution- Coordinate with the S-NFI cluster, partners, Logistics cluster, state focal points, and all other stakeholders relevant for pipeline operations- Transport procured SNFI pipeline supplies to six IOM logistic hubs outside Juba (Bentiu, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek) and any other potential partner warehouses across the country for prepositioning of stocks- Transport approved SNFI supplies to partner response locations.- Appropriate storage and management of the shelter and NFI emergency supplies at IOM warehouses in Juba, Bor, Malakal, Wau, Rumbek, and Bentiu- Regular monitoring of SNFI core pipeline supply delivery, supplies quality control, storage management, and supplies utilization- Conduct post-distribution monitoring of select SNFI partner response to gather beneficiary feedback on the quality of the items distributed, assess the appropriateness and effectiveness of the response, and assess mainstreaming of cross-cutting issues- Provide capacity-building for Pipeline partners - Undertake pipeline survey with partners for the efficiency, appropriateness, and coverage of the pipeline service- Actively participate in operations working group, technical working groups, cluster meetings, and any other forum or gathering within the S-NFI and inter-cluster operations to ensure a coordinated approach in all S-NFI activities

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

• The feedback gathered from the IOM Core Pipeline annual satisfaction survey serves as input for improving Pipeline services to deliver partners' requirements better and ultimately more efficiently serve the targeted beneficiaries.• IOM also produces reports from the post-distribution monitoring exercises shared with partners and the SNFI cluster to feed into the feedback loop and improve future response.

• During PDM field data collection, aside from randomized household surveys, the IOM Core Pipeline monitoring team also ensures that representatives of the community's vulnerable groups are interviewed to triangulate data with the survey and desk review. • IOM supports the SNFI Clusters advocacy for all frontline agencies to strengthen their feedback mechanism and community consultation through regular monitoring of partners' reporting requirements, including discussion on accountability to the affected population. • IOM Core Pipeline in select responses conducts post-distribution monitoring (PDM), which includes a randomized household survey to collect feedback of beneficiaries and key informant interviews with representatives of vulnerable groups within a community.• As IOM Core Pipeline's direct beneficiaries, partners are surveyed at the end of the year to measure partner's perception of its services and get feedback on overall pipeline management

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

- IOM Pipeline activities mainly deal with partner agencies and their focal points in S-NFI program and logistics and has only limited interaction with beneficiaries during post-distribution monitoring activities. In any humanitarian emergency, the partner agencies conduct an inclusive consultation process with the community leaders and the representatives of all groups within the community to assess the affected population's needs. Planning of the response starts here wherein the location of distribution centres and areas of assistance and services are discussed and agreed upon so that not only community leaders decide on important matters. Access and security of beneficiaries are the most important factors that are considered. Community members also take ownership of the process, and tasks such as off-loading, guarding during the distribution, enumeration, and such are given to qualified community members. During the actual response, security is also monitored by the response team. PDM surveys have questions directly related to beneficiaries' safety during and after distribution, and results are taken into consideration so that the principle of "Do No Harm" is continuously ensured across all contexts of humanitarian response. - All S-NFI approved requests from partners go through a rigorous review process by the SNFI cluster and its State Focal Points, through which assessment reports and response/project documents are required for approval. The review considers Accountability to Affected Populations and gauges how partner organizations target beneficiaries with priority given to the population's most vulnerable segments. The SNFI cluster also has a S/NFI Needs Assessment toolkit and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) toolkit that partners use as a guideline in implementing their humanitarian responses. This includes tools and templates that categorically incorporate questions on inclusion, participation, and protection to measure the effectiveness of the response in addressing the affected population's needs. Findings and recommendations are given to ensure that issues are raised and addressed for future response.- IOM has a zero-tolerance of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) by IOM staff members and the employees or any other persons engaged and controlled by IOM Contractors. Extensive training and workshop on protection against SEA have been rolled out, and all IOM Core Pipeline staff has undergone at least one training in the past year. IOM Core Pipeline also requires its SNFI partners to sign a memorandum of understanding to promote and strengthen accountability. This agreement includes clauses on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse that partners are also obligated to strictly enforce since they directly interact and deal with beneficiaries
