Project Summary

With the on-going crisis, access to essential services has been severely curtailed due to insecurity, large-scale displacement, destruction or closure of health facilities and schools, withdrawal of implementing partners, and relocation of health, education, and social workers. An estimated 246,000 children have been displaced inside the South West, 105,000 in the North West, and around 86,000 displaced to neighboring West and Littoral regions (Interagency Needs Assessment Oct 9, 2018). So far, most people are displaced in the bush in hard to reach areas. Pressing needs of IDPs in the bush/hard to reach areas include child protection needs among others as reported in various assessment missions including those conducted by UNICEF between November 2017-August 2018 but also in a multisectoral assessment conducted in March 2018 and various assessments conducted by CSO and INGOs.
As of today, the provision of humanitarian aid is mainly ensured by local NGOs at a very small scale. These NGOs are not familiar with humanitarian interventions and principles but possess knowledge of the area and can negotiate access and acceptance. UNICEF has established partnerships with non governmental partners already present in the 2 regions to increase acceptance by all stakeholders. UNICEF began implementing emergency response activities in August 2018 with a focus on IDPs in hard to reach areas, both in government controlled or not areas.
In order to protect children from violence, abuse and neglect, as a priority and main intervention, UNICEF will work on the offer of services and provide psychosocial support to 135,367 children including 69,548 girls affected by the conflict in 133 child friendly spaces or youth clubs in urban, peri-urban and hard to reach areas.
As a direct corollary, an estimated 3,764 unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) is foreseen to be identified during the implementation of the project with an estimated number of 1,910 girls. All the 3,764 children will benefit from alternative care arrangements, support and individual follow up while due to the difficult access and insecurity UNICEF plans to reunify a minimum of 740 unaccompanied or separated children with their family.
UNICEF will complement its support to children activities, work on the demand side with awareness raising and prevention activities at community-level. Concretely, UNICEF with its implementing partners will run sensitization campaigns with the aim to reach out to 122,143 persons with specific attention to parents to pass on messages on child protection in emergencies, trauma early warning signs for children, effects of family separation, GBV and other types of violence children are at risk of at the community level so that parents and community members can play an active role in child protection in emergencies.
UNICEF and its partners will also provide support to an estimated 961 unregistered, displaced children being particularly vulnerable to various forms of discrimination and abuse because of the lack of birth certificates.
Taking into account the implementation environment and the need for capacity building of implementing partners, UNICEF will build and strengthen the capacity of humanitarian actors on child protection mainstreaming throughout all sectors/clusters, but also enhance their knowledge and technicalities in child protection in emergencies through the organization a total of 5 workshops in different locations (Boyo, Mezam, Fako, Manyu and Mémé) targeting a minimum of 403 field staff responding to the crisis and its child protection component. UNICEF as lead of the Child Protection Area of Responsibility will continue to lead the child protection in emergency sub-cluster in Buea with an antenna in Bamenda, in order to consolidate the coordination and ensure alignment and complementarity of interventions in the field of child protection.

Needs

In 2018, there continued to be reports of prominent levels of violence and insecurity impacting civilian populations in many areas of the South-West and North-West regions, including incidents of killings, kidnappings, gender-based violence and destruction of property by all parties to the conflict. However, due to lack of access and insecurity reasons, there is still currently a lack of a robust protection monitoring system and referral mechanisms that allow to verify and collect fully reliable data.The two regions are affected by massive displacement of populations who are also facing a significant breakdown of social structures and disruption of access to social services in the communities. For instance, children’s access to usual social and community support and education has been disrupted since 2017. Increased insecurity and violence in North-West and South-West regions with presence of improvised explosive devices, kidnappings, shootings are also affecting the delivery of humanitarian assistance.While a total of 1,822,721 (916,028 girls and 906,693 boys) children in the four regions affected by the crisis, including Littoral and West regions, are considered as affected by the crisis, an estimated total of 302,897 children (170,551 girls and 132,347 boys) in North-West and South-West regions have experienced psychosocial distress or trauma related to the witnessing or direct experience of violence or family separation. These children are in deer need of mental health care and/or psychosocial support. Part of them have been separated from their families and are either separated or unaccompanied and have to be identified, be cared for and place in alternative care while their families are traced for further family reunification in a safe and secure place, if applicable. More specifically, crisis affected children’s specific needs are:• 266,734 internally displaced children, host community children including children victims of gender-based violence, and unaccompanied and separated children are in need of psychosocial support. UNICEF will provide psychosocial support to 135,367 crisis affected children;• 8,749 children are estimated to be separated from their families or unaccompanied and by consequence at risk of violence, abuse and exploitation, with even worse lack of access to basic social services. UNICEF will respond to the needs of 3,764 UASC for identification and family tracing, for alternative care, for psychosocial support, for reunification of 740 UASC with their families whereas the reunification will be possible, safe and secure. All these interventions will be done using a community-based approach;• Children and especially girls are in need in such context to be protected from gender-based violence. While children will be informed within the framework of the child friendly spaces and youth clubs, parents and community members will be informed and sensitized through sessions and discussion groups addressing GBV and child protection in emergency issues, including trauma early warning signs;• Within the scope of the work of the community-based child protection networks, children without birth certificates will be identified and efforts will be made to provide them with certificates or to register them by civil registration offices if running, safe and accessible. As mentioned, needs will be met through the setting up and strengthening of community-based networks, case management and referral mechanisms, capacity building of parents to detect symptoms of trauma, social mobilization to boost the prevention of violence and reporting by communities of violence, abuse and exploitation against children. For the coordinated and complementary implementation of interventions, UNICEF will continue to lead the child protection in emergency area of responsibility and work with non-governmental implementing partners who have at least five years’ experience in those two regions and are accepted by the populations.
