Project Summary

Humanitarian needs remain acute in Somalia as result of continued conflict and repeated climatic shocks. According to FSNAU’s 2019 Post Gu assessment The2019 Gu rains season performed poorly and forecasted up to 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps, high acute malnutrition, or depletion of assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through December 2019. An estimated 2.6 million people are internally displaced (IDPs)further jeopardizing the survival and well-being of children, and without urgent assistance, we face the risk of a major nutrition and food-security crisis unfolding during the long dry season of 2020.
According to the recent WASH cluster analysis, more than 9 million people are affected by the prevalent poor WASH conditions in Somalia. The same document also estimated that 2.7 million people are in severe (2.5M) and extreme (0.2M) need of humanitarian WASH assistance. An estimated 700,000 people along Shabelle and Juba basins are also identified as highly susceptible for flooding and need of acute life-saving WASH interventions during the 2020 Gu and Deyr rainy seasons. Cholera is endemic in Somalia, from December 2017 till 15th Sept 2019, a total of 8,719 cases and 46 deaths reported from 25 districts and 6 regions of Somalia, which is attributed to protracted displacement, drought, flooding together with poor water supply and sanitation services and poor hygiene practices.
During the long dry seasons, major water sources like boreholes, shallow wells, hand-dug wells, berkads, dams, water pans and springs became extremely unreliable. This resulted in higher water supply access costs to households during the dry season. For example, during May 2019 the water prices ranged from 4.5 USD to 23 USD for one cubic meter water which very high compared with water prices in neighbouring countries.
A combination of poor access to safe drinking water, lack of adequate sanitation facilities and poor hygienic practices especially in IDP settlements, contribute significantlly to overall public health risks in Somalia . Open defecation rates stand at 49 per cent in rural areas and 28 per cent at national level. Handwashing practices with soap are very low.
Lack of clean water and poor sanitation and hygiene practices not only cause diseases but also create huge burden on women and children as women and girls perform most of the unpaid labour associated with WASH in households and communities which reduces the time they have available for education, economic activities and leisure. Women and girls are often vulnerable to harassment or violence when they travel long distances to fetch water, use shared toilets, or practice open defecation. In addition, lack of proper hygiene supplies like soap and sanitary pads affects dignity of women and girls.
This emergency WASH project will comprise of situation assessments, preparedness, emergency WASH response, strengthening partners’ capacity, cluster coordination and information management. UNICEF will deliver emergency lifesaving support for emergency affected and vulnerable populations throughout Somalia by providing access to WASH services as per Sphere standards and minimize the suffering of the displaced populations and vulnerable host communities. The project will focus on assessing the WASH situation in the emergency affected areas, emergency water trucking/water vouchers, rehabilitating and upgrading urban and rural water facilities, provision of emergency sanitation services for IDPs and vulnerable communities, hygiene promotion and flood and cholera prevention and preparedness activities. The project will also support expansion of partnerships with local and international NGOs and government ministries in areas affected by different emergencies. To reach the total targeted population of 1.2 Million People a total of 39.6 Million USD is required for the year 2020.

Project Activities

Output 1: 800,000 emergency affected people (167,500 women, 147,800 men, 247,100 Boys, 237,600 Girls) have temporary access to adequate and safe water. Activity 1.1: Provision of water for 550,000 people affected by emergencies (AWD/Cholera, drought and conflict) through water trucking/vouchers, O&M services (15L/person/day for 45 days) Activity 1.2. Provision of water for 250,000 people affected by emergencies (AWD/Cholera, drought and conflict) through water source chlorination/disinfection and HHWTs. Output 2: 220,000 emergency affected people (46,050 women, 40,720 men, 67,913 Boys, 65,317 Girls) provided with access to appropriate sanitation facilities (single sex and equipped with hand washing facilities). Activity 2.1 Construction of 5,000 shared family latrines with handwashing facilities for 150,000 people. Activity.2.2 Desludging and repair of an estimated 2,334 latrines / cesspits /septic tanks in institutions and densely populated IDP settlements, to benefit 70,000 people.Activity 2.3: Support clean up campaigns and/or cash for work solid waste management to improve the sanitary conditions in IDP settlements and marginalized communities.Activity 2.4: Construction of new or rehabilitation and upgrading of existing gender segregated sanitation facilities in 60 schoolsActivity 2.5: Construction of new or rehabilitation and upgrading of existing gender segregated sanitation facilities in 40 health and nutrition facilitiesActivity 2.6: Hygiene promotion activities in 100 institutions (60 schools and 40 health and nutrition facilities) Output 3: 1,200,000 emergency affected people and vulnerable host communities (251,273 women, 221,818 men; 370,545 Boys and 356,364 Girls) provided with hygiene and sanitation promotion messages and emergency WASH supplies including Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits. Activity 3.1 Procurement, prepositioning and distribution of critical emergency WASH supplies and equipment including hygiene kits to meet WASH emergency needs for water point disinfection and household level water treatment to benefit at least 1,200,000 people. Activity 3.2: Training and deployment of 1,200 community hygiene promoters for hygiene promotion and sanitary surveillance at Households and health and nutrition facilities. Output 4: 450,000 people (94,200 women, 83,150 men, 139,000 Boys, 133,650 Girls) provided with reliable and sustained access to basic water services through rehabilitation and construction of new strategic water sources. Activity 4.1: construction of 84 new strategic water supply systems targeting 210,000 people.Activity 4.2: Rehabilitation, upgrading and/or development of existing 96 water supply systems and water points targeting 240,000 peopleActivity 4.3: Train/support community based and public private partnership management models for management of newly developed water supply systems. Output 5: Efficiency of Emergency WASH response National, Zonal and/or regional authorities, as well as partners, in targeted high-risk areas/districts are prepared to deliver emergency response. Activity 5.1 Support line Ministries and Government agencies to develop emergency preparedness and response plans to WASH related emergencies including support to conduct assessments. Activity 5.2. Support WASH cluster coordination and related cluster assessments at National, zonal, regional and district level ensuring government leadership for coordinated response.Activity 5.3: Training and capacity building of WASH cluster partners and government counterparts to improve knowledge and ability for rapid response.
