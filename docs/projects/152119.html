Project Summary

In line with the three GBV strategic objectives of the HRP 2019, the “Call to Action” road map and the centrality of protection, the project aim to strengthen protection of Women, men, boys and girls displaced and returnees against Sexual and gender based violence (SGBV) and improve the quality of integrated response in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states.
Additionally, the project will address specific issues related to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) involving humanitarian workers, Security actors and community leaders in the camps.

In order to ensure a comprehensive approach, the project will adopt UNHCR SGBV strategy based on 3 pillars: Identification, Prevention and holistic Response.
At the entry points (transit centre) and in the camps, Identification and documentation of risk points and SGBV incidents will be strengthened via community based protection mechanisms (monitors, Protection Action Groups, men and boys group, rapid alert system, community based complaint mechanisms, youth clubs and peer to peer support, etc.) in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States to support advocacy.

For the Response, the project will ensure that an integrated package is available and all identified survivors as well as vulnerable men, women, girls, boys and adolescents have safe access to the multisectoral services (PSS, referral for legal support, material support, case management, economic empowerment, etc.) with a special attention to women and girls identify as “BHI wifes”, women and girls in detention facilities and women and girls previously used or associated with armed group.

In term of prevention, the project will work to engage all women, men, boys and girls IDPs and returnees to prevent and respond to SGBV using innovative approach such as SASA methodology (Start, Awareness, Support, Action) . For this purpose, the project aims to build capacity through various trainings for IDPs and returnees leaders (religious and traditional), peer to peer groups, community volunteers to identify, prevent and respond to SGBV. Raising awareness through mass sensitization campaigns in and out of camps on SGBV and sexual exploitation abuse.
The project will explore innovative way to mitigate risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse due to lack of condiments and livelihood opportunities. Safe access to fuel and energy will be promoted through alternative source of energy.
Livelihood activities will be use as a preventive action to reduce economic vulnerability of women, men, boys and girls IDP’s and returnees against sexual exploitation and abuse.
This project will be complemented by the “access to justice” program supported by UNHCR.

Needs

Protection incidents particularly sexual and gender based violence remain a major concern in the north east of Nigeria despite multiple efforts in terms of prevention and response. Women and girls remains the most affected (95%) by SGBV. From January to June 2018, UNHCR had reported that a considerable number of returnees and IDPs experienced different types of SGBV (rape, abductions, child and forced marriage, undesired pregnancies, sexual exploitation, physical assault, denial of resources, etc.) in the hands of non-state armed groups, in countries of asylum and in the IDP camps. There is a need for a tailored psychosocial support to build resilience among survivors. According to the HRP 2019, the number of people in need of GBV prevention and response in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa states is estimated at 3.0 million. Protection need for SGBV are huge and the inter-agency capacity to respond remain insufficient, poor in quality and challenging (coverage) in the current security situation. Inaccessibility to certain areas, socio-cultural constraints and lack of adequate protection have led to under reporting of SGBV incidents by the community. It is very difficult to determine the real scale of SGBV among women, girls, men and boys internally displaced and returnees. Ongoing insecurity and lack of basic services and resources (food, NFIs, shelter, clothing education and health facilities, income generating activities) in areas of return and around IDPs camps has resulted in additional challenges for female-headed households and widows, increasing their vulnerability to rape, exploitation and abuse, sexual harassment, forced marriage as well as domestic violence. According to the DTM Round 24 Report (august 2018), 73 % of displaced persons mentioned food as the main unmet need, followed by NFI and Shelter. End of 2017, UNHCR, WFP and FAO carried out an assessment on « safe access to fuel and energy » within Borno state among IDP’s, returnees and host population. The finding showed that 95% of the interviewed population depend on firewood and charcoal for their daily cooking energy needs and 85% indicated that they face protection risk during collection of firewood including rape, sexual assault, killing and abductions. Vulnerability of women and girls has also increased due to many factors such as the reduction of assistance package and targeting of the most vulnerable represent a real risk of SEA and SGBV, the restriction of movement, the social status of women since most of them are widows or single due to the high number of men killed during the conflict coupled with the current detention of men for investigation process by the military. Women are now saddled with the responsibility to feeding their families. There is an urgent need to address these issues by creating more economic opportunities for both women, men and adolescents. Additionally, in many Local government areas, there is not yet the presence of civil authorities with almost a breakdown of law leading to impunity for SGBV perpetrator in hard to reach areas. Once liberated from hard to reach areas, Women and children identified during the security screening as having a link with non-state armed group are transferred in detention centre in Maiduguri where humanitarian have not access. Once released, they are transferred in the “rehabilitation centre”. The whole process showed weakness and risk points for SGBV and SEA. Many allegations of SEA in exchange for resources (money, assets, etc.) by security actors as well as influential community members and government agency members have been reported by IDP’s. There is an urgent need to take appropriate measures such as capacity building, sensitization and high level advocacy to prevent Sexual Exploitation Abuse.

Activities

1. IDENTIFICATION • Identify, monitor and document through protection monitoring and vulnerability screening women, girls and female adolescents at risk including unaccompanied and separated children at the transit center, in the camp, in detention centre and in rehabilitation centre • Identify, document and refer women, men, girls and boys survivors of Sexual Gender based Violence including Sexual exploitation and Abuse • Identify and assess through vulnerability screening adolescents and women at risk in need of life skills acquisition and livelihood support • Support identification, verification and referral of girls and women used or associated with armed group • Build capacity of SGBV actors in identification and documentation of SGBV incidents as well as risks points 2. PREVENTION • Establish/Strengthen community-based GBV structures for early identification, prevention and referral for specialized services (Youth club, women committee, community volunteers, peer to peer groups, groups of men and boys engaged)• Identify key male leaders in the community (religious, traditional) and engage them in prevention of SGBV and SEA and peaceful dialogue • Implement the ground-breaking model of community mobilization using the SASA methodology to prevention gender and based violence in camps • The project will explore innovative way (promotion of kitchen gardening, referral for unconditional CBI multipurpose) to mitigate SEA due to lack of condiments and other basic needs. • Implement the SAFE approach to ensure IDP’s and returnees have safe access to fuel and energy by exploring alternative source of energy. • Livelihood activities will be use a preventive action and alternative survival package to reduce economic vulnerability of women, men, boys and girls IDP’s and returnees against sexual exploitation and abuse. • Capacity building for security actors, humanitarian actors and IDPs and returnees on SEA and SGBV • Advocacy at state level for favorable legal framework for protection of women and children • Establish/strengthen PSEA community based complaints mechanism from the transit/reception facilities to the camps 3. MULTI SECTORAL RESPONSE • Provide psychosocial Support and GBV critical material support at the transit/reception facilities for new arrivals and rehabilitation centre for WAAFG• Establish safe spaces for women and girls in the camps • Establish/strengthen Case Management System in the safe spaces • Provide psychosocial support in the camps including life skill acquisition • Referral for legal support services • GBV critical Material support (dignity kits) for vulnerable girls and women including survivors living in the camps. • Contribute to the GBV Information Management System
