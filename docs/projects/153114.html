Project Summary

As WASH needs remain high throughout South Sudan, and in line with the WASH Cluster Objectives 1 (Provide timely equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs/host communities and returnees), 2 (Mitigate WASH-related GBV) & 4 (WASH in Health for outbreak control at wider community level) and SPHERE standards, ACTED aims at establishing and improving access to WASH infrastructure through community led construction and rehabilitation of water and sanitation systems, and supporting and strengthening sustainable hygiene and sanitation through hygiene promotion, to encourage behaviour change among vulnerable communities. Under Output 1, ACTED will aim to increase and provide equitable access to safe and sufficient water through the construction and rehabilitation of boreholes and shallow wells, and distribution of WASH kits. Under Output 2, ACTED will aim to increase access to hygiene promotion through trainings in schools that will promote child-to-child learning, as well as a further distribution of WASH kits. Under Output 3, ACTED will aim to improve the access to sanitation services of vulnerable populations by constructing household latrines, school latrines, and emergency latrines, as well as raising awareness and knowledge on latrine decommissioning. In particular, ACTED will target Wau, Jur River and Raja (Western Bahr el Ghazal), Tonj South (Warrap), Akobo (Jonglei), Mingkaman (Lakes), and Yambio and Maridi (Western Equatoria) through the various activities.

Needs

Years of violent conflict have left millions of South Sudanese facing one of the world’s most devastating and complex humanitarian crises, compounded by high food insecurity and rapid economic decline which has led to the displacement of 4 million, with roughly 1.9 million among them IDPs and up to 85% estimated to be children and women. In this context WASH needs are high as “only 41% of the population have access to safe water, whereas about 74% of the total population defecate in the open” (HRP 2018). WBEG/ Warrap:In particular, clashes have displaced many in the areas of Baggari and Bazia, and access and delivery of humanitarian assistance to this population remains limited. WASH needs across WBEG remain high due to high displacement that has taken place and number of IDPs. Most IDPs have few resources having fled without their possessions, leading to a rapid depletion in local resources for the host communities. Vandalism of assets during armed clashes has left boreholes and other WASH assets damaged. In October 2018, ACTED conducted an assessment in areas across Wau and Jur River Counties, where an influx of IDPs had been identified. Findings – along with data gathered by other humanitarian actors – outlined that the main challenges that IDPs face are the lack of basic hygiene standards, absence of functioning latrines and clean water sources, combined with rampant open defecation, which poses health risks and could trigger the outbreak of diseases. ACTED’s assessment also highlighted how the lack of water sources near IDP settlements force women and girls to venture into isolated areas to collect water, exposing themselves to protection risks. In Tonj South, there is a major lack of safe water sources and overall poor sanitation, and there is a high prevalence of open defecation and scarcity of latrines, particularly concerning in schools where lack of good practices and handwashing facilities pose significant risks to child health and education. Akobo: Akobo is currently hosting a large IDP population (3,615 HH) that will likely grow over the coming year, as clashes and food insecurity continue in nearby areas. Erratic rains have stressed water resources and increased distances to functioning boreholes. The county is prone to flooding, which brings waterborne diseases. According to FSNMS data, only 27% of respondents in Akobo reported having safe access to and use an improved water source (borehole, tapstand, water yard) as their main source of drinking water, and only 15% reported having access to a latrine. These factors will likely get worse with a growing displaced population, increased stress on communal resources, and an IDP community lacking resources. Mingkaman: In May 2018, heavy rains in Mingkaman caused severe flooding, resulting in shelters of roughly 60,000 IDP households being either destroyed or seriously damaged, and WASH facilities became inaccessible, forcing IDPs to revert to open defecation in inhabited areas, which deteriorated already difficult living conditions. In particular, lack of basic hygiene standards, damaged latrines and WASH structures, combined with an increasing number of people resorting to open defecation and increased stagnant water increased the risk of disease outbreaks, especially malaria and cholera. Western Equatoria: Violence in the Yambio area displaced thousands from Yambio Town, and thousands more are estimated to be displaced within communities and in smaller sites. Boreholes are few and those that exist are inadequate for the community needs, as only 6% of HHs in Yambio reported having safe access to and use an improved water source (borehole, tapstand, water yard) as their main source of drinking water, according to FSNMS data. Sanitation facilities are widely lacking, and vulnerable populations outside of the town lack access to basic resources. In Maridi, 33% of HHs reported having safe access to water sources.

Activities

OUTPUT 1 - Increased and equitable access to safe and sufficient water:1.1: Borehole rehabilitation of 44 BH to improve access to water for 22,000 persons in the targeted areas. 1.2: Drilling of 20 boreholes with hand pumps to improve access to water. Locations will be determined through an assessment. An inclusive community consultation will also be held to ensure that protection considerations are included in the final site determination.1.3: Water Management Committees (WMCs) - ACTED will support formation of WMCs representative of the target population (with women at least 50%) as appropriate. They will be trained in hand pump repair and O&M, and given a toolkit and spare parts to ensure good maintenance. They will play a key role in community sensitization, support the identification of beneficiaries, and act as a formal feedback mechanism.1.4: Construct 20 Hand-dug Shallow Wells through a CFW model, providing a cash injection to communities targeting vulnerable HH, and supervised by technical staff to ensure safe drinking sources. Beneficiaries will be selected based on a HH vulnerability criteria to be determined in consultation with the community.1.5: Manual borehole drilling pilot program: in Akobo due to access issues typical BH drilling using heavy machinery is not possible. ACTED will pilot manual borehole drilling using low cost, easily transportable manual rotary jetting equipment to dig 3 BH. Community members will be trained in operation of the equipment in order to drill additional BH in the future as needed. 1.6: Gender-sensitive WASH Kits and cash: WASH kits will be distributed to 6,500 vulnerable HH with specific WASH needs. Kits will be procured through the WASH Cluster pipeline and will focus on HH level water treatment and storage, as well as hygiene. In addition, dignity kits and/ or cash will be distributed to those identified as having protection risks, as well as SGBV survivors, prioritising women and girls. OUTPUT 2: Increasing access to hygiene promotion2.1: WASH in schools - teacher training and hygiene promotion will be carried out in targeted schools to encourage behavioural change through child-to-child exchange. WASH friendly schools will be promoted by conducting activities and campaigns on the benefits of hand washing, the Safe Water Chain and proper waste disposal.2.2: WASH NFI Kits: hygiene promotion activities will be carried out alongside the distribution of WASH kits. Key practices such as hand washing, safe water storage and treatment, food preparation, the importance of latrine use and others will be emphasized by hygiene promoters with the support of CMCs, including messaging at distribution sites. Messages on the use of items provided in the distributed hygiene kits will also be passed out. OUTPUT 3: Targeted vulnerable populations have access to sanitation services 3.1: WASH in schools: in Tonj South and Akobo activities will include the construction of semi-permanent latrine blocks to provide safe facilities for students, with gender segregated blocks to ensure privacy. The latrines will be accompanied by handwashing stands, soap, and a distribution of sanitary pads to girls of menstrual age. In some cases shallow wells will be constructed to ensure safe water supply.3.2: Facilitate construction of 3000 HH latrines, targeting high-density population areas with high risk of communicable diseases, prevalent open defecation and lowest ratios of HH/latrines using PHAST. Tools, some materials and technical support will be provided to the beneficiaries for construction. HH will be responsible for providing local materials to increase sense of ownership. Hygiene promotion teams will sensitize households on use and maintenance of latrines, including decommissioning.3.3: Emergency Latrines (500 stances) will be constructed in WBEG to provide sanitation facilities for displaced populations and minimise risk of disease and contamination of the environment.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

A community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach will be employed throughout the project to ensure that the affected population and beneficiaries are informed and included throughout all stages of the project, particularly regarding the objectives and activities of the project. In addition, the CBPP will ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community in each of the project locations, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. The CBPP approach will ensure that the project is accountable, accepted, and owned by the targeted population.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context specific, as they take into consideration the different contexts and dynamics of each project location, as well as the type of beneficiary (e.g. new or protracted IDP). In addition, the CBPP and Project Management Committees are representative of the different gender and age groups (male, female, youth, elderly), and specific information and messages are targeted towards specific groups.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

ACTED will ensure that protection mainstreaming is included throughout all phases of the project. A focal point for protection and gender complaints will be established in each location, to address the targeted communities’ concerns and queries regarding protection and gender equality. Furthermore, during verifications, assessments/ monitoring exercises, ACTED will ensure that the specific needs of women and girls are taken into consideration and prioritised. ACTED’s staff have all been trained on protection, gender equality and sexual exploitation, including on how to identify and address any issues or concerns, and in addition will be supported with technical guidance by ACTED’s Protection Manager based in Juba. Moreover, ACTED has a strong code of conduct and policy on sexual exploitation and abuse which all staff are required to review, agree to and sign.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The project is based off assessments conducted in the targeted locations that involved a range of respondents, not just community leaders. In particular, hygiene and dignity kits distributed will be based off these assessments which have identified the needs according to those participating in the assessment, which included some of the most vulnerable populations. In addition, at the onset of the project, further assessments and community consultations will take place through the CBPP to ensure that the targeted approach and beneficiaries are in line with the needs of the community.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with a dedicated Accountability Officer based in Juba that will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project locations, and oversee reporting for the project to ensure beneficiaries can objectively report complaints and feedback. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels, as well as the training of project staff on collecting and responding to complaints, and sensitization of the community in accountability practice will be utilized. All complaints will be compiled and reported on each month and the Accountability Officer will ensure that follow up is conducted by the relevant people. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and the community, and improve the quality of our programmes. Complaints can be lodged through complaints desks/ boxes set up at distribution and project sites, over the phone to the designated CRM hotline, or directly to ACTED's staff. The beneficiaries and local community through awareness sessions, as well as the CBPP process.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Accountability to affected populations and community engagement will be considered at all stages of the project. A community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach will be employed in all locations to ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. As outlined above, the CBPP will ensure the transparency, accountability, acceptance and ownership of the project for the targeted community. Project Management Committees will be established in each location to ensure that there is ongoing communication and coordination between the beneficiaries and ACTED. The PMCs will monitor the project activities from the beneficiaries' perspective, and provide feedback and identify issues through meetings with ACTED. In addition, post distribution monitoring/ rapid assessments will also be conducted over the course of the project, and will serve as another form of AAP by evaluating the effectiveness of the project in addressing the beneficiaries’ most immediate needs, according to the beneficiaries themselves, as well as their level of satisfaction. The PDM will consist of representative surveys, as well as key informant interviews (KIIs) with beneficiaries to gather a more in depth body of information.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Consideration of varying protection concerns related to age, gender and ability will be integrated at all levels of programme planning and implementation. Wherever possible, ACTED will collect sex and age disaggregated data, and will at all times maintain beneficiary privacy. Moreover, during the construction of WASH facilities, ACTED will ensure compliance with SPHERE Standards: it has been proven that the lack of latrines increases the protection risks for women and children (who have to walk on a certain distance, sometimes at night and non-accompanied to reach appropriate sanitation facilities). Consequently, it also increases health risks: women and children often do not walk to these latrines due to time constraint or security risks and prefer to resort to open defecation. Therefore, through the construction of household latrines, ACTED will also ensure that protection standards are met, as vulnerable people within households – such as children, women and people with disabilities – will be able to safely access them. Finally, ACTED will work in close collaboration with Community Leaders and existing WASH Committees to identify vulnerable people and locate latrines near their shelters.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As the project is largely targeting IDPs and areas that have recently experienced insecurity or are in close proximity to areas of insecurity, there are a number of potential barriers to access. Firstly, ACTED may have restricted and limited access to the various locations targeted by the project due to insecurity, which could delay or prevent the implementation of the activities. To mitigate this, ACTED will monitor the security situation closely, and advocate with local authorities for access if/ when safe to do so to ensure that the project is implemented. In addition, the targeted IDPs could become displaced again over the course of the project due to further insecurity or their lack of access to basic services, which could prevent them from accessing the project sites and benefitting from the project activities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As outlined above, the improvement of inadequate/ absent WASH services will directly address specific protection risks and threats to women and girls due to lack of privacy and security, as previous assessments indicated that inadequate WASH facilities (e.g. poorly illuminated latrines, water sources located in distant and isolated areas, etc.) resulted in respondents reporting not feeling safe while using them. Moreover, the project targets the most vulnerable populations with the highest WASH needs.

The project monitoring aims to measure the access of beneficiaries through specific questions about their ability to access the project sites and distributions, as well as their perceived freedom of movement/ restrictions, and reported access to various services, especially WASH facilities, through the various monitoring surveys and interviews.

Yes

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

The project monitoring aims to measure the dignity of beneficiaries through specific questions regarding their feelings and perceptions of their dignity, and if/ how these have changed over the course of the project, through the various monitoring surveys and interviews. The questions will also focus on how the beneficiaries perceived the project approach and activities contributed or not to improving their dignity.
