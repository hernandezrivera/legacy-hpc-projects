Project Summary

The project aims to provide basic health and rehabilitation services to people with disability, with particular regard to the children, and to the host community of Juba, through the action of 35-years-old Usratuna Rehabilitation Centre and the support to 5 PHCC/Us included in the MoH network, also supporting capacity building for health personnel in the field of disability.

1 - Needs

South Sudan suffers from 50 years legacy of domination, exploitation and war, which give repercussions on institutions, structures and services to the person.After a phase of relative stability, a civil war started in December 2013 caused the interruption of the first development programmes, and the arrival in Juba of more than 100.000 displaced persons. A great part of them created the so called Urban IDP Camp, thus overcrowding areas with low population density and therefore without the needed health standards. This situation resulted in the following effects:• Conflicts with the host community,(mainly) due to the difficulty in accessing the few community services available.• Extremely high mortality rate (maternal: 789 per 100,000 live births; neonatal and 5 respectively: 39.3 and 99.2 per 1,000 live births - there are no official data available by County-). The most common diseases are: malaria (50% of the morbidity in Juba), TB, diarrhea, pneumonia, intestinal parasitosis, HIV, syphilis (in some IDP camps we detected positivity rates up to 50%), cholera (from Jul. 2016 to Feb. 2018 the longest epidemic in the history of the country)• Extreme poverty: the conflict and oil depreciation led to an unprecedented inflation (the SSP against the $ raised from a change of 2.95 in Nov. 2015, to a 149.86 in Nov. 2018) that caused a global malnutrition rate above the 15% threshold in Juba. Due to the diverse armed groups in conflict, domestic food production has almost disappeared, making the country dependent on food imports and turning food into a very expensive asset due to inflation.• An enormous % of disability cases ( due to poor health conditions and the vicious disability-poverty circle → WHO): according to DFID research March 2018, Jubek State is the 2° State with prevalence of disability (present in 20% of dwellings). Analysis of current responses to needs:• Health: facing these above mentioned challenges, the Juba system is severely deficient. with regards to basic health services (i.e. primary Health Care Unit and Centre – PHCU/C), the system makes use of a variety of dispensaries, with partial support from HPF - Health Pooled Fund, in terms of staff salary incentives, and purchase of some basic drugs. Anyway there is no a common development plan, and most importantly, no network perspective.There is a a strong correlation between malfunction of health services (inadequate structures and skills) and new cases of preventable disabilities. 65% of cases led to Usratuna Rehabilitation Centre. Burning could be prevented with basic health care (e.g. cerebral paralysis due to malaria) or with a simple educational action to health care professionals. • Rehabilitation: the incidence of types of childhood disability in Juba is: physical disabilities: 53%; d. visual disabilities: 20%; d. related to language/deafness: 17%; d. intellectual: 10%; on a tot. about 50,000 children with disabilities present. The only rehabilitation service available in this regard (in addition to the Buluk Eye Clinic) is that provided by Usratuna Centre, with about 1,000 patients a year and with an ever-growing waiting list. About 7% of afferent children need surgery (often life-saving, such as cases of hydrocephalus or osteomyelitis), a service that South Sudanese Hospitals cannot offer.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1 Receiving cases of children with disability at Usratuna Rehabilitation Centre, acting as referral point for Juba and other counties, performing screening and first assessment.Activity 1.2 Managing cases of children with disabilities starting their rehabilitation cycle at Usratuna Centre (celebral palsy, post-injection paralysis, down syndrome, erb's palsy, post-burn contractures etc.). Management of cases in need of speech therapy services at speech therapy unit at Usratuna Rehabilitation CentreActivity 1.3 Performing first assessment and referring neurological cases (hydrocephalus and spina bifida) to CURE Hospital in Mbale, Uganda, and osteomyelitis, cleft lips and palate, club foot cases to CORSU Hospital in Kampala, Uganda for special surgery. Follow up of the cases at Usratuna Centre.Activity 1.4 Production and distribution of dedicated assistive devices (corner seats, standing frames, cp chairs, walking frames etc.) through the work of Usratuna Orthopaedic Workshop.Activity 1.5 Follow up home visits performed by specialized CBID workers, assessing and monitoring the patient and the family conditions.Activity 1.6 Continuous refresher training for physiotherapists and CBID workers over different diseases, syndromes and disabilities, their prevention, detection and action to put in place.Activity 2.1 Support to 5 PHCC/Us in the SMoH network through the supply of drugs and consumables, incentives to health personnel, to guarantee adequate level of health servicesActivity 2.2 Continuous Training to health staff in "Prevention and early detection of disabilities" in order to provide instrument to proper detection, first action and orientation in the referral system for disability cases.Activity 2.3 Awareness sessions in the area of Juba concerning the promotion of right and equalization of opportunities for people with disabilities to live as equal citizens in the community, to have access to health and education servicesActivity 2.4 Sensitization meetings in the Urban IPD Camps involving also the host communities, on the issues of nutrition, STDs, disability and pregnancyActivity 3.1 Prevention of disabilities caused by malnutrition conditions in the children through the nutritional support, at Usratuna PHCC, for children with MAM, and referral of SAM cases to Al Sabah Children Hospital.Activity 3.2 Ante Natal and Post Natal services provided at Usratuna PHCC, aiming to prevent pregnancy related disabilitiesActivity 3.3 Support for PLW through the distribution of vitaminized milkActivity 4.1 Management of epilepsy and Nodding Syndrome cases (first visits and follow up) at the epilepsy unit in Usratuna PHCC, performing EEG, prescribing correct therapy and providing epilepsy drugs.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project aims to provide basic health services to IDPs through the support to the health facilities located next a number urban IDP camps in Juba, granting access to basic health support and giving the possibility to receive also specific support for particular cases, such as children and people with disability, malnourished children etc. through a referral and networking system with other specilized facilities in town.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The action of the project is framed in an MoU with the Jubek Ministry oh Health wich has with OVCI a long-lasting relation. The MoH is involved in the planning, implementation and monitoring of all the activities and committed providing personnel and supplies such as vaccines and other material.OVCI has been present in South Sudan for 35 years and it has a long-lasting commitment in the Country, through the historical collaboration with the Archdiocese of Juba, owner of Usratuna Centre premises and warrant of all the activities implemented.
