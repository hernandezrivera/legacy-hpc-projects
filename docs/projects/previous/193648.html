Project Summary

The unique socio-economic conditions of blockade, with its movement restrictions and destruction of productive capacity, are further compounded by repeated cycles of conflict and political instability, undercutting employment and livelihood opportunities in the Gaza Strip.

The humanitarian needs of its more than 2 million residents were further exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic, the Ukraine Crisis and the military escalation in August 2022 and May 2021, which negatively impacted livelihoods and access to essential services in Gaza.

The HRP 2023 Strategic Objective 3 states that the capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.

UNRWA’s emergency food assistance operation provides a critical humanitarian lifeline to Palestine refugees who depend on food aid to meet their most basic food security needs. Under the 2023 HRP, UNRWA targets 864,096 Palestine refugees, that accounts for over 150,000 Palestine refugee families including families with persons with disabilities and female headed households. In quarterly bases, eligible families receive a food basket that is composed of a range of basic food commodities. The project directly contributes to the HRP 2023 FSS Objective 2: Households suffering from limited access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs by providing direct access to food.
Through its well-established monitoring system, UNRWA will continue to evaluate the food distribution system including the distribution modality that follows strict safety and security measures responding to changing environment around unpredictable situation, while guaranteeing a fair, equal and consistent support to food-insecure Palestine refugees in Gaza.

1 - Needs

Restricted access to land, water, production inputs and markets constrain productive capacities and limit economic opportunities. According to the Palestine Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS), the overall unemployment rate in Gaza was 44.1 per cent during the second quarter of 2022. These trends have a particularly severe impact on vulnerable groups including women and young people. Unemployment rate is especially high among women with 65.0 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2022), facing both lack of opportunities and social barriers to employment in Gaza. Young people between the ages of 15 and 29 are also significantly affected with average unemployment rate of 72 per cent (PCBS, Q2-2022). Despite being highly educated, young people in Gaza face the prospect of unemployment, remaining dependent on already strained families and isolated from the outside world, rather than creating an independent future for themselves. Consistently high unemployment rates and deteriorating purchasing power have eroded many households’ capital and coping mechanisms, rendering the majority of the population of Gaza dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet basic needs. The Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment in 2022 shows that “81.0% of Gaza households reported challenges to being able to afford their household’s basic needs, and 50.5% reported relying on aid and assistance as their primary source of income.” The latest Socio-Economic and Food Security Survey in 2020 shows that “all the determinants of food security suggest poor and worsening conditions for households, which is manifested in the widespread and intensifying food insecurity situation.” The result shows the increase in moderate and severe food insecurity between 2018 and 2020, from 53 percent to 64.4 percent within the 2-year period. Lack of means to afford nutritious and healthy food and significant consumption gap due to the uneven economic recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic and widespread supply chain disruption caused by the Ukraine/Russia crisis are among the main drivers of food insecurity (HNO 2023). In April 2022, the United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) published its food price index and noted that food prices are 34% higher than this time last year. Today, 81.5 per cent of individuals in Gaza, 71 per cent of whom are Palestine refugees, live below the national poverty line (UNRWA 2022, https://www.unrwa.org/gaza15-years-blockade). According to the MSNA 2022 results, 73.2% of Gaza households reported having received humanitarian assistance in the 6 months prior to the MSNA data collection, of which 93.7% were food assistance, yet 1.19 million people in Gaza are identified as in need of humanitarian assistance (HNO 2023). It proves that UNRWA’s food assistance is a vital element of social safety for Palestine refugees living in Gaza as the population would be in even deeper poverty without UNRWA support. It also contributes to wider market and food security under economic blockade and amidst widespread poverty. Persistently high levels of food insecurity in Gaza are not due to the unavailability of basic commodities in the local market, but rather to households’ lack of economic access to food, which, if unaddressed, would have lasting consequences on both the extent and severity of food insecurity. In 2023, while the target under the HRP plan is to meet the needs of 864,096 Palestine refugees in Gaza, UNRWA’s emergency response targets up to 1.2 million refugees, reflecting the broader collective impact of this sustained relief, which is a vital intervention for the stability in the blocked enclave in ensuring the most basic food needs are met and mitigating the risk of market volatility.

2 - Activities

UNRWA will provide unified food basket to all eligible Palestine refugees physically present in Gaza, with certain exclusions around employment status and stable income. The food basket is composed with basic food commodities – wheat flour, rice, sunflower oil, sugar, whole milk, lentils and chickpeas. The food basket will be distributed on quarterly bases. While all Palestine refugees who meet the specific criteria are eligible to receive food assistance, approximately a half of the beneficiaries benefiting from the food assistance are women. The beneficiaries are estimated to include 197,896 girls, 207,746 boys, 230,091 women, and 228,363 men, including 32,213 elderly and 33,844 persons with disabilities. Under the supervision of Relief and Social Services Programme (RSSP), trained social workers make systematic home visits to the beneficiaries including specific cases of wives from polygamous marriage, divorced women and separated or abandoned women to guarantee that they receive assistance fairly with dignity. Particular attention is given by the social workers to the identification and assistance of “abandoned” women and their children who, beside social stigma, are also exposed to specific risks such as not receiving any regular support from the husband and often have to rely on ad-hoc and weak social networks. UNRWA makes sure that vulnerable families do not fall through the system and are able to receive assistance independently. UNRWA will continue to ensure that food assistance is provided under safe conditions, incorporating enhanced hygiene and crowd control measures at UNRWA distribution points. An increased number of distribution points from 10 to 15 will continue to be in place in 2023 and the strict appointment system, implemented as a COVID-19 prevention measure, is now standard practice to limit the number of refugees attending at any one time. In this regard, families were provided with specific dates and time slots through SMS messages. In 2023, UNRWA is ready to rapidly adjust its operations to the local context and the evolving epidemiological situation in the Gaza Strip. The impact of COVID-19 and the Ukraine Crisis have largely affected global food prices and logistics costs. In response, the Agency will continue to use its global and local procurement channels to provide high quality food in the most cost-effective manner. Increased funding being sought for this intervention in 2023 is related to disruptions in the global supply chain and increasing commodity costs. Through its well-established monitoring and evaluation system, UNRWA will continue to monitor the prices of food commodities in the market, will implement quality assurance measures, and will actively engage with refugees to inform future planning to ensure continuous improvement in food assistance operations.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

The Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Unit, in coordination with the other implementing units, prepares internal quarterly food monitoring distribution reports including number of families received food, quality of food assistance, beneficiaries’ feedback, and any obstacles encountered. The reports are sent to the Agency’s management for information, evaluation and corrective action as required. Monitoring and evaluation reports feed into informing planning for Food assistance intervention. The collection of beneficiaries’ feedbacks through both UNRWA M&E and RSSP units contributes to continuously improve the food distribution mechanism at different levels.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

UNRWA Relief Social Services Programme (RSSP) regularly conducts focus groups to discuss key items such as the quality of the food assistance, the quantity of the food basket and the process of food distribution. UNRWA-RSSP has a complaint system in place to receive beneficiaries’ feedback. An appeal mechanism as well as a committee feedback system are also in place for the families who were confirmed “not-eligible” from the intervention based on the results of the criteria. Anyone can access to RSSP area offices across the Gaza Strip. UNRWA RSSP replies to the families regarding their status based on their complaint or appeal. UNRWA’s Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) unit is also involved through regularly conducting interviews with beneficiaries across all distribution centres as well as other distribution points, beneficiaries’ homes, or via phone calls, to collect their feedback regarding the quality of the received commodities, the behavior of the staff in charge of distributing the food and any other issues related to their satisfaction.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNRWA has a multitude of options to receive feedback and ensure accountability to the affected population, including in-person reception in all UNRWA installations, a targeted online and telephone complaints portal (nominal and anonymous), a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints; a centralized (Amman) confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints. Direct (in person) and indirect (press, social media, etc) outreach is further undertaken to disseminate the information on the above mechanisms.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a specific obligation to mainstream protection across its services and operations, as well as made a clear commitment to protect and uphold Palestinian refugees’ rights. Distribution centres and social services are regularly monitored against the four main protection mainstreaming pillars, as defined by the Global Protection Cluster and echoed by UNRWA, the assessed principles are: (i) Prioritize safety, dignity and do no harm; (ii) Meaningful access to services; (iii) Accountability; (iv) Participation and empowerment of refugees. During the food distribution cycle, UNRWA staff based at the food distribution centers including social workers and M&E teams, ensure that the targeted beneficiaries receive their food assistance with dignity and respect. In addition, regular monitoring work is conducted to collect beneficiaries’ feedback on key items of the programme e.g. the quality of the food distribution process, the content of the food basket, the overall quality of the food distribution or the behavior of the staff involved in the food distribution. These feedbacks help to continuously improve the intervention.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is targeting all eligible Palestine refugees with food assistance, without any discrimination. Information to beneficiaries, eligibility criteria and other services are available to all refugees where application and claim can be made to Relief and Social Services Programme offices, often located in the same area of the distribution centres. Location of distribution centres are following safety measures, especially for women and girls, recognizing that catchment area, distance and potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. The distribution takes into consideration specific vulnerability concerns and adopted specific SOPs to provide meaningful access and prioritize assistance to vulnerable groups (abandoned women, pregnant women, persons with disabilities, elderly, etc.). A follow up system is also in place whenever assistance is not received in order to identify and follow specific concerns and potential protection cases. Furthermore, distribution centres are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. Staff in the distribution centres, whom is regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection and gender mainstreaming, are able to refer specific protection cases to social services for follow up. A crowd control system is in place and a specific calendar is set up to avoid and limit potential incidents and/or disturbance, especially amidst COVID-19 emergency. In order to ease the process for targeted persons with disabilities, there is a delegation system in place that enables those people to delegate one of their family members to receive the food assistance on their behalf. The delegation is set during the home visits of the social workers and following their reports’ recommendations.
