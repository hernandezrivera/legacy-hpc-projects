Project Summary

Meet the lifesaving food requirements of people in South Sudan who are facing food security crises, including those living in (ex)-POCs and IDP camps, and those otherwise affected by conflict, natural disaster and/or seasonal food insecurity.

WFP will provide general food distributions (GFD) to the most food insecure people in South Sudan. At the field level, food assistance will be overseen by the WFP Field Offices and implemented by NGO partners or provided through the Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism (IRRM). IRRM is a Delivering as One approach, in which UNICEF supports WASH and nutrition activities, FAO provides emergency seeds and livelihood kits, and WFP provides food assistance and common services through a comprehensive and collaborative humanitarian intervention by deploying mobile teams, supported by cooperating partners to reach populations facing the most urgent needs in the most inaccessible areas of the South Sudan.

Complementary to food distributions, WFP will seek to offer value-added solutions (GFD+), in response to needs and priorities identified by the community. Through GFD+, WFP together with its cooperating partners and other stakeholders will provide unconditional opportunities, identified through a community-based, people-centric participatory approach to targeted beneficiaries. GFD+ adds value to emergency food assistance with a focus on solutions that address the root causes of food and nutrition insecurity and better equip beneficiaries to fight hunger. The GFD+ activity type, duration, frequency, and participation will be catered to specific local sites and may range from COVID-19 awareness and mitigation measures to nutrition education, dyke rehabilitation and communal asset management, income-generating activities, or other solution.

Mitigation measures to reduce and prevent the potential the spread of COVID-19 have been incorporated into standard operating procedures for all interactions with beneficiaries, including training of partners and stakeholders, increasing general awareness among partners and beneficiaries, enforcing social distancing, promoting handwashing, disinfecting sites, reducing the frequency of distribution cycles, and mandating the utilization of personal protective equipment (PPE). These measures are mainstreamed into all WFP activities and are part of the ‘new way of working’.

1 - Needs

The dire situation in South Sudan is triggered by unresolved conflicts at national and sub-national level, recurrent natural disasters, compounded by climate change and poor disaster management capacity. In 2020, several acute crises hit South Sudan, further aggravating the pre-existing protracted crises and creating an unprecedented humanitarian situation: a desert locust upsurge, several episodes of flooding, and the COVID-19 pandemic. Violence continues to be one of the main drivers of severe food insecurity in South Sudan. Despite the signature of the Revitalized Agreement for the Resolution of Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) in September 2018 which ended the unrestricted war, the political situation and conflict dynamics are fragile. Active fighting continues with incidents of subnational conflict. More than one million people were affected by flooding in the second half of 2020. Of which, more than 450,000 individuals were displaced, with women and children most affected. Over 380 schools were affected by the floods and a third of them were occupied by IDPs. Economic shocks, high food prices, and local currency depreciation, exacerbated by the global COVID-19 pandemic, impact food availability and household access to food, thereby increasing the need for humanitarian food assistance. The South Sudanese Pound (SSP) continued to lose its purchasing power against the US dollar. During the second week of November 2020 the SSP exchanged from SSP 500 in Maban to the highest rate of SSP 600 per US dollar in Juba and Wau parallel markets. In Jonglei State, the exchange rate was ranging from 540 to 550 SPP per US dollar. Overall, the exchange rate depreciated in the range of 2 - 14 percent. Following the removal of subsidies by the Sudan government, petrol and diesel prices have continued to increase in northern parts of South Sudan where fuel supplies come through informal cross border trade. In Jonglei, sorghum prices increased by about 5 percent. According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Real Time Quality Review (RTQR), five counties such as Aweil South, Tonj East, Tonj North, Tonj Sotuh and Akobo have been identified as having populations in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). Moreover, the Famine Review Committee (FRC) concluded that the Western part of Pibor County is classified as IPC Phase 5 (Famine Likely). The country level IPC report released by the Government of the Republic of South Sudan on 18 December 2020 projects that 7.24 million people will be facing severe food insecurity at the height of the 2021 lean season while 1.4 million children are acutely malnourished. It is critical to continue providing life-saving food assistance to support these vulnerable populations to meet their basic food and nutrition needs while ensuring food security remains a driver of peace and security.

2 - Project activities

As per its mandate, WFP concentrates its efforts and resources on addressing critical food and nutrition insecurity and provides nutrition-sensitive food assistance to crisis-affected populations comprising of IDPs, returnees and host communities. It will provide food or CBTs to crisis affected men, women, boys and girls in counties classified by the IPC as phase 5 – catastrophe/famine; phase 4 – emergency; or phase 3 – crisis. Rations will be seasonal or year-round depending on the severity of food insecurity. This is complemented by the current WFP Country Office vision which seeks to address entrenched inequity and isolation, with the goal of “leaving no-one behind” and fostering unified, interconnected and peaceful communities. Alongside its crisis response efforts, WFP seeks to build the resilience of the most vulnerable people and facilitate self-reliance by promoting value-added solutions (GFD+) that gradually reduce dependence and need for protracted humanitarian interventions by transferring knowledge, skills, and tools. GFD+ operationalizes programming along the humanitarian-peace-development nexus, which aligns with national and international priorities for South Sudan and further promotes the pathway towards Sustainable Development Goal 2 to end hunger, achieve food security and improved nutrition, and promote sustainable agriculture. In remote areas, WFP and partners deployed mobile teams to deliver food, nutrition and other basic services through the Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism (IRRM), a partnership among WFP, UNICEF, and FAO and with the support of seven implementing partners.

3 - Project indicators

Indicator 1: % of target population with acceptable food consumption score (FCS) FCS measures dietary diversity, energy and macro and micro value of the food consumed at household level. FCS score will be calculated according to WFP methodology and definition of thresholds. WFP uses PDM and survey/FSNMS for source and method of data collection. The 2021 target is >47. Indicator 2 Consumption-based Coping Strategy Index (Percentage of households with reduced CSI) The Coping Strategies Index (CSI) is a tool that measures what people do when they cannot access enough food. CSI score is to be calculated according to WFP methodology (frequency x weight). Source and method of data collection will be PDM survey/FSNMS at the household level with a representative. Target value for 2021 is <10.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

The needs assessment process includes activities to analyze household food and nutrition security as per WFP standard. Through this process, basic information on geographic and household targeting, estimation of beneficiary numbers and their profile is captured. The needs assessments also contain a market assessment component, which evaluates the functioning and trends of food and labour markets, including their regional integration, supply chains, capacity to respond to an increase in demand, the availability and quality of the food accessible at reasonable and stable prices, and the opportunities for beneficiaries to access them safely. This supports WFP’s decision to intervene in any given context in order to address identified needs and determine the suitability of a market-based response option. Market analysis should be systematically incorporated into the Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Analysis (CFSVA) and the Emergency Food Security Assessment (EFSA). The CFSVA provides baseline information prior to emergencies, while after a shock or series of shocks, the baseline is reassessed via the EFSA to capture the impact. Both assessments analyze the effects of markets on food security and vulnerability to predict possible responses of markets to potential shocks and, most importantly, to indicate the potential role of markets for food security interventions, i.e. to establish the feasibility and suitability of a market based response. If the required market information and analysis are not adequately included in existing WFP analyses or external studies, a complementary, stand-alone market analysis becomes necessary to establish the appropriateness of using in-kind food or cash transfers. All the above, in complementarity with the sectoral assessments (cooperating partners and financial capacities, retailers logistics assessments, security risk, ICT, among others) provide the necessary inputs to determine which is the best modality of assistance for each particular context while identifying related operational risks and associated mitigating measures.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Since 2008 WFP declared an institutional shift from a model of food aid to one of food assistance to improve nutrition and reduce hunger in low and middle income countries. Moreover, WFP strongly supports the Grand Bargain and will continue to play a strategic and constructive role in the implementation of the commitments, in particular through the co-leadership of the cash workstream. Therefore, for WFP, cash transfers are a cost-efficient and effective way of accelerating progress towards the Sustainable Development Goal of Zero Hunger by 2030. Cash transfers are effective where food is available in the markets but beyond people’s financial reach, whereas physical food deliveries help respond better in emergency situations where basic market infrastructure is no longer functioning. For that purpose, WFP conducts in-depth analyses to determine the most effective way, given the specificity of the situation, to achieve its humanitarian objectives. That includes assessments of local market conditions, financial service providers, nutrition requirements, beneficiaries preference, among others. The situation is closely and continuously monitored locally to be in a position to switch modalities if required. Cash transfers enable the people in need to decide what is best for themselves, thus empowering them to make the key decisions in their life. If deployed in the right context, they can improve access to basic goods, contribute to a more diversified diet, improve nutrition as well as reduce negative coping strategies such as selling production assets or taking children out of school to cover essential needs. Furthermore, cash transfers are known to have a multiplier effect on the local economy. By enabling people to purchase essential goods locally, they can help strengthen local markets, encourage small-holder farmers to be more productive and build national capacities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

In 2018, WFP developed and rolled-out its first Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) action plan. WFP will strengthen partner capacity to develop localized PSEA action and establish baselines for food security and livelihoods sector processes and safeguards for sexual exploitation and abuse. WFP is an active participant of the UN and NGO Interagency Taskforce on Protection from SEA with key outputs including the establishment of community-based complaint mechanisms in 15 areas of operation, joint community and stakeholders’ awareness raising and joint risk management audits and solutions. Key mitigation measures include mixed-sex field teams at distributions.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Together with partners, WFP will strengthen communication with most vulnerable members through community-outreach activities. WFP will also ensure that the most vulnerable and marginalized women, men, boys and girls participate and have a voice in the selection and implementation of the programme.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

WFP focuses on strengthening two-way beneficiary feedback and engagement in South Sudan. Specifically, it continues a formal complaints and feedback mechanism (CFM) comprised of community outreach, complaint and feedback helpdesks and a helpline. The implementation of a customer relationship management software enables secure management and analysis of protected data gathered through the feedback systems. WFP has been improving the consistency and quality of information shared with beneficiaries on their entitlements, assistance and on the available complaints and feedback channels. As the primary recipients of WFP’s assistance, women are the main users of the CFM.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Complaints predominantly related to information on distribution dates, ration sizes and change in modalities. The feedback prompts WFP to design an action plan to guide field offices and cooperating partners to strengthen implementation of the CFM, and to put in place periodic reviews to ensure compliance. Exclusion errors in verification and registration processes are often raised, which led WFP to strengthen its authentication process by capturing pictures of beneficiaries and their alternate food collectors.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

As a measure to strengthen WFP's direct linkage to beneficiaries to address gender and protection-related challenges, WFP and DANIDA launched Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) in multiple locations across the country. This initiative will allow outreach to communities to prevent and mitigate GBV, provide clinical and psychosocial care to survivors and support women and girls to know and access their sexual and reproductive health and rights.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

According to the latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) report, some 5.8 million people (48 percent of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity from December 2020 to March 2021. This figure includes around 1.73 million people in IPC Phase 4 and 105,000 people likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. With the onset of the lean season, food security levels will drop. Between April and July 2021, the IPC estimates that 7.2 million people (60 percent of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, with 108,000 people in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity. Hence, WFP will increase rations of food assistance in IPC 5 locations, extend lean season response from 6 to 9 months in the IPC 5 and 4 locations, and increase caseload given that there are population who are left behind. Addressing root causes of food and nutrition insecurity, WFP seeks to address entrenched inequity and isolation, with the goal of “leaving no-one behind”. WFP will employ an agile response, aligned with the continuum of the humanitarian-development-peace nexus, through a combination of unconditional resource transfers and resilience building activities to respond to the diverse needs of the population in IPC 3, 4 and 5 locations.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

In inaccessible areas, WFP and partners deploy mobile teams by air to deliver an integrated package of food, nutrition and other basic services through the Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism, a partnership among WFP, UNICEF and FAO. In more accessible areas, WFP provides assistance through its network of sub-offices and cooperating partners.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism mobile teams including nutritionists are deployed to supervise the screening and treatment of moderate and severe acute malnutrition in children, refer PLW with acute malnutrition to health facilities, and deliver nutrition messages to caretakers and local communities. WFP and partners work together to coordinate and harmonize the emergency response. WFP and UNICEF further improve the scale and quality of nutrition emergency assistance through their Joint Nutrition Response Plan and by using the same partners with extensive experience in service provision, in over 90 percent of nutrition sites. Under Nutrition cluster WFP implements nutrition activities to prevent and treat malnutrition among women and children from resident and internally displaced persons (IDPs) populations. Under the prevention programme, children aged 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) are provided with Ready-to-Use Supplementary Food (RUSF) in areas where Global Acute Malnutrition rates are higher than 10 percent and/or aggravating factors are present. The prevention programme was also part of the seasonal scale-up which was conducted in areas with high levels of food insecurity during the lean season alongside general food distributions. The treatment programme targeted acutely malnourished children aged 6-59 months with RUSF, and acutely malnourished PLWs and people living with HIV and tuberculosis (TB) with Super Cereal Plus both accompanied by nutrition messaging and counselling throughout the country.
