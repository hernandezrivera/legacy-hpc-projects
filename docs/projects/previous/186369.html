Project Summary

CHABASH DEVELOPMENT AND HEALTH INITIATIVE (CDHI) will implement a 12- month intervention aimed at improving resilience and reducing the vulnerability and impact of Gender Based Violence (GBV) on populations affected by conflict in North-East Nigeria. Through this project, CDHI will target 11,000 women, girls, men and boys in 4 Local Government Areas (LGAs) of Borno and Adamawa State with integrated GBV and Livelihood assistance. Project interventions will directly contribute to the three key strategic objectives of the GBV sub-sector of the Nigeria Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2021 and contributes to strategic objective (SO) 1 of the Early recovery sector – stabilization of livelihood and income recovery.
The main targeted outcomes of this project are:
- Increased knowledge of and capacity for self-protection and recovery from GBV.
- Strengthened formal and informal community-based protection mechanisms and services.
- Increased knowledge and skills of survivors and individuals at risk of GBV for generating income.
- Reduced barriers to income generation opportunities through the establishment of village savings and loans association for business continuity and sustainability
To achieve these outcomes, the project will focus on actions that will increase access to prevention and response services for GBV survivors and individuals at risk of GBV. 2,00 GBV survivors will receive survivor centered case management services including referrals for special services including clinical care as well as mental health services. Additionally, activities to improve resilience and coping mechanisms of women, girls, men and boys affected by GBV and fostering social cohesion will target 2,000 people through community-based psychosocial support provided in safe spaces that will be supported by the project. CDHI through this project targets to provide case management services to 200 identified GBV survivors.
Livelihood support in the form of income generation and skills training will reach 50 survivors or individuals at risk of GBV. Village Savings and Loan association will be nested in the IGA activities where individual at risk of GBV engaged in income generating activities are grouped into VSLA associations to promote saving culture for business sustainability and provide opportunities for other vulnerable members to access funds as loan to meet their basic urgent needs. Similarly, 320 women, men, boys, and girls will benefit from life skills and positive parenting sessions using the global adolescent life skill curriculum and parenting curriculum. CDHI will increase local capacity by strengthening community-based mechanisms, through training of community leaders, religious leaders, health workers, security personnel’s on GBV core concept and referral pathway. The project will also increase awareness on the impact of GBV through outreach to 10,000 individuals and will work with communities to identify GBV risks in the targeted communities and develop action plans to mitigate these risks.

1 - Needs

Now in its twelfth year, the conflict in northeast Nigeria continues to disrupt the lives and livelihoods of millions of people across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states. In Nigeria, the operating environment remains extremely volatile, particularly in Borno State, where main supply routes have become dangerous due to risk of attack by non-State Armed Groups (NSAG). The ongoing conflict continued to severely affect millions of people in 2021 and is expected to persist unabated in the next years, subjecting people to displacement, impoverishment, and threats of violence, including sexual violence against women and children. Violent attacks by NSAGs and military counter-operations have displaced men, women, and children, disrupted farming and other livelihoods, and limited the functionality of markets and other basic services. The conflict has devastated the already fragile public infrastructure, including health, education, water and sanitation and other public services capacities to protect women and children in fragile humanitarian settings. With over 8.7 million people in need according to 2021 HNO. The northeast situation is considered a major protection crisis with formidable needs. Women and girls are under threat of violence, abduction, and rape. Gender-based violence (GBV) including sexual violence, forced and child marriages are constantly reported and are attributed to the conflict, insecurity, and poor living conditions in IDP camps and informal settlement (HRP, 2021). Girls and young women are facing increased risk to GBV, including sexual violence, exploitation, early pregnancy and forced marriage in North-East Nigeria. The average age at sexual debut is roughly 15 years of age amongst adolescent mothers in Nigeria (NDHS 2003, 2008, 2013). And adolescent fertility rate is 122 births per 1,000 women aged 15-19 years. A wide range of sexual violence has taken place and people abducted by Boko Haram, survivors of rape, and children born out of sexual violence face stigmatization and are subsequently ostracized. The culture of silence, stigma and blaming women and girls who have experienced GBV is a factor contributing to the under-reporting of GBV cases; this is further exacerbated by the lack of trust in the current system. Gender norms and socioeconomic inequalities severely undermine the role of women in the community and society. In many affected communities, sexual exploitation, rape, assault, and threats of GBV while conducting daily activities such as collecting firewood and water, using latrines, traveling to nearby communities etc, has been reported by women and girls to be a time when they are most at risk to physical and sexual violence in a focus group discussion with women and girls. Additionally, focus group discussions with adolescent girls revealed an increasing number of adolescent girl’s menstrual hygiene and other reproductive needs were unmet due to the prevailing insecurity and a spike in poverty level aggravated by COVID – 19 pandemic which had adverse effects for female-headed households. UNHCR’s Vulnerability Screening shows an increase in female-headed households, with 44% of them widows in deep field locations, giving rise to susceptibility to sexual exploitation. Over 70% of vulnerable IDPs lack sufficient livelihood, while in deep field location in Borno, all vulnerable displaced households reported the need for livelihood assistance for daily sustenance. Those staying in displacement sites often live in congested shelters or isolated, insecure or inhospitable areas, and are therefore susceptible to all forms of exploitation and abuse.

2 - Activities

Conduct awareness and sensitization sessions on SRHR for women and girls of reproductive age. CDHI will reach 1000 women and girls with information and education on SRHR, including sexual health, family planning, healthy non-violent relationships, and reproductive health. During the initial period of the project, CDHI will link up with health facilities where possible to conduct sessions on SRH during activities with project beneficiaries. Where this is not possible, CDHI will liaise with the Ministry of Health and UNFPA to provide training for Case Workers and community volunteers who will engage with project beneficiaries during these sessions. Conduct community outreach services targeting community members to raise awareness on GBV prevention and response, services and to facilitate confidential references to GBV services: CDHI will use different platforms including community meetings to conduct sensitization and raise awareness on gender equality, and prevention and response to SGBV, including women and girls’ economic engagement as a protective strategy. The sensitization sessions will target 10,000 individuals including adolescents’ girls. Existing or developed IEC and BCC materials will be used during awareness sessions. Methods of community outreach may include materials distribution, and group sessions with men and women. Establish and/or strengthen the capacity of community-based protection committees and link them with community-based service providers and Case Workers to facilitate appropriate referral and support prevention activities: CDHI will conduct community-based structures mapping exercises to identify existing protection structures and their current capacity to contribute to the prevention and response of GBV. Where community-based protection structures exist, e.g., protection committees or women’s groups, CDHI will strengthen their capacity through training and follow up support. In communities where these groups or structures do not exist, CDHI will mobilize the community to establish them. The community-based protection structures will be supported with materials and key messages to enable them to facilitate awareness sessions and engage in community level advocacy initiatives. Establish and/or strengthened safe spaces to provide lifesaving, age appropriate GBV case management and PSS services to survivors and those at risk of GBV: CDHI will strengthened 4 safe space to facilitate access to information as well as PSS for male and female survivors and those at risk of GBV. The safe spaces will provide a hub to receive information on a range of issues, including the availability of GBV, MHPSS and SRH services in their communities. CDHI will set up help desk where individuals will be able speak to Case Workers on safety issues, GBV related concerns in a private and confidential manner. Activities of the safe space will be guided by UNFPA’s women and girls’ safe space guidelines. Provide individual survivor-centered case management support to GBV survivors: A survivor-centered approach to GBV seeks to empower the survivor by ensuring access to appropriate, accessible and good quality services such as health care, psychological and social support, security, livelihood and legal services. CDHI through the safe space, will provide case management services, safe referrals to other support services, community-driven risk identification and mitigation activities, information dissemination (including key messages about content and availability of services and SRH education). CDHI targets to reach 200 GBV survivors. GBV survivors and individuals at risk of GBV will be selected and trained in business enterprise, financial management and Village savings and Loan Association: The beneficiaries will be provided a business counseling session, basics of financial management and skills assessment to match individuals with potential IGAs. Selected beneficiaries will also be trained on VSLA model

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

CDHI will focus on training all its staff, committees and stakeholders involved in programme delivery activities on the four key pillars of protection mainstreaming which includes: - - Prioritize safety & dignity, and avoid causing harm: prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of your intervention which can increase people's vulnerability to both physical and psycho-social risks - Meaningful Access: arrange for people’s access to assistance and services - in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services. – Accountability: set-up appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints – Participation and empowerment: support the development of self-protection, capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including - not exclusively - the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education. The training will seek to increase knowledge and consciousness of staff on ensuring these principles are part and parcel of design and implementation of activities. A key outcome of these trainings is the identification of protection risks within the frame of planned GBV prevention activities and how to mitigate these risks. CDHI intends to have a protection mainstreaming budget line within this project to address these risks. CDHI dedicated GBV/PSS officer who is 100% on this project will ensure that identified risks will be put into consideration in program implementation and mitigation measures established and strengthened. There will be a protection mainstreaming budget line which will remain flexible to address any form of protection related issue identified. CDHI will integrated in its sensitization messages mechanism to report issues related to sexual exploitation and Abuse. CDHI have and internal system of PSEA. Those systems and mechanism will be made available to the communities. Community members will also have the complaint box, and a dedicated community level help desk which could be utilized in raising concerns of SEA or suggestions on how to prevent SEA. CDHI ensures that its programming is informed by needs; CDHI will continually conducts assessments using qualitative approaches such as focus group discussions, key informant interviews and in-depth community discussions to remain informed on the need’s communities have and these needs feed into the program design and response. In addition to this, CDHI is guided by the principle of neutrality in responding to needs and ensuring communities understand that CDHI staff are neutral and trustworthy bound by strict principles of confidentiality and integrity. CDHI employs a consultative approach to supporting communities. Feedback about the project design, implementation and monitoring continually engages communities to give their views, opinions and recommendations. These then inform all programmatic actions thereon. CDHI protection staff (funded by this project) sole’s responsibility will be to ensure the highest level of protection and gender is well mainstreamed in the project implementation.

9.1a - Specify (and give URL link if possible to) the relevant needs assessment document.

Gender Based Violence posed a direct threat to women and adolescent health as well as strain on the socioeconomic development of any community. Evidence and experience have shown that conflict exacerbate the impact of GBV as in the case of North East Nigeria where traditional gender roles have shifted often putting women and girls at greater risk of GBV. Communities continue to practice harmful traditional norms and practices that continue to put women and girls in a disadvantage position. SRHR remains a major concern for women of childbearing age especially adolescent girls. About one Nigerian woman dies every 10 minutes from pregnancy related conditions - deaths that could be prevented if women and girls had access to education and reproductive health services, including family planning. The risk of death is heightened in humanitarian crises that displace populations and undermine community social support mechanisms, limiting access to health facilities and workers to take care of women's and girls' special needs. In Borno, Yobe and Adamawa, it is estimated that 53% of IDPs are women and girls with majority of childbearing age. Child marriage occurs more frequently among girls who are the least educated, poorest and lives in rural areas. Lack of information and education on available SRH services will prevent women and girls from accessing services. Additionally, traditional gender norms and preference for more children by men, put women at great risk of complications related to SRH especially within the humanitarian context. Assessments conducted by different organizations in North-East Nigeria since the beginning of the Boko Haram insurgency continue to reveal the increase in incidences of Gender Based Violence and its impact on the population. Survivors of GBV especially those abducted by Boko Haram insurgent group and their children born out of the sexual violence continue to face social isolation and stigma adding on the psychosocial distress.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

As an integral part of CDHI’s operations in the NE humanitarian context, CDHI will endeavours to deliver the planned integrated GBV prevention and risk mitigation, SRH and livelihood interventions to crisis affected population in a way that meets priorities of all women, men, girls, and boys without compromising their protection. This will includes involving the communities in decisions related to the program delivery to ensure that activities are implemented in a safe and accessible locations. This will includes engaging the communities to decide where, when and how to deliver and access GBV/SRHR preventive services and as well GBV specialized response to survivors or individuals at risk of GBV. CDHI will ensure the inclusions of individuals with any form of disability into the program intervention and ensures that their needs are prioritized. Beneficiaries with any form of disability will be identified and prioritized in the programme implementation activities. CDHI will ensure an established complaint and feedback mechanism through a complaint box strategically located in the communities as well as established help desk to provide opportunities for members of the community to make complaints and provide feedback to CDHI. Those will be consolidated and used by programme managers to improve CDHI’s programme delivery at the project communities.
