Project Summary

The project tackles the three objectives; in terms of advocacy and enhancing documentation of education violations, in addition to enhancing safe and equitable access to inclusive and quality services, and lastly supporting preparedness and resilience during emergencies. In 2021, the Education Cluster recorded 62 education-related incidents affecting 3,200 students. These attacks on education took place when most of the country was shut down or the first month of the 2021-2022 school year. The war on Gaza and the continued blockade increased poverty and unemployment rates, lack of resources, protection concerns and disrupted children’s access to safe education. In WB and Jerusalem, the continued threat of demolitions, the increased attacks on schools located in Area C and H2, and the arrests of children at checkpoints continue to impede access to quality and safe education for children. All of these factors put children at huge risk of losing access to safe education with increased possibilities of drop-out and/or at the risk, especially CWDs and children living under poor conditions . Education related incidents have a harmful impact on students’ wellbeing and academic achievements. According to the “Danger is our Reality” report published by SCI in 2020, children identified many incidents that make them feel unsafe at school and deny them their right to access quality and safe education. In this report, children shed new light on how these threats affect their emotional wellbeing, sense of security, ability to learn, relationship with family and teachers, and how they feel about their futures. These boys and girls will need direct interventions, including psychosocial support, to mitigate the impact of attacks, and other interventions / referrals according to the type of attack. Teachers and school staff are also affected and need special skills and knowledge to deal with the situation on the ground. Gender sensitivity is needed by caregivers in order to support without inflicting unintentional harm, due to the cultural and political sensitivities in these areas.

Therefore, SCI will focus on supporting Safe Back to School by addressing barriers, and through supporting 17,000 vulnerable and underserved girls and boys, directly and indirectly, and 400 teachers, counselors and supervisors through supporting their continued access to quality education, psychosocial support, improved emergency and adaptive infrastructure in schools. In addition to extending support to MoE and UNRWA teachers on remote learning modalities., it will support conflict-affected students with disabilities with assistive devices, educational kits and IT devices to ensure their equal access to inclusive quality learning opportunities. Furthrmore supporting 400 parents on self-care techniques, stress release, and supporting children in times if emergencies. SC will also support access through facilitating transportation for students in risky areas based on needs communicated by MoE. In addition to supporting incident reporting and documentation of violations, and establishing prepared teams in the communities affected that are equipped with First Aid knowledge and documentation that can escort students to schools to ensure their safety. These will also be coordinated with MoE.
SC intends to focus on 40 schools in risky areas to be identified by MoE, in WB/G including EJ. Focus will also be on schools in EJ. SC will focus on communities surrounding the schools to ensure safe access.

1 - Needs

The project aims to address the following issues: - MHPSS effects of occupation violations on children and educational staff in risky areas. - Poor access of students with disabilities and learning difficulties in remote and risky areas to means for learning. - Insufficient incident reporting and documentation. - Poor infrastructure, sanitation and adaptations in schools and KGs in risky areas of the WB (including EJ) and in Gaza. - International Advocacy. For the first six months of 2021, children were either on a distance learning modality or they were missing entire school days. In 2020, the Education Cluster recorded 119 education-related incidents affecting 7757 students and 615 teachers and school staff as per the HNO. Although the numbers have gone down in 2021, where the Education Cluster recorded 62 education-related incidents affecting 3,200 students, this is in no way a reflection of the continued violation against school children. These attacks on education took place when most of the country was shut down or the first month of the 2021-2022 school year. The war on Gaza and the continued blockade increased poverty and unemployment rates, lack of resources, and protection concerns. In WB and Jerusalem, the continued threat of demolitions, preventing rehabilitation or building of schools in area C, and EJ in particular, the increased attacks on schools located in Area C and H2, and the arrests of children at checkpoints impede access to education for children. These left schools unsafe for children in terms of access and infrastructure and was documented by HNO and ECW. On top of the routine occupation violations, 2021 witnessed another layer of escalation. The war on Gaza, the heightened tensions and violations in EJ, and Covid-19 added additional layers of vulnerabilities to school aged children in oPt. All of these factors put children at huge risk of losing safe access to education, more trauma, increasing the possibilities of drop-out, especially CWDs and children living under poor conditions. Education related incidents have a harmful impact on students’ wellbeing and academic achievements. All this was documented in the HNO. Students have to walk long distances in area C to reach schools, through unsafe roads. Boys are usually more affected by occupation violations especially in terms of detention and physical attacks. According to the “Danger is our Reality” report published by SCI in 2020, children identified many incidents that make them feel unsafe at school and deny them their right to access quality and safe education. In this report, children shed new light on how these threats affect their emotional wellbeing, sense of security, ability to learn, relationship with family and teachers, and how they feel about their futures. They described feelings of fear, anxiety and stress on their way to/from school and during their presence at school, with physical and emotional symptoms including uncontrollable shaking, fainting, loss of self-confidence, and despair. Attacks on schools in high-risk areas affect boys and girls living in these areas. These boys and girls will need direct interventions, including psychosocial support, to mitigate the impact of attacks, and other interventions/referrals according to the type of attack. Teachers and school staff are also affected and need special skills and knowledge to deal with the situation on the ground. Gender sensitivity is needed by caregivers in order to support without inflicting unintentional harm, due to the cultural and political sensitivities in these areas. SC’s HH Assessment on OOSC reported higher rates of functional limitations than in-school children, signifying a need for access modifications to reach the most vulnerable, especially those currently excluded from education. The average age of CWD dropping out of school is 11.85-directly linked to stigmas, poorly equipped schools, a lack of specialized materials, and a lack of economic means to support transportation. Schools across oPt suffer from poor learning environments. Per ECW’s Baseline report, 52% of schools have no internet connection, 23% have no playgrounds, and around 150 schools serving around 30,000 children lack adequate WASH facilities in WBG. UNDP’s EJ Schools’ survey revealed that almost 59% of schools in EJ have no playgrounds, libraries, or science labs. Around 40% of schools lack adequate functioning drinking water fountains. CwDs, are the most affected by the deteriorating situation. Self-care and vision (9.51% & 7.69%) were seen as statistically significant functional limitations for children. UNDP’s survey indicated schools can also be risky for students with other disabilities, such as those with visual impairments. Close to 35% of the assessed vulnerable schools need additional toilets, and 45 % are in need for adaptations for CWDs. Families faced double challenges related to the unavailability of devices, poor internet connectivity, and power cuts. Children with learning difficulties and CwDs suffered as well. Just over 70% of households in Gaza have at least one child not accessing any distance education program as per the “The impact of COVID-19 on education in Palestine, Lebanon and Jordan: Reflections from teachers, students and parents” Study by the centre of Lebanese studies. Almost half (46%) of the households have reported that their children were feeling anxious or stressed about the situation. The child groups that work with SC and DCI documented the issues of access to online education and marginalization of students with disabilities and children from poor socioeconomic backgrounds. SCI, in partnership with DCI and with the support of the child-led groups, conducted a survey during COVID 19 where 50% indicated their need for guidance to relieve boredom and stress, during this period, 41% of children affirmed that psychological and material damage affected all groups.

2 - Activities

All actions are designed to ensure SB2S interventions for the most vulnerable children in the West Bank & Gaza. The project will target 20 schools of the most marginalized and affected schools in each of the locations of WB, GS, and EJ. It will directly target a total of 43,500 children (17,4000 girls and 26.100 boys), whose learning outcomes are affected by high level of distress and low level of motivation resulting from the violence, poor infrastructure, and overcrowding in schools. The intervention will also include training 400 teachers, counselors and supervisors on different issues related to supporting children on remote learning and issues that affect their learning outcome, Psychological first aid, and sessions to support teachers’ wellbeing, PSEAH and self-care. 400 Parents will also be supported on how to deal with children during emergencies, and stress release activities. 800 students (400girls, 400 boys) with learning difficulties, sensorial and physical disabilities will receive assistive devices and educational kits to support their learning. School adaptation will be implemented to support CWDs. Schools/ Governmental KGs in need will also be supported with improved emergency rehabilitation to support children’s learning environment, that are gender sensitive. Referral pathways, for identified/disclosed protection concerned to service providers (health, case management, MHPSS, security/legal etc), will ensure that protection needs identified for specific children are addressed by relevant service providers. This will ensure protection-sensitive approach to project and service delivery. Transportation in these areas will be supported to enhance safe reach to schools. This will take place through supporting rental or maintenance costs in five locations at risk as identified by the MOE. Girls and boys will have equal access to project activities and gender specific needs and risks will be prioritized. All this aims to provide tailored support to vulnerable children by addressing their distinct barriers of being at risk of dropout. SB2S responsive activities will include: 1)Provision of remedial education, remote learning and learning support to vulnerable school aged children and teachers at MoE and UNRWA. This includes provision of remedial education to vulnerable school aged children and support teachers. Project will target teachers, supervisors, counselors and volunteers with Social Emotional Learning classes (SEL). These skills are essential for children’s development, and are directly linked to improved learning outcomes, reducing toxic stress and mitigating interpersonal violence in classrooms. Teachers will be trained on Child Safeguarding, Social Emotional Learning (SEL) approach, PFA, First Aid and Inclusive Education, HEART or PD according to needs assessment. The whole process of teachers’ selection will ensure that identified teachers are properly equipped to support the learning needs of targeted children including CwDs. In addition, teachers will be supported on remote learning processes such as home- based assessments to measure learning outcomes. Teachers will be provided with needed materials and academic training with focus on either English, Arabic, math, or science. Should any change in the context take place, community -based mechanisms and forums can be considered as alternative scenarios in case schools were closed for any reason. The main lesson learnt from the experience of Covid pandemic in 2020 is the need to have caregivers and communities prepared. 2)Provision of essential and basic emergency rehabilitation to schools/ Governmental KGs and construction of Safe Play Areas (SPA) in marginalized and risky locations to ensure that school environments are accessible and safe for students and teachers of different abilities and are gender sensitive. School staff and children will participate in mapping out and identifying key needs, this will be followed with specialized engineering assessment, inter-sectorial coordination between education, protection and infrastructure units will be carried out to highlight main interventions priorities within the intervention scope, with involvement of children and school staff to agree upon final interventions to improve school environment. Save the children will also support the MOE by supporting the maintenance of vehicles to provide safe transportation to schools in risky areas. Locations will be identified by the MOE. 3) Support 800 school-aged children with different abilities with devices and adaptations to ensure their continued access to education. This will also include assistive devices and educational kits to children with disabilities, learning difficulties or multiple disabilities; especially those from poor socioeconomic background to facilitate their retention and transitions into formal school or specialized, certified service providers. 4) Provision of school-based psychosocial support to school staff, students and parents affected by the education-related violations and COVID-19 pandemic. The Action will also include recreational, extracurricular, fun days and trips. Teacher’s wellbeing sessions on self-care, PFA and stress release. Parents sessions on dealing with children during emergencies and selfcare. 5) Advocacy and Media training for children. Children and adolescents are quite advanced in the use of social media. With the support of Save the Children, and it’s partners, we will train children on using various platforms effectively to deliver messages to the global community. There will be three child led advocacy initiatives that focus on safe access to education in risky areas. As per the MOE request, there will also be a strengthening of the MRM/CAAAC documentation in the schools,. Previous needs assessments and consultations with the ministry, partners and beneficiaries will support the design of the final activities.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

SCI will follow a conflict-sensitive approach to ensure timely identification and mitigation of potential negative risks and consequences. SCI will conduct strong community engagement to ensure regular exchange of information, consultation on priorities and preferences and transparency for preventing tensions. Beneficiary selection will be triangulated by field visits, cluster consultation, and local stakeholder information. SC will maintain its independence and impartiality. Staff will not engage in discussions on sensitive political and community issues while working with beneficiaries. SCI will continuously monitor the development of the COVID-19 fragile context and risks. It will analyze the potential interaction between the planned activity, the changing context and potential implications for the relations between stakeholders and communities. SCI`s Social Tensions Tracking and Mapping system will locate geographic areas with communities vulnerable to social tensions. SCI`s strong MEAL approach will identify any emerging harmful trends and activities and introduce course correction to programming as required. SCI will strictly enforce its Child Safeguarding and PSEA policies and processes including support to partners as needed.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

To ensure the participation of the targeted people and the equitable access to the services provided, a series of discussion groups and consultation meetings will be conducted at all project stages with girls, boys, women and caregivers. This will enable communities and schools to raise their concerns and together come up with solutions to ensure that all the interventions do not exacerbate risks or create new risks. SCI considers it imperative that the project is inclusive of individuals living with physical and mental disabilities, especially given that current services outreach of both male and female people with disabilities tends to be limited. The project will prioritize for example PwDs with means to enhance their access to project services. The project gender sensitivity will be integrated and considered as a minimum standard. The project will be open to boys, girls according to the vulnerability criteria, with the option to create girls only working groups to facilitate their participation. SCI, together with project partners, will check during the project visits the problems that girls are facing and that the project activities are designed through gender and child-safeguarding (CSG) lens to prevent any harm by project activities and/or abuse by the family members. Both Men and women will be included in sessions with different interests and fields related mainly to enhancing their protection and building positive resilience and coping strategies. The Gender Advisor will ensure gender mainstreaming in the interventions. SCI’s Field Officer will supervise the implementation in cooperation with the partners to ensure equal access to the project services. Inclusion and equity are dealt with by SCI at two levels; the first through streamlining in existing activities and indicators as part of SCI policy to reach the most marginalized, and the other through promoting specific interventions to serve the children with disabilities in communities.
