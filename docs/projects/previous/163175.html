Project Summary

UNESCO’s Education in Emergencies (EiE) response in North West and South West region of Cameroon is part of its global education strategy aimed at responding to education needs in crises situations, and rapidly improving access to inclusive and safe learning opportunities for vulnerable conflict affected in and out-of-school children. To achieve this, UNESCO proposes to expand its current education in emergencies interventions under the Education Cannot Wait First emergency response and CERF Education projects (ending in July 2020) by capitalizing on the set methodology, tools and facilities to cover higher outreach in the crisis affected communities, and target 92,000 conflict affected children with following actions:
1. Expading the production of learning material to support out-of-school children (for primary levels 4,5,6, and secondary level 2) and distribution in NW/SW, West and Littoral. Under ongoing ECW project targeting NWSW regions, and while applying the Global UNESCO Avicenna Virtual Campus model, UNESCO is producing pre-recorded lessons and internet-based online/offline essential competency-based learning materials, specifically targeting out-of-school children for primary levels 1,2 and 3 and secondary school cycle 1. This project will expand the material production to cover levels 4,5 and 6 in Mathematics, English language, Science & Technology, and Life-skill, as well as Secondary school cycle 1 in Mathematics, English language, Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Life-skills. In order to ensure that learning materials are culturally, socially and linguistically relevant and appropriate to the context of NW and SW regions, UNESCO will continue to apply Universal grade level competency standards in defining the profile and needs of learners, as well as mastery of competencies developed in each subject area. Design and production of learning materials is led by renowned academicians in the field of Pedagogy and Information, Communication and Technology, from UNESCO Avicenna Virtual Campus Network. In order to facilitate and expand distribution of these learning materials to the communities and learning spaces, UNESCO will partner with local organizations and Buea University, to train local facilitators, with pedagogical background, on how to sue the materials and how to assist out-of-school children in self-learning learning process.
The production and distribution of additional learning materials in native languages will be initiated, to further boost and facilitate access to basic education, especially for primary level conflict affected children. Target 75,000.
2. Increase access to formal education for vulnerable out-of-school children and youth in NW/SW, West and Littoral: UNESCO will facilitate access to formal education for vulnerable out-of-school children through disbursement of capitation grants to selected schools (contingent upon deduction of school fees) targeting 16,600 out of school children in NWSW Regions and IDPs in West and Littoral. Selection of schools is guided by the Cameroon Education Cluster standards, aiming at supporting schools that are operating at a significantly reduced enrolment rate to enable access to education to those who are unable to pay fees, while assuring that student teacher ratio does not exceed 60:1. To complement this activity, school principals and head teachers will receive training in leadership and financial management, and psychological support programmes.
3. Resilience geared vocational training for adolescents (aged 15-17y): Additionally, UNESCO in partnership with Reach Out Youth Organization proposes to support 150 (80 female, 70 male) IDP youth gain market skills through vocational training to enable them gain employment to improve their livelihoods.
4. Evidence-based advocacy on protection of education from attacks and Safe School Declaration implementation in NW/SW Regions: UNESCO strengthen its contribution to the establishment of a reporting mechanism for.

Implementing Partners

UNESCO in NWSW regions will continue to work with the current local partners and academic institutions and the partnership will be expanded in to West and Littoral Regions as follows:
• Buea University- UNESCO will build on the current partnership with Buea University that also hosts the desk for the UNESCO chair in the region, where the Avicenna Centre is based, with hosting of open source platform for online education, train teachers on pedagogic engineering and technologies of production of online courses.
• CARITAS and AMEF are local organizations with strong community outreach, presence and activities on the ground. CARITAS, with the Catholic Church affiliation has the advantage of the vast church network in the communities that UNESCO will work with to support the non-formal learning centers. These two organizations will focus on both North and South West Regions.
• UNESCO will partner with the 6 Education secretaries that manage the private confessional schools namely: The Catholic Secretariat, the Islamic school’s secretariat, the Baptist school’s secretariat, and the Presbyterian school’s secretariat. These secretariats will be in charge of Monitoring the use of the capitation grants,
• UNESCO will partner with Reach Out, a local organization that is supporting out of school youth through vocational training.

Besoins / Needs

The education sector in NWSW regions has been severely disrupted by the on- going conflict as it continues to be a soft target, with the NSAG’s rejecting the government-based education system and directing all the schools in North and South West Regions to closed. Students, teachers and other education personnel who do not obey this directive are attacked, and some have been reportedly killed. The present project is based on the outcomes of the Needs Assessments being finalized in cooperation with the UNESCO Chair in Buea University, as well as on the analysis and assessment conducted at community level in view of the implementation of the ECW and CERF 2nd allocation 2019 projects, in addition to the ED Cluster comprehensive assessment and its strategy (2019). Since 2016, more than 80% of schools were closed. Hundreds of thousands of school aged children are denied education including more than 855,000 who have lost access to education due to school closure in the North West and South West (NW&SW) regions of Cameroon (Cameroon Education Cluster Strategy 2020, Draft October 2019). This is due to escalation of tensions, armed clashes between Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) and government forces, and significant violence against the civilian population, including children, since October 2016. School children, teachers, schools, learning centres and teaching facilities are on the frontline of violence. Classrooms are destroyed; not just because, they are caught in the crossfire but because they are targeted by the combatants. Young girls, living in the NW&SW areas, are subject every day to the threat widespread, systemic violence including rape, assault and other forms of sexual violence. Children are abducted. They are forced to military service. Number of operational schools in NWSW is alarmingly low, as per the UNESCO Risk and Acceptability assessment for October 2019 and MENA OCHA from August 2019, the data is showing that in NW Division of NGO-Ketunjia out of 242 registered schools, none are is operational, while in SW Division of Meme out of 379 schools only 5 are operational. And these divisions host the highest number of IDPs. The data analysis reflected that over 80% of IDP children do not attend school, and the top three reasons indicated are insecurity (fear), schools are closed and no teachers. School delegate office in Bamenda have reported that there are 555 registered secondary schools in the North-West Region, about 120 regularly open their doors and only 54 schools have staffs and students. The other 61 only have staffs who open school doors but no students. The expected student enrollment in the region is 107,117 students but only 25,755 students (highest number since the beginning of the school year, September 2019) are attending classes, giving only 24.05% enrolment. A total of 1,229 internally displaced students have been identified in the functional schools and are in dire need of support. Additionally, the IDP’s from the NW and SW Regions have also moved to neighbouring border regions in the West and Littoral, where the most affected divisions are Bamboutos, Menoua and Mifi in the West region; as well as Wouri and Moungo in the Littoral region (reference MENA OCHA, August 2019). These regions are dominantly Francophone and therefore with almost no Anglophone schools that are operations, even publish and private schools are overcrowded with IDP children in search of continuing their education. Because of their proximity to the Kupe-Manegumba and Meme divisions in South-West, the Moungo and Wouri divisions are the most affected by the influx of IDPs in Littoral. Similarly, in the West Region, divisions of Bamboutos, Menoua, Noun and Mifi have hosted the majority of IDPs. the functioning of basic social infrastructures has been affected. Schools of the English-speaking subsystem of education are overcrowded.
