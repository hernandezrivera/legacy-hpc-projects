Project Summary

The proposed project is an Emergency protection response project for IDPs in Sasiga woreda, East Wollega zone and in Moyale woreda, Borena Zone, Oromia region, Ethiopia to address the critical protection needs of those displaced vulnerable individuals through prospective funding opportunities.

The Multi-sectoral Desk Review of Humanitarian Situation report for Wollega and Kamashi zones by December 2021 showed that there are greater than 200k IDPs in Wollegas , of which around 150 k is in East Wollega and more than 100k IDPs in Kamashi who require critical humanitarian assistance. These displacements are due to the intercommunal conflict in the regions which results in fear and protection issues such as family separation, GBV, freedom of movement, HLP-lack of access to agricultural land, lack of access to basic services: food, water, and sanitation, health, and nutrition, shelter, non-food items. The high prevalence of CP/GBV cases is the main protection concern in these areas as most of these survivors are not able to access services due to accessibility issues coupled with the limited presence of CP-GBV partners.

According to the Meher/Hagaya non-food assessment report from November 2021. During the rain condition in Borena zone of Oromia region in the last 2021 Belg was less in amount, erratic in distributions, sever long dry spell and early withdrawal. Meher season rain was also completely absent. Altogether loss of rain in both seasons has led to the widespread loss of livestock, pasture, and vegetables. Therefore, since these people rely on their livestock for survival including economic and social benefit the starvation of this livestock have significantly affected the livelihood of the general community. From the general community, the women, children and those with disabilities are highly burdened. Women and young girls are forced to travel a long distance to fetch water which is exposing them to gender-based violence. A significant number of young children are participating in child labor to win their daily bread for themselves and their families. According to the need, assessment are more than 13,980 children in need of various supports out of which 150 are street children who are moving from rural areas to urban to search for food. In the Borena zone and the number is increasing day today since there is a flow of children from rural to urban for searching food.

As part of the protection response, FIDO is working towards creating community awareness on CP/GBV, conducting awareness creation on positive coping mechanisms to enhance IDPS physical and mental health, improving child protection case management systems including family tracing and reunification. Access will also be created to MHPSS and preventive intervention and protection response to improve resilience to gender-based violence (GBV). The project will also facilitate capacity building of woreda WoCYA, Social and Labor Affairs, and other responsible stakeholders and IDPs to identify, prevent and respond to CP/GBV cases. FIDO is also creating a transparent platform to address crosscutting issues such as accountability to the affected population (AAP).COVID-19 awareness creation is integrated with the project activities to prevent infection and the spread of the virus and increase vaccine awareness.

1. - Projects activities

Establish and train two community-based child protection committees containing 40 members (20 from each woreda) in the target areas on the basic sites of CPiE, PFA, GBV, and prevention response. Conduct two days of training for 60 foster/caregivers and biological parents on positive disciplining kills, parenting, CPiE, PFA, and GBV response. Conduct three days of training for 30 of woreda government stakeholders from WoCYA , Labor and social affairs, justice office, and health bureau on the basics of CP, PFA, and PSS and referral pathways. Recruit and train 20 community volunteers (50% women) from each woreda on the basics of CPiE/GBV, PFA, and prevention and the response of COVID19 for 2 days. Procuring and distributing megaphones. Disseminating community awareness on CP/GBV and COVID-19 related messages by using megaphones. Monthly follow-up with PSS provision by the assigned psychologist for children and parents/foster parents who are reunited.Cost reimbursing community volunteers in the community to conduct community awareness creation activities.Training of 10 Social workers on CPiE/GBV, MHPSS, and referral pathways for 3 days. Provide PSS, case management, and referral for children at risk such as USAC. Identify, trace and document 85 USAC in the targeted woreda reuniting with parents or alternative care providers in collaboration with the responsible governmental office. Provide training for 40( 20 from each woreda) community volunteers on the basics of GBV response and psychosocial first-aid(PFA) and COVID 19 awareness creation for 2 days. Provide 3 days of training to 30members from government stakeholders of regional WCYA, Police & Justice Offices on basis of GBV, PFA, and case management and referral service for GBV survivors. Provide psychosocial support, case management, and referral for boys, girls, and women who experienced sexual abuse. Print and distribute 400 posters conveying GBV, PSEA, andCOVID-19 messages. Provide 3 days of training for 8 Social workers on the basics of GBV, case management, and referral. Distribute dignity kits to adolescent girls & women of reproductive age. Post distribution monitoring. Prepare feedback mechanism by Installing suggestion boxes on the project site.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

FIDO is an active member of Protection coordination clusters at the national and regional level to the present day. It also has a good working relationship with different government offices, beneficiaries, and other UN agencies that are present at the proposed site of intervention. FIDO will actively engage in the regional ECC weekly meetings. The proposed project will be integrated with other ongoing long-term development and emergency response projects in the mentioned woredas. FIDO has long-standing development programs in the target areas and humanitarian emergency response projects such as emergency WASH, Health, Protection, and Growth through nutrition.FIDO also has an active emergency shelter project in Sasiga woreda which can be complementary to the proposed protection project and there is also an Emergency Health project in the Borena zone for the drought-affected community. The prior presence of FIDO in the operational area has a huge advantage for the success of the proposed program due to the existing organizational reputation and programmatic and logistic synergy. Besides, FIDO has a well-established organizational base and fully furnished and equipped offices in the Nekemet town of East Wollega zone which makes this proposed project very efficient and cost-effective.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Initially, FIDO will create an effective communication channel between the government stakeholders and the affected population prior to the implementation of any of the project activities in accordance with the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS) as well as sphere standards. FIDO ensures the participation of community members throughout the life cycle of the project. It will establish a clear mechanism that allows the community members to provide feedback on the project implementation. FIDO recognizes that the essence of accountability is to respect the needs, concerns, capacities, and dispositions of those with whom we work and to answer for our actions and decisions. Hence, the organization is keen and committed to mainstreaming this accountability frame throughout the project cycle management following the Humanitarian Accountability Partnership Standard. Accordingly, relevant program information, beneficiary selection criteria, and beneficiaries’ entitlement from the project will be made available to communities in a timely, accessible, and accurate manner, communities will be informed and understand and agree with key decisions relating to our intervention. FIDO will devote itself to purposely empowering communities and building their capacity to participate in all components of the program cycle. Communities will be sensitized about their right to provide feedback and complaints and community feedback and complaint procedures that are accessible, safe, and effective will be provided for beneficiaries during the startup of the program and will continue to be communicated. Information sharing and awareness creation about accountability to the affected population will be held during health education activities by using different posters, banners, and printed IEC/BCC materials. These procedures will alert communities on their rights. All staff must become aware of Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies as part of the FIDO mandate since they are obligated to uphold and implement them. Staff will be provided with an orientation on the PSEA policy and the FIDO code of conduct after recruitment. Staff will be made aware of what sexual exploitation and abuse mean to FIDO and that FIDO has a zero-tolerance policy for it, given the urgency and frequently chaotic nature of emergency operations. A complaint handling committee will be established and along with responsible government stakeholders and FIDO, project staff will involve in handling any concerns or complaints that arise from the beneficiary and acting accordingly. The committee will be comprised of women, people with disabilities, elderlies to ensure maximum community participation. Aside from the committee, FIDO will also set up alternate ways for feedback and complaint mechanism to promptly respond to the concerns of beneficiaries and adjust implementation. Mechanisms to receive this complaint will consider the preferred mechanism of the community but will mainly use the Help desk, community feedback suggestion boxes, provision of local stakeholders of contacts of FIDO staff, and selected focal representative of the community. FIDO project staff and community volunteers will be trained on humanitarian accountability and its importance to mainstream them on the ground at every level of the project. FIDO will also ensure the collection and utilization of sex and age disaggregated data that adheres to recommended age groupings.
