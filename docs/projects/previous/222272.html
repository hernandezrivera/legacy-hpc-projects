Project Summary

This project will focus on internally displaced persons affected by conflicts and floods, as well as returnees from the Sudan crisis, in priority 1 & 2 counties across 4 states and 1 administrative area i.e. Unity state, Jonglei, Upper Nile, Northern Bahr El Ghazal and Abyei Administrative Area.
Persistent flooding in the country over the past five years, with a record 10% increase between 2023 and 2024 (IPC 2024), has displaced massive populations. This has posed significant protection and health risks to an estimated 4.2 million individuals in the country due to lack of basic shelter and essential NFIs (PIN 2024).
According to IRNA findings from Mayendit and Rubkona (August 2024), numerous households have been displaced to higher ground due to recent floods. In Rubkona, households previously displaced had their shelters further damaged by heavy rains. As of August 31st, 2024, 3,659 households in 158 Mayendit villages were displaced by the floods. A CH assessment conducted on September 27th, 2024, in Rubkona County revealed that over 500 households in Rubkona/Bentiu Payam had been displaced. The situation has been exacerbated by the influx of over 44,000 returnees from Sudan, who have settled in Bentiu/Rubkona Payam (town) with others sheltering in the POC. Displaced households report a lack of shelter, sanitation services, and essential household items.
In Leer, most shelters were damaged and require urgent renovation or rehabilitation. Limited availability of shelter construction materials, such as tarpaulin, rubber rope, bamboo, and wooden poles, has contributed to overcrowding in Pereeny and Muom. Additionally, some schools in Rubinchar Payam are occupied by IDPs. A shortage of household supplies and kitchen sets, including sleeping mats, blankets, bedsheets, mosquito nets, cooking pots, plates, spoons, basins, buckets, and solar lamps, is also evident (Leer IRNA report, September 2024). This situation has exposed households to WASH-related diseases like diarrhea, pneumonia, and malaria. The influx of flood-displaced populations and returnees has put pressure on host communities and existing facilities, including shelters, health facilities, sanitation facilities, and water resources, further exposing households to vulnerabilities.
To save lives, Coalition for Humanity (CH) will provide in-kind support for the construction of new shelters and the repair or rehabilitation of damaged shelters for returnees, IDPs, and residents living in camp-like settings across the target locations. In locations with functioning markets, CH will conduct rapid market assessments and gather information from the Cash Working Group and the joint market monitoring initiatives (JMMI) led by REACH to determine appropriate cash-based modalities for those locations and determining the multi-sector survival minimum expenditure basket (MSSMEB).
CH anticipates a shift in its shelter programming approach and plans to incorporate locally sourced shelter solutions, with a particular consideration on environmental conservation. To mitigate potential housing, land, and property (HLP) cases, CH will conduct thorough due diligence, collaborate with relevant stakeholders, and integrate HLP training for returnees, IDPs, and resident communities in partnership with the appropriate authorities. CH also proposes design and construction of model shelters (in consultation with SNFI cluster) in the target location, so as to promote durable shelter solutions from the local context.
A total of 7978HHs across priority 1&2 counties (47864 individuals) will benefit from the proposed project interventions i.e., 7980M, 8000W, 15922B, 15960G.

1. Needs

South Sudan's acute food insecurity and malnutrition crisis is worsening due to economic turmoil, climatic shocks-primarily widespread flooding, with a 10% increase between 2023 and 2024-protracted conflicts, and insecurity. The influx of returnees and refugees from the Sudan conflict further exacerbates the situation, straining an already fragile country with IOM DTM tracking 2.8M returnees' population already in the country as of September 2024. According to OCHA flooding snapshot (October 2024), at least 1.3 million people have been affected by floods in 42 counties and the Abyei administrative area, with Jonglei and NBeG comprising more than half the population. Between September and November 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47% of the analyzed population) faced Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or above) levels of acute food insecurity. Of these, 1.71 million people faced Emergency (IPC Phase 4) levels, and 41,000 faced Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) levels. The Catastrophe-level population includes 10,000 residents of Malakal County (Upper Nile State) and approximately 31,000 South Sudanese returnees from Sudan's ongoing conflict. Compared to the same period last year, this represents a 500,000-person increase in the number of people facing Crisis or worse (latest published IPC report 2024). PIN 2024 indicate that an estimated 4.2 million individuals in the country lack shelter and essential NFIs. The Shelter Cluster has identified 1.37 million of these individuals as their primary target population for humanitarian shelter assistance. These individuals are primarily located in five states: Unity, Upper Nile, Jonglei, Northern Bahr El Ghazal, and Abyei Administrative Area, which are frequently affected by communal violence, natural disasters (primarily floods), and food insecurity. According to IRNA findings from Mayendit and Rubkona (August 2024), numerous households have been displaced to higher ground due to recent floods. In Rubkona, households previously displaced had their shelters further damaged by heavy rains. As of August 31st, 2024, 3,659 households in 158 Mayendit villages were displaced by the floods. A CH assessment conducted on September 27th, 2024, in Rubkona County revealed that over 500 households in Rubkona/Bentiu Payam had been displaced. The influx of flood-displaced populations and returnees has put pressure on host communities and existing facilities, including shelters, health facilities, sanitation facilities, and water resources, further exposing households to vulnerabilities.

2. Project activities

• Coalition for Humanity will engage the community and conduct needs assessments before project implementation to determine and validate the SNFI needs in the project locations. With the understanding that community groups are affected differently by disaster due to variability in vulnerability levels. CH will involve all groups in the project activities and targeting including women, girls, the elderly and persons living with disabilities. Returnees, IDPs and resident communities will form part of the target beneficiaries. • To monitor the project impact, CH will conduct post distribution monitoring (PDM) as part of project monitoring and evaluation after SNFI distribution. The report will be shared with the SNFI cluster and other stakeholders to ensure lessons learned are incorporated in future programs. • CH adheres to conflict sensitivity protocols and the "do no harm" principle. To minimize potential harm, communities will be fully involved throughout project implementation. This involvement will ensure their needs are met and potential conflicts are avoided. During the project's inception phase, all community stakeholders, including local leaders, will be informed about the project's objectives, scope, and deliverables. This proactive approach aims to prevent misunderstandings and disagreements that could lead to conflicts. • Safety audits and GBV risk analyses will form part of the project interventions. The information that will be generated from the focus group discussions with women and girls will be used to determine SNFI distribution sites, which are safe. Coalition for Humanity will also use this opportunity to share protection and GBV concerns that are beyond the project mandate with protection and GBV cluster and partners in the project location. • Greater upper Nile counties are prone to disaster. As such, CH as a static partner in the project locations will employ disaster risk reduction (DRR) mechanisms to prevent new and reduce existing risks. This includes assessment of risks, creating general awareness campaigns, training and capacity development of local authorities and putting in place early warning systems. • CH will distribute in-kind emergency standard shelter and life-saving non-food items to internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and resident community members affected by both new and protracted displacements in the target project locations. • Flood kits will be distributed to flood-affected populations in the target counties and essential emergency shelter upgrades will be carried out where necessary. • CH will develop shelter designs in collaboration with the cluster and households will be encouraged to construct their own shelters using the models that shall be constructed in the project locations using locally available materials • CH will integrate HLP training and awareness into its response to ensure that beneficiaries gain general information and knowledge about land rights, ownership, and tenure arrangements. This will be achieved through collaboration with IOM/UNHCR/NRC and the government ministries in the various counties. • Sectoral Cash and vouchers for essential emergency shelter upgrades, reconstruction, and rehabilitation will be utilized in areas where market assessments indicate that markets are functional. Coalition for Humanity will work closely with the SNFI cluster, Cash working group (CWG) and other actors to ensure cash guidelines are adhered to during responses. These activities contribute to the SNFI cluster 3-tier approach strategy of lifesaving interventions, reintegration of returnees and maintenance of IDP sites through maintenance of shelters.
