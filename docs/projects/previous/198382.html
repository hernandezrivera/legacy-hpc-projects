Project Summary

Newly displaced, existing IDPs and returnee households continue to be reliant on humanitarian support to enable them access to timely and suitable shelter solutions. Mercy Corps propose a tri-folded shelter intervention to provide a most appropriate shelter solution to meet the various needs of newly displaced at reception centres and/or at transition sites, existing IDPs in over-congested camps and the IDPs living with cost communities, returnees and the host families themselves. Mercy Corps focus will remain to continue providing customized shelter and S&S NFIs assistance in Ngala, Dikwa, Gwoza, Bama, Jere, Monguno and Damboa LGAs. Damasak town (Mobbar LGA),Soye/ Banki, Pulka, and Konduga LGA will be added as new areas considering the gaps and needs found in MSNA 2021. Mercy Corps will ensure to harmonize the assistance packages aligned to shelter CCCM sector guidelines in these locations. Assistance packages will include a) A customized new arrival kit comprising basic emergency shelter materials and S&S NFIs for 400HHs. Materials will include, tarpaulin, timber poles, rope, nails and fixtures) paired with household utensils such as bedding, blankets, floor mat, mosquito net, kitchen set, jerry cans, buckets, basin, solar torch). A communal tool kit – shared by up to five households – will also be provided, including necessary items like shovels, claw hammers, and saws. b) A tailor-made NFIs replenishment assistance for 4500 HHs c) Mercy Corps will also provide 2,000 tailor-made assistance for vulnerable households in camp settings to access the needed shelter materials such as timber poles, tarpaulin sheets, rope, and nails to maintain their deteriorated shelters. d) To assist existing IDPs and returnees living in host communities, a shelter repair package will be provided to 2,500 HHs (IDPs, returnees, and host community) living in poor sheltering conditions (i.e. unfinished, abandoned, or damaged buildings). Repair package will include access to needed materials (cement, sand, mud blocks, cement blocks, timber, doors and windows with fixtures), technical assistance (advice training, supervision), skilled labourers and HLP support. e) Distribution of 6000 energy efficient fuel stoves to female headed households (IDPs, returnees and host) on account of massive resource degradation and skipping meals due to insufficient access to fuel sources. f) 1,615 Transitional shelter kits paired with labor assistance provided to displaced HH or returnees in poor living condition with a more dignified shelter. g) 372 Movable/Collapsible shelter kits worth paired with labor assistance provided to support displaced HH or returnees in poor living condition with a more dignified mobile shelter. Extensive technical assistance will be an integral component to all the aforementioned assistance packages that will ensure appropriate utilization of the assistance provided

1 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis in the northeast has been in an irregular state for the past couple of years. Recently, military operations against armed opposition gunmen and an intensified raining season between the months of May and August have displaced populations from several local government areas in Borno State of Nigeria including Gwoza, Pulka, Bama, Banki, Konduga, Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, Damasak and Damboa. Additionally, thousands of returnee households have also been displaced and concentrated between these LGAs due to camp closures at the state’s capital, Maiduguri. The estimated displaced population that are concentrated in these LGAs as at October 2022 have been capped at 846,940 individuals, comprising 172,845 households with an average household size of 5 individuals by the BAY States CCCM, Shelter/NFI Gaps 24-October-2022. The identified immediate shelter needs of the displaced population include emergency shelter kits for new arrivals into the LGAs, shelter improvement kits for existing displaced population in the camps of these LGAs, transitional shelter repair kits for households damaged by rains in the host communities and transitional shelter construction for camp decongestion. There are ongoing efforts by several partner organizations including Mercy Corps to meet the needs of the affected population in all of the aforementioned need areas from January 2022 to date reducing the total number of households in need to the current 172,845 from 219,177 households. In a typical camp setting in Borno state, several interventions take place, including the provision of health services, WaSH, protection, OTP services in nutrition, and cash for livelihood. As indicated earlier, strong winds, heavy rains, and severe weather in Northeast Nigeria have caused the destruction of shelters and vital facilities throughout IDP camps and host towns. Reports from the CCCM/Shelter sector have emphasized the need for ongoing rehabilitation and repairs of deteriorating shelters and buildings, mostly as a result of wear and tear and the effects of severe weather. The affected population appears from all indications to have no capacity to rebuild their shelters, and thus, the identification and mapping of new arrivals, community mobilization efforts, and improved communication with the affected population—including the inclusion of those who have experienced secondary displacement—are all additional services that are required. According to Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2022, there are 2,95M IDPs in need of Shelter/NFI support and among them 1,46m are targeted by sector for support. The government-led camp closure has contributed to back-and-forth movements in camps at the LGAs further resulting in secondary displacement, over congestion, and constrained resources. The out of camp population suffer due to lack of services in the camps which prompts the need for a more inclusive, structured, and secured sites/settlements. Also, according to Northeast Nigeria: Shelter/NFIs Monthly Dashboard January – August 2022) have carried out joint, and multi-sectoral assessments in BAY states and informed on evidence-based findings on unmet needs for shelter as 300,023 HHs, 224,155 HHs are in need of urgent shelter gaps, 182,964 HHs are sharing shelters and 115,823 are living in makeshift shelter that are at risk of destruction by windstorm or rain. The report outlined recommendations for the identified needs such as: 1) lack of shelter; 2) congestion of camps and reception centers. Annually, IDPs face the rainy and dry season where their emergency shelters are affected by strong winds, flash flooding, heavy rains and outbreaks of fire have further deteriorated the situation.

2 - Activities

To meet critical life-saving shelter needs of new arrival IDPs with prepositioned resources and specialized mobile distribution team. Mercy Corps aims to provide a most appropriate shelter solution to meet the various needs of newly displaced at reception centres and/or at transition sites, existing IDPs in over-congested camps and the IDPs living with cost communities, returnees, and the host families themselves. Mercy Corps focus will remain to continue providing customized shelter and S&S NFIs assistance in Ngala, Dikwa, Gwoza, Bama, Monguno, Jere and Damboa , Banki, Pulka, and Konduga LGAs will be added as new areas considering the gaps and needs found in rapid need assessments carried out recently. Mercy Corps will ensure to harmonize the assistance packages aligned to shelter CCCM sector guidelines in these locations. Assistance packages will include a) A customized new arrival kit comprising basic emergency shelter kits to 700 hhs. Materials will include, tarpaulin, timber poles, rope, nails, and fixtures). A communal tool kit – shared by up to five households – will also be provided, including necessary items like shovels, claw hammers, and saws. b) A tailor-made NFIs replenishment assistance for 5,200 HHs living in the host community and standard NFIs to 3,700 hhs living in camps c) Mercy Corps will also provide tailor-made assistance to 1,600 vulnerable households in camp settings to access the needed shelter materials such as timber poles, tarpaulin sheets, rope, and nails to maintain their deteriorated shelters. d) To assist existing IDPs and returnees living in host communities, a shelter repair package will be provided to 1,900 HHs (IDPs, returnees, and host community) living in poor sheltering conditions (i.e. unfinished, abandoned, or damaged buildings). Repair package will include access to needed materials (cement, sand, mud blocks, cement blocks, timber, doors and windows with fixtures), technical assistance (advice training, supervision), skilled labourers and HLP support. e) Distribution of 4,000 energy efficient fuel stoves to female headed households (IDPs, returnees and host) on account of massive resource degradation and skipping meals due to insufficient access to fuel sources. f) 650 Transitional shelter kits paired with labor assistance provided to displaced HH or returnees in poor living condition with a more dignified shelter. g) 72 Movable/Collapsible shelter kits worth paired with labor assistance provided to support displaced HH or returnees in poor living condition with a more dignified mobile shelter. Extensive technical assistance will be an integral component to all the aforementioned assistance packages that will ensure appropriate utilization of the assistance provided.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Gender and protection mainstreaming will remain at the heart of the shelter & settlement assistance. During the targeting, priority will be given to families sheltering separated/unaccompanied children (below 18 years old), household headed by a woman - priority to be given to widows who lost their husband due to recent conflict, households headed by children (below 18 years old) . To help improve the privacy and safety of the women and girls, lockable doors and windows will be incorporated in the design of the shelters. The scope of the repairs will consider the safety of the children, accessibility needs of the people with special needs and privacy and safety of the women and girls. Female headed HHs will be provided with the fuel-efficient stoves that will minimize the SGBV risks associated with fetching firewood from the bush. Privacy screens will be provided to overcrowded families that will also minimize the risk of SGBV. Elderly, women and children headed HHs will be prioritized throughout and receive additional unskilled labor assistance when others are supposed to contribute. Furthermore, field supervisors, daily hire workers and the field staff will be trained on DNH principles. Mercy Corps shelter interventions indeed focus on ensuring the privacy, security, and dignity of all household members, however, an additional caseload for the families has access to a liveable shelter yet lacks privacy for women and girls. These partitions will also help in COVID-19 pandemic context for overcrowded families.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

As described above, Mercy Corps has developed and maintains Community Relief Committees (CRC) as its primary community mobilization mechanism, with representatives of diverse age, gender, ethnic, religious, and socio-economic groups, including displaced and host communities, so that their concerns are heard and addressed and to avoid community tensions. CRCs have at least two female members, helping to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of people seeking shelter and services. Mercy Corps’ Community Accountability Reporting Mechanism (CARM) will be continued in the ADAPT and THRIVE project to enable a safe and effective feedback/complaint system for bene\0ciaries and non-bene\0ciaries. The CARM includes a community-based feedback mechanism developed by Mercy Corps in Gombe in 2014 that allows people (in particular non-literate bene\0ciaries) to give feedback verbally and with discretion. Complaints will also be gathered through designated community focal points and through the use of a logbook updated by the focal point within each CRC, with electronic recording, analysis and follow up of all complaints within a speci\0ed timeline by Mercy Corps’ M&E staff. CARM will also be used to monitor protection issues, and issues will also be monitored under the Protection-sector speci\0c programming described below; that information will be used to reduce existing and newly identi\0ed risks. The program will monitor the security situation to adjust its implementation approach if the security situation deteriorates
