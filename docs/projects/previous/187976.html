Project Summary

HNAP is a vital component to ensuring quality and evidence-based humanitarian programming across the whole of Syria. Fulfilling its role as a common UN initiative, HNAP is the critical initiative supporting the Humanitarian Program Cycle (HPC) and undertook multi-sectorial needs assessments (MSNAs) in coordination with OCHA and key humanitarian partners. HNAP is a member of the Assessment and Analysis Cell participating in designing the MSNA methodology, including designing the tool, testing it and responsible for 100% of the data collection for both household MSNA as well as the different KI sectoral Assessment for HNO this also included. In addition to that HNAP is the primary source of population figures for the IDP and Population Task-force estimates. Over the past three years, HNAP was the only source of Sex-Age desegregated data and also the primary source for disability prevalence data for the HNO.

In addition to contributing to tool design, HNAP was the sole data collector for the nationwide MSNA and several supplementary KI assessments essential for the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). HNAP is the only data collection agency with complete coverage across Syria, without it the HNO and HRP would not only lack quality and verifiable data, but the deficiency of data would hinder the ability of humanitarian stakeholders to triangulate program-specific data, undermining the humanitarian imperative to access those most in need of assistance.
HNAP regularly coordinates with a multitude of sectors, including Shelter, WASH, Protection, GBV, ERL and others to provide them with essential data to inform their respective response planning. All staff have been extensively trained on protection, GBV and PSEA principles ensuring the utmost protection of humanitarian standards during data collection activities.

The programme will continue to contribute to the coordination sector by providing essential data and coordination support at the hub and WoS levels. When relevant, data will be readily available with sex, age and disability disaggregation. HNAP will continue to be a pinnacle component of timely and evidence-based response by conducting its enhanced needs assessments across all locations inside Syria, as well as providing technical information management guidance to strengthen the planning and response capacity of all humanitarian actors.

4 - Needs

Humanitarian response is only as effective as the data collected to inform population needs. As the sole data collection agency for the previous two HPC cycles, HNAP has been the most widely used source of data to inform the humanitarian community’s strategic planning and response. HNAP will ensure the continued collection and analysis of time-sensitive, impartial, geographically specific and independent data that will be shared with UN clusters and humanitarian partners through the following activities: 1. Producing regular IDP, returnee and resident population data collection (critical for monitoring population movements and priority needs across all 9,761 locations); 2. Conducting multi-sectoral needs assessments, with data disaggregated by sex, age, and disability as well as by other indicators of vulnerability; 3. Supporting the Humanitarian Program Cycle through regular household assessments with data on; household demographics, the prevalence of persons with disabilities, socio-economic conditions, the intentions of IDPs and return community conditions; 4. Leading sector-specific needs assessments (e.g. WASH, Shelter, Education, GBV, Protection, etc.); 5. Continued monitoring of pertinent issues, like water access mapping, COVID-19 mitigation measures, vaccine perceptions, access to services and monitoring of transit points status; and, 6. Coordinating ad-hoc assessments in areas affected by critical developments – for example, natural disasters, or rapid displacement.

5 - Implementation of activities

HNAP conducts regular assessments and surveys using primary data collection methods, including interviews with local key informants, direct observation and household surveys. It is the sole source of information for more than 85% of communities inside Syria and conducts the following activities: - Weekly, monthly, quarterly and needs-based data collection across all communities in Syria; - Conduct needs assessments and thematic surveys of all population groups to assess socio-economic, priority needs and shelter conditions of men, women, girls, boys and PWDs; - Implementation of real-time quality and safety control monitoring following in-depth staff training; - Technical data cleaning and analysis feed into report and dashboard production; - Circulation of products with humanitarian partners, including OCHA, UN agencies, NGOs and donors via email and through HNAP’s portal; - HNAP shares raw data with the IDP, Population and CCCM taskforces on a monthly basis (pertaining to movement), on a quarterly basis (pertaining to baseline figures), and on an annual basis HNAP provides sex/age disaggregated data to the population task-force which is used for the HPC cycle; - HNAP continues developing regression modeling to better predict return movements; - HNAP will continue conducting regular COVID-19 assessments; and, - Where applicable, data is disaggregated by sex and age, and specific PSEA questions are incorporated into surveys.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Staff and respondents may face challenges related to the sensitivity of questions as well as navigating access in areas of high-security risk. 2. Participants may be exposed to assessment fatigue which could distort information based on the perceptions of humanitarian staff expectations. 3. Coverage across the WOS may hinder consistent messaging or quality control. 4. Lack of awareness of the importance of protection, PSEA and GBV mainstreaming among the staff. 5. Protection and privacy concerns related to sharing of personal data during assessments. Mitigation Measures: 1. Do-no-harm considerations will be integrated into survey/assessment questions; staff trained in survivor-centered approach and psycho-social first aid (PFA); daily security briefings inform assessment planning; duty of care policies for staff developed and budgeted for. 2. Activities will only be conducted if they directly benefit humanitarian response while staff clearly convey the scope of the assessment and purpose of activities. 3. In-person trainers are made available inside Syria coupled with live quality control monitoring software. 4. Staff compliance with code-of-conduct, attend mandatory PSEA, GBV and protection trainings. Further inclusion and gender-sensitive interview techniques applied. 5. Respondents made aware they always have the right to not answer any questions, HNAP data is always anonymized and no personal information shared. Further data encryption applied to tools.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

All HNAP enumerators are trained in PSEA standards and upholden to the prevention standards through their codes of conduct. Given HNAP’s role as a data collection program, teams are not directly involved in the provision of humanitarian assistance, thereby minimizing the risk related to SEA. Regardless, the team has been fully trained on such protection principles as well as reporting channels to guide respondents should they suspect a possible SEA incident. Beyond field team engagement with respondents, HNAP regularly works with the whole of Syria PSEA network to conduct an annual household assessment on the prevalence and perception of SEA. The assessments and subsequent reports play a vital role in informing future PSEA planning and appeals for enhanced reporting mechanisms, membership expansion and continued funding.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks: 1. The security situation in Syria is the most prominent risk for this project. Active conflict could affect access to locations and may also impact the personal security of field staff. Assumptions: 1. Security conditions in Syria will continue to be conducive for enumerators to carry out assessments without facing personal security risks. 2. The security conditions will also continue to be conducive for communities to willingly engage in answering assessment questions. 3. Key informants will continue to provide information which is representative of the needs of all community members including IDPs, residents, men, women, boys, girls, and persons living with disabilities. 4.Effective engagement with communities and completion of assessments will contribute to appropriate and effective humanitarian response across Syria. Mitigation measures: 1. Team leaders will monitor security conditions on-the-ground daily, while liaising with the field coordinator who works in close coordination with program management in Gaziantep. 2. Data is always encrypted to minimize risk faced by enumerators and respondents. Kobo and other monitoring tools are used to ensure no storage of data in any form at the field level.3. All enumerators receive regular self-care training to build capacity in dealing with exposure to primary and secondary trauma. 4.Program coordinator remains in close contact with field teams and response agencies to minimize duplication and ensure assessment relevancy.
