Project Summary

"WFP aims to contribute to a reduction in acute and chronic malnutrition rates, and an improvement in nutritional status among refugees and local Bangladeshi communities in Cox’s Bazar through the provision of curative and preventive nutrition services for children below 5 years and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). WFP will implement its project interventions across 45 integrated nutrition facilities (INFs) in 33 camps, and 130 Community Nutrition Centres (CNCs) in the local Bangladeshi community in partnership with UNICEF, UNHCR and cooperating partners under the coordination of the Cox's Bazar nutrition sector. WFP will provide targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) for moderate acutely malnourished children 6-59 months and Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) to reduce morbidity and mortality associated with malnutrition in both the refugee camps and in the host community. A 200g daily ration of Super Cereal Plus (Wheat Soya Blend Plus -WSB++) will be provided for children 6-59 months; and 250g of Super Cereal ( Wheat Soya Blend -WSB+) and 16.66g of oil will be provided to PLW under TSFP.
An enhanced prevention of malnutrition package will be put in place to mitigate the increase in prevalence of acute and chronic malnutrition and its associated risks for mortality and morbidity and promote child development. In the camps, this includes provision of the blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP) for younger children 6-23 months and PLWs, and a nutrition sensitive e-voucher programme that allows older children (24-59 months) access to fresh fruits and vegetables aimed at enhancing their dietary diversity and promote growth monitoring and uptake of other complementary services at the INF. These children will receive a US$ 3 redeemable voucher. Social Behaviour Change Communication (SBCC) activities and utilization of tools that positively influence behaviour, knowledge, attitudes, and practice around nutrition will be conducted in both the refugee and host communities.
Active case finding through community screening, referral and follow up of malnourished children under five and pregnant and lactating women will be done. Additionally, growth monitoring and promotion for children 6-59 months will be part of the project interventions.
The underlying causes of malnutrition will be addressed through integration of nutrition sensitive approaches in ongoing general food assistance, school feeding, self-reliance, and livelihood interventions.
WFP will promote the prevention of micronutrient deficiencies particularly anemia through the promotion of dietary diversification.
WFP will conduct capacity strengthening for partners and stakeholders to improve capacity for implementation of nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive programming.
WFP will support quantitative and qualitative nutrition assessments to ensure that nutrition programmes are tailormade according to the needs of the targeted populations.
Project interventions will be adapted to ensure an inclusive approach to promote participation of women, men, older people, adolescent, people who have chronic health conditions and persons with disabilities. Additionally, protection mechanisms will be put in place including complaints and feedback mechanisms and help desks to ensure accountability to affected populations. "

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"According to the November 2021 Standardized Expanded Nutrition Survey (SENS), the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) by weight for height (WHZ) is at 13.7 percent with an upper confidence level of >15 percent. The prevalence of GAM among children 6-59 months was below the 15 percent WHO ‘Emergency’ threshold across the refugee camps. However, it is still categorized as serious (10-15 percent) based on the WHO/UNICEF classification of malnutrition and the >10 percent UNHCR standards for refugee camps, and given even higher rates of acute malnutrition among the youngest children aged 6-23 months. Chronic malnutrition remains very high according to WHO thresholds with stunting at 30.2 percent and underweight at 31.7 percent. There has been an increase in anemia rates among children and women of reproductive age at 50.3 percent and 40.3 percent, respectively, indicating a problem of public health significance. On a positive note, there has been a significant reduction in the prevalence of wasting among women of reproductive age since 2017, from >8.0 percent to <2.0 percent. Based on the Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) survey completed in 2021, the GAM rate is lower in the Bangladeshi community in Teknaf and Ukhiya sub-districts (9.9 percent and 9.8 percent, respectively) compared to the camps. Malnutrition levels among Bangladeshi households in Kutubdia and Pekua is serious at 14.9 percent and 13.0 percent respectively while in Moheshkhali the situation is critical (16.0 percent). Chronic malnutrition is also in the serious category for Bangladeshi households across all sub-districts, with the lowest prevalence in Pekua (21.6 percent) and the highest in Moheshkhali at 29.8 percent. The underweight rate also falls into the serious category across all sub-districts, except for Moheshkhali where it is critical at 32.1 percent. Acute malnutrition among pregnant and lactating women (PLW) is also poor in Moheshkhali (3.2 percent), Kutubdia (5.6 percent), and Pekua (4.0 percent). In these areas – camps and local communities, prevalence for anemia is considered a public health concern, coupled with sub-optimal IYCF practices, inadequate dietary diversity, poor care practices, high disease morbidity, low birth spacing, and inadequate WASH practices. These have been cited as key underlying factors contributing to the high prevalence rates of malnutrition the Cox’s Bazar operation. Refugee children between 6-23 months were more acutely malnourished, while slightly older children 24-59 months old had higher levels of stunting. These findings are like the 2019 nutrition causal analysis that indicated that children under 2 were more vulnerable to acute malnutrition, and that vulnerability was inversely related to the age of their mothers. These findings align with the general observation in nutrition surveys that acute malnutrition decreases with age while stunting increases with age. The findings highlight the importance of “the first 1,000 days”, from conception until the child reaches 2 years of age – a critical window in which the health and well-being of a pregnant and lactating woman directly impacts the growth and health of her child. Therefore, despite considerable expansion of nutrition treatment centres, food assistance, WASH facilities, and health services, the current situation constitute serious levels of malnutrition in need of ongoing nutritional support. "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

In 2019 WFP, UNICEF and UNHCR under the coordination umbrella of the nutrition sector initiated a rationalization process. By January 2020, the Nutrition sector had successfully implemented a rationalization exercise to bring four Nutrition services (OTP, TSFP, IYCF and BSFP) together. These services are now delivered through 45 Integrated Nutrition Facilities (INFs) in the 33 camps in Cox’s Bazar. All these 45 facilities are co-funded by UNICEF, UNHCR and WFP. In 2022 integrated treatment of moderate acute malnutrition will be conducted through 130 Community Nutrition Cntres (CNC) within the local Bangladeshi communities . In 2023, WFP will work with cooperating partners to strengthen the capacity of the office of the civil surgeon in nutrition service delivery and reporting. WFP will support WASH partner as might be required for hygiene messaging and promotion.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

WFP has been addressing the rights of persons with disabilities through its programming (including its protection & accountability policy) based on its corporate Disability Inclusion Roadmap (2020-2022) that details extend to which persons with disabilities & other vulnerable groups are included in aid delivery. It will further mainstream the rights of persons with disabilities in its internal operations. Equally, WFP ensures the collection of beneficiary data contributes to accountability,inclusion,good programming,assurance & improved coordination with partners, especially when introducing new technologies & services. WFP adopts a person-centred approach that emphasizes the identification of data protection risks & mitigation measures for beneficiaries as part of its context analysis,operational program design & delivery, including the promotion of inclusion & digital literacy. WFP works with interagency groups & clusters to integrate protection considerations across its operations.

3.2 - Gender

WFP adopted the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Gender and Age Marker (GAM) in 2012, and again in 2018 with the newly revised GAM. As a result, WFP strives to mainstream gender in all aspects of its operations. At the corporate level, WFP-wide gender and protection policies have been implemented at the operational and technical levels in Bangladesh. WFP staff have been trained in gender mainstreaming and how it impacts women, men, boys, and girls. WFP also adheres to zero tolerance on sexual exploitation and abuse, using the ISCG’s Six Core Principles on Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). WFP ensures that their cooperating partners demonstrate their capacity to integrate gender considerations into the design and implementation of their activities. WFP and partner staffs are trained on gender-sensitivity and are equipped with protection, sexual and gender-based violence, child protection and SEA referral pathways.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

WFP nutrition programme focuses on young and older children aged 0-59 months and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition or at risk of it. WFP works internally with different sectors like General Assistance, Livelihoods, Social Safety Net, and School feeding to ensure that other age groups in need of humanitarian assistance are met. WFP has strong PSEA policies that includes safeguarding of minors against any form of exploitation including sexual and child labour. WFP ensures that their cooperating partners demonstrate their capacity for child safeguards into the design and implementation of their activities.

3.4 - Diversity

WFP works with multicultural teams. At implementation level, WFP ensures a mix of local and affected communities are involved in programme activities while considering gender and age balance. WFP ensures that their cooperating partners demonstrate their capacity for child safeguards into the design and implementation of their activities.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

WFP has Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) guidelines & has recently developed a community engagement strategy for AAP (2021-2026), using a human rights approach to humanitarian & development action that considers AAP as the central focus of its operations. WFP recognizes the need for transparent & continuous two-way communication between the organization & the people it serves in order to capture, record, risks, needs, capacities & aspirations of communities. Actions & approaches which have enabled communities to participate meaningfully in key decisions throughout the WFP program cycle include assessment & analysis, planning & design, resource mobilization, implementation, M&E. In addition, beneficiaries are well-informed about their rights and entitlements & have access to Complaint & Feedback mechanisms on WFP programmes. WFP ensures that their cooperating partners demonstrate their capacity to integrate AAP aspects into the design & implementation of their activities.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. WFP will explore opportunities with its Self-Reliance unit to minimize environmental footprints in the camps. The programme will ensure that used sachets for specialized nutritious foods and edible oil bottles are collected back in subsequent visits from the beneficiaries. Self Reliance unit will support nutrition unit for safe disposal, recycling and upcycling. On a needs basis, nutrition unit will seek support from its DRR unit to ensure Integrated facilities are reinforced and impact from hazards are mitigated.
