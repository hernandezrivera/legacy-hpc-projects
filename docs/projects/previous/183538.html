Project Summary

With the Gaza Strip on the brink of a full-scale humanitarian emergency and economic collapse and over one million Palestinians living within its borders considered food insecure, Catholic Relief Services (CRS) and its local partners Life and Hope Association (LHA) and Palestinian Organization for Development (POD) propose a 12-month mult-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) project to help 1000 of the most vulnerable Gazan families meet holistic household needs. Aligned with the occupied Palestinian territories (oPt) Humanitarian Fund 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), the project will utilize CRS’ existing cash distribution system to ensure rapid and direct assistance to target households. CRS will coordinate closely with the Palestinian Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) in Ramallah to identify highly vulnerable households across all five Governorates of the Gaza Strip; CRS will validate these needs through phone calls, field visits, and UVAT when feasible and will coordinate with the Food Security Sector (FSS), other actors implementing cash and voucher assistance (CVA), World Food Programme (WFP) and United Nations Relief Works Agency (UNRWA) to maximize assistance impact and avoid duplication. Working in the oPt since 1961 and with current programming in each of the five Gaza governorates, CRS’ existing staff and established policies and networks will ensure successful implementation of the proposed project. CRS’ longstanding engagements and investments with local implementing partners intentionally builds capacity and sustainability of our interventions. In this project, local implementing partners LHA and POD will be responsible for supporting the CRS team in all project activities including assisting in beneficiary selection, orienting beneficiaries to the project, conducting monitoring field visits and collecting post-distribution monitoring data and reporting community beneficiary feedback and complaints or breach from vendors to the CRS team.

1 - Needs

Gaza has endured the social and economic impacts of a land and naval blockade for 14 years and counting. From May 10-21, 2021, this already vulnerable population endured an intense conflict, resulting in devastating humanitarian consequences for civilians in Gaza, including widespread damage to infrastructure such as hospitals and healthcare centers, water and sanitation facilities, and schools. The Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) compiled a list of 45,000 households who were displaced or incurred physical damage to their homes because of this conflict and who are in need of assistance. Even prior to the conflict, Gaza was on the verge of a full-scale emergency and economic collapse due to the impact of the blockade, years of intermittent armed conflict, and the COVID-19 pandemic. The 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview identifies over half of the population in Gaza as food insecure and in need of assistance. The unemployment rate for the second quarter of 2021 was 44.7 percent in the Gaza Strip, with unemployment among males in Gaza reaching 39 per cent and up to 64 per cent among females. The poverty rate in Gaza was estimated at 53 per cent in 2017 and is expected to have increased to 56.4 per cent as a result of the May hostilities. High unemployment and poverty rates have limited families’ abilities to purchase food and basic living supplies, eroded savings, and forced families to take out debt, hampering their ability to provide for themselves and their families, especially during shocks like the recent conflict. As a result, Palestinians are increasingly less able to cope with sudden shocks, manmade or in connection to natural events, such as spikes of violence or heatwaves and winter storms. These sudden shocks occur alongside, and exacerbate the impact of, already critical conditions, characterized by the restricted access to natural resources, trade restrictions, and persistent electricity shortages in Gaza, and inadequate water and sanitation services. MSNA (July 2021) found that food expenditure constitutes 50 per cent of household income in Gaza. Even though a relatively large number of households appear to be meeting their very basic needs supported by aid received, a high percentage of them are employing negative coping mechanisms such as buying food on credit, reducing expenditure on non-food items, and selling assets in order to meet those basic needs, which again increases their vulnerabilities. Women in the oPt are particularly disadvantaged with regard to their limited share of agricultural holdings and the limited economic opportunities available for women to generate income. This disadvantage restricts women’s ability to contribute to household food security and also increases the vulnerability of women and female-headed households to food insecurity and malnutrition, with pregnant and lactating women particularly vulnerable to the risks of malnutrition. UN Women and WFP report that depleted household resilience is also observed due to high female unemployment, women’s limited access to resources, and concentration of women in economic activities that are directly impacted by protection violations such as herding and harvesting. According to a recent study by the Shelter and NFIs Cluster in oPt, limited knowledge within vulnerable groups such as widows, women heads of households, and women with disabilities, of the services offered by different humanitarian actors and the terms and criteria for receiving assistance often results in unequal access to these services, and ultimately to humanitarian responses that fail to consider age, gender and location. Global, regional, and local research has demonstrated how gender-sensitive cash and voucher assistance can bolster women’s social protection and development, according to UN Women and WFP. It has the potential to create economic opportunities, build women’s resilience and independence and combat gender-based violence.

2 - Activities

To respond to the immediate needs of the families negatively impacted by the blockade, COVID-19, and the May escalation of hostilities, exacerbated by longer-term vulnerability and food insecurity, this project will provide unrestricted multi-purpose cash assistance to vulnerable families who are unable to meet their most basic needs. CRS and its two local partners will target 1,000 households (5,500 beneficiaries) across the five Gaza governorates with cash to meet their basic needs. Based on the transfer value connected to the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB)—MEB—approved by the Gaza Cash Working Group (CWG), CRS anticipates providing 1,046 ILS (roughly 324 USD) per month to targeted households (~190 ILS per person based on an average household size of 5.5). CRS will distribute assistance monthly for six months. To ensure the effective implementation of the project, the following activities will be: Beneficiary Selection and Verification: CRS will target food insecure HH that are registered with MoSD as in need of assistance. CRS will undertake a scoring process to ensure those most in need are prioritized, including large families, persons living with disabilities, elderly persons, people with chronic disease and female-headed households. CRS and its local partner will verify the accuracy of beneficiary data from the list shared by MoSD through taking a sample of (15%) of the total beneficiaries. CRS will cross-check with other humanitarian actors to ensure selected beneficiaries are not receiving similar assistance from others. Additional households identified by partners and through the CRS Feedback and Complaints Response Mechanism (FCRM) will be verified with MoSD and, if eligible, added to the list. CRS will consider using the UVAT for targeting, verification and cross-checking as relevant and feasible during the project period. This project will benefit men and women of all ages. Recent CRS targeting in similar projects showed the following percentages of different target groups: Female Headed Households (16.5%), PWD (2%), Elderly (2.60%), Children less 18 years (52.5%), Children under 5 years (16.50%), Women (23.5%), Girls (25.30%). Vendor Preparation: CRS will update its agreements with the existing 41 vendors (supermarkets) across the Gaza Strip. As needed, CRS will provide refresher orientation to vendors on project activities program rules and regulations, technical aspects of the transactions, the cardless code and specific responsibilities, including orientation to vendors for whom the cash-out option will be new. Beneficiary Orientation: CRS and partners will provide a detailed orientation to all selected beneficiaries through phone calls and SMS communication that includes an introduction to CRS and the project, how to use the cardless code to withdraw cash, and how to use the FCRM and share the key protection and safeguarding messages. Distribution of Information Booklet and COVID- 19 prevention leaflet: CRS and partners will provide each household with a booklet containing pertinent information, including the names of available vendors and their addresses and information on how to provide feedback to CRS via its FCRM. CRS and partners will also distribute a leaflet with COVID-19 risk mitigation messages that align with recommendations from the RCCE working group in oPt. The beneficiary will get these booklets and leaflets from any vendor participating in the project. Vendor lists will be sent via text message to each beneficiary. All information contained in the project booklet, leaflet and SMS is delivered verbally to the target HH during project orientation so that project beneficiaries living with visual impairments or people who cannot read or write can equally access project information. Transfer of assistance to beneficiaries: At the beginning of every month for 6 months/HH, CRS and partners will send an SMS to each beneficiary with a unique cardless code that is used for a contactless cash withdrawal transaction at all participating vendor shops. CRS plans to use the MEB assistance value, at 1046 ILS per family. Assistance is provided to all household members. CRS disaggregates households for all types of analysis by the following: Gender of Head of household, Number of elderly members >65 years old, Number of females below 16 years old, Number of females above 16 years old, Number of males below 16 years old, Number of males above 16 years old. Cash assistance protections: To ensure that the vendors do not use the beneficiary PIN code and ID number for personal benefit, a text message will be sent to each beneficiary automatically after each cash withdrawal, informing him/her of his/her balance details. In addition, at the end of each cash withdrawal, PoS machines will deliver two receipts: one for the beneficiary and other for the vendor. The vendor will request from the beneficiary to sign the vendor's receipt to confirm the successful completion of the transaction. The Vendor will deliver all receipts to the CRS team upon request. System monitoring, Post-Distribution Monitoring and reporting: CRS and partners will conduct on-site and post-distribution monitoring (PDM) of cash usage and satisfaction with beneficiaries. During PDM CRS collects gender of respondent to the survey (not necessarily the head of household). CRS will monitor the cash withdrawal transactions on the electronic system and through on-site visits conducted by CRS and partner staff. In addition, CRS will use the following advanced monitoring procedures to protect the beneficiaries’ balances.

3 - Indicators

CRS is committed to collecting and reflecting on data throughout implementation to inform adaptive management. Analysis and interpretation of data collected from PDM and through the FRM will be undertaken on a regular basis to ensure that activities are responsive and that appropriate, timely steps can be taken to mitigate risks. During quarterly project reflection events, CRS and partner staff will identify lessons learned based on monitoring data and information collected from community feedback mechanisms that will be used to inform “learning-to-action” discussions and ultimately to inform adaptive management. During these discussions, project staff will identify specific action points to address lessons learned, such as mitigating challenges, better responding to the needs of vulnerable populations, and building on successes. CRS will measure and report on three main indicators throughout the project life to ensure the success of the project. Due to limited in-person interaction during COVID-19, as per CRS Standard Operating Procedures and WHO guidance, CRS will conduct data collection over the phone and consults with both men and women within the household. Indicator 1: # of Households receiving Multi-Purpose Cash This indicator will count the number of households who received MPCA for one month at least. The means of verification is the online database of households who received the MPCA PIN code. The target for this indicator is 1,000 households. Definitions: Beneficiaries: Households who received MPCA. MPCA assistance: PIN code shared with households to be used during shopping at selected vendor shops Indicator 2: Percentage of households who report being able to meet the basic needs of their households (all/most/some/none), according to their priorities This indicator will measure the percentage of households who reported they met their monthly multiple basic needs during the project period. The means of verification will be a random representative sample of selected households who participated in the post distribution monitoring (PDM) tool on a monthly basis. The target for this indicator is 70%. Definitions: Humanitarian assistance: multi-purpose cash assistance through the PIN code Basic needs: HH multi-sectoral monthly needs as defined in the MEB Indicator 3: % of households who withdraw cash within 30 days upon receipt This indicator will measure the percentage of households who withdraw cash on monthly basis before the next top- up. The indicator will be tracked on monthly basis until the last top-up is activated. The means of verification is the PALPAY online system that tracks all transactions for each distinct household Definitions: Withdrawn: withdraw 99% monetary value of the cash. 4. % of Total money spent on food. This indicator aims at reporting on FSS cash delivery versus other clusters. The MPCA categories designed by CWG includes food, shelter, wash, education, health, communication, clothing, debt, energy, transportation and COVID-19. CRS will use the monthly post distribution monitoring tool to compare the beneficiaries spending on food items including fresh fruits, vegetables, meat and fish compared to the spending on the other ten categories. The target for this indicator is 40% which is consistent with CWG MEB breakdown and CRS complementary programming. 5. Percentage of beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe, accessible, accountable and participatory manner. The target for this indicator is 95%. Data will be disaggregated by sex, age and disability. CRS will use the Post Distribution Monitoring to measure this indicator. To ensure achieving the highest impact of the project and ensure that men and women of all ages benefit from the project, CRS will consider the following points in the project MEAL plan: • CRS considers gender of head of household, number of elderly family members, number of family member under the age of five in selection criteria. • CRS measures household power dynamics during PDM data collection to explore decision-making dynamics within the household, what family members go to the shops to access assistance, and potential tensions at the household and community levels. • CRS engages both men and women in dual headed households to ensure that both men and women are oriented to project details. In addition, CRS encourages both women and men to use and decide on how to use the assistance together.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

CRS will provide MPCA to targeted HH to promote dignity of choice, respond to individual household preferences and needs, and ensure dietary diversity. This aligns with CWG priorities and is endorsed by FSS during the proposal design stage. Analysis from recent complementary programming indicated that beneficiaries used 60% of assistance on diverse food, which aligns with MSNA findings that Gazan families spend an average of 50% of their HH income on food. In addition, the MSNA survey shows that 77% of the surveyed people prefer the cash modality to receive the assistance while only 20% prefer the e-voucher modality. Additional findings from CRS complementary programming show significant improvements in food security indicators from baseline to endline: there was 69% decline in reduced Coping Strategy Index scores, 62% increase in HH categorized as having “little to no hunger” on the Household Hunger Scale, and 42% improvement in Food Consumption Scores with 99% of HH categorized as “acceptable.” Moreover, 86% of HH reported being able to meet most of their basic needs in the 30 days prior to endline. CRS is currently distributing MPCA for conflict affected HH in the five governorates of Gaza funded by HF-OCHA-3rd allocation, each family receives the value of the EMEB (200 USD). Moreover, CRS has extensive experience distributing cash as part of mixed wallet modality (50% cash and 50% restricted e-vouchers) serving 4,140 HHs with mixed-wallet cash and voucher assistance in 2021. CRS uses a robust electronic monitoring system and trusted trained vendors to distribute cash, covering all the targeted areas in the five governorates of the Gaza Strip. This approach provides beneficiaries with safe, accountable, and dignified access to project assistance with high reported satisfaction from PDM respondents.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

CRS takes a client-oriented, active approach to accountability. In addition to feedback collected during routine program monitoring, CRS has a comprehensive feedback and response mechanism (FRM) that includes a toll-free hotline and dedicated email account to receive feedback or complaints from project stakeholders, both direct project beneficiaries and other community members. For CRS, the FRM provides opportunities to learn from the people we assist and to identify problems early. This allows CRS to make improvements to our programs, alongside deterring fraud and corruption among staff members and partners. The FRM data management system allows all feedback cases to be stored in one place for documentation, rapid analysis and handling.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

CRS mainstreams protection into this project so that activities (i) prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm; (ii) ensure equality and meaningful access; (iii) include mechanisms for beneficiary accountability; and (iv) emphasize participation and empowerment. CRS prioritizes the safety and dignity of beneficiaries and avoids causing harm by preventing and minimizing, as much as possible, any unintended negative effects of its interventions that increase vulnerability to risks. This project will ensure that targeted beneficiaries have safe access to assistance based on need and without discrimination. CRS and its partner will make efforts to accommodate people with specific needs, ensuring access to assistance and services. CRS’ FRM reinforces the self-protection capacities of beneficiaries, thus assisting people to claim their rights. CRS prioritized the safety of beneficiaries through several activities especially during COVID-19. All project activities can be conducted remotely to enable CRS to deliver COVID-19 safe programming. In the past year, CRS supported over 10,000 vulnerable HH in all five Gaza governorates through an approach that mitigates the spread of COVID-19 (i.e. providing a cardless code to each beneficiary to replace the e-voucher debit card, thus reducing the risk of contamination and conducting virtual orientation sessions and remote price monitoring). Beneficiaries’ rights and responsibilities are further communicated to them during project orientation -- this includes setting expectations for the roles and responsibilities of CRS and partner staff in the delivery of the project assistance. During these sessions, participants are informed about CRS’ FCRMs, through which they can request information about the program, report an issue with assistance, or file a complaint. During these sessions CRS emphasizes that all family members have a share in the assistance value and encourages shared decision-making among all household members, and particularly between husband and wife of dual-headed households. This effort mitigates the risk of unbalanced power dynamic in the household across gender and age. UN Women reports that when women and girls have fair and equal access to resources, they can meaningfully participate in and influence decision-making processes. This makes them less vulnerable to sexual exploitation and negative coping strategies, such as child marriage or forced labour. CRS promotes dignity as much as possible in cash-voucher assistance (CVA) interventions by allowing beneficiaries to withdraw their cash amount balance from more than 40 vendor shops across the Gaza strip to help them meet their multiple needs. Vendors and partners also are oriented on beneficiary treatment without discrimination. The feedback channels allow beneficiaries to reach out and express their recommendations and challenges in the program. CRS and partners will seek to prevent any harm that could come to vulnerable populations throughout the project. The proposed design seeks to prevent tensions within the community by aligning the approach with CRS’ overall Emergency Response and therefore allows withdrawing cash. Further, CRS has included the option of home delivery within the project where the beneficiary can use the phone calls to ask for delivering the cash amount to the participants’ homes. The idea helps the beneficiaries who cannot go to supermarkets because they are in home-quarantine or persons with chronic dieses and more vulnerable to Covid-19 risk. The beneficiary uses the cardless code received on their mobiles when redeeming their vouchers. After executing the transaction by vendor, the beneficiary receives an immediate automated SMS of the exact deducted amount and a transaction number.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

CRS will coordinate with MoSD and FSS to ensure non-duplication with other actors delivering similar assistance. People living with disabilities and female-headed households are prioritized in the selection criteria. CRS provides money for transportation to elderly and persons living with disabilities so that limited mobility will not prevent these groups from accessing needed assistance. CRS’ FCRM ensures confidentiality and impartiality so that all project beneficiaries feel safe to access services and report feedback. CRS also employs gender-balanced partner teams to contact beneficiary HH to verify data, conduct phone-call orientation sessions and collect PDM survey data. This will maintain a safe environment to women to share comfortably their experience during the project. CRS has trained staff and partners on Protection and Safeguarding and delivers refresher trainings as needed. All partner staff will participate in the training. Topics include: introduction to child protection, protection risk analysis and mitigation strategies, safe programming and preventing unintended negative consequences, overview of the CRS Safeguarding Policy and self-audit, safe recruitment procedures, and development of a code of conduct and FCRM for beneficiaries. CRS has also worked with partners to develop their own related policies and an acknowledgment form to ensure staff understand and uphold the CRS Safeguarding Policy. These principles are reviewed at the start of every new project to ensure CRS and partners understand their rights and responsibilities to ensure the affected population is treated with dignity and respect.
