Project Summary

This project aims to provide life-saving and protection by contributing to the reduction of morbidity associated with malnutrition to targeted war-affected vulnerable communities in Greater Kordofan (South, north and West Kordofan), through the multi-sector interventions of WASH-IWRM, Education, FSL, GBV, Child protection and NFI’s, 75,000 (WASH), 16500 (Protection) and 10000 (Education), and 13116 FSL. IAS will target IDP’s, host communities, and those affected by War between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid support forces and tribal conflict or recurring natural disasters in North Kordofan Targeting (Sheikan, Um Rawaba, and Ar Rahad localities), South Kordofan targeting (Dilling, Abugubeiha and Ar Reif Ash Shargi) and West Kordofan in (Al Lagowa, Al Meiram and Keilak localities).
IAS will work in a complementary way to address WASH, Education, Protection and FSL to the same targeted students, boys and girls and their families, for schools, IAS will make sure the WASH and Protection gaps are addressed besides the Education where the child-friendly spaces, learning materials, water supply and sanitation and hygiene services will be provided to the targeted student and their families will be provided with Food Security and Livelihoods to support their living conditions.
IAS will utilize an Integrated Water Resources Management (IWRM) approach. Activities to be implemented with WASH include drilling of 20 new boreholes equipped with hand pumps, rehabilitation of 30 HPs, rehabilitation of 6 water yards including solarization, operate and maintain of 10 water yards, conducting water treatment and water quality monitoring for 20 water point per each activity, construction and rehabilitation of 12 institutional latrines and implementation of sanitation and hygiene to scale up the sanitation and promote the hygiene practices of at least 99,590 individual in the targeted communities. IAS, within this project, will provide life-saving education services, including School kits (learning materials) for 19200 individual (9216 girls, 6144 boys, 1920 male , 1920 female) including (13,440 IDPS, 2880 returnees , and 2880 host community) moreover 19200 will access child-friendly learning spaces(9216 girls, 6144 boys, 1920 male , 1920 female),20 improve or install temporary handwashing stations, in addition 20 temporary learning spaces will rehabilitate or construct,19200 target children will access to psychosocial support in addition to support of teaching and learning materials.

1 - Needs

In Greater Kordofan the War between Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces in Mid-April 2023, with a great effect on North Kordofan as it is in the middle between Darfur and Khartoum where the armed tropes are moving, along with the tribal conflicts occurring in South and West Kordofan, according to IOM-DTM estimates that approximately 843,130 individuals (168,354 households) have been displaced internally as a result of the conflict a number of 17,000 individual were internally displaced due to the war between SAF and RSF in North Kordofan state and there is 3,508 people seek refugees in South Sudan and a number of 55,157 SSR returned to South Sudan from East Darfur, West Kordofan, South Kordofan and White Nile. The Rapid Need assessment conducted by Save the Children in May 2023 stated that the conflict has severe impacts on learning opportunities, schools in many states are either not functioning or closed. Some schools in safer areas such as White Nile, South Kordofan and Gezira are currently occupied by families fleeing Khartoum and Darfur regions, thus schools cannot be used for learning purposes Examinations are interrupted in certain states due to the occupation of schools as well as conflict restricting movement and access to schools and forced displacement of teachers and children. The assessment has highlighted the need of co-lead the EiE cluster, actions to retain learners and teachers in schools, integration with CPiE to protect children from physical threats, mental health and psychosocial support for children, access to safe and protective learning environment and the need for coordination with CP, Nutrition, and WASH to promote an integrated approach. Acute food insecurity in Sudan is escalating rapidly and worsening the already high burden of food insecurity before the conflict. According to FEWSNET, the ongoing conflict has caused a swift and unanticipated disruption to trade and market functionality, household mobility, humanitarian assistance, and basic service provision, including healthcare, banking, electricity, transport, and communication, has left millions of people facing critical shortages of food, water, and basic supplies, including in dense urban areas and in greater Darfur, which hosts a large share of displaced and acutely food insecure people. Lack of safe drinking water has been reported in all areas experiencing conflict, increasing the risk of water-borne diseases, including cholera and mosquito-borne diseases (dengue fever and malaria) due to negative coping mechanisms such as drinking unsafe water, poor hygiene practices such as handwashing, the clean food chain. Water quality treatment units, drilling rigs and other equipment have also been destroyed, exposing people to untreated drinking water. Newly displaced people find themselves in crowded IDP camps experiencing shortages of water and latrines, further increasing health and GBV risks . It is also worth mentioning that most of the displaced population from conflict-affected areas to neighboring countries are women, girls, and children. Female-headed households and persons with disabilities are more vulnerable to the consequences of conflict, including displacement, loss of income, and limited access to basic services and food supplies. Displacement, lack of basic services, and breakdown of law and order have exacerbated the situation, leaving women and girls vulnerable to sexual violence, forced marriage, and other forms of abuse.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Due to our experience and lessons learned in the multi-year Aqua 4 Sudan IWRM project, IAS uses IWRM principles to enhance the environment in the design, implementation, and closing of our WASH humanitarian projects. For 2022, IAS will be implementing Hafirs using the community-involved catchment approach, the assessments wil also be based on catchment while coordinating with WES the decisions governing all things related to the intervention, including environmental actions. Rather than continuing to use up existing precious water by drilling more boreholes, this approach guides rainwater (90% of which is normally lost) through the use of sand dams and other subsurface dams to replenish existing wells. The environmental impact for safe water provision is naturally very positive in terms of supporting healthier lifestyles, a better quality of lives, and supporting plants and agricultural projects, etc., but with the use of cement and construction materials, great care will be given to cleaning up after each project site; that each water point intervention area will be cleaner than before IAS commenced the project. Inclusive community/ main actors project design, initiation, and planning meetings will be the stage for environmental protection and planning advocacy. Beneficiaries will be tasked with developing a long-term environmental plan and will sign-off and be accountable to the authorities on their progress. For every water point, IAS will conduct Environment Impact Assessments (EIAs). Taking the data from these surveys, we educate targeted beneficiaries to take the necessary steps to improve the environment. Communities will be involved in cleaning up water points and latrine construction sites. Fences and shrubs will be set up around the area. Conservation of natural resources, especially water, will be central themes in WMC meetings throughout the project, including close-out activities, including inspection, ensuring that the environment activities were carried out and the environment enhancement plan was completed for the future each beneficiary community where IAS is intervening. As well as budgeting for water point maintenance needs, WMCs will be requested to work with the community to budget environmental enhancement activities. Finally, those receiving environmental advocacy and planning training will be encouraged to bring their knowledge into the local schools.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

In emergency responses, IAS will continue to facilitate meaningful participation and engagement of affected communities, including training and messaging throughout the program, including child protection needs assessment, response planning, and monitoring. In carrying out community-based protection services, IAS will pay attention to the choice of locations and targeting of beneficiaries to avoid perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner, conducting community safety audit and mapping of exiting services and ensuring that the results of these two exercises inform programming and advocacy. IAS will maintain transparent, inclusive, and adequate communication with communities on available services, consulting communities and duty bearers in the planning, selecting services, and feedback after service provision. Partners will also be guided on ensuring prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable, such as young children, children with disability boys and girls, children without adult care, ensuring that programming is tailored to respond to all communities' needs. Child Protection services and response interventions will continue to be based on a quality and integrated approach, including coordination and capacity building with other humanitarian actors to support Protection mainstreaming and a do-no-harm approach as well as principled response. Besides, IAS will build on and integrate, wherever possible, feedback mechanisms to strengthen accountability and support the interagency coordination mechanism to respond to relevant complaints. IAS will continue working closely with partners to monitor beneficiaries' feedback, share information, and conduct community consultations, ensuring adequate accountability to affected populations (AAP). Child Protection training for partners will often include sessions on AAP and Child Protection response. During the implementation of this project, IAS will ensure equal participation of all vulnerable groups, including children with disabilities and other vulnerable populations, in all decision-making processes concerning needs identification. As COVID 19 hits globally, IAS will ensure safeguarding the poor and vulnerable groups, including children with disabilities, through continuous massages that will be provided within all project implementation activities. During latrines rehabilitation, facilities will have enough lights to ensure girls' and children's safety during usage. During WASH activities implementation, IAS will position the water facilities in the appropriate location accessible to women and girls to ensure their safety from sexual exploitation and protect vulnerable from being trafficking and be subjected to join armed groups.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Safety and dignity for affected people, following the guidance of the Global WASH Cluster and the HCT. The minimum obligations are operationalized in the accountability framework developed by WASH and other sectors and partners, which guides partners to 1) provide the minimum information to the public, 2) involve the community in decision making, 3) learn from feedback and complaints, 4) encourage appropriate staff attitude and behaviors. 5) use information from project learning, and 6) develop programs based on participatory assessments. The WASH cluster will continue to guide partners in adopting the framework by sharing best practices and peer learning. The implementation of the frameworks will be monitored through a self-reporting mechanism, while the clusters will also advise partners on relevant indicators, they can include in their monitoring efforts (including post-distribution monitoring). As part of its community-based response, IAS will engage and involve affected populations among others, establishing community-based protection networks and committees, including IDPs, women, and youth, to foster two- way communication and feedback with the humanitarian community and mobilize capacities toward the protection of all members of the community, particularly the vulnerable. Community protection initiatives also involve the community's participation in raising awareness of crucial protection issues relevant to women, girls, boys, and men to prevent or resolve conflicts and foster peaceful co-existence. IAS will also establish feedback and complaint mechanisms, such as protection hotlines that provide information on services, suggestion boxes in child-friendly spaces, safe spaces for women, CFRM-Complaints Feedback Response Mechanism and regular trends analysis. Other accountability mechanisms include the Accountability and Impact Assessment Framework of the GBV Sub-Cluster, as well as Child Protection Minimum Standards, which supports accountability between partners and meaningful child participation in program implementation. All interventions will include SEA mechanisms.
