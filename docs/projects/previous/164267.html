Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Syria has been exacerbated by the widespread use of explosive ordnance. Explosive ordnance poses significant risks and require different mitigation strategies to reduce their impact on the civilian population and the humanitarian sector delivering aid and relief to those in need. While the full scale of contamination is not known, preliminary findings for the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that one in three communities report some form of explosive contamination, affecting some 11.5 million people. The presence of contamination in essential civilian infrastructure, agricultural lands, homes and roads is a lethal barrier to safe movement which: deprives civilians from accessing essential basic services; hampers the delivery of humanitarian assistance; endangers those seeking refuge from violence or returning to areas affected by the conflict; and precludes the resumption of livelihood activities. A comprehensive humanitarian mine action response is thus a humanitarian necessity and a vital precursor for the delivery of humanitarian aid and early recovery.
The overall aim of UNMAS project “Protecting Civilians through Humanitarian Mine Action in Syria” is to reduce the impact of explosive hazards on the civilian population and humanitarian workers in Syria through effective sector coordination and the implementation of humanitarian mine action activities across Syria, in line with humanitarian priorities as identified in the Humanitarian Response Plan, and within the framework of agreements between the Syrian State and UN agencies, funds and programmes, as well as Security Council resolution 2504 (S/RES/2504, 2020) . Since the opening of the UNMAS Damascus office in October 2018, one of the key focus is on the development of the Humanitarian mine action sector and capacity enhancement of civilian actors to conduct mine action operations within the Government controlled areas. As such, UNMAS will work on the advancement of humanitarian technical mine action standards and frameworks to support the development of a Syrian civilian capacity, ensure humanitarian needs-based prioritization in line with the HRP, and the expansion of humanitarian mine action activities from risk education to explosive hazard survey, marking and clearance related activities. Under this project, in accordance with this humanitarian response plan, UNMAS will lead, manage and coordinate humanitarian mine action activities: risk education, victim assistance and clearance as defined by International Mine Action Standards (IMAS). The delivery of risk education, tailored to age, gender, localized threats and activity patterns of different at-risk groups, is a critical, life-saving activity for raising the awareness of civilians and humanitarian personnel about the dangers posed by explosive hazards, thus directly contributing to HRP strategic objective 1: “Save Lives” and strategic objective 2: “Enhance Protection”. As the Syrian crisis has been characterized by large-scale conflict and displacement, survey, data collection, and mapping are critical to informing on localized threats, humanitarian programming, and needs-based prioritization, as well as the expansion of clearance to remove the contamination threats facing civilians, particularly in high-priority areas. Furthermore, UNMAS will support the expansion of victim assistance and provision of specialized services for persons with disabilities, in particular survivors of explosive hazards and their families, thus contributing to strategic objective 3: “Increase Resilience and Access to Services”.

4 - Needs

Mine action is a humanitarian need in Syria. The scale, severity, and complexity of the explosive hazard threat in Syria remains a major protection concern, compounding the humanitarian crisis and the vulnerability of civilians in affected areas. According to the 2020 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 11.5 million men, women, boys, and girls live in the 2,562 communities reporting explosive contamination. While all people in areas contaminated with explosive hazards are vulnerable, certain population groups are at greater risks in correlation with gender and age roles, responsibilities, and displacement status. Men and boys are considered to be the most exposed to the direct, immediate threat of explosive incidents, while women and girls are more affected by their indirect impact, such as the deprivation of freedom of movement and the exacerbation of underlying vulnerabilities. Risk education is indeed crucial to empower people at risk with greater awareness and reducing the daily risks posed by contamination. Explosive hazard accidents can result in serious injury and/or death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families, severely impacting the lives and livelihoods of those affected. Reports reveal that about 50% of incidents take place in fields and rural areas and 25% of recorded incidents have occurred in the immediate vicinity of residential buildings. A majority of injuries due to explosive hazard accidents are leading to lifelong impairments (amputation, paralysis) and disabling situations. In addition to physical and psychological impact, explosive hazard contamination can have severe socio-economic implications on families and communities, depriving them of livelihoods and sources of income, increasing vulnerabilities. Sustaining and scaling-up humanitarian mine action efforts in Syria is an essential protection measure that will have direct positive impacts on other humanitarian interventions in Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNMAS represents the Mine Action AoR within the coordination mechanism for Syria where it provides technical expertise and support, ensures that Mine Action is integrated in the planning and programming of the wider humanitarian response, therefore strengthening its effectiveness and positive impact. Risk education is the first critical step to preventing civilian casualties from explosive hazards. UNMAS plans to continue the delivery of risk education tailored to age, gender, nature of threats, and activity patterns, with a view to enhancing its efficiency, effectiveness and positive impact. Survey and Clearance of explosive hazards is the only way to fully remove the threat of explosive hazards posed to civilians.e. UNMAS currently, is in the process of identifying target areas to deploy survey and clearance capacity upon the basis of humanitarian priorities in line with the HRP and in consultation with relevant authorities. UNMAS will identify a suitable partner to implement survey and clearance activities, to be carried out in accordance with humanitarian principles in selected community sites throughout Syria determined by the humanitarian sector and frameworks, in particular HNO and HRP. UNMAS will further deploy an Explosive Ordnance Assessment (EOA) capacity to support humanitarian agencies’ work in infrastructures/areas where suspected explosive hazards impede the lifesaving humanitarian activities in support of the Syrian people. Victim assistance for persons with disabilities, including survivors of explosive hazard accidents, is underfunded and under implemented whilst population needs are growing. In coordination with relevant stakeholders, UNMAS will continue its victim assistance initiatives, including the provision of specialized services such as physical rehabilitation, Mental Health and Psychosocial Support through referral pathways with specialized partners. Further, UNMAS aims to set-up a Victim Assistance framework within the Syria hub.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Activity: Risk education Risk: Familiarization causes civilians to become overconfident or complacent to the threat of explosive hazards and expose them to greater risk. Mitigation Measures: 1.Emphasis on key “Stop, Warn, and Report” messaging in risk education; 2.Conduct regular surveys to monitor increased awareness; 3.Constantly review awareness material and training to the context, based on information gathered during the sessions. Activity: Clearance Risk: Humanitarian personnel are injured by explosive hazards or other security incidents. Mitigation Measures: 1.Security updates are provided regularly to inform field teams on security developments; 2.Delivery of risk education training, including a first aid component, to humanitarian personnel to increase awareness of the explosive threat; 3.A quality management system is in place to observe operator’s compliance with international standards; field visits are conducted regularly by UNMAS personnel. Risk: Humanitarian clearance-related operators perceived as discriminatory and non-neutral. Mitigation Measures: 1.Use of clear and visible identification as humanitarian workers; 2.Minimize non-essential interaction with armed forces and armed non-State actors; 3.Include HLP considerations when setting MA priorities to avoid land disputes and other HLP issues. Risk: Reports of clearance activity leading to IPDs returns in unsafe/partially contaminated areas. Mitigation Measures: 1.Conduct community liaison with affected communities to spread awareness; 2.Use of visible, clear marking for explosive hazards. Activity: Victim Assistance Risk: Local population may become reliant on victim assistance provided, eroding long-term sustainability. Mitigation Measures: 1.Coordinate with development/health organizations to ensure the establishment of local capacities. 2.Develop projects that can be sustained on a long-term basis 3.Beneficiaries requiring long-term assistance are referred to specialized health provider

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Key assumptions: 1.UNMAS is provided access to conduct activities in accordance with UNSCR 2504(2020) and the MOU with the Syrian Arab Republic. 2.UNMAS operations conducted in line with Humanitarian Principles are facilitated by relevant authorities, as necessary. 3.Implementing partners are identified in a timely manner and accredited by relevant authorities and allowed access to in-need communities. 4.Mine Action AoR partnerships and collaboration are sustained and respected. 5.Sustained and predictable funding of humanitarian mine action in Syria by donors is provided. UNMAS identified the following risks and appropriate mitigation measures for the successful implementation of the project. Political: Risk: The political and/or security situation deteriorates Mitigation: UNMAS will adapt, adjust and maintain operational readiness as required in accordance with needs. Operations: Risk: Access restrictions limit freedom of movement, hamper prioritization of efforts and ability to respond to the most at need communities. Mitigation: UNMAS will maintain active engagement and advocacy efforts for humanitarian Mine Action activities with all relevant stakeholders, in-country and at Headquarters. Sustained restriction of its ability to act in compliance with humanitarian principles may result in the suspension of its operation. Risk: Delay in registration of IP/Contractors, and delays in Governmental approval negatively affecting operations. Mitigation: pro-active advance preparation and consultations with the authorities. Security: Risk: Conflict and/or safety concerns for humanitarian workers limits accessibility Mitigation: UNMAS remains abreast of conflict and security developments to inform planning and maintains a flexible implementation approach. Financial: Risk: Actual costs vary outside of tolerance levels foreseen in its budget Mitigation: Continuous resource mobilization, including with other humanitarian partners support and contributions.
