Project Summary

The overall aim of this project is to contribute to improving overall health, wellbeing, gender equality and resilience of Rohingya Refugees and vulnerable people in host communities of Cox’s Bazar District through community engagement and participation. The project has mainly three sub sectors covering Rohinga Refugee camps and the host communities. The target populations of the project are 260,312 Rohingya refugees living in eight refugee camps and 100,000 vulnerable Bangladeshi people living in Teknaf and Uhkia in Cox’s Bazar District.
Improved access to safe water supply: The project will continue to support routine repair, operation and maintenance of existing piped water supply systems and hand pump wells. In addition, areas not covered by the existing piped water systems, will be identified and assessed for options for the continued provision of safe drinking water services. Small- scaled water supply systems and extension of pipeline networks of existing water systems will be prioritized to ensure equitable distribution of safe water supply to vulnerable populations. The project will support water quality surveillance which will be conducted by DPHE with support of WHO and UNICEF. In addition, the project will support Dhaka University for ground water monitoring, and establishing and running Groundwater Modelling Sub Centre in Cox’s Bazar.
Safely managed sanitation: For sanitation, the project focuses on operations, maintenance and improvement of existing latrines and bathing spaces to ensure functionality, quality, safety, privacy and addresses the needs of women, girls, PwD, the elderly and other vulnerable people in the camps. The project will upgrade or rehabilitate/reconstruct WASH blocks for inclusive access to sanitation facilities and services. The operation and maintenance of Fecal Sludge Management (FSM) will be continued.
New FSM sites will be constructed to fill the critical gaps and increase the efficiency. The project will support the expansion, operation and maintenance of SWM sites for composting and segregation units. The targeted Rohingya refugees will also benefit from effective Solid Waste Management services in their camps. Through this project, house to house waste collection will be implemented instead of the communal waste collection.
Hygiene promotion and community engagement: The hygiene promotion will focus on community engagement changing identified high-risk behaviors through the participatory approaches such as CCCPLTH (Clean Camp Campaign-People Let Total Hygiene) and RANAS (Risk, Attitudes, Norms, Abilities and Self-Regulation) approaches as well as supporting participatory community monitoring and feedback mechanisms to contribute to AAP (Accountability to Affected Populations). Gender, GVB and Disability inclusion will be mainstreamed in all interventions. Awareness-raising amongst women and adolescents of reproductive age, men and boys on MHM will be prioritised and strengthened.
Life-saving WASH supplies: The project will continue to support the provision of WASH supplies such as soap, Aquatab and MHM materials. The proejct will procure emergency WASH supplies for prepositioning and/or replenishment of emergency stocks of supplies for natural disasters and other emergency situations.
WASH in host communities: In host community, the project will support with piped water supply systems, development of water safety plans (WSP), capacity development of community groups for sustainable management for water supply systems. Promotion of good hygiene practices and MHM among reproductive age women and girls will be a priority. The project will also support on improvement of WASH services in institutions mainly schools and health centres. In addition, the project will also support the creation of open defecation free (ODF) communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf through CATS approach and sanitation marketing. Priority will be given to the adjoining communities of eight refugee camps.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Access to quality and equitable WASH services and improved hygiene behaviours in the Rohingya camps remains challenging, where UNICEF and partners' support is still required. Ensuring gender needs, protection, and privacy concerns of women and girls, including PwD remain a challenge. Although around 70 per cent of the Rohingya refugees have access to chlorinated/treated water from piped water systems, half of Rohingya households face difficulties getting enough treated water. Most households only have 14.3 litres/person/day of treated water compared to the WASH sector standard of >=20liters/person/day. This impacts more on women, girls and boys, who are disproportionately responsible for fetching drinking water for household use. While 92 per cent of community water points/sources (tube wells) are free from E. Coli contamination, only 61 per cent of water stored at the household level is free of E. Coli contamination (figure 1 below). E. coli contamination of water household level is high. Generally, latrine coverage in most of the camps is above the WASH sector standard of one latrine per 20 users. However, in four of the eight UNICEF supported camps (6,7,8E and 8W), the average number of persons per toilet is more than 22 persons per toilet due to high population density and inadequate latrines. The JAM 2021 Report showed that 59 per cent of the refugees reported inadequate access to sanitation facilities due to waiting times, cleanliness, distance and overcrowding. Women and girls have expressed concerns about the distance to latrines and grave fears of sexual abuse when using them. PwDs also faced difficulties in accessing latrines. An Age and Disability Inclusion needs assessment conducted in 2021 found that 30 per cent of persons with disabilities could not use latrines without support. This is because the type of standard latrines constructed within the camps are twin pit latrines and are not adapted to the needs of persons with disabilities. The hilly terrain complicates desludging activities of latrines and has increased the need for additional FSM sites to provide sufficient treatment capacity and reduce sludge transportation distances. Solid waste is a major challenge in the camps as only 51 per cent of the refugee households are disposing of waste in designated areas. While hygiene knowledge and access to soap are high, access to menstrual hygiene management (MHM) materials remains a challenge for many women and girls due to taboo/restrictive practices and limited preference or choice. Around 31 per cent of women and girls of reproductive age expressed dissatisfaction over the frequency of MHM distribution (distributed twice/year), MHM material runout, and the supply of panties was not enough. Cox’s Bazar is one of the most socioeconomically vulnerable districts in Bangladesh and has the worst WASH indicators. According to the Cox’s Bazar district WASH Mapping Report, 24 per cent of households in the district do not have access to basic water supply, and only 18 per cent of them has access to safely managed water supply. The report also showed that 29 per cent of households have access to safely managed sanitation, while only 36 per cent have access to basic sanitation. The influx of Rohingya refugees has put an enormous burden on the poor WASH situation of the host communities. It has also impacted prices, labour wages, and agricultural lands of the host community, including groundwater. It is thus increasingly critical to address the needs of the host communities to reduce tensions and increase the prospects of a peaceful relationship between the refugees and the host community.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

UNICEF and its implementing partners will fully comply with the "Principles of Rationalization" in the Rohingya Refugee Response by ensuring that all Rohingya refugees have equitable access to WASH services in a predictable, efficient, and timely manner. As part of the operationalisation of rationalisation, UNICEF introduced the "one camp one organisation" approach in the eight camps since the beginning of 2022. There are six implementing partners (BRAC, CARE, NGO F, World Vision and VERC) in eight camps. CARE and NGO Forum have taken responsibility for providing WASH services in two camps each. In each camp, there is only one WASH focal agency which is responsible for coordination with the government and humanitarian agencies. The remaining four partners are responsible for one camp each. Therefore, this approach has standardised WASH service delivery to the refugees in eight camps in equitable and cost-effective ways. By implementing the "one camp one organisation" approach, there are no overlaps of services in the camps, and critical gaps in services are addressed by the relevant implementing partner responsibly. In the host communities, Upazilla level WASH sector coordination groups have been set up to provide WASH services to refugee crisis-affected people in a coordinated and equitable way.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

The project will ensure the protection, dignity, and privacy concerns of affected populations, especially women and girls, including persons with disability (PwD), to access WASH services without facing maltreatment. The project will also work on supporting women and girls in addressing gender and protection needs and empowering them to actively participate in the decision-making process toward safe and sustainable WASH services. Regular monitoring will be continued through Community Feedback and Response System, establishing Community Structures and MHM facilitators groups etc. Gender-specific topics will be incorporated into WASH awareness messaging as well as training and orientation of partners staff and front-line workers. Partners staff as well as community people will be oriented on gender-based violence (GBV) in WASH programs to ensure protection issues appropriately. UNICEF and its partners will work closely with the GBV sub-sector.

3.2 - Gender

Gender-Transformative WASH Programming in UNICEF is a new agenda reflected in the GAP 2022-25 that focuses on expanding transformative approaches. Specific emphasis is placed on supporting adolescent girls’ leadership and well-being besides all other women, PwD, and disadvantaged people. UNICEF through its implementing partners likes to be achieved gender-transformative changes by reducing. Gender inequality in the WASH programme will address by identifying structural and social root causes of gender inequality, increasing the allocation and distribution of resources, and advancing gender equitable norms, behavior and practices including by disrupting discriminatory beliefs and stereotypes. The current programme will be further reviewed before developing approaches toward gender transformations. The project will also address the issues related to gender inequalities to access WASH services and ensure privacy and meaningful participation of women and girls in decision-making position.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

Children’s protection from any harm (like: violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect) while access to the WASH services. It will be ensured by taking safeguarding measures for those age under the age of 18 children. All kinds of discrimination must be reduced by responding to children’s specific needs and preventing potential risks through WASH interventions. Safeguarding is also needed for those aged 18 years or over who may need care services because of disability, older age, or illness. Ensuring that all age groups, including males and females, will be served, focusing on children of under-five age groups. Partners staff and workers will be trained and will be able to take appropriate actions related to age, including child safeguarding under the WASH programme.

3.4 - Diversity

JRP WASH project will ensure supporting diversity including Rohingya Refugees and ethinic minotrity groups/ultra-poor in two Upazila. The project will seeks to work on leaving no one behind. UNICEF is guided by human rights and children’s rights-based approaches. The project will ensure non-discrimination and equality; inclusion and participation; and accountability and transparency. The project will include people from different social and ethnic backgrounds, genders, and sexual orientations which will be identified through in-depth assessment within Rohingya and host communities. Involvement of diverse community leaders and stakeholders through stakeholders mapping. Environmental features will be addressed by developing a climate-resilient WASH programme as new shifting.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

UNICEF and the implementing partners will continue to promote compliance with AAP commitments on participation & partnership & information, feedback & action. Community consultations will be conducted during the planning, implementation and post-project phases. WASH facilities will be designed, repaired or upgraded with the engagement & participation of the community. The project will strengthen accountability by engaging local community volunteers while developing program interventions, promoting information & community feedback mechanism among the communities, & collecting WASH-related complaints & feedback. Different methodologies for collecting feedback are being used, with a combination of solicited & unsolicited approaches. This includes complaint box, FGD & house visits from CBVs. These methodologies are used to ensure that the voice of the most vulnerable is being heard (such as women) & provide them with an environment of safety & confidentiality to express their opinions.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

WASH project will have strong integration with DRR and climate change while raising awareness of WASH issues and designing implementing facilities. Relevant environmental aspects will be considered in terms of forestation and groundwater issues. DRR and climate change will be prior integration with WASH as Cox's Bazar are historically climate change threatened and disaster-prone, and two JRP Upazila and camps are the most vulnerable considering their coastal locations.
