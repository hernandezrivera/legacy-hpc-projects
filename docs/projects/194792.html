Project Summary

This project will be implemented in the 10 Counties of Kapoeta East, North, South, Torit, Budi, Ikwotos, Lopa/Lafon, Magwi, Ibba and Maridi to provide essential integrated quality health care services to improve access and scale - up response to the most vulnerable populations targeting 45,600 (18,545 male & 19,302 females) under 5 years (3,799 boys and 3,954 girls) in need for humanitarian Emergency health care services through provision of lifesaving emergency assistant in line with national and international humanitarian standards, and also the epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, disease burden seasonality, and trauma-related emergency requirements. Prevention, preparedness, response readiness, response, and mitigation tailored to activities with special attention given to providing vulnerable groups including women, children and persons with disabilities.
Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment and Needs (OPEN) will support different intervention modalities suited to the needs of affected people, including Ebola Virus Disease (EVD), COVID-19 control and prevention interventions that Contributes to a continue prevention, preparedness, and response readiness to complement Health partners in the two States. OPEN will further support static and mobile health facilities, and community-based healthcare systems to serve communities who do not have access to health care facilities and are affected by emergencies through static health facilities in crisis-affected areas by identifying communities that are still not reachable and providing services through mobile outreach as well as strengthening support to static health facilities that will strengthen the Community Based surveillance project in the 10 Counties.
Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment and Needs (OPEN have over 7 years of experience in implementing Clinical health packages in both emergency and development in the Ten Counties of Eastern and Western Equatoria State and support community based surveillance, COVID-19 vaccination, EVD outbreak responses, EPI routine vaccination, Risk Communication and Community Engagement, support point of Entry of Nimule and Pogee with funding from USAID and also supported other health interventions through Christian connection for International health (CCIH), World Relief International, Save The Children, World Vision International and Christian connection for International health to improve access to essential health services that contribute to the overall reduction of the high maternal mortality rates and under-five mortality rates and mitigate the transmission of vaccine-preventable diseases and other infectious diseases.
With this current appeal, OPEN will strengthen support to the patients and health staff including infection prevention and control (IPC) and WASH measures), COVID-19 and EVD appropriate prevention and management activities complementing the ongoing Community based surveillance in the 10 Counties.
The response plan will also prioritize epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, EVD surveillance, cholera hotspot areas, measles outbreaks, malaria seasonality with response modalities community surveillance. IDSR, EWARS, attacks on health care and disability tracking tools inform the determination of the feasibility of the response through guidance from health cluster to save lives by providing timely and integrated multi sectoral assistance to increase service access for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders to reduced acute health needs in a realization of the three health cluster objectives:
1. Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations.
2. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases.

1 - Needs

The Population Eastern and Western Equatoria States are both pastoral and farmers communities. In Eastern Equatoria, the trends of movement is seasonality drives with reported cattle rustling, Food insecurity and the food security situation projection is estimated 507,000 people (46.2% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 367,000 people are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3), and 140,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity in Counties of Budi, Kapoeta North, and Kapoeta South will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, while the rest of the counties will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity according to FEWSNET: Additionally, the entire State is experiencing internal displacement resulting lack of food due to failed rains, poor agricultural practices and insecurity caused by cattle keepers, and reliance on pastoralist livelihoods, scarce/ very low access to water for household and livestock use that led to over 5,000 IDPs in kapoeta North from Jie Payam of Kapoeta East and also the current over 1,680 households (10,547) individuals displaced in Magwi County and therefore, distance, insecurity, and inadequate numbers of functional health facilities across Eastern and Western Equatoria States due to closure of Health pooled Support Primary acre units impact people’s ability to access medical facilities, care and appropriate medicine in hard to reach areas. Also lack of enough healthcare workers was also commonly reported across the States as low pay and security concerns affected health workers and consistently malaria remained the main reported cause of death and much higher proportions of (between 80%-86%) reported malaria as the main cause of deaths in all the Counties. These factors combined with low access to health facilities are likely to result to poor health outcome. Incidents of cattle raiding, driven by lack of access to water resources, may have reduced the perception of safe access to preferred water points for these communities, in particular because women and children are commonly the ones to collect water. The risk of waterborne disease and possible epidemic outbreaks remained relatively high. Furthermore, the Needs contextual analysis from, IRNA, IDSR, EWARS and EPI Reports indicated that, incidence rate for common diseases standing at 19 % in Kapoeta South with CFR for common diseases 68% in Kapoeta North while Measles coverage gaps for children from 6 months – 15 years 60 %, PENTA 3 coverage gaps of 71% in Kapoeta East. Hence, due to the high disease burden resulting from common diseases, epidemic outbreak, pandemic and neglected diseases the population targeted for humanitarian healthcare services is based on multiple drivers including, displacements, malnutrition, high disease burden from vaccine preventable and communicable diseases including Seasonal outbreaks of common diseases including cholera and measles with continued endemicity of malaria is posing a challenge to a health system which is already fragile.

2 - Project activities

This intervention will focus on provision of Medical consultation and treatment for common diseases, maternal child health such as immunization, ANC, delivery & Post-natal care, EVD outbreak responses and COVID-19 vaccination and strengthening integration of (WASH, Nutrition GBV etc.) in Health care facilities. Also - psychosocial counseling, distribution long lasting insecticide treated nets and support active surveillance for epidemic prone diseases such as malaria, cholera, measles, COVID-19, hemorrhagic fever and meningitis, referrals for Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CeMONC), minor trauma and surgery, scale up TB/HIV/AIDS/Disability care, Scale up integrated response to SAM with medical complications and Procurement 5% of emergency health pipeline to fill in gaps for medical supplies and other essential equipment' are key project activities. To achieved desired results, Organization for Peoples’ empowerment and Needs (OPEN) will use both static and mobile intervention modalities in Kapoeta East, North, South, Budi, Torit, Ikwotos, Lafon and Magwi Counties.

3 - Project indicators

Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment and Needs (OPEN) will collect, analyse and use monitoring data to measure performance against a set of indicators for the response plan on a Monthly basis. The data analysis for response planning, monitoring and evaluation will include Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) data, Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS) data, assessment reports from Initial Rapid Need Assessments (IRNA), multi-cluster and sectoral initial rapid assessments, public health situation analysis, health service functionality, 5Ws, the health resources availability monitoring system, public health risks, outbreak investigation reports, and mortality and morbidity estimates. OPEN will strengthen capacities of data collection and information analysis for planning team, response and evaluation. The indicators are aligned with response activities that aim to improve living conditions of vulnerable people and are set to identify incident rates for selected diseases, case fatality rates, health facilities supported with essential health care commodities, outpatient consultations, functional health facilities, assisted deliveries by skilled birth attendants, attacks on health care, facilities and beneficiaries for CMR (Clinical Management of Rape), MHPSS and disability services. Insecurity and attacks on health care and workers have limited movement and access limiting documentation of cluster response. OPEN will provide training on data management for improved reporting and use various community communication and engagement modalities, including robust complaints and feedback mechanisms and community discussions. It will monitor affected communities’ perception of and satisfaction with service provision through community feedback mechanism such as health committees. It will support the collective monitoring of whether the targeted population feels consulted and informed throughout the response.
