Project Summary

The emergency this project will address is the high GAM rate in Fangak, Nyirol and Uror counties for children under 5. All three counties are rated at the severity of need classification 4 for acute malnutrition and food insecurity. GAM rates of U5 children are 19.6% in Nyirol, 22.2% in Fangak and 25.5% in Uror (NC PiN for 2023, 10 Nov 22). Malnutrition prevalence for PLW is 53.3% in Fangak, 71.9 % in Nyirol and 78.2% in Uror. The project will serve the payams of Manajang, Greater Pulita, Mareang and Phom in Fangak with a population of 115,524; the payams of Chuil, Pultruk, Thol, Nyambor, Pading and Waat in Nyirol with a population of 119,041; and the payams of Pathai and Tiam in Uror with a population of 61,840.
Flooding from past years along with insecurity continue to cause disruption of nutrition service delivery. The project will use a combination of approaches to strengthen sustainable nutrition outcomes, specifically: 1) Increased program of preventative actions that reduce the rate of malnutrition and improve the sustainability of treatments; 2) Community engagement by taking services closer to households, through the roll out of the revised CNV guidelines and Family MAUC screening; 3) Facility-based data analysis and feedback information to improve overall performance of service delivery and timely coordination of response decision making; 4) Intensified integration of nutrition with complementary sectors by gap analysis and coordinating with co-located partners in FSL with NPA, ADA and Oxfam, in Protection with FCA and NP and in WASH with ACF, SI and Oxfam. At all sites, nutrition will be completely integrated with health services with HFO in Fangak and with CMA in Nyirol and Uror.
The project will use a combination of response modalities - RRM/ICRM, static, outreach and mobile approaches with nutrition survival kits to ensure timely response to emergencies. Services will be delivered from 24 static OTP-TSFP sites, and these sites will support mobile outreach services to cover displaced populations in 23 additional sites. The response activities will cover (1) case-finding screening using the Family-MUAC method from static units and mobile outreaches to IDP locations; (2) admission to OTP services for severe acute malnourished U5 children and TSFP services using expanded criteria where needed including malaria testing; (3) provide SC services for SAM children with medical complications; (4) deliver expanded MIYCN services to PLW and care-givers of children under 23 months through counselling PLW, care-providers and MtoM support groups, including vitamin A supplements, de-worming and kitchen gardens.
The critical outputs will be treatment of: 1) U5 SAM children – Fangak 1002, Nyirol 3024, Uror 1107 (Total 5133); 2) U5 MAM children – Fangak 1928, Nyirol 4950, Uror 1625 (Total 8503); 3) MAM for PLW – Fangak 908, Nyirol 3685, Uror 945 (Total 5538).
The project will mainstream protection and disability inclusion and emphasize gender and age considerations. To improve sustainability, this project will strengthen local capacity of CHDs, project staff and CNVs through job-relevant training. To address insecurity risks for PLW and U5 children, CMA will conduct safety audits and collaborate with the targeted communities to implement protection practices to reduce risk of SGBV.
By applying IASC guidance, specific needs of men, women, boys and girls will be identified and addressed through a gender equity and social inclusion approach. In relation to AAP, the project will provide the information beneficiaries need to make informed decisions on accessing nutrition services, provide feedback on the interventions, share concerns and submit complaints. CMA will work with local authorities, leaders and beneficiaries, to ensure their inclusion in decision-making throughout the program cycle.

1 - Needs

Conflict, flooding and macro-economic challenges continue to worsen the nutrition emergency in Fangak, Nyirol and Uror counties of Jonglei State. FEWSNet projects the entire State will experience AFI IPC 4 Emergency for the October 2022 to January 2023 period (Key Message Update, FEWSnet, 30 Sep 2022). NC reports that U5 SAM and MAM admission rates for the January to September period are significantly higher than the past 3 years, and Fangak, Nyirol and Uror are among the counties with the highest increases (NC Program Updates, 14 Oct 22, slides 4, 5 and 8). All three counties targeted in this project are rated at the severity of need classification 4 for AMN. GAM rates of U5 children are 19.6% in Nyirol, 22.2% in Fangak and 25.5% in Uror and malnutrition prevalence for PLW is 53.3% in Fangak, 71.9% in Nyirol and 78.2% in Uror (NC PiN for 2023, 10 Nov 22). UNICEF released its indicative 2023 SAM admission targets on 24 Oct 22 showing Fangak at 11,110, Nyirol at 10,104 and Uror at 9,464 children. Uror is now rated as one of the 10 worst affected counties (Analysis of Humanitarian Need, NC, 10 Nov 22 pg. 1). While OTP/TSFP treatment services remain essential, there is clear need to strengthen preventative services as shown in the intersectoral analysis (Analysis of Humanitarian Need, NC, 10 Nov 22 pg. 2). To improve maternal and young child feeding practices, there is need for MIYCN services to increase kitchen gardening to improve access to minimum acceptable diet diversity (MADD) coupled with vitamin A supplementation and deworming. In addition, continued emphasis on training mothers/caretakers to monitor their own and their children’s nutrition status by conducting Family-MUAC screening provides community-based capacity to detect and respond to malnutrition, and reduce its severity among vulnerable young children. ACF reports that persistent gender inequality of women and girls hampers their access to nutrition and other social services and participation in nutrition programming (from Analysis of Humanitarian Need, NC, 10 Nov 22 pg. 2). The SS HNO 2022 (pg 65) shows GBV ranked severity 4 Extreme in Fangak and Uror and 3 Severe in Nyirol. The drivers of GBV remain unchanged from past years. Young children are vulnerable because of their age. Women and adolescent mothers are vulnerable because of their gender. The principal threats to women, children, the elderly and disabled are inter-communal clashes and flooding. GBV mainstreaming through a GESI approach is essential to counter the risks noted above and provide unimpeded access to services. Gender-balanced teams of frontline workers are essential to provide for the safety and dignity of those who most need nutrition services. These efforts are essential to improve gendered quality and safe access to nutrition services. It is universally recognised the sustained improvement in nutrition status can only be achieved when food security improves, when people have access to improved water and sanitation infrastructure, and adopt improved hygiene practices, and when people have access to prevention and treatments of malaria, diarrhea and other high prevalence diseases. To address these multiple drivers of chronic malnutrition, “deliberate efforts and investment to scale up multi-sector solutions are required” (Analysis of Humanitarian Need, NC, 10 Nov 22 pg. 2). This project will work with co-located partners in FSL with NPA, ADA and Oxfam, in Protection with FCA and NP and in WASH with ACF, SI and Oxfam to effectively combat the current nutrition emergency. In the context conflict and flooding, the project will use a combination of responses to reach the displaced with nutrition survival kits to ensure timely emergency response. To reach the displaced, there is need utilize mobile approaches by vehicle, boat, motorcycle, bicycle, canoe and walking, to link affected populations to nutrition services.

2 - Project activities

This project will focus on the implementation of a minimum package of actions to improve diets, services and practices for better nutrition outcomes. The project will be implemented in close collaboration with the national Ministry of Health-Nutrition Department and other clusters (WASH, Health, FSL & GBV). The project will provide for a gender-balanced team of frontline workers where 42% are female. Field personnel have been trained on CMA’s PSEA policy and all are expected to sign a PSEA code of conduct. The activities to achieve output targets are defined below:Sectoral Objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality preventative nutrition specific services for children, adolescents and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2023. To achieve the above objective, the respective activities will include the following: 1.1 Prevention of malnutrition through provision of blanket supplementary feeding programme (BSFP) 1.2 Prevention of malnutrition through behavior change communication, 1.3 Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies through micronutrient supplementation using campaign approach. Sectoral Objective 2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition in prioritized locations by the end of 2023. For objective 2, the following activities will be prioritized: 2.1 Early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and pregnant and lactating women affected by acute malnutrition, 2.2 Conducting Monitoring & Supportive supervision visits, 2.3 Scaling up capacity strengthening efforts through on job training (OJT)& mentoring 2.4 Advocating for integration of SC in the health facility service delivery 2.5 Promoting community participation for a responsive nutrition service delivery 2.6 Supporting CNVs to undertake home visits 2.7 Conducting spot exit interviews to assess the level of satisfaction of nutrition service delivery. Sectoral Objective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2023. Corresponding activities for objective 3 will include the following: 3.1 Supporting FSNMS survey to integrate nutrition indicators, 3.2 Supporting planning and roll- out of population-based surveys, 3.3 Conducting barrier analysis for MIYCN services 3.4 Improving facility-based data collection and management 3.5 Supporting roll-out of the Data Quality Assurance tool, 3.6 Improving nutrition indictors in DHS2. Sectoral Objective 4: To increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and integrated programming targeting vulnerable groups in prioritized locations by the end of 2023. Under objective 4 these activities will be undertaken: 4.1 Provision WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites. 4.2 Sensitize households on the correct use of aquatabs at by the CNVs, 4.3 Sensitize communities in the priority counties with hygiene and sanitation messages 4.4 Collaborate with Health in the Management of diarrhoea in the health & nutrition sites 4.5 Provide Zinc and ORS to children with diarrhea at health and nutrition sites, 4.6 Prevent, test and treat malaria among children under five years with acute malnutrition, in nutrition sites 4.7 Establish or maintain demonstration kitchen garden at nutrition sites for or in the vicinity 4.8 Integrate child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM 4.9 Mainstream child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Under the direction of the facility CMAM Supervisor, CMA’s on-ground personnel undertake patient exit interviews, focus group discussions (FGD) and key informant interviews (KII) as part of the planning and monitoring processes to ensure ongoing effective feedback. The CMAM Supervisors ensure that the most vulnerable disabled, illiterate, women and children are included FGD and KII sessions. At all sites, the affected population groups that CMA consults in the planning and feedback processes are as follows: (1) local women and men leaders, focus groups and key informant women and men; (2) Mother-to-Mother Support Groups, other social groups representing the most vulnerable including individuals living with disabilities and (3) discharged patients women, men, girls and boys including individuals living with disabilities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Needs assessment on entry to new locations and on an ongoing basis thereafter ensures that project activities are tailored to the different needs of community members’ especially adolescent mothers, new IDPs, the elderly and disabled. Information is disaggregated in respect of gender, age and the needs of different population groups to improve suitability of planned interventions. In locations already served and new locations, CMA will apply its established strategy and model to ensure accountability to affected populations. The structures and mechanisms established enable affected populations to participate actively in planning services, learn their rights and entitlement with respect to nutrition programs, receive information they need to make informed decisions on accessing services, provide feedback on interventions, voice and submit complaints and participate in program decision-making. CMA’s programs are implemented in collaboration with local authorities and leaders of host communities and IDPs. At all locations, monthly meetings with leaders, Mother-to-Mother Support Groups are conducted to report on project output and obtain community feedback. The Facility Clinical Officer or CMAM Supervisor will conduct discharge interviews with a 10% sample of the patients to obtain feedback. These consultations provide the most vulnerable of affected populations full awareness and opportunity to utilize the complaints mechanism, the opportunity to actively participate in planning and monitoring the project and to learn their entitlements for accessing project services and render complaints. CMA’s on-ground teams will ensure that at least 75% of affected populations are aware and able to utilize the complaints mechanism by practicing the principles of transparency, communication and provision of information linked to the complaints mechanism, and engaging the target populations to proactively seek out their feedback

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CMA has completed the rollout of its revised PSEA policy with training of all management at Juba and County levels and the facility based frontline personnel. Additional PSEA training is planned for CNVs and refresher on-the-job training will be conducted for all facility-based and mobile teams of frontline workers during the coming years. A core piece of the training covers dissemination of information about PSEA. The dissemination will be conducted through meetings with community groups, Mother-to-Mother Support Groups and local authorities. The dissemination will be conducted by the CMAM Supervisor at the facility level. Further, SC nurses, Nutrition Assistants and MIYCN Counsellors will ensure the patients they are treating are thoroughly informed about PSEA. Beneficiaries are advised on: (1) the content of the PSEA policy; (2) available channels for reporting abuse; (3) confidentiality rules in reporting abuse cases; and (4) the mechanisms and locations available for victims of abuse to access support and treatment.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The Facility CMAM Supervisor will be responsible for coordinating the engagement with communities and actively seeking their feedback. The consultation activities will be recorded and reported monthly to CMA’s County Officers, the M&E and Nutrition officers of the CHD and CMA project management team, highlighting what new actions (if any) need to be taken. These measures will ensure that an effective system of representation of affected populations is in place for the purpose of improving project results. Further, the Project Manager will visit and supervise the project (once per quarter) to conduct monitoring and evaluation so that regular assessment of beneficiary knowledge on project progress and their satisfaction with project performance is collected and new measures put in place as required in response to beneficiary feedback. Further, the Project Manager will conduct meetings with local leaders of host and IDP communities and local authorities to ensure effective implementation and robust monitoring of the complaints mechanism is in place so as to achieve effective accountability to the populations being served.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

8.1 High Risk Planned Mitigation Measures: Beneficiaries face exposure to violence while accessing facility-based services because of long distances to locations where project services will be centered. To mitigate this risk, CMA will employ mobile and outreach approaches to go to the locations where at-risk populations live in order to reach beneficiaries in a safe way. Further, the project will promote community-based strategies and practices to provide protection for the most vulnerable community members (children, PLW, IDPs and disabled). The project will engage men and women leaders to take responsibility for mobilizing protection for disadvantaged and vulnerable individuals, so that they have unimpeded access to services. To adhere to the principles of “Do-No-Harm”, the project will strive to deliver services in a balanced manner so that IDP and host community populations will have equal access to project services. To achieve this balance, CMA will implement a strong awareness campaign to engage both IDP and host community leaders.8.2 High Risk Planned Mitigation Measures: Women, girls and boy with and without disabilities feel unsafe and are at risk of sexual violence and abuse. CMA will conduct community assessments for delivery of services from static, mobile and outreaches, and apply the “do-no-harm” approach to reduce the risk sexual of exploitation and abuse. The project will conduct safety audits at all static and mobile services, and implement all recommended measures to improve safe access to services. The safety audits will be led by CMA Safety Audit Focal Person, and implemented by female staff members in respect of common protection protocols. These measures will ensure access and minimize exposer of women and girls to violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse as they access to project assistance.8.4 High Risk Planned Mitigation Measures: To address the need for available and effective protection assistance and referral services for abuse survivors, project sites will be linked to health services where CRM service will be provided for victims of SGBV including psychological support for the trauma experienced by child survivors of conflict and of SGBV. Through promotion and awareness sessions, both those in treatment and the wider community will be made aware of the appropriate referral pathways for the services that survivors need. Further, local authorities and community leaders will be made aware of available protection assistance and the GBV referral pathway for CMR and psychological support for survivors of SGBV.8.5 High Risk Planned Mitigation Measures: Exclusion of marginalized communities/minority groups from consultations and decision making on project approaches and interventions will be mitigated by adopting strategies to ensure communities: (1) have information they need to make informed decisions on accessing services, (2) can provide feedback on the project intervention and submit complaints. As women, girls, boys, the disabled and elderly are primary targets of this project, and to ensure they are all included in consultations and decision-making, CMA will organize specific forums (KIIs, FGDs) and meetings to engage these vulnerable groups. This will include engaging women’s groups, and women community leaders in consultation and decision making in the planning, throughout the implementation and at evaluation and this will also include discharge interviews. To reach the disabled and elderly, CMA will conduct case seeking interviews and consultation with these groups to ensure their situational context, needs and limitations/barriers are understood and accounted for in project planning and implementation decision-making.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

At present, Fangak and Uror are rated at severity of need level Extreme and Niyrol is rated at severity of need level Severe for GBV. Displacement caused by flooding and intercommunal conflict are the main causes of the high severity rating for GBV. This project will directly contribute to mitigation of GBV by delivering the minimum package of services and activities for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV in the following 4 ways: 1) Rollout on-the-job refresher training for all frontline nutrition workers based on CMA’s policy and practices for PSEA, and implementation of this PSEA policy at the facility level; 2) Training of frontline workers on how to recognize victims/survivors of GBV, and ensure all frontline workers are knowledgeable of the referral pathway for survivors to access appropriate care including the clinical management of rape, psychosocial support and counselling; 3) Conducting safety audits of the delivery of nutrition service from facilities at least once per year, to help provide safe and accessible nutrition services for the mothers, women, girls and boys requiring treatment for acute malnutrition and to adjust facility structures and/or services approaches to ensure patients feel they can access services safely; 4) Conduct spot exit interviews of beneficiaries, evaluations and safety audits of the methods and approaches to nutrition service delivery and reach unserved patients through mobile approaches and help establish community-based protection mechanisms to minimize the risk to vulnerable female clients when they are required to cover long distances to access services. Through the measures listed above, the project will contribute to the objective of providing safe and timely access to services for case management and psychosocial support including capacity to provide such services. The measures set out above will be implemented in 24 locations (6 in Fangak, 13 in Nyirol, 5 in Uror with a catchment population estimated at 296405 (Fangak 115524, Nyirol 119041, Uror 61840) which is about 2% of the national population and provide support to the vulnerable women and children who need to access to nutrition services the most.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

At present, Fangak, Nyirol and Uror counties are classified in IPC 4. Toward reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%), this project will make 5 direct contributions: 1) Curative treatment of children 6-59 months for SAM - 5133 and MAM - 8503 (51% female), which is 1.6% of national target; 2) Treatment for MAM of pregnant and lactating women – 5538, which is 3.7% of National target; 3) For prevention of malnutrition, provision of VAS to children 6-59 months - 51438, which is 2.4% of national target, and Albendazole 45160 to children 12-59 months, (51% female), which is 2.6% of national target; 4) Provision of MIYCN counselling to pregnant and lactating women and the caregivers of children under 23 months to improve food quality and hygiene, and dietary diversification as means to prevent acute malnutrition - 24307 which is 2.4% of national target; 5) Establishing demonstration kitchen gardens and undertaking cooking demonstrations reaching an estimated population of 35000 (70% female) through behaviour change communication on young child feeding practices and new knowledge of the nutritional value of a diversity of foods and how to produce and prepare a more nutritional diet for young children and pregnant women. These curative and preventative measures combined with diversity in food production, preparation and consumption will help reduce the number of people in IPC 4 by the end of this project.
