Project Summary

After 9 years of crisis, Syria is still experiencing a catastrophic impact on the availability and the quality of education opportunities. A generation of children and youth are left unprotected, disconnected, and often have no access to education opportunities. 1/3 of schools are out of service, because they are either damaged, destroyed or sheltering IDPs. Functioning schools are poor and crowded, and the education system is weak. Economic pressure on crisis-affected families left them exposed to adopting negative coping mechanisms, including child labor, mainly boys, and young-girls’ early marriage, which increase the number of out of school children and youth.
Similar to other areas, the education system in Salamieh District is weak from the impact of the crisis. According to AKF’s assessments, the rate of out-of-school-children is 14.6% (6 to 8 years). Dropouts are driven by low household's economy and disability of children unable to access services. 72,800 children (ages 6>18) enrolled in 121 active schools within the District. Overcrowding, poor educational support, variable teaching quality, and low salaries for teachers are contributing to overall poor education efficiency. Households with economic means are seeking auxiliary educational support (i.e. homework support, additional classes), yet, this service is not accessible for many due to cost barriers. In addition, education in preparatory and secondary levels is not compulsory, and weak system at schools to follow up the status of dropped out children, which worsen the drop out problem.
The project aims to increase opportunities for 960 adolescents between the ages of 10 to 18 years (out of school or at risk of dropping out), with a particular focus on girls, PwDs and IDPs, to pursue education in safe learning environment in Salamieh City. The project will work with local teachers and community groups to establish safe learning spaces to deliver non-formal education (NFE) for out of school children or those who are at risk of dropping out; develop and deliver education curriculums designed for target students which is gender-sensitive, and integrates child protection, psychosocial support, special education and self-learning; and conduct awareness-raising campaigns to parents and communities about the role of NFE in providing a pathway for children to continue their education.

4 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria has forced 2.1 million, over one third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Due to a shortage of functional classrooms, classes are overcrowded and over one million children are required to attend double-shift schools. An extreme shortage and under payment of qualified teachers also limits the quality and provision of education. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labour (HNO 2019). Salamieh District has a population of 223,935 people, over half of which are female, 5.3% PwDs, and 19.6% IDPs as per AKF’s studies in 2019. 24.5% of household members are in the formal school age between 6-18 years. However, 14.6% are out of school, of which 18.1% have not attended at all. The top reason reported for out of school girls were financial constraints, while for boys it was failure at school the financial limitations. 24.6% children between the ages of (5-18) are receiving private tutoring sessions in Salmieh. Of them, 52.6% are in primary school, 24.3% are in middle-school and 13.6% are in secondary school. Yet, for many households these services are not accessible due to financial barriers. Furthermore, in 2019 AKF consulted with girls and female youth, parents, NGOs and community leaders to identify the social, cultural, economic, and structural barriers that prevent girls from exercising their right to education. The evaluation determined the following: lack of decision-making ability; a high risk of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV); personal safety in and around school; underqualified teachers; inadequate materials; norms around appropriate subjects for girls; and a lack of quality, gender responsive and inclusive learning environments. Girls also reported feeling particularly vulnerable to early marriage, as they face family pressure to marry due to family financial burdens.

5 - Implementation of activities

• Establish a Community Consultative Group (CCG) (50% F) comprised of representatives of CSOs, community members, including those who have experience in teaching school’s subjects, to design and oversee the action plan and progress of the project. CCGs will select 8 Lead Teachers (LTs) (4 F) and establish 4 NFE centers. Each center will have a management center committee (MCCs) comprised of 10–12 members (50% F). AKF will provide training to CCG and MCCs on soft skills, GBV, and gender equality (GE). CCG and MCC will select 120 non-formal teachers (50% F) for the 4 centers.• Develop an education curriculum designed for out of school children or those who are at risk of dropping out which is gender-sensitive, and integrates child protection, psychosocial support, special education and self-learning. The curriculum will be based off of UNICEF’s standard operating procedure for NFE. Technical experts in education and protection will be consulted in developing this curriculum. • Train and mentor non-formal teachers and school leaders to deliver this curriculum to target children and refer children and establish safe learning environments. • With selected teachers, LTs will prepare a plan for each center which includes types and time of classes. MCCs and LTs will supervise the activities, and submit reports to the CCG including general information, and cases of children who should be referred to mental health specialists, or to education institutions. • Regular meetings and awareness sessions about NFE and PSS for children will be conducted for parents. In addition, promotional materials (2 short videos and 6 types of printed publications, including 2 success stories) will be developed and disseminated for wider distribution among communities. • Process and outcome monitoring activities under the project will include baseline and endline assessments of the target group, ongoing feedback with project beneficiaries and FGDs with project stakeholders.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

A. Adolescents, mainly males, commitment to attending the classes might be compromised due to their commitment to work. likelihood: medium, impact: medium. Mitigation measures: The project will conduct community awareness sessions for parents, and schedule classes in relevant times after consultation with local community.B. Local community might not accept, support or express their discomfort with some sessions’ contents such as SGBV subject or Gender component. likelihood: medium, impact: medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will mitigate this risk through developing a context-based training that promotes and highlights positive habits, experiences, and examples that community already has.C. Spaces and classes’ environments might be incompatible with protections standards which put adolescents, mainly girls, under risks of harm. likelihood: medium, impact: medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will mitigate this risk through conducting an assessment prior setting the spaces, and prepare them to be compliant with protection standards.D. The project might face pressure or even might be criticized or accused of bias in selecting the beneficiaries due to the limited capacity to absorb eligible youth; affecting reach and leading to local community complaints. likelihood: low, impact: medium. Mitigation measures: AKF will develop a solid communication plan led by CCG to explain project capacity limitation as a prototype that will be expanded in the future.E. Safety and Security concerns in relation to male and female youth exposure to physical or verbal harassment on their way to the centers. likelihood: low, impact: high. Mitigation measures: AKF will ensure safe and secure environment through careful selecting of the center locations of centers in safe and easy-to-access areas, along with providing transportation.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Youth number and commitment might decrease during summer when targeted students are on holidays. Likelihood: medium. Impact: low. Mitigation: AKF will open the window for school students who would like to join the classes to enroll.Risk 2: Some teachers might be unable to attend morning classes due to their jobs. Likelihood: Low. Impact: high. Mitigation: AKF will mitigate this risk through designing the classes to be implemented on weekends and afternoons.Risk 3: Involved teachers and adolescents might be unable to attend due to the cost of transportation to the centers. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: AKF will provide transportation for both teachers and adolescents.Risk 4: The teaching methods in formal schools differ from the ones in the centers, so school students will be confused. Likelihood: high. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will conduct orientation sessions and coordination meetings with related school teachers so they understand the project, and to maintain coordination and cooperation with them.Risk 5: Governmental education authorities might not provide the required support or show little interest in the project. Likelihood: Low. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF and CCG will approach related authorities to present the project and its advantages to facilitate their support.Risk 6: AKF financial and operational procedures might be challenging to CCG to follow which will create some delays. Likelihood: medium. Impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will conduct a workshop for the CCG on AKF financial and operational procedures, and assign one Community Engagement Officer to follow up and bridge this gap.Risk 7: Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the project budget.
