Project Summary

Clinic-Based Nutrition Services: Building off the established rapport in Tel Hajar clinic, SP will address the pervasive rates of malnutrition and poor nutrition practices by using the clinic as a base for outpatient management of MAM and uncomplicated SAM. The mobile clinic will team conduct screening for PLW and children 6-59 month and provide malnutrition treatment MAM and SAM cases and malnourished PLW. SSP will have a strong supply management system and ensure no stock out in RUTF, RUSF, and CMAM medicine. Nutrition screening, education, and referrals will continue after SP's partnership ends; nutrition services will continue to be provided at a high capacity to the community.
Community-Based Mobile Nutrition Services: Three full-time CHWs will work under the supervision of the existing CHW team lead in the rural communities in Hassakah, Serekaniye and Washokani IDP camps, estimated by the NES Nutrition WG to host a combined total of 56,000 children aged 6-59 months with SAM or MAM. CHWs will target communities within a one-hour radius of the clinic with systematic nutrition assessments to screen for and identify children ages 6-59 months and PLWs in need of RUTF. The Nutrition Specialist will accompany the CHW TL and CHW team to do malnutrition treatment, conduct physical examinations of beneficiaries, and provide nutrition education on an established schedule.
Confirmed target locations include Washokani and Serekaniye camps, the Hassakah National Hospital, Tel Hajar Clinic and the Shaddadi PHC. Other target locations to be selected by highest need.
The program will use the most updated data (HNO, SMART surveys) for monitoring and implementation. The Program Manager will monitor all staff and support technically. When needed, the PM will refer to SP's headquarters with expert nutrition and health support and oversight. All staff are trained in relevant topics to monitor the program.

3 - Needs

In Syria, there are approximately 3,770,000 children 0 to 59 months impacted by chronic and/or acute malnutrition, and 1,732,000 PLW in need of nutritional support. A SMART survey conducted in Northwest Syria in 2021 found more than a five-fold increase in the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) from 2019 to 2021 (0.90% to 4.70%). This is partially attributed to increased rates of moderate acute malnutrition (MAM), which, when left untreated, deteriorate to severe acute malnutrition (SAM). Unfortunately, the nutrition situation in NES continues to worsen in 2022 due to high inflation, crop failure, poor water quality, the CoVID-19 pandemic, and increased food and fuel prices. Food insecurity is a primary concern among residents and IDPs – more than 50% of the population in all three governorates in NES are impacted by food insecurity. Food insecurity is reported at even higher rates among the IDP population (55% of children in need, 45% of PLWs in need), with a precipitous increase since the beginning of 2022. One of the contributing factors to childhood malnutrition in the first two years is sub-optimal IYCF practices. Especially in humanitarian emergencies, breastfeeding is crucial to protecting infants from malnutrition and its associated complications. In NES, only 13.2% of infants under six months were exclusively breastfed, and only 49% 6-23 months consumed the minimal acceptable diet. In 2023, changes will be made to include IYCF education.

4 - Implementation of activities

Pervasive malnutrition rates and poor nutrition practices will be addressed through outpatient management of MAM and uncomplicated SAM from Tel Hajar clinic. SP will identify and treat by providing ready-to-use therapeutic food for PLWs and children aged 6-59 months. Patient education on nutrition practices will also be emphasized, with attention to infant and young child feeding education, community mobilization, and nutrition. Additionally, two mobile units will work in the rural communities of Hassakah, Serekaniye and Washokani camps. SP will give RUTF (according to protocol), conduct physical examinations. In addition, patient education on nutrition practices, with special attention to IYCF, CMAM, community mobilization, and nutrition education via CHW and nutrition specialist-led campaigns and support groups. Attention will also be given to promoting education centered on breastfeeding, health, hygiene, waste management, and diet education. Nutrition education will incorporate family life and mental health principles, such as supporting and promoting mother-infant interaction and prevention of violence and abuse. M&E: Patient data workbooks are updated and maintained daily, noting patient progress, interventions performed, prescription, and consultation schedule. The tracker contains details such as patient demographics, diagnosis, treatment, unique/returning visits. 4Ws will be reported to the respective WG/Cluster as requested. All will be supervised/monitored closely

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

a)Staff or patients assaulted en route to/from the mobile unit locations;b)Risk to beneficiaries related to the receipt of ready-to-use therapeutic food (nutrition program) and/or assistive devices and/or medication, the perceived economic value of which may make the beneficiaries or their families targets within the community. c) Risk to beneficiaries around inequitable access to programming for females and children, due to cultural context which gives preference to adult males as recipients of programming and/or programming commodities. d) Risk to female beneficiaries of forced pregnancy in order to have them qualify for nutrition programming benefiting pregnant and lactating women. All staff involved in the project are trained in managing and/or referring specific gender and protection concerns and have an operational awareness of protection risks/issues. A safeguarding focal point is assigned, and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off-site locations and acted upon. SP will assess the contextualized assistance needs of other vulnerable groups, including PWDs and ensure their equitable participation and inclusion in the project. Across NES, the numbers of disabled people are quite high – HNAP's 2021 report puts the number at 37% of the population.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risks identified:• Contracted doctors violating PSEA policy;• Beneficiaries exploited by SP staff members, partners, and/or vendors. Samaritan's Purse mitigates PSEA risks with the following measures:• Background checks on all personnel, safeguarding training for all staff, protection messaging displayed in the clinic, clear reporting system for violations. There is an HR and complaints processing department to support this risk mitigation.• A safeguarding focal point is assigned, and the complaints/feedback mechanism means that feedback is captured from remote/off-site locations and acted upon.• Reporting mechanisms for each project.• Hotline.• Email hotline.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Security situation or border restrictions cause the team to lose access to NES for a period of time. This is mitigated through the CoH staff and SP staff being able to continue activities in the interim period. • COVID-19 cases amongst health worker staff, SP or partner. Continued training and enforcement of infection prevention and control protocols. • Pockets of higher risk area due to tribal tension and ISIS sleeper cell activity. Mitigated through security assessment of the area to identify processes and protocols and coordination with local community actors and members. • Consistent provision of medication and consumables. The supply chain has remained relatively stable as SP procures all medications and consumables locally within NES. Samaritan's Purse has been able to successfully mitigate medication and supply shortages resulting from supply chain instability by setting up contracted agreements for the supply of 6-9 months' worth of medication from several local vendors. This medication supply is delivered to the clinic monthly or bi-monthly, which allows vendors time to plan well in advance, taking advantage of times the roads are open, to have adequate stock in their warehouse to continue to supply the clinic.
