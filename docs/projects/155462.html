Project Summary

The project seeks to stabilize livelihoods and rebuild the foundation for resilience of the drought affected communities in Kariba and Makoni districts. The response will leverage on on-going humanitarian efforts with early recovery activities that include rehabilitation of productive assets through cash for work, start up grants targeting young people who would have benefited from vocational skills training to grow and recapitalize their small enterprises. AAZ will facilitate rapid scoping studies to identify and map youths skills requirements and gaps and link them with service providers and private organizations that are supporting new skills and providing attachments and certification programs. The intervention will support value chain strengthening initiatives that have been started under the Zimbabwe Resilience Program that are agro-based targeting especially livestock interventions. ActionAid leading a consortium of partners under the Zambezi Valley Alliance name supported communities and stakeholders with IT based early warning systems infrastructure that includes computers, servers, internet connectivity which will be harnessed to provide relevant commodity market situations and support organized farmers to aggregate both inputs and outputs thereby reducing marketing related costs. The same platform will be used to strengthen sub-national capacity especially for Makoni to extent the high frequency monitoring system which was implemented successfully in Kariba District. In Makoni the intervention will strengthen provision of WASH related infrastructure and consider labor saving technologies targeting community institutions and facilities and support software related health interventions. These interventions will be implemented in close collaboration with specific government line ministries at local level, Rural District Council and their related structures and with affected rural, urban and peri-urban communities.

Needs

The predicted El Niño exacerbates the pressure on poor, vulnerable rural and urban populations, who depend on rainfed agriculture and related economic activities. This has significantly affected resilience building initiatives in the targeted areas. Participation especially of young people in resilience initiatives have been hampered by limited access to financial services which in turn affects livelihood options that can be undertaken. Agro-business ventures that have been promoted over time in rural areas have been focusing on production and not on the whole value chain options available thereby appealing to households with access to land, owning significant numbers of livestock. These implementation processes have over time further marginalized the young people in the targeted areas. Young people generally are interested in projects/initiatives that have a quick turnaround time in terms of revenue generation and some of the traditional income generating activities are still difficult to interest youths in. In the targeted areas graduation rates of young people from school to tertiary colleges is very low and this has been worsened by nonexistence of vocational training facilities in the district. This limits innovation and incubation of business ideas by young people that have the potential to grow into sustainable micro enterprises thereby widening livelihood options that have the potential to contribute to resilience and shorten the need for humanitarian assistance. The predicted El Nino will increase rural to urban migration due to lost livelihoods puts additional pressure on urban infrastructure and livelihoods in communities that are already impacted by the depressed economy, and high rates of unemployment in the urban and peri-urban areas. This coupled with pressure on the weak local authorities to provide for basic services such as water and sanitation facilities has resulted in current dual outbreak of cholera and typhoid. This has been worsened by mushrooming of unstructured marketing places that do not have the requisite WASH infrastructure and communities generally lacking information to prepare and respond to these potential hazards. Proliferation of communication platforms presents an opportunity for social behavior change campaigns which will be a tool to combat disease outbreaks. The projected future climate change impacts of decrease in rainfall and more frequent and intense droughts, makes it highly important to address early recovery now.

Activities

Strengthen the resilience of the most food insecure communities to mitigate against the impact of the deteriorating economic situation1. Facilitate scholarships for youths on short term (3-month long) agriculture and non-agriculture vocational training skills.2. Link youths with internship/apprenticeship opportunities on their chosen trades3. Support youths (individuals or groups) with start up capital.4. Rehabilitate/upgrade community infrastructure (health centers, market places) focusing on WASH. 5. Establish and capacity build community health clubs linked to the community infrastructures rehabilitated.6. Facilitate mobile based social behavior change campaigns especially in the urban and peri-urban areas on communicable diseases and prevention.
