Project Summary

WHO defines health as a complete state of physical, social and mental wellbeing but not merely the absence of disease or infirmity. Drawing from the above definition, CRADA intends to implement health in Akobo, Uror, Pibor and Nyirol, Maban, Melut, Panyikang, Tambura, Kajo-Keji and Magwi Counties in South Sudan to prevent and treat morbidity in children under 5, 5-17years, 18-60years, >60years, PWDs, Returnees and IDPs while incorporating all the components of Primary Health Care. Cases shall be got through community outreaches, self-referral to the facility and the network of the Community Health Workers and Home Health Promoters that shall be established in the communities. The organization already has a health project in Akobo West (Ulang & Wechreat) and would like to scale up and increase coverage in this area as well as in Nyirol. In the latter, we have an FSL project which would play a key role integrated with nutrition, animal health in Akobo, Nyirol, Uror.
Therefore, we aim to reach a total of 49,000 beneficiaries with a total budget of 1,665,176 USD. 1 - Needs

Ten years after independence and three years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. All the years of conflict and suffering in addition to the impact of COVID-19 & floods have resulted in trauma and mental health conditions for a large proportion of the population. South Sudan remains one of 10 countries with the lowest life expectancy in the world at 58 years. The under-five mortality rate remains high with 96.2 per 1,000 live births. One in 10 children is not expected to reach their fifth birthday (HNO 2022). Around 75% of all child deaths in South Sudan are due to preventable diseases, such as diarrhoea, malaria & pneumonia. Poor access to health services, a limited number of health workers and lack of access to health services have produced some of the worst health indicators in the world. (UNICEF South Sudan 2022)With only one physician per 65,574 individuals & one midwife per 39,088 population individuals South Sudan reports just one-tenth of the number of medical doctors & nurses in comparison to other countries (The Borgen Project 2021). The health system & infrastructure are among the poorest in the world with government's contributions only 4% of GDP, with the addition of an under-performing economy thus poor health outcomes. Health facilities are poorly equipped & staffed. A quarter of internally displaced IDPs & returnees reside in settlements further than 5km from a functional health facility. Approximately more than half of the country’s 2,300 health facilities are non-functional. Nearly 60% of the functioning health facilities are supported by humanitarian & development organizations but many of them are close to displacement sites & returnee settlements and may not be easily accessible by established communities. Access of communities to health services is further hindered by insecurity and harassment on the way to facilities. Women & girls face additional barriers due to the lack of female health workers & limited relevant supplies for them. Incidents have occurred in remote areas where victims of violence or survivors of sexual assault had little to no access to health services. Only 63% of the population can access health facilities in one hour time while 80% reported barriers in accessing health services. 34 counties have less than one PHCU per 15,000 people while 23 counties have less than one primary health care centre per 50,000.(HNO 2022)Extremely low immunization coverage (45% Penta 3 coverage) increases children’s’ vulnerability to vaccine-preventable diseases. The destruction of WASH facilities, inadequate hygiene and sanitation facilities and lack of accessibility to clean drinking water all contribute to poor health conditions, with communities at risk of water-borne diseases such as diarrhoeal diseases, cholera & Hepatitis E. The most at risk individual include particularly those with chronic medical issues, older persons, separated and unaccompanied children, persons with disabilities & pregnant women, among others. People’s vulnerability will continue to be impacted by weak public health services which limit people’s access to timely health services. During 2022, the health needs are likely to remain high due to disease outbreaks, low immunization coverage, chronic poor hygiene & sanitation practices, with insufficient access to safe drinking water leading to cholera. Epidemic diseases and infection, including malaria, measles and Hepatitis E will likely continue to impact vulnerable people due to the lack of healthcare capacity and gaps in contributing sectors such as for adequate shelter, water, hygiene & sanitation. The porous border & limited capacity to curtail COVID-19 spread will likely increase the risk of transmission, exacerbated by the low vaccination coverage. The recurrent Ebola outbreak in neighboring countries continues to pose a significant public health risk in South Sudan (HNO 2022).

2 - Project activities

1.1 Epidemic disease surveillance, prevention & control including outbreak detection, investigation & response Disease surveillance at boma & payam levels, recruit surveillance officers, report through the IDSR system, focus on malaria incidence & other suspected outbreaks 1.2 Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases & procurement of outbreak response commodities & emergency antigens for immunization & cold chain commodities. Engage in EPI activities, integrate EPI into nutrition, participate in NIDs by offering financial, logistical & technical support during the designing of micro plan & implementation. 1.3 IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies & protocols & continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities Employ five-step approach to IPC improvement to reduce HAI & AMR by proper staffing, enough PPEs & prepositioning of WASH items such as hand washing facility, clean and safe toilet etc, HW education on IPC, HAIs surveillance, appropriate legislation & basic infection control measure. 1.4 Immunization against vaccine preventable diseases & procurement of outbreak response commodities & emergency antigens for immunization & cold chain commodities. Vaccinate all children below 1 year with penta vaccine against diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis (DPT), hepatitis B & Hemophilus influenza type b (Hib) with 1st dose 6 weeks after birth, 2nd & 3rd at 10 & 14 weeks of age respectively. 1.5 Community health service provision linking with facility-based services using initiatives such as BHI & ICCM Recruit CBDs to implement ICCM with HHPs & CHWs to treat mild cases in community & serious cases in health facility. 1.6 Provision of life-saving essential healthcare services including management of NCDs through static & mobile healthcare services, procurement of essential medical kits & training of HW. Conduct deliveries, consultations, MHPSS & CMR services to SGBV survivors, screen for NCDs & offer H&N counseling & prescription of drugs, procure essential medical kits with dressings & bandages & latex gloves, OTC medicine for cuts, burns & injuries and so on. Train staff on first aid & CMR. Provide standard protocols in use for CMR, emergency contraception, ARVs for PEP & presumptive STI treatment. CMR response mechanism like supportive communication, history taking & examination, forensic evidence collection, compassionate & confidential treatment & referral for further services. The six WG-SS used to identify PWDs. The reports to be disaggregated according to age & sex. 3.1 Provide technical & operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services & AAP Routine Supportive Supervision visits to health facilities with CHD & other IPs & key opinion leaders for transparency. CFM fully equipped with a desk, file, cabinet & the person who anonymously files & addresses with feedback. 3.2 Conducting community consultative meetings within the community regularly & implementing feedback mechanism & ensure compliance Regular community consultations to identify community needs, ensure community participation & reduce negative impacts of the project on their lives. Complaints are raised and feedback received in the next consultative meeting to ensure community ownership thus sustainability. 3.3 Provide technical & operational assistance to heath facilities through supportive supervision visits to ensure quality provision of health services Routine supportive supervision to ensure programs meet professional standards. Regular capacity building trainings for staff. 3.4 Promote humanitarian-peace-development nexus bringing humanitarian, peace & development actors together to promote synergies & optimize use of available resources. Proper coordination with other humanitarian actors such as FSL, Nutrition, Protection to ensure an integrated approach and provision of a holistic humanitarian package to persons in need.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The Gender & Protection Officer’s role shall include among others gender & protection mainstreaming & integration into the project activities, establishment of complaints & feedback mechanisms in collaboration with the Community Mobilizer. Through this, beneficiaries can report any program related issues through the complaint desk or by directly calling the openly displayed Hotline Number +211919067218. Staff shall be trained on the complaints and feedback mechanisms with a clear emphasis on the referral pathways. Complaints shall be received written, audio or verbal depending on what best suits the complainant with strict emphasis on confidentiality and anonymity. Through focused group discussions with the various vulnerable groups, the project shall be able to acquire important feedback. With 19 years in the humanitarian world in other locations in South Sudan, we have accumulated and learned from experience so that the support we give to the beneficiaries improves their lives over time. We as an organization know that resources for project implementation can never be enough and not everyone can be served. It is therefore due to this that we try to efficiently utilize the limited available resources to ensure that the beneficiaries get the most out of it by targeting the most vulnerable. The organization shall liaise with other IPs in the area in order to combine our knowledge and resources to provide a holistic package to the beneficiaries. We shall directly or indirectly provide support that helps the community recover and makes them resilient.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The Home Health Promoters are key in disseminating messages to their fellow community members; it is such people who shall be actively used especially during community mobilization sessions such as mass awareness campaigns, community outreaches and during NIDs. We owe it to the community we serve to ensure the people we employ to serve them are professionals with enough skills and experience so as to provide the best services possible.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CRADA abides by the six core principles in relation to sexual exploitation and abuse. 1. Sexual exploitation and abuse by CRADA workers constitute acts of gross misconduct and are therefore grounds for termination of employment. 2. Sexual activity with children (persons under the age of 18) is prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age of consent locally. Mistaken belief regarding the age of a child is not a defense.3. Exchange of money, employment, goods or services for sex including sexual favors or other forms of humiliating, degrading or exploitative behavior is prohibited. This includes exchange of assistance that is due to beneficiaries.4. Sexual relationships between CRADA workers and beneficiaries are strongly discouraged since they are based on inherently unequal power dynamics. Such relationships undermine the credibility and integrity of humanitarian aid worker. 5. Where a CRADA staff develops concerns or suspicions regarding sexual abuse or exploitation by a fellow staff whether in the same agency or not, he or she must report such concerns via established CRADA’s reporting mechanisms. 6. All CRADA’s staff are obliged to create and maintain an environment which prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and promotes the implementation of their code of conduct. Managers at all levels have particular responsibilities to support and develop systems which maintain this environment. As such, information on PSEA shall be provided by the Gender & Protection Officer whose main role is to act as the PSEA Focal Point for the project. The Officer is to ensure that staff are trained on PSEA and the community also gets to know about this. Posters & visual aids (IEC) materials shall be pinned at the sites for use. Through community dialogues and focused group discussions, the community shall be able to discuss issues around PSEA with concrete examples. The Annual 16 Days of Activism shall be used as an opportunity to pass out PSEA messages to the community through role plays, dramas & songs. The community shall be made aware of the PSEA Hotline Number for CRADA +211919067218 and email psea@crada.africa

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries know what they want, they just don’t have the means of getting them. We shall understand the beneficiaries’ needs through stakeholder consultative meetings, focused group discussions, and community dialogues. The organization shall use chiefs & sultans as part of the volunteers program. These people best understands their community and have a say in whatever enters their area. Feedback on project implementation shall be received and analyzed. Communities are diverse and not everything works everywhere. Based on this, the project shall be contextualized and well adapted to the community needs as analyzed from the feedback. For example, the TBAs shall be chosen based on community reference. Casual laborers & security guards shall be selected based on their character and morality as indicated by the community.The Community Mobilizer shall be tasked with collecting community feedback and ensuring that all health facility sites have a complaints and feedback mechanism fully equipped with a desk, file, cabinet and the person to receive the complaints. All feedback got from the community shall be treated with utmost importance and used to inform and adapt the project design so as to meet their needs.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

- The project does not directly contribute to Collective Outcome 3 but rather helps to mitigate its effects on the affected population. This is because the project teaches kitchen gardening of which flood victims can still be able to practice sack mount vegetable gardening so as to enjoy dietary diversity and adequacy in the absence of land due to flooding. The project also teaches essential hygiene actions as well as the 5 critical handwashing moments which can enable reduction in disease incidence in a displacement camp due to floods.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

- This project has some GBV activities which shall help to mainstream and integrate GBV into the whole project design. The project is set to recruit Gender and Protection Officers whose roles shall be setting up of clear and safe referral pathways for GBV survivors. Through this, the project shall contribute to Collective Outcome 2.
