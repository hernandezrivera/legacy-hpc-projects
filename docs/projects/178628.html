Project Summary

The proposed program aims to enhance the wellbeing and resilience at affected population by providing a holistic approach psychosocial support intervention and prevention. In addition to the mental health treatments for people who are suffering from psychological disorders or concerns through a safe referral to specialized services within 23 PHC centers, in eight districts of Aleppo and Idleb governorates; Afrin, Al Bab, Harim, Azaz, Jarablus, Idleb, Jabal Saman and Tell Abiad, targeting reproductive-aged women, PLWs, and U5 children of the host and IDP communities.
IDA will provide integrated protection services, including specialized gender-based violence and child protection case management, advanced and focused, and/or those who are survivors or at risk of gender-based violence and abuse – especially women and children. The program will target at least 23,950 beneficiaries, 10672 W, 4146 M, 5412G, 3720B(1755 PWD), Attention will be given to appropriate entry points such as existing women’s groups and support networks. Areas of operation are selected in coordination with Clusters and health directorates.
Case management services will be tailored to beneficiaries’ individual needs and youth taking into consideration family and caregivers. Psychosocial support will help beneficiaries to improve their coping mechanisms, manage stress, and find solutions to their challenges.
Group work will provide space for beneficiaries with similar issues, problems or circumstances to come together for mutual support. Outreach services will spread information on positive mental health and protection risks, as well as assist in reaching the most vulnerable in communities and making appropriate referrals to services.
Training will target both community members and front-line service providers to increase their capacity to help and support others.
Awareness-raising activities aim to decrease stigma around accessing services through encouraging people to be more aware of protection risks faced.
Activities will be accessible by all, efforts committed to creating an accessible environment for PWD and elderlies and balanced between the staff gender for better inclusion the communities.

The activities are covering; psychosocial support, community outreach, group work, awareness-raising activities, training for service providers and community members on mental health and protection topics, and integrated mental health services for the most vulnerable people, including people with disabilities, people facing mental conditions, psychological distress and trauma, also will target children and women assessed to be at high risk of abuse, exploitation, neglect or violence, children found to be associated with armed forces and armed groups, unaccompanied or separated children, persons with mental disorders or psychological distress, the elderly, chronically ill and people with disabilities.
All activities will be based upon the principles of the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings, Minimum Standards for Child Protection, and Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action and GBV SC SOPs and guidelines. The NGO will work to improve the availability of holistic response for the most vulnerable by conducting training targeting a range of humanitarian actors in order to strengthen response with a survivor-centered approach.

4 - Needs

HNO 2021, 42 per cent of the assessed communities reported presence of at least one of the assessed eight protection issues as very commonly occurring and in 53 per cent of the communities’ respondents have reported presence of five or more out of the assessed eight protection issues. At least five of the eight key protection issues were reported to be occurring in all 14 governorates. According to HNO 2021 CP concerns remain major issue in NWS; separation from caregivers is a persistent issue across Syria, death of a caregiver, economic need, child marriage, and child recruitment are among the top cited reasons for family separations. According to the CP cases reported to the IDA, most of prevalent types of cases identified were 36% Neglect, 28% physical abuse, and 13% psychological distress. The absence of a continuum of alternative care options, including formal options, is a concern. On the other hand, the fragmented protective environment in NWS is continue to diminish the resilience of affected people, increase trauma and push the population to turn to negative coping strategies, such as forced marriage, abuse within the household, and suicide, and Increase the risk of harmful coping strategies, risk of exploitation, sub-standard living conditions, and negative consequences on mental health. The most prevalent type of GBV cases reported was physical assault (36%), psychological-emotional abuse (36%), denial of resources (16%), sexual assault including rape (10%), and forced/early marriage (2%). The majority of reported cases involved domestic violence perpetrated by men towards their wives and/or daughters.

5 - Implementation of activities

Case Management: Integrated GBV, CP, MHPSS CM will be provided to women, men youth, and children including CWD by Case Managers with close supervision by team leaders and Technical advisers. the services will be integrated into PHCs centers for access, confidentiality, with strong referral mechanisms to services such as CMR and specialized Mental Health and other services available in the areas.Community-based Awareness Activities and Outreach: Community-based group PSS sessions targeting the wider community will be facilitated by CM with technical support provided by TLs to response to collective trauma, lack of social cohesion, shared stressors and difficult living conditions, aiming to promote resiliency, limit psychological effects of conflict, reduce protection risks and stigma and provide an avenue for support adopting GBV SC awareness raising toolkit.Capacity Building: The IDA will provide training to service providers, humanitarian staff stakeholders, community members in order to build capacity, raise awareness, improve referral mechanisms and strengthen coordination. Trainings include protection mainstreaming, GBV and Child Protection Guiding Principles, (PFA), PFA for Children, Problem Management, Self Help and Coping Skills,CSG, SIR, PSEA.M&E Plan: Activities will be monitored and evaluated using beneficiary satisfaction survey, access to service assessments, individual client functioning and wellbeing assessments, training tests, focus group discussions with community members and close program supervision using GBV SC M&E toolkit. CP case management will be monitored through case management files including care plans and follow-up progress, databases, and CP CM 4W.
