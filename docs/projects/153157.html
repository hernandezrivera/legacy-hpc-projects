This project seeks to provide life-saving Mine Risk Education (MRE) in alignment with the HRP Strategic Objectives 2, Protection Cluster Strategic Objectives 2, the priority strategic objectives/actions identified by the Mine Action Sub Cluster and MRE Working Group, and in alignment with the South Sudan National Mine Action Strategy 2018-2021.
The project prioritizes Tier 1 locations Juba (CES) and Maiwut (UNS), Tier 2 in Melut (UNS) and Tier 3 in Gogrial East (Warrap) and Aweil North (NBEG). These locations reflect the priorities of the MASC and MRE Working Group, and are based on World Vision South Sudan (WVSS) existing, long term humanitarian programming locations with static infrastructure and logistics support. WVSS maintains Child Protection, Education, Health and other life-saving multi-sector programming in each of these locations to facilitate MRE and ensure a high-impact, integrated approach.
The project will target a total of 22,000 individuals, including Women: 6,000 / Girls: 5,000 (Adolescent: 2,500) / Men: 6,000 / Boys: 5,000 (Adolescent: 2,500).
In all locations, the project will implement the following MRE activities, according to IMAS, NTSG, World Vision approved MRE SOPs and in alignment with the MASC – MRE Working Group and South Sudan National Mine Action Strategy 2018-2021: Training and accreditation of MRE Teams through approved third-party trainer/training programme; MRE Assessment; Community engagement / mobilization of women, girls, men and boys; Provision of integrated MRE and Community Liaison, linked with Child Protection and Education to particularly reach girls and boys in and out of school, and facilitate integration of MRE in schools; Use of approved/standard MRE materials (i.e. banners, leaflets, warning signs, etc) and age-appropriate/gender-sensitive means of communication; Use of all IMSMA tools and forms for complete and timely information management/reporting per SOPs; Implementation of Quality Monitoring System including Quality Assurance (internal, external) and Quality Controls; Advocacy to greater prioritize MRE as a life-saving humanitarian intervention, especially for children.

The project reflects the feedback of beneficiary consultations and includes accountability mechanisms, and considers the unique needs and adapts its design based on gender and age considerations/vulnerabilities (Code 4M). The project approach seeks to place communities at the centre of interventions and contribute to localization through capacity building mine risk awareness, and identification and reporting of hazards, and strengthening referrals in relation to victims assistance. The project design prioritizes a Do No Harm approach with an explicit intention to address protection issues experienced by women, girls, men and boys, recognizing that respect for safety and human rights is a key enabler of the South Sudan humanitarian response strategy.

Needs

In 2018, the humanitarian crisis continued in South Sudan despite the signing of the peace agreement in August. The 2019 HNO (draft) highlights how women, girls and boys continue to bear the brunt of the crisis, as an estimated 62% of the 1.9M IDPs are children. As a result of the most recent conflict, but also decades prior civil war, South Sudan is one of the most contaminated countries in the world in regards to mines/UXOs. According to the South Sudan National Mine Action Strategy citing IMSMA data, 93,142,182m2 of land across the country is either suspected or confirmed as contaminated by landmines or ERW. Of that, 52% of all contamination is recorded in Upper Nile. However, the Strategy notes that the outbreak of conflict in July 2016 that affected the formerly more stable areas in the Greater Equatorias has likely resulted in further mine/UXO contamination; most areas have not yet been comprehensively surveyed and the impact of armed violence in the region remains to be quantified. Insecurity and access challenges to recent conflict areas has made it difficult to establish the extent of the mine/UXO problem. Protection Trends data indicates the high human cost of mine/UXO contamination, and underscores the life-saving nature of Mine Risk Education (MRE) to mitigate the risk of death or injury, especially for children. In 2017 alone, 5 boys and 2 girls were killed, while 33 boys and 4 girls were injured as a result of mines/UXOs. Since 1980, a total of 4,959 casualties have been registered in South Sudan. Since 2013, the largest proportion of incidents have occurred as a result of play/recreation, and more specifically has impacted children. Boys and some girls are at risk of seeing mines/UXOs and believing them to be toys to play with, or otherwise failing to recognize their danger. Due to their traditional roles and patterns of behaviour, men and boys are more affected by the risks of mines/UXOs, recognizing their greater involvement in conflict, pastoral movement with cattle/small animals, and allotment of time for play and recreation, in comparison to women and girls. The scale of contamination, the high rate of unknown/unconfirmed mine/UXO presence, the low levels of basic education and the social normalization of conflict combine to create a high risk for women, men, boys and girls to be killed or maimed. In this context, MRE is a life-saving intervention, especially for children. Following the peace agreement, it is anticipated that 2019 will begin to see returnee movements, of both IDPs and refugees. As women, men, boys and girls attempt to return to their villages, using roads/footpaths and re-cultivating their fields, the risk from mines/UXOs is never higher, and education to be aware of these risks, what to do, and reporting of hazards is paramount. Finally, MRE within the MASC and overarching Protection Cluster remains one of the least funded sectors of humanitarian intervention according to OCHA FTS, despite the life-saving impact and the high risk in the post-conflict period. Among the advocacy priorities of the South Sudan National Mine Action Strategy 2018-2021 is to highlight these gaps and better communicate the impact that can be achieved for the safety of civilians, especially children, with a more significant investment in MRE. This is essential to realizing the overall national strategy, as well as reducing the life-threatening risks for women, girls, boys and men from mines/UXOs.

Activities

Protection Objective 2 – Prevent, mitigate and respond to Mine Risk issues through enhanced preparedness and resilience (PC )Key Activities- MRE Assessment- Training and accreditation of MRE Teams (dedicated team leader and educators)- Implementation of age and gender sensitive MRE continuously provided to women, girls, boys and men- Support integration of MRE into schools- Printing and/or Use and/or Distribution of MRE Materials (Banners, Leaflets, Warning Signs, etc)- Use of tools/forms for IMSMA Reporting- Quality Monitoring System- Advocacy for MREIn alignment with the MASC & South Sudan National Mine Action Strategy, the project will contribute to Strategic Goal 3 – Mine Risk Education. As a non-technical actor, World Vision South Sudan (WVSS) will continue to focus exclusively on MRE, integrating age & gender sensitive approaches to MRE with Child Protection, Education and other humanitarian programming. All MRE activities will be according to WVSS’ approved/desk accredited MRE SOPs (July 2018). The project will include dedicated MRE Teams to ensure prioritization of MRE objectives and compliance with International Mine Action Standards MRE Programming and Implementation (IMAS), Quality Assurance & Controls, Reporting per IMSMA. MRE Teams will complete annual MRE Training provided by approved UNMAS/NMAA third party trainers, with training plans reviewed and approved prior; this will be followed by annual accreditation by UNMAS/NMAA for all team members. Training will include Assessment and Quality Monitoring Systems. Each MRE Team includes 3 individuals – 1 MRE Team Leader, 2 MRE Educator, mixed gender, local staff with ability to use local language/dialect. WV current has 4 accredited MRE Teams (12 staff – 8M, 4F). MRE will be based on completion of and informed by assessment, guided by MRE Working Group (NMAA, UNMAS) assessment tools and standards, and conducted inter-agency where recommended. MRE Programme Implementation will focus on an integrated MRE and Community Liaison approach, and integrated MRE and Child Protection (reference NTSG Chapter 4 Para 4.5.1). This approach shall ensure that there are complimentary activities to achieve the overall goal of reduction of mortality and morbidity from mines/UXOs. Stakeholder involvement and coordination with all relevant actors will be prioritized, ensuring those at most risk are identified by the local community. Communication Strategy and Channels use standard messages/materials and follow World Vision’s approved MRE SOPs, with specific consideration for communication strategies that consider the unique needs and patterns of women, girls, boys and men. For example, inclusion of the unique gendered risks women/girls are likely to face (ex. Collecting firewood), compared to men/boys (ex. Moving with cattle or small animals) in stories. MRE session timing/location will also consider gender and age, for example targeting Nutrition centers to reach women; farming plots/cattle markets to reach men; schools & child friendly spaces to reach in & out of school girls and boys. All MRE content and methods will follow approved IMAS/NTSG/MRE SOPs. The project will procure, print and/or receive in-kind approved MRE banners, leaflets and warning signs (international, local) to facilitate MRE. All MRE will be reported per MRE SOPs and IMSMA, including the completion and timely submission of MRE Weekly Report, Monthly Progress Reports. Reporting will also include for Hazards, Victim & Accident Reports per SOPs. In alignment with the NMAS 2018-2021, World Vision will also contribute to Strategic Goal 1 of increasing advocacy and communications on MRE, specifically the impact of mines/ERWs impact the safety and well-being of girls and boys. As a child-focused humanitarian actor with a global scope, World Vision South Sudan will prioritize advocacy for resourcing MRE, streamlining in Child Protection and Education and other key priorities as identified by MASC, NMAA.

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Yes.Consultation is broad/representative through community mobilization/stakeholder engagement, particularly through CL and within Quality Monitoring System (QMS) implementation. MRE sessions are participatory and seek audience feedback from women, men, girls, boys during and at the conclusion of each session, not only to identify hazards but on the quality/approach of MRE interventions themselves. While Key Informant Interviews and feedback is collected from local authorities, community leaders and faith leaders, the majority of influencing data and feedback is sourced from women, men, girls and boys within the community broadly.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes.Physical barriers to access are assessed in each location of operation. Efforts to mitigate access barriers are made, such as moving services closer to boma/household level to avoid long distance travel and providing sessions in locations already being accessed by participants (ex. In schools, at health facilities, at farmer group cultivation sites). Other barriers can include those that are security related, in which case WVSS works closely with local authorities, community leaders and relevant UN Access Negotiation teams. Implicit barriers resulting from gender inequality and social norms specifically affect women and girls from participating in MRE sessions; efforts are made to address this type of barrier through community awareness raising in promoting women’s and girl’s equal rights and gender equality, as well as targeting MRE sessions to locations women and girls attend already (Ex. Nutrition sites (OTPs), health facilities, girls classes in schools).

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes.The project is designed to specifically address protection threats experienced by women, girls, boys and men related to the physical safety issues from mines/UXOs, which specifically includes the risk of killing and maiming, especially for girls and boys. MRE is designed to increase the awareness of women, girls, boys and men of the risks of mines/UXOs, what these look like, what to do and not do, importance of reporting hazards for clearance by technical mine action actors to reduce community level risk of physical harm. Community liaison is designed to identify the specific risks experienced by women, girls, boys and men based on their gendered roles and resulting patterns of behaviour, for example highlighting risk mitigation for women in firewood collection or for men in farming/cultivating.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes.MRE activities are completed in a manner integrated with Child Protection and other multi-sector WVSS programming (i.e. Education, Food Security & Livelihoods, Health, Nutrition, WASH). WVSS completes Safety Audits for each project site and/or service point (ex. School, Out Patient Therapeutic [OTP] centre, Child Friendly Space, etc) prior to conducting activities to identify safety risks or concerns, and identify/implement recommendations to improve the safety of the project site/service point. This is complemented by Safety Perception Mapping, which captures the safety issues or unsafe areas perceived by beneficiaries, which may be different from observation; this is completed quarterly or ad hoc as necessary and used comparatively to inform project implementation and any adaptations as necessary. MRE sessions are only conducted in project sites, service points or community gathering locations where a prior Safety Audit has taken place and recommendations implemented.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes.Dignity of women, girls, men and boys is reviewed in overall project methodology, as well as specifically for MRE activity approaches, with an emphasis on promoting beneficiary dignity. Monitoring to measure is completed through tracking/addressing complaints and feedback, and through mid-point/end-point surveys with beneficiaries where complaints or feedback mechanisms may not be regularly utilized by beneficiaries. Measurement may also be through UNMAS/NMAA external Operations and/or Quality Assurance visits and uptake of resulting report recommendations.
