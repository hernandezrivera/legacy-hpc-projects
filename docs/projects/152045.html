Project Summary

Prior to the insurgency, the Northeast had some of the worst MNCH indices in the country. PHC systems have collapsed as a result of the armed conflict and MNCH services have been slower in response and recovery than other PHC services.
This project aims to rehabilitate BEmONC and expand CEmONC in health facilities in returnee and host communities. Using a double pronged strategy, rehabilitation and expansion will be done to a level in congruence with emergency needs and minimum standard expected of the level of care the facilities offers in the PHC system i.e. PHC post, clinic or center. Children under five (especially neonates & infants) and women of child bearing age (especially pregnant women & new mothers) will be reached through demand creation activities, fixed and outreach service delivery mechanisms.

Inadequate MNCH service package in NGO supported mobile clinics & health facilities- Even though many partners have midwives as members of health facility support & outreach teams, minimum RH service package is often not included, so commodities & equipment are often not available. RH services are delivered in specialised sites and not part of the PHC package.- Existing support for PHC activities do not extend to MNCH specific strategies e.g. line-listing and compliance tracking of RI eligible children, tracking compliance of neonatal and immediate post natal care- Even though many partners have midwives as members of outreach teams, minimum RH service package is often not included, so commodities & equipment are often not available- Clinical management of rape is currently provided in specialised centers, it should be available in PHC centers as part of the RH package 2. Needs by PHC Thematic Areai. HRH- Inadequate local availability of nurses & midwives- Inadequate amenities (suitable accommodation, potable water, power) to attract midwives from outside, inadequate amenities lead to high attrition rate of available midwives- Lack of commensurate increase in wages for midwives posted to communities in need- Inadequate; HRH, Task Shifting & Sharing Policies and guidelines to fill gaps. Detailed HRH needs and gaps are not always considered when planning health worker roles and responsibilities.- Inadequate capacity of available health workers to fill gaps; majority of available PHC workers take on tasks they have not been trained for e.g. environmental health cadre of workers providing child health care and community health extension workers providing perinatal careii. InfrastructureBasic infrastructure such as first stage & delivery rooms, efficient storage for pharmaceutical products & accommodation for midwives, are lacking in most functional primary health care centers. For adequate coverage, national guidelines stipulate at least one fully functional PHC center per ward, supported by PHC clinics and posts.iii. Drugs and commodities to support servicesiv. Community-Facility Linkage/Community Ownership and ParticipationAs a result of poor coverage of communities by MNCH services, majority of births and deaths occur in the communities without linkage to health facilities through adequate outreach services. Available outreach services by government are not enough to cover communities, and outreach services offered by partners are not linked to PHC systems outreach services for gap filling and capacity building of state staff. Community Owned Resource Persons for demand creation activities are inadequate and ill-trained.

Activities

i. Outcome 1: Increase in ANC and PNC- Staff midwives delivering facility based services and supporting other cadre of health-workers- Train and equip tiers of health workers on perinatal care- Resource based task shifting and sharing at LGA, ward & health facility level- Map communities, design and implement outreach services integrated with PHC system outreach activities- Targeted community health education, mobilisation and community based tracking by community gatekeepers, traditional social support structures, village and ward development committees, trained and equipped TBAs supported by trained skilled health workers- Rehabilitate, expand, build reproductive health units, including furnished living quarters for skilled birth attendants- Train and equip health workers to provide clinical management of rape- Provision of supplies and commodities ii. Increase in skilled birth attendance- Community mapping, identification and training of traditional birth attendants- Training across tiers of health workers- Provision of supplies and commodities e.g. maternity packs, neonatal survival packs- Rehabilitate, expand, build reproductive health units, including furnished living quarters for skilled birth attendants- Establish community based referral system, including emergency transport service through available public transport facilities, train and equip community gate keepers, including TBAs to accompany women in labour iii. Reduction in neonatal and perinatal mortality- Rehabilitate, expand, build and equip Primary Health Care Centers (the highest level of PHC) to provide CEmONC- Regularly roving medical doctors delivering services in PHC Centers and on the job training for other cadre of health workers in the PHC centers and from surrounding PHC clinics and posts within the catchment area- Train and equip tiers of health workers in perinatal care for facility and community based service delivery- Rehabilitate, expand, build and equip child health units with adequate resuscitation facilities- Establish community supported referral system for early presentation to health facilities- Rehabilitate ambulances and support service- Train and equip community health workers and medical professionals to provide as appropriate; uterine balloon tamponades, blood transfusion, provide misoprostol, provide non-pneumatic anti-shock garment in ambulances, intubation
