Project Summary

DASA will provide comprehensive quality health care services to the crisis-affected people, prevent and respond to health threats, and strengthen the health system. This proposed intervention supports the 2024 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) objectives of saving lives, reducing health risks, and enhancing coping and recovery. Our proposed intervention will focus on delivering life-saving health services according to humanitarian and SPHERE standards. During this intervention, DASA will provide epidemiological and surveillance information on diseases, and conditions that affects the population of Kapoeta East County. DASA proposed to provide an effective yet integrated interventions in synergy with other partners working in Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), and Nutrition. This comprehensive intervention will address the needs of the whole affected population.
DASA will provide quality health services to the affected people by health events, including maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, common and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, MHPSS (Mental Health and Psychosocial support) and GBV (Gender Based Violence). The intervention will also improve sexual reproductive health services of basic emergency newborn and obstetric care (BEmONC), family planning, PMTCT (prevention of mother to child transmission), Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and supplies for vulnerable women and girls. We will also use referral mechanism to enhance efficiency, integration, and quality of care. Our intervention will also continue EVD (Ebola Virus Disease) prevention, preparedness, and readiness activities, as South Sudan faces the threat of EVD and other viral hemorrhagic fevers. The response will support health facilities and community-based systems to serve people in need.
The proposed intervention will also strengthen technical, operational, and field coordination with partners and authorities. The intervention will also support disease surveillance and reporting system and preposition medical supplies and equipment in areas affected by Sudan crisis, internally displaced persons (IDPs) influx, returnee’s repatriation, droughts, and floods. This project will target: Total Beneficiaries 20,050 (19,204 host communities, 781 internally displaced persons (IDPs), and 64 returnees), 4,330 Under-five (Male 2185, Female 2145) 7598 Beneficiaries aged 5-17 years (Male 4250, Female 3348) 6, 676 beneficiaries aged 18-60 years (Male 3248, Female 3428) and 1444 Beneficiaries aged 60 years and above (Male 688, Female 756)

1. Needs

A weak health system has characterized South Sudan Health Sector (SSHS) with challenges ranging from inadequate skilled human resources, underfunding (Health Sector budget is only 2.1% of national budget 2023/2024) (Ministry of Finance and Economic Planning, South Sudan), frequent stockouts of medicines and vaccines due to last mile delivery bottlenecks (UNICEF, 2023), high staff turnovers, poor referral systems across the tier of health service delivery worsened by insecurity, and flooding and damaged infrastructures (Health System Assessment Report,2020). This has consequently led to lack of support to half of the health facilities in the country and lack of Boma Health Initiative (BHI) support to 60% of the population countrywide (Crown Agent, 2022). As a fragile state (index 108.5) , South Sudan has the highest maternal mortality ratio (MMR) of 1,223 per 100,000 livebirths in the world according to the recent estimates by UNICEF, WHO, World Bank Group, UNFPA and UNDESA (United Nations Inter-Agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation-UN IGME, 2022) . The high MMR is attributed in part by low birth (19%) been attended by skilled birth personnel and high proportion (87%) of home deliveries (UNFPA, 2020). Child mortality report 2022 indicated that in the year 2021, 12,000 children died before their 28 days of life, Under-five mortality rate was 99 per 1000 live births, Infant and neonatal mortality rates stood at 64, and 40 deaths per 1000 live births respectively (UNICEF,2022). There is high risk associated with delivery and postdelivery periods. Besides, conditions surrounding children birth, malaria, diarrhoea, and pneumonia contributed to 75% of the deaths in under-five children (UNICEF,2022). Routine Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) coverage is low (WHO,2020) due to lack of skilled human resources at grass root level, insecurity, flooding, droughts, Sudan crisis as well as outbreaks of Measles (December 2022) , Cholera (April 2022) , Hepatitis E (April 2023) and Jiggers (October 2023) (MoH, 2023). Despite of the ongoing outbreaks of communicable diseases, there is an upward trend of non-communicable diseases like hypertension etc. There is also high prevalence of wasting (16.2%)(UNICEF, 2021) among the young children taking a heavy toll on their survival and development. The Inter-Agency Rapid Needs Report (INRA) conducted in March 2023 in Jie Payam of Kapoeta East County indicated that the drivers to the humanitarian needs were macroeconomic crisis, climatic shocks particularly dry spells, conflict and insecurity due to cattle rustling as well as food shortages. The key priority needs for health response plan were: lack of basic emergency newborn, maternal and obstetric care (BEmNOC) in Kasengor, lack of integration of mobile outreach services in Lopeat with static primary health care services (PHC), nonfunctional PHC service centers, weak Boma Health Initiative (BHI) and poor infrastructures. Furthermore, poor water supply for human consumption and domestic needs (only one open water point, no boreholes) has contributed to transmission of water borne and sanitation related morbidities. Global acute malnutrition (GAM) in Lopet was estimated to be at 22% among children 6-59 months and 23% pregnant and lactating women (PLW) were found with moderate acute malnutrition in Lopet. Kapoeta East County has been classified as IPC 4 by Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring Survey (FSMNS, 2023). The overall malnutrition prevalence in the county is 11% among the under-five children (INRA Report,2023). Lack of access to sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) service points though SGBV is widespread. Though there is a limited access to mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, there is a high burden of mental health disorder with 2.5 million estimated cases in the country (Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2022).

2. Project activities

The overall objective of the project is to improve equitable access to life-saving essential comprehensive quality health care services and enhance resilience to promote coping and recovery to the crisis affected populations in Kapoeta East County. The specific objectives are: Objective 1: Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child, and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment of common illnesses, disability, MHPSS, and GBV health services to crisis-affected populations. Objective 2: Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordination of responses to epidemic prone diseases. Objective 3: Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms. The key activities are as follows: Under Objective 1, DASA will reappoint and train 50 BHI Boma Health Workers and Supervisors on BHI package in newly selected Bomas; we will conduct refresher training to Health workers in the 10 selected facilities on BPHNS, SAM, CMR, PMTCT, ANC, etc; provide monthly incentives for BHWs, BHI supervisors, and provide BHI equipment for newly nominated bomas and replacement; distribute drugs and medical supplies to 10 health facilities; support weekly integrated outreach and 2 mobile clinics in areas with non-functional facilities; conduct joint integrated supportive supervision with CHD to monitor delivery of essential health services. DASA will also provide incentives to 128 MoH staff at Primary healthcare Centres(PHCC) and PHCUs to provide consultations and related services in the facilties; Support 10 program staff to effectively deliver program activities at headquarter and field levels; Conduct training for 24 health care workers on BEmONC; provide HIV/PMTCT services and SGBV friendly services; Provide Maternal and New-born health services: ANC, Safe birth attendance , postnatal care, family planning in health facilities and through mobile outreach activities; support referrals of patients to next level of care; and provide Vitamin A, Folic Acid and de-worming tablets to children and pregnant women at health facilities and communities; DASA will implement infection prevention and control activities in all supported facilities. DASA will prepare detailed health facility micro-plan to implementation of immunization services including PENTA3 ;conduct daily health facility based routine vaccination; conduct weekly integrated outreach and mobile vaccination services; facilitate the logistics of vaccine and immunization supplies; conduct refresher training for vaccinators on immunization in practice; and support the delivery of Immunization service delivery including last mile delivery assistance, trainings, supportive supervision. DASA will coordinate to ensure integration of rehabilitation for people with disabilities (PWD) as well as mental health psychosocial support services (MHPSS) to the people. Under Objective 2: DASA will coordinate the detection and response to epidemic prone diseases, respond and manage cases of malaria and other morbidities; provide reactive measles campaign vaccination; conduct risk communication and behavioural change session to the community; establish and implement community feedback mechanism in all supported health facilities. Under Objective 3: DASA will work with State Ministry of Health (SMoH) and County Health Department (CHD) to support the PHCCs & PHCUs and Mobile outreach clinics This entails provision of training to CHD; supporting monthly coordination meetings; conducting quarterly consultative meetings to identify needs, responses and impact; and carrying out quarterly meetings with humanitarian, developmental and peace partners.
