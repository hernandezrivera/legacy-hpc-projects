Project Summary

The objective of the proposed actions is to respond comprehensively to the protection risks that vulnerable populations face in the East and South of Libya. The proposed action will focus on identifying and addressing the needs of vulnerable communities namely, IDP, Returnee and host communities. The geographic scope of intervention for the proposed action will largely focus on the East (with Benghazi, Ejdabiya and Derna being the focus) and in the South (with a focus on Sebha, Ghat and Ubari).

Needs

The protection environment in Libya remains greatly challenging to responding actors. As noted in the Human Rights Watch World Report 2018, Libya continues to suffer from weak national and localized institutional actors, poor rule of law, and a static conflict situation between military actors. The political instability in Libya has left a vacuum of effective governance and leadership which continues to deteriorate the protection space. Within this context, the situation for IDPs, returnees, and host communities has steadily degraded. With household financial capacities steadily reducing their ability to meet their basic needs due to the ongoing liquidity and inflation crisis. In the REACH Protection Factsheet for Benghazi May-July 2018, it was clarified that 54% of households assessed had reported at least a member with a serious or chronological illness. As well as the median times of IDPs displaced since 2011 being 4. Coupled with this, the REACH 2018 MSNA dataset illustrates that the presence of explosive remnants of war in communities, high rates of conflict induced psychological trauma, and collateral damage to infrastructure have heightened needs for basic services and life-saving assistance.According to Gender Concerns International , in the past , women in Libya have been actively involved in the development of the economic, social and political sectors. Nonetheless, a number of restrictions which hindered women emancipation and financial independence were imposed in the last decades. This critical situation persisted and even worsened after the 2011 Libya’s revolution. Basing on UNDP 2018 Human Development Reports , the Labour force participation rate for women is 25.8%, compared to 79% for men. Gender-based violence is widespread and has been exacerbated by the conflict in Libya. Due to the conflict and the resulting vacuum of formal authority, collapse of the judicial system and absence of rule of law women and girls are even more unlikely to report crimes of gender-based violence and sexual violence.Violation of human rights and of children and women’s rights, are also widespread, according to UNICEF mid-year Humanitarian situation report 2018, 353 200 children are in need of protection. Given the small number of protection actors operating within the target areas, there is a notable gap in protection services available despite a high level of need.

Activities

1) Community level assessments: Continuing from the implementation of activities in 2018, ACTED team will contextualize community knowledge to guide the identification of particularly vulnerable households for interview. This is achieved by conducting key informant interviews across respective area of concern (local authorities, community leaders, women's group etc.) Topics covered in community level monitoring may include but is not limited to, population characteristics, trends of displacement and return, freedom of movement, shelter and security of tenure, access to registration, basic services and assistance. Additionally, the focus will also be to further develop the current key informants network.2) Household level Protection Monitoring Visits: Households are purposively sampled to ensure that the most vulnerable cases can be connected with services. The data collected enriches and complements the information obtained at the community level, and is related to a range of potential protection risks linked to health, disability, legal assistance, psychosocial, gender-based violence, and basic services. The focus of this proposal will be to increase the geographical expanse of the vulnerable HHs covered by ACTED through the protection monitoring.3) Operation of the Protection Helpline: A protection helpline is already in place and operational in Benghazi. This service provides two dedicated toll-free protection numbers with the two main telecommunications companies in Libya (Libyana, and Almadar). Easy to use and accessible via phone call or SMS, the helpline is operational during working hours and is operated by protection staff trained in humanitarian, confidentiality and non-discrimination principles as well as helpline caller management and follow up processes, outlined in the ACTED protection hotline SOPs. 4) Awareness raising sessions: ACTED will conduct awareness raising sessions on SGBV, and protection concerns through focus groups in areas of high vulnerability. In each of the selected neighborhoods, Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) will be conducted with community members using semi-structured interview guides, the sessions will be facilitated by ACTED trained protection and AME team. Convenience sampling will be used to recruit participants for the FDGs (10 participants per group). A total of 12 FGDs will be conducted, disaggregated per population strata (men, women, youth). Among the groups, participation of returnee, IDP, and host community population will be ensured, at least 120 persons will participate to the sessions. 5) Referral of cases to external service providers: ACTED will review its protection monitoring/helpline caller registration forms to identify which external referral would be the most suitable for vulnerable HHs identified. The referral is made based on a mutual understanding between the beneficiary’s need and the most appropriate referral response determined by the protection monitors. This also requires ACTED to monitor and communicate consistently with the service providers that are present to ascertain what their capacities are in terms of response. ACTED will also support local service provides through the provision of small grants, targeted capacity building activities and training. This will increase the quality and the availability of protection services at local level and will allow to broaden the network of service providers 6) Emergency Protection Cash Assistance: ACTED aims to support specific cases of individuals identified through the protection monitoring with emergency protection cash assistance. The cash component, is unconditional and aims to empower beneficiaries to support their protection needs which can be addressed with life-saving cash assistanceWhile conducting all these activities, ACTED will ensure to coordinate closely with the various relevant WGs, especially with the Child Protection and Gender-Based Violence WGs, and the Protection Cluster globally

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

A way ACTED measure access safety and dignity is through monitoring activities within their established protection Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM). ACTED aims to continue building the capacity of CRM within their 2019 activities. The CRM allows beneficiaries and other community members to contact ACTED directly via phone or email in order to ask questions and express areas of concerns. According to ACTED procedures the complaints will be addressed with a maximum delay of 15 days. The CRM is a key avenue for beneficiaries to voice their concerns and an opportunity for ACTED to tailor and improve activity accountability and delivery. The CRM tool supports ACTED in solidifying a bottom up approach to identifying vital areas for improvement, communication with beneficiaries, and to monitor the consistency of service delivery. ACTED’s activities also encompass a do-no harm approach, and ACTED will expand on this by streamlining the questions asked as part of protection monitoring and the helpline to ensure that information that is collected can serve multiple organizations and clusters, so that beneficiaries do not have to answer multiple questionnaires. Furthermore, ACTED understands that to compliment protection monitoring, it is important that awareness raising activities and Focus Group Discussion (FGDs) are conducted with community representatives and beneficiary groups to gain in depth knowledge of the protective environment that beneficiaries live in and to ensure that the response offered is in line with the needs identified. ACTED follows strict data protection procedures in order to ensure confidentiality and safety of the beneficiaries. For example access to data is restricted to the protection team and confidentiality is maintained within the case management server. The vulnerability of beneficiaries will be verified through HHs visits and phone surveys. Moreover monitoring surveys assessing the satisfaction of service providers and beneficiaries will be elaborated and conducted. Finally, with regards to its Policy against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, ACTED adopts a zero tolerance approach towards sexual exploitation and abuse. ACTED is committed to the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse both within the organisation and within the framework of ACTED programmes and ACTED beneficiary populations.In this context, the aim of ACTED’s PSAE policy is to define expressly sexual exploitation and abuse, to set up a reporting process in the case of a violation of the rules and a specific disciplinary sanction procedure based on the seriousness of the acts committed.
