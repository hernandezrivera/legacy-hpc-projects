Project Summary

The prolonged crisis in the North-West and South-West (NWSW) regions, alongside the Lake Chad Basin Crisis in the Far North region of Cameroon has intensified child protection risks, leaving over 19,000 children unaccompanied or separated due to displacement and exposing adolescent girls to heightened risks of sexual violence, child marriage, and exploitation, as revealed by the MSNA Round 10 (2024) findings. Additionally, only a fraction of at-risk children has access to timely case management services, while 65% of children and caregivers display signs of psychosocial distress.
Children in Cameroon’s conflict-affected regions face severe protection challenges, underlining the critical need for robust case management and strengthened local child protection systems. Findings from the Child Protection Needs Assessment carried out by Street Child of Cameroon (SCOC) with funds from UNICEF (awaiting publication) reveals widespread psychosocial distress, with 48.5% of children in the Far North, 33% in the North West, and 37% in the South West feeling unsafe at home. Only 38.3% of children report feeling happy most of the time, and limited psychosocial support leaves many vulnerable, as only 37.4% of parents report access to adequate services. Additionally, 34.3% of children in the Far North and significant numbers in the North West (30.3%) and South West (27%) are unaccompanied or separated (UASC), with limited case management to address their needs. Child labour, forced labour, and harmful practices, including sexual violence and child marriage, remain pervasive, with sexual violence reported by 59.3% of parents, particularly in the South West (72.4%).
This project builds on the successes of the CARE Project to address these urgent needs by advancing case management services, improving mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), and strengthening local child protection systems. Focusing on enhancing case management services, the project aims to build local child protection systems by empowering local child protection actors, while fostering government collaboration for systemic and sustainable solutions. By training 250 local actors and revitalizing 50 community-based child protection mechanisms (CBCPMs), this initiative ensures sustainable, high-quality child protection services.
Through direct support to unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), children associated with armed forces and armed groups (CAAFAG) and GBV survivors, capacity-building for community-based child protection mechanisms (CBCPMs), and expanding mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, the initiative will ensure vulnerable children and families receive timely, high-quality protection services.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The project, "Building Resilient Futures: Strengthening Case Management and Local Child Protection Systems in Cameroon," will address several pressing child protection needs identified through the MSNA Round 10 (2024) and the UNICEF & SCOC CP Needs Assessment (2024). These needs include: i. Separation from Caregivers: Over 19,000 children are unaccompanied or separated due to displacement, and the high rates of unaccompanied and separated children (UASC)—34.3% in the Far North, 30.3% in the North West, and 27% in the South West—require urgent attention. The project will focus on improving case management services for UASC, including family tracing and reunification, interim care, and ensuring that these children are provided with the necessary protection and support. ii. Child Associated with Armed Groups (CAFAAG): The prevalence of children associated with armed groups, particularly in the Far North (60.2%), presents complex reintegration challenges. The project will prioritize the rehabilitation and reintegration of these children, including providing tailored case management to address their specific needs, such as trauma healing, education, and community reintegration, thereby supporting their transition to a safer environment. iii. Gender-Based Violence (GBV): Adolescent girls, in particular, face significant risks, with over 60% of GBV cases involving sexual violence and exploitation. The project will enhance the capacity of local child protection actors to respond to sexual violence and exploitation, by improving case management systems, providing survivor-centered care, strengthening referral mechanisms, and offering psychosocial support for GBV survivors. iv. Limited Case Management Services: Less than 20% of vulnerable children receive necessary interventions, and there is a limited capacity among local child protection actors to provide comprehensive case management. The project will focus on training and supporting local child protection workers, improving case management processes, and ensuring that vulnerable children—especially those impacted by conflict and displacement—receive timely, effective, and holistic support. v. Weakened Community Mechanisms: With only 35% of Community-Based Child Protection Mechanisms (CBCPMs) functioning effectively, the project will work on strengthening these local systems by providing resources, training, and improving coordination. This will ensure that communities are better equipped to identify, report, and respond to child protection concerns, thus improving the overall protective environment for children. vi. Psychosocial Distress: Given that 65% of children and caregivers are exhibiting symptoms of trauma from displacement and conflict, addressing psychosocial distress is crucial. The project will integrate mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) into case management, providing services such as child-friendly spaces, individual and group counseling, and community-based trauma healing programs to help children and caregivers recover from the emotional and psychological effects of conflict and displacement. By addressing these key needs, the project aims to strengthen local child protection systems, improve case management services, and provide comprehensive support to vulnerable children and their caregivers, ultimately building more resilient communities and improving the well-being of children in conflict-affected regions of Cameroon.
