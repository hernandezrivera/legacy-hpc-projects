Project Summary

Caritas Bangladesh is proposing a targeted Shelter/NFI program under the JRP 2024 of SCCCM Sector, focusing on specific camps 13 & 19 and the host community under Ratna Palong and Raja Palong union. Our plan aims to assist a total of 15259 households (76,140 individuals) including Camp 13 (9,231 HHs,),Camp 19 (5,428 HHs,),and 600 Households in the host community (Ratna Palong and Raja Palong union).This initiative has planned based on the identified needs through J-MSNA report,shelter standard assessments report, and internal monitoring/assessment report by Caritas.The Joint Response Plan (JRP-2024) outlines key activities, including emergency NFI support (400HHs),regular NFI support (1,000 HHs),Rapid damage verification (RDV) (2204HHs), emergency materials prepositioning (1000 HHs),Tie down kits (TDK) (14,659 HHs),Household level Shelter Improvement (6,902 HHs),New Mid-Term-Shelter (400 HHs),regular shelter reinforcement(TSA, SUM and/or R&M, MTS R&M etc.) (9,202 HHs), continuation of treated bamboo support from IOM (9,602 HHs),solar lamps distribution/repair (1,000 HHs),tree plantation (5,428 HHs),solar streetlight repair/new installation (100 Nos.) in the camp and research-based pilot on alternative shelter materials solutions including construction materials waste management both for camp & Host community.Moreover, In the host community, we have also considered shelter Repair (200 HHs) and rural road construction/repair under Cash for Work (CFW) intervention (500 HHs). Families who has already received shelter support in the FY23, will receive regular shelter assistance in FY24 again but they will be considered for emergency shelter/NFI support (if needed).Emergency NFI support, RDV related shelter materials, relocation shelter construction, and emergency TDK will be ensured from IOM pipeline and distribute with technical support from caritas.Catchment area/camp focal points will be assigned for emergency support,shelter/protection-related referral services in the targeted camps 13 & 19. Persons with disabilities and extremely vulnerable individuals including elderly aged peoples will receive porter and construction support for the targeted-shelter/NFI-activities.
Community based approach including integrated approach with maximum community engagement in all phases all through the program cycle (assessment, project design, decision making, implementation and monitoring) for each targeted intervention whether male, female, adolescent girls, boys, elderly aged peoples, person with disabilities will be engaged and specially women & elderly aged people will be engaged as daily labor with lightweight work. Inclusion of women in gender-segregated training, and recruitment of women volunteers will be ensured in FY24. The program aims to create a durable, safe, secure, dignified, and environmentally friendly, more disaster resilient shelter/NFI program ensuring social cohesion. JRP2024 has considered rationalization of SCCCM Sector. The overall program will include policy related to PSEA,child protection,safeguarding,complain/feedback mechanism,enhancing Protection/gender Mainstreaming , Gender sensitive & transformative Shelter/NFI programming. Technical support for design and implementation of specific accessibility and provision of additional materials for access of persons with disability people will be coordinated with ADWG/Protection sector. Caritas will coordinate with Energy & environment network on the climate action strategy particularly for the activities like solar lamps & Solar street lights, tree plantations, research on environment friendly alternative shelter materials. CRS Bangladesh will provide technical support to Caritas throughout the Program cycle in FY24. Effective coordination with relevant sectors and humanitarian actors including CiC, RRRC, CIMO, DC Office, Site Management, UNO Office, union parishad etc. with effective participation in the meetings, reporting etc. will be maintained.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

"In 2024, the situation in the camp remains challenging, exacerbated by a decrease in funding across the sectors. The critical needs for both shelter materials and non-food items (NFI) persist both regular phase & emergency, making them top priorities. Shelter essentials such as bamboo poles, tarpaulins, and ropes require regular replacement due to their limited lifespan and exposure to harsh weather conditions like cyclones, flooding, landslides, heavy rain, and winds. Additionally, the risk of fire adds another layer of urgency to this matter. To maintain decent living conditions, continuous provision of shelter assistance through emergency responses, SUM, and the repair and construction of new MTS is imperative. These interventions directly contribute to protecting the affected populations, ensuring security, privacy, and health for the beneficiaries. The need for an increased budget to address repairs and maintenance of shelters has become evident in recent years. According to the J-MSNA report in 2023, 41% of refugees reported not receiving any shelter assistance, with 60% citing a lack of humanitarian support and 34% indicating insufficient aid. Issues such as leaking during rainfall (69% of households), inadequate lighting (66% of households), and privacy concerns (64% of households) persist, even after six years of response efforts. The Shelter Performance Standard report 2023, highlights deficiencies in fire-protecting walls (27% of households) and internal partition walls for privacy (24% of households) and 22% shelters Rainwater enters through the roof . Furthermore, the report emphasizes the need for household-level site improvement activities, including drainage, retention walls, pathways, stairs, and the use of durable materials. Only 12% of households reported having functional drainage. Additionally, 43% of households expressed a need for solar lamps for safety during the night, emphasizing the importance of continuing provision alongside repair and maintenance activities. On the NFI the J-MSNA report underscores that key items for affected populations are still insufficient. Instead of random in-kind NFI distribution, a voucher approach is recommended to ensure freedom of choice for beneficiaries. However, the Government has restricted shelter assistance to non-durable materials, necessitating annual replacements. To slow down the deterioration of rate of bamboo and reduce the need for replacement, the procedures for bamboo treatment were put in place by the UN & INGOs/NGOs. Though sector is exploring alternative materials for shelter construction but until all those materials the overall needs will be unchanged. To address wastage and maximize resources, CB is actively exploring research on construction and demolition waste management and reuse in shelter components. Shelters in host communities also face risks, with 7% of households lacking support, and 45% reporting issues, particularly with rainwater leakage. Support in host communities should prioritize shelter reconstruction or repair, training on building back safer shelters, and household-level lighting initiatives. Cross-sector coordination, especially with Energy & Environment, WASH, and Shelter Task Force, is crucial. Micro-level site planning for new shelters and household-level site improvement are deemed necessary to mitigate additional risks faced by shelters. Conversely, the evolving needs for NFI items include solar fans, mosquito nets, kitchen sets, and portable solar lights. In recent years due to deforestration the is huge cut of trees which was a negative impact on environment so plantation also taken as a priority needs. "

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

In the proposed 2024 initiatives, CB is committed to actively promoting rationalization principles in the operations of camps 13 and 19 and host community Ratnapalong & Rajapalong union. This systematic approach seeks to establish a cohesive strategy, enhance coordination, facilitate cost-sharing, minimize operational expenses, and optimize resource utilization and integrated holistic approach. Since 2019, CB has steadfastly adhered to rationalization principles, particularly working on the protection sector in camp 19. In FY24, additionally CB strategically plans to expand its focus in camps 13 and 19 to include the livelihood and skill development sector, emphasizing sustainability. CB shares common area to comprehensively support all CB engaged sectors and actively engages in coordination platforms at the camp. The organization welcomes partners, collaborating and following joint response mechanisms in consultation with sectors. Transparency of data and alignment of strategies between CB and stakeholders are prioritized. As an integral member of the Cox's Bazar PSEA Network, CB has appointed PSEA and Child Safeguarding Focal to ensure ethical practices. CB possesses the flexibility to share resources across different sectors within the same camp. For each sector, CB ensures provision of standard service packages, addressing identified gaps and preventing overlaps through timely and proper approvals. This systematic process is carried out collaboratively with sectors, CIC, RRRC, UNO, DC, ISCG, UN agencies, and both international and national organizations. The comprehensive strategy extends to activities in both the camp and host community, maximizing the impact of rationalization principles. Operating principles include consultation with relevant sectors, adherence to minimum standards, adoption of an integrated approach, ensuring continuity, flexibility, predictability, timely responses, and transparency in information sharing with partners.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Protection mainstreaming will be diligently implemented through active participation and accountability to the affected populations, ensuring access, safety, privacy, and security throughout the project cycle. The selection of targeted HHs will follow specific criteria based on protection needs, assessments, referrals from the protection sector, and concerns raised in the FCRM platform. Priority will be given to maintaining data confidentiality, adhering to humanitarian response protection principles. To ensure the utmost confidentiality, guidelines and policies of CB from Protection Against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Child Protection and Safeguarding and the broader protection sector will be strictly followed.CB will coordinate with the PSEA network, CPSS, GBVSS, and the protection sector, utilizing designated focal points for PSEA, child protection, and safeguarding. Transparency and accountability toward beneficiaries will be a top priority. Special focus will be given to addressing the needs of vulnerable groups, including extremely vulnerable individuals (EVIs), female-headed households (FHHs), households with special needs (HHwSN), and the LGBTQ community. Initiative such as stairs with handrails, ramps, partition walls, and separate working crews for male & female,training rooms , female training with female facilitators, etc will be provided for relevant households, ensuring inclusivity. The project will uphold protection principles, ensuring confidentiality, coordination with relevant sectors, and prioritizing transparency and accountability. The specific needs of vulnerable groups will be addressed through tailored activities, fostering a holistic and inclusive approach.

3.3 - Age & Disability

"CB is committed to promoting equal opportunities for all groups in both camp and host community activities. Discrimination against any group will be actively avoided through transparent information sharing and decision-making processes. CB staunchly prohibits the engagement of child labor (below 18 years old) in project activities, prioritizing child rights promotion and ensuring the safety and security of children during construction and repair works. Inclusivity is a key focus, and CB will provide labor and porter support to women with children, pregnant women, etc., ensuring their participation in meetings, training, assessments, implementation, and decision-making processes. Priority will also be given to the elderly, with specific arrangements made for meaningful participation. CB recognizes the importance of SADDD to identify the distinct needs of different groups. Special attention will be given to community sensitization, messaging, and involving the preferences of elderly people and adolescents in decision-making processes. During distributions and training, CB will create designated children's playing spaces to ensure their safety. Volunteers comprehensive training in child safeguarding, child protection, PSEA, and a code of conduct to minimize risks. Construction and porter labor engagement for CFW will typically target individuals aged 18-59. However, for those over 59 who are physically able and willing to participate in CFW with lighter task. The project aims to improve access to services for persons with disabilities through activities and referrals, with coordination maintained through meetings and capacity building with the Age & Disability Working Group. CB allocates an inclusive budget to address the specific needs of persons with disabilities to fostering an equitable and accessible environment. People with disability will be supported by disable friendly access to the shelter with handrail and household level site improvements"

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

CB is dedicated to upholding accountability to affected populations and all stakeholders throughout the program cycle. Embracing a community-based approach in SCCCM activities, CB actively involves the community in needs assessment, project planning, implementation, monitoring, and ensuring project sustainability. To maintain transparency and responsiveness, CB has established internal , camp-level & host community level Complaint Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) processes. These mechanisms collect and address complaints and feedback from the community ensuring timely resolution throughout the project cycle. Community participants can submit their concerns through various channels, including complaint/feedback boxes, hotline numbers, and the CFRM help desk. Submissions undergo verification by the Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) team, FCRM focal points, safeguarding focal points, and key community personnel. The verified results are communicated back to Rohingya/Host community participants. Adhering to the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) principles, CB shares the program plan with the community before initiating project activities. Crucial information about the project is disseminated through project inception and lesson learned meetings, providing a platform for engagement with stakeholders. Referrals from Site Management/Protection teams or project participants are promptly addressed after verification by CB's dedicated FCRM and Safeguarding teams. Verified results are communicated to the Site Management/Protection team, who then share them with beneficiaries. The project ensures coordination at both camp and sector levels. CB is committed to ensuring community-wide awareness of the FCRM process, and the MEAL team plays a crucial role in effectively communicating and promoting understanding of these mechanisms within the community.

To ensure comprehensive understanding and compliance, CB will conduct regular training and refresher orientation sessions on PSEA for all project participants including direct benificiaries by allocating funds accordingly. Within project cycle, CB will ensure the visibility of PSEA awareness materials such as posters, flyers, and videos at distribution points, training centers, and office compounds, reinforcing key PSEA messages including separate training rooms and distributions points.. In the project areas, CB establishes visible communication mechanisms, including Complaint Feedback Mechanisms/pathways to facilitate open channels for complaint & feedback. During any intervension or Traning there will be compulsary shaaring on PSEA referral/compalint mechanisms. Furthermore, Caritas Bangladesh prioritizes risk management and PSEA by designating focal points for these aspects within the project. These appointed individuals play a crucial role in overseeing risk and PSEA management. In handling complaints, CB is dedicated to maintaining confidentiality, accessibility, and cultural appropriateness. The organization will take immediate and necessary actions upon receiving a complaint of SEA, following the SOP outlined for SEA Complaint Referral in the Rohingya Refugee Response in Bangladesh. Through these comprehensive measures, Caritas Bangladesh strives to create a safe and respectful environment within its project, actively working to prevent and address any instances of PSEA.
