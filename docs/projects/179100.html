Project Summary

Hope Revival Organization (HRO) aims to continue providing GBV services in the WGSSs established in Idleb (Kherbet Eljoz and Foz-Zuf) and will provide protection services in our already existed community centres in Idleb (Jisr Ash Shugur, Darkosh, Ariha, Mhambal, and Kafr Jales) and in Aleppo (Afrin) and establishing a new community centre in Aleppo (Afrin) that will be funded by other donors and through outreach visits to the widows’ camps (2 in Janudiyeh and 2 in Darkosh) to target the widowed women with services such as GBV case management, where some of them will receive case fund, Micro Grant, vocational training (in the WGSSs or in widows’ camps) to reduce the violence against women, in addition to establishing women committees in these camps and building their capacities, and to ensure that a greater number of people have access to the information related to GBV services in their communities. HRO will also provide cash and voucher assistance through Case management and individual protection assistance (IPA) to ensure that GBV risks are mitigated during project period. The planned number of centres is (9) where women and girls can socialize and gain access to lifesaving GBV services, including case management, referrals, PSS for survivors focusing on vulnerable groups such as divorced, widows, females with disabilities, as well as targeting male survivors of sexual violence. Also, they will gain access to other GBV activities such Life Skills, Free spaces for women and girls, vocational Training, Awareness raising by using GBV Tool kits, GBV emergency response plan that will be activated during new displacement, evacuation, and continuing to distribute GBV IEC materials, training of Humanitarian actors (non-specialist) on GBV risk mitigation and referral to specialized services, in addition to conducting safety audits. HRO will also provide Community-based protection services to ensure that IDPs and host community are effectively involved in the project lifecycle from design to implementation to feedback through the already established protected feedback channels to help them report suggestions, complaints, and PSEA concerns. 14,444 beneficiaries will be targeted with the project’s activities including 9,244 Females, 5,200 Males (1,320 Elderly, 8,378 Adults, 4,640 Children, 106 Young Children, and 722 PwD) where the disaggregation at governorate level will be as follows: (Idleb: 10,000 beneficiaries including 6,400 Females, 3,600 Males /900 Elderly, 5,790 Adults, 3,240 Children, 70 Young Children, and 500 PwD/) (Aleppo: 4,444 beneficiaries including 2,844 Females, 1,600 Males /420 Elderly, 2,588 Adults, 1,400 Children, 36 Young Children, and 222 PwD/).through 9 centres including 29 protection staff (9 outreach teams, 15 case managers, 2 supervisors), in addition to M&E teams to ensure the quality of the activities implemented and the application of the accountability mechanisms, considering the gender equality. HRO considers the accountability to the affected populations as essential and priority relying on HRO’s code of conduct, M&E feedback, GBV M&E Toolkit and complaint mechanisms.
HRO will ensure smooth coordination with the protection NGO partners, local councils, stakeholders, and camp mangers throughout the project period.

4 - Needs

As of January 2021, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance, including 6 million in acute need, due to a convergence of factors arising from a sharp reduction in purchasing power, the loss of essential livelihoods and income, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services such as health, WASH, education, nutrition and critical protection services, inadequate shelter conditions as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic as indicated in HNO (March 2021), where 25% of them have disabilities and 75% of them are women and children, where children with and without disabilities comprise 44% of the people in need across Syria. As of February 2021, approximately 1.7 million IDPs were living in 1,385 camps or informal sites (including 24,800 people displaced in January and February 2021 alone), Women and children represent 80% of this caseload.According to the Voices 2021, Economic deprivation, lack of livelihood opportunities, discrimination, exclusion, and socio-cultural norms have led to the continued normalization of GBV and severely impacted the psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights, including those with disabilities, with 62% of communities confirmed that cases of early marriage affected adolescent girls between the ages of 12 and 17.And according to the Protection Need Assessment conducted by HRO during February and April 2021 in Jisr Ash Shugur, Kafr Jales, Darkosh, Gandoura, and Afrin, 43% reported that the violence has increased during COVID-19 outbreak.

5 - Implementation of activities

After ensuring the preparedness of the project logistics and recruiting procedures, staff will be trained on GBV basics, GBV Case Management, inclusion of PwDs, and the mechanisms adopted to submit the MoVs. Afterwards, teams will proceed with conducting the following activities: GBV prevention and mitigation including empowerment activities, GBV case management, training for humanitarian actors, including PwDs, to target 21,546 beneficiaries through the fixed facilities and outreach teams, as planned.GBV awareness raising sessions, Life Skills Activities (Girls dreams program), Free space: the WGSS will focus on (adolescent girls, divorced and widowed women, women, and girls with disabilities and elderly) to socialize and re-build their social networks, in addition to providing Vocational Training and Information/sensitization sessions about GBV related services.As for the response activities: Specialized GBV services including (case management, case fund, referral, micro grant) within the WGSSs, communities, and especially widows’ camps, in addition to implementing safety audits in the widows’ camps. Also, there will be capacity building activity for humanitarian actors and local authorities on GBV SOPs and GBV risk mitigation, in addition to building the capacity of M&E staff on GBV SC M&E Toolkit and training the project’s staff on verifying the MoVs.Vocational training including first aid, hairdressing, computer skills, learning languages, sewing.M&E will follow daily monitoring, measuring progress against project indicators. Compliance mechanism is in place to ensure quality, transparency and good access of vulnerable groups to all available services.
