Project Summary

In the aftermath of famine and massive displacement, Somalia continues to suffer from turbulence in its political, economic, social and environmental conditions. Due to low revenues and limited government-to-government aid, Somalia’s government institutions remain weak and cannot adequately provide goods and services to support the well-being and productivity of its citizens. This, coupled with ongoing conflict, has exposed communities to harmful shocks from frequent droughts, with women and children particularly at risk. Dhuur Relief and Development Organization seeks twelve months funding from SHF for WASH project in Baidoa. WASH interventions will emphasize reducing diseases and hazards among host communities and IDPs displaced by the Al-Shabab in southern and central Somalia, which has contributed to poor environmental health conditions, unsafe disposal of feces, water-borne diseases, unsafe water sources, and poor sanitation and hygiene practices. Access to water, hygiene and sanitation services remains low in the targeted areas of Bay region. Increasing access to sanitation and safe water services and attending to hygiene behaviors are key aspects of a comprehensive approach to lowering malnutrition and disease.7 During periods of poor rain, there are extreme water shortages, which raise water costs, especially in urban centers. Reports from the WASH cluster indicate that there have been no active WASH programs in the Bay region for the past two years. Most IDPs lack quality WASH supplies (soap, clean water storage containers and safe drinking water) to achieve good levels of hygiene and sanitation. This has been compounded by limited knowledge of good hygiene practices and poor environmental sanitation due to the shortage or lack of latrines, which has contributed to the spread of WASH related diseases including acute watery diarrhea (AWD) outbreaks. In spite of an increased humanitarian presence in Baidoa, IDPs and the host communities have inadequate access to potable water and appropriate sanitation facilities.11 Like much of Somalia, there are no boreholes yielding high-quality non-saline water in Baidoa district. Shallow wells are the only water sources with non-saline water. Few households treat water before drinking. The local practice for “treating” water at the household level involves dipping special tree roots into untreated water. This method assists in separating mud from water but does not improve the water in any other respect. The average distance to a water source is 4.8 kilometers during the dry period with an average waiting time of one hour and ten minutes at the water point during the dry period and 22 minutes during the rainy season, falling far short of the Sphere minimum standards of 500 metres and 30 minutes. Water collection is performed by mostly women (70% of the households), and to a lesser extent the duty is also shared by men and children; only 7% of households expressed that water collection is a shared responsibility.12 Women face risks including sexual violence in traveling such long distances to access water from shallow wells. Access to clean and safe water remains limited in Baidoa, and the recent AMISOM offensive against AS in south central has led to overcrowding in IDP camps in Baidoa. This, together with limited government control of water resources, has led to acute water shortages in Baidoa Town

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1.1 Rehabilitation/upgrading of shallow wells. To increase the sustainability of safe water sources for IDPs and vulnerable community costs, the project will rehabilitate/upgrade 15 shallow wells in various states of disrepair with 10 hand pumps in Baidoa town and nearby host villagesActivity 1.2 Construction of animal water troughs. To decrease the competition between humans and animals at the water sources, and to protect the sources themselves, animal water troughs will be constructed and attached to the five rehabilitated wells which are currently being used by both animals and people. Improved livestock water access has two immediate benefits to the human population: it reduces the water stress on human water points and enhances resilience of the beneficiaries.Activity 1.3 Build 80 latrines in IDP concentration areas and host communities. To ensure availability of sanitation and hygiene facilities to promote safe human waste disposal by the IDPs, DRDO will construct 100 latrines with areas of greatest need and the concerns of safe access prioritized when sites are selected. The exact locations of the latrines within the IDP camps and surrounding areas will be determined in conjunction with community members and leaders from Baidoa town, with care taken to maximize input from women, children, the elderly and persons with disabilities.Activity 1.4 Construction of Institutional Latrines. DRDO will build 4-four door institutional latrines in BayHaw hospital. These latrines will be constructed according to WASH cluster and UNICEF designs recommended for health and school institutions in Somalia. They will have hand washing facilities and de-sludge able offset pits with tightly fitted manholes.Activity 1.5 Establish 40 hand washing facilities. DRDO will provide 50 hand washing facilities in the Baidoa IDP camps. These hand washing facilities will be located near newly built latrines constructed by DRDO. The communities will be trained through hygiene promotion on hand washing including ensuring the hand washing facilities are filled with water and have soap or ash available. To ensure greater impact, the hand washing facilities will also be situated near MCH sites, child-friendly spaces, and among other communal latrines, with sites selected with gender and protection concerns in mind.Activity 1.6 Conduct Hygiene Sessions. Among the ever-increasing population in the target area, beneficiaries still suffer from waterborne or hygiene related diseases due to unsafe drinking water, unclean domestic environments, and improper excreta disposal.Activity 1.7 Ten Water Committees Trained. In an activity that will support all WASH sub-sectors, the project will implement water committee capacity building workshops for ten water committees. The water committees include women and men, a chairman, vice chairman, secretary, the treasurer and others community members, system operators and a representative of persons with disabilitiesActivity 1.8 Distribution of WASH NFIs to improve hygiene and sanitation practices. DRDO will provide WASH NFIs to complement WASH activities being implemented in Baidoa. The WASH NFIs will assist IDP households regain assets lost.Activity 1.9 Sanitation tool kits for garbage pits. Assessment report indicates that Baidoa Town are in crisis in regards to environmental hygiene.36 Sanitation tools kits will be provided to the hygiene and sanitation committees formed during the hygiene sessions to ensure safe solid waste disposal.Activity 2.0 Construction of a standard incinerator with a waste separation zone and waste bin in BayHaw hospital. Bayhaw hospital lacks an efficient solid waste management system. This intervention will seek to put a functional waste separation zones for segregation of biodegradable from non-biodegradable materials, incinerator and a landfill. This will reduce the current trend where dangerous waste are being disposed-off inappropriately within the hospital.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

(FGM/C) is a common practice in Bay region and is undertaken when girls approach puberty. Women’s voices are often marginalized and minimized from a full role in household decision-making especially among more rural communities. Men and women are not seen as equals. Gender roles based on sex are clearly defined, and Somalis are expected not to defy these social norms. Women are the traditional caretakers within the family structure or within the home, and maintain traditional roles by staying within the home. Because of a complex religious structure and being ruled by former Islamists, women’s voices and roles have been marginalized. Somalia is mainly a patriarchal society, where men are traditionally the leaders and make decisions for both communities and families. Maternal risks are high; one in 16 women is likely to die of maternal causes in the course of their lifetime. Thus, gender analysis will guide the selection of project activities, the selection of sites and times for those activities, and the community sensitization undertaken during the project. Among the steps to ensure that women’s unique concerns are addressed will include: · The project will increase the protection of women and girls from abuse by ensuring that the water points and sanitation facilities will be located in safe places that are easily accessible. · The project will ensure that all facilities such as latrines are designed with adequate private space for women and girls to be able to clean and to dispose sanitary materials with dignity. Latrines will be made separate for men and women in addition to being lockable. · Women and girls will participate in the selection of sites and the nature of water points and sanitation facilities. Women will be members of all WASH committees. The project will consult with women, men, boys and girls about location, design, safety and appropriateness of facilities. · The project will reduce the workload of women and girls who are generally tasked with fetching water for domestic use. Women and girls are the ones who are in charge of household hygiene, so better access to water and other WASH facilities will lessen their burdens. · For enhanced protection of women and girls, Community Health Workers will be trained to detect, respond to and refer cases of violence and abuse, and exploitation of boys and girls and WASH facilities like toilets, water points and bathing areas especially in IDP camps will be located in safe places, with good visibility. This project will work to ensure that disability and age, as well as gender, never constrain the ability of all people to access WASH and health services, and that DRDO’s minimum standards for protection mainstreaming are applied in all project activities. The design of water and sanitation facilities will enable easy access and use by people of all ages, especially children and the elderly, and people with unique needs such as physical disabilities. People living with disabilities will participate in project activities through election in project committees, where they will contribute to decision-making; no such committee will be recognized if those with disabilities are not represented. During community outreach meetings, both men and women will discuss and contribute ideas on how the improve project operations. DRDO will adopt a participatory monitoring and evaluation approach involving men and women, boys and girls and the elderly.
