Project Summary

The programme complements the introduction of formal schooling with the Myanmar curriculum by delivering life skills education (LSE) and other extracurricular opportunities to adolescents in Rohingya refugee camps and host communities in Cox’s Bazar. The interventions will address the protection (i.e. gender-based violence [GBV] and child marriage) and health (i.e. sexual and reproductive health [SRH]) outcomes that both exacerbate and are the result of the lack of access to education and other age-appropriate recreational activities.
UNFPA will implement LSE programs - i.e., the Girl Shine Programme for adolescent girls and the Champions of Change program for adolescent boys - equip participants with skills & knowledge on gender equality, GBV, SRHR, menstrual health and hygiene management, and other essential social and emotional skills. These programs were developed using international methodologies and standards and have been translated, contextualized and tested within the Rohingya refugee context.
The LSE programs will primarily be conducted as an out-of-school education intervention in UNFPA's Adolescent & Youth Centers, but can be replicated in other educational facilities and multipurpose centers. UNFPA will also explore the feasibility of implementing LSE within the formal education system in schools, including through curricula that have been developed at the national level. Throughout the year, UNFPA will also contribute to building the capacity of teachers, youth mentors, and other education stakeholders on the abovementioned LSE topics.
UNFPA will also conduct awareness-raising and sensitization efforts among caregivers of adolescents and the community at large to address the sociocultural barriers to the continued participation of adolescents in formal and informal learning opportunities, particularly adolescent girls who are pulled out of educational activities upon their menarche, which in turn increases their risk of child marriage as well as negatively impacts their protection and health outcomes. UNFPA will implement the Girl Shine Caregiver Program to engage male and female caregivers in promoting positive family dynamics and gender equality within families, including encouraging the holistic development of the adolescents in their care through educational, recreational, and social activities. Interventions to ensure that girls are able to manage their menstrual health and hygiene and are not deterred from participating in public life by their menstrual status will also be implemented, including the distribution of MHM kits, sessions on reusable menstrual pad production, and ensuring access to MHM-friendly toilets. These will be complemented by conditional cash transfers to the family of vulnerable girls to reduce their likelihood of dropping out due to economic reasons and/or to child marriage.
Finally, UNFPA will organize innovative activities that engage the community and raise awareness on GBV, child marriage, SRH, and other issues. This will include Sports for Development and Theater for Development activities, and other creative modalities for community engagement. These activities also serve the purpose of providing adolescents with positive recreational and social activities to improve their overall wellbeing.
The programme will primarily target adolescent (15 to 18 years old) girls and boys, as well as their male and female caregivers. A whole-of-family approach will be adopted in beneficiary targeting - i.e., including the adolescent girl and boy and their male and female caregivers in the respective LSE program. The approach aims to mobilize family units to catalyze social norm change within their communities while also providing the adolescents who undergo LSE programs with a conducive environment to put into practice what they have learned. Referred individuals and households who have been targeted by other interventions or that are indexed as vulnerable will likewise be prioritized.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

In both refugee camps and host communities, deprivation of education has been increasing since 2019 (REVA-6), though access to formal schooling has long been limited even before their mass displacement - less than 60% of Rohingya children who came into Bangladesh have ever attended school in Myanmar, while less than 10% graduated beyond primary-level (Joint Education Needs Assessment, 2018). Households with intersectional vulnerabilities, such as female-headed households and households with PWDs are also generally less likely to have access to educational opportunities, creating a ripple effect in their health, protection, and livelihood outcomes (REVA-6). There is also a significant gender gap in participation in educational activities. The 2023 MSNA found that girls aged 12 to 18 have the lowest rate of enrollment and regular attendance in educational activities - 21% compared to the 51% of adolescent boys and the 85-90% of children aged 5 to 11. Adolescent girls also most commonly miss education because education is not a priority (21%), she needs to help out at home (19%), there is a lack of male-female separation in facilities (18%), or she has reached puberty (9%) or is already married/pregnant (8%). The Menstrual Health & Hygiene Management Survey and FGDs conducted by UNFPA (2023) corroborate this as parents and caregivers opposed the continued education of adolescent girls after their menarche, resulting in limited access to educational opportunities, as well as economic, social and recreational opportunities. A report by NRC in 2022 revealed that refugee adolescents and youth remain mostly on the margins of humanitarian programming with limited interventions that target and are responsive and appropriate to the developmental phase of the age group. This contributes to the increased rate of child marriage, participation in informal (child) labor, and negative coping mechanisms like drug peddling. Forcibly displaced adolescent girls also experience a disproportionate burden of poor SRH, including high rates of GBV, child marriage, maternal mortality, unintended pregnancies and unsafe abortions. These risks are exacerbated by the lack of information on and taboo nature of GBV and SRH topics leading caregivers and other authority figures to provide limited or incorrect information to adolescents and youth on puberty and other topics. Child marriage is also quickly becoming a growing concern as both a form of and a contributing factor to GBV. Prevalence estimates vary, but more recent studies peg the child marriage (before age 18) among Rohingya women aged 18 to 24 in camps at 13.5%, and at 36.6% among women aged 20 to 24 living in host communities in Cox’s Bazar. FGDs and consultations with both Rohingyas and Bangladeshis in Cox’s Bazar also suggest that families, adolescents and young people resort to marriage and cohabitation as a response to the lack of educational, economic, and recreational opportunities. Inversely, global evidence suggests that keeping girls in school significantly reduces their risk for child marriage. Given this, it is critical to provide access to LSE skills development opportunities to equip adolescents and young people with knowledge, attitudes and values that will empower them to realize their well-being and dignity, develop respectful social and sexual relationships, consider how their choices affect their own well-being and that of others, and ensure the protection of their rights throughout their lives. The programme will also contribute to dismantling taboos around GBV to build a gender equal society and to contribute to the improvement of the enrollment and attendance rates of adolescent girls in education. UNFPA's goal is to reach 7,248 learners and an additional 32,720 beneficiaries, including teachers, parents/caregivers, and all components under Objective 3 for community engagement. The overall budget amounts to US$1,200,000, translating to a cost of US$165 per learner.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

In line with Principle #3, UNFPA will capitalize on its organizational mandate and strengthen the integration of GBV and SRH services in its Adolescent & Youth Center where the livelihood and skills development interventions will be implemented. Additionally, UNFPA will adopt an integrated approach to improve service delivery particularly through ongoing coordination efforts with partners from the Education Sector, as well as across various sectors, including: the Livelihood and Skills Dvelopment Sector on the interventions' alignment with prevocational training interventions; the Health Sector on the interlinkages with SRH; the Protection Sector, including the GBV and Child Protection Sub-Sectors on the interlinkages with child marriage and GBV; and the WASH Sector on improving menstrual hygiene management. Prior to initiating the project, UNFPA has also conducted consultations with targeted populations at the community-level in Cox's Bazar, as well as engaged in discussions and coordination mechanisms with humanitarian actors. UNFPA has been one of the active partners in the inter-sectoral coordination mechanism since the beginning of the influx and always maintained respectful partnership and networking with relevant stakeholders and government officials. In line with Principle #4, UNFPA will ensure predictability and continuity by continuing to operate in the same areas where it has an already-established presence and with the same well-established IPs. UNFPA has been prioritizing multi-year programming and funding in its resource mobilization endeavors, and part of the proposed interventions will be financed through already-mobilized multi-year projects. Additionally, UNFPA engages local implementing partners in Cox's Bazar and continuously supports the development of their technical and institutional capacity to operate on the ground, thereby contributing to building local capacity to deliver livelihood and skills development interventions.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project promotes the protection of affected populations and prioritizes safety, dignity and rights of all. The principle of "do no harm" is incorporated throughout the project cycle, and is applied to facilities, services, and outreach activities. Through the project, UNFPA will continue to ensure that protection mechanisms and principles are in place by applying protection mainstreaming assessment tool kits, safety audits & related IASC protection standards across UNFPA-supported facilities and services.This will be monitored through regular needs and vulnerability assessments and participatory discussions with the target populations. The results of these assessments will be used to further support the special needs of vulnerable groups, such as children and adolescents, persons with disabilities, members of female-headed and youth-headed households, among other groups. Finally, UNFPA will continue to promote the engagement of the affected populations through community engagement and other awareness-raising efforts on protection topics and referral mechanisms.

3.3 - Age & Disability

UNFPA both adheres to and supports the capacity development of partners on the IASC Guidelines on Working with and for Young People in Humanitarian and Protracted Crises to provide age-responsive and adolescent-friendly services. UNFPA co-chairs the Youth Working Group under the Education Sector of Cox's Bazar to mainstream the concerns of young people across sectors. UNFPA has a repertoire of resources, including toolkits and checklists, on disability inclusion that it implements in its planning, implementation and monitoring mechanisms. UNFPA also facilitates the participation of people with disabilities and young people in its regular feedback and consultation mechanisms to ensure that their voices are heard throughout the project life cycle. When possible, the project will hire community teachers who are refugees and prioritize the recruitment of staff with appropriate language, gender, age and cultural competencies, including staff with disabilities, to work with Rohingya populations. Data collected will also be disaggregated by sex, age and disability to better understand the service uptake among different groups. In Cox’s Bazar, UNFPA plans to further improve the accessibility of its supported facilities in 2024 through measures. As needed, accommodations will also be made to improve the accessibility of services to people with various disabilities based on the feedback received from them.

3.5 - Is your agency/organization and are your IPs members of the PSEA Network?

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

UNFPA has zero tolerance for sexual exploitation & abuse (SEA) and sexual harassment (SH). UNFPA has been a member of the ISCG PSEA Network in Cox's Bazar since 2017, and has appointed PSEA Focal Points for the organization. UNFPA provides training on PSEA, including obligations to report wrongdoing, for staff and non-staff personnel,IPs, vendors, and service providers. UNFPA's Office of Audit & Investigation Services (OAIS) ensures that staff can easily report misconduct through an online platform/hotline or through PSEA focal points. The organization also has a policy to prevent retaliation to protect survivors, victims, and whistleblowers. UNFPA conducts awareness-raising sessions among communities and visibly posts IEC materials in all its supported facilities to ensure that communities are aware of SEA & reporting mechanisms. As needed, UNFPA will conduct capacity assessments and support the implementation of capacity strengthening plans to ensure IPs are maintaining the required PSEA and safeguarding mechanism.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNFPA will ensure full adherence to DRR/DRM standards, including the Government of Bangladesh’s National Plan for Disaster Management (2021-2025) & Action for Disaster Risk Management Towards Resilient Nation. UNFPA Community Mobilizers are trained to support the response to disaster-related emergencies in the acute and post-disaster phases. Basic DRR information is also shared with the beneficiaries of UNFPA’s interventions such as its Life Skills Education program, while UNFPA-supported facilities, including the Adolescent & Youth Centers, are used as temporary shelter during emergencies. UNFPA also prepositions and stores customized Dignity Kits and emergency Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) Kits that are quickly distributed to its project beneficiaries during emergencies to reduce the vulnerability of affected women and girls. Finally, UNFPA has its in-house climate change specialist, and will continue to explore and adopt implementation modalities based on contemporary understanding and best practices in DRR and climate change adaptation.
