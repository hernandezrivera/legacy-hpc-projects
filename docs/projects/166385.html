Project Summary

During the COVID-19 crisis in Bangladesh, on top of the emergency WASH interventions SI is undertaking to raise awareness about COVID-19 and provide hygiene and sanitation facilities, it is important to provide Multi-purpose Cash support to the most vulnerable people whose livelihood have been undermined and who cannot access basic needs. SI will identify Financial Service Providers (FSPs) to collaborate with to identify feasible cash transfer modalities. When possible, a technology based cash transfer will be in place to avoid person to person contact in order to minimize the potential exposure of staff and beneficiaries to COVID-19. The MPCA will be transferred as unrestricted cash transfer to people affected by the crisis to enable them cover the cost of their basic needs with dignity and allow maximum degree of choice to prioritize their needs. The MEB was calculated considering the cost of food, household essentials required to prepare food, medicine, hygiene and personal care, energy or fuel and communication costs. The intervention will be done according to recomendations for Cash Working groups in terms of criteria and amount t be distributed (60% of MEB). The project aims to provide needed assistance to host communities at this difficult time while it ensures social cohesion between host and refugees indirectly though the project supports only host communities. SI will coordinate with other humanitarian actors who are implementing similar and related activities in the project area to avoid unecessary overlap. This coordination includes crosschecking of benficiary list (when possiblpe and necessary) with partners inplementing for the other startegic objectives. In addition, close coordination will be ensured with the FS Sector, Livelihoods Working Group, District and Upazilla level authroties, and financial service providers for better project delivery. Through the SI monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning framework, baseline and end line assessments will be conducted to measure existing situation and project impact to compare results. SI will ensure the full participation of affected population in the design and implementation of the project. the existing CRM system that has been in place in the project areas will be used to collect and respond to complaints that might arise from benficiairies and relevant stakeholds. SI will take necessary measures to address complaints throughout the project cycle and beneficiaries feedback will be used to inform decision making. Beneficiaries's participation will be ensured in the design as well as in the lesson learned workshops and their feedback will be used as a program input for further improvement. SI is committed to ensure accountability to affected popultaion and will strive for the fulfillment of international standards which will contribute to the maximum protection of project participants. SI considers accountability a core principle of humanitarian action and a core process for the responsible use of power and, accordingly, aims to place affected communities at the centre of its work, decisions and actions. Accordingly, SI maintains several policies and mechanisms to prevent and respond to exploitation, harassment and abuse of beneficiaries, including a Professional Whistleblowing Policy, Protection from Sexual Exploitation & Abuse Policy, and Code of Ethics and Conduct (COE).

Objectives

SO1: Expand support to improve food security and compensate for loss of livelihoods of the most vulnerable Bangladeshis in Cox’s Bazar whose livelihoods will be impacted by the pandemic, through food, cash support according to need. This objective is linked to ISCG Objective-2 and 3.

Needs

According to a study by FAO (2020) , the COVID-19 pandemic has a potential to disrupt economic activity where they studied the impact on the food and agricultural system. Unless measures are taken at the early stages of the crisis, both the supply and demand side of the market for agricultural commodities will be negatively affected where the most vulnerable people will suffer the most. Poor and vulnerable households who rely on labour for their livelihood loss the means of income and are not able to afford for their basic household needs. The MPCA will be provided for 3 months on the rate of 3,000 BDT/HH/month according to the recommendation of the Cash Working Group. While the project focus on supporting people who rely on daily labour and fishing for their livelihoods, specific criteria of targeting female headed households, families who have pregnant and lactating mothers, people with disability, elderly and households with chronically ill family members will be prioritized considering they are the most vulnerable and the COVID-19 crisis would add additional burden and vulnerability to these groups. The project will provide MPCA which is standardized rate by the CWG. Therefore, assistance will not be tailored on the basis on specific needs of the target group as the assistant is calculated based on the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB).
