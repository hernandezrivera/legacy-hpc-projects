Project Summary


The project aims to improve the protective environment of IDPs and the most vulnerable host community through provision of ESKs, NFIs and Permanent shelters where applicable. Through the project WRRS will also seek to achieve the cluster objective. WRRS will achieve this through distribution of emergency shelter kits, non-food items and construction of shelters. WRRS will also support the achievement through provision of sustainable shelters with improved security land tenure, NFIs distribution site planning and mapping of basic services. Facilitate access to durable solutions for IDPs who are willing to locally integrate and return to their places of origin. WRRS will achieve this by providing safe, habitable permanent shelters in places like Kismayo, site planning secure land tenure and disaster resilient mainstreaming. As a general approach, WRRS will promote linkage with camp coordination and camp management, and relief and development by promoting local building culture and building back safer. WRRS will ensure that assistance provided is appropriate and acceptable to the target people by engaging on community consultations before project implementation and beneficiary accountability through participation and feedback. Accountability to affected people will be supported through formation complaint. The project will target IDP women, Men, boys and girls who are exposed to risks and other vulnerabilities. Women and girls from the IDPs will be given first priority as they are considered at risk and have no protection mechanism. The project will be implemented in three regions that is Lower Juba, Kismayo, Afmadow, Dhobley, South West state, Baidoa district, Gedo region Luuq and Dolow and Sool Region.

Needs

IDPs continue to face the brunt of the humanitarian emergencies in Somalia. In 2017 and 2018 alone prolonged drought conditions led to the displacement of over 900,000 people (2018 HNO), totaling to over 2 million the number of IDPs in Somalia. According to UNHCR estimates, about 1.1 million Somalis or approximately a tenth of the population are affected by protracted displacement while additional newly displaced persons have added to the case load. Internally displaced persons (IDPs) continue to live in deplorable conditions in protracted unplanned displacement settlements with no access to basic services. About 70 to 80 per cent of the IDPs are women and children (UNOCHA) who are also exposed to protection concerns. The environment within which they live exposes them to various risks and hazards. IDPs continue to face risks to their lives due to lack of protection from weather hazards, physical safety, lack of access to basic services, insecurity and a life without dignity. IDPs are disproportionately at risk of gross abuses of human rights, especially women and unaccompanied children. Sexual and gender-based violence is widespread, even in areas of Somalia enjoying relative security. Many IDPs from minority clans suffer pervasive discrimination since they often lack vital clan protection and connections. The shelter needs for over 2 million protracted IDPs and newly displaced people remain alarming. Data collected through an infrastructure mapping exercise and surveys by partners in Afmadow, Baidoa, Dhobley, Kismayo and Luuq. underlines this alarming situation. For example recent assessment by WRRS indicate that only 7 per cent of all internally displaced persons in Kismayo and Afmadow live in planned settlements), 68 per cent did not have any formal land tenure agreement and 17 per cent openly reported to be paying rent. In addition, 85 per cent of shelters were categorized as buuls. The proposed project by WRRS will target 30,000 people.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Serious protection concerns persist in Somalia, putting IDPs and the most vulnerable host community’ lives at risk, forcing many to flee, exposing them to multiple risks while displaced, and impeding durable solutions. Compounding the conflict and its inherent risks to civilians, is a massive increase in drought related displacement and further exposed civilians to serious protection risks. Women, children and marginalized communities are especially at risk and face specific protection concerns. The protection crisis is also characterized by entrenched but fluid societal divisions, resulting in competition for resources across social groups. severe drought and the ongoing conflict continue to cause displacement. It is estimated that the number of IDPs have surpassing two million. Displacement dynamics have consistently been directed mainly towards urban centers and merging with urbanization dynamics of increasing pressure on services in larger towns and other urban cities. IDP settlements, many reside in overcrowded areas with poor living conditions and limited security provision. Where the basic needs of IDP are not met due to inconsistent service provision, exclusion from accessing services, or from the sheer absence of services due to poor targeting of settlements. Of specific concern is the large number of drought-affected IDP communities from conflict areas, who are often made up of separated families with men and youth remaining behind, increasing the exposure of women and girls to protection risks related to hazardous livelihood activities and reduced protection to GBV. WRRS through the proposed project will mitigate some of the mentioned risks through training on site planning, mapping of community services and ensure durable solutions.Distribution of ESKs,NFIs and shelter construction will ensure that IDPs have proper shelters and have access to services and are protected from further risks.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Discrimination against women is widespread and systemic, and they are subject to exclusion in many decision making processes and especially in Somalia, women are the most disadvantaged group in the society making it a risk factor to their protection. Women continue to face barriers to their participation, and are vastly under-represented in their communities and especially the minority clans. There are also gender differences in terms of inclusion in the labour market. Most women work in the informal economy, which is characterized by job insecurity, poor working conditions and low pay. The persistence and reproduction of women’s exclusion is also supported by social norms and religious and cultural values. In most Somalia communities, traditional barriers still prevent women from going out of their homes to work. For some women, having primary or sole responsibility for household duties, including childcare, also prevents them from working outside their homes or areas of residence. The target population for this project will be the most vulnerable IDP women, girls and boys from the minority clans who are at risk of exclusion and discrimination. The design for the project will ensure that all are involved in the process from the design to the implementation of the project. They will be involved in major decision making process to ensure that there is ownership and project sustainability is achieved.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

WRRS has a longstanding presence in Somalia, especially South Central Somalia with offices in Dhobley, Afmadow, Kismayo, Garowe While in other Regions, WRRS has offices in Baidoa in Bay region, Doolow and Luuq in Gedo region and Dhobley in Lower Juba since 2004 and has good understanding of the security context. WRRS has in place Standard Operating Procedure for security in Somalia, in order to guarantee a systematic approach to safety in humanitarian actions and specific rules of conduct in such a challenging context of operation. WRRS works with other agencies (i.e. UN and Government) on security matters by sharing information. Teams involved in operations are regularly informed on major and minor risks that may arise during the course of operation in field missions. A security checklist is provided on monthly basis by the Field Security Focal Points at WRRS's field offices across the regions that WRRS operates in. Before traveling to the field offices, especially in South Central where security level is at high risk (SL 6), WRRS staff submit travel authorization requests to the Security Coordinator for approval. Considering the specific security threats related to operations in Somalia, the organization has conducted in 2017 a Risks Assessment and defined a set of risk mitigations measures, including security risks. This protocol is reviewed and updated on regular basis.
