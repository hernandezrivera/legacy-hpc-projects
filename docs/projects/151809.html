Project Summary

The project entails increased access to basic nutrition among PLW and children between the age 6 - 59 months by screening and provision of life saving and preventive nutritional supplements to the children. Facility and community based (BNSP) will cover both curative and preventative interventions targeting children U5 years (boys and girls) and pregnant and lactating women.
While at it the project looks at identification, referral and treatment of cases of SAM at facility based as well as mobile OTP teams. It also entails referral of complicated cases of SAM to SC and MAM to TSFP. The project also undertake deworming and routine immunization services A full package of community and facility based nutrition, health and WASH services will be implemented.
An important resilience approach will be provision of key skills in health, WASH and hygiene to support prevention of infection and/or fatalities related to poor hygiene especially in PLW. The project also aims at capacitating women on breastfeeding.

Needs

Recurrent droughts in Gedo region have impacted food security negatively. For a community whose main source of livelihood is pastoralism with pockets of farming along the riverine areas, the erratic weather conditions have significantly affected the nutritional status of many families across the region and by extension the entire Somalia as a country. According to 2017 Post Gu Seasonal Food Security and Nutrition Assessment the Gu rains were below average to poor in almost all parts of Somalia including Gedo Region leading to high levels of malnutrition and food insecurity. In Gedo region with special focus in the North, including within Garbaharey, 12,400 children were found to be MAM while 2,200 children to be severely malnourished. North Gedo pastoral had a GAM rate of 17.5% while with a SAM rate of 3.2% while the riverine areas of North Gedo had a GAM rate of 15.7% and 1.8% SAM rate. Further down to IDP camps including within Dolow the figures are not any better. The GAM rate was reported at 17.6% with a SAM rate of 3.1%. Additionally, for the IDPS in Gedo region most population were found to be in crisis and emergency (IPC Phases 3 and 4). FSNAU FEWSNET technical release of January 2018 figures large scale humanitarian intervention had averted crisis across Somalia, Gedo region, just like many parts of Somalia, was still in classification as crisis and emergency (IPC Phase 3 and 4) with generalised regional figures pointing out that about 1,000 people still in emergency food crisis (IPC 4), 16,000 in crisis (IPC 3) and 174,000 stressed (IPC 2). Measured against the total regional population estimated at 508.403, the figures are still worrying. More worrying is that North Gedo region wherewith Garbaharey falls says that North Gedo pastoral Region have Critical prevalence of acute malnutrition (≥15% GAM) or 10.7 percent or more of children have Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) below the 125 millimetres (mm) threshold. Activities

1. Identification, referral and treatment of children suffering from severe acute malnutrition through community, facility based approaches and OTP Mobile teams. Complicated cases of SAM will be referred to SC and MAM to TSFP centres. Mother-led MUAC approach will be used to identifying and diagnosing malnutrition by mothers. Mothers will use MUAC tapes to measure the Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC). Mother led MUAC training will be conducted to support mother’s to screen, detect oedema and self-referral of their children to nutrition sites. Efforts will be made to ensure integration of OTP and TSFP sites where possible. To ensure the sustainability of IMAM interventions technical local capacity will be strengthened for integration of IMAM into the Primary Health Care system through the functional MCH facilities. Additionally, the capacity of nutrition staff will be strengthened for effective service delivery at facility and community level. In coordination with the Child Protection section, survivors of gender based violence (GBV) identified at the nutrition sites and catchment areas, will be referred to multi-sectoral services. 2. Prevention services will include deworming for children 12-59 months and pregnant women in the 2nd and 3rd trimester, counselling and promotion on infant and young child feeding. Community mobilization and screening as well as micronutrient supplementation for PLW (multiple micronutrients and Iron folate) and children 6-59 months with Vitamin A supplements. The program will promote home based fortification to enrich the diets of young children 6-23 months through the distribution of micronutrient Powders.3. Provision of routine immunization services to children and pregnant women in these project. 4. The community resilience approach will be overlaid with basic service provision of key health, WASH and nutrition basic services for prevention, promotion and referral and will be done by trained community workers.
