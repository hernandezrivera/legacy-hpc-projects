Project Summary

Contribute to improve the food security status and reduce the negative coping mechanisms of food insecure people in north-west Syria through the distribution of i) monthly food baskets for vulnerable displaced persons and host community members in need and ii) ready-to-eat-rations (RTERs) for newly displaced households as part of a rapid response mechanism.

4 - Needs

The food security situation in Syria has deteriorated compared to last year, with a total of 9.8 million people in need; of which 569,477 people are estimated to be severely food insecure; 7.3 million estimated to be moderately food insecure while 1.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity. The governorates estimated to have the highest levels of severity are Aleppo, Idleb, Hasakeh, Deir-ez-zor, and Dara. At least 37 percent of the food insecure population are in North West Syria. Of the total PiN, the ratio of total male to female is 51 to 49, with a substantial number of children (at least 42%) while adults and elders represent 54 and 4 percent of the PiN respectively. In particular children under 5 and pregnant and/ or lactating women face the highest risk in terms of a deterioration of physical and mental well-being as a result of inadequate food and poor dietary diversity (HNO 2020). According to the Food Security (FS) Cluster, food insecurity in Syria is defined by continued conflict causing mass and prolonged displacement, loss and lack of livelihood opportunities contributing to high poverty levels and reduced production capacity hampering financial and physical access to food, high inflation and soaring currency depreciation. Taken together, this has reduced the purchasing power of the most vulnerable populations and continues to deplete the livelihoods options. To respond to the increasing food costs, Syrians sometimes adopt harmful coping strategies such as reduced food consumption, spending of savings, child labor and early marriages. 65% of food insecure households are adopting crisis or emergency harmful coping strategies. According to HNO 2020, across Syria, 85% of surveyed communities have reported a high occurrence of child labor preventing school attendance.

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM activities will be as follows: • Initial assessment of FCS and CSI scores of targeted locations• Selection of the implementing partner and vendor for food baskets and RTERs• Selection of beneficiaries based on vulnerability evaluation criteria set by FS Cluster. Registration of beneficiaries and distribution of aid identification cards.• Verification of beneficiaries by M&E• Production of food batches • Laboratory testing of food samples at source to ensure safety and quality • Storage of food baskets in vendor warehouse if required• Transportation of food baskets into Syria• Storage of food baskets in IP warehouse selected in coordination with IOM• Conduct sensitization sessions with site managers, IDP committees and IDPs • Distribute food through door-to-door (where feasible) distributions• Conduct food basket distribution monitoring and exit interviews by IOM TPM Ready-to-eat Rations (RTERs): • Production of RTERs• Laboratory testing of RTERs samples at source to ensure safety and quality • Storage of RTERs in vendor warehouse if required• Transportation of RTERs into Syria• Storage of RTERs in IP warehouse for emergency distribution in coordination with IOM• Approving RTERs distribution request upon receiving displacement emergency info from CCCM/FS Clusters.• Registering beneficiaries by IP • Distributing of RTERs • Conduct RTERs distribution monitoring and exit interviews by IOM TPM • Conduct satisfaction survey for the regular food distribution at the end of the project to measure the change in FCS and RCSI

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks:1. IOM response activities unintentionally lead to intra-community tensions arising from the beneficiary selection or limited resources and response capacities.2. The principal of “Do no harm” is not guaranteed due to unclear objective based on the community needs, validated through a transparent community consultation and participation process.3. Sexual abuse and exploitation by implementing partners or by other actors in the community.4. Risk of violence in communities (protests) or general security incidents5. Pressure on the implementing partners from the local authorities on who should or should not receive the assistance. Mitigation measures:1. Perform conflict analysis before planning the response, ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the community or communities concerned and keep them informed of the project implementation2. Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from a representative community board3. Establish a feedback mechanism, raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report, and train staff on community-based complaint mechanisms4. Ensure registrations/distribution processes are well organized and explained clearly to community leaders5. Raise awareness with implementing partners about the importance of principled humanitarian assistance, and reporting any incidents of interference

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:1. Security situation in Aleppo and Idleb Governorates allows to implement IOM response activities.2. Activities to be implemented in coordination with the local authorities3. Proper beneficiary selection procedures are followed by the implementing partner4. Commodities are stored in appropriate warehouses inside Syria. Risks:1. Security situation in Aleppo and/or in Idleb Governorate do not allow for viable IOM response activities to be undertaken2. Local councils attempt to influence IOM response activities. 3. Influence of local council on beneficiary selection. 4. Commodities are stored in inadequate conditions and exposed to weather conditions Mitigation measures:1. Establish a security and accessibility plan for the targeted areas, and contingency planning to identify alternative response modalities. Develop a ‘duty of care’ strategy for local partners.2. Implementing Partners (IPs) ensure full coordination with local councils from initial stage until the delivery of assistance. Community Engagement Teams (CETs) and have a participatory role throughout the process.3. Wider and transparent call for beneficiary registration by the IPs. Conduct 100% beneficiary verification by IOM Third-Party Monitors (TPM). Beneficiary selection based on beneficiary scoring system.4. Complete the distribution of commodities within 10 days from off-loading at the warehouse. Conduct warehouse verification before off-loading and during distribution of commodities.
