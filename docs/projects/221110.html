Project Summary

The North West and South West regions make up 30% of the total population of Cameroon. The population of the North West region is 1,969,000, while that of the South West region is 1,553,000, giving a total population of over 3,522,000 (2015 population statistics - Cameroon). This population is on the brink of falling into worse living conditions as standards keep dropping daily due to the ongoing crisis. The Integrated Rapid Response Mechanism (IRRM) is crucial at this point to save the population in these regions from worsening situations. The people face difficulties accessing WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene), health, food security, education, and protection.
Every day in remote rural villages in the South West and North West regions, hundreds of mothers and babies die at childbirth. Many mothers in these villages give birth at home, some even in the bushes, which poses a significant risk to them and their babies. A big part of the problem is the lack of access to sterile supplies in rural communities where many women live and work. The broken healthcare chain, due to the ongoing crisis that has destroyed many villages and forced many to flee into the bushes, exacerbates these issues. Traditional birth attendants sever umbilical cords with rusty blades, infecting babies with tetanus, and midwives use their mouths to suck out mucus from the nostrils of babies.
Having identified these challenges, CADEF is seeking support to procure mama kits from Teheca (customized prepared mama bags) to be provided free to all mothers to promote safe delivery. After years of witnessing midwives suck mucus from the nostrils of newborns to prevent birth asphyxia, women giving birth on bare floors, and birth attendants using rusty blades to sever umbilical cords, this project will equip birth attendants in their communities and IDP camps with skills and exposure through training. This will address the challenge of accessing sterile supplies at childbirth in rural communities. These kits mean more mothers will have a healthy and safe delivery and the supplies they need to care for their precious newborns.
Project Goals
The project aims to train 650 traditional birth attendants (TBAs) and 3,000 community health workers (CHWs), and distribute 5,000 mama kits to expectant mothers to improve maternal health among rural young girls and women. The project targets pregnant women and girls living in IDP camps, bushes, hard-to-reach areas, and secondary schools. It provides a sterilized, ultra-affordable delivery kit containing essential supplies a woman requires at childbirth to ensure she has a clean, safe, and hygienic delivery. Our goal is to ensure that every baby gets the opportunity to live their potential and every mother gets the opportunity to watch her baby grow.
The mama kit, also known as a clean delivery kit, aims to ensure mothers undergo safe and protective delivery. The kit contains:
500 grams of cotton wool
Baby soap
Two razor blades
Two polythene sheets
Two gauze pieces
Three surgical gloves
An immunization card
Teheca-designed mama kits come in a bag that includes a manual for the whole family to read, explaining precautions in case the baby arrives before reaching the hospital. The mama bag will play a significant role in improving maternal reproductive health in rural communities by including men in the process.
Main Aim of the Project
To contribute to the reduction of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality through clean and safe delivery.
Objectives of the Project
To provide information to women, men, and communities on the importance of delivering in a clean environment.
To provide mama kits to all women who deliver in camps, remote areas, health units, and communities.
To train traditional birth attendants (TBAs) and community health workers (CHWs) on proper hygiene and clean environments at delivery, and to improve the Extended Program for Immunization (EPI) activities in camps and hard-to-reach areas

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Assessed Needs: The North West and South West regions, making up 30% of the total population of Cameroon, are facing severe crises that have drastically reduced living standards. The combined population of over 3,522,000 is struggling with access to essential services such as Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH), healthcare, food security, education, and protection. The ongoing crisis has exacerbated these issues, leaving many villages destroyed and forcing numerous individuals to flee into the bushes. Consequently, there is a significant lack of access to sterile supplies and proper healthcare, leading to high maternal and infant mortality rates. Traditional birth attendants often use unsafe practices, increasing the risk of infections and complications during childbirth. Project Activities and Implementation: Training Programs for Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs) and Community Health Workers (CHWs): Objective: Equip 650 TBAs and 3,000 CHWs with essential skills and knowledge. Implementation: Conduct training sessions focusing on hygiene, safe delivery practices, and the Extended Program for Immunization (EPI). This will be done through workshops, seminars, and practical demonstrations in rural communities and IDP camps. Distribution of Mama Kits: Objective: Provide 5,000 mama kits to expectant mothers to ensure clean, safe, and hygienic deliveries. Implementation: Distribute kits containing essential supplies such as cotton wool, baby soap, razor blades, polythene sheets, gauze pieces, surgical gloves, and an immunization card to pregnant women in IDP camps, remote areas, and secondary schools. The kits will come with a manual for safe delivery practices. Awareness Campaigns: Objective: Educate the community about the importance of delivering in a clean environment and the benefits of using mama kits. Implementation: Conduct community meetings, workshops, and use local media to disseminate information. Engage men and community leaders to ensure broad support and understanding. Healthcare Access Enhancement: Objective: Improve access to healthcare for women, including those affected by the ongoing crisis. Implementation: Partner with local health units and mobile clinics to provide medical services such as cancer screenings and care for women victims of war. Link to Assessed Needs: The project directly addresses the critical needs of the target population by improving maternal and child health through the provision of essential supplies and training. The training programs will enhance the skills and knowledge of TBAs and CHWs, ensuring safer delivery practices. The distribution of mama kits will provide the necessary supplies for clean and hygienic deliveries, reducing the risk of infections and complications. Awareness campaigns will educate the community on the importance of proper delivery practices, and healthcare access enhancement will ensure that women receive the necessary medical support. Expected Outcomes: Reduced maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality rates in the North West and South West regions. Improved skills and knowledge among TBAs and CHWs, leading to better healthcare practices. Increased community awareness and support for safe delivery practices. Enhanced access to healthcare for women in crisis-affected areas. This project aims to ensure every baby has the opportunity to live to their potential, and every mother has the chance to watch her baby grow in a safe and healthy environment.
