Project Summary

For more than three years, the province of Cabo Delgado in northern Mozambique has experienced attacks by Non-state Armed Groups (NSAGs), causing over 700,000 people to be internally displaced. Attacks have increased in severity and frequency since they began in October 2017, with continued increases in violence and subsequent displacement in 2020 as well as a significant rise in internal displacement following launched attacks in Palma Sede from 24 March 2021.
The DTM is an information system and set of tools developed to gather and analyses data to disseminate critical multi-layered information on the mobility, vulnerabilities, and needs of displaced and mobile populations that enables decision makers and responders to provide better context-specific and tailored assistance. In Mozambique, the DTM can add value in attending to the needs of displaced populations through the continuation and reinforcement of its comprehensive information management platform designed to provide accurate and up-to-date information to Government and humanitarian actors and, in fine, improve the overall humanitarian response. Monitoring tools are methodologies are designed to ensure representations of affected populations include the specific needs/vulnerabilities of persons with disabilities, elderly, separated/unaccompanied children, pregnant women as well as persons with chronic diseases.
In addition to being systematically deployed in medium to large-scale humanitarian response operations, DTM has also proven to be highly effective as a preparedness tool (the mapping of potential evacuation and displacement sites and the establishment of data collection mechanisms before a disaster strikes).
Building on IOM’s established DTM presence in the northern region, IOM proposes to continue its operations by strengthening coverage capacity and teams on the ground. In this phase, responding to the complex mobility trends and intentions of disaster and conflict affected communities, IOM will continue its current operations and further improve its methodology through:
List Activities:
1. Bi-monthly Baseline assessments
2. Weekly Emergency tracking Overviews/Ad Hoc Movement Alerts
3. Bi-monthly Multi sectoral site assessment/Population count
4. Ad hoc Return assessment
5. Ad hoc Thematic Surveys
IOM further proposes to continue capacitating relevant focal points from the provincial level (governor’s office technicians, INGC, Provincial Directorate of Gender, Children and Social Action) on information management, data collection and other DTM components. Through coordination with relevant focal points operating in Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa- data protection, general protection, disability, and inclusion as well as PSEA standards in data collection efforts will be incorporated in refresher training modules. All capacity building efforts will be complemented by operational and technical support for information management exercises, both recurring or ad hoc, conducted by Government counterpart’s representative of men and women.
Overall People Targeted (1,538,000 individuals, indirectly) with 52% Non IDPs, 48% IDPs and 33% Returnees (migrants, refugees)
Vulnerable Groups Targeted: Children under 5 years (21% of total targeted population), School Age Children 5-17 years (3%), Elderly 60+years (5%) and Persons with special needs/vulnerabilities (15%)

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Building on IOM’s established DTM presence in the northern region, IOM proposes to continue its operations by strengthening coverage capacity and teams on the ground. In response to the complex mobility trends and intentions of disaster and conflict affected communities, IOM will continue its current operations and further improve its methodology through: 1. Bi-monthly Baseline assessments: Continued monitoring of affected households in all accessible localities to assess changes in displacement dynamics (including inflows, outflows from sites and host community locations and return movements), sex and age disaggregated data, routes, push and pull factors, population needs and vulnerabilities, and sectoral information and gaps at the bairro/quarteirão/Povoado level.2. Weekly Emergency tracking Overviews/Ad Hoc Movement Alerts: Continued emergency tracking in key areas receiving displaced populations (triggered by both natural disasters and conflict).3. Bi-monthly Multi sectoral site assessment/Population count: Conducting Multi-Sectoral Location Assessment (MSLA) at relocation/resettlement sites and/in displacement locations providing in-depth information on mobility, sectoral needs, gaps and vulnerabilities, push and pull factors, population needs, sex and age disaggregated data and vulnerabilities. DTM continued collaboration with CCCM cluster to conduct population count exercises in sites where the cluster is providing coordination and service monitoring.4. Ad hoc Return assessment: Conducting return assessment in areas of origin that displaced population are returning to; to understand the gap, vulnerabilities, livelihood, living conditions of the returnees.5. Ad hoc Thematic Surveys: This includes, movement intentions, facility mapping and community perceptions surveys at household level.6. Training modules and sessions inclusive of data protection, general protection, disability, and inclusion as well as PSEA standards in data collection efforts. Overall People Targeted (indirect): 1,538,00 TOTAL Male (49%), TOTAL Female (51%) Boys (49%), Girls (51%) Persons with special needs/vulnerabilities (15%)

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Monitoring of affected populations will be conducted in close coordination with Protection Partners as well as representative managing Complaint Feedback Mechanisms. Where household-level assessments are conducted, protection partners will be on-ground to facilitate referrals for men, women, boys, girls, the elderly as well as persons with special needs.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

Data collection activities are inclusive of key messages detailing humanitarian minimum standards (neutrality, impartiality, independence), Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), community feedback mechanisms (including Green Line 1458, Child Speak 116) as well as general protection considerations. Training modules for enumerators and key informants are regularly revised in consultation with focal points in Protection (including disability inclusivity) as well as PSEA. Modules are inclusive of age, sex, vulnerability disaggregation as well as available Protection Referral pathways as well as PSEA referral pathways, ensuring that enumerators and key informants have the ability to know how, when and why they will intervene when a protection concern is raised. Diversity practices are additionally prioritized to ensure age and sex inclusivity amongst enumerators and key informants. All data collection tools mainstream indicators that monitor the use/existing gaps of available community feedback mechanisms for further planning of improved support services amongst affected communities

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

IOM will also ensure that gender, GBV risk mitigation, disability inclusion, age and diversity analysis is conducted so that multi-sectoral needs, vulnerabilities, capacities and coping mechanisms of all affected populations are incorporated in evidence-based humanitarian response and planning

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

No impact on environment envisaged. However, DTM has also proven to be highly effective as a preparedness tool (the mapping of potential evacuation and displacement sites and the establishment of data collection mechanisms before a disaster strikes).
