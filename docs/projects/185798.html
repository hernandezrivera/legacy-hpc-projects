Project Summary

The project aims at ensuring that SGBV survivors have access to comprehensive and well-coordinated SGBV services, including medical, psychosocial and livelihood support to survivors in six LGAs of Yobe State - Damaturu, Geidam, Bade, Gujba and Yunusari – and Kaga LGA in Borno State. The project targeting 19.000 beneficiaries, includes an explicit focus on strengthening capacity within civil society - including but not limited to CBOs, community-based committees, schools, youth and women groups, religious groups/associations – and governmental counterparts to mitigate risk factors and coordinate a survivor-centered response which includes early identification, timely and well-coordinated referral to specialized services. The project seeks to provide survivors and people at risk - with particular attention to women, girls and boys –access to income generating activities to help establish support networks and livelihood opportunities.

1 - Needs

Limited availability of specialized services for SGBV survivors such as psychosocial, medical, safety, security and legal services remains a challenge in Yobe State despite the efforts developed through the Protection Sector Working Group (PSWG) and the SGBV sub-sector to build adequate prevention and response programs. According to assessments conducted by UNHCR and other protection actors, internally displaced women and children are disproportionately vulnerable to SGBV. Unaccompanied girls, adolescents, single heads of households, child mothers, child spouses, and women and girls living with disability are the most at risk. The existing culture of impunity coupled with a weak justice system hampers prevention and response to violations against women and children. Most survivors do not report SGBV incidents for fear of reprisals by perpetrators and stigmatization by their own communities, as well as having no confidence that the authorities will hold perpetrators accountable. North-East Nigeria still faces immense humanitarian and protection challenges. An estimated 1.7 million people (1.6 million children and 100,000 caregivers) need protection services; this includes an estimated 21,000 children and caregivers living with disabilities.. Vulnerable girls, women and boys are lacking the fundamental protection of a caregiver, whether parents, extended family members or care professionals and therefore they are exposed to a heightened risk of sexual violence, physical abuse and exploitation. They have also been victims of serious violations, abduction, sexual and gender-based violence, separation or loss of family members, destruction of property, loosing of food and livestock, and forced displacement. These events result in psychosocial distress. The analysis of data showed that women and children under 18 continued to bear the brunt of this crisis. Forced marriage, physical assault, rape, survival sex, sexual assault and denial of resources were the major incidents (UNHCR 2019 Annual Report on Sexual and Gender-Based Violence Northeast Nigeria).Based on the identified gaps, the project seeks to increase access to comprehensive and well-coordinated SGBV services to survivors in six LGAs of Yobe State - Damaturu, Geidam, Bade, Gujba and Yunusari – and Kaga in Borno State where COOPI holds a strong relationship with key community actors and the State Ministry of Women Affairs (SMWA). The project’s community-strengthening initiatives will directly engage community leaders and stakeholders to create a conducive environment for reintegration.

2 - Activities

COOPI has been addressing the needs of unaccompanied and separated children in Yobe since 2015 witnessing substantial gaps in SGBV prevention and response services and coordination mechanisms for effective service delivery.1. Conduct an SGBV assessment through focus groups and key informant interviews with women, men, girls, boys and relevant community actors and groups; 2. Establish and maintain a confidential individual file management system to register and track SGBV survivors, and record the assistance they require and receive; 3. Training of SGBV focal persons, community leaders, community groups on how to identify SGBV survivors and referral pathways; 4. Provision of immediate support to survivors (information, counselling, psychosocial support and referral to emergency care especially for cases reporting within 72 hours and other specialized medical services); 5. Provision of medium-term support to survivors and people at risk (PSS, assistance to access legal services, skill training and income-generating activities); 6. Mapping of existing SGBV prevention and response programs and services in each target location and existing functional SGBV coordination mechanisms/structures; 7. Monitoring the effectiveness of the referral mechanism and the care provided by the designated services to SGBV survivors reported in the target locations to ensure needs are met and accountability is strengthened; 8. Training sessions and field coaching for Social Workers of the SMWA; 9. Training of CBOs on SGBV response programs and livelihood support; 10. Training of teachers, as well as headmasters and school administrators, on SGBV prevention strategies in schools and the importance of engaging boys in prevention activities; 11. Sensitization sessions with Imams/religious leaders and existing Child Protection Committees (CBCPC) around SGBV; 12. Sensitization activities at the community level, including focus group discussions with men, women, youth, boys and girls, teachers and educators within the existing Child-Friendly spaces (CFS); 13. Distribution of NFI Kits.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The action will adopt a participatory approach where affected communities & community leadership will be the centre of the action. Special attention will be given to the involvement of vulnerable groups and people that may be discriminated and stigmatized based on gender, ethnicity, disability, experienced violence, and abuse, ensuring they are not left behind. The staff involved will be trained and sensitized on the COOPI Code of Ethics and COOPI Nigeria Child Protection Code of Conduct. All trainees will be asked to sign COOPI’s Code of Conduct documents to ensure accountability and reporting after the completion of the training sessions. Beneficiaries and community leaders will be sensitized on PSEA and the COOPI internal policies and Code of Conduct in addition to reporting channels such as the internal COOPI whistle blower policy via email or helpline phone number. COOPI Protection Officers, PSEA Focal Person and PM coordinate each other, so that at the programming level, protection is mainstreamed. The COOPI staff on site will be recognizable by wearing visibility and reflective jackets. COOPI will mainstream the protection strategy in all components of the design and the implementation of the project. COOPI conducted a protection, gender, and AAP assessment in July 2021 to assess the current protection trends and protection concerns of the vulnerable population in the assessed locations where assistance is provided by COOPI. The key finding will be implemented to inform current and future interventions with regards to identification of measures to mitigate protection risks and negative impacts. Whilst gender will be considered during the selection of the beneficiaries. The PMCs formed by COOPI will have a strong woman representation (at least 50%) and will be involved in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the project. Gender and protection will be considered in the entire project implementation by ensuring that social behavioral change communication is mainstreamed in the proposed project. The strong presence of women in the project and their continuous protection will indulge COOPI to make sure the project respects the safety, the dignity and the integrity of the women, men, girls, and boys receiving the assistance. COOPI monitoring team will ensure that the rights of all beneficiaries are fully respected and prevent gender oppressive stereotypes. The CRM teams will also make sure that beneficiaries are aware of how to report any gender related issues (through the complaint desk, the complaint boxes). COOPI commits to the International Human Rights Law which stipules that every person has the right to work, to freely choose their employment and to be protected from unemployment. In fact, People with Disabilities are at higher risk of unemployment rate due to social bias, lack of access to the workplace and poor access to information and finance (IASC Guidelines on Inclusion of Persons with Disabilities in Humanitarian Action). To integrate People with Disabilities in the proposed project, COOPI will have a global approach integrating families and local NGO partners in the identification of barriers and to ensure representation in the activities. The water points and markets that will be built as part of the project will be accessible to people with disabilities. COOPI will prioritize PSN (people with special - disabilities) during the selection and identification of beneficiaries’ processes. COOPI will ensure that the stakeholders such as PMC’s and traders/ vendors that are involved in the project, ensure People with Disabilities accessibility.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

COOPI will build on previous experience of monitoring and evaluation of protection, emergency and early recovery programs to ensure that the proposed project's strategy is appropriate to respond to context-specific needs as well as to demonstrate changes in outcomes to which the programme contributes. The monitoring system will include regular site visits, supervisory visits by the management team and/or technical staff, and community feedback and accountability mechanisms. It will be used to inform periodic management meetings in which the team will review data, identify trends, and propose changes to interventions when necessary. COOPI will establish a community feedback mechanism and Project Management Committees in alignment with guidance provided by the Protection Sector, GBV and Child Protection Sub-Sectors, UNFPA, UNICEF and other donors. COOPI will ensure regular and representative consultation of affected populations before, during and after the implementation of activities to capture their preferences, their respective priority needs and document barriers they face in accessing to safe and meaningful assistance and information. Dedicate protection and AAP officers will be recruited that speak the local languages and are gender balanced to facilitate communicating with communities. Dedicated Community Feedback assistants will be recruited for the help and feedback desks. They will be equipped with computers/tablets to reduce the risk of data mismanagement/breaches in data protection.
