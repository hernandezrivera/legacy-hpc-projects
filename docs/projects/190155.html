1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

SC will implement a comprehensive set of primary health care services, including sexual and reproductive health (SRH). Training or revitalizing 15 Community Health Committees; strengthening three Health Referral Units with surgical supplies, capacity building (including training and mentorship/supervision) of health workers (PHCs/CHWs) and Health Committees and conducting health promotion activities. In WASH in resettlement centers we will do distribution of Hygiene and dignity KITS, construction of 4 water boreholes and rehabilitation of 15, creation and training of water management committees, construction of latrines, promotion of hygiene and improvements in sanitation infrastructure in KATAPUA and NANLIA (biological pits, incinerators, installation of water tanks and painting. In relation to Child Protection, it will train and mentor case management workers on the Prevention of Sexual Abuse and Exploitation including GBV, train staff (government social workers and partners) on the Operational Standards and Minimum Child Protection Standards. It will also strengthen Family and Community tracing and reunification services and alternative care for Separated and Unaccompanied Children.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

CS recognizes the challenges and impacts of humanitarian emergencies, especially on the most vulnerable populations, and that negative response mechanisms are often used which impact on health and well-being. Risks to girls and women are particularly high in conflict, and the most marginalized and deprived people, including children, women and persons with disabilities, may be excluded from responses due to pre- existing discriminatory practices. SC will ensure that these groups are not neglected through the use of inclusive and child-friendly mechanisms and feedback messages. To ensure that vulnerable groups continue to have safe access to health services, project leaders will continuously monitor and make changes in response to feedback. In addition, SC will train health facility staff on protection/PC guidelines and key information to consider during patient admission and patient profiling, e.g. consultation on child welfare, and on referral of vulnerable children for services and follow-up.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

Mostly through the involvement and active participation of communities, having as a fundamental element the revitalization of 15 CHCs that are responsible for the implementation of the community action cycle and establishment of priorities, a participatory approach that empowers communities in the solution of their problems. additionaly, through the use of community scorecards, which make communities participatory in assessing the quality of services and empower them to identify joint solutions with health authorities.the project brings mobile health brigades to hard-to-reach communities at no (or very low) cost which provides access to healthcare to populations which otherwise do not have regular access. Communities are empowered through capacity building on nutrition, for example through breastfeeding couseling and culinary demonstrations, and integrated activities such as hygiene promotion. this increased knowledge contributes to community members empowering one another for better health and nutrition outcomes

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Disability Inclusion Explain how the The CS response will prioritize the safety of these groups, including their health and psychosocial well-being. Selection criteria will be based on the groups most at riskand informed by community consultations, and the different health needs of persons with disabilities, the elderly, persons with disabilities and children will be taken into consideration in the Mobile Health Brigades and Referral Health Units. SC will work to involve these vulnerable groups in decision-making processes and engage them throughout project implementation. Health workers will receive training on medical ethics to provide essential primary health services, and on the different needs of vulnerable groups, including identifying vulnerabilities and counseling or treatment for specific groups. Targeting the most vulnerable requires CHWs to be aware of and design community mobilization activities in order to avoid exclusion, and is responsible for inclusive services.In addition to understanding and responding to these most vulnerable groups, the SC will disaggregate data by age and disability as feasible and adjust programming as needed. During minor rehabilitation of the health facility, the SC will ensure that disability-friendly designs are incorporated to facilitate access and use of the health facility or mobile health service area.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

To minimize the adverse impacts of health and commodity- related medical waste on the environment, SC will adhere to the Mozambique Infection Prevention Protocol. Medical waste disposal infrastructure will be established at the Mobile Health Brigades and Referral Health Units and disposal procedures will be strengthened. Waste from the Mobile Health Brigades will be collected and taken to the Referral Health Units and will be disposed of directly at the Referral Health Units following the waste management guidelines of the Ministry of Health. SC has extensive experience leading capacity building sessions for MEWs/CHWs on medical waste management policies and will promote best practices in waste disposal
