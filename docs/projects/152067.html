Project Summary

Since the beginning of 2015, the North-East of Nigeria has witnessed an increase in violence perpetrated by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG), causing a major humanitarian crisis. The intensification of attacks has resulted in prolonged insecurity, exacerbating the plight of vulnerable civilians and triggering waves of forced displacement as well as violation of the human rights resulting into forms of Gender Based Violence (GBV) including Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). More than 1.9 million individuals have been displaced due to the conflict and displacement continues to be a significant factor in 2018, with the highest number of displacements and returns taking place in Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe State ((IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) Round 23). . Despite the fact that some displaced individuals have started to return home after relative security has been restored and more areas are now accessible to humanitarian actors, the situation on the ground remains dire and several remain in inaccessible areas.

Needs

Insecurity, triggered by the insurgency and military operations against NSAGs, has further resulted in a large displacement crisis in north-east Nigeria. 2018 has seen an estimated 130,000 new arrivals in various locations (IOM Emergency Tracking Tool (ETT) data, as of September 2018), bringing the total number of displaced persons to 1.9million. Population movements present major humanitarian challenges to the already stretched resources. While camp management structures have been established under the lead of NEMA/SEMA in the main sites hosting IDPs, gaps in terms of camp management staff presence and multi sectorial response remain. Since January 2018, there have been about 80,000 new arrivals to camp and/or camp-like settings in the northeast. Moreover, the Displacement Management (CCCM) and Shelter/NFI Sector has recorded that over 40 camps out of the 148 camps in Borno state are in a situation of over-congestion, i.e. 1000 individuals above camp capacity – the most congested camp has 36,000 individuals above camp capacity (Muna Garage). Due to the longstanding trend of new arrivals from hard-to-reach areas, and an anticipated continuation of this trend, as well as the already large IDP population numbers, services in IDP sites will continue to be necessary. Site planning and coordinated inter sectoral improvements are critical to ensure a dignified environment for displaced populations, and reduce protection and health risks motivated by unsuitable living conditions and overcrowding. LGAs hosting a large number of IDPs need improved coordination and management of assistance provided to IDPs. Widespread displacement, new waves of displacement or secondary displacement and steadily increasing number of returnees have also highlighted the need to reinforce the data assessment mechanism through DTM, and to expand the flexibility of interventions conducted in camps, host communities, in areas of return and in areas expected to have humanitarian access within the next few months. Since 2014, when detailed widespread data collection begun, over 60% of IDPs have been displaced more than once confirming the continued, protracted and precarious plight of the displaced. Thus, continuous monitoring and analyses of the displacement situation is essential. At the same time, since August of 2015, more than 1.5 million persons have returned to their place of habitual residence, requiring the need for detailed assessments on the conditions of the locations of return, identification of livelihood opportunities as well as ways of both rebuilding and improving infrastructure across these locations.Moreover, as additional work continues to improve conditions of affected population in the crisis across the board, detailed information on needs of the most vulnerable and deprived is needed in real time. Given that the security situation continues to be extremely fluid there is need for round-the-clock monitoring of new displacements or arrivals and capturing of their needs for adequate humanitarian action. Timely and accurate information is needed not only for planning but also for avoiding duplication of efforts by different humanitarian actions, making better use of limited resources. As the North-East of Nigeria faces a myriad of other challenges including communal clashes, escalating crime and natural disasters, IOM’s DTM Unit (with its ETT and Flash Reports) is able to bring to light movements caused by a range of factors. Through its comprehensive and periodic assessments (in over 2,400 locations), DTM is able to provide better understanding of the numbers and needs of the affected population as well as an analysis of sector-wide needs, including shelter and non-food items, water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH), food and nutrition, health, education, livelihood, security, communication and protection. The growing numbers and needs of returnees are similarly captured, analyzed and widely shared with humanitarian partners.

Activities

1. Site facilitation capacities are enhanced in IDP sites in formal and informal camp or camp-like settings to facilitate provision of adequate services to existing populations and new arrivals • Deploying dedicated site facilitation teams and mobile/roving camp management teams(including male and female staff), and strengthening existing teams to continue carrying out monitoring, sensitization and awareness raising • Establishment of addressing systems on sites for both formal and informal IDP camps • Establishment and maintenance of reception centers (including items stockpiling, referral, as well as construction and maintenance of transit sites)• Establishment of site management offices• Establishment, maintenance and management of reception centres for new arrivals, while ensuring availability of safe areas for women and children, lighting, privacy and segregated WASH facilities for men and women to mitigate GBV risks 2. Displacement management, capacity-building and on the job-training for IDP representatives, camp committees, site managers, and government partners.• Trainings for site managers, IDP communities, national authorities and partners 3. Provision of site planning assistance in setting up new camps or expanding existing sites, to ensure space for all the basic services, and that minimum standards are met.• Site selection and assessments • Flood-risk assessments using GIS • Site planning, including density mapping, gender analyses, etc.• Land preparation activities 4. Site upgrade, care and maintenance support in IDP sites in close collaboration with other camp management partners and the Government.• Coordinated drainage improvements. • Site cleaning and site infrastructure repair and maintenance.• Establishment and/or maintenance of Site Maintenance Committees and provision of adequate skills, tools and materials. • Cash based interventions to IDPs providing labor in site maintenance. 5. Improved two-way communication with affected population to ensure that IDPs have access to critical information regarding the displacement situation and available services and they are able to provide complaints and feedback on needs and services.• Development, translation and dissemination of communication materials for affected communities • Capacity-building of field staff in interpretation and translation• Set up and maintenance of multilingual feedback mechanism through hotline and audio recorders and dissemination of responses to community feedback 6. Improved information management mechanisms for displaced populations in northeast Nigeria to facilitate planning, gap analysis and rapid response.• Displacement Tracking Matrix: Track and monitor displacement, including gender and age disaggregation, service delivery, and key needs and gaps across the affected states of Nigeria, in new humanitarian operational areas and host communities.• Biometric registration of IDPs and affected population in the new humanitarian operational areas through registering of new caseloads as well as update of previously registered caseloads to include new births and deaths.• Reinforcement of monitoring and first alert mechanisms for rapid referral in close coordination with sector partners and Government counterparts through rapid paper registrations in order share data within 24 hours of a displacement (not including personal data).• Use IOM distribution application to track assistance provided to beneficiaries over time to prevent duplications of efforts by organizations and better enhance assistance provision.• Continued surveys on returnees (intentions and basic requirements as part of the DTM) and case by case analysis to identify the return conditions in areas of origin. 7. Improved support for field sector/operation coordination (Yobe, Adamawa and Borno State)• Sector working group meetings conducted. • Coordination Mechanisms set up for deep field locations and hard to reach areas.
