Project Summary

GC implements integrated specialized and nonspecialized protection services to prevent, mitigate, and respond to harm exploitation, and abuse for the most vulnerable boys, girls, men, and women in northern Syria in Idleb, Hassakeh, Raqqa, and Deir Ez-Zor governorates, by providing direct implementation through comprehensive protection services, covering CP, GBV, and GP/PSS through static facilities and mobile teams. GC/CHF supports vulnerable IDPs and host community members through services in and out of camps and informal settlements where there is no access to support and children and women score low on protection indicators such as sense of safety and well-being and resilience. Under this program, GC will manage 13 static safe spaces and 13 mobile teams and will also conduct online activities as needed to respond to and mitigate GBV, CP, and GP/PSS issues within the target communities. Safe spaces, in the form of Community Centers(CCs), Child-Friendly Spaces (CFS) and Women/Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) will provide vital protection response services including case management, PM+, SH+, IPA, Cash for Protection, PFA and structured PSS, as well as prevention and empowerment activities including recreational activities, one-off and structured awareness sessions, parenting skills, life skills courses and a robust community-based approach to ensure strengthening of community structures and sustainability. In addition, in coordination with the protection cluster, one of the centers in Atmeh camp in NWS will be available to be set up as entry point to support emerging displacement. While the outreach teams will primarily be focused on the provision of prevention services including awareness-raising and engaging men and boys in the prevention of GBV through accountable practices to ensure meaningful impact. Moreover, there will be specific focus on strengthening existing community-based groups with preparedness and resilience to respond to heightened existing protection risks and those as a result of future crisis/displacement focusing on and prioritizing the needs of different equality groups, as well as focus on strengthening individual skills and autonomy to feed into a longer term sustainable empowerment and development approach to reduce aid dependency and the risk of exploitation and negative coping mechanisms. GC/CHF will also complement the protection activities provided in the areas of coverage and will ensure inter-agency referrals so that people in need are linked to additional required service. Specific consideration will be put in place to ensure active inclusion of all gender and age groups, as well as the various equality groups such as PWD, elderly and minorities throughout the program using the best approach as needed. Considerations will include active outreach to and consultation of these groups in the areas of coverage to take their views and needs into account through the project’s activities, supervision visits, monitoring tools and visits, periodic community consultations & safety audits, evaluation & learning cycles and the FBM and provide them with tailored assistance to access centers and participate in the activities or design the best approach to do so. Also, GC/CHF will continue using GBVIMS+ Primero for the GBV CM data in NWS, CPIMS+ for CP CM in NES, and internal IM systems providing segregated data and analysis to inform the program. GC/CHF adheres to all international protection standards and SOPs & guidance developed by the Protection cluster and GBV & CP AoRs. GC will monitor the project through a variety of offline and online tools and MoV. All data will be disaggregated by age and gender to allow for tailored analysis. CHF will continue to participate in the Protection WG, CP and GBV sub-WG in NES. As for NWS, GC has been an active member of the Protection Cluster, CP AoR, co-lead of the GBV AoR and a member of the GBV SAG, CP CM WG, CP awareness raising TF, Gender WG, Inclusion WG & PSEA Network.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

GC/CHF has been present in the area of coverage since 2016 and have established good rapport and trust with the local communities who refer to our teams for support and consider the centers as safe spaces they can access when they require a specific service. Considering the HNO Syria 2024 Report Finding, the number of people who require GBV assistance in NWS and NES has increased, 93 percent are women and girls. Intimate partner violence and domestic violence, as well as sexual violence, including rape and sexual harassment, continue to be of concern. According to HRP 2024, CP risks in Dana, remains a critical concern requiring urgent attention. There for, GC/CHF will maintain the centers and presence in those area and plan to continue working in these locations throughout 2024 to target 15,500 beneficiaries in NES and 28,303 beneficiaries in NWS by providing direct implementation through comprehensive protection services, covering CP, GBV, and GP/PSS. In addition to response services, GC/CHF will implement empowerment activities that focus on self-awareness, self-esteem, and communication, community support, literacy, art, trust building, the cycle of violence, self-care, gender norms and roles, and communal cohesion towards a better future, through a combination of awareness raising, recreational activities, structured PSS sessions and a robust community-based approach. Following the continuous demand of the local community to expand on such activities in NWS, GC will continue to provide Life Skills courses based on community consultations and light market assessments, to include courses relevant for the self-dependency and sustainability, especially for female head of households who lack a stable income and youth who are at the start of their career path. Courses will be feed into the gender transformative approach, offering a variety of topics such as advanced paint and sculpting, literature, music, financial planning and small business start-up to support the talents identified in previous cycles, as well as new ventures in women and girls in science and politics to open up a door for women and girls into fields that were predominately occupied by men, and encourage a subtle long term shift in gender roles. As for the protection community groups, GC/CHF will continue working with the groups established under each sub-sector and provide them with a number of protection trainings to improve their understanding of protection and risk analysis, as well as emergency preparedness, and build a strong rapport with people from their community to provide immediate support at time of needs. Each group will then have a protection-oriented community action plan and funds to support those initiatives and help reduce the identified risks. Longer term plans include identifying key members of the group who showed good capacities and interest, to then receive further support for potential establishments of Women Led or Community Service Organizations.
