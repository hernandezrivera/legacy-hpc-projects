Project Summary

The objective of the project is to provide life-saving Risk Education to at-risk populations in Northeast Syria (Aleppo and Ar-Raqqa governorates), in concert with Non-Technical Survey (NTS) and Clearance activities specifically enabling people who have been displaced to safely return to their homes and land. This is in line with the HRP Protection objective 4: “Reduce the impact of explosive hazards [ related to strategic objectives,1, 2 and 3]” and will contribute to addressing the high levels of explosive hazards contamination across NES (according to the 2021 HNO 1/3 of communities are contaminated).

4 - Needs

In 2020, more than 25,000 explosive incidents were recorded in 10 months across Syria, with 1,674 incidents reported in Ar-Raqqa (which is one of DCAs main focus areas for RE, NTS and Clearance). One in 3 communities are believed to be contaminated with explosive ordnance (EO). In a rapid needs assessment conducted by DCA in Ar-Raqqa in January 2021, it was reported that the vast majority of the population had seen EOs in the areas where they live and work (92% of respondents), and that local populations either do not to have the necessary knowledge to protect themselves from the dangers of EOs (67% respondents) or have insufficient information (33% respondents). Whilst entire communities are vulnerable to the hazards, men and boys are more directly at risk from the immediate threat, while women and girls are impacted by indirect threat and long-term consequences, exacerbating existing vulnerabilities. In 2020, children and adolescents constituted 67% of population groups exposed to explosive EOs in Ar-Raqqa. While children are affected physically by EO accidents, they are also affected psychologically and economically (family’s prosperity affected by loss of providing parent). Therefore, it is essential that children in communities affected by EOs receive awareness about the risks and how to avoid them.

5 - Implementation of activities

* Risk Education (RE): DCA will provide age and gender tailored RE sessions to at risk populations, specifically targeting IDPs fleeing or returning to highly contaminated areas. RE sessions will provide targeted populations with knowledge and skills to adopt safer behaviours with regards to ERW and IED. In order to address the COVID-19 pandemic, basic COVID-19 awareness raising has also been integrated into the direct RE sessions. * Community Focal Points (CFP) will be trained by DCA on sharing key simple safety messages to their communities, thus increasing the reach and sustainability of the action, and the retention of RE messages within communities even after DCA and other humanitarian actors leave the area.* Explosive Ordnance Safety Briefings (SB) and specialized trainings of Humanitarians on Safe Behaviour and Messaging (TSM), explaining the dangers of explosive hazards and related safe behaviours, will be delivered to humanitarian workers (from local/international NGOs and community based organizations) to help them protect themselves and their beneficiaries, with TSM enabling recipients to share key simple safety messages with their beneficiaries.*Survey, Marking and Clearance of Explosive Hazards: DCA will conduct Non-Technical Surveys (NTS) during which they will gather information about levels and types of contamination in residential areas. These surveys will include information about the impact of the contamination and will help to prioritize the project clearance activities strictly based on humanitarian needs. Clearance teams will focus their activities in residential areas, based on data gathered during the NTS, in order to help save lives, and enable safe and sustainable returns.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Community threshold of acceptable risk increases as a result of living in contaminated areas for a longer period of time, thus leading to more accidents.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Project staff will ensure that RE sessions include topics on effects and consequences of risky behaviour leading to explosive hazard accidents. Together with community members they will discuss safer alternatives and encourage behaviour change. Community Focal Points will also encourage positive behaviour change. Protection Risk: Once marking of contaminated areas begins, communities mistakenly assume any area not marked must be safe.Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: During Community Liaison activities, project staff will inform community representatives and members that unmarked areas may be contaminated with explosive hazards and will instruct or remind them how to recognize warning signs and dangerous areas. RE sessions will also include the same message. Protection Risk: During the clearance and survey of houses, land and properties there is the risk of an unintended detonation. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation Measures: Coordination with local authorities and the Mine Action Office (MAO) will be made before clearance and technical survey is conducted to ensure residence are aware of activities and not within the corresponding danger areas.

Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints box will allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint. DCA also follows Do No Harm principles in all its activities.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: The organization has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security SOPs and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place. Risk: Local authority-imposed COVID-19 curfews in NES prevent project staff from moving and/or working, and/or local authority-imposed curfews in NES/KRI prevent hawala agents from transferring money for distribution and salaries.Assumption: Project locations will remain accessible and DCA will continue to have permission to operate (through modified activities to protect beneficiaries and staff), and Hawalas will be able to continue transferring money. Mitigation measures: DCA staff will follow local authority-imposed curfews and restrictions related to COVID-19. DCA will regularly communicate with hawala agents to ensure supply chains are operational. DCA will communicate with other NGOs and NES Forum should selected hawala agent not be able to transfer funds.
