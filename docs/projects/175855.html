Project Summary

The project will provide life savings and support services to the most vulnerable, conflict and flood-affected girls, boys, adolescents, youths, women, and men in South Sudan who are exposed to child protection and COVID-19 risks in targeted areas of Jur River, Wau, Aweil East, Fangak and Pochalla counties of Western Bahr El Ghazal, Northern Bahr El Ghazal and the Jonglei States respectively.
The services will be delivered through different modalities namely: static, outreach and mobile. The project will be provided through the following key sub-thematic services to at-risk and vulnerable children, adolescents, youths, people with disabilities (PWDs) and community members in the targeted areas:
1. Provision of quality age and gender appropriate individual, group and specialized Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) and parenting skills through community-based interventions including mobile teams to extremely vulnerable girls, boys and their caregivers (CPMS standards 10, 15);
2. Provision of comprehensive case management including through cash-based assistance (CPMS standards 18, 11, 13, 20);
3. Provision of family tracing and reunification for UASC (CPMS standards 13, 18);
4. Provision of alternative care and reintegration services to UASCs, CAAFAG and other OVCs (CPMS standards 11, 18, 19)
5. Establishing and strengthening of referral mechanisms (CPMS standards 14, 16, 17);
6. Capacity building to frontline service providers and volunteers on Child Protection including PSS, case management, general CPiE and on COVID-19 (CPMS standards 2, 18);
7. Strengthening of community structures and mechanisms including community-based child protection committees (CBCPN) and child protection help desks (CPMS standards 16, 17);
8. Conduct child protection and COVID-19 awareness-raising & prevention messaging and Peacebuilding and peaceful coexistence activities; conduct child protection assessments and monitoring using the existing and recommended tools (CPMS standard 6).
For effective delivery of the above-outlined services Hold the Child will work closely with other sectors not limited to food security, livelihoods, education, health, nutrition, WASH, shelter and settlement and camp management as per CPMS 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28 respectively.
To complement the efforts of the CP sub-cluster towards localization Hold the Child will continue co-leading the CPWG in Western Bahr El Ghazal

1 - Needs

Two years after signing the revitalized peace agreement, its implementation has not reduced the South Sudanese people's humanitarian needs. South Sudan remained a protection crisis in 2020. Lack of durable peace and limited investment in basic services hold people back from stability and sustainable development. In 2020, communities were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. Some 1.6 million people remained internally displaced and another 2.2 million as refugees in the region. Insecurity, lack of basic services, and unresolved housing, land and property issues prevent people from returning home in large numbers as Western Bahr El Ghazal (Wau, Jur River) records the highest figures 147,812 IDPs/returnees. About 6 May 2020, IOM's Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) conducted a field assessment mission to Mapel village in Kuajiena Payam to obtain information about recent displacement caused by clashes between farming and cattle keeping communities from neighbouring Tonj South and Tonj East counties. In reference to the South_Sudan_TWG_Key_Messages_Oct_2020-July_2021 report, the most severe acute food insecurity conditions are in counties where chronic vulnerabilities have been exacerbated by a combination of shocks, including floods, insecurity incidents, the macro-economic crisis, and the effects of COVID-19 measures. Between October and November 2020, 34 counties were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. Counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity (i.e. Fangak, Pibor and others in Jonglei State; Greater Bahr el Ghazal region had 10 counties classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity (i.e. Cueibet,among others; Aweil South in Northern Bahr el Ghazal State; In the first projection period of December 2020 to March 2021, 22 counties are classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, with the number likely to increase to 33 counties in the second projection period of April to July 2021. In reference to South_Sudan_flooding_situation_report_31 Jan 2021 by Inter-Cluster Coordination Group, an estimated 1,066,000 people were affected by floods in South Sudan from 1 July 2020 to 31 January 2021. Jonglei state and Greater Pibor Administrative area are the most affected (495,000 people where Fangak and Pochalla contribute to this figures); women and children were identified as the most affected and in need of urgent assistance. The security situation continues to deteriorate throughout South Sudan. In reference to South Sudan HNO for 2021, an estimated 2.7 million people, including 2.3 million (83% of people in need) children, are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection 2021. Children continue to be disproportionately affected by crisis and exposed to multiple protection risks, including recruitment by armed groups, psychosocial distress, family separation, violence and exploitation in 61 of the most affected counties. Over 26,848 children (12,506 girls and 14,342 boys) have been registered as unaccompanied, separated or missing and at-risk most vulnerable children in reference to the UASC report of 12th February 2021, 13,431 children (6,153 girls and 7,278 boys) as active. Despite the government efforts of re-committed itself to address grave violations against children by signing a comprehensive Action Plan on all six grave violations, over 19,000 children are estimated to have been recruited to armed forces and armed groups, with the recent incidents of recruitment in some parts (cantonments) of WBeG.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Hold the Child will provide safe reporting channels to the beneficiaries through the project cycle. Hold the Child key requirement; reporting of PSEA related incidents will be mandatory through the project cycle. Beneficiaries will access information on PSEA through awareness-raising campaigns and through designed and distributed IEC materials. All staff, associates and volunteers should receive regular and refresher training on child safeguarding and PSEA, do no harm, psychological first aid, and risk assessment. Sexual exploitation and abuse are critical issues South Sudan faces; through this protect specific measures and activities will be tailored. Our child safeguarding policy and other related policies like PSEA will be maintained and always will be referred to for guidance. All staff must sign a code of conduct before providing frontline services and engagement with beneficiaries. Through awareness of other services and referrals, will all contribute to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse? UNICEF and UNFPA will strengthen staffs capacities in PSEA policies and including mandatory reporting of sexual exploitation and abuse allegedly committed by humanitarian actors.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Hold the Child will help the affected communities claim their rights through information and documentation and support efforts to strengthen respect for rights. Hold the Child will have a duty to advocate for the full respect of children’s rights and compliance with international law that supports a stronger protective environment. All children and other targeted beneficiaries should be able to access solutions (such as legal actions at local, national or international levels) and claim legal rights (such as inheritance or restitution) that may influence their ability to protect themselves and to claim other rights. Children should be supported to become informed and engaged citizens. Hold the Child will implement simple, accessible, child-friendly, anonymous programme feedback and reporting mechanisms at each site. Affected populations will/must be aware of how to use the mechanisms and what response they should expect when raising a concern or report an incident. All general programme and Hold the Child’s feedback will be addressed promptly and shared with staff and associates in team meetings and performance reviews following confidentiality principles and need to know. A friendly feedback and complaint mechanisms such as suggestion boxes, one on one, a focus group discussions, among others, will be explored in all our target areas. Through regular field visits will enable them to have open meetings with the communities of the affected populations; therefore, the beneficiaries will be always be given a chance to express themselves safely. Reports will be collected from the community-based child protection members close with the beneficiaries hence strengthening the linkages of feedback and complaints.
