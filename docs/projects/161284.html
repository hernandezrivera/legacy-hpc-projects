Project Summary

UNESCO through this project aims to improve quality of education in emergencies and mitigate the impact of emergency on out of school young girls in five former states (Upper Nile, Jonglei, Lakes, Central and Eastern Equatoria) in South Sudan. It aims to provide life saving and life sustaining skills to out of school young girls between age of 14-18 years, focusing particularly to girls rescued or released from armed forces and armed groups and at risk of joining armed groups. For improving quality of education, the project aims to review the current package of Education in Emergencies (EiE) training to teachers that builds nexus of humanitarian-development and peace sectors and provide training to teachers and teacher educators. The project is targeting 3,000 out of school young girls to participate in functional literacy, life-saving and life-sustaining skills and provide EiE training to 2,000 teachers and teacher educators benefiting to about 63,000 learners.
The project will also provide teacher incentives and teaching-learning materials to about 100 most needy schools in the targeted states.

1 - Needs

Children and youth (aged 3-17) represent 45 percent of the population (NBS, OCHA, and IOM 2019). Years of conflict have left many children without access to education services; multiple crises and economic challenges have further exacerbated this situation. Continuous displacement and widespread conflict have put pressure on this vulnerable group. Children are at the forefront of current crises; often, families engage children in income-generating activities; this increases the likelihood of exposure to protection risks, i.e. abduction, sexual violence, forced recruitment, child labor, and early marriages. Long walking distance to the school, increases the risk of GBV for girls and boys. According to MoGEI, 76% of girls in South Sudan are missing out on an education because of the conflict and food crisis. The fact that South Sudan’s school-aged population (6-18 years) is estimated to reach 4.9 million in 2020 further challenges the organization of education supply, particularly in areas that may experience an influx of IDPs or returnees as the conflict eventually subsides.The conflict has halted the government’s capacity to supply quality teachers through teacher training institutes (TTIs). In South Sudan, there are 36,858 teachers, of whom 82 percent are male and 18 percent female (MoGEI 2017). However, only four percent of them have a secondary education certificate or above. As a result, South Sudan finds itself caught in a vicious circle of the low quality of education, leading to a large number of out-of-school-children leading to poor quality of human resources available for professional work like teaching. Moreover, lack of regular or adequate remuneration, lack of access to support and wellbeing services, and professional development opportunities are key factors affecting teacher supply, quality, and attendance in school. To reverse this trend and reach out to more out-of-school children and children at risk of dropping out, more adequately qualified teachers and more schools are required.Education Need Assessment in 2018 reveals key issues hindering education include lack of teaching and learning supplies, inconsistencies in the provision of teacher incentives, and quality of teaching due to missed opportunities to train teachers. As outlined in the government’s GESP, 2017-2022, South Sudan requires 23,000 primary and 2,300 alternative education system (AES) teachers to reach the enrollment targets. A short-term teacher training approach is necessary to improve the quality of education in emergencies and meet the exceptions (GESP 2017). According to Plan International Report-2019, across the sites in South Sudan, adolescent girls exhibited consistent concern about kidnappings, sexual violence, and criminal activities such as looting. Likewise, there is a considerable gender discrepancy in terms of levels of attendance and enrollment into education with lower enrollment and high drop-out rates of girls due to various reasons including early marriage, engaging in household work, and lack of female teachers in schools. National estimates tend to underestimate the numbers of children with disabilities; data generated by the MoGEI in 2015, covering seven former states, reported that 18,000 primary school students displayed a range of impairments. The UNICEF report indicates that 5,988 children were recruited in armed forces and armed groups between the first quarter of 2014 until the second quarter of 2019, of which 955 (317 girls and 638 boys) were released in 2018. The Cluster's objective on improving the quality of education and mitigating impact of an emergency on young girls is aimed by providing learning and cognitive development of children and youth through teacher training, essential education supplies, and providing life-saving and life-sustaining skills to out of school young girls. This project is targeting to benefit 63,000 children (40% girls) and 2,000 teachers through this project.

2 - Activities

Output 1: Equip teachers with EiE skills to provide an improved teaching and learning experience for emergency-affected children.- Building on existing EiE teacher training package aligning with humanitarian-peace-development nexus- Printing of EiE teacher training package- Mapping of schools and teachers who have not received EiE training in the proposed project states- Conduct ToT for master trainers (30 master trainers)- Cascade teacher training for Primary and Secondary level in four former states (2,000 teachers)- Provide teacher incentives following set criteria of Education cluster/ MoGEI (100 teachers)-Provide teaching-learning materials to 100 needy schools in project locations. Output 2: Improve access to inclusive and protective lifesaving education (non-formal) for out of school and conflict-affected young girls, particularly those rescued and released from armed forces and armed groups- Establish temporary learning spaces for out of school girls between the age of 14-17 years, with particular focus on girls rescued of armed troops and arm groups (100 TLC) - Provide Alternative Education Programme, life-saving skills, functional literacy, and marketable skills training to 3,000 girls/young females- Establish referral mechanisms to link appropriate education interventions for out of school children including access to formal education- Provide psycho-social and recreation activities to create an enabling environment for out of school children and youth for joining education interventions at the learning centers.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The project is designed considering the target group as right holders than beneficiaries of the project. Consequently, the rights of the target groups (out of school young girls rescued or relased from armed group and armed forces, children from IDP, returnee and host communities, and person with disabilities) are legally recognized, acknowledged and well respected throughout the project cycle management.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project locations are identified where one out of three beneficiaries will be IDPs or returnees. According to UNESCO 2018 report, majority of IDP/ returnee children are out of school and thus this project is focusing to IDPs and potential returnee areas of the Education Cluster prioritized locations. The project will be targeting at least 50% IDP or returnee children including 50% women participation. Considering the funding crisis for teacher's incentives in 2020 as the EU funded IMPACT project is likely to dis-continue the incentive payment, the project also plans to provide teacher incentives to mitigate the crisis and enhance capacity of schools to accommodate new enrollment of returnee children.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, output one of the project is directly linked to the government system as data of EMIS of MoGEI will be used for mapping of schools and teachers for the training. The training will be organized and delivered in close coordination and collaboration with the national as well as state level MoGEI, Ministry of Gender, Children and Social Welfare and the Ministry of Youth, Culture and Sports (MoCYS). Likewise, Output two of the project will be implemented in close consultation with the community, young girls in and out of school, child clubs and local government in terms of identifying out of school young girls, selection of locations for temporary learning spaces and setting up protection measure to address protection risks and creating safe learning environment for young girls.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the project is aligned with the National Education Cluster Strategy, General Education Strategic Plan, Education Policy, UN Country Framework, National Gender Policy and Strategic Plan, and National Action Plan on UNSCR 1325 among others.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The project will be implemented in consultation with the local community, targeted groups (out of school young girls and teachers), education stakeholders based in project locations, local government and other key stakeholders on ground to ensure that the protection measures are in place to maintain safe teaching-learning environment. Moreover, UNDSS security measure and OCHA's access maps will be followed throughout the project span to prevent and mitigate associated risk.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the project is build up keeping in mind the triple nexus approach of humanitarian-development-peace sectors as South Sudan is in protracted crisis where it is important to link all three sectors for gaining comparative advantages of each sector to reduce need, risk and vulnerability following the recommendations of the World Humanitarian Summit (WHS) and in accordance with the 2030 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) agenda. It aims to design and implement interventions considering nexus of all three types of policies and strategies bringing together humanitarian, development and peace actors to work in more cohesive manner. Consequently, ensuring that the outcomes of the project are sustainable beyond the project lifespan. Moreover, the project will be implemented in coordination with the MoGEI and other relevant ministries and thus efforts made by the project will be sustained beyond the project period.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

8.1 and 8.2: Due to patriarchal structure deeply rooted in social belief, behavioral pattern and practice; it is likely that women and girls are not included in consultations and decision making process concerned to them. Appropriate guidelines and tools will be developed to ensure that girls, women, members of marginalized communities and minority group including person with disabilities and women are consulted in decision making process since inception phase of the project. 8.4: Life skills will be one of the key modules in teacher training package to ensure that teachers are gender sensitive and able to create safe and secure learning environment for girls. For protection of out of school girls, UNESCO will take and innovative approach to reach out young girls through existing mechanism such as Community Learning Centers (CLC) and Community Girls Schools (CGS). UNESCO will coordinate with protection cluster and other relevant clusters to collect information on local service providers and set-up referral mechanism. In case if the services are not available, the relevant cluster Coordinator will be informed and seek resource mobilization to mitigate the gaps.8.10 and 8.11: Often girls are reported abused on the way to/from school. There is an issue of early marriage of girls and being pregnant even before the age of 14. The project aims to address practical as well as strategic gender needs such as separate sanitation facilities for girls and boys in school, promoting recruitment of female teacher, information and services on comprehensive sexuality education, psycho-social and mental health support through coordination and referral mechanism.8.13: To prevent disclose sensitive and personal data of the target group, a Code of Conduct will be developed for the human resources responsible for data collection, management and its use. In addition, confidential data and information will be coded and de-coded while preparing reports to maintain confidentiality.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Reaching out to the most vulnerable target group: The project will mobilize existing community based organizations, NGOs, faith based organizations, youth groups, community leaders to reach the targeted vulnerable population and held interactions to receive feedback and suggestion contributing to effective design and implementation of project activities. Illiterate or semi-literate young girls: At the inception phase, pictorial method will be used to collect feedback. Also conducive environment will be provided to avoid any influence or pressure and receive fair feedback from the beneficiaries. Depending on the context, Focus Group Discussion (FGD) will be conducted on periodic basis to collect feedback on project services. Individuals living with disabilities: The project will collect information on persons with disabilities through secondary data and verify it collecting primary data following snowballing method at the local level. Individual interview and observations are the key tools among others used for collecting feedback from such target group. Ensure regular communication with target groups: The project will set up two-way communication among master trainers, trainers and target groups including students, parents, School Management Committees (SMC) to obtain feedback and suggestions.Pre and post teacher training evaluation of : The project will develop tools to conduct pre and post evaluation of participants of MTOT and teacher training that will contribute to improve quality and management of training.
