Project Summary

Sudan has become now one of the worst displacement crises in the world, since the start of the conflict on April 15th, 2023. More than 6 million Sudanese are internally displaced, about a third of them residing in schools, public buildings, camps, and other informal settlements (DTM-IOM). Since the beginning of the conflict Acted has been leading a mobile CCCM response to respond to the needs of the IDPs, maintain and repair critical assets in the IDP sites as well as strengthening humanitarian coordination pathways. Acted and its partners will intervene in official IDP sites, in hosting states (Gedaref, Sennar, Blue Nile, White Nile, River Nile, Red Sea, Central and West Darfur). The intervention will be led according to a two-way approach whereby INGOs and NNGOs will be mobilised to deliver critical life-saving assistance and the IDP communities will be fully integrated into decision-making and management mechanisms. In frontline areas, Acted and its partners will provide technical and financial support to help better manage the sites inhabited by conflict-affected individuals, thus improving the latter’s living standards. Upon arrival in the official IDP sites, safety audits will be conducted along with an overall assessment of the IDP sites, available services and humanitarian actors. Along with the mapping exercise of the sites, this will enable to clearly identify aid and management/coordination mechanisms which will be used to guide the interventions in the target IDP sites. Based on the results of the various audits, assessments and mapping, Acted, and its partners will strive to establish management, cooperative and CRM systems as well as effective referral pathways to enhance a smooth running of the IDP sites, thus improving the quality of life of its residents. For NNGOs, Acted will provide capacity-building workshops to strengthen their CCCM skills, thus ensuring a more sustainable impact of the action after its end. In IDP sites, Acted, its partners and other humanitarian actors will make the necessary repairs to key infrastructures, especially WASH assets, and shelters in a coordinated manner. This will allow to mitigate against disaster risks and spread of diseases such as cholera. In fighting zones, Acted will financially support the frontline responders which will enable to make reparations to essential assets, including WASH assets. In the initial assessments of IDP sites, Acted and its partners will also be able to quickly identify the IDPs in need of urgent humanitarian assistance. They will either be referred to other humanitarian actors or their case will be directly handled by Acted and its partners where appropriate. Acted, will be able to provide Protection services for those requiring GBV, CP or General Protection services. In IDP sites, Acted and its partners will aim at establishing a protective and safe environment for all residents through the creation of community-based site management committees. These structures will be run by IDPs residing in the camps and will be tasked with the maintenance of assets, decision-making over priority interventions, identification of at-risk residents and conflict-resolution. The site management committees will also be involved in awareness-raising sessions, led by Acted, its partners and NNGOs on various topics including hygiene and Protection.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention envisages specific needs faced by different socio-demographic groups and encourages a gender-balanced participation of target communities– with a zero-tolerance policy towards discrimination– throughout the Action, which is embedded in the “Do No Harm Principle” and follows a conflict-sensitive approach. Preparation Phase: - Before and during the intervention, Acted will assess the security context and protection risks, and continue monitoring the situation throughout the project’s lifespan. - The whole community will be sensitised on the project’s scope and activities and informed on selection criteria, which were designed to address protection risks and cover sectoral needs of most vulnerable (e.g. single women, PLWs, children, the elderly, PwDs).Program Implementation Phase: - Acted mainstreams gender-adapted and keyprotection messages during community sensitisation and trainings. - Waiting time and safety during traveling will be addressed through adhoc protection measures. - First aid kits and protective wear are available on distribution sites. Accountability: - Acted has an independent MEAL unit with experience in setting up Complaints andResponse Mechanisms (CRMs). A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage the CRM, through which beneficiaries can report complaints/feedback in a safe and confidential way. Besides its staff deployed in the field daily, Acted will also conduct regular spot checks. Acted will evaluate if a complaint was triggered due to flaws, shortcomings or gaps in procedures or implementation strategy. In addition to case-by-case handling, anonymized complaints reports will be shared internally to possibly reshape the proposed intervention if needed, and inform future projects’ design.Human resources: - Acted has a Code of Conduct that is consistent with the U.N. Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) Task Force on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse in Humanitarian Crises. Acted also complies with The Code of Conduct (CoC) of The International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement and UNHCR’s CoC. Acted’s CoC is implemented in each program location and is under the direct supervision of the Program Manager. Any incident would be immediatelyreported to coordination through a standardized communication pathway and actions would be taken by the appropriate levels of coordination. - For the intervention, Acted will set up a gender-balanced implementation team to ensure a safer environment,improve protection mainstreaming and favour inclusion mechanisms. In all activities, Acted will promote an equal gender participation.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

Acted Complaints and response mechanisms. - Acted will establish Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) for beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback in a secure way, and receive response in a timely manner. A dedicated staff will be responsible to manage CRM permanently installed in Area Office as well as in all target locations throughout the project. At the beginning andduring implementation, target communities will be sensitized on the mechanism, and on how to submit complaints. Complaints will be confidentially recorded and reported each month at country-level. CRM will ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between Acted and community, pay specific attention to gender-related concerns, and improve programs quality, moving from intention to prevent/mitigate any adverse social/environmental/health risks.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

As part of its accountability strategy, Acted will mainstream protection and PSEA elements during all activities, particularly through its CRM. Acted has appropriate and proportionate procedures and controls in place to prevent, report, detect, respond and report on safeguarding issues & allegations. Recruitment: - Acted prevents employment/deployment of unsuitable individuals through rigorous screening. People employed by Acted already have obligation to read and sign the code of conduct and internal policies. Administration and employee cycle: - Signature of Code of Conductfor staff; - Monitoring of trial periods for cases detected - Disciplinary measures follow-up on cases detected in conjunction with country coordination. - Trainingprogram related to safeguarding issues. - Acted has an in-house training related to safeguarding issues: i) Annual training on the Code of Conduct and Policies in all countries and at HQ for 7,000 staff; ii) Pre-departure training for international employees. ii) Specific training on SEA & Child Protection in intervention countries. Acted will also train the members of the Camp Management Committees (CMCs) members on Acted’s Code of Conduct and PSEA policies to ensure that these are respected, and a protective environment is established in the IDP sites. CMCs will also take part in the monthly Camp Coordination Meeting which participation will enable to identify issues and find effective corresponding solutions. In addition, Acted will make sure its suppliers respect Acted’s CoC and PSEA policies by including them in contracts, thus preventing any protection offense that might be committed by Third Parties. Complaint’s investigation &disciplinary measures -Acted has capacity to investigate safeguarding issues in an independent and confidential way, while protecting both the alleged victim and thealleged perpetrator (Acted SEA Policy, Articles 19 to 21; Acted Child protection policy,Articles 20 to 22). Acted must always ensure that the author of a report does not suffer from any harm as a result. Acted also endeavours to protect victims ofprohibited acts (Acted SEA policy, article 23). Acted has psychologists available tosupport individuals who would have been impacted as a result of a failure to comply with Acted's safeguarding policies. The HQ HR department will assess allegations to design an investigation plan and select an investigation team. Depending on investigation conclusions, different measures can be taken including disciplinary actions or reporting to prosecuting authority.
