Project Summary

The project will contribute to improve food and nutrition security of vulnerable households through provision of seeds, tools and agriculture trainings on staple crops and vegetables. Targeted households are supported to increase their income through various activities, such as temporary employment in Cash for Asset activities during lean season, but even more so by training them on income generating activities for additional income as well as organising themselves in VSLAs which allow them to access funds in times of need. Supporting improved post-harvest management, especially improved storage facilities, will also help to make households more resilient to inflationary price developments.
Furthermore, the communities are supported and train to identify the risks of possible external environment impacts and to respond to mitigate such risks with the construction of dykes and drainage channels for flood protection or irrigation ponds. These measures will help to limit the impact of droughts and floods and thus help to reduce harvest losses and damages to basic infrastructure.
In addition, the capacities of both individuals among the target group as well as of the local state actors are developed through trainings and skills-development and local structures are strengthened which will ensure that developed infrastructure will be managed and maintained. Social infrastructure in the communities will be rehabilitated through construction of feeder roads, but also construction of one school building.
The direct beneficiaries will be 6,000 vulnerable semi-pastoral rural households in two counties in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (Aweil West and North), i.e. around 40,000 individuals. The indirect target group is the entire population of the 12 payams, approx. 246,500 people. Wherever possible, the project will target the most vulnerable, high risk groups, especially women and women-headed households, IDPs and recently returned households, as well as children and old and chronically ill people, as these groups are most at risk for food insecurity and malnutrition and are often marginalised in their communities. The project aims to target approx. 21,600 women and 18,400 men.

Needs

The north-western State of Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) where the project will be implemented has remained relatively unaffected by the conflict due to the mainly homogenous Dinka population as well as the areas remoteness, only very few encounters occurred. Nevertheless, NBeG traditionally has been one of the states with high levels of food insecurity across South Sudan. As per September IPC update, over 53% of the population are in IPC phases 3 (crisis) and 4 (emergency) and thus acutely food-insecure. Since 2016, the UN World Food Programme is providing food distributions during the lean season from ca. March or April to September to ca. 70,000 people in the project area in Aweil North and West counties, which provide the most vulnerable households with 10 to 15-day food rations over a period of 7 month per year. The reasons for this wide-spread severe food insecurity are manifold. NBeG suffers a structural food deficit as local production is insufficient to feed the population. According to the South Sudan Crop Watch report, a study of FAO and the National Bureau of Statistic, NBeG had a cereal deficit of 25,089 mt. The cereal harvest per hectare is very low with only 1.1mt/h. This is mainly due to un-adapted and non-sustainable farming techniques but also the average cultivate area per household is small with only 1 to 2 fedan, i.e. 0.42 to 0.84 hectare, per household. In addition, the area is regularly hit by dry spells and drought conditions during the dry season and flooding during the rainy season, both of which destroy or reduce harvests regularly. The population therefore depends on markets to access food but the area lacks employment and income-generating opportunities to generate sufficient income. The continuing countrywide economic crisis has led to severe increases in food prices, according to the National Bureau of Statistics, year-on-year inflation in March 2018 stood at 161%.In addition, NBeG has only poorly developed infrastructure which hinders access to markets und thus to productive inputs and assets as well as to basic social services. Due to the conflict and the lack of government capacities, many basic social services are massively supported or entirely provided by humanitarian organisations. A study by FAO, UNICEF, WFP, the South Sudanese Government and several NGOs , which was presented in the FSL Cluster, identified two main underlying drivers in the society for structural food insecurity in NBeG: one, the massive influx of returnees from Sudan, who returned to NBeG without any productive assets, and two, changing traditional wealth structures between the formerly better-off and poor rural populations through the economic crisis, which caused e.g. a loss of employment opportunities for unskilled labour, as well as changing control structures of productive assets, especially livestock. Similarly, a study by the SUDD-Institute finds that the high levels of poverty as well as a non-sustainable adjustment of livelihoods, moving away from subsistence towards increased dependence on cash income and markets in interplay with a deepening economic crisis and rising prices as the cause of food insecurity. Against this background, Welthungerhilfe has designed this project which will on the one hand strengthen the capacities of the target group to improve and stabilise their livelihoods by increased food production and by generating new income opportunities, and on the other hand developing local basic infrastructure and strengthening the resilience to withstand additional shocks.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Project has established CRM and CFM system where information are collected from beneficiaries, but are also back-channeled to them on a regular basis and project-specific information are shared. Household surveys incl. KAP surveys, focus group discussions, continuous monitoring of activities will also help to inform beneficiaries and capture information how project provided services are utilized. Findings will be communicated to respective community leaders but also target group directly. Post-distribution monitoring will be carried out after each distribution.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are formulated in a gender-sensitive manner, gender-sensitive issues are addressed with all project stakholders to strengthen gender equality. Focus group discussions are segregated by sex and age groups.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Welthungerhilfe has clear policies and code of conducts in place which emphasize zero tolerance on fraud and corruption and sexual abuse and exploitation and any such behaviours will be met with disciplinary action including dismissal of staff.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Target groups are already engaged in a previous project and were frequently consulted during regular field visits on satisfaction with activities, difficulties and progress in preparation of the new project. Community members engage directly in CRM, CFM and PDM where their opinions are captured. Next to target households traditional authorities, local authorities were represented during consultations.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

To provide the target group a safe and private option to file feedback or complaints regarding the project or Welthungerhilfe’s staff behaviour, Welthungerhilfe will establish Complaints Response Mechanism and also incorporate existing community-based ones into its system. In addition to already existing communication channels, such as community meetings or the Payam Development Committees, this will allow the target group to submit sensitive complaints in a safe and private manner. Welthungerhilfe will record all complaints and forward them to the respective departments for addressing them, and give feedback to the complainant. Sensitive complaints regarding fraud and corruption and sexual abuse and exploitation will be forwarded directly to the respective departments in Welthungerhilfe’s HQ in Bonn which will initiate an investigation if the complaint is found to be valid.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Welthungerhilfe will monitor the progress and impact of the project with established Welthungerhilfe monitoring tools. At the start of the project, Welthungerhilfe will conduct a baseline study to establish the status of the project indicators before the action. The results will serve as the base for a comparison of indicators before and after the project, indicators will be assessed again in an endline survey to identify the changes and thus the impact of the project. The baseline and endline surveys will be done through household questionnaires and may be complemented with focus group discussions or key informant interviews as required. Welthungerhilfe will conduct the surveys internally under guidance and leadership of the interna-tional MEAL-expert based in the CO. Welthungerhilfe will use electronic data collection tools for the surveys.In the beginning of the project, Welthungerhilfe will develop a monitoring plan which outlines which data will be assessed and in which frequency to ensure progress and impacts are well captured, and responsibilities and resources will be assigned for the monitoring. The implementation progress will be shared with the CO Juba every month through a developed format. After all distribution activities, especially the agricultural inputs (seed fairs) and cash distributions, Welthungerhilfe will conduct every 2 to 4 weeks Post-Distribution Monitoring with a sample of the target group to assess the satisfaction of the target group with the distribution as well as the use of the seeds and cash. The PDM will also include questions on security of the target group during and after distributions as well as other accountability aspects. The regular progress monitoring and PDM will be conducted by the project team with support of the MEAL expert in Juba.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Briefly describe how

Risk analysis will be done in a participatory session during project inception in community consultation. Protection risk analysis is also based on outcomes of needs' assessment.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Wherever possible population groups with specific and higher needs will be integrated in the project approach, e.g. IGA will be tailored to physical handicaped persons in the population wherever possible and they will be trained on skills that require less physical labor (financial management capacities) .

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Welthungerhilfe has only limited measures to react to intra-family conflicts, because staff members could be-come target of such conflicts. Family conflicts will be referred to traditional community leaders, if applicable WHH refers families concerned to partner organisations that work in “protection”.

Project activities address inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Distributions will be organized as far as possible decentralized in particular communities to keep risks for target group low. Activities are planned in a way that target group will be back home before nightfall. Whenever needed WHH will collaborate with organisations working in “protection” to analyse causes, incidents and solutions. Target group themselves has well-established mechanisms to minimise security risks for community members.
