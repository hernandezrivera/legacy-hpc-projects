Project Summary

WFP is committed to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and is guided by a strategic, programmatic and governance instrument to operationalize its plan and achieve its project objectives to:
• End Hunger by protecting access to food and by improving dietary diversity; and
• Support the implementation of the SDGs by enhancing the capacities of national institutions to implement food security strategies and operations.
This project directly contributes to strategic objectives of the Humanitarian Response Plan by meeting the basic food needs of severely vulnerable Palestinians and by improving their dietary diversity.
WFP’s interventions focus on high food insecurity levels, including the malnutrition levels recorded through national surveys, and supporting vulnerable families in vulnerable communities with livelihood activities. By prioritizing assistance poor and severely food insecure Palestinian households in Gaza and Bedouin and herding communities in West Bank’s Area C, and by disseminating information on food and nutrition practices, WFP addresses the basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians and tackles one of the root causes of some of the food insecurity levels recorded by the government institutions. These interventions aim at improving the overall food security levels including nutrition and health status of the vulnerable Palestinians and therefore at increasing their coping abilities to sudden shocks in the market for access to food.
The project also contributes to the Food Security Sector’s objectives as well as to the National Policy Agenda 2017-2022 (policies on escaping poverty and strengthening social protection as well as meeting the basic needs of the communities) to ensure that the vulnerable Palestinians households basic food needs are met, by assisting households in enhancing their food security levels and by strengthening national institutions in implementing food security strategies and social security safety net programs to best identify and target food insecure and vulnerable Palestinians.
The project will focus on providing unconditional food assistance to targeted poor and severely food insecure households in the Gaza Strip, in Area C, and areas with poverty and high food insecurity levels in the West Bank. WFP and their implementing partners in the Gaza Strip and West Bank will provide cash based transfers (including electronic vouchers and cash assistance) and in-kind assistance.
The target beneficiaries include 419,470 non-refugee poor and severely food insecure people
including 38,350 individuals from the Bedouin and herding communities in Area C of the
West Bank that will be assisted as part of the joint emergency food assistance programme
in partnership with UNRWA. UNRWA will distribute the assistance to the Bedouin and herding communities.

1 - Needs

According to initial results from the inter-agency 2022 Palestine Humanitarian Needs Overview, the 2020 Socio-Economic and Food Security Survey (SEFSec) findings, and household monitoring results, some two million Palestinians (36.7% of the population) are estimated to be moderately to severely food insecure. Poverty and unemployment, the key drivers of vulnerability and food insecurity in Palestine, are at their peak: in Gaza, nearly seven out of ten people are poor, and half of the workforce is unemployed; in the West Bank poverty more than doubled, from 14 to 30 percent. Food insecurity is driven by several inter-linked factors including limited economic access to food, arising from restrictions on movement, trade and investment, high unemployment rates, erosion of agricultural infrastructure, land confiscation, limited access to land and water, and an overstretched government safety net. The combined impact of these drivers has resulted in exceptionally high numbers of people in need, of which 73% are in the Gaza Strip and 27% in West Bank (of which 82% are in Area A & B, and about 11% are female headed households). Nearly 70% of those are suffering from poor access to nutritious and sufficient food. There continues to be widespread use of negative strategies, such as reducing the quality of food consumed and taking on debt, to cope with the deterioration of livelihoods and socio-economic conditions by food insecure households. The COVID-19 pandemic has further exacerbated the humanitarian food insecurity situation and negatively affected the socio-economic development of food insecure households. As a part of its COVID-19 response, the government and international partners prioritized social protection interventions, including by cash and in-kind transfers, to provide rapid relief to households impacted by the crisis. Recently, the government's ability to maintain the National Social Safety Net (SSN) is at risk, and in 2021 116,000 most vulnerable families registered under the SSN only received one round of assistance out of three. According to the 2021 multi-sector needs assessment, 36% of households reported permanent or temporarily job loss as a result of the COVID-19 outbreak. The highest percentages were recorded in East Jerusalem (50%), Area A and B (43%) and Area C (34%). 53% of households in Gaza and 68% in the West Bank reported a decrease in their monthly income. 68% of the interviewees reported an increase in debt as a result of the pandemic. Gender inequalities play a significant role in the food security and nutrition status of individuals; women are especially affected by food insecurity and poverty. The prevalence of food insecurity among households headed by women is 6.6 percentage points higher than that among households headed by men – 39.1 versus 32.5%. Furthermore, women’s economic empowerment is impeded by lower wages and domestic work evidenced by unemployment statistics: uunemployment rate for the second quarter of 2021 was 44.7% in the Gaza Strip and 16.9% percent in the West Bank. Unemployment among males in Gaza reached 39% and up to 64% among females. In the West Bank, the rate stood at 14.3% among males and 28.5% among females. Studies have shown around 20% of school children are anemic. Daily food consumption patterns show unhealthy trends with approximately 90% of adolescent consuming white bread daily full of “empty calories”, and only 35% of school aged children consumed at least one fresh fruit daily. Schoolchildren have higher nutritional needs and require more protein and energy than any other age group - approximately 2,420 kcal per day. Adolescents also have gender-specific nutrient needs; studies have shown the optimal diet to support adolescent girls’ healthy development is more expensive than the diet required by an adult man. The dual burden of malnutrition (i.e., the co-existence of under- and over-nutrition) is a growing problem in Palestine and poses a serious threat to the health of adults, children, and adolescents alike.

2 - Activities

WFP will carry out the following activities: Activity 1: Provision of unconditional food assistance -through cash based transfers (including electronic vouchers and cash assistance) and in-kind modalities. WFP will provide unconditional food assistance to targeted poor and severely food insecure households in the Gaza Strip, in Area C and areas with poverty and high food insecurity levels in the West Bank. WFP will provide $10.3 per person as a food voucher. This amount is calculated based on the staple commodities market price plus a 20 percent margin to absorb any fluctuations in the market prices and to make sure that the beneficiaries can secure the most needed food. Also, WFP will provide unconditional cash assistance to selected beneficiaries with 878 ILS. This transfer value was calculated based on the minimum expenditure basket and was agreed upon by the cash working group members. The target beneficiaries include 419,470 non-refugee poor and severely food insecure people including 38,350 individuals from the Bedouin and herding communities in Area C of the West Bank that will be assisted as part of the joint emergency food assistance programme in partnership with UNRWA. Around 30% of the targeted households are female-headed households. WFP contracted retail shops offer voucher beneficiaries quality and diverse food at market prices. WFP works with several partners to monitor food prices and issues monthly food price monitoring reports, as well as carries out process monitoring at the shops, providing an extra layer of oversight and fiscal controls. To the greatest extent possible, shops are owned by women, offer local products produced by women-led micro, small and medium enterprises such as honey, olive oil, olives, and produce, and promote women’s economic empowerment. The secondary impact of cash based transfers on the local economy has shown to increased sales turnover, increased number of clients, creation of new jobs, and VAT generated. Furthermore, the program adopts a NEXUS-approach by linking humanitarian, development, community resilience, and stabilization. WFP’s humanitarian assistance supports targeted households as well as the local economy which enhances stability by giving participating shopkeepers increased bargaining power ability to negotiate prices, discounts, and bonuses with suppliers, which have been passed to customers through price discounts and special offers. WFP also plans to positively disrupt markets by giving beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries access to digital/social phone applications which will transparently show market prices of key food items. Disability is also a key vulnerability factor taken into consideration in targeting poor and food insecure households and included in the data collection tool that is used in targeting. Disability of food insecure people is an eligibility factor to receive assistance under the National Social Safety Net programme. Operationally WFP considers disability a factor in the selection of distribution points and shops to ensure accessibility. WFP utilizes available nutrition data to design and implement interventions per the nutritional - and not only the caloric - needs of the beneficiary population. This will include developing and delivering Social Behavior Change and Communication interventions in collaboration with the Ministry of Health and UNICEF targeting pregnant, lactating women and mothers of children below 5 years old with communications approaches, activities, and tools to positively influence behaviors for improving overall nutrition. WFP also links families and youth who are bypassed by mainstream economic opportunities - especially women, young people, people living with disabilities, small holder farmers, and other vulnerable and food insecure persons - with livelihood and employment activities opportunities in agriculture- and non-agriculture value chains. The program aims to increase participants’ self-confidence, develop their technical and life skills, provide opportunities to network with skilled professionals, support entrepreneurial initiatives, and build opportunities to access income enabling them to escape household debt burdens and reliance on external assistance. Opportunities are mapped to local market demands and will include rubble removal, food processing, information technology, mobile phone repair, trade skills (carpentry, plumbing, electrical maintenance, etc), and marketing. WFP’s programming applies the “Do No Harm” framework to address risks that humanitarian action could exacerbate conflict. In particular, WFP’s commitment to protection ensures that programmes are safe, dignified and respect people’s other needs, rights and capacities. The adoption of Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) approach in Palestine focuses on three key areas: a) providing accurate, timely and accessible information about WFP assistance; b) consulting affected persons and inviting feedback throughout each stage of the project cycle; and, c) providing a complaints and feedback mechanism to affected people to voice complaints and provide feedback in a safe and dignified manner through secure boxes placed in retail shops for CBTs, WFP ‘s dedicated hotline, and collecting feedback during monitoring visits. WFP empowers beneficiaries by addressing inequalities in financial inclusion and aims to bridge digital divides by giving access to platforms for two-way communication as well as integrating technology in food assistance and livelihoods.WFP’s program design maximizes the benefits of different transfer modalities (multi-purpose cash, cash for food, electronic vouchers, in kind food parcels) to ensure protection risks are mitigated, particularly related to exacerbating inter-household and inter-group sensitivities. WFP household monitoring shows that its programs are implemented safely, appropriately, and respectfully without exposing them to protection or safety challenges.

WFP works closely with Food Security Sector stakeholders to continually improve the analysis and identification of the food insecure population in Palestine and to target food assistance. The targeting of WFP’s food assistance is based on a Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF) developed by the Ministry of Social Development. The PMTF takes into account changing socioeconomic factors which influence household expenditures and the determination of households that cannot meet their basic needs. Food assistance will be provided in a manner that reduces the exposure to risk for beneficiaries (especially that of women) and at the same time reduces the burden associated with carrying food items, particularly for female headed households. WFP uses a robust monitoring system to ensure that all beneficiaries are verified and monitored after receiving assistance to ensure that the beneficiaries meet the criteria of falling below the PA’s threshold deep poverty line. The deep poverty line is calculated by taking into consideration household consumption levels of three categories of goods and services: food, clothing and housing. Prior to any activity implementation, WFP will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities and beneficiaries with line ministries offices, and with any other organization working in the same area and sector of activities.WFP will collect and calculate indicators about protection mainstreaming: 1) Proportion of targeted people receiving assistance without safety challenges disaggregated by gender, 2) Proportion of targeted people having unhindered access to WFP programmes disaggregated by gender

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Cash based transfers in the form of electronic food vouchers are provided to targeted beneficiaries who meet geographical and vulnerability criteria – they live in urban centers near one of approximately 320 participating shops in the voucher network and they are unable to meet their basic food and nutrition requirements. Beneficiaries receiving cash based transfers as part of a integrated program - aiming to support livelihoods, increase the household’s shock responsiveness, build economic empowerment, or support gender equality – are selected based not only on vulnerability but also taking into account program criteria including participant capacity, access to assets, education and interest. WFP`s Food Assistance through in-kind food parcels is selected for beneficiaries who do not have easy or secure access to urban centers where participating shops in the electronic voucher network are found. These beneficiaries often face movement restrictions and have significant protection concerns. While all WFP beneficiaries are verified using the Proxy Means Test Formula (PMTF) as its main targeting methodology by determining household income, expenditures and socio-economic vulnerability, beneficiaries of in-kind food parcel distribution modalities are also targeted based on their food consumption patterns which identify a need for increased consumption of food items provided by WFP, beneficiary preference for in-kind food parcels, and protection concerns. Beneficiaries targeted with Cash assistance – including cash for food and multi-purpose cash – are identified and targeted to reach those households who are most in need. Targeting criteria includes a) the highest consumption gaps (the gap between the national deep poverty line and the household expenditure), and b) lowest food consumption scores and vulnerability criteria such as households headed by women, elderly, with special needs and health problems.
