Project Summary

Kuwaiti patients Helping Fund (KPHF) proposes to assist protracted displacement population, returnees, new arrival and host communities affected by displacement and conflict by supporting 22 PHC centers 3 in Draij & Otash IDPs, 3 in Dimsu and 3 in Gereida in South Darfur State and 2 in El Fasher rural, 4 in Twella ,4 in Daresallam and 3 in Sarfomera localities in North Darfur State. The Project will support provision of quality, high impact lifesaving primary health care Interventions to above targeted communities .The proposed programming will support 342,647 direct beneficiaries including men(60,200), women (220,500), boys(30,970) and girls(30,977 ) comprising of protracted IDPs, returnees , new arrival and host communities experiencing protracted crisis and low access of primary health care services. The project will intervention through provision of basic primary health care services under these activities which include outpatient consultations, essential medicine, basic laboratory investigation, referrals of severe cases to secondary care level, improve access to maternal and child health care services, provide EPI services and IMCI services. Also provide reproductive health services which include ANC & PNC services, safe and clean delivery, family planning and EmOC services. In addition to, KPHF will strengthen the capacity of project and SMoH staff through medley and intensive trainings and on-the-job trainings related to management and delivery of primary health care services, contribution in early detection, preparedness and response to emergencies and public health threats and make sure that surveillance system is in place and that epidemics are prevented, reported and controlled. In the same context, the above mentioned staff will be trained on disease surveillance, prevention and control. Accordingly, they will send weekly EWARS reports to the SMoH and WHO and report any outbreaks within 72 hours. KPHF along with SMoH will conduct community awareness campaigns in these targeted camps and surrounding areas on different topic such as AWD, STIs, RH and others to promote good health and hygiene practices and to reduce the risk of outbreaks of communicable diseases. KPHF has taken into account men, women, boy and girls in assessing the needs for and design of this project, and will continue to do so throughout its implementation. KPHF is very cognizant of the importance of gender equality and will provide equal access for men, women, boys and girls to health care services at the clinics and ensure that health education is provided to both genders. As women and children are considered the most vulnerable, KPHF will prioritize maternal and child health services KPHF strongly believes in shared responsibilities, strong linkage and communication among partners including SMOH, national and international NGOs and the community. To find common solutions to problems, and exchange ideas and experiences, KPHF will continue to participate in the weekly health coordination meeting chaired by WHO and SMoH at national and state levels.

1 - Needs

People living in North & South Darfur suffer from low access to health care, high disease prevalence &mortality rates & frequent disease outbreaks. The proposed locations have been in many cases affected by malaria outbreak. KPHF used information collected from the Ministries of Health, implementing partners, community partners and relevant primary and secondary health data and information. KPHF prioritized localities as per the national priorities described in the SHF 2019 allocation document. The health systems in Darfur remains weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic lifesaving health services and respond to outbreaks. In North Darfur, it is reported that 157 (26%) of the health facilitiesare not functional and 71 HF are not functional in South Darfur (HeRAMS –Q2 2018).Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) coverage in health facilities is only 30% in North Darfur and 43% in South Darfur. Only 40% of clinics in North Darfur and 61% in South Darfur States provide Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI) services. The coverage of measles vaccination is only 55.9% while only 38.3% of children are fully vaccinated. Only 54% of the clinical OPD cases are managed according to the protocol despite training and supportive supervisions, which signals the need for strengthening support and follow up to ensure adherence to protocols. Detection and referral of pregnant mothers is weak in both North & South Darfur because of low coverage of community outreach activities. The clinics are not yet fully staffed and still lack the necessary equipment and physical structure to provide comprehensive health services. KPHF’s health records shows that ARIs, malaria, diarrhea, & eye infections are the leading causes of morbidity &mortality especially in children. As these are commonly caused by communicable diseases there is a high need to strengthen the health sector & create awareness in the needy communities to improve the situation. Other field level findings from ongoing health interventions show that community health seeking behavior in both states still needs to be further strengthened.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The project have medium impact to the environment which include all medical wastes from RH, immunization, laboratory and nursing sections. KPHF will mitigate the impact of these waste by disposal through incineration and damping to the west safely that aim to mitigate the negative impact of the waste from the environmental. In addition, the delivery rooms in all RH units will have soak pits and proper drainage to ensure that fluids and water used for cleaning are properly disposed of in a hygienic manner.

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

KPHF recognizes the importance of mainstreaming protection in its projects in order to make sure that its efforts would ensure full respect for the rights of individuals and groups. The organization recognizes the importance of the human rights of IDPs, returnees and all groups especially the most vulnerable groups such women, girls and minority groups. While implementing all projects, the organization will make conscious and intentional efforts to avoid all situations which expose beneficiaries to risk, threat, violence and abuse of their rights. Through the Community Committees, partner and staff members, PHF work to foster trust and mutual understanding between various groups and to minimize conflict and reduce or stop violence. Also PHF focus on building the capacity of the staff to strengthen their understanding on the fundamental human rights and serving beneficiaries with dignity, gender and protection mainstreaming to ensure that vulnerable beneficiaries (women, girls, men and boys and minorities) are respected and feel protected.
