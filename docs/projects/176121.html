Project Summary

The project intends to deliver life saving static and mobile services to the most vulnerable children and their caregivers affected by floods, conflict that causes displacement ,diseases and famine. This comprises of returnees, Host community and internally displaced population ( men women, boys , girls , young people and adolescents) living in Jonglei state ( Akobo and Pibor counties ) and Central Equatoria State ( Lainya and Yei counties). The total target population is 28,000 most vulnerable individuals ( 10,000 girls, 9000 boys, 5000 women and 4000 men). The primary purpose of the project is to address compounding effects of converging factors i.e severe food insecurity /loss of livelihoods, economic decline, protection threats and lack of services which contributes and aggravates the prevalence of violence against children This project will focus on both preventive and responsive aspects of Child protection and work closely with GBV , FSL, Health ,S/NFI WASH, Education and Nutrition partners to ensure an integrated approach for a holistic service delivery that entrenches resilience and sustainability of the services rendered to the affected children and their care givers affected by multiple shocks. The intervention will be guided by the IASC guidelines for child protection ,national SOPs and cluster strategy.The response is strategic to the attainment of Cluster objectives and align to activities, indicators, and outcomes of the HRP 2021.The project activities focus on mitigating child protection risks, preventing and responding to child protection cases through; CFS programming, capacity building of frontline service providers on CPiE, Case management including FTR services , Structured/non-structured PSS, community Outreach and dialogues, and safe referrals. It is expected that by the end of the project period, lives of vulnerable women and girls,young people and their caregivers will be saved, they will have increased access to multi sector services , life skills and positive coping mechanisms entrenched in them.

1 - Needs

According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021 54% of children account for the 8.3M people in need of humanitarian Assistance in South Sudan. The situation for children has deteriorated over 2020, with continued incidents of recruitment, abuse, exploitation and other grave violations. Over 26,848 children (12,506 girls and 14,342 boys) have been registered as unaccompanied, separated or missing as per recent UNICEF report 12th February 2021. According to IRNA assessment of Akobo county conducted in October 2020, sub-national violence and floods among communities of Akobo and Pibor counties has led to displacement of children and their caregivers with over 166 cases of abduction of children ( boys and girls)reported. Akobo and Pibor are in IPC 5 status which further complicates accessibility of child protection services with increased child labour cases and transactional sex among young girls. In Yei and Lainya counties, community displacement is very intensive due to a combination of insecurity and food insecurity; cattle raiding and revenge killings are, respectively, the main problem and criminal offence recorded in the county; The situation has further been exacerbated by COVID 19. According to OCHA’s humanitarian Snapshot of January 2021 , Clashes between an organized armed group and cattle keepers in Lainya County, Central Equatoria, displaced more than 9,400 people. Episcopal church of South Sudan in Lainya reported a total of 32 UASC as a result of the displacement with several cases of sexual violence against women and young girls. Measures adopted to curb the spread of COVID-19 like lockdowns and closure of schools have contributed to a rise in sexual violence incidents affecting children. Social structures and family bonds are breaking; children are separated from their families, and they have very limited access to basic services, which hinders their physical and mental development. IRNA assessment for October 2020 on Akobo county indicated massive abduction, revenge killings and forced early marriage of girls attributed to subnational violence and revenge attacks among Pibor and Akobo communities . Adolescent girls are particularly vulnerable to multiple forms of violence and have limited support systems and access to information. The sub national conflicts have led to displacement, children being abandoned or encouraged to live alone. Reach Assessment of January 2021 in central Equatoria indicates that the Rebel Militia Group in Yei continue to cause insecurity ,displacement and fear. In total, it is estimated that 300 UASC children will need Comprehensive Case Management and additionally 80 children including OVC’s, children with disability and children with mental health disorders will need individual protection assistance in Lainya. Boys are at high risk of recruitment into armed groups and girls are exposed to sexual abuse. More so the proximity of cantonment sites and the use of children to obtain food can amplify the risks, including eminent threats to their lives through recruitment into armed forces, abduction, rape and other assault.About 3600 caregivers and foster-care parents will also need training on appropriate and culturally acceptable child care practices. CIDO intends to coordinate with other sectoral partners like GBV, Health, Nutrition and Education to provide integrated basic services to children. By establishing community child protection and support groups for prevention and support of children with long-term psychosocial needs, about 160 community child protection committee members will need training on age appropriate PSS. For successful implementation of planned activities, there is need for the establishment of 4 more community child friendly spaces using local available materials in Yei, Lainya, Pibor and Akobo Counties.

7.4. Community Engagement Strategy

Yes

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During AAP sessions , CIDO team and committee on AAP and GBV will provide information on PSEA to all beneficiaries including how to identify and report PSEA cases. The beneficiaries will be made aware that vendors of CVI, CIDO contractors, CIDO staff and volunteers are all required to sign codes of conduct and commit to protect beneficiaries against sexual exploitation and abuse. IEC materials on PSEA will be sourced from the Lead (UNFPA) and translated and distributed in project locations.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

CIDO will establish a Complaint and Feedback mechanism and establish Help desks. There will be Community communication and engagement (CCE) before the project begins to harmonise the expectations of the community in regards to the project activities vs the available services. During CCE , consultations will be made with communities to establish committees which will oversee the GBV issues and AAP issues. The committee on AAP will ensure that voices of the most vulnerable including children are heard .The team will be careful to provide opportunity to caregivers living with disability, women and girls to air their views through separate FGDs in local languages and ensure that they are included in various leadership roles like being part of the complaint committees.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community Initiative for Development Organization (CIDO) is committed to ensuring that frontline staff engage in timely and proper Community Communication and Engagement (CCE) while being guided by the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) guidelines all through the project implementation. CIDO will conduct entry and exit meetings prior to the end of the project implementation to have discussions with not only community leaders but also directly with all targeted beneficiaries who are women, men girls and boys to capture their interests and recommendations and asses the vulnerability. In addition, CIDO will conduct awareness sessions on AAP rights and how to use the established Complaint and feedback mechanisms (CFMs) to childrena nd their caregivers. The AAP committee composed of community members representing men, women, elderly, youth , people with disability will be established to ensure communities rights and dignity is respected by all stakeholders.There will be an open-door policy, complaints, response, and feedback mechanism (CRFM) boxes, hotline and CRFM desks set up at each distribution site where beneficiaries will be given opportunity to air their feedback to the project team through using both leaders and open-door policy. The hotline will be linked directly to the management. This will facilitate Communication, transparency, and Community full engagement.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

CIDO team will compile feedback from the commitees, children and their caregivers and present to the management in Juba who will confirm the grievances and compliments . Feedback on how the complaints have been addressed on a daily basis on ground will also be shared with the management and will be used to define further actions to improve service delivery. There will be project review meetings where project staff and management will analyse the usefulness of the CRFM , information gathered and how the system can be made better to encourage all beneficiaries to use it freely. Feedback from beneficiaries will be used to improve programming and adjust weaker areas accordingly.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

CIDO will ensure all staff are trained on data management and security. The management will provide lockable safe boxes in all field locations for keeping confidential files and encourage the use of passwords to protect soft copies of data.HLP matters (e.g., rental/lease agreements). CIDO will also consult with all groups within affected populations to identify most effective and appropriate methods of awareness-raising. CIDO will be guided by the cluster safety audit while mainstreaming protection, conflict sensitivity and AAP. CIDO in close collaboration with Chiefs, AAP committees and Protection partners will conduct HLP messaging while at the same time addressing existing and emerging HLP issues throughout project implementation in line with the cluster humanitarian shelter and land rights due diligence guidelines for S/NFI actors in South Sudan to ensure smooth intervention. Community engagement and communication including with the local leadership in all aspects of the project will mitigate external interference likely to spark conflict. CIDO will adhere to principals of protection and the Do no Harm policy while engaging with all stakeholders .CIDO will work closely with county security organs and local authority to prioritize safety of staff, lives and property of community members through local dialogues and peaceful negotiations. To promote beneficiary access to CRFM mechanisms, CIDO Project team will use Community Communication and Engagement (CCE) strategies while being guided by the Accountability to the Affected Population (AAP) guidelines all through the project implementation. CIDO will put in place AAP committees composed of young people and their caregivers in all project locations to monitor and promote rights of beneficiaries as far as access to quality services is concerned.To mitigate the risk of explosive hazards in the project locations, CIDO will coordinate with Mine Action partners through sharing data in subnational and national cluster meetings and use their expert recommendations to advise on safety of both beneficiaries and staff.

12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

CIDO team will coordinate with other protection and sectoral partners to ensure beneficiaries are aware of the services available and the referral system/pathways. Notably referral systems and frameworks contain vital information on Health, Psychosocial support/Mental Health, Legal, livelihood, safety etc and contacts of service providers. Joint activities will be carried out with sectoral partners in all project locations which will include safety audits, dissemination of referral pathways and outreach messages conducted at payam level. CIDO staff will be trained on safe reporting of GBV cases and will be provided with GBV pocket guides for non-GBV actors to equip them with basic information for identifying and assisting GBV survivors. Women facing HLP issues will be assisted and referred to HLP partners and legal actors for further support.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

This will be achieved through coordination with other sectoral partners to integrate disaster risk mitigation strategies known to cause displacements and aggravates food insecurity like conflict and floods. CIDO will coordinate capacity building activities on life-skills, coping strategies and durable solutions with partners while promoting actionable risk mitigation strategies like building flood control structures (dykes), conflict sensitivity and promoting peaceful co-existence among communities with active involvement of young people and their caregivers as stakeholders This project will coordinate with FSL partners to conduct joint distributions of fishing kits, Survival kits and essential NFIs with clear messages on durable solutions. CIDO will refer women and girls for livelihood support to the FSL partners.In addition, CIDO will conduct joint outreach messages with other sectoral partners on community led disaster risk mitigation , early warning and response mechanisms and protection of livelihoods from loss.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The cluster , ICCG and OCHA encourages joint assessments with other partners and sectors and the cluster /project assessment tools capture the needs of beneficiaries based on gender, age and disability. CIDO will participate in Joint interventions with WASH, FSL, Health and Nutrition to provide cross-sectoral basic services . The project team will use the existing referral pathways to refer beneficiaries to other sectoral services in circumstances where the services cannot be provided. In addition, the project will make use of gender analysis reports to adjust activities accordingly and meet the needs of both men and women, boys and girls in dignity and safety.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project will conduct Joint assessments and joint outreach campaigns with other sectoral partners in Health, Nutrition and WASH to provide clear messaging on the linkages of child protection services i.e caregivers sessions, to reducing malnutrition among children under 5 years. The activities will also allow communities to provide useful feedback to be used by partners in advocacy for nutrition.•CIDO team will make use of the referral system and ensure that malnourished children who accompany their mothers to care giver sessions are referred appropriately for services.
