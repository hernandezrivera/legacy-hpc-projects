Project Summary

In response to the ongoing food insecurity crisis, Save the Children proposes a livelihood based emergency project in response to the ongoing conflict and its associated impacts in eleven counties, in four States of South Sudan. The proposed action aims at saving lives, addressing the immediate and medium-term needs and improving access to improved agricultures and livestock services, therefore improving the food and nutrition security of the conflict affected communities and increasing their resilience to famine and disaster risks and shocks mainly intercommunal violence and flooding.
The project targets primarily conflict and flood affected children and their families in the internally displaced people (IDPs), returnees and host communities. The key interventions proposed through this action are food assistances, cash and voucher based assistances as part of rapid responses; and agricultural and fishing inputs provisions and productions with training packages on improved agronomic practices including improved kitchen gardening; and livestock vaccinations and treatment services against diseases greatest economic importance.
The design of the proposed project is informed by findings of the multi-partner and multisectoral assessments, official appeals of the government, security and risk analysis and recommendations which have been conducted and released in the last six months.
The proposed emergency response is also developed in line with Cluster Objectives (CO1, CO2 and CO3) of FSL Cluster and to contribute to the HRP 2021 Strategic Objectives (SO1 and SO2). The Action aims at reaching out to 349,800 people, which fall under IPC 5, 4 and 3 in four States of South Sudan, more specifically Northern Bahr El Ghazal (Aweil East), Jonglei (Akobo, Nyirol, Bor South), and Eastern Equatoria (Lafon, Budi, Torit, Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Magdwi) and Upper Nile (Maban). The proposed counties are selected based on food insecurity and malnutrition status, SCI’s operational presence and experiences in the areas, good partnership and potential integration with ongoing multi-sectoral programs in the areas.

1 - Needs

Driven by intercommunal and armed conflicts and associated displacements, South Sudanese face the high Driven by intercommunal and armed conflicts and associated displacements, South Sudanese face the high levels of acute food insecurity in the country. The crisis is further exacerbated by flooding, low crop production, economic crisis, effects of COVID-19, limited access to basic services, and the cumulative effects of prolonged years of asset depletion and loss of livelihoods. The continuing currency devaluation and impacts on commodity prices that resulted in 40% inflation for year-on-year (WFP/VAM, March 2020); and incomes coupled with the effects of COVID-19 continue to erode the purchasing power of vulnerable households who are reliant on market purchases for their food and other basic needs. Seasonal scarcity of food, the flooding in 2020, and the static levels of humanitarian food assistance in the face of increased needs, resulted in an increase of acute food insecurity during the projection periods. The combined effect of these drivers have resulted in the displacement of roughly 3.5 million people in South Sudan, of whom 1.5 million are internally displaced people (IDPs), while over 2 million are refugees in neighboring countries. While this hardship reaches all communities, IDPs as well as refugees returning from countries of asylum are particularly vulnerable to severe food insecurity. The Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO 2021) indicates that over 93% of South Sudanese are facing food insecurity i.e people in need by sector out of total people in need. In addition to this, the South Sudan IPC November 2020 report shows that 6.48 million people (approx. 53% of the total population) would face severe food insecurity countrywide and in need of humanitarian assistances. In the first projection (December to March 2021) approx. 5.89 million (48%) in 67 counties; and in the second projection period (April to July 2021) approx. 7.24 million people (60%) will likely be facing the same situations in 72 counties. Over 24,000 people are reported in catastrophe situations (IPC 5) in six counties. The same report indicates that 1,392,259 children (6-59 months) are acutely malnourished; and 483,382 Pregnant or lactating women acutely malnourished. Escalated incidents of inter-communal violence from early to mid-2020 with significant impacts were reported mainly in Jonglei, Lakes, Warrap and Unity that led to displacement of over 75,000 people. An analysis by Conflict Location and Event Data (ACLED) for the period January to July 2020 2020 shows that incidents were reported frequently in Jonglei and counties in its adjacent States Related to the incident of flooding, UNOCHA (October 2020) highlights that South Sudan has experienced unusually heavy rainfall and flooding since July 2019, affecting an estimated 856,000 people; 400,000 people displaced and in need of immediate humanitarian assistances. This includes internally displaced people, refugees and their host communities across some 37 counties in Jonglei, Upper Nile, Lakes, Unity and Central Equatoria. (Source: UNOCHA, December 2020) Per UNICEF Gender-Based Violence Report released in December 2019, GBV ranked as the most critical threat to the protection and wellbeing of women and children in the country. 65% of women and girls have suffered physical and/or sexual violence. Child marriage and child abuse remain high as a result of conflict, poverty, and harmful social norms. Despite their roles mainly in agriculture compared to men, women have limited power in controlling and making decisions on their own produces and incomes from the sector. Thus, food security and livelihoods emergency responses are proposed in SCIs impact and newly targeted areas aiming at addressing the immediate life-saving, short and medium term needs of the community at the same time increasing the coping mechanisms of 58,300 households (349800 people) in those priority locations.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

This modality is chosen because of the comparative advantages. In addition to its flexibility, cash transfer is relatively cheaper and quicker than other modality in meeting immediate needs such as livelihood support. Cash assistance can also help in reducing the amount of aid that is wasted, and thus the resources used to produce, transport and distribute the aid.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

PSEA is among the cross cutting issues of the Action. awareness on PSEA and the roles and responsibilities of the stakeholders will be provided during the various meetings - inception, training session to monitoring. The awareness will involve the different sections of the community that takes into account age, sex, vulnerability and social status.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Save the Children will arrange a different option that aim at reaching out to people with disabilities and individuals who are literate women and girls such as:- For PWD, who can read, develop brochures about the project in their local languages- Develop aid materials (pictorial) that consider local culture- Organize visits to villages where they can be meet considering COVID-19 preventive measures and brief about the project which include objective, interventions or entitlement, targeting or selection criteria, complaint feedback arrangements. - In areas where it is not possible to reach directly to those sections of the community, Save the Children will use their representatives (including family members) to get their feedback and convey messages. - Save the Children will also use local community radio for the dissemination of key messages about our responses and also organize live talk shows for the audiences including PWD and illiterate to participate or provide their view. - Awareness training will be provided to Accountability to Affected Population Committee (AAPs) to improve their knowledge and commitment in including PWD and others wo are disadvantaged during information sharing and receiving their feedback for considerations.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Save the Children will facilitate kick off and project inception meetings at the project sites level. It involves project team, local authorities, community representatives and target beneficiaries. Beneficiary entitlements, work plans and budgets and reports will be shared with key stakeholders on a regular basis. A two-way feedback system will be established to reinforce this practice and encourages flow of information between the beneficiaries and the organisation. A stakeholders’ project inception workshop will be organised to enlighten the stakeholders, including the beneficiaries, about the project and the scope of activities to be implemented and how they can participate in the project.The project will facilitate post-distribution, post harvest and monitoring that invlove the different categories of target beneficiaries. The feedback will be used to improve the gaps and beneficiaries will be provided with the actions taken and resolutions for further follow up.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

SC will conduct community sensitization and information sharing sessions on project activities and explore ways of strengthening or setting up joint complaint and feedback mechanisms to enhance accountability and community participation throughout the project period. Feedback from the community and stakeholders would also be collected during needs identifications, KAP surveys, assessments and meetings with beneficiaries. The target groups will be representation from girls, boys, women and men, together with community and government leaders and community structures from onset of implementation, to ensure communities are well informed about the project and getting assistances considering their safety and consultation with identified individual needs and vulnerabilities. Any complaints from the beneficiaries about the project activities will be collected and feedback will be given by the Boma level project management committees. The Action will take actions in line with the agreed resolutions or adapting the responses in line with feedback from the beneficiaries. In addition to existing experiences, we will work with the communities to determine the most appropriate and suitable complaint response mechanism (CRM) in order to facilitate beneficiary complaint/feedback collection, complaints handling and response mechanism.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The proposed action will contribute to the decrease in the number of populations facing IPC 5, 4 and 3 through providing combinations of assistances and services : --The livelihoods support will help target beneficiaries in meeting lifesaving and immediate food needs and also contributing to agriculture productions and livelihoods diversifications and building disaster management capacity of vulnerable communities. - The improved agricultural and livestock productions can increase the availability and affordability of nutritious food and hence improve dietary diversity to women and children. - Combinations of food security and livelihoods support will help the target households to improve their food and nutrition status, build resilience and reduce vulnerability to malnutrition and other shocks.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

Given its vast geographical presence and implementation of multi-sectoral projects, Save the Children will work with the local authorities and communities making sure that a given household is receiving one or more types of sectoral assistances to maximize impact. More focus will be given for sectoral integrations and complementarity where the response will make sure that beneficiaries benefiting from other sectoral interventions are also linked to livelihood assistances as additional support to improve their resilience to shocks including conflict and flooding. Towards ensuring equitable access to services, Save the Children will use the Guideline for Multi-sector integration, which helps the different sector experts to work together during needs assessment, developing joint targeting criteria, joint work plan, case referrals for additional support form other sectors, coordination, monitoring and review of project activities.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The proposed intervention area is known for a high prevalence of severe acute malnutrition. The project design recognizes that the causes of malnutrition are multi-sectoral. To decrease its prevalence and associated impacts of malnutrition in children under five, the project will implement nutrition-sensitive agriculture to maximize the positive impact of the food system on nutrition outcomes and place a nutrition lens on the food and agricultural sector, without detracting from agriculture sector goals, which focus on increasing production, improving income, and reducing malnutrition. This will be materialized through supporting beneficiaries in the productions of nutritious foods (variety of vegetables) that are diverse and available for their families mainly children. This will be ensured by providing vegetable seeds (6-8 types), grow and used for household consumptions. Awareness training will be provided to women and adolescent girls that focus on vegetable gardening, management, harvest, post-harvest and storages techniques, cooking and feeding practices. Informed by assessment of the local knowledge and attitude practices and gap analysis, our nutrition experts will support towards ensuring a positive social and behavioural changes in food habits, diversifying nutritious food productions, feeding for children and pregnant or lactating women. We will use mother to mother support groups (MtMSGs) and community nutrition volunteers to develop positive attitudes and behavioural changes. During those trainings, messages on nutrition and hygiene promotion are among the key topics to be disseminated, which include dietary choices, efficient cooking methods, food preparations and child feeding practices.
