Project Summary

The WASH services in Gaza Strip have deteriorated significantly because of the lack of effective maintenance and overhaul of the ageing WASH infrastructure due to the prolonged blockade and repeated escalation of hostilities. This has caused major interruption in the delivery of WASH services across the strip. Consequently, some 800,000 people experience major limitation to their access to basic water supply. Furthermore, over 60% of the sewage generated in Gaza was discharged into the sea untreated on partially treated. Overflow of sewage treatment and pumping stations also resulted into the flooding of some communities with sewage, causing contamination of water sources and the environment, with major public health concerns.
In Gaza, more than 93 per cent of households are connected to the domestic water network, but only 3% use it for drinking. And 96.4 per cent of water resources are contaminated in terms of chloride and nitrate content. Households refer to private vendors for the provision of drinking water, while, in the Access Restricted Areas in Gaza, boys and girls fetch water from the closest desalination plant or public and free filling points. Gazans have limited access to adequate WASH facilities severely impacted during the 2021 escalation. Wastewater treatment plants functioned at a reduced capacity due to lack of fuel. For women and girls, limited access to WASH negatively impacts their health, including menstrual health and hygiene, household chores, and school attendance.
Access to sanitation services especially outside urban areas remains a major challenge both in terms of coverage and quality of service especially for women and girls and people with disabilities.
UNICEF and its partners is planning to address the urgent needs of the population of the Gaza Strip as outlined in the humanitarian needs overview for 2023. UNICEF and partners will focus on increasing access to WASH services among the most vulnerable communities and institutions through the restoration, sustaining and expansion of coverage to reach the most vulnerable populations with a clear multisectoral approach and build on the humanitarian-development nexus. This project will ensure that the most vulnerable women, boys and girls, and men have sufficient domestic and drinking water storage capacity whenever it becomes available and have better access to safe water and sanitation facilities and services. UNICEF and its partners will also address the critical gaps in humanitarian needs, especially targeting female headed households, including widows. UNICEF also discussed with its partners the importance to have the voice of the targeted community heard, including the ability to file a complaintt. For that, UNICEF is strengthening Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) in its programs by systematizing commitments in agreements with all implementing partners. As part of the UNICEF framework on Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and for the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) the office had established two complaints mechanisms and SOP have been developed to handle the complaints.
This project therefore aims at Improving access to basic lifesaving WASH services for 750,000 boys and girls, men and women especially among vulnerable communities in the Gaza Strip and considering the need of provision of chlorine and chemical to all water facilities in Gaza . The project will address the negative coping strategies employed by vulnerable people, such as reducing the frequency and duration of handwashing, sharing sanitation facilities, and overcrowding at public water points, due to limited or lack of access to water supply and sanitation facilities, engaged in resulting from the lack of access to WASH services . These coping strategies undermine infection prevention and lead to increased public health and social economic consequences coordinated with other key areas such child protection, health and education

1 - Needs

Recent WASH Cluster partners’ assessments and consultations for 2022, including the Gaza Strip flooding hot spots, WASH in schools' vulnerability mapping, WASH in HCFs Vulnerability mapping, and WASH vulnerability mapping identified four main key issues generating people’s need for WASH assistance: insufficient water supply to households, poor sanitation and solid waste services, limited WASH services to public facilities, and risk of flooding. The continuous materials restrictions applied on WASH infrastructure in the Gaza Strip, has worsened the already fragile, inadequate and poorly maintained system due to the prolonged blockade and restriction of access to essential operation and maintenance components. Significant efforts are being made by WASH sector partners to support Palestinian Water Authority (PWA) and the Coastal Municipalities Water Utilities (CMWU) with the expensive operation and maintenance (O&M) of WASH utilities. For example, the WASH Cluster is working on an O&M strategy with the first product being a WASH infographic on O&M. This product will be used as an advocacy tool to highlight the actual gaps and costs in the WASH sector. In coordination with relevant authorities and cooperation with the Palestinian Water Authority and Coastal Municipalities Water Utility, the WASH Cluster in Gaza prepared several vulnerability mappings in 2022 for WASH; this includes the vulnerability mapping for WASH in institutions for both schools and Health Care facilities, in addition to identifying the flooding hot spots and sharing the vulnerability mapping for WASH public as well. The mappings reflected the current situation in schools and health care centres and presented the WASH needs for communities. As a result of shared assessments and mappings, UNICEF with its implementing partners in coordination with Ministry of Education and Ministry of Health will address the WASH facilities needs in the targeted schools and Health care facilities and strengthen the emergency preparedness for the WASH facilities in the health centres through contingency planning and prepositioning supplies. The WASH Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023 and MSNA results identified 2022 key WASH findings for water and sanitation and flooding impact, UNICEF through its partners, who conducted separate focus male and female group discussions and technical assessments across the Gaza Strip, proved that the demands at the household level are still very high, and the flooding mitigation measures are urgently required. Therefore, urgent interventions to improve WASH facilities at the households’ level, including rehabilitation of latrines and provision of water tanks and hygiene kits, considering the needs of PWDs, are required. Many households who have members with disabilities in the family lack the financial ability to afford hygiene materials to maintain personal hygiene and house cleanness and sufficient storage capacity for water during the interruption of water supply and shortage of power. In addition, stormwater interventions should be completed to mitigate the flooding risk in the hot spot areas. All the assessments and focus groups conducted over the past two years took into consideration the specific gender needs of men, women, boys and girls and PWDs with disaggregated data collection. The discussions with various target groups, predominantly girls and women, in different localities estimated that families spend more than 20% of their income to access water. Women and girls identified access to hygiene products, including menstrual hygiene management, as a major financial burden on the head of the family, and when the family has PWDs, the burden increases. In most cases, they have resorted to negative and unhygienic coping strategies to cut costs. The use of shady and unhygienic sanitation facilities in schools was also noted as one of the obstacles to keep girls and children with disabilities in schools.

2 - Activities

In collaboration with the Palestinian Water Authority, Coastal Municipalities Water Utility, Ministry of Education and Ministry of Health UNICEF will work to implement the following activities in Gaza to support:1. Improve access to critical sanitation and hygiene at household level:i. UNICEF will work with the CMWU and partners to install new and extend existing wastewater networks, connecting households to the municipality networks, to address the urgent needs of lack of access to improved sanitation in the AlDaraj, Maan, Shajaeya, Khan Younis City, and Shiekh Radwan communities in the Gaza Strip. In Gaza. UNICEF will install an estimated 6,000m of sewer network of various pipe diameters, fittings, and maintenance access structures. UNICEF will also undertake the decommissioning of existing septic tanks that are being used in the target communities and have been identified as the major source of environmental pollution and water contamination due to their recurrent overflow and leakage.ii. UNICEF, in partnership AAH and OXFAM will undertake the rehabilitation of household sanitation facilities for vulnerable households. The rehabilitation includes the connection to the nearest sewer line, internal plumbing, and internal structural modification to ensure accessibility, privacy, and ease of use considering disability friendly facilities for the 180 households that include a family member with physical disabilities (including the aged). iii. To alleviate the significant financial burden experienced by women and girls for accessing hygiene products and prevent negative coping strategies related to the access and use of these products, UNICEF and its partners will undertake the delivery of family hygiene kits to the most vulnerable 1,500 identified households across the Gaza Strip selected through households' assessment. These comprehensive family hygiene kits that include dignity and menstrual hygiene management products to meet the needs of a household for one month will be provided to the community through e-voucher modality, allowing beneficiaries to decide when and how to redeem the vouchers.2. To complement the above three sanitation improvement activities, UNICEF and partners will also undertake social and behavior change mobilization and hygiene promotion activities in targeted localities, to improve hygiene practices in the communities. Focus will be on infection prevention related to poor sanitation and other WASH related infections. As part of the sensitization, the various feedback and complaint mechanisms and awareness of PSAE will be integrated in the activities. 3. Undertake flood prevention and response measures in identified key flood-prone communities:UNICEF, in partnership with CMWU, will undertake the construction of storm drainage network in the flood prone areas of Alshaykh Nasir Qa' al Qurein in the Khan Younis Governorate. This activity intends to address the recurrent flooding problems faced by an estimated 20,000 residents in. the community. The activity will include the installation of 840m sewer network and all the required connection and maintenance fittings, and connection of community depressions to the storm drainage system. 4. Improve access to critical WASH services in Schools and Health Care Facilities.i. In partnership with AAH, OXFAM, MAAN, and We-World GVC will undertake the rehabilitation of critical WASH facilities in five Health Care Facilities (HCFs) to improve access to safely managed sanitation for both staff and facility beneficiaries. This activity will include, the installation of water storage tanks, water cisterns, handwashing stations, latrines, and sewer connections in Moghraqa, Jort AlLout, Al Horeya, and Shohada Al Daraj health centres and Al Dorra Hospital. The activity will also include the modification of existing sanitation facilities to improve accessibility, privacy, hygiene and operation and maintenance. ii. UNICEF and partners will also undertake the rehabilitation of WASH facilities in ten schools to improve access to safely managed sanitation for 6,592 school boys and girls. This activity will include, the installation of water storage tanks, water cisterns, handwashing stations, latrines, and sewer connections. The ten schools will benefit from hygiene awareness campaigns. The activity will also include the modification of existing sanitation facilities to improve accessibility, privacy, hygiene and operation and maintenance, and to decrease the obstacles to keep girls at schools.iii. To complement the rehabilitation interventions, UNICEF and partners will also undertake School Sanitation and Hygiene Education (SSHE) in the targeted schools. The SSHE activity will include training of school pupils in hygiene promotion, social and behaviour change promotion and hand hygiene. The trained pupils are mobilized to conduct hygiene promotion activities among peers in targeted schools. 5. Operation and maintenance of critical WASH facilities In continuation to its operation and maintenance support to the WASH sector in Gaza, UNICEF will support the PWA and CMWU with the provision of essential water production and treatment chemicals for the three major desalination plants and water production wells across Gaza. Under this activity, UNICEF will work with the CMWU and PWA to identify gaps in chemical needs for Gaza and provide the requisite monthly quantities to ensure uninterruptable water supply in the Gaza Strip. As an initial support, UNICEF will provide 1,000 m3 of chlorine through its internal long-term procurement agreement. For the rest of the chemicals, procurement and delivery will be based on identified needs; this is necessary to prevent overstocking of chemicals with very limited shelf-life.UNICEF will also support the utility with the provision of critical operation and maintenance spares, materials and tools for both water and sanitation facilities as part of the O&M strategy adapted by the WASH cluster.

3 - Indicators

UNICEF and its implementing partners will supervise the projects daily to ensure timely and effective implementation. As part of the hygiene awareness campaigns, women and men’s participation in workshops will strengthen social relations between family members and unify them in their efforts to assure their long-term water and sanitation needs are met. Focus group discussions will ensure separation of women and men to create an open environment where women are free to discuss key issues. A detailed survey, field visits and water analysis will be conducted to supervise, monitor, and evaluate the activities. A Knowledge, Attitudes and Practices (KAP) survey will be conducted to assess and evaluate the impacts of the activities on women, men and boys, girls, persons with disabilities and the elderly as part of the household’s activities. UNICEF and its implementing partners will contribute to the exchange of information/lessons learned, fact sheets and stories among the cluster community, which are to be used for advocacy purposes. The monitoring procedures include regular programme visits to the field where implementation progress, achievements of results and means of verification are systematically checked against the agreed framework including the projects indicators. Recommendations in response to the field visit findings, including bottlenecks are documented and systematically followed up on. Project activities will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation, and the results will be communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneficiaries. UNICEF has expanded support to the SAWA hotline to be able to handle SEA claims at the interagency level, which includes referral of claims among agencies.
