Project Summary

UNHCR’s 2019 education project complements the Ministry of Education and Education partners’ efforts to strengthen the capacity of education system, increase safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis affected and returnee children aged 5-17 years and improve the quality of formal and non- formal education of children ages 5- 17 years within protective education environment.
UNHCR’s activities will adhere to needs based approach to programming to implement its rehabilitation of schools and capacity building of teachers’ activities in areas with high education severity scales targeting the most disadvantaged and crisis affected population. To this end, UNHCR will rehabilitate 77 damaged schools in 47 most crisis affected locations or in locations with increased trend and / or potential areas for refugee returnees. The rehabilitation activities will include repairing of damaged classrooms and WASH facilities that is gender sensitive and disability friendly that would ensure protection needs of boys and girls as well as needs of children with disabilities allowing adequate access to the school premises and WASH facilities. In addition UNHCR through this project will provide capacity building for 3000 teaching staff and education personnel on child – centered social counselling, non- violent communication, behavior modification, active learning and other life skills to redress psychosocial needs and / or other special needs of children in schools.
UNHCR contributes to the future adolescents and youth populations that are able to contribute to resilience and social cohesion in their communities as the result of provisions of education opportunities through access to school undertaken in this project programme and thus empowering of adolescent girls and boys to inspire and lead changes in their communities. 107, 800 children and 3000 teachers will directly be targeted through this project.
Accountability to affected people in the project will be recognized through participatory engagement of communities and outreach programmes conducted through community volunteers’ network. In addition, UNHCR has in place a CwC strategy and SOP to engaging communities on their needs and planning for interventions

Needs

As per the HRP 2019, 2.1 million children are reported to be out-of-school and 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Most of out of school children are at primary school age and/or in hard-to reach and besieged locations. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labour. The escalation of violence has led to increasing war related psychological trauma for children, their families and education personnel. Exposure to violence decreases attendance, leads to drop out and affects learning outcomes. In some areas, over 50% of children with disability have an education related unmet needs. The high proportion of crisis-affected children and adolescents out of the public education systems is of high concern since they are at bigger protection risks situations and / or in the long term miss out important opportunities to build their future. During the eight years of crises over one in three schools were damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Consequently many children were forced to stay out of school or if managed to access schools, they were often exposed to unsafe and overcrowded classrooms. The destruction and damages of schools have led to an acute need for additional learning spaces for both host community children and internally displaced children residing in these areas. Teaching capacity remains overstretched with more than 150,000 teachers and education staff who have left their positions since the start of the crisis, the capacity and capability of available teachers are being severely over-stretched. These shortages have limited the quality and provision of education. The changes in control in central and south Syria in early and mid-2018 as well as increasing hostilities in northwest Syria in September 2018 have caused new waves of civilian displacement into Rural Damascus, Idleb and Aleppo. Existing education capacity in these areas was already limited prior to recent displacement influx. Schools in IDP/returnee receiving communities have limited absorption capacity for newly arriving students and services are usually insufficient or non-existent. However, despite that, the shift of control have opened some accredited education opportunities to school-age children in the newly accessible areas, in the Central and South West Syria. Parallel to this, the year 2018 experienced a flow of over 987, 216 self-organized returns of IDPs and refugees from neighboring countries to areas where some stability has been restored. However education systems in those areas of return are fragmented, school facilities are already overcrowded and poorly equipped and overburdened with multiple needs and limited resources to absorb the increasing numbers of returnee students. Therefore such shortfalls require redressal through education systems strengthening, school and classrooms rehabilitation, capacity building of teachers and facilitation for reintegration of refugee returnee children back to school through advocacy, coordination with the Ministry of Education to scale up access to education for both returnee and crisis affected children.

Implementation of activities

UNHCR will rehabilitate 77 damaged schools in 47 most affected locations and in locations with increased trend and / or potential areas for refugee returnees. The rehabilitation activities will include repairing of damaged classrooms and WASH facilities in a gender sensitive and disability friendly means to ensure protection needs of boys and girls as well as needs of children with disabilities allowing adequate access to the school premises and WASH facilities therein. The rehabilitation activities will be carried out through PUI, ADRA and SIF with which UNHCR has project partnership agreements in close consultations with UNHCR engineer staff members. Rehabilitation needs and related costs will be assessed and on the basis of which a targeted rehabilitation plan is designed and then implemented. In addition UNHCR through this project will provide capacity building for 3000 teaching staff and education personnel on child – centered social counselling, non- violent communication, behavior modification, active learning and other life skills offering teachers with knowledge and skills capacity to identify and redress psychosocial needs and / or other special needs for children at risk. As the result of the teachers’ capacity building training activities, 144,000 children will indirectly benefit from psychosocial support, social counselling and child center support

Protection Risk Analysis

1)Risk: Inaccessibility to education project sites due to lack of safety and insecurity including contamination with explosives and mines. Mitigation measure: Ensure flexible and context informed approach mechanisms to deliver project activities of school rehabilitation so that it could be implemented in a changing context and / or explosive cleared project sites. Ensure that relevant authorities are aware of and are engaged with the project and are committed to ensure basic safety/ access for project implementation and ensure that staff members, teachers and education personnel are trained in safety and security measures including in mine risk education. 2)Risk: Insufficient safety and health standards at schools Mitigation measure:Planning and on going communication with local education authorities, community leaders and school management resulting in clear agreements on every actors' responsibility to create and maintain a safe learning environment; regular monitoring to verify adherence to these agreements.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk:Threat of military offensives and disturbance in safety and security of some project area or changes of security situation restricting implementation. Mitigation measure:Ensure that projects on school rehabilitation in insecure locations have security plans for their staff and the project beneficiaries have sufficient resources to consent to those plans.
