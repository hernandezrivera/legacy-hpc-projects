Project Summary

(i) Provide effective coordination by strengthening area based coordination and leadership, reinforcing sub-national effectiveness and linkages between the various coordination and working group levels from ICCG in the capital and intersectoral working groups in the regions to HCT level engagements with the Government; (ii) Promote humanitarian access in a complex operating environment, where the security situation remains volatile; (iii) Strengthen civil-military coordination at the regions and capital levels to improve response preparedness, rapid and safe delivery of humanitarian assistance, and foster enhanced predictability of civil-military coordination processes and platforms; (iv) foster greater accountability of the humanitarian response, raising awareness of the crisis, promoting best practices and international law, and bringing the voices of affected people to the forefront; (v) support locally-led identification of strategic priorities through the Flagship project, promote HDP collaboration which will inform the multi-year Humanitarian Response Strategy framework (2023-2025) (vi) advocate for principled humanitarian action by relevant authorities and sharing its coordination expertise based on global best practices. This will include an enhanced interface and support to the Government of Niger, especially the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs; (vii) Enhance partnerships with existing and new humanitarian donors through improved bilateral and multilateral engagement; (viii) Increased accountability of the humanitarian system to meet humanitarian needs.

1 - Besoins

Niger faces a complex humanitarian crisis stemming from extreme climatic events, growing conflict-related violence, and socio-economic challenges. The security crisis arises from repeated attacks by non-State armed groups (NSAGs), mainly in the Lake Chad basin in the southern part bordering Nigeria, but also in the three-border region (Niger - Mali - Burkina Faso), where armed groups perpetrate attacks against security forces and civilians; the use of Improvised Explosive Device (IED) by the NSAGs remains also one of the major security constraints. Increased activities of NSAGs in the country and neighboring areas have led to severe protection concerns. More than 1,450 protection incidents were recorded from January to June 2024. While the majority concerns physical violence, kidnappings and extorsion, violence against children remains highly problematic. 2024 was also marked by the deterioration of the security situation in the regions of Tillabéri, Tahoua, Diffa and Dosso. the country has registered forced displacement of 887,000 people, in which 507,000 people are internally displaced. In addition, Niger has faced severe flooding that has impacted more than 1.5 million people across all regions with 396 deaths, and huge socio-economic losses (destruction of crops, animals and social infrastructure). Food insecurity is a long-standing issue affecting millions of people every year. According to the november 2024 Cadre Harmonisé, 2,2 million people will be food insecure (phases 3 and 4) during the lean season (June-August 2025) or 8 percent of the population. Furthermore, there are 412,412 severely malnourished children out of 1.7 million people affected by acute malnutrition according to Consumer Index Price (IPC in French) analysis of 2024. As a result of the insecurity , the precarious socio-economic situation is further aggravated. Access to health, education, and markets become more limited. In addition, the Benin border closure, and the impact of sanctions implemented by the CEDEAO lead to soaring food prices on the local market. The resilience of households to deal with such price fluctuations is limited. Therefore, loss of purchasing power is one of the biggest humanitarian risks in Niger.In 2025, 2.6 million people will be in need, compared to 4.6 million people in need in 2024. The decrease in the number of people in need in 2025 compared to last year 2024 would be linked to the limitation of the scope of the analysis, but not necessarily to the improvement in the humanitarian situation. With no immediate improvement foreseen soon, humanitarian needs will likely continue to increase.The humanitarian community continues to rely heavily on OCHA's coordination functions, primarily strategic coordination at the capital Niamey and operational coordination at regional levels, information management, access, and civil-military coordination. The challenging and volatile security situation limited this work in most areas in the country. In such a context, OCHA will have to : 1) respond to the humanitarian crisis and continue advocating for humanitarian coordination mechanisms to be adapted to ensure that they are fit for purpose to respond to the above challenges. OCHA will, through its main office in Niamey 2) support the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) and the Inter-Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG); 3) lead the planning process of need and humanitarian response plan, and continue to co-chairs working groups, e.g.MSCWG, CMCoord/access, AAP, localization, Gender, etc. 4) spearhead public information, advocacy efforts, humanitarian planning and resource mobilization. 5) support national and local capacities in coordinating humanitarian assistance and advocate for the greater involvement of development actors in addressing structural issues, 6) facilitate access of humanitarian workers to provide adequate and timely assistance and 7) strengthen local coordination in humanitarian hotspot communes.

le bureau OCHA s'engage à soutenir l'élaboration de stratégies nationales ou régionales de solutions durables qui nécessitera des analyses de lacunes et des capacités en place, des évaluations de besoins et d’intentions, et des efforts de suivi des personnes déplacées, ainsi que des analyses du contexte et des risques de protection des populations affectées par les déplacements forcés dans les zone d’accueil, de transit, d’origines, de retour et de réintégration afin d’assurer que leurs droits en matière de solutions durables inscrits dans les règles et principes de droit international et national soient respectés, et d'informer les initiatives de solutionsdurables en faveur des PDI et des réfugiés déplacés depuis plusieurs années. Le CTTNUD dont fait partie OCHA, fournira des efforts pour la mise en place des cadres de concertation dans les régions qui permettront aux acteurs régionaux de travailler de manière plus coordonnée non seulement au niveau de chaque secteur mais également au niveau multisectoriel.
