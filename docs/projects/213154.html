Project Summary

The number of people estimated to need humanitarian assistance across the country in 2023 has jumped from 15.8 million to around 25 million people following the clashes that erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in multiple areas of Sudan on 15 April. As of February 2024, the recent conflict in April had displaced more than 9 million people inside Sudan and 1.7 million people to neighbouring countries, mainly Chad, Egypt, Ethiopia, and South Sudan. Intercommunal clashes and violence in some areas of the country, mostly in Darfur and Kordofan regions, also contribute to the high numbers of internal and cross-border displacements.
This project seeks to ensure that Refugees in Gedaref and Kassala have access to relevant multi-sector humanitarian services and their basic needs met. The intervention has five major outputs, each one addressing the following humanitarian sectors: Shelter & Non-Food Items (NFI), Water, Sanitation & Hygiene (WASH), Food Nutrition Security (FNS), and Protection. Through widespread awareness campaigns, communities are being educated on general protection topics, empowering individuals to recognize and mitigate risks. Child-friendly spaces are being established and maintained, providing safe havens for children amidst adversity. Moreover, targeted assistance is being extended to children with disabilities, ensuring their care and well-being are prioritized.n combating gender-based violence (GBV), extensive community outreach initiatives are underway, coupled with capacity-building sessions to equip individuals with the knowledge and skills to combat GBV effectively. Addressing the vital aspect of livelihoods, agricultural support is being provided to bolster food security and economic resilience. Additionally, vocational and skills training programs are being tailored to empower youth, enhancing their prospects for sustainable livelihoods. Water and sanitation interventions are pivotal in ensuring access to clean water and hygienic facilities. From the installation of hand pumps to the rehabilitation of communal latrines, efforts are being made to enhance water access and promote hygiene practices within communities. Support for shelter and non-food items is also being extended, ensuring affected households receive essential supplies and assistance for housing. Furthermore, initiatives such as home gardening and solar lighting installations contribute to long-term sustainability and community resilience. Nutritional support and health capacity-building initiatives are integral components of the intervention. From micronutrient supplementation for children to counseling on infant and young child feeding for women, efforts are being made to address nutritional deficiencies and promote healthy lifestyles. Furthermore, the training and re-training of health staff enhance the provision of essential health services to communities in need.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

WHH'S programming aims to ensure protection mainstreaming by applying the following principles to its work: Centrality: Gender equality must be considered as an integral part of all Welthungerhilfe’s policies, programmes and projects. Equality: Equal access and opportunities for women and men to economic resources is essential to achieving sustainable livelihoods and development. Achieving gender equality does not mean that women become the same as men. Equality means that one's rights or opportunities do not depend on one’s gender. Women's social and economic empowerment is central to achieving gender equality. Equity: Achieving gender equality requires the recognition that every policy, programme and project undertaken by Welthungerhilfe affects women and men, girls and boys, and those of marginalised groups including those with disabilities, differently. Therefore, different (equitable) approaches must sometimes be taken with some groups to ensure equal outcomes. This may also be required to overcome historic and systemic oppressions faced by some groups. Intersectionality: Different groups and individuals may have different, intersecting needs and requirements based on their multiple characteristics including gender, disability, socio-economic background, ethnicity, religion and race. Representation: The gender-balanced and active participation and representation of women and men is a precondition for the success of Welthungerhilfe’s programming. Context-appropriateness: Interventions, strategies and programmes in support of gender equality must consider the specific political, economic and socio-cultural context in which they are designed to operate and must be informed by contextual understandings. Do No Harm: A ‘Do No Harm’ approach must be integral to all programming, with detailed risk assessment, monitoring and mitigation strategies developed to minimise the risk of backlash against women and girls for gender-responsive programming.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project is designed in a manner that humanitarian assistance is provided in line and in proportion to the needs identified as needed the PoCs, in adherence to UNHCR guidelines. Informed participation and voice of communities and affected people in decision making is central in the project assessment, design, implementation, and monitoring and is done via group via group consultations, group discussions, meetings with community leaders and individuale consultations. WHH will provide space for communities to shape their own recovery and for the project to better deliver on commitments to stakeholders, including the people WHH serves, and the resource used. WHH will ensure timely and transparent information sharing among stakeholders regarding the objective and activities of the project as well as involve the target in the beneficiary selection and verification process. Feedback channels not only respect the right of affected communities to have a say, but often also improve the efficiency and effectiveness of aid delivery. In line with WHH Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) policies and procedures, WHH will keep consult the beneficiaries in expanding an appropriate feedback and response mechanism to give every PoC the opportunity to identify, report and seek redress for program quality and protection concerns. The system address both sensitive and non-sensitive issues while upholding confidentially and non-retaliation principles.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

WHH is committed to international external and internal standards, codes and policies that affirm our commitment to accountability to our stakeholders and, in particular to the communities with whom we work. Our actions are framed by a set of international laws underpinning the rights of the people we aim to assist. In our commitment to continuously strengthen our accountability to our stakeholders, WHH is led by the nine commitments of the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS). Welthungerhilfe is a signatory to the CHS Alliance and is guided by an internal Accountability framework which ensures minimum standards for accountability in projects implemented by Welthungerhilfe and partners. Accountability, informed participation, and voice of communities and affected people in decision making is central in the project assessment, design, implementation, and monitoring.This includes as well sensitive issues like Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) by humanitarian or development workers. During the project period, WHH and partner staff will be trained and oriented further on PSEA. Examples of the format of each mechanism the project is considering include monthly feedback meetings between WHH/partner staff and community members, a phone number where communities can report sensitive issues, and community leadership committees. Specifically, WHH will link project beneficiaries with existing feedback mechanisms in the area for formats like: (1) a complaint desk, where beneficiaries can lodge a complaint and receive a response in a reasonable timeframe; and (2) Active outreach to project beneficiaries by the Accountability Officer, who will take findings from random beneficiary satisfaction visits to regular program management meetings, or (3) suggestion boxes to offer confidential feedback as appropriate or (4) The mail address for those who are able to share their concerns via a digital channel. Communities will be able to share their feedback and complaints about the quality and implementation of the project outputs and activities through a system for accountability, reporting, complaint, and feedback. Feedback received will be communicated with the participants, including the staff, and to all key stakeholders whose opinions were sought during the design of the project. WHH will ensure data privacy by strictly adhering to EU data protection laws and ensuring that the target group are informed of, understand, and approve of the purpose, scale and nature of the use of their data.
