Project Summary

As per the IPC 2018 classification Pibor is currently in emergency phase 4 and it is projected that from October to March of 2019, it will escalate to catastrophe phase 5. In May 2018 IMC conducted a pre harvest SMART survey in Pibor and the GAM was 20.7% which is above the emergency WHO thresh hold of 15%. Sharing of limited food and floods have exacerbated the already vulnerable food insecurity in the area
Deliver quality life-saving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at-risk under-five children, pregnant and lactating women: Under this cluster objective, HACT will establish 3 mobile sites in Nanam, Agoy and Mamok since they don’t have existing structures and 2 static sites Kasingor and Lotila PHCUs. In OTP service provision, 1,418 SAM children girls and boys 6 to 59 months will be treated (567 girls and 851 boys). For MAM TSFP cases, 2980 girls and boys 6 to 59 months will be treated (1986 girls and 2980 boys). For TSFP PLW cases 843 adolescent mothers and women will be treated.
Increased access to maternal infant and young child nutrition programs preventing under-nutrition for the most vulnerable and at risk: For BSFP MAM PLW cases HACT targets to treat 843 adolescent mothers and women and for BSFP MAM cases 6 to 59 months, HACT will treat 3,974 children boys and girls. IYCF will be an important component in HACT nutrition service delivery hence primary care givers consisting of adolescent and women PLWs will be targeted. M2MSGs will be formed and lead mothers will be identified/selected by the women. 50 groups will be formed and each group will consist of 20 women totalling to 1000 reached on IYCF messages. Kitchen gardens will be established with inclusion of cooking demonstrations at the site levels. 6,000 children (3,000 boys, 3,000 girls)boys and girls <5 will be provided with vitamin A and 6,000 (3,000 boys and 3,000 girls) boys and girls >1 will be provided with deworming
Increased access to integrated nutrition, health, WASH, FSL and protection interventions/responses in counties: HACT is currently implementing WASH programme in Pibor and will ensure that water treatment tablets are provided to the sites so as to have clean and safe water for the beneficiaries. Toilets will be ensured that they are functioning and supervision will be done in order to ensure that the facility workers clean them regularly. During OTP, TSFP, BSFP and MIYCN gatherings, WASH messages will be given to the beneficiaries including correct washing of the sachets and hands. Water sources that provide water to the nutrition sites and beneficiaries/community will be done water quality testing in order to ensure that there is availability of clean safe water and hence reduce water borne diseases. In collaboration with UNICEF/IOM WASH, HACT will provide soaps and jerry cans to the nutrition sites and beneficiaries in the community. Use of HACT Home Health Promoters to spread health, nutrition and WASH messages at house hols level.
HACT will ensure that all the SAM cases of children 6 to 59 months 1,418 are tested and treated for malaria. Access to safe and clean water is recognised hence HACT will ensure that all sites both static and mobile have water that it’s beneficiaries and staff can use. Due to prevalence of high child protection and GBV issues in the community, HACT will train 5 nutrition staff on SGBV and child protection so that they will be able to be the linkage of referral from the community to partners providing the services. Also women who bring their children for OTP, TSFP, BSFP services if found to be going through GBV or lost a child then the nutrition staff will be able to guide them in the right direction. Micro nutrient supplementation will also be monitored and reported in the ANC and delivery.

Needs

Four years of wide-spread conflict across South Sudan is eroding the capacity of households to meet their basic food needs. Even in the most-likely scenario, which assumes the delivery of assistance to 2.5-3.3 million people a month, many households will still face extreme food consumption gaps. This is due to the fact that assistance at current levels meets less than half of the estimated population in need. Most households lack access to sufficient food through other sources, given that local production is well below pre-crisis levels and extremely high prices are preventing households from purchasing even minimal amounts of basic staple foods.In a worst-case scenario of a persistent absence of food assistance would be likely because this would remove a primary food source and would likely drive increased levels of conflict over remaining scarce resources. In turn, higher levels of conflict would increase movement restrictions, preventing households from accessing food from other sources therefore food security can deteriorate rapidly among populations who face extreme movement restrictions. The 2017/18 Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission (CFSAM) reports production was below last year and the five-year average due to conflict-related disruptions, crops losses from Fall Armyworm, and the continued outflow of people from South Sudan.Dependence of the economy on oil production has depreciated the local currency to currently 200 SSP/USD from 5 SSP/USD before the war. As per the IPC 2018 classification Pibor is currently in emergency phase 4 and it is projected that from October to March of 2019, it will escalate to catastrophe phase 5. The driver of this deterioration is conflict, specifically cattle raiding, Intercommunal fighting and age group fighting for dominance. The dominate livelihood system in this county is pastoralism and cattle raiding and diseases are impacting access to food and income. Communal fighting also affects community access to health and nutrition services and at the same time causing mass human movement. In July of 2018, the Jiech community fought with the Murule over stolen cattle which lasted for a number of days and lead to casualties. The community then moved from Koradep to Kasingor which is a 24 hour walk to look for safety. These IDPs are not getting access to services as humanitarian organizations are not present in the area. They therefore have to walk long distances to get assistance. In May 2018 IMC conducted a SMART survey in Pibor and the GAM was 20.7% which is above the emergency thresh hold of 15%. Lack of enough food due to poor harvest last year and into 2018 due to floods, disease burden is also especially among children <5 has also contributed to the overall dire nutrition situation. IRNA report for Pibor April 2018 shows Cattle-raiding is a typical incident which normally create insecurity annually in the area especially during the dry season in grazing and water sources areas. However, the local authorities reported that this cattle raiding was a bit different as compared to the past because the attackers were organised and well-coordinated with confiscated weapons. During this attack, more cattle were taken, more people killed and boys and girls abducted. Community also reported that more properties were destroyed to extend you can even observe some undressed women and children. Rampant child abduction prevents mothers/caregivers to access health and nutrition services due to fear. Extreme poor WASH conditions in the area which include open defecation very low ownership of latrines at house hold level lead to waterborne diseases especially during the rainy season and all the dirt ends in the streams and rivers used by the communities. PLW,children girls and boys under 5 and the elderly caregivers are the most affected by the aforementioned conditions. Gender roles in the community also contribute, low levels of education among girls/women, lack of knowledge on their rights

Activities

1. Treatment of severe acute malnutrition among children 6-59 months Treatment of moderate acute malnutrition among children 6-59m months. This will be achieved through both at the health and community level. CNVs will be used to screen, identify and refer and at the static sites the nutrition assistants will treat those referred from the community and OPD. The mobile sites will treat those screened and or referred to by the CNVs. 2. Train health and nutrition care providers on management of acute malnutrition. This will be done in the HFs3. Provision of BSFP for under-fives and PLWs. This will be done within the static sites and also together with the mobile teams4. Provision of quality MIYCN and micronutrient supplementation services in nutrition sites and community level. M2MSGs will be formed and IYCF messages disseminated. micronutrient supplementation will be done both at the community and static sites. 5. Conduct nutrition SMART surveys in prioritized counties. This will be done countywide in collaboration with the state and CHD6. Test of malaria among children with SAM aged 0/6-59 months 7. Provide WASH NFIs to nutrition sites and community 8. Conduct sensitization to make raise community awareness of systems in place for representation of their different interests and needs.9. Establish a community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities by placing a suggestion boxes in the OTP/TSFP sites, initiating community focus group discussions with members of the community10. Provide training to the nutrition programme staff to understand how to serve best the beneficiaries using the QAAP approach.11. Raise awareness and provide information to beneficiaries on their rights and entitlement in the nutrition sites12. Involve the communities in design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation on the planed or on-going nutrition project

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Messages will be printed in the local language targeting women and children so they fully understand what it is about and feel that it is part of them. HACT will also work together with county authorities and partners on ground to curb this and also bring awareness about beneficiary rights. If cases are found, then HACT will follow the proper channels of referral.
