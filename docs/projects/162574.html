Project Summary

As part of Mercy Corps integrated multi-sector programming, the project will incorporate a WASH component into work in communities with displaced, specifically oriented around improved hygiene and nutrition behaviors and access to potable water.
Hygiene promotion activities and distribution of hygiene kits containing buckets and water containers and hygiene consumables such as soap and aquatabs will take place in all communities of intervention. We expect to provide WASH kits to approximately 500 households, which is a subset of the households to be reached through WASH hygiene promotion activities coupled with Nutrition activities. Hygiene/nutrition promotion activities on the other hand will take place in all targeted villages, and hygiene consumables (eg soap, aquatabs) will be distributed during these activities. A certain number of WASH NFI kits may also be distributed as a part of hygiene promotion activities, in line with good application of hygiene practices promoted.
For the water supply component, Mercy Corps will repair or construct up to 27 water points (with a target of 8 reservoirs earmarked for pastoral use).
This will include those that are dysfunctional and are at risk of contamination, including
pumping devices on wells and boreholes and the protection and/or repair of wells. Priority will
be given to those communities hosting displaced persons who have no current potable water
sources or sources which are overwhelmed by demand. To identify these water points, the project will consult with communities, and inspect water points for infrastructural deficiencies that could
lead to contamination of the water point. The project will also measure the flow and recharge rates of the water points and carry out basic water quality tests (bacterial contamination, pH, turbidity).
Rehabilitation and repair activities may include equipping wells or boreholes with the necessary
pumping system to prevent water source contamination and reduce time spent at the water point; installation of aprons and drainage systems to prevent runoff or seepage contamination;
installation of fences to keep animals away; or reparation or installation of impermeable lining to
prevent deposit accumulation or well collapse and reduce water source pollution and contamination. The program can also ensure well purification by scrubbing lining, rehabilitating the bottom gravel pack, and conducting dewatering and hyper-chlorination. Overall, the purpose
of rehabilitation activities is to increase access to safe drinking water for the beneficiary
population.
Assistance in the WASH sector will also center on needs of communities facing difficulty
maintaining hygiene and providing adequate and clean water to displaced and host
populations due to increased population pressure. Assistance offered to date by rapid
responders has been inadequate in this sector, so we will seek to provide emergency latrines
or latrine repair in areas with increased rates of open defecation, and water supply assistance
in areas where longer-term displaced persons are increasing pressure on the water supply.
Hygiene promotion activities will be implemented in communities receiving sanitation and
water infrastructure interventions.
The project takes a conflict sensitive approach, for example complementing the targeting of main IDP area of concentration ('primary villages') by also including 'secondary', non-IDP hosting villages should the social dynamics in the area indicate not doing so would contribute to intra-community tensions. There are also small community-level conflict mitigation trainings included in the project.
