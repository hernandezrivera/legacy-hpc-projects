Project Summary

The project aims in 2023 to continue providing health and nutrition assistance to Nigerian refugees in the refugee camp of Minawao and surrounding host communities in the Mayo Tsanaga Division of the Far North region. This includes an estimated 75,352 Nigerian refugees living in Minawao camp, other Nigerian refugee living out of the camp and the host population living around the Minawao camp without any distinction of age or sex. Intervention will focus on; increasing equitable and sustainable access of children under five, pregnant and lactating women, teenagers, the elderly and vulnerable communities to safe, nutritious, adequate, diversified and secure food and malnutrition prevention services. Increased equitable and sustainable access of new-borns, children, teenagers, women and men to quality services for the prevention and treatment of diseases and malnutrition. The capacities of the national health system are strengthened to provide essential, high-quality services to all, and respond effectively to emergencies and epidemics. Refugees and IDPs are able to return and reintegrate socially and economically.
In 2022, IMC UK focused on improving the progressive transfer of services to the government represented by the Mokolo health district while providing technical and logistic support to the team of qualified health care workers who which became almost entirely of health district volunteers since July 2021. This was done through frequent formative supervisions, capacity building of the staff and coaching in the realisation of quality healthcare services. In 2023, the project seeks to improve the health status of 75,352 Nigerian refugees living in the camp of Minawao and the other vulnerable host population around the camp through the delivery of quality health care services. IMC UK will pursue the same objective and will contribute to the improvement of access to health and nutrition services for refugees and host communities surrounding the camp through an impact-based approach as part of the implementation of the multi-year strategic plan for 2022-2026 developed by UNHCR. In line with the vision of empowerment and health resilience of the Minawao refugee camp initiated in 2020 and expected to be fully realized by 2026, IMC UK will contribute to the progressive ownership of services and activities to integrate refugees into the comprehensive health care system and services provided by the integrated health centre in Minawao camp. IMC UK will continue to work closely with UNHCR to continue advocacy for the transformation of the Minawao health posts into a subdivisional hospital or Medicalised hHealth Centres (Centres Médicales d’Arrondissement (CMA).

1 - Besoins / Needs

Cameroon’s population is estimated at 34 million inhabitants, and ranks 153 out of 189 countries in the Human Development Index (HDI). The rate of multi-dimensional poverty rate is 44% and life expectancy is 59 years.2. Nine out of ten of the country’s regions continue to be affected by three concurrent, complex humanitarian crises: the Lake Chad basin conflict, the North-West and South-West (NWSW) crisis and the Central African Republic (CAR) refugee crisis. As for the Llake Chad basin crisis, Cameroon is the second-most affected country after Nigeria.3 This crisis in the Lake Chad Basin has caused an influx of 115,000 Nigerian refugees and 350,000 internally displaced persons (IDPs) into the Far North region of Cameroon, fleeing violent conflict between the government defence forces and non-state armed groups (NSAGs) since 2014.4 Humanitarian needs in this region are complicated by the poor climatic conditions (drought and exceptional flooding during the rainy season), inadequate infrastructural development, high poverty levels and the presence of epidemics like the recent cholera outbreak, measles and Covid-19 outbreaks. As of September 31, 2022, the Far North region was host to 640,245 persons of concern (PoC) including 377,877 IDPs, 130,690 returnees and 131,678 Nigerian refugees (with 75,532 living in the refugee camp of Minawao).During the implementation of the 2022 project, comprehensive primary health care including reproductive health and referral & counter referrals for secondary and tertiary care services and nutrition services were provided to the target population. Never-the-less some challenges were noted in the areas of; insufficient drugs for the management of chronic diseases and mental health conditions, the lack of qualified staff for the mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services, frequent stock outs of essential drugs and some laboratory reagents and rapid diagnostic tests, the high cost for the management of patients in secondary care institutions and the absence of some important medical equipment which if present could facilitate medical and laboratory interventions. Until September 2022, a total of 748 cases of mental health are followed in the MHPSS services with epilepsy representing 70% and psychosis 18%. The donor BPRM supported the MHPSS services in Minawao camp since 2015 by ensuring the provision of a complete package of interventions to respond to the needs of the sector. Since its departure in 2021, the MHPSS sector has suffered from some major gaps including lack of specialised staff for clinical and psychosocial support services (a clinical psychologist and a social worker for example) and insufficient drugs for the management of patients. Patients remain longer in the programme than normal due to insufficient psychological follow-up after their medical treatment because of the absence of social worker for this sector who could facilitate the linkage of the community with the clinic in terms of follow-up of medication at home. The service is presently managed by only one nurse, specialised in mental health who is responsible for the clinical and psychosocial aspects in the health facility and as well as in the community. A nutrition SMART survey was conducted by the Ministry of Public Health with technical and financial support from UNICEF and UNHCR between the 16 of February and 14 March 2021 in 4 vulnerable regions of the country (Far North, North, Adamawa, and East) at the same time as the SENS survey in the Nigerian refugee camp of Minawao and among Central African refugees living in camps and in the host communities. It aimed at assessing the nutritional status of children under 5 6-59 months and women aged 15-49 years, as well as assessing infant and young child feeding (IYCF) practices.
