Project Summary

In line with IRC Bangladesh strategic action plan, GBV global guidelines and the JRP 2021 strategic objectives, IRC aims to respond and prevent GBV in Rohingya camps and the affected host communities of Cox’s Bazar. IRC will empower women and girls and provide safe, supportive, and responsive spaces through which women and girls can access services and receive professional and peer support through integrated interventions in 3 WGSS (camps 5, 18, 25), 3 PHCC (camps 2E, 6, 25), 2 GBV service points (Ratnapalong and Holiapalong) and 8 nutrition centers (camps 13, 14, 15, 16, 22). IRC’s programmatic approaches include: integrated GBV-SRH and GBV-nutrition services, prevention and behavior change interventions and life skills while ensuring the needs of multiple intersectional identities, do no harm (PSEA) and a survivor centered approach.
Safe Space Programming: IRC will implement run 3 WGSS and 2 GBV service points. Recreational activities, including sewing and arts will be available to enable opportunities for women to learn skills, bond and share experiences. The WGSS’ will also offer women and girls information about GBV and SRH and serve as spaces that facilitate disclosure.
GBV Response Activities: IRC will provide GBV case-management including PSS referral services. Dignity kits will be provided to women and girls on an as-needed basis. Service providers will be trained to strengthen adherence to regularly updated, confidential and timely interagency referral systems. GBVIMS and GBVIMS+ will ensure proper storage, analysis, and data sharing to understand the overall GBV situation and to advocate for women and girls. IRC will ensure coordination between GBV and CP actors to ensure proper case management services to child GBV survivors. IRC will also ensure coordination between GBV and SRH actors to ensure safe referrals of cases. IRC will work with the government authorities and advocate for women and girls while ensuring data confidentiality.
GBV Prevention Activities: Girl Shine will focus on adolescent girls to support, protect and empower them. Structured psycho-social support for both male and female caregivers will help to ensure a healthy family environment. The women’s economic empowerment interventions will focus on building life skills for women and create a pathway for income generation. This will be combined with group discussions using EASE model to ensure women and their spouses are engaged in dialogue on joint financial decision making, communication and negotiation benefits in the household. EMAP is a primary prevention approach which aims to reduce violence against women and girls by addressing its root causes by engaging men. SASA! Together is a community mobilization approach that supports communities to create positive and sustainable changes around norms that perpetuate violence against women. Community engagement and outreach will be conducted to facilitate women’s and girls’ access to services provided at the WGSS. Volunteers will also conduct outreach awareness on issues such as COVID 19, GBV risk mitigation, availability of services and PSEA. The outreach content will be data driven.
The IRC’s advocacy initiatives will be informed through safety audits, gender assessments and GBVIMS. Capacity development of IRC and its partner staff will be key to ensure interventions are of high quality in line with international standards and methodologies. Response activities will be implemented by the IRC while prevention activities will be implemented by the IRC’s local implementing partners. The IRC will continue to lead the GBV Case Management Task Team and GBV-CP Case Management Integration Working Group and ensure coordination between different case management actors and advocate where necessary. In camps 25 with the co-existence of Rohingya and host community, IRC will conduct dialogue sessions between women to ensure social cohesion in the IWC.

1 - Identified Needs

Women, girls, men and boys experience crises in different ways and face different protection risks. IRC’s reports have found that in Rohingya and host communities, gender roles relate to power structures that are shaped by the deeply rooted patriarchal structure, conservative social and cultural norms and religious beliefs. The deeply entrenched beliefs give men, and boys more power over women and girls. The IRC’s GBV screening data (November 2020 to October 2021) has found that one in every four women screened are survivors of GBV. IRC’s GBVIMS analysis from the Rohingya camps and host communities from January to October 2021 revealed worrying trends; of all the reported cases 52% were physical assault, 26% were of emotional abuse, 16% were of denial of resources and 94% reported cases were of intimate partner violence (IPV). Moreover, 98% of reported cases took place at the survivor’s home revealing that women and girls are not safe at their home. 97% of reported case were received from women whereas 3% of the cases were from adolescent girls. The prevalence of GBV has been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic; related infection preventive measures such as lockdown and movement restrictions have created direct and indirect negative social, economic, and psychosocial impacts on women and girls and other marginalized groups. IRC has found that the understanding of izzot (honor) has specific implications for women and often controls their decision-making power, access and control over resources, agency, and mobility. Women’s honor is policed and restricted by social norms and dictated by men. Men are the heads of household and main decision makers in the family. Women, on the other hand, are responsible for the household, children, and domestic work. The burden of household chores and lack of resting time has increased for women during the COVID-19 pandemic according to an IRC safety audit (February 2021) conducted in the Rohingya camps. 74% of key interviewees reported that women and girls face barriers while seeking health and GBV services. The key interviewers also shared women and girls’ fear of being identified as survivors (69%), fear of contracting COVID-19 (62%), distance of facilities (60%), unsuitable opening hours (50%), denied permission to access to seek GBV services (70%) and health services (43%) by their family members and unaware of available services (52%) as the main reasons behind the barriers to seek services. The safety audit has also identified security issues: inadequate lighting and locks on doors and inadequate separated and maintained toilets for women and girls has aggravated GBV for women and girls, especially at night. FGD discussions show women and girls lack control over their sexuality and decision in family planning following with dire health and well-being consequences. The GBVIMS trends analysis reports for Rohingya and host community have also found that polygamy is one of the driving forces behind IPV. Moreover, the prevalence of early child marriage has increased during the COVID 19 pandemic due to economic constraints on families. A safety audit (July 2021) conducted across 4 camps have found that the vulnerability for women, adolescent girls and people with disability increased during disasters such as flooding and fire. The report has shown that immediately after a disaster, women and girls face GBV in their temporary shelters which includes the homes of relatives and neighbours. This is exacerbated for women and girls who face multiple intersectional identities. The situation in the host community is like that of the refugees as can be seen from earlier safety audits. While reported levels of GBV remains low, IRC’s reports indicate that GBV often goes unreported due to the patriarchal society. Therefore, there is a dire need to scale up the existing GBV prevention and response work while engaging women, girls, men, and boys with multiple intersectional identities.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1 - Community mobilization through SASA! Together will be conducted - SASA! Together is a community mobilization approach that supports communities to create positive and sustainable changes around norms that perpetuate violence against women will be implemented. This will be implemented in 3 camps and Holudiapalong.Activity 2 - Safety audits and assessments will be conducted to identify, document and develop strategies and advocacy initiatives to address identified issues around women’s protection and empowerment. Activity 3 - GBV and non-GBV actors including local government and local partners will be trained and coached on GBV programming. IRC will ensure coordination with government actors and keep them informed of all GBV programming. They will also take part in advocacy initiatives that will advance the needs of women and girls. The need for data confidentiality will also be discussed. Activity 4 - Ensure functional referral system with effective field level mechanism is available and accessible through coordination with government authorities and service providers Activity 5 - WGSS and GBV service centers will be functional and accessible for women and girls – This includes the running of the WGSS and GBV service centers and recreational activities for women and girls. Each of the WGSS and GBV service centers will have a women support network who will link IRC's safe space programming with the community and will play a leadership role to increase the resilience of the community. Facilities will be accessible to women and girls with multiple intersectional identities (e.g. people with disability, elder, widows) to ensure no one is left behind. Complaint and feedback mechanism including on PSEA and safeguarding will also be ensured. Activity 6 - Quality and inclusive GBV response services will be ensured in line with a survivor centered approach. Quality SGBV case management will be available and accessible for women and girls including survivors with disabilities, survivors engaged with sex work. Dignity kits will be provided to women and girls on a need basis. Case management data will be collected, stored, analyzed and shared inline with the information sharing protocol (ISP) using GBVIMS and GBVIMS+. GBV screening will be conducted at all facilities. Integration between SRH-GBV and Nutrition-GBV will be ensured to maximize support to GBV survivors. Midwives will also be trained on ensuring survivor care and referrals during emergencies (e.g. COVID 19 lockdown and disasters). These activities will be conducted in camps 2E, 5, 13,14,15,16,18, 25, Holudiapalong and Ratnalong. Activity 7 - EMAP will be conducted to engage men in transformative individual behavior change with women in the center to prevent GBV. This will be implemented by IRC's local implementing partners in all locations. Activity 8 - Specialized life skills activities through Girl Shine will be conducted to improve adolescent access to SRH and GBV prevention and response services. Activity 9 - Empower women by enabling them challenge existing gender norms by focusing on their economic and social empowerment EA$E program. Activity 10 - Community outreach activities will be conducted on GBV response and prevention including GBV risk mitigation, SRH, PSEA, availability of services and DRR. Activity 11 - IRC will actively coordinate with the GBV Sub Sector, GBVIMS Task Team, PSEA network, govt. and non-govt. actors. IRC will lead the Case Management Task Team and GBV-CP Case Management Working Group.Activity 12 - IRC will respond to disasters in line with emergency preparedness and response plans, through specialized capacity building for staff and volunteers. There will be a contributory stock of dignity kits and other emergency items. Safety audits will be conducted post disaster. Mobile case workers will be deployed after an emergency to provide PSS support. IRC and its partners will respond within the PERU, MMT and ERT
