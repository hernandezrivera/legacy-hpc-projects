Project Summary

This project proposal aims at improving food security and livelihood options and building resilience to IPC3+ households in Nyirol, Uror, Ayod, Fangak, Duk, Bor in Jonglei state and Renk, Ulang in Upper Nile state. This will be achieved through FSL cluster objectives CO1: Food assistance, CO2: Emergency food production; and CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs with mainstreaming of COVID-19 prevention protocols, gender, protection, persons with disabilities and climate. The targeted beneficiaries are 13,390 households (80,350 individuals; 24% women, 16% men, 36% girls and 24% boys) consisting of 5,356 hosts communities, 2,009 returnees, 1,071 refugees and 4,954 IDPs. Beneficiary by type will be determined through baseline surveys.

Food assistance will be conditional through Food for Assets in Ulang county targeting 3,613 households impacting the lives of 21,680 individuals.
Food security will be enhanced through;
i) in-kind distribution of crop, vegetable and fishing kits to 9673 households (Ayod 1200, Duk 680, Fangak 640, Nyirol 2433, Uror 2160 and Renk 2560
ii) Restocking poultry and shoats in Ayod and Duk counties to 500 and 680 households respectively
iii) Livestock support; treatment and vaccination campaigns to 5773 HHs in Ayod (500), Duk (680) Uror(2160) and Nyirol (2433) counties targeting an estimate of 200,000 livestock heads through 120 trained Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) (40 per county)
Resilience building and reducing dependency on food and agricultural inputs aids (CO3) will be realized through
i) Support to seed fairs using locally sourced seed; seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support market functionality e.g. assist traders to re-supply markets/ local procurement for FAO ITFs & aggregation of produce for WFP assistance in Renk county targeting 560 seed producer farmers
ii) Support value chain addition to fishery groups and fishery values chain actors in Bor South county targeting 630
iii) Training & capacity building through farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots vocational training cutting across all the project locations targeting 15% of the total targeted households translating to 2,009 house holds. The trainings will cover good and climate-smart agronomic practices, natural resource management, countering post-harvest losses, sustainable income generation activities, value addition with special emphasis on minimising spread of COVID-19.
iv) Conflict related meetings to strengthen local peace structures also done across the all the targeted project location to promote peace thereby allowing the project participants to fully engage in agricultural activities in safe public spaces without fear of displacement and loosing their produce/harvest through conflict related effects.
Community based participatory approach will be adopted through Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) committees (consisting of RRC representatives, Boma chiefs, Women leaders, Youth Leaders, County Agricultural Department Representative) and through Project management committees. Indicators like household status (host, IDP, returnees and refugees), gender markers (targeted male and female) and special/minority groups like Child-headed, Elderly and Female-headed households under IPC 3+ will be screened before approval of the beneficiary list.
Continuous monitoring will be done throughout the project cycles to ensure quality project outcomes are realised within the allocated timeframe. Community representative committees (AAPs and PMCs) and donors will take part in the monitoring process and also act as a platform for complaint-feedback mechanisms. These feedback will be included in the implementation and project designs where applicable.
Sustainability of the project will be realised through active involvement of the community stakeholders through out the project cycle making them adopt and propagate the knowledge acquired.

1 - Needs

October-November IPC 2022 IPC analysis identifies Jonglei and Upper Nile States as amongst the most food insecure states where more than 50% of their populations are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse. In Jonglei 68.0% population is facing acute food insecurity while in Upper Nile (58.5%) is affected. The April-July 2023 IPC projections estimate combined populations of 431,000 people with Emergency food needs (IPC phases 4; Ayod-78,000, Bor South-51,000,, Duk-40,000, Fangak-89,000, Nyirol-50,000, Uror-69,000, Renk-19,000 and Ulang 35,000) with an estimated 20,000 people classified in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity in Fangak; one of the worst in South Sudan. The food insecurity and high malnutrition has mostly been attributed to reduced employment and income of household members, floods destroying crops, loss of livelihoods and incidences of insecurity (HNO 2022). Ulang has a GAM rate of 16.1%; Bor 14.4%; Duk 13.3%; Fangak 19.0%; Nyirol 19.0%, Ayod 14.8% and Uror 14.4% and Renk 13.5%. With most of the population having lost their livelihood and with missed planting seasons consecutively for three seasons, coupled with insecurity, the host community and IDPs have been left food insecure and high malnutrition caseload. The economic situation continues to worsen over the years and monthly market price factsheet January 2022 a household in South Sudan on average basis requires 99 USD to meet SMEB. Ulang largely and primarily depends on Humanitarian Aid, and devaluation, which increases the prices of food items including staples, continues to negatively affect their purchasing power. Most families are without employment; most populations heavily reliant on humanitarian assistance (WFP) and so without Humanitarian Assistance, large food consumption gaps resulting in heavy acute malnutrition and excess mortality extreme loss of livelihood assets that lead to food consumption gaps in the short term. Food insecure households in Uror, Ayod, Bor, Nyirol, Fangak, Ulang and Renk have resorted to negative coping strategies including as reduction of meals taken per day, only children eating with devastating effects on pregnant and lactating women and children under the age of five years, child marriages, child labour, intensification of cattle rustling and conflicts. There has also been gaps in skills needed to enable the agro-pastoral communities reap maximumly from their fishing, farming and livestock keeping ventures and also create assets. Protracted inter and intra communal conflicts caused by cattle raids, revenge killings among others have also played significant roles in creating food security and livelihood gaps. This calls for the need of integrating peace components as in case of Humanitarian Development and Peace Nexus in livelihood programming. Climate change has been one of the major direct and indirect drivers of food insecurity. This is witnessed through the upsurge of waters of river Nile causing floods to the various locations in Jonglei and upper Nile. This creates the need of including Climate Smart Agricultural practices among the farmers through trainings in the farmer field schools. This call for immediate food assistance, asset creation to mitigate flood impact, in kind support of fishing, crop and vegetable kits, livestock treatment and vaccination campaigns, promote seed fairs and ITFs and peace building in order to protect and safeguard lives and also build the resilience capacities of the targeted communities to shocks.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

This will be done through having a representative from these vulnerable groups that will act as points of contact between the community and project staffs. Separate meetings will be conducted at regular intervals with these point of contact persons and collect any feedbacks which will be relayed and handled in a confidential manner. There will also be help desks established in each Boma where the vulnerable individuals would visit and give their feedback.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CMD programming follows a non-discriminatory approach in which all people regardless of race, gender, affiliation, and disability status. The programme are designed to reach the most vulnerable groups within the populaiton including PLWDs and others. The CFM mechanisms are robust including face to face interface with staff, words of mouth, written. The complaint and feedback mechanisms will be implemented accordingly with intention to reach all sections of affected population.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

GBV trainings to both project staffs and targeted beneficiariesMessaging on PSEA in IEC materials distributed through stickers and leaflets Sensitization on PSEA during meetings with community representative committees including AAPs, PMCs. The information will be provided during project inception or awareness stages, at distribution site and through community level meetings. Information on awareness on gender and protection pathways will also be made available to ensure beneciaries can access such services in case of GBV and protection related concerns. Mechanisms such as help desk and exit and PDM interviews will be conducted to gather feedback from the different sources.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiaries feedback will be sought during project design, implementation and during monitoring. During project design phase: Assessment including Baseline survey, need analysis, KAP survey, SLP study and will be done to inform on the actual needs, make realistic targets, know what the community value and would be interested among others giving room for contextualize the project deliverables which would be beneficial to the targeted community. During the project implementation phase: Community feedback will be gathered to gauge their satisfaction of the project deliverables, any change that need to be done in terms mode of delivery, targeting, any change in project/beneficiary dynamics e.g displacements and reaching out to the community in their new locations, security concerns among others. CMD will ensure all the concerns raised are addressed on a timely and regular basis in order to ensure targeted beneficiaries receive inputs and services in safe public spaces and as per the needs. During project monitoring and evaluation, the project staff in collaboration with the community representative committees will ensure any complaints raised is addressed in a timely manner. Special emphasis will be put on feedback from both Male and Female genders, persons with disabilities across the male and female genders, different age groups including child headed households, youth headed households, elderly headed households across the male and female genders, minority/any disadvantaged ethnic group. These special grups will have their representatives who will be the point of contact to enable the groups share their views/feedback.

12.1. Does your project contribute to any of the three Collective Outcomes, i.e. Improved Food security; Enhanced support for prevention and response to needs of survivors of Gender-Based Violence; and Flood displacement?

Yes

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The asset created like dykes, roads, water ponds and vegetable gardens will reduce the vulnerability of the people affected by floods by preventing loss/destruction of grains harvested, enable transportation of food items to the affected location, prevent death of livestock, and stored thereby reducing the risks of food insecurity. Dissemination of early warning signs of the impending floods will also help the community move with their assets to safer locations

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Through regular meetings of peace building and protection, emphasis will be put on PSEA, promoting safe public spaces for women and girls involved in different livelihood activities. Contact persons, help desks and referral pathways will also be established for handling the GBV cases in confidential manner without exposing the victim to further victimization. CMD will utilize the GBV referral pathways for referring of case for management

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

Through conditional food distribution, number of people in IPC 4 and 5 will reduce because this will alleviate immediate hunger. The asset created like dykes, roads, water ponds and vegetable gardens will reduce the vulnerability of the people affected by floods by preventing loss/destruction of grains harvested, enable transportation of food items to the affected location, prevent death of livestock, and stored thereby reducing the risks of food insecurity.
