Project Summary

This project aims to provide integrated gender-based violence (GBV) prevention, risk mitigation, and response services to vulnerable women and girls in the counties of Panyikang, Malakal, and Maiwut in Upper Nile State, as well as in Akobo County Jonglei State. Over the course of 12 months, we will target a total of 8400 individuals who are representing internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host communities.
To achieve our objectives, ACRA will conduct GBV awareness sessions that focus on identifying GBV risk factors, implementing prevention measures, informing participants about available services, and encouraging timely reporting. These sessions will be delivered through community campaigns in social spaces and house-to-house visits, ensuring broad outreach and engagement. Additionally, ACRA will distribute dignity kits for risk mitigation efforts, providing essential items that promote health and dignity.
ACRA is committed to supporting GBV survivors by reaching out through with specialized response services. These services will include comprehensive case management, referrals to appropriate resources, psychosocial support, legal aid, and the establishment of Women and Girls Friendly Spaces (WGFS) where survivors can find safety and community support. Furthermore, we will enhance the capacity of frontline service providers by training 200 individuals (120 women and 80 men) on GBV minimum standards, ensuring that all humanitarian actors are equipped to integrate GBV considerations into their responses.
In addition to direct support for survivors, the project aims to provide psychosocial support to through individual and group activities that promote mental well-being and community cohesion. ACRA will also work to strengthen GBV referral pathways and update standard operating procedures by conducting service mapping in the targeted counties, ensuring that survivors have access to the necessary services.
To further our prevention efforts, we will implement primary prevention activities that fostering awareness and reducing the risk of GBV in the community.
Community engagement is crucial to our approach. We will establish community-based protection monitoring committees with a focus on including 65% women and girls, as well as persons with disabilities. ACRA’s strategies align with Protection Cluster Objectives by providing critical protection assistance tailored to the needs of affected populations, considering age, gender, and disability. We are committed to upholding human rights and strengthening coordination among humanitarian actors to reduce protection risks, while promoting a nexus between emergency humanitarian assistance and long-term development to address the vulnerabilities faced by women, girls, and persons with disabilities.
Throughout the project, ACRA will engage with communities to ensure their concerns are addressed and integrated into our implementation strategies. All activities will adhere to a “Do No Harm” and conflict-sensitive approach, ensuring the inclusion of persons with disabilities and prioritizing safety, dignity, and the avoidance of harm.

1. Needs

Protection concerns for women and girls in South Sudan are alarmingly high, with gender-based violence (GBV) posing the most critical risk to their safety and well-being. The Protection Risk Monitoring System identifies GBV as the primary protection risk in 38% of the 2,497 communities surveyed in 2024. An alarming 65% of women experience some form of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) in their lifetime, including child marriage and rape (UNDP, July 26, 2022). This situation is rooted in entrenched gender inequality, abuse of power, and significant human rights violations, further exacerbated by harmful cultural practices, intercommunal conflicts, climate shocks such as floods and droughts, and a high influx of returnees from the ongoing crisis in Sudan. Forecasting indicates that 2.75 million people will require GBV interventions in 2025 due to escalating risks. GBV prevalence is particularly high in key areas, including Baliet, Panyikang, Maiwut, Malakal, Fangak, and Akobo counties, all categorized as priority 2 areas for urgent intervention. Women in these regions often feel unsafe performing daily tasks like collecting firewood, accessing water, and visiting markets. The ISNA report (2024) reveals that 38% of women and girls avoid specific areas due to safety concerns, while 53% of households face barriers to essential services. A survey of GBV experts indicates that core GBV services are limited in 35% of counties, further compounding vulnerabilities for women, children, and individuals with disabilities, who lack access to lifesaving services such as psychosocial support, healthcare, and legal aid. In January 2023, Baliet County saw an influx of over 9,000 returnees from Jonglei State due to communal conflicts (Protection Cluster Upper Nile State – Flash Report, February 2023). Women and girls reported attacks by unknown individuals while gathering firewood, severely impacting their ability to provide for themselves and increasing their vulnerabilities. Reports of conflict-related sexual violence have surged, with the absence of safe spaces for women and girls and lack of referral pathways severely limiting GBV reporting and protection assistance. In Maiwut County, an Integrated Rapid Needs Assessment (IRNA) on August 15, 2023, reported over 9,200 returnees (6,624 females and 2,576 males) from Ethiopia and Sudan due to hunger and conflict. This influx has strained already limited clean water resources, leading to overcrowding and conflicts at water points, forcing many women to collect water from distant areas where they feel unsafe. The lack of adequate shelter has compromised women's privacy, resulting in increased forced and early marriages as families attempt to cope by marrying off young girls for income.The challenges associated with GBV, compounded by floods and food insecurity, continue to threaten women's and children's safety in Akobo County, while flooding limits protection assistance. Many adolescent girls are unable to attend school due to financial constraints, increasing the risk of child marriage. Recent updates (April-June 2024) by the Protection Cluster identified significant service gaps in case management, psychosocial support, and referrals in the five targeted counties, highlighting inadequate coverage of GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response services. Through this lifesaving project, ACRA aims to collaborate with community leaders, local actors, and persons with disabilities to combat GBV and dismantle gender barriers, creating safer environments for IDPs, returnees, and host communities. The project will address needs for awareness, material support, access to services, and capacity building to effectively mitigate the alarming levels of GBV in these counties.

2. Project activities

Project activities:The following activities will be implemented:Activity 1.1: GBV service mapping to establish or strengthen GBV referral pathways in four counties. Through the service mapping workshops, partners offering various services in key areas of psychosocial support, health, safety, security and legal aid will be identified and their key roles highlighted on the referral pathway. Quarterly updating of the referral pathways will be ensured to reflect any changing situation and ensure survivors are receiving proper care and service.Activity 1.2: GBV risk awareness on GBV prevention, mitigation, available services and timely reporting. This will be done in public spaces, house to house visits, focused group discussions and mass media. This activity will ensure 32 awareness sessions have been conducted across the 4 counties targeting 7040 individuals (3082 women, 792 men, 2270 girls and 896 boys).Activity 1.3: Provision of capacity building trainings on GBV minimum standards to 200 staff/frontline service providersActivity 1.4: Establishment of Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote survivor centered healing, well-being and empowerment.Activity 1.5: Provision of individual and group Psychosocial Support services including reactional activities to ensure empowerment and recovery reaching 400 individuals (181 women, 130girls, 70 boys, 19 men).Activity 1.6: Dignity kit distribution and programming targeting vulnerable women and girls of reproductive age as part of GBV risk mitigation. 400 dignity kits will be distributed to 225 women and 175 girls as part of GBV risk mitigation.Activity 1.7: Provision of legal services that protect the rights of GBV survivors and promote their access to justice.Activity 1.8: Provision of needs-based capacity building trainings to 48 (28F, 20M) non-GBV humanitarian actors on integration of GBV in their sectoral responses.Activity 1.9: Rollout and implementation of primary GBV prevention interventions to addresses harmful practices, systemic gender inequality and power imbalances (e.g., SASA, EMAP, community cares programming) reaching 120 individuals (61 women, 28 girls, 20 men and 11 boys). Activity 1.10: Provision of cash assistance support to 140 individuals who are vulnerable 120 women and 20 girls.Activity 1.11: Facilitating training on the molding and use of fuel efficient stoves and Income Generating Activities for 200 women.Activity 1.12: Establishment of community-based protection networks to provide protection assistance and psychosocial support to women, girls and vulnerable people. 11 community-based protection committees with 10 members each totaling 110 persons (65% women and girls) in each location including PWDs.
