Project Summary

The project aims at promoting children’s return to school, sensitization and psychosocial support through recreational activities for children aged from 3 to 12 in the aftermath of the Cyclone Kenneth in the district of Ibo and the city of Pemba.
According to the data reported by the Provincial Department of Pemba in the area of Cabo Delgado the cyclone Kenneth damaged 129 schools that normally take in 40.944 children and 885 teachers of primary and secondary schools.
The action is structured on a three-layer intervention and seeks to enhance the quality of education, the child protection and the hygiene and health of the children affected. An estimated 11.681 of children aged from 6 to 12 will be the beneficiaries of quality education while another estimate 778 children between 3 and 5 years will be specifically targeted to ensure and recreational activities.
In particular, the action will focus on awareness on GBV prevention and on good hygiene practices paying particular attention to emergency settings with the purpose of bridging the gap between a rapid response engendered by the humanitarian emergency and the following development actions. AVSI’s proposal aims at restoring normalcy in learning environments and, at the same time, gives inputs for quality and accessibility improvement of education in the existing school system.
1. Providing access and ensuring quality:
- 5 TLS will be set up in the districts of and Ibo and Pemba;
- Distribution of Books and Learning kits for 11.681 children
- Distribution of 10 Recreational kits for 5 TLS;
- Distribution of 32 rehabilitation kit for 32 schools affected.
2. Increasing psychosocial support and awareness of children and caregivers on Child protection and GBV violence
- Training for 100 teachers (ideally, 50% male and 50% female) on psychosocial support (in line with MINEDH’s quality learning standards)
- 5 CFS will be created in Ibo and Pemba
- Distribution of 5 ECD kits in 5 TLS created
- Creation of 10 children clubs aimed at prioritizing needs of girls and vulnerable categories of children such as orphans, and people with disability or special needs.
3. Preventing diseases in emergency settings while improving the level of knowledge on health and hygiene
- Realization of an awareness campaign for children parents and teachers of target schools;
- Distribution of health and hygiene kit for the families of the children affected by the cyclone.
The activities outcomes will be regularly monitored and supervised by relevant local authorities (SDEJT, DPEDH, SDSMAS and DPS) so that to guarantee a gradual and sustainable exit strategy in the aftermath of the emergency phase.
The added value of AVSI is the collaboration, from 2013, with the Provincial Directorate of Education of the area of reference.
Brief overview of outcomes with respect to:
1. Access
The project will establish 5 TLS in 5 public schools allowing the schools to accommodate classrooms activities and provide education to a number of children in need.
2. Continuity
The project will provide school material to 11.681 children and will train 100 teachers from 32 schools. These will provide needed tools for children reducing drop out and allowing to them to have teacher more skilled and able to respond to their psychosocial needs.
3. Gender Equality and Equity
5 Peer groups and 5 CFS activities will disseminate GBV and gender equality messages. Hygiene promotion activities will focus on female in reproductive age and will strengthen the capacities of the school of supporting female education.
4. Protection
CFS activities and peer groups will allow also the staff to provide PSS activities to children and to collect specific needs that can be referred to specialized agencies
5. Quality
The training of 100 teachers and the hygiene campaign for 32 schools will improve department of education capacity of responding of children needs in situation of emergency and will improve the quality of education services in the area
