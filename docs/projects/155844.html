Project Summary

Early recovering of Syria community from conflict is one of the core responsibilities of Bonyan and every active humanitarian actor. Bonyan designed its Early Recovery interventions to be in line with the strategic objectives of both the HRP and ER Cluster guided by an analysis of the severity of needs in accordance to assessment findings and key indicators.
Supporting the spontaneous efforts at self-recovery and cover the critical gaps in attaining decent livelihoods opportunities, and improve access to basic services and repairing the social fabric is the main goals of the designed program in which its layout is divided to 2 thematic components, targeting 39,480 individuals (20,924 Females, 19740 Children (<18), 11,370 Adults (18-35)) in Aleppo and Idleb governorate.
***** Increase access to livelihoods and basic services and infrastructure targeting 26,580 persons (12,493 Males, 14,087 Females ~ more than 75% are children and youth)
After eight years of crisis, many community members are not able to earn a minimum income to address their basic needs. Therefore, Bonyan, in 2019 ERL Program focus on creating income-generating opportunities for people -particularly the most vulnerable groups (female headed households, people with disabilities, youth and family with chronic illness person)- through:
[1] Improving access to productive and market infrastructure to restore local economic through rehabilitating five basic, local economic infrastructures such as irrigation canals, public markets and public plants (bakery, seeds cement soils). Along with rehabilitating 10 schools by improving 160 classrooms and 40 latrines ~ benefiting 4,500 children 2,200 boys – 2,300 Girls. The school and infrastructure will be assessed, and unique rehabilitation plan will be put. Rehabilitation will be conducted with CfW modality to Create short-term work opportunities for 80 individuals in cash for work activity.
[2] Delivering vocational training for 100 youth (18-35 years old) in the most critical vocational gaps which will be identified by market-base need assessment. Trainees will receive in-kind support to start their new careers. Along with management and finance skills to improve their employ-ability.
[3] Providing grant support for 100 entrepreneurship and 100 rural enterprises. by distribution of productive assets to help them to rehabilitate, expand, or initiate their enterprises. Also, skills training will be delivered to beneficiaries on Marketing, management, and cost, price estimation.
***** Improve the resilience and cohesion of the community targeting 12,900 persons (6,063 Males, 6,837 Females)
Long time of crisis and unsuitable housing conditions have affected the cohesion of the community and reduced members’ ability in find positive adaption to address their basic needs. Moreover, the absence of banking or credit services is pointed as a major problem for many people according to observations and assessments of Bonyan.
In response to these gaps at community level, Bonyan will:
[4] Provide requisite assistance to 100 households by upgrading their housing conditions. Technical team will assess each house and prepare BoQ of needed materials. After procurement of materials, CfW workers will undertake rehabilitation under supervision of Bonyan Engineering team.
[5] Establish three “village micro-credit associations” to be as a small window for micro-credit. Bonyan will constitute the management committees, formulate rules of operation, establishing the capital window and advice in managing.
Throughout this program, Bonyan will create youth groups to empower their participation in assessing needs, designing, and implementing activities. CfW will target youth manpower for the Rehabilitation works. empower youth initiatives through capacity building and participation in establishing selection criteria and community monitoring for the project

Needs

HNO2019 indicates that• 1.4M displaced people, including 42,000 refugees, are estimated to have spontaneously returned they concentrated in the north of the country• 8.7M people are estimated to need ERL support (4.3M males / 4.3M females – 0.9M children<5 - 2M children 5-17 – 4.6M adults 18-59 – 0.4M Elderly>59)• Half of potential members of the workforce is without access to sustained employment. According to latest HNAP report:• Top 3 reasons for returnees: Improvement of economic, and Security situation, and Need to protect assets/properties• Top 3 priority needs for returnees: Livelihoods. Basic service, Health Bonyan conducted assessments in both Aleppo and Idleb governorates and indicate the following:• 62% of IDPs and 49% of Local Communities are unemployed in the targeted locations• 70% of the targeted area either suffer from shortage of products or services• Top 3 hardest to get products are: production inputs, Electrical, and Electronic items. Because of high prices, available in distanced areas only and unstable market and fluctuating prices • Full report is shared with cluster members Communities report spending savings, running up debts buying on credit, depleting household assets and reducing food consumption as the most common negative coping strategies to compensate for the lack of basic relief items. These coping mechanisms are ultimately unsustainable and, in the absence of humanitarian interventions or an overall improvement in the socio-economic situation, may continue to lead people to resort to increasingly exploitative activities when their financial and material resources have been exhausted. Such coping mechanisms disproportionately affect the most vulnerable groups, such as children. 82% of child labor prevent school attendance within communities. In addition, 69% of communities perceived the occurrence of early marriage, exposing girls to loss of self-esteem, significant personal protection risks, health issues and depriving them of an education. Furthermore, *** Limited access to livelihood The unemployment rate was estimated to be 53%, (75% among youth 15-24 years). This has precluded opportunities for safe and sustained access to livelihoods. Combined with an upsurge in the cost of living, this has led to a severe erosion of people’s purchasing power. For instance, up to 1.2M families cannot afford rent costs, with a recent report on Housing, Land and Property (HLP) issues in northwest Syria reporting ‘unaffordable housing’ as a concern in 100% of sub-districts surveyed. More than 83% of Syrians lived below the poverty line, and more recent indications suggest that it has worsened such as Insufficient incomes, decreased purchasing power, limited employment opportunities and displacement have also contributed to an erosion of coping strategies, scarce livelihood opportunities and limited agricultural recovery that have continued to impact food security. As a result, youth may resort to harmful coping strategies, for it is estimated that 15% of labor force are engaged in ‘armed struggle’ or ‘illegal’ economic activity (fighting, smuggling, trafficking), in many cases as this is the only livelihood opportunity available to them. *** Social Cohesion and Community Security The presence of IDPs in host communities increases the stress on social services and physical spaces inside homes, leading to potential rupture in the social fabric as well.Adolescents and youth are amongst the most economically and socially marginalized groups in Syria. Youth have very few opportunities to participate in economic activities and lack opportunities to participate in decision-making that can contribute towards the realization of their rights. They lack formal channels to effectively participate in social, civic and economic life. Ultimately, young people feel disenfranchised and have little faith in the existing participation platforms to have a positive influence on their futures.

Protection Risk Analysis

R1AResources and response capacities being limited and far below know needs, there will always be perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries or of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tensionLikelihood: High Impact: HighMitigating Measure:1. Perform conflict analysis before planning the response. 2. Ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation, and that this is openly documented and made transparent.3. Make sure those in similar situation of need, at least within the geographical areas of response, area reached equally. Use the existing or establish community mechanisms to identify needs and arrive at a consensus as of who should be assisted. Support such mechanisms with advice and guidance. 4. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the local authorities and communities concerned.5. Efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to (Affected) Populations’ by keeping the local authorities and communities concerned regularly informed of the project progress.6. Make sure that some process of receiving feedback and of responses to it is put in place.7. Have in reserve a strategy in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension (e.g. add-on of other activities or change in activities, in consultation with concerned communities.8. Manage donor expectation on how long it takes to gain community trust and set up activities

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Targeted locations are secure, safe and accessible during implementing the project.- In asset support activities, beneficiaries will not sell assets and they will use it to improve their livelihoods. Risk:R1BEscalation of the security situation.Likelihood: Medium Impact: HighMitigating Measure:1. Ensure there is a process established that allows to consult the communities on how to proceed with the proposed response in a deteriorating security environment2. Targeted locations are included in de-escalation agreements. In case of unexpected military actions, possible solution is to conduct activities in another locations.
