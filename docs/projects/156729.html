Project Summary

DRC will provide support to repair damaged water systems, including repair of public water networks and boreholes, and sanitation systems including sewage lines and connections to treatment centres, in Rural Damascus, Dar’a, Homs, Hama, Aleppo, and Deir-ez-Zor, benefiting 232,500 unique beneficiaries.

Needs

WASH needs are acute in Syria, where access to clean water has been reduced by 50%. Extensive damage to water, sewage and electricity infrastructure is estimated to total more than $8bn in repair costs (ESCWA 2018). This negatively impacts health, depletes resources, and impedes early recovery in affected communities. The latest data shows that more than 15.4 million people in Syria are in need of WASH assistance and more than 6.183 million are in acute need for WASH assistance. According to DRC rapid assessments (2018) in heavily conflict-affected areas, WASH is a critical concern, with the health and resilience of affected populations put at risk by reliance on water trucking (often from unsafe and/or improperly constructed boreholes) and are exposed to risk of disease from leaking or improperly laid sewage systems are causing spillage into streets and into groundwater, triggering grave health concerns. This is especially the case where conflict-damaged boreholes have caused new boreholes to be improperly dug near to leaking sewage systems.Meanwhile, continued instability and fighting makes continued displacements a near-certainty in 2019. Displaced populations, especially if part of sudden, mass displacements, will be in acute need of life-saving WASH support through provision of emergency sanitation facilities in displacement sites. DRC rapid assessments in Dar’a al-Balad, Yadoudeh, Mzeireb, and Nawa have demonstrated critical damage to sanitation and water infrastructure with outbreak of hepatitis in Mzeireb and Nawa attributed to sewage contamination of the water system. Approximately 50% of the population in these areas is without access to water through public networks. In affected areas of Hama and Aleppo, influxes of IDPs in addition to returnees has overwhelmed existing WASH systems, especially where damage has been sustained during the crisis and has layered with multiple years of neglect/lack of proper maintenance. DRC rapid assessments in East Ghouta and Ar-Rastan, as well as consultation with key actors on the ground (including SARC) demonstrates a similar situation in the most heavily affected areas, with the resilience of the population degraded by reliance on water trucking more than six months post-reconciliation and damage to sanitation systems risking the health of the affected population. For example in Kafr Batna, East Ghouta, DRC has identified a river in which sewage is currently being emptied into, leading to contamination of groundwater and agricultural systems with effluent. Lack of access to safe and appropriate WASH heightens the vulnerability and poses life threatening risks to different age and gender groups. Disease spread by sewage is particularly life-threatening to young children and older adults while lack of safe water (for example water which is contaminated by nitrates) is dangerous particularly to young children. Lack of lockable, private WASH facilities impacts the safety of girls and women in particular, heightening the risk of gender-based violence (GBV) and pervasive feelings of threat. Girl children in particular may be withdrawn from school if no WASH facilities are available, as reported to DRC by parents and school principals.

Implementation of activities

DRC has field staff present in all areas of operation who will conduct technical assessments and supervise works delivered by private contractors. Wherever possible, local contractors will be preferred, multiplying benefits to local economies. Hand-over of the repaired infrastructure will be made to local authorities after six months of works completed; a warranty period of six months will be put in place to ensure full completion of works in accordance with WASH sector minimum standards. The end-of-warranty visit will be conducted in collaboration with local authorities facilitating handover. DRC gains feedback of the affected population through informal consultation at assessment and project design phase as well as while works are ongoing and at final project close-out/handover phase. This is gauged through key informant interviews and consultations with key community figures including IDP leaders, mukhtar, local government, SARC focal points, as well as informal discussions with beneficiaries as works are ongoing. Close coordination and collaboration with the relevant ministries (including the Ministry of Local Administration (MoLA), in addition to SARC, will be conducted throughout the project. DRC will conduct WASH infrastructure repair with an integrated approach, aiming to comprehensively restore clean water supply through public networks and access to safe sanitation in target areas. This means that the majority of beneficiaries for this project will benefit from more than one intervention. Kindly note that the target for #1.2, Repair, rehabilitation and operational support to sanitation/sewage and solid waste management systems, will cover repair of sanitation/sewage systems only as DRC will not implement solid waste management. DRC will ensure safety measures to protect young children and children at worksites are put in place e.g. cordoning off steep trenches. Clear lighting around mobile WASH facilities will increase accessibility of the facilities including to older adults.

Protection Risk Analysis

PR1: Unequal distribution of servicesinadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors. Risk: Medium; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; organise joint planning culture and agree on minimum standard; Include community and authorities in targeting; ensure sensitive information is handled with care. Needed resources: inclusion of unequal distribution of services in standard monitoring activities and monitoring formats; WASH sector minimum standards. PR2: Theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by groups and used in conflict activities. Likelihood: Low; Impact: High. Mitigation measures: MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities; promote cost recovery systems for longer term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership Needed resources: support from WASH sector in development/application of cost recovery systems. PR3: Toilets presenting a GBV risk. Risk: high; Impact: high. Mitigation measures: ensure SPHERE standards are followed (including that toilets are lockable, well lit, in a safe place); set up system of ‘focal points’ to monitor WASH units to ensure functioning of a feedback mechanism; protection training for field staff. Needed resources: coordination with protection sector; checklist from WASH sector for field monitoring.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Should any of the planned areas of intervention become inaccessible due to safety concerns, DRC would consider alternate locations in line with its standard targeting criteria and procedures.2. Should water in public water systems be unsafe for drinking, DRC would liaise with the WASH sector to determine a way forward. Should water be safe for most but possibly present a health risk for the very young/elderly/infirm, DRC would consider awareness campaigns in coordination with other actors to ensure the affected population adapts their usage of the water accordingly. However, should the risk level in local water systems be too high (clear health risks identified), DRC would shift implementation to other areas after informing the relevant authorities and actors of the issue. 3. Should sewage systems also require support to restore treatment centres beyond the scope of DRC’s interventions, DRC would coordinate with the local authorities and WASH sector partners to inform competent actors of the issue and coordinate a joint response. 4. Should rapid population movements mean that the beneficiary population moves from the target site for emergency WASH interventions, DRC will attempt to move WASH facilities to other sites where needs are high, in coordination with SARC. 5. Should exchange rate fluctuations between donor currencies and the Syrian pound mean that DRC had less actual funds available in Syria; and/or, should this project not be fully funded, DRC may be unable to deliver all activities as planned. DRC would prioritize those communities in most urgent need of Protection assistance according to verified data from the field. 6. Should all necessary approvals not be received from relevant stakeholders (including and especially SARC) and/or governmental entities for activities, DRC would as a last resort return the related funds to donors.
