Project Summary

After decades of conflict, massive displacement of the population, widespread insecurity, reduced public funding, closure of health facilities. With this, the health care system as well as the health status of the people of South Sudan has suffered enormously. This is evidenced by some of the health-related indicators, Maternal mortality in the country is one of the highest in the world, with 789 per 100,000 live births, the under-five mortality rate and maternal mortality ratio are among the highest in the world at 91 per 1000 live birth s and 1100 per 100,000 live births respectively. Protracted communal conflicts, cattle raiding, economic hardship coupled with climatic shocks (flooding and droughts) and COVID-19 effects have continuously devastated the lives and
According to HNO 2023, extremely low immunization coverage (49 per cent Penta 3 coverage, 69 percent Measles) Immunization services are very poor in the country. As of October 2022, twenty counties in eight states have reported Measles outbreaks. and also, utilization of maternal-child health services is wanting. Cold chain availability and Limited health facility functionality, capacity, and poor coverage and transmission of infections prevented by immunization like measles remain very high. According to ISNA 2022, the main barriers to access and utilization of care services were lack of medicines, staff and equipment transportation cost, insecurity, floods, long distance to health facility and high cost of healthcare (mentioned by host communities, IDPs, and returnees). The 12- month project proposes a comprehensive health package response of emergency life-saving primary health care services to host communities and IDPs in Akobo and Duk Counties of Jonglei State and Maiwut County of Upper Nile states; Medicair will align her responses with Health Cluster strategic objectives 1). Improve equitable access to life-saving essential quality health care services including maternal, child and adolescent health, sexual and reproductive health, treatment common illnesses, disability, MHPSS and GBV health services to crisis affected populations. 2) Reduce excess morbidity and mortality by timely detection and coordinated response to epidemic prone diseases3 . Enhance resilience and promote humanitarian-development linkages to strengthen health system recovery and coping mechanisms to vulnerable population of Akobo, Duk and Maiwut Counties.
Medicair will employ static and mobile teams to provide health care services in the targeted locations (1 PHCUs and 2 mobile teams in Akobo, 1 PHCU and 2 Mobile teams in Duk and 1 PHCU and 2 mobile teams in Maiwut Counties) to improve accessibility of hard to reach persons cut off by floods, conflicts who cannot access health services.
The target Population is based on severity of vulnerable population and will include 34,370 in total men 9,967 (29%), women 10,655 (31%), boys 6,530 (19%) and girls 7,218 (21%) while persons with disability being 5,85 (1.7%). 17,872 (52%) of the population will be women of the targeted population.
The activities include i) carry out disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection prevention and response, ii) OPD consultation, iii) case management of common illnesses, iv) immunization against vaccine preventable disease, v)Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC)(ANC,PNC and skilled deliveries), vi) Clinical Management of Rape, vii) Mental Health and Psychosocial Support, viii) Capacity Building, ix) infection prevention and control, x) community health service linkage, mobilization and consultative meetings

1 - Needs

Humanitarian assistance is needed to support an estimated population of about 8.9 million people in need. According to South Sudan health service functionality report (October 2022), out of 1980 health facilities, only 11 percent are providing the full package of BPHNS and 46 percent are moderately functional, implying inadequate access and unavailability of some health services to some communities in many parts of the country. 58 percent of the Bomas in the country are still not having Boma health teams (Community Health system). South Sudan still records one of the highest Maternal mortality rates in the world, only 39 percent women are availing recommended four Antenatal Care Services and 14.7 percent skilled birth attendance. According to an ISNA survey, approximately 112 under-five children per 1000 live births are dying every year and at least 18.5% households reported losing a relative (HNO,2023).Jonglei and Upper Nile states are among the 10 States in South Sudan with highest health access gaps due to compounding shocks, weak health system, health facility destruction with floods, increase in displaced and returned population time and again. Weak WASH system and limited placement of WASH facilities in health facilities mostly in Jonglei and Unity State with May 2022 Cholera outbreak has been declared in Rubkona county, Unity State. April-July,2022 IPC report had projected localized conflicts, revenge killings, cattle raiding and intercommunal conflicts as likely to disrupt normal livelihood subjecting an estimated 897,000 (78.1%) to IPC Phase 3 in the County. According to IPC projection Dec 2022-March 2023, counties in Jonglei (Duk) will likely be in Catastrophe due to clashes resulting to civilian displacement, destruction of properties, and access impediments to humanitarian actors. Additionally, prevailing food insecurity is driven by the effects of significant underlying vulnerabilities that have built up over time due to the protracted conflict and recurrent shocks.These targeted counties in Jonglei and Upper Nile state were seriously affected by the ragging floods and most communities were cut off by poor infrastructure and flooding made it difficult to reach people in need with health services (HNO 2022). Given the Covid-19 pandemic and third wave underway in South Sudan, preventive measure and follow of protocol is still poor since more of COVID-19 measures dependently from across the borders which hamper COVID-19 measures (HNO 2022). Lack of WASH facilities, and High GAM prevalence above emergency threshold of 15% in these targeted counties compound the already precarious health situation, and therefore integrated health service delivery will be key in reducing maternal mortality rates. This will ensure, protection needs, nutrition needs, WASH interventions in health facilities, and provision of quality health services to reduce the mortality and morbidity rates in all the target counties

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through convening project inception, community engagement meetings and also ensure there is a focal point person to ensure most community members understand what is required by them. Use of suggestion box as well as help desks at the health facility levels to allow those who can not read and write be given feedback and support. Participatory Learning and Continuous Improvement through Boma Health Committee will play an integral part of this project delivery. Medicair will organize regular program performance and quality review meetings with staff, beneficiaries and partners to assess progress towards objectives and work plans, monitor project performance and quality based on monitoring and beneficiary feedback, identified risks and risk management strategies during implementation. At the end of the project Medicair will carry out a project evaluation and document the process used to achieve project outputs and outcomes. The report will also be shared with the beneficiaries

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Medicair will engage male and female in an equal balance thereafter train them on Prevention, Reporting of Sexual, Exploitation, Abuse and Harassments (PRSEAH), ensure all staff are oriented and taken through PSEA policies and later sign code of conduct on PSEA. The respective project staff leading this project will be guided by the standard operating Procedures (SOP) of PSEA and where applicable will use the community based complaint Mechanism to address cases of SEA. Medicair will ensure that PSEA IEC materials are used and are visible in all designated areas of service delivery. In collaboration with other partner, efforts will be consolidated to conduct capacity building of front line workers on PSEA and extend applicability of SOP to other NGOs at grassroots level. Identification, counselling and proper referral for victims of gender-based violence, including rape victims, will be strengthened both at facility and community level through training of health staff, BHI teams and other community stakeholders, health promoters, mother to mother support groups (M2MSGs), traditional birth attendants (TBAs) and youth groups, and non-medical staff working in health facilities on Psychosocial First aid (PFA), GBV principles and referral pathways. Special efforts will be made to engage women’s groups, adolescents and people living with disability, given gender dynamics in South Sudan, as women may be reluctant to discuss issues with men but prefer to raise and discuss issues and concerns with other women.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

All the beneficiaries complainants and feedback will be taken very positively, analyzed to inform change in project implementation design to cater for the needs of the vulnerable populations. These feedback will be used to influence project selection criteria of health facility sites, recruitments, project targets, assessments and other WASH facilities design.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Risk 8.1 Medium Likelihood, High Impact Before the conducting treatment/registration/distribution event, Medicair will work closely with the local authority, humanitarian partner to identify the appropriateness of treatment/distribution site including the distance between the location of targeted population and planned treatment/distribution site, and space of site. In addition, Medicair will help identify potential protection risk during this distribution exercise and will respond accordingly. Technical assistance – for example in the form of training and partner agreements – will also be provided. Risk 8.4 Medium Likelihood, High Impact In its project locations, Medicair established that survivors – especially those living in remote communities – find challenges in accessing protection/referral services due to insufficient coverage of the said services, social stigma, and/or not being able to afford transportation cost. Similarly, survivors have little to no awareness of the aforementioned services due to insufficient information on available services. To counter this, Medicair will engage with available service providers and communicate their functionalities to concerning communities. This will be done through direct awareness raising and empowering multiple entry points – community chiefs, local protection structures such as Women Groups, Youth Groups – to equip them for further awareness raising, post-incident support and referral. Furthermore, Medicair will disseminate information in at least one other local language to reach the majority of the population. It will also engage influential figures, men and boys to secure their support in addressing the socio-economic barriers for survivors to access post-incident care services. Furthermore, Medicair will work with referral pathway and protection actors to review and upgrade the accessibility and quality relevant services. Finally, when there is a protection case where survivors either face direct threats or require support to access post-incident services, Medicair will facilitate access and deter threats through protective accompaniment and referral. Risk 8.5 Medium Likelihood, High Impact As part of its community engagement effort, Medicair will make a conscious effort of including marginalized groups, PwD and Older persons, women, boys and girls into its consultations, decision-making processes and/or supported community protection structures. Whenever engaging with a community, Medicair will identify most at-risk persons such as PwD, Older persons, single mothers, widows etc., and by passing this information to humanitarian partners Medicair could ensure access to humanitarian assistance. Besides, Medicair will required provide support and/or protective accompaniment to certain humanitarian assistance, for example distributions. Specific and appropriate measures – such as the use of visual/hearing aid – will be used to ensure feedback is collected from at-risks persons. Once feedback is collected, Medicair will disaggregate data by age, sex, and PwD, thus ensuring age, gender and disability specific concerns are captured and can be used in project cycle. Risk 8.9 Medium Likelihood, Medium Impact To mitigate the risk of land dispute and tenure insecurity, Medicair will facilitate dialogues between host and returnee communities. At project start, Medicair will engage host community to set a peaceful ground for returnees by conducting sessions on peaceful co-existence and social cohesion. In cases of actual disputes, meetings will be facilitated as soon as tension arise or violence erupts. Regular communication channels, engagement of local authorities and community protection/peace structures.

12.1.3. Collective Outcome 3 - Flood related displacement is 30% lower in 2025 than in 2021.

The project is designed to offer health services to all groups of beneficiaries without discrimination. The project will also ensure community engagement is achieved to support in building dykes around health facilities and as well as household levels, this will ensure floods do not displace the community from their original homes.

12.1.2. Collective Outcome 2 - By 2023, a minimum package for prevention, risk mitigation and response to GBV rolled out in 80% of priority locations, focusing on safe, timely access to quality case management, psychosocial services and capacity building.

Medicair will ensure a dedicated SGBV focal person is included in the project to mainly ensure that all survivors and beneficiaries receive services timely. Establish inclusive referral system and feedback mechanism to allow interaction with the community in terms of reporting GBV cases and hove to respond to the cases effectively. Staff trained on case management to ensure. We will improve on monitoring, reporting and management of cases of GBV including psychosocial support and clinical management of rape.

12.1.1. Collective Outcome 1 - By 2023 (lean season May – July), there is a reduction in the number of people in IPC 4 (by 20%) and IPC 5 (Zero).

During health service delivery, Medicair will be doing growth monitoring which is key in the identification of malnutrition and manage or refer cases for treatment and thus reducing GAM rates. The project will ensure Management of medical complications among children U5 with SAM and surveillance through MUAC screening for under 5 children who seek medical care at the health facilities and refer all malnourished children to the nearest nutrition sites, Convergence of health and nutrition activities across all health facilities and enabling Joint health and nutrition education/promotion initiatives. Community Health Workers will form part of the Community based Case Management and the mobile team spearhead MUAC screen at the community level, refer and later trace and track any defaulter from the nutrition sites. The project will also coordinate with FSL sector to ensure that kitchen garden demos are established within nutrition sites situated within health/nutrition as well as hand washing & sanitation facilities.
