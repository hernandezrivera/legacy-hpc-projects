Project Summary

This project targets 4 Counties in 3 States. These include Yambio and Ezo Counties in Western Equatoria, Aweil South County in Northern Bahr El-Ghazal and Bor South County in Jonglei.
The project aims to integrate WASH response with Health to reduce morbidity and mortality caused by water borne/related diseases, increase access to WASH services and build WASH resilience through offering sustainable solutions (long lasting water points), establishment of water committees, registration of water points in national registry, GBV mitigation, disability inclusion and conducting conflict sensitivity analysis prior to implementation.
The prolonged conflict and limited Government investment have had a devastating impact on the country’s infrastructure and basic service delivery. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. In addition, spikes in national violence, COVID-19 that has had a devastating and multi-faceted socio-economic impact on people, including severe economic contractions, soaring in prices of basic commodities, loss of livelihoods particularly in urban areas, increased protection risks, and disrupted access to basic services. Therefore, living standard and wellbeing of an estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices in 2021. (HNO, 2021)
The project targets the host communities, IDPs and Returnees. The total beneficiary figure is 57,872 people. The targeted recipients of this response are disaggregated as follows: 11,574 Women, 17,535 Girls, 11,401 Men and 17,362 Boys. The beneficiary figures are inclusive of the persons with disabilities in the communities. The intervention aims to reduce morbidity and mortality through integrating WASH response with Health, provide safe, equitable and dignified access to WASH services through increasing access to quality WASH services by drilling boreholes, rehabilitation of non-functional hand pumps, rehabilitation of solar powered Mini Water Yards, establishment of water committees, mitigating GBV, promotion of community hygiene and sanitation through CLTS triggering, WASH related disease prevention including COVID 19, solid waste and excreta management.
The project will build resilience of beneficiary communities by strengthening existing structures and establishing long lasting water points.

1 - Needs

Poor access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition conditions across South Sudan contributes to one of the highest child mortality rates in the world (HNO, 2021). At national level 70% of households report members affected by a water or vector-borne disease. National access to safe water is at 35% and only 10% of the population has access to improved sanitation (UNICEF, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) in South Sudan Briefing note (July to September 2020), December 2020, available at https://uni.cf/3bE7zJ2). In Yambio 21-40% HHs have access to Safe Water, Ezo has >20%, Aweil South has 21-40%, and Bor South has 61-81%. Yambio and Bor South are among the Counties with the highest reported water/vector borne diseases. Yambio having 87.1% and Bor South 83.6% HHs reported with Water/vector Borne Sickness. Across South Sudan, 61% of the Households (HHs) reported having access to at least 3 liters of water per person per day with 17% of people unable to collect enough water for drinking, and 39% of people reported having insufficient access to water for hand washing. According to the SSD_WASH_PIN_2021_Severity_booklet, proportion of HHs with Access to Water Quantity are as follows, Yambio >20%, Ezo 41-60%, Bor South <80%, and Aweil South 21-40%. Only 50.5% of HH in Yambio have access to enough water quantity. Proportion of HHs without access to Hand washing and soap are as follows Bor South 21-30%, Aweil South 6-10%, Yambio 11-20%, and Ezo 11-20%. New health-related WASH needs arose in 2020 because of COVID-19 and will persist into 2021 hence the need to promote hygiene and sanitation including hand washing practices. In Aweil South only 0.93% HHs have access to sanitation. At the Country level, open defecation is practiced by 73% of the population. Access to sanitation remained low, with only 17% of households reported owning a latrine, 4% have access to a shared latrine, and 2% have access to a communal/institutional latrine. Northern Bahr el Ghazal had 9% HH with access to sanitation while Jonglei had 10%. Limited access to appropriate and dignified sanitation and hand washing facilities, in particular in urban and peri-urban settings, increases the risk of cholera and other diarrheal or vector-borne diseases (HNO, 2021), women children and people living with disabilities are at higher risk. Only 35% of households have access to an improved water source in less than 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns (FSNMS Round 26). Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection face an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. In 2020, 16% of the people using water facilities reported safety concerns. Ezo has very high concern for water related protection concern at 46.3% of HH that reported concerns while Bor South reported between 11-20% of HH having protection concerns. These two counties have a high prevalence of GBV at WASH facilities. This project aims at reducing the risk of violence for vulnerable groups, especially women and children when accessing WASH services. This project will contribute in providing sustainable safe quality and enough water, improve hygiene and sanitation of beneficiary communities and reduce WASH GBV incidents especially amongst the most vulnerable groups like women and elderly. This project will also build WASH resilience of the targeted communities through working with local existing structures, building capacity of water committees and proving long lasting water solutions.

2 - Project activities

DRILLING OF NEW BOREHOLES (6). Drilling of new a borehole involves geo-physical survey, drilling with mud or air rotary method, installation of casings (plain and screen casings), and well development, pump testing, construction of platform, installation of hand pump and water quality testing.REHABILITATION OF BOREHOLES (60 BOREHOLES). This involves assessment of the broken down boreholes to determine the spares required and local materials needed. Water Quality Analysis is conducted after completion of the rehabilitation.REHABILITATION OF MINI WATER YARDS (4 MINI WATER YARDS). This involves assessment and replacement of damaged parts such as solar panels, control boxes, submersible pumps, tower, cables, water tanks, water collection points, and fence and drainage channel. Water Quality Analysis is conducted after completion of the rehabilitation.WATER COMMITTEE TRAINING (70 WATER COMMITTEES). The Water Management Committees are established and trained. They are equipped with knowledge to operate and maintain the borehole. TRAINING OF PUMP TECHNICIAN GROUPS (4). This training is to create WASH sustainability and create resilience amongst the beneficiary communities.GBV RISK MITIGATION ACTIVITIES: Through conducting conflict sensitivity analysis/safety audits in all the 70 water points. And communal latrines constructed. Conduct GBV Focus group discussions including all the different categories of people (men and women). This will identify any possible risks to the project and beneficiaries and then mitigate them in collaboration with all the stakeholders.RURAL WATER DEPARTMENT SPARE PARTS AND REVOLVING FUNG MANAGEMENT TRAINING (4): Hold training for RWD Units at County level and communities for overall WASH supervision; spare parts revolving fund management, sanitation and hygiene promotion.STRENGTHEN WASH MIS/DATABASE SYSTEM (4). Strengthen the WASH MIS/database system for effective monitoring and input to community and state WASH databases through continuous data collection in the 4 Counties.CONDUCT WATER QUALITY TREATMENT TRAINING (4): Train MoH, State on sustainable water quality treatment methods at the sources.CONDUCT WASH ASSESSMENT IN 4 COUNTIES: Conduct participatory WASH inventory assessment and data mapping using KOBO CONSTRUCTION OF 4 BLOCKS OF INCLUSIVE SEX DISAGGREGATED INSTITUTIONAL VIP LATRINES (8 BLOCKS OF 4 STANCES EACH). These will be constructed in 2 Health Centres. They will be disability friendly. Installed with hand washing facilitiesCONSTRUCTION OF 10 BLOCKS OF SEX DISAGGREGATED INCLUSIVE COMMUNAL LATRINES (10 BLOCKS OF 2 STANCES EACH). These latrines will be constructed in crowded settlement areas where there is poor sanitation that will impact negatively on the people. Install hand washing HYGIENE EDUCATION AND PROMOTION AT COMMUNITY LEVEL (79). Disease prevention (WASH –related diseases and COVID-19), safe excreta management This will include, radio talk shows, use of animator groups to pass messages, celebration of Global hand washing day, World Toilet day, and World Water day. Procure and distribute IEC materials INSTALL 25 HAND WASHING FACIITIES. In 25 public places. This is to fight the spread of COVID-19 Virus.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

• International Aid Services adheres to the UN policy of zero tolerance to Sexual Exploitation and abuse. • IAS has a PSEA policy which can be found on the site, www.ias-intl.org• In community meetings, IAS management shares contact information of the senior management members with all the beneficiaries who will contact them in case any staff mis conducts him/herself with beneficiaries whom they are supposed to protect.• International Aid Services also conducts training and awareness about Prevention of Sexual Exploitation such that all stakeholders are aware.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

• The project will identify the most vulnerable members in the community (persons with disability, those at disadvantage socio-economically, elderly etc), identify and address environmental/institutional attitudes and barriers and enablers for people with disabilities to participate.• The project will make consultations directly with the different groups of vulnerable members and their representatives (associations) where necessary. This will ensure feedback is captured from the most vulnerable that have been neglected.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

• The project will provide information to the beneficiaries during the launching of the project where the project document will be shared to all the stakeholders, the project details will be shared to all beneficiaries during community meetings.• During the mid and end term project evaluations, data is shared with all the relevant stakeholders. This helps the beneficiaries are able to track progress of the project and eventually own the project.• IAS has a Complaints and Feedback mechanism tool which captures different categories of complaints ranging from issues dealing with project design, transparency, accountability, M and E, Sexual exploitation, violation of human rights and early warning. These complaints are captured in data base and responses registered.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

• The beneficiary feedback helps in shaping programming. The feedback is analyzed and used to make managerial decision that will improve the project impact. For example feedback may be used to change or make additions on project design, change of human resource, become inclusive, avoid conflicts; early warning from beneficiaries may help in re-scheduling of activities. Feedback leads to transparency and accountability to the beneficiaries about the project. Removes aspects of the project that may violate human rights and may not be culturally acceptable amongst the beneficiary community.
