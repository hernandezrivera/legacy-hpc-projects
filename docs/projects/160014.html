Project Summary

Somalia is suffering from a complex humanitarian emergency for over two decades of internal conflicts. The prolonged conflict destroyed most of the basic social services infrastructure including hospitals and healthcare facilities. This has led to the flight of doctors, nurses and other medical professionals and the Somalia National Government needs international support to renovate and restore necessary services (Landinfo, 2018). Although the FSNAU post GU 2019 findings showed that marginal decrease in the median prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition(GAM of 13.8% down from 14% of Post GU 2018 and 17.4 post Gu 2017. However, over million children suffer from acute malnutrition of whom 178,000 children are projected to be affected with life threatening severe acute malnutrition across the country/ The report further cited the lack of life saving medical emergency services to the populace in Banadir region as well as poor and inadequate health referral system.
Aamin organization is currently implementing SHINE project under consortium led by ACF through support of DFID in Banadir region. Aamin Ambulance provides a twenty four hour, unparalleled free ambulance service in Mogadishu, Somalia. From July to September 2019, Aamin ambulance transported and facilitated 638 affected people (427 female, 211 male) within the ten districts covered by the SHINE project. This was a significant increase compared to last quarter by 284. The increase can be attributed to increased awareness of the availability of the free ambulance service in the ten districts. Therefore, Aamin ambulance seeks to bridge the gap of unreliable health and emergency response system through this free ambulance and first aid system to the people of Mogadishu. We take cognizance of the needs the vulnerable in the community and hence our project also plays focus on the needs of the vulnerable in the community including women and children. Through a recent needs assessment conducted by Aamin ambulance, it was established that over 2100 expectant mothers in our target districts are in need of maternal health services (including referrals) to ensure a safe pregnancy and delivery, including emergency obstetric services. The situation is even life threatening when the mothers are in need of these emergencies during the wee hours of the night. Our emergency project hence seeks scale up efforts in ensuring that no woman or new-born dies during birth by avoiding prolonged labour. Currently Aamin ambulance has 16 fully equipped ambulances staffed with qualified and well trained professionals to provide pre-hospital care to patients. The project currently operates in Mogadishu with a future plan to expand operations to three other states; Hirshabelle, Southwest, Galmudug and Jubbaland. Through this project Aamin ambulance will targets 22,270 affected people with OPD consultation, treatment of common illness, incidences, and early referral (e.g. complicated deliveries/2100 pregnant women) for further treatment and care. The target group will be disaggregated based on the monthly outcome.
Aamin Ambulance will adopt mobile clinic strategy to reach out to vulnerable people in IDPs areas and bring service close to their community. The team will provide Out-patient services to the people at the convenience of their villages. The composition of the health expert that will be attending the mobile clinic will include Medical doctors, nurses and clinical officers. Aamin Ambulances target to reach out to 20,000 people through our mobile clinic OPD services with a composition of: 5,000 women, 6,000 girls, 6,000 boys and 3,000 men. Since Aamin will go further to the most remote area, we believe we will attain the set target and deliver high quality service the community has been yearning for. However, since the gap for health service is big, Aamin ambulance will bridge it by bringing service close to the people and providing free referral services for people in critical condition.

Mitigation Measures

Risk 1: Protection of civilians• We shall ensure the access routes to the health facilities and services are safe by identifying routes for our ambulances to operate and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence.• The project shall employ staff with skills and experiences working with children patients • We shall consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether Aamin ambulance interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. Risk 2: Exclusion and marginalization • We shall ensure that beneficiaries know of available health services and assistance where/how to obtain it. • We shall communicate information through various means to ensure communication reaches the broader community • We shall involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to health needs. • Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalised persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for emergency health services. • Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response Risk 3: Protection mainstreaming • In cooperation with protection actors, establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidents concerning hospitals to advocate against attacks on hospitals and military use of hospitals • Ensure the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe: • We are aware of how power dynamics between our staff and the patient can affect the patient’s response and attitude towards the staff/organisation and service provided • Ensure all our staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with the beneficiaries understand, sign and adhere to Aamin Code of Conduct • In cooperation with protection actors, we shall enhance the capacity of community health workers to monitor, report and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures.
