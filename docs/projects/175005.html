Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis which has rocked the North West and South West Regions for over 4 years today has resulted in massive population displacements and the shutting down of educational facilities across both regions. Aside from the numerous food, WASH and security challenges faced by these populations, there is an urgent educational need for these displaced children in host communities. UNICEF statistics hold that 600,000 people are out of school in the rural and semi-urban areas in the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon. A study by LUKEMEF shows that 36.7% of these people aged 14-17 years say they will not go back to school no matter what. These children are likely never going to get any formal education. In response to this and mindful of the fact that regular education is challenged in the Northwest and Southwest region by armed fighters, and mindful of the fact that majority of the IDP population resides in the South West, West and Littoral regions, while we advocate for all children to get back to school, our intervention will be to provide these 36.7% (220,200 children) with an opportunity to get a life of dignity through formal education in the West and Littoral regions. Within the life of this project, we in tend to provide structural support to schools in view of building of classrooms, provision of chairs and tables, provision of food through school cafeterias( food will be directed to the most vulnerable students (500 in each of the six most populated schools ) via a food program, where in students will be placed based on their vulnerability. The food program will run for the first and second term which is 6 months, and food will be handed out to students once a day during their launch break and will comprise of water, fruits, vegetables and an indigenous dish) and increasing the schools’ ability to mitigate the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic and other health issues by buffing up their dispensaries and providing WASH and COVID-19 safety equipment. Also, the project seeks to support school children through the provision of school needs to the most vulnerable IDP and host communities’ children.
The inability for IDP children to attend schools and for host communities’ schools to provide quality education to the greatly increased number of children in need of education is primarily hinged to financial adequacy on both parties. In that regard, this project comes in to meet this need and enable schools to serve the children who are in serious need of education if they are to carve out a life of dignity for themselves in the years ahead.

Plan fields

1 - Besoins / Needs

Out of School Children: The concentration of IDPs within the Littoral and West regions of Cameroon has greatly increased the pressure on available resources, especially educational resources. A multitude of children are out of schools and on the streets hawking in a bid to support their families at the expense of a dignifying future for themselves. This is supported by UNICEF statistics which hold that 600,000 people are out of school in the rural and semi-urban areas thanks to the North West South West crisis; and a large portion of this population have migrated to the nearby West region in search of survival and safety.. The result of children and youths who are out of school is an increase in crime wave and an introduction to a life of crime for vulnerable children in a bit to meet ends meet. The inability for IDP children to attend schools and for host communities’ schools to provide quality education to the greatly increased number of children in need of education is primarily hinged to financial inadequacy on both parties. In that regard, this project comes in to meet this need and enable schools to serve the children who are in serious need of education if they are to carve out a life of dignity for themselves in the years ahead.
