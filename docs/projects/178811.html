Project Summary

This project will support the monitoring of population movements across non-Government-controlled areas in northwest Syria to make available reliable and verified data for the humanitarian response in an efficient and coordinated manner. An online monitoring dashboard will provide humanitarian actors with the latest information per community level, regular/monthly overviews and trend analysis will be shared with cluster members, and ad-hoc emergency updates will be produced and shared when required. Technical guidance will be provided to cluster members to support data collection.

4 - Needs

Due to the lack of access to areas in northern Syria and the remote management of the cross-border operation, obtaining comprehensive and accurate data on populations and displacements is a challenge for humanitarian actors. In 2020, the CCCM Cluster tracked over 2 million individual displacements between January and December 2020, which include second or third displacements, meaning that some people may have been forced to move multiple times within the same period and that the reasons for movement may vary. On average, the cluster tracked over 33,000 population movements on a monthly basis, noting the huge increase earlier in the year (1 million in February). The CCCM Cluster has developed an efficient inter-agency system to collect data in a coordinated manner, triangulate, and when possible, verify the data. UNHCR’s partner will use Rapid Displacement Monitoring (RDM) system to collect near real time snapshot about current location displaced people in order to produce categorized (age, gender, vulnerability and shelter type) number of IDPs within 24 hours and identify their top three needs on community levels. In addition, the Cluster also relied on specialized organizations to provide data and information on population movements to ensure sufficient geographical coverage and technical capacity. Through its partner, UNHCR aims to continue providing such dedicated support to the CCCM Cluster under the IDP Situation Monitoring Initiative.

5 - Implementation of activities

Muzun For Humanitarian & Development will cover 9 districts that host 1,302 IDP sites listed (ISIMM Dec 2020 Update) and other spontaneously established locations that could be identified in future within coverage area. The areas that would be covered are: Jisr Al-shoughour/ Jabal Saman/ Jarablus/ Al-bab/ Azaz/ Afrin/ Ariha/Idleb/ Harim MHD has established a realistic work plan for 5 months that includes all types of data collection as outlined by UNHCR, therefore, it will contribute significantly to the CCCM cluster response considering the strict timeline of cluster’s data collection activities of IDP Site Integrated Monitoring Matrix, ISIMM Plus & ad-hoc reporting. Below are activities in which MHD is planning to implement in the planned, informal, listed and unlisted IDP sites, throughout this project: 1. IDP Site Integrated Monitoring Matrix: ISIMM is a tool developed by CCCM Cluster that includes all identified IDP sites by CCCM cluster in NW Syria. 2. The ISIMM Plus3. Displacement Tracking of IDPs and Host Community.4. Ad-hoc data collection and reporting, including: 1) Distress calls in IDP site: 2) Incident reporting for any events that impact IDPs and IDP Sites. 3) Ad-hoc surveys and assessments.REACH/ACTED project 2021 intervention is a continuation of the IDP Situation Monitoring Initiative, which began in 2016 as a partnership between CCCM Cluster and REACH. REACH, an inter-agency initiative of ACTED and it’s global partner IMPACT Initiatives, will continue to monitor the movements of IDPs and Spontaneous Return populations across accessible, opposition-held areas of Idleb, Hama & Aleppo governorates in NW Syria to make available frequent, consist & verified information to inform humanitarian Community

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Physical and sexual exploitation and abuse. Mitigating measures: UNHCR ensures that partners have a code of conduct (CoC) in place, supports developing/reviewing PSEA prevention materials, organizes internal and external CoC/PSEA sessions, and follows-up on PSEA on a regular basis. PSEA focal points are identified in the operation whom individuals can contact and/or report misconduct by humanitarian staff. Risk 2: Assessment fatigue among beneficiaries and cluster members. Mitigating measures: The purposes of monitoring and assessments are clarified to beneficiaries without raising expectations. The tools and questionnaires are developed to be as light as possible.

UNHCR is co-leading the PSEA Working Group in Syria. UNHCR will work closely with other stakeholders, including the GBV Sub-working Group and PSEA network to ensure there is an adequate and multifaceted response in place with due regard to the “do no harm” principle. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Security and lack of access Mitigating measures: Partners are supported to develop and implement Safety & Security and Duty of Care policies. Programme design provide flexibilities for partners to suspend activities when security of its staff is at threat. Risk 2: aid diversion. Mitigating measures: Capacity building of partners to improve vulnerability assessments in accordance with AGD. Emphasize importance of impartiality to partners and systematically monitor adherence and feedback from beneficiaries, with corresponding corrective measures. Establish an active and informed complaint mechanism. Establish independent third party monitoring arrangements. Risk 3: Interference in humanitarian assistance by armed groups. Mitigating measures: Open communication lines and relationship are maintained with partners on local developments and specific incidents, and IA coordination is informed about (major) incidents to ensure unified and effective messaging to Armed Opposition Group (AOG).
