Project Summary

Displacement caused by conflict and natural disasters significantly affect school aged children and the education system is vulnerable to both natural disasters and conflict-related emergencies. According to (OCHA; March, 2022) in Amhara only 4,107 schools (25 per cent completely damaged), affecting more than 1.8 million children; and at least 200 schools in Afar damaged, affecting more than 150,000 students and more than 1,000 schools were damaged or looted in Tigray affecting an estimated 160,000 students. The current drought is also highly affecting education of children in Ethiopia. According to (UNICEF; https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/prolonged-drought-pushing-families-ethiopia-brink),over 155,000 children in the lowlands of Somali and Oromia regions have dropped out of school so they can help fetch water - often travelling long distances - or look after other children while their caregivers try to find water for their families and cattle. Children who are out of school are at risk of exploitation or are being pushed into dangerous coping mechanisms.
In conflict-hit northern Ethiopia like Tigray, Amhara and Afar a large number of children are out of school. Government sources indicated around 1.4million children in Tigray and the same figure in Amhara region are made out of schools due to the conflict. Education of more than 88, 000 children in Afar was affected due to the conflict. Many schools are occupied by IDPs. Teachers are absent in schools as many of them displaced and the available are not trained enough on EiE. Schools are damaged, looted, lacking equipment, furniture and WASH facilities to cater to the needs of all children including IDPs.
Education response plays a protective function that communities can rely upon to enhance the safety and wellbeing of their children. The project targets 212074(101017F) crisis affected children in Tigray, Amhara, Afar, Oromia and Somali regions and it will operate in 19 woredas. The objective of the project is to ensure equitable access to safe, protective and conducive (pre-primary and primary) learning environments for 270, 603 (149, 868F) crisis-affected girls, boys including Children with disabilities. With this project Save the Children avails a safe and conducive learning environment by establishing temporary learning spaces. Children will receive learning materials. The project will conduct safe back to school to enhance children return to learning. Teachers will be trained on education in emergencies, psychosocial supports and on interactive learning approach like ELM and Literacy and Numeracy Boost approaches. The project also works to strengthen education coordination system and build capacity of school leadership. The project works towards gender equality that both boys and girls will get equal access to a safe, protective learning opportunity. To this end, our emergency education services and learning practices are gender sensitive. Teachers will be trained on gender sensitive teaching and learning methodology, Gender Based Violence (GPV) and Psychosocial supports. To support education of adolescent girls and ensure their enrollment and class attendance the project will provide dignity kits for girls.
Save the Children will work partnering with National Non-governmental organizations (NNGOs) and strengthen local institutions, exchange experiences. Save the Children will also involve local structures in all project cycle management, build their capacity and work towards community ownership to ensure sustainability.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

In regards to accountability, Save the Children’s accountability procedures and guidelines will be applied throughout project implementation in accordance with the CHS (Core Humanitarian Standard). Accordingly, Save the Children will share the program activities and progress reports to beneficiary community (including children) and stakeholders in ways that are accessible and appropriate to the context. In addition, Project information (project goal, major activities), staff code of conduct and child safeguarding issues will be communicated through different channels such as, meeting, and public gathering using megaphones. This creates opportunities for target groups to follow up implementation and provide feedback to improve the quality of program. Equally, Save the Children is committed to making itself accountable to the children and communities we work with and for. A key element of this commitment is the need to establish Complaints Response Mechanisms (CRMs) in all of Save the Children’s programmes. In this regard, project/context specific accountability systems will be established, and the project will have a detailed accountability plan. Technical Project staff will be oriented on the implementation of accountability systems and response mechanisms. In addition, focus group discussions (FGDs) with target beneficiaries will be conducted in randomly selected districts and Kebeles on a regular basis in order to receive complaints and feedback from target beneficiaries. In addition, different meetings will be held with target beneficiaries at community level, on critical process such as on setting selection criteria, beneficiary selection and verification of selected beneficiaries to receive the complaints and feedback from target beneficiaries, children and other stakeholders. Complaints and feedback collected through different means will be gathered and will be updated Complaint Response Mechanism database that will be periodically analyzed and synthesized for decision-making and to take corrective measures to improve the implementation of the project. What is more, the project will consider the do no harm principle in all its actions. SC will ensure that no harm comes to beneficiaries as result of their participation in the project. SC will keep beneficiaries’ information confidential to protect them from risks that could come because information sharing. It will also regularly assess the risks of the projects on beneficiaries to prevent doing harm to beneficiaries despite the good intention.
