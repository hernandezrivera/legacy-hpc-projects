Project Summary

Somalia is currently experiencing triple crises caused by; resurging drought, COVID-19 pandemic and desert locust invasion. The FSNAU and OCHA update September 2021 indicates 5.9 M people are in in need of Humanitarian assistants. Out of these, 3.5 million are classified to be in IP3 and 4 levels ( IPC 3- Crisis and IPC 4- Emergency ) from Aug 21 to December 2021 and require immediate lifesaving support. In total, there is about 2.9 million displaced people in Somalia and an estimated 1 million of these are school-aged children. Currently 1.4 million school children are in need of humanitarian assistance. According to the 2022 EIE Cluster priority, it is envisaged the cluster will target 833,477 (375,065 girls) education in emergencies assistance. These children are at high risk of displacement and dropping out of school unless scaled up support is provided during this period of drought. Rising food and water prices are putting additional stress on families who are still struggling with the secondary impact of COVID-19. Many families are no longer able to meet the educational costs of their children as essential commodities of food, water, and health take precedence in family expenditure
CARE Somalia need assessment conducted in September 21 established that 788 rural schools in the target areas with an enrollment of 130,000 are currently in need of EIE support. 64% of these schools are faced with shortage of safe learning spaces and latrine facilities and insufficient access to water. 72% of the school also reported to face shortage of qualified teachers and lack teachers’ incentives to hire local volunteer teachers. 70% have also indicated to have limited access to learning materials and lack trained teachers to deal with psychosocial support for children. Close to 91% of the schools reported lack of schools as major challenges limiting access to education. 65% also reported barriers for children with disabilities to access education. These include negative attitudes, unfavorable learning environments without necessary assistive devices, and teachers lacking training on inclusive education. 75% also reported need for need for school feeding. Other major issues reported include lack of uniform (55%) and inaccessibility of menstrual kits for girls (53% )
The project will provide basic education support to Crisis-Emergency affected boys and girls who are out of school or are at risk of dropping out of school in the regions of Sool, Sanaag, Bari, and Mudug. It also introduces innovative ways to address challenges hindering access to safe quality and protective learning in schools.
The Proposed interventions aims at:
(1) objective 1: Improve access to a safe and protective education for emergency and crises affected children
(2) Objective 2: Ensure vulnerable children and youth are engaged in lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion
The project will target 34000 school aged children (17000 boys & 17000 girls ) who are either out of school or at risks of dropping from schools. This is inclusive of 1,200 children living with disabilities.
The response will undertake wide range of EIE activities prioritized by EIE cluster and Ministry of Education aimed at expanding access to quality education and improve retention for crisis affected school aged girls and boys while putting priority to the needs of each gender group. The project will mainstream the HRP 2022 strategic pillars including CVA, protection centrality, localization, community participation, gender mainstreaming, accountability and advocacy and extending the support to children living with disabilities. The design of the project activities will take into consideration the needs of the disable groups. This will be done through consulting them on their needs, integrating safely to classes of normal children and ensuring they benefit from all project supplies and infrastructures like classrooms and latrines by having access trams

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will provide Teaching Learning materials through Cash Voucher Assistance (CVA), to 8000 school aged children ( 4000 boys and 4000 girls ) selected from the most disadvantaged households with newly enrolled children in schools. The project areas have vibrant markets which can have good supplies of Teaching Learning Materials. (TLMs) as local traders are able to quickly stock adequate TLMS supplies once demand is created through local Cash injection. Currently, the major problem facing the vulnerable households (IDPS and rural host communities ) in the area is widespread loss of income sources as most lost significant portion of their livestock’s to the persistent droughts in the last five years. This has considerably reduced the purchasing power of the vulnerable households, forcing many of them to prioritize the little cash they can access to meet basic needs –Food, Water, medicine- at the expense of the Education. Subsequently most parents in the project target areas cannot adequately meet the costs of Education for their children specially fees and purchase of Teaching Learning materials. The use of Cash Voucher will not only help parents to acquire TLMs for their children but do this at the most convenient time of need. In addition provision of teachers incentives through direct cash transfer to teachers on monthly bases will reduce the costs of school fees in many schools located in the IDP and rural pastoral rural settlements. In addition, CARE Somalia has been implementing large scale CVA based intervention a cross most parts of Somalia for the last five years, including the three regions targeted by the project. CARE CVA interventions covers sectors of Food security, Livelihoods, WASH , Nutrition and Protection. The adoption of CVA in the EIE sector will widen the organization foot prints and deepen its experiences and knowledge on CVA applications across diverse sectors of programming. On the other hand, the local target communities are well versed with the approach of organization using CVA to provide humanitarian assistances. As the result, CARE envisages their would be minimal resistant’s / challenges from the communities in adopting this kind of support. Furthermore, the use of CVA would save operational costs and time for the organization to speed up timely implementation of project activities as it avoids the tedious logistic work involved in the purchase, delivery and distribution of in kind supplies. CARE also anticipate the use of the CVA under this program will effectively reinforce its other cash based relief and recovery programs as the children from the families who are benefiting from such response will equally benefit from the CVA in Education support. This will inevitably enhance the recovery rates of the affected families as the money that would have been used for Education is saved for other households needs especially like acquiring productive assets during recovery.To ensure the voucher correctly achieves its intended purposes of providing essential TLMs to the crisis affected children, CARE will adopt controlled ( conditional ) voucher token, which clearly indicates the items that can be used to redeem with it and the price limits for each of the indicated items.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Cash Voucher Assistant based intervention to provide Teaching Learning Materials will only be applied in schools where this is possible which will be informed by market assessment to be conducted before the implementation of the activities. Most of the major settlements in the project target areas have functional markets and CARE has been doing Cash based intervention using both direct Cash and Voucher paper to target beneficiaries in sectors of Food security, WASH, Protection and Nutrition. The implementation of the CVA component will be coordinated closely with MoE and EIE cluster and Cash Working at Sub cluster level and it will done in areas where market are accessible and teaching and learning materials are available throughout the year from shops near the schools. Comprehensive feasibility market assessment will be done, greatly borrowing from past CARE Market assessment experiences. Finding will be documented and shared with EIE cluster. Priority area will be given to to schools located close to major market centers where children and parent can easily and safely reach to redeem the vouchers Based on a market assessment conducted, information on the cash delivery mechanism, the transfer voucher value based on market prices, the items included in the voucher, the number of transfers as well as the timing of the transfer(s), and monitoring of voucher use will be clearly notified to the cluster through regular briefs which will be coordinated with other Cash actors specially the Cash Working groups which CARE is already an active member.
