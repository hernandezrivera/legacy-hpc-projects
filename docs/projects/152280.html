Project Summary

This project aims is to improve access to basic items and services to meet the basic needs and improve social protection outcomes for the most vulnerable and marginalized boys, girls, male, female and elderly through well coordinated multi-purpose cash grants (MPCG) programming.

Needs

The conflict has had a negative impact on Ukraine’s economy and industrial and agricultural production in Donbass. Unemployment has increased, decreasing household’s income while living costs have increased. The cumulative impact of the conﬂict over the past 3 years has eroded people’s coping capacity, pushing them deeper into vulnerabilities. According to the 2018 Humanitarian Needs Overview, 3.4 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection. Their needs are different in sector, type and scale. Socio-economic exclusion for people living in NGCA and along the ‘contact line’, disruptions/loss of income and livelihoods across much of the east, and higher cost of living trigger unsafe returns and have a knock-on effect on other aspects, including access to healthcare.Between 2013 and 2017 the percentage of the population living below the actual minimum subsistence level increased from 20% in 2013 to 74% in 2017 in Luhanska oblast and from 22% to 66% in Donetska oblast (“Analysis food security and socio-economic situation 29 march 2018”). The reduction of food insecurity on GCA is also a result of the economic improvement in the country, including significant increase of the real salary reaching pre-crisis level of 2013 as well as a pension reform: from 1 January 2017 to 1 January 2018, pensions have increased by 56% in Donetska and 52% in Luhanska oblasts with an average increase of 39% across Ukraine. However, by 1 January 2018, 79% of pensioners received pensions below 3,000 UAH (Actual Minimum Subsistence Level is 3,128 UAH) . People with average salary could cover minimum costs of living for more than two persons, while average pension could cover only 75% of the costs.Conflict has had a negative impact on employment security as almost one-fifth of active households lost employment since the conflict began. Some 45 per cent of the IDPs have difficulties in finding a new job, while youth unemployment rate is 21% and the level of unemployed youth IDPs is 42% compared to 35% of unemployed IDPs (with 68% of unemployed IDPs being women ).The application of (negative) coping strategies due to lack of money to buy food has increased in NGCA to 87% (up from 40%), while in GCA the situation has become slightly better at 53% applying (down from 55%). Female-headed households (57% of total) make up a larger proportion of unemployed households (39% vs 25%), making them particularly vulnerable to negative coping strategies.In 2016 women earned on average only 73.7% of men’s average monthly wage, which has not changed in nearly 20 years.[1] Such gender-based wage gaps create further vulnerability to poverty for women, especially female-headed households. Level of unemployed youth IDPs is 42% compared to 35% of unemployed IDPs[2] (with 68% of unemployed IDPs being women[3). A higher economic vulnerability increases the risks for women and girls to engage in negative coping strategies, including prostitution.[1] USAID Gender Analysis[2] National Employment Service data, 2016[3] ILO, Employment needs assessment and employability of internally displaced persons in Ukraine, 2016

Activities & outcomes

Vulnerability criteria: HH with members with disabilities, HH with members with special needs, female headed households, HH with orphans, etc) Results:Result 1: 13,500 targeted conflict affected boys, girls, men and women have increased capacity to cover their essential basic needs strategies through provision of cash assistanceActivity 1.1: Community mobilization (integrating child protection awareness session and orientation on accountability mechanisms) and beneficiary selectionActivity 1.2 Market price monitoringActivity 1.3: Disbursement of first wave of cash transfers through national post office and/or banksActivity 1.4: Intermediate verification of cash utilization.Activity 1.5 Disbursement of second wave of cash transfers through national post office and/or banksActivity 1.6: Lessons learned session with partnersResult 2: Targeted conflict affected households enrolled in multipurpose cash are resilient against shocks and continue to invest in their children’s survival, learning and protection and reduced negative coping strategiesActivity 2.1: Conduct baseline to beneficiaries prior to disbursement of cashActivity 2.2: Monitoring of cash utilization prior to 2nd disbursementActivity 2.3 Post distribution monitoring and endline after distribution of all tranchesAccess to markets According to Joint Market Monitoring there is no significant changes in availability of financial institutions and markets since the previous round of MM. Working ATMs and banks were reported by 100% respondents in market sub-centers, while in periphery the financial institutions have continued to be mostly non-available. The reorganization of markets has occurred, and new linkages have emerged, which still might lack the capacity to service changing demand. Some locations have limitations in access to construction materials, non-food items etc. Residents of these locations reported that they travel for such materials and items to semi-peripheral markets or market sub-centers. Accountability to Conflict Affected Population• Conflict affected people (male, female, boys, girls & elderly) will have access to mechanisms (social media, hotline) to give feedback• Conflict affected people (male, female, boys, girls & elderly) is to be consulted during the whole project cycle• Information sharing to all conflict affected population (boys, girls, men and women, elderly)CoordinationSave the Children will engage regularly in the meetings of the Cash Working Group and the other Clusters (e.g. FSC, Shelter & NFI, Protection) based in Kyiv and in the sub cluster meetings in Donetsk and Luhansk oblast. SC is working bilaterally with agencies engaged in cash transfer programming (CTP) in the same operating areas particularly on Multi Purpose Cash Grants, as well as through the interagency Cash Working Group led by OCHA, to ensure that transfer values are both harmonized across agencies, and sufficient to meet basic needs as well as ensuring intended program and protection related outcomes.To mitigate risk of beneficiaries duplication while implementing activities there will be cross-check verification with partners and other stakeholders applied.PartnershipSCI will implement through its existing local partner organizations in GCA and NGCA. These local partners have been already trained and have experience in community mobilization, distribution and implementation of shelter related activities.
