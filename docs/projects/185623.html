Project Summary

"The goal of the proposed project is to create protective environment and supportive community for the vulnerable older men, women including PwDs where they can be satisfied and feel safe, secured, protected life in both Refugees and host communities. The theory of change of the project is - if the PwDs and older peoples have provided necessary support assistance and knowledge, if accessibility is increased, if protection risk is reduced, and if community people supportive, then, they will be satisfied, feel safe, secured and protected life and will be less exposed to further psychological harm and other health problem contributing to enhance protection situation.
To create protective environment and supportive community, HelpAge of proposed series interventions that can be contributed to address chronic, acute and multi-layer geriatric and PwDs specific barriers and needs, increase accessibility and thereby reduced protection risk with the provision of specialized and community-based services.
HelpAge is one of the lead organization for protection mainstreaming. ADWG is a technical partner of Protection working Group where HelpAge has been chairing.
This will be achieved through providing assistive products among those have functional difficulties in mobility, visioning and hearing difficulty, physiotherapeutic care with necessary advice, equipment and kits to reduce pain, provide dignity kits and care support assistance to manage daily activities for those having difficulty in self-caring, provide psychosocial support, first-aid toolbox and legal support if required through referral for those affected by AGBV, VAN, PSEA.
Create awareness including caregiver/family members through providing information and messages on AGBV, VAN, PSEA, DRR and others emerging issues. Building capacity of the humanitarian staffs and community peoples through providing information about specific barriers/difficulties, adaptive features, facilities, needs, protection risks, and provide technical support to generate SADD and appropriate use of WG-SSQ.
To ensure AAP, HelpAge will focus on strengthening community-based protection networks through creating awareness, building capacity to address barriers, increase sensitivity regarding PwDs and OPs issues, risks including DRR.
The project will also provide support in one host communities of income generating input support, skills training and linkages with local government bodies to ensure social cohesion.
The proposed project will implement in total 18 refugee camps including 8 existing HelpAge operated camps (Camp-8E, 11,12,13,14,15,18 and 19) and 10 new camps (Camp-2W, 4E, 7, 8W, 10, 20, 20E, 21, 23, and 25) where scarcity exists of PwDs and OPs services.
Firstly, establish total 18 (existing 8 and new 10) Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM), named, “Older People's-Helpdesk” in refugee camps to identify specific difficulties/barriers and unmet needs to address specific difficulties/barriers of individual beneficiaries, institution and social barriers and reduce unmet needs. Each desk operated by one Complaint Response officer (CRO) and two (Community volunteers), aiming to identify, list preparation, linkage and referral through both internally and externally, follow-up to ensure identified needs.
In new areas, only “OP-HELPDESK” exist, and two mobile/outreach team (each comprised with 1 MO, RO, & PSS Counsellor) will provide at outreach for those PwDs unable to reach static point and homebased for shelter-bound where the as HELPDESK team make list with specific needs earlier. No static center will be established there. On the other, hand in existing areas static, mobile/outreach and homebased services will be continued, as HelpAge currently providing since October 2017."

1 - Identified Needs

"Cox’s Bazar is one of the country’s poorest and most vulnerable and disaster prawn district in Bangladesh. Tropical cyclones, storm surges, and flash floods and landslides are the common natural disaster and fire incidence, and water logging also compounded the risk. The situation of older men, women and PWDs have difficulty availing transport facilities and reduced ability to earn money due to lack of capital and inadequate skills. According to age and disability inclusive need assessment- May 2021, of REACH, 12% of individuals found PwDs ranging from 2% of age 2-4 years and 51% with age 60 years and above. Overall, 35% households reported at least one disability persons. Among aged 5 and above, very high proportions of individuals reported by difficulties in functional in anxiety and depression domain, followed by Mobility among adults. Of person with disabilities, 52% faces difficulty regarding mobility with shelter and 76% around the camps. In addition, according to the Needs and Gaps Assessment (NGA) -January 2021, Of HelpAge, Bangladesh, in 4 refugee camps (i.e., camp-11,13,14 & 26) of the older people (age 50+), found highest 68% PwDs in age 80+, followed by 57% with age 70-79 years, then 40% and 34% with age 60-69 and 50-59 years, respectively. Among the age 50 years and above, very high proportions of functional difficulties found in mild to severe cognitive impairment (76%), followed by mobility difficulty (65%), then sensory impairment, i.e., visioning (58%) and hearing (28%). About 4% shelter-bound (cannot do at all as per WG-SSQ) including 1% bed-ridden. Among those reported difficulty in mobility, sensory impairment 94% did not use any kind of assistive products. Of PwDs with age (50 years and above) reported 93% faces difficulty from exit and enter the shelter and 53% of them faces difficulty to move inside the shelter. Difficulty in self-caring (washing overall and dressing) reported 36% including 12% with lot of difficulty and reported to needs care support assistance.Barriers in accessing facility, overall, 38% of the Rohingya individuals with age 50 years and above, reported that, cannot reach neither static health center nor distribution point and 25% reported to facing difficulty to carry their food ration and 62% said to needs regular medicine as they have single or multiple chronic disease condition (e.g., hypertension, cardiac problem, diabetes and COPD), 90% of them older persons with disabilities. About 56% faces problem with use e-voucher to buy food from e-shop. 75% of older persons with disabilities faced difficulty to use WASH facility including very high proportions with latrine, followed by water point and bathing. Total 11 types of difficulties regarding access to WASH, health, use of e-voucher and distribution points reported by the OP and older person with disabilities and 23 specific barriers addressing factors including age and disable friendly adaptive features, new types of facilities and specific needs also identified by those faces difficulties, that have significant effects to make facility accessible by them. The main challenges to adapt facilities accessible for the PWDs and OPs, is the lack of information, knowledges about age and disable friendly features, what are the new types of facilities and specific needs make facilities accessible by them. And lack of technical capacities about how to adapt that features with the existing facilities to make facilities accessible. However, the social factors like AGBV, PSEA, VAN are also common problem in the refugee camps, about 12% reported to affected by AGBV/VAN on the other hand Including physical, mental and financial abuse and neglect not only at household but also at service point and communal place and affected by family members/caregivers, community people including humanitarian staffs and safety security force as well. The rate is signifyingly higher among lower age group with 50-59 and slightly higher among women than men.
