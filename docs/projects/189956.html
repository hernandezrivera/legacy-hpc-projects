Project Summary

Ethiopia is experiencing one of the most severe La Niña-induced droughts in the last decades following three consecutive failed rainy seasons since late 2020. The prolonged drought is compromising fragile livelihoods heavily reliant on livestock and deepening food insecurity and malnutrition. The coping capacities of these already fragile communities that had not fully recovered from earlier droughts, and some affected by localized inter-communal violence, is being further eroded. More than 8 million people are currently affected across southern and south-eastern parts of the country, including Somali (more than 3.5 million people), Oromia (more than 3.4 million), SNNP (more than 1.1 million), and South West1 (more than 200,000 people) regions.
The drought is expanding to more areas affecting an increasing number of woredas as the weather conditions evolve and further impact the drought scale and intensity. The situation is not expected to improve rapidly due to the high probability of a fourth consecutive failed rains forecasted for this year. “Updated forecasts for April from the regional IGAD Climate Production and Applications Centre (ICPAC) point toward dry conditions in parts of Ethiopia, while April to June seasonal forecasts from ICPAC and global meteorological agencies (NOAA, ECMWF, IRI, UK MET, WMO) all show an increased probability of below-average rains in areas already affected by recurring drought and other food insecurity drivers (e.g., conflict, insecurity, displacements, and high cereal prices)2 .” Should this happen, it will further lead to massive socio-economic consequences for these communities. Even with good rains however, the affected population’s recovery will take time, until which time they will require continuous support.
Urgent additional funding is required across sectors to scale up assistance to the drought-affected population and to avert another major humanitarian crisis in the region. Priority needs are food, nutrition, WASH, health, shelter and NFI, education, protection and livestock support (feed and vaccines). Fuel shortage across Somali Region is hampering response efforts, including but not limited to water trucking services.
Christian Aid proposes to provide a life-saving Emergency Shelter and NFI kits to respond to the urgent needs of 18,015 individuals to reach, out of this 2702 are disable people to be reaches individuals) affected by drought in Dasenech woreda, South Omo zone SNNPR. So that crisis-affected people can have access to adequate, appropriate shelter and essential household items and they can live in safety and dignity.
The project will support the target beneficiaries in provision the full ESNFI kit and for all beneficiaries including the people with disability. types of items that will be provided are plastic sheet /tarpaulin ,rope ,bed sheet bed mat mosquitoes net , kitchen set and hygiene set according to the national shelter standard

1. - Projects activities

Christian Aid proposes to provide a life-saving ESNFI kits to respond to the urgent needs of 18,015 IDPs affected by drought in Dasemech woreda. The major activities include:• Conduct introductory meetings to share clear communication about the selection criteria and ensure that all stakeholders understand the process.• Beneficiary identification and registration through a committee established for registration of beneficiaries. The relevant parties from woreda DRMO, kebele officials and committee will work as a team, with Christian Aid, to do the registration.• The final verification will be done independently by Christian Aid.• Procurement, packaging and delivery of in-kind ESNFI items.• Distribution of ESNFI V2 Kit for Lowland Drought Response for targeted 18,015 IDPs. • Distribution of ESNFI V2 Kit Disable for Lowland Drought Response for 15% (2,702 people). During the distribution of ESNFI kits, Christian Aid will put measures in place to prevent the spread of COVID-19.• Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) two weeks after the distribution through survey a sample of households that received assistance trained enumerators using tools adapted to this project.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

Christian Aid (CA) has an operational presence and collaborative working relationship with local government sector offices, other humanitarian actor and grassroots communities in the area. Christian Aid will implement the project. Christian Aid has a strong humanitarian team. From the emergency response team, Humanitarian Program Officer will be dedicated to this response with support from Senior Humanitarian Programme Officer /Advisor. Further to this there is a dedicated procurement and logistics team to rapidly conduct procurements and transportation of items and a finance team to support the entire process. All activities of this project will be carried out in close liaison with the Zonal Disaster Risk Management office (DRMO), the coordinating body for this emergency response process. Christian Aid will explore and build strong partnerships with local government, affected people and humanitarian organizations working in the area, and DRMOs and national and sub-regional ES/NFI cluster. Christian Aid will liaise with humanitarian agencies (IRC, PIN, Red Cross) and collaborate so that different beneficiaries are reached and ensuring larger coverage. Each agency is providing information on resources, number of beneficiaries targeted and location, duration of projects, this is resulting in a better coordinated and cohesive response. This coordination experience we have is crucial as it promotes cooperation, avoids duplication, promotes coordinated delivery of interventions to beneficiaries and creates synergy with existing Christian Aid interventions and between other humanitarian agencies. Christian Aid has referral pathways to report/refer any unmet needs and sensitive complaints to concerned agencies and working groups. People with specific needs identified in target communities that the planned response does not respond to will be referred to mandated humanitarian partners and clusters.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Feedback and complaints systems set up jointly with the communities for the different age, disability and gender groups. Confidential feedback systems will be developed using confidential phone number or community confidential persons as appropriate for the different groups. The system of feedback and complaints will be explained to the beneficiaries. CA will use its Community-Based Approach to Accountability and Community Accountability Assessment (CAA). Multiple channels will be put in place to enable communities send feedback including community focal points, face-to-face feedback, or help-desk where applicable. The system provides live updates of feedback collected from communities using phones. CA will regularly track feedback on weekly/bi-weekly basis and use the analysis of feedback and trends therein to improve the response. CA and partner staff will regularly close the feedback loop with affected communities to ensure timely action is taken and information shared back with them.Affected community will be clearly informed of the type and amount of the assistance, selection criteria, and the start and end dates as well as the source of the assistance. We will share information on expected staff behavior, what constitutes breach of Code of conduct and how can communities report this. Discussions will be held with the affected communities on the different options for providing feedback including how to log confidential complaints. Feedback channels such as help desks during distributions clearly marked for beneficiary access will be set up. Feedback collected will be recorded and tracked into Christian Aid tracking system to ensure that feedback is monitored, and feedback loop will be closed with communities and their satisfaction ascertained. The confidentiality of the complaints will be ensured.Christian Aid will also coordinate with other actors to ensure referral of community needs raised through the system that is outside Christian Aid scope of response. Close consultation and systems will be set up to ensure safeguarding processes. During targeting and selection, priority will be given to women, households with chronic illness, people with disabilities, elderly people, female-headed households, and child headed household. Women, girls, children, older persons and people with disabilities will be protected against any form of manipulation or attack by ensuring distribution points are at a good distance from home and that they are comfortable with the location of distribution. During distributions, ensuring the beneficiaries are safe when carrying the items home and the weak, elderly and disabled are supported in carrying the items home. The project will construct demonstration shelters with the IDP community to assure that methods to create SPHERE standard shelters are understood; these demonstration shelters will then serve the needs of the most vulnerable households who otherwise would not be able to construct a shelter themselves. Beyond this, mobilize community to offer labor to households unable to construct their own shelter.
