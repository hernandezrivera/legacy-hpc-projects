Project Summary

The main objective of this project is to improve access to legal identity and housing, land and property rights for returnees, displaced persons, refugees and host communities in Mayo-Sava, Mayo-Tsanaga and Logone-et-Chari divisions in Far North region of Cameroon.
Violence perpetrated by armed groups in the Far North of Cameroon has led to forced displacement of populations and caused many human rights concerns. In Round 21 of the DTM, a total of 78,386 households of 494,144 individuals were identified, including 321,886 IDPs, 48,769 out-of-camp refugees and 123,489 returnees. The mobile population in the region increased by 8% compared to the previous round (December 2019). This increase confirms the worsening climatic and security conditions in the Far North in recent months, leading to more displacement. The Abuja Action Statement was signed in 2016 between the four countries of the Lake Chad Basin and is highlighting the issues of Protection of IDPs and host communities in the region.
For instance, according to the National Civil Status Registration Office (BUNEC), an estimated 400,000 pupils in the Far North do not have birth certificates and more than 41,000 pupils in the last year of primary school will not be able to take national examinations during the academic year 2019/2020 due to lack of birth certificates. Moreover, according to a baseline study conducted in July/August 2019 by NRC in 20 localities of the Far North region, a rate of 31,5% birth registration was reported including 23,2% for women and 42% for men. According to men, women, girls and boys, during 2020 November assessment, the birth certificate remains by far the civil document they need most. The National Identity Card (CNI) comes second for both women and men. The reasons mostly given by respondents to explain the low birth registration rate and low possession of civil documents in the region are the lack of income; the increased distance to civil registration centers as many have been closed due to the conflict so people have to go further; as well as the limited knowledge on procedures and conditions to obtain legal and civil documentation by refugees and IDPs. However, for the community as a whole, COVID has not brought about any change in this process.
According to NRC assessment in august 2019 made in 20 villages affected by displacement in the Far North region, only 21% of displaced persons reported having secured access to farming land. Moreover, conflicts between IDPs/refugees and host communities are frequent in these localities. 20,6% of people affected by displacement has access to land with a certain level of security of tenure, and 50 % of refugees reported having experienced HLP related conflicts with host communities. In general, IDPs and refugees arrange informal agreements to access land or rent houses and farms. As such tenure is rather insecure, this can result in forced evictions. Another HLP issue in the Far North is related to military operations conducted by the Cameroon army against armed groups. In fact, trenches were dug by the army to hinder armed groups from entering the Cameroonian territory. Those military operations affected around 7500 households who were either evacuated from their homes and access to their farming lands was blocked.
The project targets to reach 7100 persons (3479 M, 3621 F) including 3550 IDPs (1740 M, 1811 F); 710 refugees (348 M, 362); 1420 Host communities (696 M, 724 F); 1420 Host Communities (696 M, 724 F). Assistance will target primary children in exam classes, girls, child heads of households, women heads of households, the elderly, widowed women, and people living with disabilities.

Plan fields

1 - Besoins / Needs

1. Legal identity:1.1 Beneficiaries including children affected by displacement have access to legal and civil documentation: Information and counselling sessions on legal identity: Mobile ICLA teams will organize sensitization sessions on the birth registration procedure (including late birth registration) and the importance of the birth certificate. Legal assistance for the provision of birth certificates: NRC will organise mobile court hearings in order to facilite establishment of birth certificates for children (in particular girls) whose births have not been registered within the legal time limit of three months. This activity will bring together magistrates, clerks, civil registration officers and secretaries, and parents and children. Advocacy for the promotion of the rights of displaced people to access to civil and legal documentation: NRC will collaborate with members of the civil documentation working group in order to identify advocacy actions that can facilitate access to civil documentation for the greatest number of people. 1.2 Communities affected by displacements, administrative authorities and relevant stakeholders increase knowledge on legal and civil documentation related regulations and procedures Capacity building on legal identity: NRC will strengthen the capacity of institutions, local authorities, as well as humanitarian actors (e.g., civil registration centers, courts, traditional leaders, etc.) .2. Housing, Land and Property (HLP):Challenges in relation to HLP rights during displacement and upon return can result in major conflicts among displaced and host communities, leading to disruption in social cohesion and escalating tensions. The community as a whole stated that COVID 19 would not have had an impact on obtaining HLP rights, however, a problem of stigmatization and social cohesion with the proximity of the borders could be envisaged.2.1 Government officials, local and customary authorities and humanitarian actors increase knowledge on HLP rights and responsibilities Capacity building in HLP rights and CDR: NRC will build the capacity of local and traditional authorities on HLP issues, including through the provision of training and coaching on HLP rights and collaborative dispute resolution (CDR). 2.1 Persons affected by displacement are able to claim and enjoy their HLP rights Information and counselling sessions on HLP rights: NRC will provide support to displaced populations and vulnerable host communities to access their HLP rights. ICLA will deliver information and awareness sessions to IDPs, returnees, refugees, and host communities on HLP issues. ICLA mobile teams will also provide orientation and counselling in displacement and return areas, including IDP sites and refugee camps. Legal assistance on HLP: Additionally, NRC will provide legal assistance to conflict-affected population (IDPs, refugees, returnees) in need of securing their HLP rights. Priority will be given to female heads of household, widows, displaced persons, and vulnerable or affected host community members. Coordination meeting: NRC will continue to facilitate the coordination mechanisms and working group on HLP at the national, regional and divisional levels. This coordination meeting will be co-chaired by MINDCAF and NRC and will work within the framework of protection cluster. Humanitarian actors involved are those from the Protection, Wash, Shelter, Food Security and GBV sectors in addition to key administrative authorities and community leaders. This platform will propose advocacy axis in order to address HLP issues. Advocacy for the promotion of the rights of displaced people to HLP rights: Advocacy initiatives will be organised to reduce any other barriers to access HLP rights. NRC will organise meetings with decision-makers and key stakeholders to draw their attention on priority actions essential for the exercise of HLP rights in displacement and return contexts.
