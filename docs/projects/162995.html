Project Summary

This project aims to improve full access of refugees including South Sudanese to adequate protection services through prevention of protections risks and violations and response to urgent protection needs .The proposed activities will be delivered at individual and community levels. The planned project has designed to target person of concerns (PoCs) among refugees and asylum seekers in Khartoum with special focus on Sexual GBV (SGBV) survivors and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) through integrated protection services.
The intended intervention will enhance the refugee community’s capacity through training of 26 community based protection networks (CBPNs) in Khartoum,North Darfur and White Nile and hence allow them take an active role in the implementation of the project activities, community sensitization and mobilization and creating peaceful coexistence with their hosting communities. This will be implemented through focus group discussions, awareness raising sessions, community meetings, and public events (around 26 campaigns and social days) which target 68,890 refugees and hosting communities’ members.
Additionally, NADA will assess children at risk who have been exposed to or at risk of violence, abuse; neglect and exploitation in particularly UASC using best interest assessment (BIA) to support the identification of children at risk for effective case management targeted 1000 UASC and SGBV survivors. The assessment will be carried out in collaboration with UNHCR, COR and other partners using UNHCR's guidelines. Furthermore, NADA will maintain effective referral systems with the mentioned partners to ensure better access of PoCs to services.
Through this project, NADA intends to improve the well being and living conditions of refugees including South Sudanese in camp by expanding the services to a total number of 10,000 persons with specific needs (PSNs) through identification and provision of direct services or systematical referral to other service providers. The intended intervention will provide community based psychosocial support and awareness raising sessions on GBV, general protection and Child protection targeted 5,460 individuals among refugees and host communities in addition to awareness raising session on gender and women related issues targeting 1,260 women and girl. Local authorities and key community leaders will be trained on child protection/rights, GBV, protection, case management, referral pathways and PSS. During the course of the planned project, NADA will reinforce effective referral systems in collaboration and coordination with relevant stakeholders and services providers.

1 - Needs

Sudan has been hosting refugees from various countries for many years. Increasing conflicts & civil unrest combined with natural disasters have increased the flows of refugees &asylum seekers arriving from several countries to Sudan. In addition, the Arab spring in some Arab countries has also led to increase number of refugees Eritreans, Ethiopians, Syrians, Yemen fleeing to Sudan which has an open door policy for refugee. With its already fragile systems &infra-structures, refugees are facing critical challenges in accessibility of basic services, namely, medical, psycho-social, legal, educational, livelihood & other vital protection services. This remains an emergency, with women & children making up about 82 % of the refugee population. In camps settlement, present of high number of refugees in land which is owned by hosting communities contributed to intercommunal conflicts. As refugees struggle to meet their basic needs in an increasingly difficult economic environment, with limited access to educational, trainings, livelihoods, services and assistance, women and children are at heightened risk of harassment, exploitation, neglect and abuse. As such, SGBV remains a very sensitive issue, further compounded by limited knowledge, information and understanding available on the matter. Some of the existing cultural & traditional practices are also known to encourage occurrences of SGBV. There is still little information available regarding the situation of persons with special needs among refugees in urban &camps settlements. Series of recent assessments & interactions carried out by NADA have revealed that multiplicity of protection risks & hazards continue to affect daily lives of refugees and asylum seekers The focus group discussions during a rapid assessment conducted by NADA in project areas in September 2019 highlighted that existing community support networks and coping mechanism needs additional capacity to respond to the community protection. NADA has identified the need for strengthening the capacity of targeted community including CBPNs, community volunteers, local authorities, community leaders to be able to roll out planned project activities. NADA assessments have revealed that, elderly & people with disability are considered among the most vulnerable groups as they are socially excluded & face major difficulties in accessing humanitarian services hence they spent many hours per day as care givers for children, disability & limited mobility. There are no any specialized support mechanisms or social networks for supporting elderly & disability in project areas. Thus, elderly & PWDs left with very limited support. The assessment has showed that the elderly & PWDs represent at least 10% of total population of PoC. Problems faced by victims of GBV are exacerbated for women living in refugees gatherings, as they do not have access to various forms of assistance to victims/survivors

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through this intervention, NADA is committed safeguard Right holders from violence and discrimination and is aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the individuals. NADA will implement the project in impartial and non-discriminatory ways that promote the safety, dignity and integrity of the people receiving assistance during assessment, design of project and delivery of services. The project team will ensure all elements of accountability to affected population to be captured through extensive engagement and consultation with the community. Project is designed to inform all displaced about the availability and location of services. The project ensures safe and equal access to inclusive and non-discriminatory provision of services. The intervention will also carefully apply Do No Harm approach to avoid any negative impact that it might bring to the area or the targeted communities. Manage information in a sensitive manner so that the security of informants or others who may be identifiable is not jeopardized. The assistance and protection measures will not undermine local capacities for self-protection as well as support the efforts of the affected population and local communities to find security and restore dignity.
