Project Summary

One year after the 2017 drought, the humanitarian situation in Somalia remains grave due to residual impacts of the drought, ongoing displacement, conflict and seasonal floods. Access to improved water source is still a challenge to many across the country, with studies showing it is generally below 40%. In most urban and sub-urban hubs and IDP settlements, access to an adequate quantity of water is often granted but water is unsafe and often microbiologically contaminated due to cross-contamination by poorly design water supply and sanitation systems. More than half of the population lack of access to adequate sanitation. The safety of latrines being used is very low across the IDP settlements in the country, rendering users at high risk of violence during use of facilities. The rate of handwashing with soap stands at only 33% with access to soap and hand wash facilities very low.
In drought affected regions in Somalia, water scarcity and poor quality are still among the leading causes of population displacement and conflict. Heavy rainfalls affected more than 700,000 people in 2018. WASH infrastructure suffered from important damages in more than 25 districts. Most of these infrastructure have not been repaired yet. Because of the floods and the chronical lack of investment in WASH infrastructure, Somalia continued to suffer from AWD/Cholera in 2018 and risk factors for an outbreak persist in several hotspots (Banadir, Lower Shabelle, Lower Juba and Bay).
In Galmudug open defecation practices are common amongst those in the peri-urban areas due to lack of sanitation facilities. Poor hygiene and sanitation practices and lack of sensitization are still rife. 90% of the new IDPs in the peri-urban areas lack safe drinking water sources. This forces women to collect from distant underground water reservoirs known as Barked thus exposing them to protection risks. Salt concentration in the water is high in the coastal areas, forcing people in the areas to buy costly drinkable water brought from distant places.
CPD, through participation of the beneficiaries, is proposing to increase access to long term safe water supplies, construction of safe sanitation facilities and provision of hygiene services to 11,800 households. CPD will also train community committees on the management of the water supplies and sanitation facilities for sustainability. The committees will also establish regular hygiene activities in the communities to prevent diseases such as AWD/cholera and other water-borne diseases.

Activities

Water1. Rehabilitation of 8 strategic boreholes, 15 strategic shallow wells and 12 strategic water berkads and distribution of ceramic water filters. This will be done in Galmudug, South West State and Banadir regions. It is expected that women, girls, boys and men from 5,000 Households will be involved in the design and implementation of these projects and will have access to water as a result of the rehabilitation of these water sources.2. Distribution of 20 water bladders (10m³ each) to increase water access at strategic water points that lack adequate storage facilities. This will enable 5,000 households get access to water in the four target regions. Women and girls will have easy access to water within reasonable distance.3. Carry out 10 Water Management Committee trainings for 35 WASH committees (for 8 Strategic Boreholes, 15 Strategic shallow wells and 12 berkads). This will be done ensuring that at least 50% of members are women. 4. Establish emergency WASH systems like water trucking, bulk chlorination, household/point of use water treatment and safe storage and cash based programming.Sanitation5. Construction of 300 lockable gender segregated Emergency/communal latrines with hand washing facilities for IDPs and host communities in Galmudug, South West State and Banadir regions. This will include construction of 60 gender segregated twin latrines for 10 schools in the target areas. Facilities designs will be done in consultation with Camp Management Committees.6. Carry out 3 days hygiene promotion training for 100 hygiene volunteers (both women and men) selected from the IDPs and host communities in the targeted areas. Thereafter, disseminate health and hygiene promotion messages to the target populations by the 100 trained hygiene volunteers using IEC messages, radio programs, personal hygiene awareness, latrine use and food handling.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

CPD will ensure the projects increase the quality and quantity of water and sanitation services. The communities will be enlightened on safe water handling to ensure safe quality of water up to point use. Hygiene education will be imparted to the communities benefitting from the project to prevent communicable diseases cropping in. The projects will ensure the most vulnerable displaced and disaster-affected women, girls, boys and men have increased, equal and sustained access to safe and appropriate water, sanitation services and hygiene promotion, including complementary activities with Nutrition, Health, Education, Livelihood, and Food. CPD will make sure the projects will be linked to government/ community systems through the committees that will be established. The committees will be given management training and the projects handed over to them for management. Monitoring will be done to ensure they are being run smoothly before being left on their own to manage. The community committees will be registered with the local authorities as the legal owners of the assets of the projects.CPD will ensure that all stakeholders will be involved in all steps of the project life cycle, using an approach that is all inclusive in targeting. With the assistance of the community leaders, the most vulnerable households will be identified and given priority.Community Committees will be established to help to help bridge the gap between CPD and the community. A complaints mechanism will be set up where community members can raise issues in different ways. Information sharing will be through the Community Committees, CPD staff, frequent field visits by project staff (Project manager), through the accountability system and through provision of summary reports both on accountability/feedback and the project progress. CPD will use principle of "Do No Harm' in carrying out activities to ensure the community also accepts the interventions.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Most displaced populations settle in camps that are at times set up hurriedly and haphazardly. Most of these camps lack basic facilities that the populations need to live a decent life. CPD will provide safe clean water by constructing water points near the settlements, and appropriate sanitation facilities designed after consultation with the beneficiary communities in order to take their concerns into consideration.CPD will give refresher courses to staff on how best to implement CPD’s child protection policy that emphasizes impartiality and non-discrimination while promoting safety, dignity and integrity of the beneficiaries. CPD will also work in close collaboration with the Protection cluster while coordinating with the various actors with diversemandates and experience to provide the primary responsibility to protect all individuals within their jurisdiction, especially the most vulnerable (women, children and elderly and people with disabilities) groups. CPD is also aware of the universal acceptance that humanitarian assistance must meet the distinct needs of women, girls, boys and men to generate positive and sustainable outcomes.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women and girls face the risk of sexual violence as they seek sanitation services in less secure environments. The projects are going to be designed after consultation with the different categories of community members so that they are sited in safe environments with security features like lockable doors and lighting in the facility areas to prevent incidents of sexual violence.The proposed projects are also derived from HRP which has been subjected to gender analysis of needs assessment which justifies the activities and expected outcome that support gender equality. CPD recognizes that project assessment, design and implementation must pay special attention to minorities and vulnerable groups; such as women headed households, older people and people with specific needs. Our team will ensure project delivery on the basis in lights of different needs; equal opportunities within the targeted community. The project design and delivery process will be sensitive to the needs of the most vulnerable segment of the target groups in terms of gender inclusion and safety and security of the most vulnerable groups.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

CPD will incorporate issues that cause conflict due to scarce resources in the designing of projects for displaced communities. For communities that have been displaced because of water resources conflicts, CPD is going to identify strategic water sources and improve them to serve both communities sufficiently.The project will also take a Do No Harm and participatory approach, ensuring that interventions and interactions are context specific and involve the community in all aspects of the project, before, during and after implementation thereby reducing risk of conflict over resources.
