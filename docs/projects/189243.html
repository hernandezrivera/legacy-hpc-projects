Project Summary

This project aims at providing integrated life-saving humanitarian assistance for 150,000 most vulnerable populations affected by conflict and disasters including IDPs, returnees and hosting community under extreme severity with sufficient attention given to women, girls and children, by improving access to basic WASH services and the conditions of the living environment, reducing malnutrition cases and mitigating GBV risks among them targeting 6 counties of Melut, Renk in Upper Nile State, Juba and Terekeka in CES, Magwi and Torit in EES.

1 - Needs

According to 2022 South Sudan humanitarian need overview, there is an estimated 6 million people’s living standards and their wellbeing will be impacted in 2022 as a result of inadequate or lack of access to safe water and improved sanitation in South Sudan. Drivers compounding people's well-being are interlinked and include limited access to sufficient quantities of safe drinking water, poor hygiene practices, and poor sanitation institutional and community facilities.The findings from the 2021 Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) and its analysis indicate that access to WASH facilities by women and girls, boys, persons with disabilities and elderly and services remains a challenge. Latrines were often overflowing also creating the fear of disease. Severe flooding in 2021 resulted in the destruction and damage of water facilities, reduced access to sanitation and loss of hygiene items which resulted in the urgent need for water, sanitation and hygiene services. Displacement, insecurity, and the economic downturn have compounded the impact of the low coverage of functional water infrastructure in rural areas. Commonly reported issues in accessing WASH facilities include long lines and congestion at water collection points, leading to disputes and tensions. Women and girls frequently face physical and sexual assault and harassment at water collection points, bathing areas and latrines. Peace Winds Japan’s humanitarian response plan for 2022 will cover the states of Upper Nile, CES, EES targeting Renk, Melut, Juba, Terekeka, Magwi and Torit counties. According to 2022 HNO, in Upper Nile State, Rent County has over 145,000 people in need with 96,900 being in severe need, 29,100 being in extreme need and 19,000 in catastrophic needs, while Melut County has over 84,000 people in need, with 51,900 people in severe need, 19,500 in extreme and 13,000 being in catastrophic need. In CES, Juba has 243,900 people in need, with 113,300 people in severe need, 78,400 in extreme need and 52,300 in catastrophic need while Terekeka county has over 175,400 people in need with 110,800 in severe need, 38,800 in extreme need and 25,800 are in catastrophic need. In EES, Magwi County has 91,900 people in need with 52,200 in severe need, 26,500 in extreme need and 13,200 in catastrophic need while Torit County has 35,900 people in need with 20,100 in severe need, 9,400 in extreme need and 6,400 in extreme need. Towards the end of 2021, PWJ held several meetings with the community leaders, local authorities in some of the counties/payams/boma in Melut, Juba, Magwi and Torit counties to find out the WASH situations. The State Director for water and sanitation in the targeted counties expressed that the capacity of the staff in their department is inadequate. They also mentioned that there are no spare parts and tools for rehabilitation of water points and they have not managed to meet the standard of training water committees per hand pump for sustainability of existing water points. The counties' plan to monitor water points and identify risk factors is strained by funds, security and bad roads. Poor access to basic WASH facilities especially safe water supply, improved sanitation facilities and access to good hygiene practice knowledge in both the institutions and at the community levels are key concerns raised in the counties. Most populations in Juba, rely on river Nile water through expensive water trucking systems and direct fetching by most people in Terekeka, Melut and Renk Counties. Majority of people practice open defecation in the targeted counties since large portion of the temporary latrine and bathing facilities in most IDPs sites in the targeted counties were constructed back in the year 2018-2019 and most of them are damaged.PWJ is operating and implementing basic WASH services in CES, EES and Jonglei since 2014 and secured another funding to support WASH in IDPs in Melut County and Juba County in 2022.
