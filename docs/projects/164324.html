Project Summary

Nasaem will provide olive-pit fuel which is known and used as a natural source of bio-fuel (Birin) in the area and has increasingly been used by the target communities during the crisis. This is a bio-fuel, locally produced and ecofriendly. Besides, Nasaem will distribute/replenish 500 heaters with accessories to those households that have no heaters or have theirs worn.
The estimated number of targeted beneficiaries will be around 15000 including 2500 women, 1850 men, 4150 boys and 6500 girls.
Under the proposed project Nasaem will distribute heating materials for 2600 of the most vulnerable households (70% IDPs, 30% host community) in Maret Mesrin, Armanaz, Salqin, and Jisr Ash-Shugur sub-districts of Idleb province. Out of these, 925 households in urban areas will receive 360 litres per HH of diesel distributed over two batches throughout the winter season. The other 1700 households living in rural areas will receive 900 kg per HH of olive pits distributed over two batches during winter. To achieve the goal of this project, Nasaem will distribute/replenish heating diesel/Olive pits stoves for 500 households out of 2600 households receiving heating materials.

4 - Needs

As the Syrian crisis enters its ninth year, the humanitarian situation for people in northwest Syria remains alarming. From 1 December 2019 to 14 January 2020, almost 350,000 people were displaced in northwest Syria, mostly from southern parts of Idleb area, 80 per cent of whom are women and children. Many fled their homes within the space of hours without any belongings while others managed to take with them what they needed to survive, believing that they would not be coming back. The ongoing conflict has forced families to flee their homes with no even the most basics of necessities available to them. Of the four million people in northwest Syria, an estimated 2.7 million are in need of humanitarian assistance. HNO 2019 also mentioned that there are 4.4 million people require NFI assistance as well as the number of people in need of NFI assistance in Idleb has increased by over 600,000. However,within the situation in some areas, such as the northwest, is, in fact, deteriorating amidst an increase in people in need. Furthermore, needs remain substantial, both in terms of humanitarian life-saving needs, particularly those among the high numbers of newlydisplaced IDPs requiring core, and the supplementary and seasonal assistance. An additional risk factor is the seasonal fuel scarcity and the resulting increase in fuel prices which is expected during the wintertime. In addition, there is a need for increased fuel provision,storage, contingency stock and winterization fuel for IDPs shelters/medical centres for cooking, heating and other essential services. Fuel has been reported to be a priority need by most age and gender groups with the Elderly (60+ y) citing it as the second priorityneed. This humanitarian crisis has been exacerbated by the increase of all basic needs and services. Populations in areas which experience cold conditions during winter continue to need seasonal support, with communities surveyed reporting heating-related shelter deficiencies.

5 - Implementation of activities

Nasaem will provide olive-pit fuel which is known in the area and has increasingly been used by the local communities during the crisis. This is a bio-fuel, locally produced and eco-friendly. According to the resulting framework issued by the S/NFI cluster, stated that the provision of seasonal and supplementary NFIs aims to protect the targeted beneficiaries from seasonally harsh conditions. Besides, Nasaem will distribute/replenish heaters with accessories to those households that have no heaters or have theirs worn. - Nasaem will notify the targeted communities, communities' actors and other stakeholders about the project. Nasaem will explain the aim of the project, the number of households in each community, main activities, selection criteria, amounts of materials, and start and end dates of the project. Nasaem will strive for an impartial, nondiscriminatory and transparent mechanism for targeting those who are most at risk and in greatest need. Once the items are available, Nasaem will also make sure that vulnerable individuals and households can access both information and distributions. -Nasaem will focus on the vulnerability of the crisis-affected population and determine the most people in need using the following selection criteria (Single female-headed household caring forchildren, Elderly people, women-headed households, IDPs who undergo multiple movements, PLWs and persons with disabilities, and household with no source of income. Conducting the final evaluation to measure the impact of the project and to evaluate the action's implementation and dynamics to ensure that results and indicators are achieved.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: A breach of ethics. Mitigation: Nasaem will train the staff on the code of conduct, child safeguarding, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, and ask them to sign them.Risk: Staff may find sensitive protection cases. Mitigation: Nasaem’s field staff will receive training on the referral of cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to the available service providers in the area based on the updated service map.It might be a risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members, Nasaem will use the S/NFI cluster guidance on minimum standards and recommended content as well as for quality and specification of items.If different to recommend standard, we will identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions. Nasaem will ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. Nasaem will make its best to inform all the targeted households by multiple tools.It may be a risk of increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members. Nasaem will identify and exclude from variance any items particularly prone to create tensions, also ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on the status as well as Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed.Also the risk of physical harm at distribution points, Nasaem will make well-organized distributions to avoid overcrowding and will train to staffs on establishing and managing distributions.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Risk of tensions between IDPs and host communities. Mitigation measure: To avoid creating tensions among host communities and IDPs as not all of them will receive assistance, Nasaem will discuss the project with the local councils and will also explain the project’s goal and vulnerability criteria to all communities, in addition, to ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs. Nasaem will make efforts in cooperation with the S/NFI cluster to target % of vulnerable host residents especially for those who have greatly affected by the crisis.Nasaem team will ensure all staff and volunteers have complied with the policies, read and signed the Code of Conduct. Also, Nasaem will develop a range of measures and internal procedures to identify staff who are perpetrators of abuse and exploitation and act accordingly. Nasaem will hire male and female to participate equally in all project phases.Risk: Risk of fire. Mitigation: Nasaem will provide awareness sessions in coordination with the local councils to all beneficiaries about using the olive-pit fuel. Nasaem will print a sticker warning and guidance on each heater that will be distributed.Assumptions: Nasaem has a good rapport and coordination with the local councils in targeted communities and has been providing humanitarian aid in multiple sectors for many years ago at those areas, so Nasaem is acceptable in the community. Access to target locations remains possible. Risks of theft will be reduced keeping minimum the stock of items to be distributed through contracting suppliers to supply the fuel, olive pits and heaters at certain times and to certain locations. Mitigation measure: The warehouse will be mainly used for short-term storage, with minimum amounts of the returned item to for preparations that precede distributions to smaller villages where the supplier will not supply the materials.
