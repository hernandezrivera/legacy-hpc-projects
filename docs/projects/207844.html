Project Summary

Since 2019 Nabolok has been providing life-saving LPG support to FDMNs and host community (HC) for ensuring safe and dignified living conditions and contributing to environmental sustainability and decreasing the dependency on forest wood for cooking purposes. In continuations, for the next JRP-2024 the targeted beneficiaries are 1932 Rohingya HHs in camp-25 and 450 HC HHs at Shahporir Dwip (ward no # 7, 8 & 9) in Sabrang Union, Teknaf. The total targeted population is 9610 FDMNs and 2184 HCs. The LPG refill distribution for FDMN and HC will be provided on a monthly basis, as per pre-prepared schedule and on the basis of family size and category determined by the SCCCM Sector. Nabolok will also provide new LPG packages (cylinder with refill, stove, regulator) as well as safety use training on LPG and cooking stove using guidelines for the nuclear families in FDMNs. To reduce the probability of fire incidents through expired LPG stoves, Nabolok also planned to repair all the existing damaged stoves for both FDMN and HC HHs which were distributed in 2023 and also distribute new one when it is required. To address any natural and manmade hazards, Nabolok will allocate some contingency funding in its project proposal so that it can provide LPG support among the affected beneficiaries by providing a new LPG package just after the incidents and if relocated. To reduce vulnerability, Nabolok will arrange a wide queue place with shed to minimize waiting anguish of the targeted beneficiaries. For EVIs, Nabolok will provide home delivery services by porter support. Nabolok will also arrange a separate distribution queue as well as disable friendly space for women and men to avoid and reduce any kind of harassment issues. In this connection, Nabolok will hire appropriate volunteers who will perform some small-scale work just for crowd controlling & management, helping beneficiaries in carrying the LPG etc. Besides, project’s staff will conduct regular house to house visits both in FDMN and HC to follow up and monitor if the distributed LPG assistance is appropriate or not and also take beneficiaries’ feedback. From the beginning to work in the Rohingya response, Nabolok has also been installing SSLs, and repairing & maintaining the installed lights. Nabolok had installed SSLs both in camps and HC to ensure targeted beneficiaries’ safety and security at night. The lights were installed beside WASH facilities, roadside, and many gathering places so that a wide range of beneficiaries (both primary and secondary) are benefitted from the lighting facilities. In 2023, Nabolok had ensured 100% SSLs regular repair and maintenance to keep the lights functional. In continuation of this, Nabolok has a total of 229 SSLs which are 35 in camp-24, 135 in camp-26 and 59 in HC under Teknaf Upazila. For 2024, the targeted beneficiaries of the existing lights are 17000 FDMNs and 5900 HCs (100 beneficiaries per lights). As the lights were installed a long time ago, these are being damaged suddenly and need to be repaired immediately. In this connection, to keep the existing lights functional Nabolok has planned to continue its regular repair and maintenance activities of the existing SSLs both in FDMN and HC. Nabolok will conduct regular follow up, monitoring, cleaning, repair and maintenance activities for the existing lights to keep the lights functional. To ensure the existing lights functionality, all age groups of beneficiaries especially women and adolescent girls will be able to use WASH facilities and move from one place to another more comfortably during the night. In addition, to prevent the lights from theft, Nabolok will also conduct SSL stolen prevention campaigns regularly to increase awareness among the beneficiaries. Besides, project’s full-time staff will conduct house to house visits to take beneficiaries feedback regarding SSLs repair requirements.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Shelter/NFI Strategy for 2023-2025 stated that, in late 2021, approximately USD 34 million to USD 39 million is needed annually to supply all 190,000 Rohingya HHs with LPG in Cox’s Bazar. The NFI Assessment-2022 report stated that 99.5% of FDMN HHs are using LPG for cooking. Up to October 2023, Nabolok had covered 100% of targeted FDMN HHs in camp-25 and 25% of HC HHs at Shahporir Dwip, Sabrang Union, Teknaf by distributing LPG refill, new LPG packages and safety use training for nuclear families, home delivery service to the EVIs in camp, and now we are appealing to keep this percentage in the upcoming year as well. To reduce fire risks, it is essential to continue providing LPG refills and promoting safe cooking practices. In this connection, we have planned to continue our LPG refill and cooking set distribution in camp and HC to develop living status of the targeted beneficiaries which will also contribute to environmental sustainability and decrease the dependency on forest wood for cooking purposes. Sabrang Union is a mostly vulnerable area for natural disaster (like-cyclone, tornado) and as it is the southernmost part of Bangladesh and located in the side of Bay of Bengal, the targeted beneficiaries are always suffering from cooking fuel deficiency. According to our previous working experience in LPG refill and cooking set distribution and by communicating and coordinating with the Shelter/NFI Sector Agency, CiC of camp-25, the District Administration, Cox’s Bazar and UNO, Teknaf, we have identified the mentioned areas. By visiting the HHs we have also found that continuous refill distribution is very essential in the targeted areas. For continuing our LPG refill and cooking set distribution activities we will hire some volunteers both from Rohingya and host communities and it will be helpful for them to contribute to their family expenses and create self-income source. We will allocate some contingency funding in this project proposal so that, we can distribute LPG cylinders and cooking stoves in case of fully damaged by any incidents in our working areas at both camp and host levels. CCCM: In the report of “needs and priorities of Rohingya refugees and host communities in Cox’s Bazar since 2017” published in 2022, it has stated that the lack of adequate lighting is a persistent concern among Rohingya households and remains one of the primary reasons people feel unsafe in camps at night. To ensure the targeted Rohingya and HC beneficiaries’ safety previously Nabolok had installed SSLs in camps and HC areas. According to our previous working experience, we have got specific ideas about the light’s necessity in the project area. It has also been emphasized by the Sector Agency, CiC, UNO regarding SSLs functionality confirmation. The beneficiaries are also concerned about SSLs importance. As there is no specific brand for SSL’s parts, lights are damaging constantly which requires constant repair. Most of the targeted areas especially the camp areas are highly risky for robbery, kidnapping, trafficking etc. Especially, the women and adolescent girls are in high risk of being abused and harassed and it can be happened more due to darkness at night time. By considering all these issues, we understand that regular repair and maintenance of the SSLs is very essential in both camp and HC areas. Due to budget limitation and costing high expenses for new installation of SSLs, we have planned to provide repair and maintenance assistance of the existing lights to ensure safety and meaningful access of other facilities during night time.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project committed to address the crisis and reduce the dependency on forest firewood collection and improve social cohesion of the Rohingya and HC. Apart from this, Nabolok also committed to ensure safety, security, protection and dignified access to the WASH facilities, safe movement in the marketplace/health center, medicine shop at night by ensuring sufficient lighting effect in our targeted areas. To adopt an integrated approach the project will ensure camp-based inter-sectoral coordination and communication. The project will build a strong coordination with SCCCM Sector, SMS, CiC for further planning for Rohingya HHs and with the District Administration, UNO, Local Governmental Representatives for further planning for the HC HHs. For filling up any gaps and to ensure cost efficiency regarding LPG assistance and SSLs repair and maintenance, Nabolok will always follow the guidelines of the SCCCM Sector. To ensure predictability and continuity of the project activities Nabolok will follow the multi-year strategies produced by the SCCCM Sector. As an active project implementer in SCCCM Sector, Nabolok will comply with its project activities with the technical standards and policies set by the relevant SCCCM Sector. Apart from this, the project will also prepare a need assessment and collect approval from CiC, and RRRC including budget portions. The project team will attend monthly coordination meetings at CiC Office and SCCCM Sector and update the progress. The project will also prepare and send the monthly progress report to the CiC Office and SCCCM Sector on a monthly basis. Besides, Nabolok will also include nuclear families into the distribution list prior approval from the CiC and will always follow SCCCM Sector’s guideline regarding SSLs repair and maintenance and cleaning.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

Nabolok will ensure meaningful access of every assistance both for FDMN and HC. For LPG distribution, different facilities will be ensured e.g. waiting shed, necessary fan for air, safe drinking water, elderly support including disable and pregnant women. Nabolok will also provide home delivery services by porter support for EVIs. To reduce vulnerability Nabolok will provide wide queue place with shed to minimize their waiting anguish. There will also a separate queue for women and men so that they will not face any kind of harassment and allocate disable friendly space. It will also be ensured that the SSL assistance is accessible for all age groups including the PWDs and SSLs functionality will always be ensured so that project’s targeted beneficiaries can be able to move without fear at night especially the women and adolescent girls will able to use WASH facilities at night without any fear of abuse and sexual harassment for darkness. Considering any disaster related incident or hazard, Nabolok will keep a contingency fund allocation in its project budget proposal so that it can provide emergency assistance immediately after the incident occurred. To ensure protection of project’s targeted beneficiaries and risk reduction for any disaster related incident Nabolok will aware them by sharing information and disseminate warning messages by coordinating with the SCCCM Sector and other relevant actors. Nabolok will also ensure effective coordination with Sector coordinators and the ISCG EPR focal point to address any potential issues regarding DRM. Nabolok will also ensure sharing information to the beneficiaries to promote their active participation and empowerment so that they can able to protect themselves from any harmful effects as well as develop themselves including aware about their rights. Nabolok will also coordinate with other actors engaged in protection assistance to increase its knowledge about protection, avoid duplications and to address risks.

3.3 - Age & Disability

By considering all age groups and PWDs, Nabolok will plan its LPG refill distribution and SSLs repair and maintenance activities both in Rohingya and HC. Project’s full-time staff will ensure easy access and enough and meaningful access to the facilities without any discrimination, gender, disability or age-related barriers. Nabolok will pay special attention to individuals and groups (e.g., PWD, elderly, children, etc.) who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services on LPG refill and cooking set distribution and SSLs. To ensure easy access of the LPG refill service Nabolok will ensure the distribution point in a suitable place from where all the targeted beneficiaries can take the refill without any hassle. The project will also give special priority to the Extremely Vulnerable individual (EVI). Considering all the age groups difficulties, Nabolok will keep home delivery support during LPG refill distribution and will provide immediate repair of the lights. According to the Child Protection Principles no children (below 18) will be considered to receive the LPG cylinder, but if there is no capable adult for that household to receive the LPG cylinder then Nabolok will support him/her by giving home delivery assistance. Considering the different ages women, men, adolescents, women-headed PWD families, ethnicity, different cultural backgrounds, and various lifestyles, project’s LPG assistance will be in place. To ensure DRR for all age groups including PWDs Nabolok will keep contingency fund allocation with its budget proposal for immediate pre-preparedness, warning message delivery, training for the volunteers and for meaningful access of all assistance (LPG distribution & SSL) after disaster. Nabolok will also engage its beneficiaries including all age group with disabilities for decision making and building committee regarding DRR. Nabolok will also take special recommendations and consent from the CiC, SMS and SCCCM Sector.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will accountable to ensure dignitary and quality support for the AAP. The project activities system will be accountable and effective, and take the beneficiaries opinions by providing them decision-making power. Through the commitment to provide accountability to affected people, Nabolok aim to ensure that its humanitarian action protects and preserves the rights and dignity of the targeted beneficiaries, remains relevant and effective, leaves no one behind and upholds humanitarian principles. Nabolok will also build trust and acceptance from the targeted beneficiaries to make its project activities more impactful. The report of JMSNA-2023 stated that, 52% of FDMN HHs reported being aware of feedback and complaint mechanisms to reach aid providers about community needs, assistance received, problems with assistance, reporting, of which, 22% of households reported having made a complaint. For ensuring 100% of FDMN and HC HHs of targeted project areas, are aware of feedback and complaint mechanisms to reach Nabolok, the project will design a coordinated plan for disseminating messages on what assistance has been delivered and what lies ahead, as well as what has been done with the complaints and feedback and also coordinate with the Sector Focal Point for further improvement of project’s assistance. Nabolok will establish a mechanism to monitor our provided assistance to the affected population on a regular basis and ensure adequacy, quality and timely information provision and engage them in decision-making. The project’s staff will play a positive and supportive role to ensure quality support in time for the affected population. The affected population will be encouraged to share their suggestions, feedback, and complaints with Nabolok verbally, and in writing by using a complaint box. Nabolok will conduct regular monitoring and door-to-door visit for identifying the problem and collect feedback about its services.

3.5 - Is your agency/organization and are your IPs members of the PSEA Network?

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Nabolok is an active member of the PSEA Network of Cox’s Bazar. It has 2 PSEA Focal Persons (Teknaf based) to address SEA related issues. The Focal Persons are well-trained up by the organization regarding PSEA as well as they are regularly attending PSEA Network meetings. To avoid any incident of SEA, every month the Focal Persons conduct internal PSEA training among the 100% staff and volunteers. The trained volunteers deliver SEA awareness messages to the beneficiaries through conducting daily group sessions at the field level. Nabolok also has complaint boxes and maintains a complaint & feedback register to address SEA related complaints raised by the beneficiaries as well as its staff or volunteers. Furthermore, Nabolok always prioritizes safety, dignity, well-being of the survivor while addressing SEA related complaints including its reporting and investigation. The identity of the victims and complainer is always kept confidential. For further improvement of PSEA mainstreaming in JRP-2024, Nabolok will actively engage in good communication and coordination with the PSEA Network of Cox’s Bazar and our Focal Persons will receive ToT conducted by the PSEA Network. Nabolok has also a plan to use awareness materials such as-posters, videos to deliver proper messages to the beneficiaries including its 100% staff and volunteers. Nabolok will also actively engage to ensure the targeted beneficiaries are aware of their rights, services and where/how to access them, that sexual exploitation is not tolerated, and how they can report any concerns related to SEA. There will be an effective, safe, and accessible feedback and response system to report any suspicions of SEA of the beneficiaries. Nabolok will arrange integrated PSEA information campaigns, awareness-raising, training and meetings with the targeted beneficiaries and ensure that victims of SEA have access without delay to the assistance and support that is required with a victim-centered approach.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes. As Nabolok has been working in Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar from 2019 and it is a natural disaster-prone area of Bangladesh, so Nabolok has in-depth experience in pre-preparedness and response in any kind of natural disasters. In Hazard Response Plan-2022 it has mentioned that, “SMSD Sectors incident reporting within the camps from 2018 to 2021 indicated that the key risks likely to impact the camps are: wind/rain storms, floods (flash-flooding and water-logging), slope failure/landslides and fires. In addition, there is a periodic risk of cyclones during the two cyclone seasons from April-May and October–November. According to the Bangladesh Meteorological Department (BMD), between 1960 to 2017, seven Severe Cyclonic Storms (max wind speed of 220 km/hr) and two Cyclonic Storms (max wind speed of 115 km/hr) directly hit Cox’s Bazar.” In this connection, Nabolok will allocate a contingency fund for addressing any environmental or climate change related incidents in our project areas. Nabolok will has an emergency response plan regarding DRR to disseminate warning messages to the beneficiaries, keep ready emergency response team, training for the team, ensure sufficient assistance after disaster as well as we will ensure effective coordination and communication with the Sector coordinators and the ISCG EPR focal point and government entities (DRRO, RRRC, CPP) to address any potential issues.
