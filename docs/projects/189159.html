Project Summary

IRC through the Women Protection and Empowerment program will provide specialized GBV response services and prevention interventions targeting the crisis affected populations in Mayendit, Rubkona and Juba counties. This will be through the provision of life saving GBV case management services guided by the survivor centered approach. IRC will ensure the service provision is in coordination with other service providers for a multisectoral approach which comprise of medical, legal, safety and psychosocial support services. A total of 472(325W, 123G, 12M, 12B) will be targeted for case management services across the proposed locations. IRC will continuously engage women and girls and the community at large to identify barriers in accessing the GBV services and collaboratively work together to address them through the existing community-based protection structures. In addition, quarterly reviews and updating of the referral pathways as well as continuous feedback sessions will be held to ensure that the pathways are functional across the proposed locations. The women and girls’ safe spaces (2 Rubkona, 2 Juba and 2 Mayendit) will serve as avenues of empowerment for women and girls with a major focus on provision of contextualized psychosocial support interventions which seek to support in the healing, recovery and building resilience of women and girls. The Women and Girls Safe Space toolkit will be instrumental in providing guidance on the operationalization of the safe spaces especially in ensuring accountability as well as encouraging the women-girls led processes and ownership. Tailored curriculums for the engagement of women and girls such as Girl Shine for the adolescent girls and their care givers as well as Women Rise a psychosocial support tool kits for the women will ensure each age group has appropriate and contextualized PSS intervention. 4,500(3,180W, 1,320G) will be targeted for the WGSS activities. On GBV prevention IRC will engage communities through SASA! Together and EMAP as the primary GBV prevention approaches. The two approaches seek to provoke discussions on power imbalance as the root cause of violence while analyzing negative social norms, beliefs, behavior, and practices that contribute to violence against women and girls. Additionally, IRC seeks to build the capacity of communities to respond and prevent GBV through training and community led dialogues which will equip them with knowledge and skills. For GBV risk mitigation measures, IRC will work closely with women and community led structures to carry out GBV safety audits where action points to address the identified risks and concerns will be tasked to the relevant actors. Findings from the safety audits will inform other interventions such as training on Fuel Efficient Stoves for the women as well as production of reusable sanitary materials with the locally available raw materials. In coordination with the GBV SC, IRC will distribute dignity kits to the women and girls of reproductive age at risk of GBV. The proposed interventions aim to target 54,715 (27,904 Women, 19,150 Girls, 4,924 Men and 2737 Boys) where 78% IDP will be targeted, 18% host communities and 4% returnees. Out of the total target, 10% will be people living with disabilities. IRC currently has presence in Juba (Juba IPD camp 1 and 3, Don Bosco IPD camp, Mahad IPD camp, Juba way station and Juba Teaching Hospital), Rubkona (Bentiu IPD camp and in host communities) and Mayendit. In all these locations, IRC is providing GBV response services, GBV community engagement activities including EMAP, SASA! and awareness raising activities, psychosocial support activities including skills building activities and capacity building activities to community leadership structures and partners and community members.

1 - Needs

GBV is one of the most critical threats to the protection and wellbeing of women and girls especially in South Sudan who continue to face extreme levels of vulnerability to violence, exploitation, and psychosocial distress. In a context where GBV was already rife prior to the conflict, structural gender inequalities, lack of law enforcement and harmful gender norms continue to render women and girls vulnerable to multiple forms of abuse. The HNO 2022 indicate that around 8.5 million individuals are estimated to need humanitarian assistance across the Country with 2.6M in need of GBV services. In addition, it further indicates that Sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) as well as sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) remain a major protection concern. Mayendit and Rubkona are classified among the 13 counties with the highest level of GBV needs (level 5) while Juba County has severe needs (level 4). The unprecedented flooding where an estimated 208,000 people were reported to have been affected by the flooding in Unity State since May 2021. This has exacerbated the already existing protection threats against women and girls further exposing them to risks of violence and different forms of exploitation. In Rubkona County, for several months now, physical access has remained a challenge for humanitarians to reach affected people as roads are impassable and floodwaters cut off communities. Shifts in traditional gender roles continue to add more burden to women who are forced to take up additional roles as bread winners for their families which exposes them to more risks of GBV. Findings from an Inter-Agency Flood Assessment conducted in Panyijiar County- August 2021 , highlighted that the safety and security of adolescent girls and women had largely been impacted by the ongoing flooding which has further exposed them to multiple GBV risks especially when they travel long distances collecting water and firewood from relatively non-flooded villages. In addition, adolescent girls are at heightened risk of early marriage and sexual exploitation and abuse. The situation has further been worsened by limited access to humanitarian services as most of the villages are inaccessible due to the massive flooding. In Mayendit an Interagency Flood Assessment report conducted in August 2021 indicate that women and girls were at risk of GBV given that most of the displaced communities had sought refuge in the relatively non flooded areas where they were hosted by other families which led to overcrowding. Limited access to dignity kits also impacted on the menstrual hygiene of the women and girls of reproductive age. With the recent climate change that has caused massive flooding in Rubkona and Mayendit Counties coupled pockets of inter-ethnic conflict and global COVID-19 pandemic, communities coping mechanisms have significantly weakened because of the cumulative shocks, which has increased the vulnerability of women and girls to GBV as well as led families into adopting negative practices such as early/child marriage. GBV remain a major protection concern for women and girls especially in the Juba IDP camps where their vulnerability continues to have heightened following the transition of the former protection of civilians’ sites. An interagency GBV safety audit exercise conducted in April 2021 at the Juba IDP camp 1 and 3 indicated that insecurity in the camps had increased significantly attributed to lack of accountability, breakdown of rule of law, and economic hardships among the IDP communities which remain the greatest triggers of GBV. Limited access to GBV services coupled with cultural and structural barriers in addition remain a major challenge in most parts of the Country.

3 - Project indicators

Success will be measured using the IRC’s IMPACT standards which identifies a set of monitoring actions across the project life cycle. The project will dedicate funds to recruit monitoring and evaluation staff that will be charged with setting up project M&E systems and check points to track project implementation progress and to document learning. The project will be implemented in line with the overall strategic objectives of the Humanitarian Needs Overview and GBV Sub-Cluster strategic objectives to ensure that activities align with the overall prioritized needs of affected populations. The indicators tracked will be in line with the GBV Sub-Cluster priorities and will be disaggregated by age, sex, nationality, residency and disability. This will further inform access and utilization of project assistance by vulnerable groups in the community. At the project design, indicators to measure the project success will be identified together with the targets as outlined by the GBV SC. Indicator trackers, activity tracking tools and project dashboard will be developed at the onset of the project while data resulting from the project implementation will be routinely collected and analyzed to measure the progress vis a vie the targets set and program timelines. WPE program will continue utilizing available monitoring tools including GBVIMS to record and store GBV survivors’ data, psychosocial functioning tool to monitor and evaluate survivors’ psychological wellbeing, WGSS participation, satisfaction, and knowledge survey to assess women’s and girls’ experience in WGSS, EMAP and Girl Shine monitoring tools to evaluate knowledge, behaviors, and altitude of participants before, during and after engagement in the respective interventions. The Senior Manager will provide technical support to the field teams on tools while the program managers will do regular follow-up to ensure the tools are consistently used and data is recorded in the right database. Analysis of data will also be done on regular basis to inform the program on progress towards the milestones, adjust implementation and reporting to donors, beneficiaries, and other stakeholders. In addition to that, indicator tracking table will be developed to ensure indicator’s progress is updated on a monthly basis. Additionally, monitoring of the work plan will be conducted to keep the project team well informed on the timelines for conducting the activities contributing to the achievement of each indicator.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Experience and research has shown that if beneficiaries do not know what has become of their feedback, then they will lose trust in the feedback process. At the IRC, we respond to beneficiaries’ feedback by asking them how we should respond, selecting the appropriate channels, identifying the timing for the response, planning and allocating resources, recording and keeping track of client feedback, and monitoring and reviewing our feedback mechanisms. Complaints and feedback will be analyzed and presented during weekly field site meetings and monthly project cycle meetings where practical actions to address them will put in place with clear timelines and responsibility allocated. These actions will then be followed up in subsequent meetings to ensure they are implemented and to measure the results. Key actions to adapt project assistance will include, but not limited to, making programmatic changes on assistance delivery, changing policy, provision of goods and services, providing information on available assistance, providing linkages with other projects, providing external referrals and including the needs of clients in project funding proposals.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

IRC will collect, analyze and use feedback from beneficiaries regardless of their age, nationality, gender, residency status, education status, disability status and other diversity factors. IRC will use the Washington Group Short Set of Questions to disaggregate feedback and complaints by disability status. The IRC’s feedback channels will disaggregate feedback and complaints by gender, age, nationality, residency status and disability status to enable us to understand the needs and perspectives of different clients, including the most vulnerable members in the communities we serve. We will pay special attention to the most vulnerable and gather their feedback through key informant interviews, focus group discussions and community meetings. The community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements include persons with special needs to ensure their needs are into account.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

IRC will strictly adhere to the protection principles throughout the project periodacross the proposed locations. All project staff will be trained on prevention ofsexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) messaging will be incorporated in the community awareness raising activities to ensure the community members areaware of what PSEA entails, the available reporting mechanisms and theimportance of reporting. IRC will build the capacity of PSEA networks or focalpoint (s) who will be included in the community sensitization activities toensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms.IEC materials with PSEA messages will be developed, translated into the locallanguage, and distributed to the communities.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be informed on the scope of the project, timelines, key decision gates and community accountability mechanisms and systems during project opening meetings, project review and coordination meetings, stakeholder and community forums, information boards and using IEC materials. IRC has established community-led feedback management committees in IDP camps and refugee settlements who will inform community members on the available client feedback and complaints mechanisms that they can use to provide feedback about project assistance. The community-led feedback management committees will also follow up with IRC on actions taken on the feedback and complaints provided to ensure that the feedback and complaints received from clients are included in adaptive program management. As a client-centered organization, the IRC has a well-structured client responsiveness and accountability system in place where clients’ complaints and feedbacks are collected, analyzed, and used to inform program decision making. We will use both the proactive and reactive feedback channels to solicit client feedback that will guide project implementation and strategies for improvement. This feedback will be used to inform decision-making and adapt interventions to achieve better outcomes for beneficiaries in line with their priority needs and preferences.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

IRC will continue to strictly adhere to the protection principles throughout the project period across the proposed locations. PSEA messaging will be incorporated in the community awareness raising activities to ensure the community members are aware of PSEA which includes the available reporting mechanisms and the importance of reporting. The humanitarian workers through the various coordination forums will be reminded of the protection principles to ensure that their interventions are not causing more harm to the beneficiaries. IRC will scale-up the community sensitization activities to ensure communities are well versed with the available GBV services and in addition, enhance their participation in developing the community referral pathways from the onset of the project. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the IRC to handle any compliant report and feedback. IRC will explain its procedures, decisions from the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries to allow women, girls, men and boys to hold IRC accountable.
