Project Summary

The project aims to strengthen the coordination of community engagement across all agencies by focusing on mainstreaming and harmonizing existing tools and linking community input to decision making. The Communication and Community Engagement (CCE) Working Group will be the forum where these activities will take place. ACTED, as a co-chair of the CCE Working Group, in coordination with the other two co-leads, Internews and IOM, will link the Working Group to the humanitarian coordination architecture by strengthening the links between the group and the different clusters. `
To enhance program quality at every stage of the humanitarian programme cycle and contribute to strengthening accountability to affected population, the project will ensure that affected communities are adequately engaged and provided with timely and accurate information about how and why humanitarian aid is available to them, know and demand their rights from the humanitarian responders. The CCE activities also ensure the inclusion of the different vulnerable groups, such as women, elders, persons with disabilities and children, by making sure they are informed about their rights; they are aware of the available complaints and feedback mechanisms; by facilitating awareness raising campaigns, including the strengthening of prevention of SEA.
The role of the CCE Working Group remains essential in this process since despite the existence of some mechanisms at an agency level, coordinated messaging, complaints and feedback systems, enhancing two-way communication systems through the promotion of a standardized platform where partners can obtain meaningful analysis on complaint and feedback systems across the humanitarian response remains essential and yet under-performed.
Under this project, ACTED will lead the capacity building component of the suggested activities always in coordination with Internews and IOM.

1 - Needs

Community engagement is a two-fold communication between the affected communities and the service providers as well as amongst the different groups of the communities. Through this process, it is ensured that affected persons are informed on a timely manner about the response and they also provide feedback, express their needs and the humanitarian response is being designed and implemented taking into consideration the capacity of the communities.In South Sudan, there are different examples of CCE activities; training of community leaders in CwC and humanitarian activities; addressing the challenges faced by the so called “left-behind” youth/gang members through listening groups, radio broadcasts, and other peacebuilding activities as warranted; supporting persons with disabilities, adolescents (boys and girls) and women in PoC sites and relevant surrounding areas to constructively engage as active agents of their own recovery and peaceful future; to maintain a radio service for the PoCs for the service for better community relations, information access and Humanitarian accountability and engagement; building and supporting a “Boda Boda Talk Talk” (BBTT) operations; and strengthening town hall meetings as open forum with the learning objective of strengthening CCCM partners’ ability to foster regular, two-way communication, and to use community concerns to adapt humanitarian programming and interventions accordingly and to ensure AAP. However, most of these activities take place in the PoCs or in specific locations. Despite the inclusion of AAP on HRP and SSHF proposals and the CCE WG’s presentation on CCE and AAP to the different clusters, many partners still do not have a clear understanding on how to include CCE elements in their response. In addition, despite some CCE activities conducted by partners, CCE is not embedded in all the phases of the programme cycle. Humanitarian agencies need support to start integrating CCE activities to their responses to benefit from the engagement of the community. It starkly highlights the need for investment and capacity building and strengthening in response coordination to better integrate community engagement, enabling community priorities and feedback to inform operational and strategic decision-making at the inter-cluster/sector and HCT levels. Despite the strong two-fold communication examples mentioned above, a functioning, comprehensive and common Feedback Platform Mechanism (CFPM) is needed to complete AAP strategies with feedback systematically informing decision-making, programming and strategy.

Act. 4: Develop and Pilot of Complaints and Feedback Platform (CFP)4.1: Develop operational guidelines for common complaint platform whereby partners operating in same geographic and service areas can feed into mechanism to address service gaps, complaints, and community feedback issues in a coordinated manner4.2. This activity will be led by ACTED in coordination with Internews and IOM.6.2: Provide technical support to humanitarian agencies on best practices in conducting communications campaigns, operating mass communication systems, and ensuring widespread awareness raising and sensitization6.3: Support implementation of PSEA measures in coordination with PSEA Taskforce, through enhancement of Community Based Complaints Mechanisms (CBCM) Activity 7: National Accountability campaigns, dissemination, and mainstreaming7.1: Conduct National Accountability Campaign (national observance day) for AAP and community engagement7.2: Produce guidance, visibility and field guides on CCE

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Through technical support to all clusters, the CCE working group can promote and mainstream inclusive communication strategies. As a common platform for information sharing and standardized approaches to community engagement, the CCE working group can play a central role in ensuring a humanitarian response that is accountable and transparent. This project is specifically intended to build the capacity of partners in AAP, through ensuring use of approaches which include specific groups such as minority groups, women and youth in consultations and decision making and ensure their equal access to assistance. Training will target equal numbers of men and women.
