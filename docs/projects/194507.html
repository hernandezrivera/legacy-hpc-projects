Project Summary

The purpose of this proposed nutrition response plan is to increase access and scale-up to lifesaving essential and integrated nutrition interventions by establishing 10 OTP/TSFP mobile or static sites in Kapoeta South, Bor South, Duk and Ibba counties to benefit about 97,534 beneficiaries (i.e. 12,865 under five years children and 8,232 PLWs. A total of 4,204 children (2,134 girls and 2,070 boys) with SAM, 8,661 children (4,733 girls and 3,928 boys) with moderate acute malnutrition and 8,232 pregnant and lactating women will be reached by CRSF in the three project locations.
Additionally CRSF plans to reach 23,128 with MIYCN services, 50,140 with VAS and 14,221 BSFP services for under five children and 8,232 BSFP for PLWs in Kapoeta South, Bor South, Ibba and Duk counties.
3 OTP/TSFP mobile sites will be establish at Lonyangat Machi I, Pwata and Longeleya payams in Kapoeta South in Eastern Equatoria state. The 4 other mobile OTP/TSFPs will provide an avenue to reach children under 5 years in hard-to-reach locations in Anyidi, Makuach, and Baidit payams respectively in Bor south County, Jonglei State while 3 OTP/TSFP mobile sites will be established in Payuel, Padiet and dongchak payams in Duk county.
The activities will aim at linking nutrition Interventions with the existing FSL, WASH, and Health and protection activities by coordinating with other partners in the three locations. CRSF is proposing 2023 HRP activities which is aligned to the nutrition cluster’s objectives, indicators and activities for the HRP 2023.
CRSF will coordinate with the existing Nutrition Partners in four locations to avoid duplication of interventions and to ensure effective referrals . These partners includes MaCDA & SCI in Kapoeta South, SCI in Bor South, and AFSS in Ibba. CRSF will engage and coordinate with UNICEF and WFP for core supply pipeline support for RUTF, RUSF, CSB++and other routine medications and therapeutic rations for the beneficiaries in both SAM and MAM programs.
CRSF will strengthen the management of SAM and MAM in Health facilities by linking with health partners in Kapoeta South, Bor South, Ibba and Duk counties. All Nutrition staff will be trained on protection package to identify and refer children with protection concerns for specialized care.
The project will ensure recruitment of 20 Nutrition Assistants who will be charged with management and treatment of children and PLWs discovered with acute malnutrition in line with the SS CMAM guideline.
Through the established mother to mother support groups, CRSF will ensure that women and youth are engaged in the project design, planning and implementation as well as ensuring that complaints and feedback mechanisms are incorporated into this project. Further, CRSF will ensure that vulnerable caretakers are trained on nutrition sensitive agriculture for production of varieties of vegetables, establish 10 demonstration gardens at 10 mobiles OTP/TSFP sites or health facilities. CRSF will ensure that the response targets children (boys and girls) under five living with disabilities.
To ensure improved coverage of nutrition services in hard-to-reach areas, CRSF will coordinate with other nutrition partners to plan a scale up nutrition response through establishment of 4 mobile OTP/TSFP nutrition outreach services integrated into routine Health outreach activities, Food Security, WASH, services while a close coordination with other partners will be maintained to ensure that Nutrition staffs are trained on Protection and GBV referral mechanisms in the four project locations. Currently, CRSF secured funds from Care Plus Foundation International (CPFI) to support integrated nutrition sensitive interventions in Bor South and Kapoeta South Counties. The activities support women and adolescent girls with skills on cooking demonstrations and setting up kitchen gardens for dietary diversity as well as training them on family MUAC. CRSF has gained sufficient experience and strong presence.

1 - Needs

• According to results of the Food Security and Nutrition Monitoring System (FSNMS) Round 28 and 2022 SMART surveys, the proportion of children 6-59 months suffering from acute malnutrition remains above the 15% WHO emergency threshold at 16.0% (15.4 -16.7) in lean season (FSNM report, Oct 2022). When compared with 2019 FSNMS survey results at 16.2% GAM , there is no significant improvement while the current 2022 nutrition programme data shows increasing trend in admission of children and women with acute malnutrition by 24% compared to 2021 (Nut. Cluster, 2022). Majority of the affected population are children <5 and PLWs.•The Feb 2022 IPC report informs tha; 1,341,635 children 6-59 months are acutely malnourished with 302,163 Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and 1,039,472 Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) while 675,710 PLWs are acutely malnourished in South Sudan (SS IPC 2022). Covariates including floods, conflict, and acute food insecurity and sub national violence in many parts of the county continue to drive acute malnutrition while the Russia-Ukraine conflict and COVID-19 pandemic effects has indirectly impacted nutrition of the vulnerable children <5 and PLWs; poor MIYCN practices continue to contribute negatively impact the nutrition situation of the most vulnerable groups. Therefore, a continued scale up of treatment and preventive measures for acute malnutrition targeting vulnerable children <5 and PLWs in insecure and inaccessible areas needs to be prioritized so to improve their nutrition status.• In Bor South, severe flooding displaced local populations living in communities of Makwach, Baidit, Anyidi and Bor town resulting into IDPs comprised of women and Children who were observed to be wasted (IRNA report 2020). While in Ibba county, sub national violence in the neighboring Tambura, Nagero and Ezo have led to scores of IDPs in Nampaziya payam and in Kapoeta south county, the observed dry spells of drought and wind is putting children and women especially PLWs at heightened risk of high food insecurity and consequently high risk of acute malnutrition. Currently, the nutrition situation in Bor and Duk is alarming with GAM rates as high as 22% & and and and 25% respectively (Nut. Cluster,2022). • Other drivers include; natural, man-made, floods, ethnic conflicts and violence, and macroeconomic shocks resulted into mass displacement and limited access to crops, livestock, and markets while Kapoeta south, Bor south, and Duk counties continue to settle in Emergency IPC Phase 4 by March 2023 (IPC projections, Oct 2022). Although, there are static nutrition interventions, the displaced IDPs have no access to nutrition services due to low coverage of available nutrition interventions . CRSF will reach the affected population by establishing mobile OTP/TSFP services and provide integrated MIYCN services, agriculture linked to nutrition sensitive interventions and establish Kitchen gardens at nutrition sites. Further, CRSF will train nutrition and health workers on best agronomic practices, train caretakers/mothers of children <5 on best cooking practices, establishing school gardens and nutrition clubs in schools as part of a wider multisectoral response in Kapoeta South, Bor south, Ibba, and Duk counties. CRSF will ensure a strong coordination with other static nutrition partners to identify gaps where mobile nutrition interventions are needed for CRSF to offer nutrition services so as to avoid duplication. CRSF is currently implementing a nutrition project on distribution of micro nutrient supplements like Vitamin A supplements to children under five with in-kind support from Vitamin Angels international in Bor South, Kapoeta South and Ibba counties and thus with static presence in Jonglei, EES and WES. CRSF has technical and experienced nutritionists and thus able to deliver quality interventions. CRSF is currently supported by CPFI to implement nutrition sensitive interventions in Bor South and Kapoeta South.

This project will support the realization of the Cluster strategic objectives by implementing the following activities; o Routine management of SAM & MAM in under-five & PLWs in mobile and static OTP/TSFP siteso Conduct mobile outreach services in hard-to-reach locations in Bor South, Duk, and Kapoeta South Counties.o Conduct Vitamin A supplementation, Albendazole tabs, and integrated with mosquitoes net distribution.o Conduct routine Rapid Malaria test, treat and track for under-five with SAM/MAM in OTP/TSFPs siteso Conducting MIYCN individual and group counselling of PLWs and caregivers of children 0 - 23 months using MIYCN counselling cards. o Training health/nutrition staff on MIYCN guidelines, kitchen garden, CMAM guidelines, SAM-ITP, and NIS capacity development.o Establishing baby mother friendly areas/shades for care and key messages education sessions. o Conduct routine and mass MUAC screening of children in the OTP/TSFP site and community trained community nutrition volunteers (CNVs). o Conducting distribution of VAS supplements and deworming tablets for under-fives and PLWso Conducting Monitoring and Evaluation of nutrition activities/community consultation meeting about the quality of nutrition services. o Preposition of nutrition supplies at nutrition sites and Equipping nutrition sites with data collection tools. o Submitting monthly nutrition reports in NIS on a timely basis. o Training of nutrition workers on Malaria testing for children admitted with SAM and MAM. o Establishing kitchen gardens for demonstration to PLWs and caregivers of children with SAM. o Procuring and distributing child-play kits to all nutrition sites for stimulation of children admitted with SAM with medical complications at SCso Training of health/nutrition workers on GBV/CP referral.o Conducting GBV risk assessment and safety audits for all nutrition sites.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

People living with disabilities will form the key target beneficiaries of this project. To ensure feedback from these groups, CRSF will ensure that people living with disabilities are part of the focus group discussion and stakeholders mobilization meetings. In these forums, they will be given priority in terms of voicing their opinions and getting feedback from them.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

CRSF will conduct stakeholders mobilization and sensitization meetings and focus group discussions through which information regarding the project will be provided to the beneficiaries . Through the same meetings, we expect to get feedback from the community members and beneficiaries as well as complaints with respect to the project.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

At Organization level, CRSF has a strong policy on PSEA. In this project, all nutrition staff recruited to implement this project will be given an orientation training on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse. Beneficiaries will be empowered to report any case of PSEA. CRSF will provide suggestion boxes at all mobile nutrition sites to receive and obtain complaints and feedback from the beneficiaries in a confidential manner. All staff will be governed and sanctioned by CRSF's PSEA policy in accordance to IASC guideline.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Since the beneficiaries form a critical component of this project in terms of tailoring appropriate strategies for a successful implementation. CRSF nutrition project manager and program team will take issues raised by the beneficiaries to be discussed in a meeting in Juba with other senior staff as well as with the donors so that they are jointly addressed and adapted to suit the beneficiaries opinions.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During the planning phase of this project, CRSF will ensure that the population at risk (women and girls) are involved and consulted during the implementation of this project. CRSF will do this through holding of routine focus groups during implementation to have the views, options and strategies of the vulnerable groups incorporated in this project to maximize their participation and impact. CRSF will also ensure that the complaint feedback mechanism is working so that community members/beneficiaries can report complaints related to the services provided. CRSF will do this by placing suggestion boxes, holding stakeholders’ meetings, and empowering support groups of women and adolescent girls to share the challenges, successes and what is not working well with the project. CRSF will work coordinate and work closely with protection partners and ministry of gender to ensure that GBV is integrated into this project. CRSF will ensure that train nutrition staff members are trained on the GBV related issues to be able to identify, report and referral mechanism for case finding and management
