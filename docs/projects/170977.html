Project Summary

The nutrition situation in the country is serious and has not improved since last year and recent FSNAU Post Gu 2020 assessment reported a median GAM rate of 13.8% which translated to almost 1 million children expected to be malnourished in 2021, out of these 162,000 will suffer from sever form of acute malnutrition (SAM) and 800,000 will be moderate malnourished (MAM). The malnutrition situation in CARE operational areas range from serious to critical with 9-16.2 % in Sool and Sanag regions, 15.8% in Bosaso IDPs and 12.4% in Kismayo IDPs according to the latest FSNAU Post Gu 2020 assessment. The malnutrition situation is likely to worsen in pastoral areas and amongst IDPs as they face higher shock of seasonal food insecurity. From October to December 2020, food insecurity is expected to deteriorate among poor households with limited livestock or low capacity to cope with harvest losses as well as IDPs who are unable to meet their minimum food needs without external assistance. IDPs and pastoral communities are vulnerable to high food insecurity across the regions and this is mainly due to low purchasing power, lack of diversified income sources, leading to food scarcity, irregular meals and this also contributes to high diseases incidence resulting from lack of appetite and poor feeding practice and poor child spacing. Childhood morbidity is also leading to malnutrition as a result of poor hygiene and access to clean water. The water scarcity in the northern regions has triggered high water prices which are further expected to worsen during the dry seasons. CARE has also conducted IYCF KAP survey in Sep 2020 across 5 districts in Sool and Sanag and has found sub-optimal feeding practices which are further exacerbating the malnutrition situation in these areas. In light of this and to continue building on the gains made in 2020 CARE will continue to implement an integrated nutrition and health intervention which will also be linked with other CARE wash and food security programs. This integrated nutrition and health program will consist of several components and cover both curative and preventative interventions targeting 30,000 children U5 years (boys and girls) and pregnant and lactating women living in Bari, Sool and Sanag regions covering 10 districts (Bosaso, Badhan, Lasqoray, Ceelafweyn, Erigavo, Ainabo, Lascanod, Talex and Kismayo). The intervention will provide access to basic and emergency nutrition and health services through support to key MOH staff at mobile and fixed clinics to enable delivery of outreach services. Nutrition activities will include identification and screening of acute malnutrition at both the community level and facility level; admissions into OTP, TSFP and SC services; referral of complicate cases; micronutrient supplementations including Vitamin A and IYCF counselling and promotions services at community and facility level. In addition to beneficiaries will also receive basic health services including outpatient disease consultations and treatments; referral of complicated cases for inpatient care; EPI immunization; antenatal and postnatal case service; referral to BemoC services and delivery by skilled birth attendants. CARE will partner with MOH and WASDA and support 33 fixed sites in Badhan, elafweyn, Erigavo, Lascanod, and Afmadow and 40 mobile sites in Bosaso, Ainabo, Badhan and Taleh in Puntland, Somaliland and Juba land. Pandemics such as COVID-19 present more opportunities for exploitation and abuse. As the need for aid increases, with supply of and access to aid decreasing simultaneously, these unequal power dynamics are exacerbated further. Concurrently, due to the restrictions placed on movement, domestic violence, intimate partner, and gender-based violence are also likely to increase. As a result of these vulnerabilities CARE will train all its staff on PSEA in the nutrition centres in order for them identify and refer risk groups and know what signs to look out for.

Project Activities

This project will consist of the following components/activities which are all in line with the nutrition cluster strategic objective 1,2,31. Community mobilisation, screening and referral of children aged 6-59 months and PLW with acute malnutrition. Children identified with SAM (severe acute malnutrition) will be admitted to Outpatient therapeutic program (OTP); Children and PLW (pregnant and lactating women) with MAM will be admitted to (TSFP); and all children with SAM with medical complications will be referred to the nearest SC. Treatment will be done using the established CMAM Somali protocols. 2. Provision of IYCF prevention services to both children under 5 and PLW attending the nutrition and health program. These services will include: IYCF individual counselling, nutrition, health and hygiene promotions. During the emergency the COVID 19, focus will be put on IYCF-E to support, protect and promote infant feeding in emergencies. Communication for development will be strengthened to support key optimal behaviours and negate against the key barriers to optimal IYCF in emergencies. Additionally, key MOH staff will be orientated on IYCF-E/IYCF to provide counselling support to mothers attending the clinics and sites. Mother to Mother peer support groups will be established in the community to scale up and further improve community based IYCF counselling with COVID 19 measures such as socali distancing and the use of face masks being full in place. 3. Provision of Micronutrient support to PLW and children<5 attending OTP, TSFP and IYCF services. They will receive micronutrient supplementation including multiple micronutrients, iron folate, Vitamin A, to prevent micronutrient deficiencies. They will also be dewormed to prevent malabsorption of nutrients and to prevent malnutrition. 4. Provision of routine immunization services to children in the OTP and TSFP program This service will be offered at the nutrition sites both the fixed and mobile ones in order to ensure children in the OTP receive the full vaccination 5. Provision of IMCI (integrated management of childhood illnesses) services to children in the OTP and TSFP programs and will identify sick children who need treatment for common childhood illnesses such as diarrhoea, malaria, measles etc. This will ensure that all children needing both nutrition and health services are given a holisitic service every time they visit a CARE nutrition program. 6. Provision of reproductive health care services for PLW including focused antenatal and postnatal services and delivery by skilled birth attendant for all mothers attending the nutrition sites. 7. CARE will conduct IYCF KAP surveys in Sool and Sanag to measures the acheivements and progress made so far in the communities we serve. The study will also inform program design and IEC development.

Mitigation Measures

For the risk of marginalised being excluded from accessing nutrition services CARE will request the systematic participation of representatives of marginalized communities including IDPs in consultation meetings with local leaders. We will establish direct contact with leaders from marginalised groups to get information of whether they can access services without discrimination from anyone. If any issue comes up CARE will raise the issue with the concerned people and ensure a solution is reached and that these groups are represented in all the services. There could also be risk of not accessing the complaing and feedback menchanism and to combat this CARE will ensure confidentiality at all level of engagement with the community and inform them that their feedback will not be passed to any third party nor will their be any negative consequences on our part. Community members will be offered mny avenues to make their complaint including hotlines, suggestion boxes. There is also a risk of SGBV for caregivers attending the nutrition services and to mitigate against such risk CARE will:- Keep children and women safe while accessing nutrition services by considering the working hours, distance to the centre among other factors.- Ensure that children are not left at home unattended while caregivers or siblings attend nutrition appointments- Ensure that treatment programmes avoid family separation and take into account the impact on siblings- Ensure that those working in nutrition centres have signed up to and been trained in a code of conduct and are fully briefed on child protection concerns- Know appropriate ways to handle children – e.g. allowing parents themselves to place the child into the hanging weighing scales etc. There is also a risk of violence when there is food distribution and to combat this CARE will engage community leaders to conduct community mobilisation of the day of food distribution to ensure that the community understand the target population of the nutrition program. CARE will post of the billboard the exact ration every patient is allowed so as to not cause confusion and keep correct perception of what we offer. There will also be community nutrition volunteers which will inform the beneficairies and refer cases from the community. CARE will provide services in protected and gated areas to avoid looting of nutrition supplies.
