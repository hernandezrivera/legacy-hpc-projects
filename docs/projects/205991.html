Project Summary

Concern will continue to respond to food insecurity in Aweil West and Aweil North counties of Northern Bahr El Ghazal state with emergency livelihoods support and cash based interventions. Concern will provide agricultural inputs (seeds and tools) to targeted vulnerable households to enhance their food production. The will include both vegetable and main season staple crop seeds. The interventions aim at improving household dietary diversity score through growing and consumption of vegetables at household level in addition to staple crop production. Concern will ensure the farmers/beneficiaries receiving seeds equally get the technical support from the project technical staff to improve their production practices, increase yields and reduce the wide hunger gap. In the face of the changing climate, Concern will continue to tailor main and promote climate smart agriculture (CSA) practices as part of efforts aimed at preparing communities to develop resilience to shocks and adapt to climate change. This will be achieved through trainings and follow up visits to targeted households by Concern technical staff and the County Agriculture Department staff with the support from Community Based Agriculture Extension Workers (CBAEWs). In partnership with WFP, Concern will continue to implement food and cash for asset interventions, focusing on conditional food and cash transfers with targeted households engaged in both household and community asset creation activities.

1. Needs

South Sudan is ranked last out of the 191 countries in Human Development Index and ranked 8th in the Global Climate Risk Index. INFORM estimates South Sudan's humanitarian crisis and disaster risk to be very high at 8.5/10 with Hazard and Exposure ranked at 7.3/10, vulnerability ranked at 8.8/10 and lack of coping capacity is at 9.4/10. The protracted conflict, paired with recurring climatic shocks, triggered population displacement; disrupted agricultural production; eroded trade, markets and basic services; exacerbating the local adaptive capacities and increased dependency on humanitarian aid including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan. The trends shows 5% annual increase in people in need (PIN), reaching its highest level in 2023 where 9.4 million people (76% of the population) are reported to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2023 (HNO). The primary factors driving needs is food insecurity, contributing to high levels of acute malnutrition among children and women. The latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) and Acute Malnutrition (AMN) analysis, released on November 6, 2023, projects NBeG State to face high level of food insecurity in 2024 with 56% of its population in IPC 3/4. The IPC analysis indicated an emergency level (IPC 4) of food insecurity in Aweil West and Aweil North Counties, while 40,000 are likely to be in Catastrophe (IPC 5) acute food insecurity. The persistently high level of food insecurity is leading to high rates of malnutrition among children, women and older people. Both the counties are among highest with burden of acute malnutrition Aweil North, GAM prevalence of 24.7% and SAM prevalence of and in Aweil West SAM is 2.2% and stunting 20.5%. The major factors contributing to acute malnutrition include high prevalence of diseases, poor WASH conditions, inadequate feeding practices, poor diet and unequal access to food which is further compounded by limited access to infant and maternal health care. While subsistence agriculture remains the main livelihood activity of the vast majority of rural communities, Concern plans to provide emergency livelihoods kits (crop, vegetable) to the vulnerable rural households in 2024. The livelihood kits support is aimed at improving access to quality basic agriculture inputs to enable farmers embark of production for self-sufficiency. Concern equally considers provision of agronomic trainings alongside the livelihood inputs as key to achieving the results. Experience working with farmers and communities in the target counties over the years shows prevalence of traditional farming practices. Concern’s 2024 agriculture interventions will include farmers training using demonstration plots to enhance hands-on learning process. Alongside trainings and provision of farming inputs, Concern will also distribute food rations and cash for targeted vulnerable households to address immediate food needs and build local coping mechanisms to food insecurity.

2. Project activities

CO1: Concern will partner with WFP to implement Asset Creation and Livelihoods program in Aweil West and Aweil North. The intervention aims at addressing immediate food needs for vulnerable households through conditional cash assistance (directly provided by WFP). Concern will ensure households are engaged in asset creation activities (crop and vegetable production), and community asset creation activities (access roads, dykes, etc.) as a condition for access to cash transfers. This will promote community resilience through developing community infrastructure, climate smart agriculture production and food production at local level. Given the unrestricted nature of cash, households will be able to access their top priority needs. Cash transfers will be made on monthly basis. Concern has streamlined cross cutting themes including gender and protection issues, inclusion, conflict sensitivity and accountability in the programme design. At inception stage and targeting, Concern will work closely with local leaders, women, youth and chiefs to ensure cross cutting themes are fully integrated in implementation processes. This will include forming Project Management Committees (PMCs) that are 50% male and 50% female, and working with youth, women and local leaders to ensure inclusion of persons with special needs in the programme. CO2: The agriculture interventions aims to address immediate emergency needs, enhance recovery, and contribute to the goal of preventing widespread food insecurity among vulnerable populations. The smallholder farmers will be supported to improve their agricultural production through technical guidance and access to seeds and farm implements. Alongside agronomic trainings, the farmers will be supported with training on Drought Animal Technology (DAT) use either ox-or donkey ploughs to improve tillage practices to enable and improve food production and crop yields to ensure enough seeds are produced and stored for subsequent planting seasons. Established farmer organizations and SMEs will be capacitated through trainings tailored to their identified gaps/needs and linkages to markets facilitated for agricultural marketing of products. To improve food and nutrition security of targeted HHs, Concern will support vegetable production through home gardening and vegetable production groups in all targeted counties. Mainly the nutrition beneficiaries, mother and father groups will be supported with vegetable seeds and tools to improve their access to nutritious food and increase their dietary diversity. While Concern will not implement food assistance program, its conditional cash assistance program will enhance access to food with cash entitlements depending on the targeted household priorities. The conditionality of cash assistance is attached to household asset creation activities (crop and vegetable production) which are directly linked to CO2. The same households are also engaged in community asset creation activities (flood control dykes, construction of community access roads, etc.) for long term enhancement of resilience building. The program will be implemented in partnership with WFP who are responsible for cash transfer processes, with Concern directly responsible to ensure agreed conditions are met by beneficiaries as well as facilitating cash distribution activities with a WFP contracted Financial Service Provider (FSP).CO3: Concern will procure locally available staple seeds through fairs and promote seed security & multiplication strengthening. Capacity building will focus on both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation and support village saving and loan association (VSLA). Concern will continue to collaborate with County Agriculture Department (CAD) through supporting them and engaging Community-Based Agriculture Extension Workers (CBAEWs) from CAD to deliver services and agronomic trainings to improve resilience.
