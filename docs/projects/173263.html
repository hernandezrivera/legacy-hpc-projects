Project Summary

HOPE is planning to work in increasing safe access to education and CP services for children and adolescents in Central Darfur State, which will contribute to the HRP education and CP strategic objectives (for 2021). This project is an integrated project, some CP beneficiaries such as out of school's children will be benefits from school activities as well as the education beneficiaries will be benefited from CP activities.
In this Education component HOPE will target 4 basic schools, 2 schools in each locality of Gharb Jabal Marrah (Nertiti), and Um Dukhun to provide Primary School Kits for pupils, EiE kits for Teachers, EiE training for teachers, Uniforms & Hygiene Kit for pupils, classrooms construction & rehabilitation, WASH in school, PTA training, support Schools plan and implementation, community campaigns and ALP/catch-ups activities.
In the CP component, HOPE will target 4 communities, 2 in each locality, the main objectives are: (1) Improve access to comprehensive case management services for vulnerable girls and boys with protection concerns including unaccompanied and separated children, (2) Strengthen the capacity and resilience of children and communities to prevent and respond to violence through community-based mechanisms, coordination and evidence-based response, (3) Provide structured and specialized child protection services for girls and boys at risk of violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect in targeted locations. The activities are Provide Case Management and referral services, Establish or construct semi-permanent safe spaces, Place and support Unaccompanied and Separated Children in Alternative care, Provide Family tracing services and reunification services to UASC, Capacity building activities, including mentoring and coaching, for government, NGO staff and community-based child protection structures, Provide legal assistance and counseling on civil documentation (including birth certificates and other civil documents), Assistance to children with Disability, Run and Maintain of Child-Friendly spaces, Provision of Mental health counseling services to children, Provide Structured and sustained PSS services, conduct community-based group activities, and community engagement and awareness.
HOPE will consider accountability to affected people through full community participation, strengthen the current community mechanism/ PTAs and CBCPN as well as do follow up for all benefit targeted separately to assess the project, identify challenges and provide give recommendations to improve the project outcomes.

1 - Needs

After the longtime of conflict and prolonged humanitarian crisis, the emergency situation in Darfur raised intensively, and the conflict between the government and armed groups and then between tribes as well as negatively affected children especially in Jebel Marra areas and Um Dukhun. The Education and CP severity for both localities are 4 or 5, the People In Need (PIN) for CP are 33,624 in Um Dukhun and 41,769 in Gharb Jabal Marrah including IDPs. In both localities, and based to HOPE assessments findings (Jan-2020) in both targets localities, the access and quality of education are very low, based on the following: - The number of pupils/children per classroom is more than 90 pupils, in addition to that, it is not at an adequate level even lack learning environment. - Lack of WASH facilities including latrines, water points, handwashing, and hygiene promotion, even the schools have access to WASH facilities, there are partial facilities of it. - There are more than 40% of school-age children in IDP camps are out of school meanly in Um Dukhun. - Lack of school children seating. - Lack of textbooks, exercise books, and teaching aids, the ratio of the textbook. Also, based to HOPE assessments findings (Jan-2020) in both targets localities, a huge gap for child protection services for the below reasons: - Ongoing conflicts have been and newly displaced resulted in severe negative impacts on people in Jebel Mara; casualties, trauma among children, females, and other groups. - Child, women, and young females have been at risk and live within a very destructive environment; do not enjoy their rights, possible incidents of GBV, poor health services, and many other issues affecting their lives. - Lack of protection services especially services to address Sexual & Domestic Violence and Family tracing & reunification, and referral system. - Lack of awareness of parents of Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC). - Poor capacities local mechanisms of Community-based Child Protection Networks for effective protection issues and concerns resolution and absence of follow up for action taken. Women and girls continue to face the risk of GBV including sexual violence when they leave the villages. Fear of reprisals from attackers often prevents rape survivors from seeking medical help. The conflict in Darfur has also increased the number of women who are the sole providers for their families. Sudan also faces issues with more chronic levels of malnutrition with one in every three children under the age of five years affected. As a result, these children will have lower lifetime earnings, and lower productivity as adults, influencing the economic growth and potential of the entire country.The child protection needs in both localities are lack of case management and referral services, lack safe spaces in communities, lack of legal assistance including civil documentation, and trauma experiences. Due to the above situation, HOPE looks for improving access to Education and CP services. HOPE, through MoU that is signed with SMoE in Central Darfur, has access to schools with high needs in target localities as well as HOPE will fully coordinate with UNICEF and other Education and CP partners in both localities.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

To ensure protection mainstreaming, HOPE will use the below actions: - Conduct regular consultations with particularly high-risk groups of the affected population such as women, girls, and children with disabilities. - Support community engagement and interaction with schools through PTA meetings.- Establishment of complaints and feedback mechanisms.- Foster school-based Disaster Risk Reduction plans/strategies; to support activities that contribute to child protection and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention including psychosocial support, and referral pathways; - Teachers, learners, and parents, will be oriented on topics such as protection, human rights, sexual and gender-based violence, and non-discrimination approaches to access humanitarian aid. - Ensure that all school construction is in line with accessibility standards for persons with a disability, cash-based assistance will be provided to children with disabilities to access tools and materials to enhance their learning.- Pay attention to the choice of locations and targeting of beneficiaries to avoid perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory or non-equitable manner,- Conducting community safety audits and mapping of exiting services and ensuring that the results of these two exercises inform programming and advocacy. - Maintain transparent, inclusive, and adequate communication with communities on available services; and by consulting communities and duty bearers in the planning, selection of services, feedback after service provision. - Guided on ensuring prioritization of services to groups considered particularly vulnerable, such as young children, children with disability adolescent boys and girls, children without adult care, the elderly, and children and adults with specific needs ensuring that programming is tailored to respond to the needs of adolescent boys and girls.- Involve community actors (especially CBCPN, PTAs, school teachers in this project) in the project planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation to promote participation and empowerment.- Involving and consulting CBCPN, and PTAs in planning and conducting a monthly meetings at the community level to avoid causing harm and prioritize safety and dignity.
