Project Summary

The reports from the protection working groups in Areesha camps indicate that the need for protection activities is increasing in the camp, due to the small number of organizations that operate in it, this lack of services has negative effects on all aspects of residents lives and leaves them in an extremely stressful and vulnerable situation which creates an environment for violence against women and children to thrive, and leaves persons with disabilities and elderly people with little or no support. At the same time, the lack of knowledge of the camp's population about the available legal services and information leaves many children and families without documents and limits their access to other services such as education and health services. .
the project aims to Increase the capacity of conflict-affected communities to prevent, mitigate, and respond to protection risks and enhance their positive coping mechanism. through the project will improve the well-being of the targeted group as a result of their participation in the project's interventions. the project works to ensure that the Survivors of GBV have safe access to basic, quality case management services, and psychosocial services
the intervintion will include GBV case management services to survivors of GBV using a survivor-centered approach that prioritizes the needs, confidentiality, dignity, and safety of the survivor to promote survivors’ healing. In-kind distributions will complement it. Individual PSS sessions will be tailored to the needs of women and girls. A structured PSS and skills-focused sessions for women and adolescent girls to Support the development of abilities for adaptive and positive behavior to enable women and adolescent girls to deal with demands and challenges faced across cultures. for women and adolescent girls Structured individual GBV PSS and group interventions for women and girls at risk will be the project will ensure that women, girls, and community members take part in GBV prevention and mitigation activities and receive accurate messages on service availability and accessibility.
the project activitties will engage women and girls at the WGSS and provide them with information on available services. Also targeted sessions on GBV prevention will be rolled out with men and boys.
The project staff will conduct information awareness-raising sessions targeting women, girls, men, and boys providing information on services available and delivering GBV prevention messages to the community including topics such as ending forced/early marriage, becoming allies to women and girls, and speaking up against intimate partner violence.
the GBV survivors get successfully referred to internal or external services. also, women and girls have safe access to WGSS , Also, enabling access for women and girls to quality women protection and empowerment activities through Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS). Activities will be delivered per WPE minimum standard in Humanitarian Action and include GBV case management, psychosocial support, awareness raising, and distribution of dignity kits.
Protection Risk Analysis: 1. GBV Risk Assessment: A thorough assessment of the specific GBV risks and vulnerabilities in the targeted Areesha Camp will be conducted. This assessment will consider factors such as social norms, power dynamics, displacement, and access to basic services. It will provide critical insights into the prevalence and forms of GBV in the project areas. 4. Risk Mitigation Strategies: Based on the identified protection risks and vulnerabilities, appropriate risk mitigation strategies will be developed and integrated into project activities. These strategies may include community engagement and mobilization, awareness-raising campaigns, capacity-building initiatives, and the establishment of referral mechanisms to ensure that women and girls can access timely and appropriate suppor.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Among the residents, females make up 53% of the camp population. Within this group, there are specific vulnerabilities across different age ranges. Approximately 19.3% are children aged 0-4 years, 38.2% are children aged 5-17 years, 39.4% are adults aged 18-59 years, and 3.1% are elderly individuals aged above 60 years. Residents in Areesha camp face numerous protection risks, such as child labor, child marriages influenced by cultural traditions and financial hardships, children leaving education prematurely, increased bullying targeting children with disabilities, intimate partner violence, and the abandonment of women and girls to assume the responsibility of raising children and providing income. Other concerning issues include polygamy, frequent pregnancies in short intervals, and low literacy rates. Despite the efforts of the existing organizations, the lack of additional support contributes to the struggles faced by residents, amplifying the challenges experienced by women in particular. Evaluation processes: The results that show the development of the beneficiary will be analyzed on a phased basis, as the follow-up and evaluation team will conduct interviews with the beneficiaries and review the pre- and post-evaluation forms, and their other comments will be analyzed, including the results of the focus groups, and accordingly we will issue our reports per month and we share it with the partner.Accountability: We use the Community Accountability Reporting Mechanisms (CARM) system which is located on the SALAM.org website, we receive comments and complaints through WhatsApp, email, and compliant boxes that are located on different project sites. The boxes can only be accessed by the MEAL coordinator. This will ensure that beneficiaries provide comments and express their concerns and suggestions, their comments will be reviewed and addressed on at least a weekly basis and those comments will be included in our periodic reports to improve the implementation of activities and services.Our organization is committed to the methodology of documenting and learning from the results of our activities. This process will result in analyses and useful observations summarized in periodic reports on the results and effects of participation in project activities on beneficiaries when participating in any activity in the center.Lessons learned and important observations will also be discussed in the monthly meetings of the project management team and transferred through the appropriate communication channels to the internal departments in SALAMor the main stakeholders whose work is related to these observations.
