Project Summary

The targeted sub-districts in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor governorates are severity 4-6.The estimated cost per beneficiary is around 33 USD.Estimated 2% of targeted beneficiaries may have disability Total funds received in 2019: around 1.1 m USD.Total estimated number of beneficiaries reached in 2019: 36,691 individuals.The project is not funded at this stage.Considering the needs and in line with the first pillar of No Lost Generation Initiative,context analysis and demographic trends, the intervention aims at re-establishing basic education services in the targeted communities, which are return areas where services have to cope with the additional stress of population growth. GVC opts for community support, preparing the ground for durable intervention to benefit boys and girls and improving resilience of the whole communities. The Action aims at increasing access to safe, inclusive and quality education for children and adolescents affected by the crisis in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor governorates,relying on GVC expertise in NFE(Non Formal Education),solid reputation and knowledge of the areas. First, to increase access to education, GVC will implement tailored activities targeting out of school children (OOSC) through a Back To Learning (BTL) campaign (including door to door visits, information sessions, interactive theatre) and PSS (Psychosocial Support) events to encourage them and their families to re-enrol in school, possibly, or consider alternative education pathways as well as identify children in need of additional support. The activities will be carried out in cooperation with Directorates of Education (DoEs) and Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) in Aleppo and Deir-Ez-Zor. Access to school will be enhanced by providing Remedial classes (RCs) to children at risk of drop out, in line with the standards set by the Ministry of Education (MoE) and the Education Sector. In line with the same goal, GVC will involve OOSC in Self-Learning Programs (SLP) supporting the re-enrolment of girls and boys who are out of the school system.Children and teachers in NFE programs will also receive stationery and school bags.Students and OOSC will be involved in PSS activities during the NFE classes and as community outreach events aimed at improving children’s life-skills and KSAs (Knowledge Skills Attitude).Safe and inclusive education will be ensured through rehabilitation of schools, which will include solutions to enable access to students with special needs,and gender and disability-sensitive WASH facilities.The rehabilitation works will increase the number of available learning spaces,having a positive effect in enrolment rates.Furniture, teaching and learning materials(school desks and School in a Box)will be delivered to the schools in need.Finally,GVC intervention will contribute to quality education by training teachers in Active Learning (AL), PSS and Risk Education (RE). The trained teachers will provide SLP and RCs, under the supervision of GVC.

4 - Needs

The intervention sub-districts were selected based on: Sector’s HNO, assessments conducted by GVC in November 2019, OCHA severity scale, returnees’ flows, GVC long-lasting presence in the field, evaluation of completed projects; secondary data (e.g.DoE); infrastructure analysis conducted by GVC engineers.Main problems identified.Insufficient safe and protective education spaces. Deir-Ez-Zor: data show that before the crisis there were 209 operational schools for 84.121 students. For the year 2019-20,74 schools - 35% of the existing educational facilities - are operational for 30.727 students (i.e. decrease of 63% compared to 2011 data).Aleppo: in several villages,schools are closed(e.g. in Hader, there were 7 active schools before the crisis,now only 3 are operational)and students attend classes in inadequate spaces.High rate of school dropout: it has different causes, including limited number of functioning schools in the resident areas,child labour,high number of students per classroom,low quality of education provided,lack of teachers.Deir-Ez-Zor: OOSC number is likely to raise due to flow of returnees and the high birth rate in the city.Based on the results of the assessment conducted by GVC, above 1.000 OOSC are in need of support.Aleppo:enrolment in school is low. In Maskana, Khafsa, Menbij, Tall ed daman and Hader sub-districts, more than 1,900 OOSC have been identified by GVC. Lack of gender and disability sensitive WASH facilities:serious barrier to access to learning and source of GBV. Lack of teaching and learning materials and furniture: schools lack furniture and didactic materials, while families cannot bear the costs of stationery and books for their children. Poor quality of education and lack of teachers: Formal and NFE services are weak.Quality of teaching dropped during the crisis as many teachers fled or were forced to quit their job.Educators are receiving insufficient training to deal with needs of OOSC,including psychosocial vulnerabilities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1.Insuﬃciently safe learning facilities-Risk:Learning facilities or their surroundings are structurally unsafe or do not provide suﬃcient protection from the elements (rain, snow, cold, dust, etc.)Likelihood: medium Impact: medium/high Mitigating Measure: GVC will rehabilitate learning facilities to ensure that NFE activities take place in safe and protected locations. GVC engineers will monitor the works and ensure Child Safeguarding measures are in place. Risk: Unsafe learning spaces and insuﬃcient/non-existent WASH facilities contribute to children not attending learning facilities Likelihood: medium Impact: medium Mitigating Measure: the rehabilitation of schools implemented by GVC will include WASH facilities and guarantee sex and age segregation as well as accessibility to people with disabilities. 2.Insuﬃcient support from, or undue inﬂuence by, members of the community or local authorities Risk: Delay or non-approvals of planned activities or planned processes. Likelihood: high Impact: high Mitigating Measure: a.GVC will work closely with national stakeholders(MoE/SARC) and involve community members through local partners (DoE/SARC local teams) ensuring support and beneficiaries’ endorsement of the project’s activities; b.GVC elaborated a “exit strategy” of the project and local authorities have a clear understanding of it.3.Students are not suﬃciently protected Risk: Children are exposed to the risk of harm by school personnel, organization staff or related entities. Likelihood: medium Impact: high Mitigating Measure: a.the application of learning and NFE activities will be monitored by GVC as part of ongoing professional development and support; b.the pressure on teachers will be reduced through measures like reasonable classroom sizes and reasonable working hours; c.teachers/educators will be provided with training that includes CP inputs, prior to the start of the activities; d.opportunities for children to be alone with adults will be avoided.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: a.Security context in Deir-Ez-Zor city and Rural Aleppo is stable; b.Access to targeted areas is safe for staff/companies to conduct rehabilitation works and activities; c.If schools assessed are not accessible anymore because of security reasons,DoE will select new schools in need; d.Learning spaces are accessible for SLP and RCs and beneﬁciaries can easily reach them; e.The relevant authority approves the activation of NFE programs(SLP and RCs centers); f.Local communities and other actors in the areas of intervention are positive with regard to the services provided; g.Market prices are not subject to huge variations than those already foreseen and materials are available on local markets. RISKS:1.Security situation may deteriorate and prevent the implementation of activities.Mitigating measure: GVC ensures continuous coordination with local partners and international agencies (UNDSS) to monitor the situation. In addition,GVC adopted a security plan and SOP in case of major accidents; 2.Shrinking of the humanitarian and operational space in some areas due to military operations. Mitigating measure: policy environment is monitored through the attendance to Cluster, Working Groups and coordination mechanisms 3.Major demographic changes in the areas of intervention requires modiﬁcation in the strategy. Mitigating measure: GVC monitors population movements and liaise with other agencies and with the Sector 4.Major change in attitude of authorities and local partners toward planned intervention. Mitigating measure: GVC ensures good relationship with authorities in the area, informing them of planned activities. It ensures a transparent and accountable way of working 5.Diversion of aid from staff and volunteers of the local partner. Mitigating measures: GVC ensures direct monitoring and evaluation of the activities by ah hoc tools (including complaint mechanisms) elaborated for each activity and utilized by field staff during daily-based monitoring visits.
