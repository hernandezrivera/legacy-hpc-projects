Project Summary

In 2016 common law lawyers in the English-speaking regions of Cameroon now the North West and South West Regions decided to hold a peaceful protest to denounce the transfer of civil or French-speaking magistrates to adjudicate on common law matters in the English-speaking regions. The government responded by sending security forces to beat up the lawyers and as a result provoked anger amongst English-speaking Cameroonians. Shortly after that, the English speaking teachers joined the Lawyers on grounds that French teachers were sent to teach in English schools without having any idea of the English educational system. Within a short span of time all other sectors in the English-speaking regions Cameroon had joined the strike under the umbrella of The Cameroon Anglophone Civil Society Consortium which now demanded for a return to the two-state federal system as agreed in 1961. The government responded by shutting down the internet in English-speaking Cameroon, arresting some key leaders while others went in to exile. This further led to radicalism amongst English-speaking Cameroonians who became more defiant and decided to protest on the 22nd of September 2017. The government again responded with arbitrary arrests, detentions and extra judicial killings in of the peaceful protesters. As usual the radicalism increased and on the 1st of October 2017, some Southern Cameroonians decided to symbolically declare independence. The government further increased her clamp down on the political activist, with arbitrary arrests, detention and extrajudicial killings. As a result, different separatists’ groups in the English-speaking regions decided to take up arms. In response, the president of Cameroon Paul Biya, decided to declare war against those who have taken up arms against the state.
Since 2016, the conflict has led to the dead of more than 4000 people mostly civilians including women, children and elderly. Over 65000 refugees in Nigeria, and about 1.3 million internally displaced, the burning of over 1500 plus villages have been totally or partially destroyed. The destruction of properties has let to many Housing, land and properties and legal identity problems faced by IDPs and returnees especially women. Most the HLP problems in these affected regions have been; land grabbing, forced eviction, destruction of houses and properties, inherritance issues, double sale of properties and encroachment.
HLP problems has further affected many women who are internally displaced, some who are returning to their former dueling and non-indigenes. Some of these vulnerabilities are; Access to property documentation (system) creates insecurity of tenure, Discriminatory practices against women and girls, Tribalism, Limited access to justice, Traditional leadership system and Inadequacy of the legal framework.
Additional problems mostly faced by women and girls has be lack of civil and legal documentation such as birth certificates, identity card, marriage certificates, death certificates which has hinder them from securing jobs, livelihood supports and education to enable them claim and excise their HLP rights. In the South West and North West region, Human Is Right proposes an action that will support communities affected by displacement through protection assistance. This action will also include coordinating with local and international organizations for better program implementation and strengthen advocacy on women’s HLP rights.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Human Is Right conducted a need assessment in the proposed project South West locations in coordination with other local civil society organization in December 2021. Specific locations for the need assessment were chosen based on the IOM data on displacement, information shared at cluster level, Human Is Right field visits, the current context and actor’s presence mapping. The process began with a session with local and international organization for a common understanding on the way forward including selected locations, resources mobilization, and timeframe and data collection instrument that focus on addressing needs in a holistic manner. A total of 1446 household questionnaires were administered and key informant information was obtained from 46 individuals in target locations. In addition to the current, this proposal also draw inference from some assessments and evaluations conducted by other partners.In the North West and South West regions, social and political claims in 2016 met with violence from security forces, escalating in 2017 in a crisis that has paralysed the two regions for several years. Since 2018 clashes between non state armed groups and the security forces have caused major displacements in the two regions and to West and Littoral regions and subsequent humanitarian needs. Over 15,780 individuals from 2,625 households were reportedly displaced in the NWSW regions in June 2020 due to ongoing violence and this number continues to increase. 84% of IDPs especially women in the target regions have difficulties finding decent and affordable housing, and are likely to suffer forced eviction, as they cannot meet up with rent and secure tenure. In the North west, South West, West, and Littoral regions, some 781,000 people affected by protracted population movements have shelter and NFI needs. 93 percent of returnees find their homes damaged or destroyed. 45% of displaced population are with host families, while another 20% are renting. In addition, the vast majority of the IDPs do not have formal tenancy agreements with house owners largely due to a lack of awareness or an unwillingness of the landowners to do so. If evicted, these IDPs are forced to move again and settle in the bush or other rural areas. The living conditions are deplorable in both the urban centres and the bushes, especially during the rainy seasons. The displaced persons in the bushes don’t have shelter to protect them from the harsh weather while those in the Urban centers are crowded in small houses.
