Project Summary

The project aims at providing tailored and timely multipurpose cash assistance to assist shock-affected and socioeconomically vulnerable households with limited financial capacity to cover survival and/or basic needs in the Gaza Strip with a special focus on southern governorates for the second result. 800 households (4,400 individuals; 2,230 males, 2,170 females, 2,097 children, 2,127 adults, 175 elderly persons, 188 PwDs), affected by natural or manmade shocks, will receive one-off emergency MPCA (754 ILS/SMEB-based transfer value endorsed by CWG in August 2022) to cover most urgent survival needs. Moreover, 400 socioeconomically vulnerable households (2,200 individuals; 1,115 males, 1,085 females, 1,048 children, 1,064 adults, 88 elderly persons, 94 PwDs), affected by the long-standing blockade, deteriorated economic situation, high unemployment levels, extreme poverty, and the associated protracted stressors, will receive a regular MPCA (1,081 ILS/MEB-based transfer value endorsed by CWG in Aug 2022) for three monthly rounds. The action will enable the targeted vulnerable households to meet their basic needs without a further adoption of negative coping mechanisms. In all its beneficiary selection processes, PUI will give a strong priority to HHs with Persons with Disabilities (PWD), Female-headed households (FHH), child-headed households (CHH), large households, HHs with high dependency ratio, HHs with chronically ill persons, HHs living in substandard housing units, and HHs with long-stay patients in Gaza hospitals.
It is worth mentioning that the planned intervention was built on the priority needs identified through the different assessments carried out with the target groups as well as the multi-sectorial needs assessment conducted by UN OCHA in July 2022. Furthermore, the action was fully coordinated with Gaza cash working group (GCWG) and the ministry of social development (MoSD). Both GCWG and MoSD confirmed the priority needs and the relevance of the proposed intervention. In addition, extensive consultations were held with other relevant stakeholders (including active CWG members) to discuss the different aspects of the proposed action, prioritize the most affected groups, and avoid the overlapping of intervention areas.
Prior to any cash response, PUI and its partner will conduct a rapid market assessment in the project’ intervention areas to ensure the functionality of the markets, check the availability of goods/commodities, and anticipate any changes to the prices. Also, PUI will liaise with OCHA, cluster coordinators, humanitarian actors, and relevant technical ministries to refer households with specific unmet needs for complementary sectorial humanitarian and/or development interventions.
The intervention will contributes to the achievement of the 2nd overarching strategic objective as well as the MPCA Chapter main objective.
PUI will implement the proposed action in partnership with its strategic partner UAWC. The disaggregation of roles and responsibilities will be done based on their respective fields of expertise as well as experience in the intervention areas. UAWC’s support will be instrumental in all stages of the project, notably for beneficiary selection, field monitoring, awareness-raising on activities, MEAL and PSEAH, and follow up on emerging needs. PUI will prioritize support and capacity building to its partner throughout the action, notably in MEAL and CVA.

1 - Needs

• Shock-affected households.According to OCHA, the escalation of hostilities in August 2022 resulted in the death of 49 Palestinians, including 17 children and four women, 360 injured, including at least 151 children and 58 women. The immediate needs and funding requirements reported published by OCHA after the escalation indicated that 2,865 vulnerable families, affected by the escalation, were in need of multipurpose cash assistance for 1-3 months to cover their basic needs and restore coping capacity. One year before, in May 2021, the Gaza Strip endured even much more intensive Israeli military attacks that resulted in the deaths of 260 Palestinians, including 66 children and 41 women. At the peak of the offensive, 113,000 persons had to seek shelter outside of their homes. According to a report issued by UN in the gendered needs of HHs affected by May 2021 escalation, 86% stated that they preferred cash assistance (versus 41% for vouchers and 25.6% for in-kind assistance). During the first three quarters of 2022, 876 protection incidents were recorded against farmers and fishers in the access-restricted area (ARA) including land levelling, destruction of agricultural assets, confiscation of boats/fishing equipment, and destruction of boats/fishing nets. PUI provided emergency MPCA to 124 farmers and fishers, whose livelihoods are affected by these IHL/IHRL violations, during the period between January and October 2022. The PDM survey demonstrated that households were able to cover their basic needs, alleviate their economic burdens and provide better for their families through the provided assistance. All the surveyed HHs expressed their satisfaction with the assistance as it was tailored to their needs, timely and enabled them to recover from the incident and cover their immediate needs until having the capacity to restore their affected livelihoods. They confirmed that the cash transfer assistance was their preferred assistance modality, rather than in-kind, giving them flexibility and freedom to manage their expenditures and prioritize their needs.• Socioeconomically vulnerable households.Based on the multi-sectorial needs assessment (MSNA 2022), here below are findings documenting the socioeconomic vulnerability of Gazan households:- Primary source of income: 50.5% relying on NGO or charity assistance, 36.7% daily-wage labor, and 28.1% employment; female-headed households, especially older FHH, reported being more financially reliant on support from their family, friends, or community as their primary source of income at higher rates than other population groups.- 57.3% of the households reported a decrease in their monthly income compared to the previous year;- 83.4% reporting having existing outstanding debt (47.4% have debts exceeding 5,000 ILS, while 32.3% have debts exceeding 10,000 ILS);- Heavy reliance on negative coping mechanisms, particularly those related to practices of taking on credit or borrowing: 84.6% of the aid-recipient households reported having taken on recent debts in 3 months prior to the data collection;- The largest portion of household expenditure was dedicated to food (55.7%);- 81% of households reported financial difficulties in meeting any of their household’s basic needs (70% for food needs, 65% for health needs, 54.8% for utilities, etc.);- 68% of the households employed crisis or emergency livelihood coping, an alarming figure indicative of the difficulties faced by households in meeting their most basic needs;- 91.8% of households reported a preference for cash and voucher assistance in any modality.According to FAO’s survey on the impact of stressors on food system (Sep, 2021), food insecurity levels remained high in the Gaza Strip with seven out of ten respondents (70%) continuing to experience poor or border line food consumption levels throughout the month. The poverty rate in Gaza was estimated at 53% in 2017 (PCBS), and is expected to have increased to 56.4% as a result of the May hostilities according to World Bank (HNO, 2022). In addition, the households living under deep poverty line in Gaza Strip was estimated by PCBS at 33.8%. 60% of female-headed households reported NGO or charity assistance as their primary income source (compared to 50% of male-headed households) and 39% reported community support as a primary income source (compared to 17% of male-headed households). This indicates the higher vulnerability of female-headed households. Compared to 13% of male-headed households, 22% of female-headed households reported having spent more than 75% of their household expenditure on basic needs. According to MSNA2022 Crosscutting report, female-headed households were found more likely than male-headed households to be headed by an older person, to include a person with disability, and to include a household member with a chronic illness.In October 2022, 2 focus group discussions (FGDs) were held by PUI and its partner with 12 socioeconomically vulnerable households (7 female-headed, 5 male-headed) and 14 shock-affected households (11 female-headed, 3 male-headed) from Khan Younis governorate. Challenges identified included lack of job opportunities, lack of income to cover basic household needs, debts accumulation for basic needs, and lack of access to health services, especially for PwDs. The high reported rates of debts taken on to secure household food and other basic daily needs not only limit households’ resilience to future shocks and ability to save, but also create a risk of imprisonment for debtors defaulting on their debts.Priority interventions included provision of cash assistance through either cash or voucher, creation of job opportunities, income generating activities (IGAs), and housing repairs/upgrades. Cash and voucher were the ultimately preferred assistance modalities by all the interviewed households.

2 - Activities

PUI and UAWC will conduct a rapid market assessment in the intervention areas prior to and throughout the cash response to ensure the functionality of the markets, check the availability of goods/commodities, and anticipate any changes to the prices which could be provoked by the planned intervention. In addition, PUI will liaise with OCHA, clusters and relevant ministries to refer HHs with specific unmet needs for complementary sectorial humanitarian and/or development interventions. PUI will also strive to ensure linkages with existing national social protection systems to lay the foundations for future longer-term assistance. • Result 1: The critical survival needs of the shock-affected households are met in a dignified and safe manner through providing one-off emergency MPCA.• Activity 1.1: Coordination with relevant ministries, data verification, and selection of beneficiaries.Based on the nature of the emergency and the affected group, PUI will initiate coordination with the relevant line ministries, e.g. MoSD, Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MoPWH), in order to obtain the initial list of affected HHs. PUI/UAWC will then carry out a verification exercise for the whole caseload through phone calls or home visits, pending security situation and permissions granted by local authorities. In parallel, PUI will share the list with the MoSD and Ministry of Labor (MoL) to get information on the socioeconomic vulnerability of the affected cases. Besides, PUI will coordinate with Gaza CWG and other humanitarian actors to avoid any potential overlapping or duplication. Based on the eligibility criteria, vulnerability scoring matrix, as well as the feedbacks received from different actors, PUI will issue the final lists.+ Main selection criteria: Shock-affected HHs, including HHs affected by escalations and the recurrent IHL/IHRL violations in the ARA.+ Vulnerability prioritization parameters: PUI will give a high priority to the HHs with PWDs, FHH, CHH, large households, HHs with high dependency ratio, HHs with chronically-ill persons, and HHs with long-stay hospital patients.• Activity 1.2: Provision of one-off emergency MPCA to the selected HHs.PUI and UAWC will target the 800 HHs (4,400 ind; 2,230 males, 2,170 females, 2,097 children, 2,127 adults, 175 elderly persons, 188 PwDs), affected by natural and man-made emergencies, including but not limited to floods, harsh weather conditions, escalations of violence, and IHL/IHRL violations in the ARA, from all governorates. Each of the selected HHs will receive a one-off emergency MPCA amounting to 754 ILS (SMEB-based transfer value endorsed by CWG in August 2022) to cover critical survival needs. The cash will be released within 3 days of the list’s validation. The payment modalities will depend on the nature of the emergency event, affected group, size of the caseload and functionality of the financial service providers: - Bank cheques in case of a few cases affected by individual incidents.- Cash over counter through Bank of Palestine (BoP) or PALPAY (cashing out from 350 accredited agents using PIN code) in case of large caseloads (i.e. above 100 HHs). • Result 2: The basic needs of the socioeconomically vulnerable HHs, impacted by the protracted crisis, are properly secured without a further adoption of negative coping mechanisms through providing a regular MPCA.• Activity 2.1: Coordination with relevant line ministries, data verification, and selection of beneficiaries.PUI will organize a meeting with the MoSD at the beginning of the project to provide the relevant information about the activity, eligibility criteria, nomination mechanism, and obtain the lists vulnerable HHs. PUI will also consider vulnerable HHs referred through the official referral pathways.PUI/UAWC will then carry out home visits to the nominated HHs in order verify the information, using the unified vulnerability assessment tool (UVAT) developed by GCWG. In parallel, PUI will share the list with the MoL to get detailed information on the sources of income and the socio-economic vulnerability of the assessed HHs. Moreover, PUI will coordinate with GCWG and other humanitarian actors to avoid overlapping or duplication. Based on the pre-defined eligibility criteria, vulnerability scoring matrix, as well as the feedbacks received from different actors, PUI will issue the final lists of BNFs.+ Main selection criteria: Acutely vulnerable HHs with no regular source of income, and no other assistance from other organizations and/or government-led social protection schemes. However, HHs with regular source of income below the deep poverty line could be considered as a second priority target group.+ Vulnerability prioritization parameters: HHs with PWDs, FHHs, CHHs, large households, HHs with high dependency ratio, HHs with chronically-ill persons, HHs living in substandard HUs, and HHs with long-stay patients.• Activity 2.2: Provision of regular MPCA to the selected HHs in 3 rounds.PUI/UAWC will target 400 socioeconomically vulnerable HHs (2,200 ind; 1,115 males, 1,085 females, 1,048 children, 1,064 adults, 88 elderly persons, 94 PwDs), in Khan Younis and Rafah with a specific focus on FHH, HHs with PwDs, and HHs of long-stay patients. Each will receive a regular emergency MPCA amounting to 1,081 ILS (MEB-based CWG-endorsed transfer value) for 3 monthly rounds. The payments will be disbursed to the HHs within 3 days of the final list’s validation, through PAYPAL, or if not available/less efficient, through BoP (cash over counter). PUI might consider providing additional top-ups/rounds for hardship cases with specific vulnerabilities. For both 1st and 2nd line MPCA, PUI will conduct orientation meetings with beneficiaries to provide detailed information on the project’s activities, timeline, selection criteria, feedback and complaints mechanism as well as PSEAH policies and complaint mechanism.

5.2 - Please briefly explain why the specific modality/ies for this response were chosen.

Cash modality is recommended by the affected population and the humanitarian actors as long as the markets in the affected areas are functioning, the financial service providers are operating with an adequate capacity, and goods are available. According to MSNA (July, 2022), 91.8% of households reported a preference for cash and voucher assistance in any modality (physical cash, mobile money, bank transfer, prepaid card, food and non-food vouchers). In Gaza Strip, goods are available in the local market, but the affected population has lost the means to buy them. This is why cash transfers can be securely provided to the acutely vulnerable households who can choose how to use the money to cover their priority needs in a dignified manner. Evidences showed that cash transfers, in GS and other contexts, are often more efficient and effective than other forms of aid. The cash transfers modality provides affected people with the means and flexibility to decide and priorities their life-saving and recovery needs. Moreover, it also supports the local markets, contribute to boosting local economy, and lays the foundations for communities’ recovery and resilience. Cash transfers are effective in addressing negative coping strategies such as poor diets and debts. Given its multi-purpose nature, cash is central to addressing people’s needs in a coordinated and demand-driven way. Cash can also be linked to the existing national social protection systems and/or longer-term recovery and development interventions to ensure sustainable impacts even after the end of the intervention.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

PUI’s feedback and response mechanism (FRM) is systematically capturing the views and feedback from affected people to ensure they inform decision-making, and close the “feedback loop”. For example, the idea of increasing the number of installments provided to the vulnerable households was a result of the collected feedback and recommendations raised by former beneficiaries in similar PUI interventions. In the same way, the choice of distributing cash rather than in-kind derives from beneficiary feedback, as it provides them the flexibility in the management of their expenditures. MEAL team will strive to ensure that feedback, whether positive or negative, will collected and followed up on by PUI staff independent from project team and be dealt with respect for the principles of confidentiality and privacy, without affecting the possibility to receive assistance in the future. All relevant feedback and recommendations will be shared with the project manager and relevant staff for improvement. Feedback received through the FRM will be documented and shared in writing, via email, between the MEAL Officer and the program team (in a secured and confidential manner). Decisions and steps taken will be duly reported as well, until the closure of the case. More informal feedback received by any PUI staff in the field will be dealt with depending on its nature: either through oral response to complainants, or through the opening of dedicated case file, as described above. Monthly internal reporting will include analysis of the received feedbacks, as well as during the yearly strategy exercises. Project modalities or objectives will be discussed with the program and technical team, in order to better respond to the needs and concerns of the served population.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

PUI uses various two-way communication channels: oral, written, easy to read, face to face, via email and through meetings; to ensure that feedback and complaints mechanism is easily accessible by all beneficiaries including people with specific needs such as PwDs and children. These communication channels include (but are not limited to) feedback boxes, hotline number, email, and the pages of the organization on social media. PUI designed a kids-friendly and interactive hand-out card including the hotline number, drawings, and painting exercises to increase their awareness regarding their rights, and to encourage them in complaining in case they faced a misconduct/misbehavior from humanitarian workers or the community. Additional publications were designed to the same purpose i.e. posters and leaflets.During all the beneficiary selection processes, orientation meetings with the beneficiaries, occasional door-to-door visits and field visits, PUI’s team systematically provides detailed information on the feedback and complaints mechanism, sharing the details of the contact person FRM leaflets are handed out and the process is also explained orally, to account for those who cannot read. Also, PUI tries to disseminate the information of the FRM through local partner CBOs and key community leaders, handing out cards, posters, and other means of information dissemination regarding PSEAH and the FRM mechanism, ensuring cultural appropriateness and inclusivity. Meanwhile, feedbacks may also be provided verbally in the field, through text messages, email or phone (using the dedicated address and phone number provided on information cards). The MEAL team will be providing an initial feedback in less than 24 hrs.Moreover, diversity among PUI staff in terms of gender and capacities will allow an inclusive and gender- sensitive approach, where beneficiaries complaints will be handled as much as possible (and systematically for female complainants) by same-gender staff.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PUI has developed a country level Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, which is updated yearly, to reflect on the type of actions required on each stage of the project cycle to ensure protection and gender principles are adequately mainstreamed. Based on the Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Action Plan, a project specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming action plan will be developed at the beginning of the Action. Some of the key intervention areas will be: • A protection and gender risk and mitigation measures analysis for the project, with the solicitation of direct feedback from the target community, regarding the perceived protection risks, needs and capacity. The analysis will have special focus on the risks of gender-based violence (GBV) and challenges for people with disability. According to the initial Protection and Gender risk analysis, project specific and nuanced mitigation measures will be adopted to respond to the identified risks and gaps. In this case, considering the nature of the activities and the particular risks linked to distribution, specific attention will be brought to analyzing risks for targeted populations, taking into account diverse groups and their vulnerabilities. Chosen distribution modalities already seek to reduce these risks, but additional mitigation measures may be taken based on the result of the protection risks analysis. Those modalities are informed by PUI’s previous experience of several years on similar CVA projects, and by feedback received from beneficiaries through PDMs on their preferences and needs.• Community consultation, through different means (FGDs, Key informants Interviews, surveys, etc.) will be conducted throughout the project implementation to monitor the progress of the mitigation measures, as well as to detect any new or emerging protection concerns. All tools, SOPs and materials used during each of the project steps will include ProM considerations. Protection and Gender Mainstreaming indicator(s) will be measured and monitored regularly through different data collection sources (observations, consultations, FRM, etc.). • To ensure PSEAH, PUI has and is currently further strengthening its SEAH reporting mechanism. The reporting mechanism is accessible through different channels for beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders. PSEAH training has been provided in 2021 or 2022 to all staff members. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH will also be disseminated to project beneficiaries. • Project beneficiaries with or without specific vulnerabilities in need of more specialized protection or psychosocial support services will be provided with safe and timely referrals to other dedicated service providers. In order to ensure safe protection referrals, a protection and PSS service mapping for the area of operation will be accessed. Furthermore, staff members have been trained in 2022 on a “Safe Protection Referrals” training module, to include “Communicating and responding to GBV disclosures”. • A protection capacity building plan will be drafted action onset, to reflect on the needs identified during the initial Protection and Gender Risks Analysis, as well as subsequent consultations with staff and beneficiaries. Some on the topics to be included in the capacity building plan will be: Protection, age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, GBV, child protection, psychological first aid, safe referrals, PSEAH and child safeguarding, and inclusion of people with disabilities. • To ensure all protection and gender mainstreaming activities are in the forefront of the project, selected field staff members, both from PUI and from its implementing partners, will be trained and mentored to act as protection mainstreaming focal points. They will be tasked with specific protection and gender mainstreaming activities, while also raising the awareness of other staff members and beneficiaries on protection related issues.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Through the use of interviews, focus groups and surveys, PUI aims to involve vulnerable groups and people with specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle and promote the general concept of age and gender diversity (AGD) in selecting the beneficiaries (BNF). PUI will also ensure assessment methodology complies with standards and includes protection considerations; put additional effort to include representative from all different groups within the targeted community; ensure the assessment tools are accessible for different groups of people (for instance by including the Washington Group Short Set of Questions on disabilities in its questionnaires to gather information on barriers, needs and priorities); conduct vulnerability mapping and socioeconomic survey to all the potential BNFs, and besides promotes the Disability Mainstreaming through the project’s cycle. Groups or individuals found to face more barriers in access to assistance (because of their gender, disabilities, etc.), will be prioritized in beneficiary selection, as presented above in the activities section (FHH, PwD, older persons, etc.). Specific arrangements may be made to ensure thy have meaningful access, such as home visits to inform and accompany them, interaction with same-gender staff, specific provisions taken for distribution, etc. In the same way, individuals identified as most at risk of SEAH would also benefit from adapted measures to mitigate protection risks.Moreover, PUI has a specific budget and human resources dedicated to protection mainstreaming, and thus is able to ensure support for different people and to offer them equal access to our services. Accessibility is not only physical but psychological as well, thus, all PUI staff received several trainings on different disciplines (such as Protection Mainstreaming, PSEAH, conducting inter-sectorial referral, child safeguarding and child protection) in order to ensure that they can promote and defend inclusion in our day-to-day implementation. Regarding PSEAH specifically, PUI has strengthened its SEAH reporting mechanism with the opening of our whistleblowing mechanism to others and different channels for beneficiaries, staff, and other stakeholders. Accessible and clear awareness raising and key messaging on PSEAH to project beneficiaries come to complete the scheme. Besides, PUI staff members and its partners’ will also be further trained on age, gender and diversity (AGD) mainstreaming, psychological first aid, safe referrals, GBV and inclusion of people with disabilities.
