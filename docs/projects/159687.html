Provision of life-saving nutrition interventions to the most vulnerable women of reproductive age and children under 5 years in the occupied Palestinian territory

Project Summary

The objective is to respond to the cluster objective to ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving nutrition interventions that will address the needs of the most vulnerable target group identified by the Cluster. Target groups include most vulnerable pregnant women, women of reproductive age and children under 5 years (U5) suffering from malnutrition.
The project will address critical gaps in the health sector response for maternal and child nutrition services in the West Bank (Hebron H2 and Area C) and the Gaza Strip. It will address life-threatening micronutrient malnutrition among the most vulnerable women of reproductive age and U5 children including moderate and severe wasting through provision of appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counselling, food and nonfood items, including essential micronutrients and treatment jointly delivered by UNICEF, WFP and Save the Children.
UNICEF will procure and deliver the annual requirement of essential micronutrients such as Iron and folic acid (Iron 65mg + Folic Acid 0.4 Tablets) for at least 36,000 pregnant and lactating women (PLW) (Gaza: 24,000; WB:12,000) and Iron syrups for 40,000 children aged 6 to 59 months (girls: 19,600; boys: 20,400) in Gaza and the West Bank (Gaza: 30,000; WB: 10,000) who are attending the Ministry of Health (MOH) and NGO facilities.
WFP will provide U$ 40 Unconditional Cash Transfers (e-voucher) on a monthly basis for most vulnerable PLW and mothers of children (2-5 years) with high anemia and micronutrient deficiencies, to purchase healthy food and WASH kits, to meet their increased nutritional needs. To synergize efforts and have a comprehensive Maternal Infant Young Child -Emergency project, WFP will target 900 PLW (600 in Gaza and 300 in the West Bank) and mothers of children (2-5 years old) for e-vouchers, who will receive supplements from UNICEF, in addition to 100 men and 100 children. The provision of the Unconditional Cash Transfers will be complemented with Behavior Change activities to increase the dietary impact of the cash.
WFP will target 600 women, 50 children (girls: 25) and 50 men in the Gaza Strip; as well as 300 women, 50 children (girls: 25) and 50 men in Area C and H2 in Southern Governorates of the West Bank.
In the Gaza Strip, Save the Children (SCI) will focus on building the capacity of 80 selected health workers (female: 60) in providing quality maternal and infant and young child nutrition services, including maternal nutrition, IYCF counselling and detection and management of moderate and severe malnutrition. The support will help detect and treat cases with moderate and severe wasting (2,000 U5 children) (girls: 1,000) and provide basic equipment and supplies to ensure and support quality nutrition services (5 nutrition clinics) in access restricted areas of Gaza (ARAs). The SCI component will provide counselling and support services for pregnant women and mothers of children under age 2 (Gaza: 2,200 PLW). Special attention will be given to infants who are not breastfed (<6 months), a group that is at particularly high risk of mortality and morbidity (Gaza: 200 infants under 6 months) (girls: 100) with the provision of individual counselling, home visits, appropriate infant formula hygiene and breastmilk substitute (BMS) kits in line with the IYCF in emergencies (IYCF-E) operational guidance 2017.
Total beneficiaries (including U5 children, PLW, health workers and men) will be 81,600 (UNICEF: 76,000; WFP: 1,100; SCI: 4,500). It is estimated that 39,100 high-risk women (UNICEF: 36,000; WFP: 900; SCI: 2,200); 42,300 U5 children (UNICEF: 40,000 (girls: 19,600; boys: 20,400); WFP: 100 (girls: 50); SCI: 2,200 U5 children (girls: 1100)); 80 health workers (60 females); 20 protection case workers (10 males); and 100 men (under WFP Behavior Change activities) will benefit from urgently needed lifesaving nutrition interventions.

1 - Needs

In humanitarian situations, people are at higher risk of undernutrition and micronutrient deficiencies. Pregnant and lactating women and young children are most vulnerable to undernutrition. They have a greater need of nutrients and are more susceptible to the harmful consequences of micronutrient deficiencies. Young children in emergencies also face higher risks of diarrhea and pneumonia and thus are at higher risk of undernutrition. Malnutrition is an acute issue in Palestine. The Micronutrient survey (2013) revealed 30.7% of children 6 to 59 months were anemic, and the 2020 HNO puts this figure at 40%. Children from 6 to 23 months with anemia are at high risk of poorer cognitive, motor, social-emotional, and neurophysiologic development in the short- and long-term. Most of these, if not treated, are irreversible. According to Ministry of Health (MoH) and UNRWA, anemia prevalence in Gaza in women and children is at the level of public health concern. Based on a data review (2006-2018), anemia prevalence amongst PLW has been consistently at the border line and above public health significance (40%). In the West Bank, anemia prevalence among pregnant women was 27.5% in 2018. Anemia during pregnancy leads to significant negative health outcomes both for mothers and children. It is widely acknowledged that exclusive breastfeeding protects infants from iron deficiency anemia for the first months of life. The MICS 2014, however, revealed low levels of exclusive breastfeeding (36.5%) in oPt and the prevalence of early initiation dropped from 66% to 41%. A recently concluded nutrition multi-sectoral assessment in Gaza shows, that in the most vulnerable communities, the rate of wasting is estimated at around 4%. The recent data from the MoH presents a sharp increase in maternal mortality ratio in Gaza from 8.6 (2017) to 16.7 (2018) deaths per 100,000 live births. In the analysis of maternal deaths in 2019, experts concluded that 77% of women who died in 2018 were anemic. Of them, 70% had severe anemia. A recently conducted assessment outlines a deterioration in the nutritional status of the most vulnerable children under 5, pregnant and lactating women in Gaza. There were 18% of pregnant women and 14% of lactating mothers in access-restricted areas (ARA) who were undernourished. Above 6% of infants were never breastfed and 86% of children under 5 living in ARA did not consume a minimum acceptable diet. 210,000 children in Gaza under 5 are particularly vulnerable due to micronutrient deficiencies, that is 7 out of 10 children. There is a pocket of 36,400 children under 5 living in ARA who are particularly vulnerable and prone to incidences of watery or bloody diarrheal disease and respiratory infections, exacerbated by the deteriorating access to safe drinking water and health services. The combined effect of the poor maternal nutrition status, sub-optimal infant and young child feeding practices, child morbidity and inadequate water and sanitation may lead to an increased risk of acute malnutrition, an impaired physical growth and cognitive development. The findings from the OCHA H2 multi-sectoral household assessment revealed high food insecurity. Only 6% of households indicated that the family had enough food. This could be considered as anecdotal evidence that malnutrition, anemia and food insecurity became evident given the economic degradation and restricted access to goods, services and employment. Moreover, the access to quality maternal, newborn and child health and nutrition is further compromised by the inconsistent supply of essential medical items including for treatment and prevention of anemia and other micronutrient deficiencies. The Central Drug Store of the MoH in Gaza reported that 69% of essential maternal and child health drugs are at zero stock (August 2019), showing no improvement from June 2019. Lack of micronutrients significantly jeopardized access to maternal and child health services.

2 - Activities

UNICEF, WFP and SCI will partner with Health and Nutrition Cluster members to address identified needs and provide lifesaving maternal and child nutrition interventions by implementing a comprehensive package of nutrition in emergencies interventions in the 5 Gaza governorates and H2 area of Hebron city and Area C. The procurement of iron syrup and folic acid will be done by UNICEF in close coordination with UNFPA who has recently received a grant to procure these items. To reach the 2020 HRP objective “Ensure the availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality of essential lifesaving health services to the most vulnerable communities in Gaza including through health system strengthening, preparedness and community resilience building” the following activities defined under the Health and Nutrition Cluster will be implemented: All activities are under the STANDARD HNC Activity 1: Ensure that people have access to healthcare and nutrition that prevents excessive maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality by ensuring that clean and safe delivery, essential newborn care and emergency obstetric and newborn care services are available at all times through provision of essential supplies, training, community awareness and establishing a clear referral mechanism for obstetric and newborn emergencies by working at the community, primary healthcare and hospital level.
