Project Summary

The overall objective of the program is to Improve the Household Food Security and Livelihoods of the Small Scale Farmers, Pastoralists and other Vulnerable in North Darfur , Blue Nile and Kassala and South Kordofan States contributing to the achievement of the outcomes of the 2023 HRP by providing life-saving support livelihood support and basic needs for small scale farmers, pastoralist and other vulnerable community members to improve crop production for farmers, increased animal production and productivity of livestock owners as well as increasing livelihoods of the target communities.
The project will target 8000 (4, 160 Female; 3 840 Male) vulnerable individuals in the 4 states in line with the sector objectives (1) Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through lifesaving and life sustaining food assistance and the sector objectives (2) Support self-reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives.
Improving access of agricultural inputs such as improved seeds and agricultural tools will lead to increase crop production and productivities and generate income from selling the surplus of their products, Similarly the providing of animal treatment and training to animal owners and organizations, the animal product and production will increase which will be reflected in the livelihoods of the HHs resulting from selling eating the product of their animals.
On the same direction, opening of the animal route demarcation and opening of fire lines in pasture areas will increase fodder for animal and protect it from burning. similarly, the animal route will help livestock owners to easily access their pasture area and move from place to place safety and the farms of farmers will be protection form animal attack, this will create settlement and peace among both groups; farmers and pastoralist ill will contribute positively to increase in both farming and livestock rearing.
In addition to above; The project will support the vulnerable people in the conflict affected area such as Blue Nile through provision of life saving activities, who suffer as well from floods during this year.
The project will be implemented in close coordinator and collaboration with the relevant stakeholders including the relevant government ministries and departments; community organizations and its network.
Gender and environment will be mainstreamed and integrated in the project interventions through involving women development organizations in project life cycles; women related interventions such as women farms, and building the capacities of beneficiaries on environment related activities including the social and environment impact assessment which will be implemented prior project implementation.

1 - Needs

The Humanitarian need overview which consolidated by OCHA, on behalves of the humanitarian Sudan team and partners, reported that, during 2022, in Sudan, the acute food insecurity has reached higher than usual levels due to the combined effects of economic crisis, conflict and low agriculture production. Flooding and dry spells in successive years have affected property, infrastructure, and crops, forced people to displace and resulted in livestock losses. Intensified fighting during the year disrupted livelihoods, lost assets (particularly livestock), and disrupted the provision of humanitarian aid and other essential services. The ongoing and protracted macroeconomic crisis—along with currency depreciation—has also led to rising food prices, diminished household purchasing power, and made food unaffordable for a sizable percentage of the population.Up to 11.7 million people are acutely food insecure during the lean season, an increase of 2 million from earlier estimates, according to the most recent IPC analysis (IPC Phase 3 or worse). There are 3.1 million people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and 8.6 million in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis).It is expected that in 2023 compared to 2022, the scope and severity of food insecurity would grow and deteriorate. An increase in localized conflicts, particularly in Darfur and the Kordofan states; desert locust in the east of the country; recurrent flooding in places still recovering from the floods and dry spells; a protracted macro-economic crisis; rising food costs; and a depreciating local currency are all posing threats to people's food security. The seasonality of livelihoods, grain stockpiles, pricing, weather, animal migration, and human nutrition status are other factors that will affect the situation regarding food security. According to the physical existence of NUMAD organizations the project will focus on targeting 4 states including; Kassala, Blue Nile, North Darfur and South Kordofan.Based on rapid assessment conducted by the organization and supported by WFP reports; the following are the key findings in the 4 targeted states:North Darfur: The level of food insecurity in North Darfur increased by 18 percent, from 38 percent in 2021 to 56 percent in 2022. Kassala: The level of food insecurity in Kassala remained at the same level (17 percent) in 2022 compared to 2021. However, multiple localities in Kassala have high levels of food insecurity, such as Hamshkoreeb (39 percent); Rural Kassala (19 percent); and Aroma (19 percent). 7 percent in Hamshkoreeb are severely food insecure. Blue Nile: The level of food insecurity in Blue Nile state increased by 19 percent, from 31 percent in 2021 to 50 percent in 2022. South Kordofan: The level of food insecurity in South Kordofan increased by 2 percent, from 28 percent in 2021 to 30 percent in 2022. During confit and emergency situations women and children are the most affected categories, mainly women who shoulder the livelihoods of families and always stay at home with her families when people fled from conflict and hard conditions, this why they need special consideration when the activities of this project was design such a supporting HH headed by women and families with disabled women. To meet food security requirements for different population groups, the project will provide emergency agricultural and livestock interventions, and a group of vocational training activities and rehabilitation for about 8000 people in the 4 states targeting the communities affected or living in high-risk regions that are vulnerable to natural or man-made catastrophes; Vulnerable people in the host, internally displaced, returnee, and refugee groups who experience degrees of food insecurity as a result of the limited accessibility and availability of enough, sufficient, and nourishing food; Small-scale farmers who struggle with farming due to high production costs, post-harvest losses, lack of access to fields, markets, and information.
