Project Summary

ACRA (Agency for Child Relief Aid) proposes a 12 months provision of integrated GBV response and risk mitigation intervention to support vulnerable women and girls at risk of GBV in Upper Nile state (Baliet and Maiwut Counties) and Jonglei state (Akobo County). ACRA implementation strategies will contribute to Protection Cluster Objective 1. Ensure mobile or static provision of critical protection assistance and services, including specialized response services, tailored to the specific needs, considering age, gender, and disability. presence/missions to field and deep field locations, 2. Ensuring that human rights are upheld and promoted for the persons of concern by strengthening coordination, advancing centrality of protection among humanitarian actors to reduce protection risks, and addressing coordination gaps/issues through close dialogue with partners and effective protection monitoring to inform programming, advocacy, and response and 3. Promoting nexus to development and solutions as the transition between the delivery of emergency humanitarian assistance, systems building and the provision of long-term development assistance to meet peoples’ needs, mitigate risks and vulnerabilities, exacerbated by systemic and socially reinforced exclusion of women and girls and persons with disabilities. The project will target 5,527 beneficiaries disaggregated as: 3047 women, 1636 girls, 443 men and 401 boys reached. To achieve this, ACRA will conduct 56 GBV awareness sessions on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures, available response services and timely reporting through different methods such as house-to-house visit and campaigns. The project will ensure distribution of 1180 dignity kits to vulnerable 356 women and 824 girls as part of GBV risk mitigation. ACRA will establish and strengthen referral systems in the 3 targeted counties as well reaching 535 GBV survivors (54 boys, 160 girls, 58 men and 263 Women) with GBV response specialized services (GBV case management, referrals, Psychosocial support, legal aid, and WGFS programming). ACRA will build capacity of frontline service providers on GBV minimum standards as well as train 29 (15F, 14M) non GBV humanitarian actors on integration of GBV in their sectoral responses. To ensure empowerment and recovery, the project will provide individual and group Psychosocial Support services including reactional activities to 3200 individuals ( 289 boys, 980 girls, 278 men and 1653 women). ACRA will work with other GBV actors to strengthen GBV referral pathways and updating GBV standard operating procedures (SOPs) by conducting GBV service mapping in Baliet, Maiwut and Akobo. ACRA will implement primary GBV prevention interventions reaching 240 individuals (58 boys, 28 girls, 107 men and 47 women). The project will Provide socio-economic support to vulnerable women and girls including trainings on income generating activities like training on molding and use of Fuel Eﬃcient Stoves to 171 women and 76 girls. 36 girls and 89 women will be supported through cash and Voucher assistance. 11 community-based protection monitoring committees with 10 members each totaling 110 persons (65% women and girls) in each location including PWDs will be formed. ACRA has operational presence in Baliet, Akobo and Maiwut counties implementing Health, GBV, FSL and WASH. This will ensure productive relationships with key stakeholders, knowledge on the targeted locations and informs critical strategic planning. We shall also hold consultations with communities throughout the project to ensure their concerns are included in the project implementation. All activities will take a “Do No Harm and conflict sensitive approach”. IDPs, returnees, host communities will be targeted. ACRA will take account of and include persons with disabilities in all project activities, by applying an age, gender and diversity lens and considering their specific needs, while prioritizing safety, dignity and avoid harm.

1. Needs

Eleven years after independence and four years after the signing of the revitalized peace agreement, people in South Sudan continue to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. The situation is worsened by endemic violence, conflict, access constraints, disease outbreaks, floods and localized drought. Protection concerns remain high, especially for women and girls. (HNO 2023).In South Sudan, about 65 percent of women experience some form of sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV) such as child marriage and rape in their lifetime (UNDP July, 26,2022). GBV is reported to be high in many parts of the country including Baliet, Akobo and Maiwut counties which are targeted for this project. Women feel unsafe to carry-out their routine duties such as collection of firewood, access to water points, latrines and access to market places. The vulnerable women, children, older persons and persons with disabilities are more exposed to risks of exploitation with no access to justice, rule of law and many GBV cases are under reported.In January 2023 Baliet County received influx of over 9000 returnees from Jonglei state due to communal conflict that occurred between October and December 2022. (Protection cluster Upper Nile State – Flash report Feb 2023). Women and girls reported incidents of attacks by unknown persons while going to the bush to collect firewood. This impacted on their ability to provide food and shelter for themselves hence increasing their vulnerabilities. GBV cases were reported to be rampant and incidents of conflict-related sexual violence were reported too. There are no safe places for women and girls and lack of clear referral pathway has limited GBV reporting and protection assistance to survivors in the County. In Maiwut County, IRNA led by UNHR and partners on 15th/8/2023 reported an influx of over 9200 (F6624:M2576) returnees from Ethiopia and Sudan as a result of hunger in Ethiopian refugee camps and Sudan crisis respectively. The large influx of returnees is straining the existing limited clean and safe water resources, resulting in overcrowding, delays due to long queues at water points resulting into GBV related conflicts at water collection points. As a coping mechanism, women and girls collect water from distant locations where they feel unsafe which raises the risk of GBV prevalence. The County continues to report cases of GBV due to a lack of adequate shelter for returnees and compromised privacy for women and girls due to overcrowding in shared shelters. Forced and early marriages have been reported to be on the rise as returnees try to cope up with the situation hence marrying off their young girls to acquire cows that would later earn them income. REACH rapid assessment conducted on March 2023 in Akobo West reported that the internal conflict that took place from December 2022 to March 2023 caused heavy displacements worsening the already vulnerable population leaving women and girls homeless hence exposing them to high rates of gender-based violence. The hazards associated with GBV that women and children continue to face in Akobo County are heightened by floods and food insecurity. Physical access challenge caused by floods continue to limit protection assistance. Many adolescent girls cannot go to school because their parents cannot pay school fees, which increases the risk of child marriage, thereby worsening the already dire protection situation. Recent GBV assessments and coordination activities in the 3 counties also identified service gaps for case management, psychosocial support and referrals to other response services and yet the coverage of GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response services remain inadequate. Through this lifesaving project, ACRA will work with women, girls, boys, men, leaders, local actors and persons with disabilities to combat GBV and gender barriers and create safe environment for vulnerable persons among the IDPs, Returnees and Host communities.

2. Project activities

The following activities will be implemented; Activity 1.1: Conduct GBV awareness on GBV risk factors, mitigation measures, available response services and timely reporting through house-to-house visit, focused group interventions, campaigns, and mass media. This activity will ensure 56 awareness sessions conducted across the 3 targeted counties.Activity 1.2: Conduct dignity kit distribution and programming targeting vulnerable women and girls as part of GBV risk mitigation. 1180 dignity kits will be distributed to 356 women and 824 girls as part of GBV risk mitigation.Activity 1.3: Provision of case management services including coordinated care and support. Activity 1.4: Establish and strengthening GBV referral systems and linking GBV survivors to available response services and support. Activity 1.5: Implement Women and Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) programming to provide services, information and activities that promote healing, well-being and empowerment.Activity 1.6: Provision of legal services that protect the rights of GBV survivors and promote their access to justice.Activity 1.7: Provide capacity building trainings on GBV minimum standards to staff/frontline service providers. Activity 1.8: Provide individual and group Psychosocial Support services including reactional activities to ensure empowerment and recovery reaching 3200 individuals (289 boys, 980 girls, 278 men and 1653 women).Activity 1.9: Conduct GBV service mapping to strengthen GBV referral pathways and establishing / update GBV SOP. Activity 1.10: Conduct needs based capacity building trainings to 29 (15F, 14M) non-GBV humanitarian actors on integration of GBV in their sectoral responses. Activity 1.11: Rollout and implement primary GBV prevention interventions to addresses harmful practices, systemic gender inequality and power imbalances (e.g., SASA, EMAP, community cares programming) reaching 240 individuals (58 boys, 28 girls, 107 men and 47 women).Activity 1.12: Provide socio-economic support to vulnerable women and girls including trainings on income generating activities.Activity 1.13: Facilitating trainings on molding and use of Fuel Eﬃcient Stoves to 247 individuals( 171 women and 76 girls)Activity 1.14: Establish community-based protection networks to provide protection assistance and psychosocial support to women, girls and vulnerable people. 11 community-based protection committees with 10 members each totaling 110 persons (65% women and girls) in each location including PWDs will be formed.
