Project Summary

With the aim of saving lives, the project will center on providing multi-purpose cash-based support to the most vulnerable households to meet immediate food and basic needs with dignity. The multi-purpose cash-based support is combined with support to local markets, distribution of productive assets (fishing gear) and training support to producer and market groups. Combined, these activities are expected to protect productive livelihoods assets, lower the use of irreversible and damaging coping strategies, and reducing the increasing aid-dependency, increasing overall resilience to shocks.

Needs

Reduced household production of crops and livestock has led families to access food primarily from markets, humanitarian assistance by changing livelihoods strategies to fishing and by gathering wild foods. Continued insecurity has resulted in displacement of farmers in Greater Equatoria, Northern Jonglei, and Upper Nile; resulting in a large area of agricultural land left uncultivated. Some areas of Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria and Northern Jonglei have experienced continued large-scale fighting in 2018 and populations have been forced to move on several occasions often due to shifts in political control. An increasing part of Upper Nile is experiencing Emergency (IPC Phase 4) food insecurity, especially in Nassir, Manyo and Fashoda where ongoing conflict creates continuous displacement. Access to available farming land and food is continuously decreasing. Both Jonglei and Upper Nile have reported 'serious' GAM rates of 10%-14.9% for 20 counties as well as 'critical' rates of 15%-29.9% in 31 counties (FEWSNET, September 2018).In Eastern Equatoria, 38.7% of the population is in a state of Crisis, Emergency & Humanitarian Catastrophe (FEWSNET, September 2018). In the targeted areas, as found by DCA's market and needs assessments as well as post-distribution monitoring conducted on a regular basis, most households buy more than 75% of the consumed food at the market, but conflict, poor infrastructure, hyperinflation, and economic crisis have raised food prices and left many households unable to buy sufficient food. The central markets in Upper Nile, Jonglei, and Eastern Equatoria States function to some extent and have the capacity to supply the key commodities, mainly food items. Cereals, pulses, sugar, salt, fish and sometimes meat and milk are the most common commodities sold in the markets in Fashoda, Ulang, Nasir, Maiwut, and Nimule. This information has been collected by both DCA and its implementing partners on a regular basis throughout 2018. While markets in the targeted locations are functioning (DCA market and needs assessments, market surveys and PDMs), the need to support traders to ensure a continuous supply of goods at a reasonable price has been identified. High inflation, poor infrastructure, low stock and capacity to restock, and the lack of access to financial services are some of the challenges faced by traders. Strengthening local markets and market actor groups is aimed at restoring the supply chain, increasing trade options and improving the availability, quantity, and quality of food and other basic commodities at the local markets serving the target population.

Activities

6,151 of the most vulnerable households (36,910 individuals) will be targeted with multi-purpose, unconditional cash assistance to allow people to buy food and key commodities/services according to their household needs and preferences. DCA chooses a cash-based intervention. Money is easier to move, therefore responding with cash where markets are functioning is faster and more efficient. At the same time, it gives people choice and dignity.Through the provision of production inputs, such as crop and vegetable seeds and tools, at least 1,516 households (9,097 individuals) will be targeted. Cereal crop seeds and some tools will be procured locally while vegetable seeds are often not available in the target areas and where that is the case, will be procured in Juba or Sudan / Ethiopia depending on the trading routes. 2,816 households (16,893 individuals) will receive fishing support. Fishing kits will enable the beneficiaries to address immediate food security needs and reduce their vulnerability.50 fishing groups of 30 members each will receive basic training on preservation, utilization, and nutrition, as well as equipment repair, marketing and value addition. Especially, the subsequent processing of the catch will target women as they are often not able to participate in the actual fishing due to cultural restraints. 40 farmer groups of 30 members each will receive training in improved agronomic practices.The project will repair key community assets through cash for work modality where able-bodied young men and women will be selected to repair community assets which will be identified by the community through consultation and participants of cash for work will be paid per task. Assets have in the past included vital infrastructures such as bridges, roads, and dikes.Aside from the injection of cash into local markets through cash transfer programming, the project will provide critical support to 8 markets and 175 groups (petty, retail and whole sellers) so that markets recover and grow and will be more resilient to shocks in the future. Interventions will be based on ongoing assessments of markets and activities could include training and provision of equipment to store, transport and preserve food products; cash grants for transport to markets; small initiatives for income generation; improved storage facilities for traders; small cash grants for traders; provision of canoes/boats to business collectives. At the same time, support to local markets and traders will ensure availability of and access to goods in areas where market-purchased food is becoming increasingly important and food insecurity and high inflation are rampant.

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Beneficiaries are informed about the project throughout the cycle. Given traditional acceptance and low levels of literacy, information is shared with beneficiaries through community meetings. This ensures that both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries are fully informed, expectations are managed and it reduces the potential for tension within the targeted communities.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context specific. DCA targets both men and women while aiming for the inclusion of 60% women in all activities. All adult age groups are included, based on vulnerability criteria and capacity. For example, elderly beneficiaries may receive cash, while younger able-bodied beneficiaries may be involved in CfW or agricultural activities. In the same way, women's participation is planned with cultural acceptability and gender-specific protection needs in mind.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

DCA has strong safeguarding policies and complaints handling mechanisms in place and ensures that its implementing partners have their own policies and mechanisms in place as well. This means that beneficiaries can complain at multiple levels, with male and female focal persons in place. All complaints are recorded, responded to and addressed in more depth where necessary.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

The targeted communities are consulted through meetings and focus groups discussions, separated for different segments of the community, e.g. women, youth, traders, etc. In addition, household level interviews are conducted, as well as more in depth key informant interviews.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Yes, both the implementing partners and DCA have feedback and complaints mechanisms in place. Feedback and complaints can be offered at several levels, i.e. field, national, HQ. This is possible through direct communication, written statements, email and phone. These different option are in place due to limitations in literacy and communication infrastructure.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

DCA and its partners consistently consult with the communities in the targeted areas. The establishment and support of community groups and committees, who support the project throughout its life-cycle is a significant part of this effort.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through consultation with the targeted communities, including separate discussions with men and women and with different age groups, DCA and its partners analyse the specific protection needs of different population groups. Protection is mainstreamed throughout the project, including key issues such as careful selection and sensitisation of financial service providers, distances beneficiaries have to travel to markets and distribution sites, security around cash distributions, gender-specific protection needs, and monitoring of beneficiaries' protection concerns. Gender and protection are integral parts of all assessments, monitoring efforts and community consultations, which enables DCA and its partners to identify and respond to any problems and trends, including gender-based violence. In addition, the integrated complaints handling mechanism allows beneficiaries to voice concerns, which can then be addressed by DCA and partners.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As an integral part of the project planning and design phase, potential barriers are identified and mitigation measures, such as additional support to and prioritisation of certain groups, are considered. In addition, the targeted communities are sensitised regarding the importance of inclusiveness and the targeting rational. Through the establishment and close collaboration with community-led cash committees, DCA and its partners monitor access and inclusiveness on an ongoing basis. Early identification of changes in the ability of beneficiaries to access assistance is addressed and mitigated. An example of a mitigation measure is the provision of transport to physically challenged beneficiaries to allow them to access distribution sites and markets. The choice of distribution site also takes into consideration the distance beneficiaries have to travel.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Through careful consideration of protection risks and existing inequalities and in consultation with targeted populations, specifically with vulnerable and marginalised groups, DCA and its partners aim to address inequalities through inclusion and participation.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes, throughout the project cycle access to markets and services is assessed. Monitoring any changes allows for early intervention through, for example, market support.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Safety of beneficiaries is monitored throughout the project cycle. both actual incidents and perception of safety is measured and any trends are closely monitored and followed by further assessment.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

While it is difficult to measure dignity in a direct way, the project measures if beneficiaries are able to make choices and access what they have chosen, it measures increased self-reliance and is intended to provide the bridge from saving lives to leading self-reliant and dignified lives.
