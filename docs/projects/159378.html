Project Summary

American Refugee Committee's (ARC'S) proposed health project will be designed and implemented in close coordination with the MOH/D/RMOs in each region, as well as WHO, UNICEF, UNFPA and other health cluster partners maximizing effective and efficient coordination mechanism.
ARC plans to respond in Banadir and Jubaland of South- Central Somalia and Sool, Sanag and Togdeer of Somaliland, where the humanitarian need is critical due to ongoing impacts of drought, displacement and conflict. The intervention will support existing government health facilities to provide quality primary and reproductive healthcare services to 172,800 individuals as direct beneficiaries while reaching to estimated 460,000 individuals as indirect beneficiaries through meaningful community participation and engagement of all groups. Access to essential health services remains a priority for the conflict affected populations in both gender and age groups in proposed project target locations. Building on longstanding presence, vast capacity, integration and feedback mechanisms to strengthen accountability and inform adjustments in the response, including safe reporting mechanisms on the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA).
ARC health project will provide emergency and basic primary and reproductive health care services that include treatment of common illnesses, management of injuries, psycho-social counselling, reproductive health care services, maternal and child health care, health education, provision of essential medicines, management of rape/counselling and control of communicable diseases within the community. Protection will be mainstreamed into all project activities, through a comprehensive package of training of HF staff on identification and referral of severe cases of S/GBV. The intervention will reduce morbidity and mortality among the displaced vulnerable population by primarily focusing on ensuring that target facilities provide an integrated package of primary health care services with emergency referrals, disease surveillance and the inclusion of reproductive and mental healthcare services. ARC will closely coordinate with the MOH and respond in locations where significant gaps in health service delivery have been jointly identified.
ARC will invest in local health capacity, strengthening the existing physical structures and human resource, ensuring that staff incentives are in line with MOH scale paving the way for transition to locally and nationally owned and managed government facilities. Through the lifetime of the project, ARC will work with the MOH to pave the way for a proper handover, and discuss transfer of responsibility slowly or in phases to D/RMOs where and when possible.

Project Activities

Identifying, treating and providing case management for epidemic prone diseases to improve access to quality primary health services for crisis affected populations aimed at reducing morbidity and mortality due to disease outbreak driven by climatic shocks and conflict by providing emergency health care services for the inaccessible and vulnerable communities targeting number of outpatient consultations per person per year (Target > = 1 new visit/person /year). Through quality service provision, ARC will contribute to improvement in physical and mental well-being of the population affected by conflict and displacement. ARC will coordinate closely with the MOH and UNICEF to ensure all children below 5 years have been immunized to ensure a great proportion of children under 1 year received Penta3 vaccine. ARC will continue upgrading national facilities which monitor and respond to diseases outbreaks, ARC will support MOH facilities and will facilitate training on EWARN, disseminate weekly EWARN reports, and send alert for notable and outbreak-prone diseases. Proportions of communicable diseases outbreaks investigated and responded to in the first 96 hrs. (target for ARC is 80%) . ARC will procure, distribute trauma kits, emergency health kits, common disease medication, reproductive and diarrheal kits to supported facilities for optimal and continuous supply of critical lifesaving essential medicines and medical supplies-Number of equipped trauma management centers / 250,000 population (target > = 1) ARC will establish referral pathways for different conditions and ensure immediate movement of emergency and critical patient to secondary level of care. Building the capacity of the health work force ARC will ensure all the project staff have the necessary skills and will be trained on standard treatment guidelines, iMCI, CMR, PMTCT, BemNOC/CemNOC, IDSR, EPI and Emergency preparedness and response and any other training need identified during the project implementation. ARC supported facilities will provide specialized reproductive health care for pregnant and lactating women that includes skilled deliveries to increase proportion of births assisted by a skilled birth attendant. ARC supported health staff will be trained on how to better manage and referral services for survivors of sexual or gender-based violence.

Mitigation Measures

ARC has conducted an analysis of the protection risks that potentially threaten ARC’s ability to provide assistance that is impartial, does not endanger or harm beneficiaries, allows for feedback to be incorporated into the program monitoring, plan and promote community empowerment. The first protection risk facing the target population is the risk of being targeted for attacks by armed groups or explosions due to association with organizations receiving a grant from donor or perceived their enemies. All beneficiaries are vulnerable to this risk. In order to minimize this risk, ARC will operate in low profile with minimal or no visibility in such context where such risk is imminent. ARC will ensure that the aid delivered is seen as ‘Somalis helping Somalia’ by supporting teams from the local community. The second main protection risk is the risk of beneficiaries being asked to provide sex in exchange for goods and services. The most vulnerable group to this risk is women, especially girls and young women. ARC believes the risk is low but recognizes the problem of sexual exploitation and abuse exists within humanitarian spaces, and must therefore take all measures to prevent its occurrence on the part of its staff, partners, vendors and other associates. ARC has a fully adapted Policy on Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and has operationalized PSEA throughout its recruitment and procurement practices. ARC requires all partners and contractors, to sign its Sexual Harassment, Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) policy at the time of agreement, and all staff and consultants at the time of offer letter, so that the contracts are not valid without a signed code of conduct in place. The third main protection risk is exclusion, denial of assistance or services or other factors that render accessing the services dangerous or impossible. Those who are most vulnerable to this threat are older persons, persons with disabilities, children, women and unaccompanied youth. ARC will ensure that the health assistance provided across all the project supported health facilities are accessible to these vulnerable populations by targeting these groups within needs assessments, monitoring patient data to ensure that all age and gender groups are represented and persons with disabilities are being served, providing the aforementioned complaints mechanism, and assessing the Health Centers and their locations to ensure that physical and security challenges do not prevent access for any population group. The 4th risk is exclusion of service due to gender affecting particularly the women. ARC is incorporating gender mainstreaming throughout. With previous institutional experience implementing health programming, ARC is acutely aware of how women and girls face different challenges than men and boys in the current context. To this end, ARC will worked tirelessly with medical facility staff in all program locations to ensure the inclusion of female medical staff and special attention is given to reproductive and family planning response. 5th risk is lack of feedback Mechanism or inability to report concerns. ARC uses the real time beneficiary feedback system, Kuja Kuja. Kuja Kuja is a tool to allow ARC capture the ‘pulse’ of customer satisfaction with services and to allow ARC service teams, technical teams, and support functions both at the local and global levels to understand real time issues being faced, see opportunities for immediate and strategic improvement to service delivery.

Please provide further explanation

The proposed program builds on the successful model of ARC’s current health programming which supports to Health Centers and Medical team in hard to reach and hot-spot locations in proposed region. The proposed interventions will reduce morbidity and mortality among the displaced population across all age groups/gender by primarily focusing on ensuring that target facilities are well supported to provide an integrated package of primary health care services in line with EPHS through Medical teams with emergency referrals, disease surveillance and the inclusion of basic reproductive and mental healthcare services. ARC is committed to implement the proposed project hand-in-hand with the local community across all age groups (women, girls, boys and men) and in full cooperation with the MOH, the Health Clusters and sub-Clusters as well as with UNICEF, WFP, WHO and UNFPA. ARC maintains a strong reputation in building strong local support and community buy-in, recognizing that such support is critical for program success. Further building on its existing networks, ARC has developed strong networks of reliable key informants in proposed project locations and will capitalize on this to successfully implement the proposed project.
