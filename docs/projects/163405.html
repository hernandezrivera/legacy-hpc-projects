Project Summary

This project aims to strengthen water, sanitation and hygiene response to affected population in Far North region of Cameroon with a focus on IDP camp, communities hosting IDP and returnees. In water domain, construction of small water supply network powered with solar energy will be prioritized. On hygiene and sanitation issues, communities approached for total sanitation will be implemented at municipality level. Construction of community latrines and showers will be limited to IDP sites. The area targeted cover the division of Mayo Sava, Mayo Tsanaga and Logone and Chari. About 60 000 people will be targeted along these interventions. The main deliverables
• 10 000 household benefited of WASH kits
• 3 000 women and girls benefited of MHM kits
• 30 Water points constructed
• 40 water point rehabilitated in communities hosting IDP or affected by cholera
• 5 Small scale water supply networks constructed
• 3000 women and girls received menstrual hygiene management kits
• 50% of SAM children who received a WASH kits with key hygiene message in targeted area
rehabilitation of 50 water point in communities affected by cholera in North
This project will also include a component relative to capacity building of WASH actor at regional level on key issues such as design, installation and maintenance of solar system for water supply, cholera response, protection and gender mainstreaming in WASH sector.

Besoins / Needs

Situation In Far North region iof Cameroon, internally displaced persons living in the main IDP sites of Mayo Sava (Kolofata, Mémé) and Mayo Tsanaga (Zamai) do not have 15l / person / day and are more than 20 people per latrine. Investments made in 2018 did not significantly improve this situation at standard level due to the continuous increase in people in the different IDP sites and insufficient investment in this sector.A multisectoral evaluation was carried out in 2018 where needs of different groups were collected. Off-site, in general, the situation is most critical in the Mayo Sava division. The practice of open defecation is a common practice in the region regardless of the sex of the individual. It is more common among men, women, girls and boys of IDPs, off-site refugees and returnees than among host populations. The most affected divison is Mayo Sava where at least 34% of the population practices open defecation and more than 25% of IDPs. Also in Mayo Tsanaga, there is a very high rate of open defecation (at least 26%) among refugees outside the camps. The highest proportion of people who share latrines is observed in Diamaré with 47.8% of households (63% of women and 37% of men) while in Mayo Sava this proportion is 35% including 41% among women and 31% among men (OCHA, 2018). Which increases the risk of gender-based violence and undermines the privacy of female and male usersTo this situation is added the risk of spreading from these regions of the cholera epidemic declared since May 2018 to the whole of Cameroon and in the Lake Chad basin.The lack of access to basic water and sanitation infrastructure contributes to a deterioration in the health and nutritional status of the population. About 40,000 severely malnourished girls and boys will be expected in the various health facilities in the regions North and Far North. Water collection remains between 80% and 100% a distributed task, mainly carried out by women under the age of 59 and children (girls and boys under the age of 18) (MSNA 2018). This can have a negative effect on the productivity of women and the school education of children.Activities The main activities planned among wash sector member based on needs assessment and field situation includes:  The provision of safe drinking water. This will be done by • Construction and rehabilitation of boreholes• Construction of small-scale water point through new borehole or exploitation of non-functional borehole with a minimum of 2m3 /hours of water• Water trucking will only be used in sites hosting new IDPs and should be limited to a period of up to 3 months from the start of the influx. Provision of basic sanitation services. Interventions will be limited: • Construction of emergency latrines in relocation and transit sites. Attention will be paid to the dignity and safety of users: The shower blocks and toilets will be separated and differentiated between women and men thanks to pictograms. • the construction of family latrines will be done through CLTS and PHAST in the host communities hosting internally displaced persons, returnees and in sites hosting displaced persons from more than 3 months. • Improving access to basic sanitation within institution. This will be limited to schools, child-friendly spaces and health facilities in the affected areas. Promotion of good practices related to water, hygiene and sanitation. This will be done through community leaders mainly youth at community level and children (boy and girl) at school. This will include Menstrual Hygiene Management and distribution of WASH kit to girls and women below 59 years who have menstrua. Affected population will be consulted as far as concern the content of WASH kits and an end user evaluation will be done after each distribution.  Capacity building of Implementing partner.UNICEF will support the technical and logistic organization of Implementing partners training on key subject’s design,installation,maintenance
