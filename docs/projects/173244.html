Project Summary

This project responds to critical humanitarian and early recovery needs in the Darfur region, the Two Areas and Sinar State. The overall objective of the project is to restore food and livelihood security of displaced population alongside vulnerable host communities through emergency agriculture support, emergency employment, income generation, restoration of critical productive assets and enhanced conflict prevention and mediation among the most vulnerable populations thereby enhancing resilience and creating opportunities for durable solutions. It will strengthen the self-reliance of returnees and host community members alike and facilitate durable solutions for displaced populations therefore ensuring sustained peace and stability in the target areas. The project will also promote environmentally sustainable natural resource management whilst resolving natural resource conflicts through the strengthening of local collective institutions as well as community-based conflict resolution mechanisms.

1 - Needs

Poverty, economic crises, hyperinflation, localized conflicts, protracted displacement and price hikes have all combined to overstretch the capacity of Sudanese people to manage current shocks and stresses. The situation has further been exacerbated by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and recent floods which have seriously affected the food security and livelihoods of the most vulnerable. The Darfur region, the Two Areas and Sinar states being among those heavily affected. IPC analysis in June – September 2020 indicates a significant increase in the number of highly food insecure people in Sudan with around 2.2 million people facing an emergency acute food insecurity situation (IPC Phase 4) and around 7.4 million people are facing Crisis acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3). The FSL sector estimates that 9.6 million people are estimated to need assistance in order to save lives, reduce food consumption gaps, restore and protect livelihoods. IPC report shows that on average, approximately 2 million people were analyzed in each State in the Darfur region, the Two Areas and Sinar for acute food insecurity phase classification. The report shows that an appreciable percentage of the analyzed population in those States fall in IPC phases 3&4. (i.e. 38% of the population analyzed in South Kordofan are classified in Phases 3&4; 34% in Blue Nile fall in phases 3&4; 18% in Sinar State fall in phases 3&4; 38% in Central Darfur fall in phases 3&4; 32% in North Darfur fall in phases 3&4; 29% in West Darfur fall in phases 3&4; 21% in South Darfur fall in phases 3&4 and 10% in East Darfur fall in phases 3&4). Soaring inflation and price hikes have eroded the purchasing power of many families with food, transportation, health and housing being among the areas that show the highest spikes. Three of Darfur States registered the highest rate of inflation in July 2020 (namely East Darfur - 240.9%, West Darfur - 216.7% and North Darfur - 190.9%). UNDP’s Economic Analysis for September 2020 indicates that rising prices have been disproportionately taxing low-income families more heavily than wealthy ones. The COVID-19 lockdown measures significantly decreased commodity movement, market function and cross-border trade, and compromised livelihoods, affected jobs, reduced household purchasing power and food access of the vulnerable population and likely to affect the performance of the agriculture season. The economic impact of the pandemic is likely to worsen the humanitarian landscape during 2021 and may push low-income vulnerable households into negative coping mechanisms if not supported.According to OCHA and HAC, over 900,000 people were affected by the recent floods in Sudan. The destruction of homes and displacement due to floods led to further disruption of food security systems and access to markets of vulnerable population. FAO assessment in September estimates 2,216,322 ha of planted areas were flooded, with an estimated production loss of 1,044,942 tons of crop. Blue Nile and Sinar States are amongst the highest affected States whose agricultural land were affected by the floods. Coupled with shortage of fuel prices and the increased cost of production, it is expected that the food security of the most vulnerable will worsen in the next few months.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Emergency Agriculture Support to the most vulnerable - UNDP will work closely with FAO and Government Ministries in supporting the restoration of agriculture production by ensuring vulnerable households have access to high quality of locally adapted seeds and improved tools on timely basis, irrigation kits, gardening kits etc. to enable them to restore and stabilize their food production capacities. UNDP will initially work with FAO to do seed distribution using seed vouchers and seed fairs and later to initiate local seed multiplication initiatives to strengthen local seed security systems. It will also involve livestock restocking to the most vulnerable working closely with FAO to ensure the improved livestock health. Working with FAO and the Ministry of Agriculture and Natural Resources, UNDP will provide community-based extension services benefiting. 2. Support emergency employment and Income Generation – This will include the establishment and reactivation of agricultural producer associations and cooperatives; provision of agricultural machinery such as tractors, harvesters, millers etc.; reduce post-harvest losses by training on improved agronomic practices and establishing or rehabilitating community-based warehouses; maximize farm incomes through value addition, processing through introduction of low-cost appropriate technology e.g. milling machines, oil processing machines, solar driers; and market linkages; emergency employment opportunities using cash for work and cash for asset modalities in the establishment and rehabilitation of productive infrastructure as well as opportunities in agricultural fields; improve income generation support through diversified off-farm and non-farm activities. 3. Establish/rehabilitate productive assets/infrastructure - UNDP will work with relevant Government Ministries to establish and or rehabilitate critical assets through cash for work and cash for assets modalities. These will include rehabilitation of basic productive infrastructure that is time critical to restore local economy. They may include collective assets such as public markets; rural roads; lighting in public areas; shared production and storage assets; dams, irrigation canals and embankments; milling facilities; water yards, community/livelihood centres etc). 4. Enhancing Social Cohesion - UNDP shall adopt a conflict sensitive approach to crises response in the FSL sector by ensuring the interest of all groups are addressed and to prevent conflict in the exercise of their rights to food and livelihood security. Farm protection committees will be established in each community to prevent and or mediate conflicts arising between farmers and nomads or other groups. Emergency peace-building activities such as community dialogue forums shall be implemented to enhance peaceful co-existence of different livelihood groups. 5. Mainstreaming Environment and Natural Resource Management - Access to fuelwood has been both a livelihood, protection and environmental concern. Women walking to distant locations in search of firewood are exposed to sexual and gender-based violence. In addition, the dependence on firewood and the continued excoriation of vegetative cover especially in areas of return constitutes a huge threat to the environment. In order to promote the utilization of wood whilst protecting the environment, UNDP will provide solar lamps and fuel-efficient cookstoves and or solar cooking stoves with 100% reliance on radiation energy to the most vulnerable households. UNDP will also support communities to embark on tree planting and environmental waste management. 6.Building Capacity of Government and Implementing Partners - UNDP will provide capacity support State and locality authorities to assume the responsibility to plan, prepare and coordinate crisis response in all project locations.

UNDP will also support communities to embark on tree planting and environmental waste management. Lastly, UNDP will also promote environmentally friendly agricultural practices through improved extension services and farmer field schools.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The intervention will mainstream protection concerns in food security and livelihoods activities in order to address, mitigate and reduce these threats to particular vulnerable groups especially women. UNDP will prioritize safety, dignity of vulnerable groups and avoid causing harm; promote equitable and impartial access to all groups, enhance accountability, promote participation and empowerment of the affected population.
