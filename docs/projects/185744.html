Project Summary

UNICEF will provide support to ensure quality, equitable, and gender-sensitive maternal, newborn, child, and adolescent health and immunization services, as well as provision of sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services including counseling and referral for GBV and support to pregnant and lactating women inclusive of children with HIV, with attention to people with disabilities. Focus and emphasis will be on adherence to the Minimum Package of Essential Health Service for Primary Health Centers in the Rohingya refugee camps. We will collaborate with other partners from the health sector to bridge any gap of skilled human resources and supplies ensuring that the minimum package is adhered to. The project targets a vulnerable population of 482,404 people across the Rohingya refugee and host communities. To respond to the basic health and nutrition needs of Rohingya and host community women and children, UNICEF will continue providing a Bangladesh Government approved Minimum package of essential health services through the 13 health facilities in the camps (5 PHCs, and 8 HPs) and 2 Diarrhoea Treatment Centres (DTCs) as well as strengthening government health systems and facilities in adjacent host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf (2 union sub-centers and 16 community clinics). A special focus will continue to support the continuation of well-functioning Newborn Stabilization Unit (NSU) with Kangaroo Mother Care, across all Upazila Health Complexes, special newborn care unit (SCANU) for children born with low birth weight and preterm at the District Hospital as a referral center with a focus on providing comprehensive primary health care services through the Essential Services Packages (ESP) as per Government’s Guideline and SOPs including maternal, newborn care, immunization, integrated management of childhood illnesses, nutrition, acute watery diarrhoea prevention and management, prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV services and community engagement. UNICEF will continue to play her leading role in supporting vaccine logistics and supply ensuring vaccine availability across service provision points in all camps and Cox's Bazar district. We will provide technical support, supply, and transportation of vaccines and logistics, enhancing cold chain capacity and advocacy and social mobilization for strengthening immunization services across all camps. UNICEF will also strengthen the Health Management Information System (HMIS/DHIS2) through technical support with 01 HMIS Consultant (based in Civil Surgeon Office) and capacity building of services providers on DHIS2 in coordination with all health sector partners including government counterparts, and coordinate with the team at National level in Dhaka to address technical issues in the system. UNICEF has developed the Emergency Response Plan to provide support during the emergency/disasters inclusive of strengthening Infection Prevention Control Measures for containing Covid-19 through SARI-ITC, support IPC measures with capacity building and supplies across the 13 supported facilities and continue to advocate and support COVID – 19 Vaccination programme.

1 - Identified Needs

The influx of Rohingya refugees from northern parts of Myanmar Rakhine State into Bangladesh restarted on 25 August 2017. The Fully Vaccination Coverage (FVC) in Rakhain State was very much low. As a result, the migrated children with their families remain vulnerable and prone to disease outbreaks. UNICEF is primarily focusing on preventive Health services in close cooperation with the Government and other partners such as WHO as a high number of partners are working on the curative side of Health. This aims to provide emphasis on routine immunizations and mass campaigns on immunizations.: UNICEF and WHO supported routine immunization through the government to reach 30,485 Rohingya children (male: 15,662; female: 14,823) under 1 year of age. 268 vaccinators work in 75 outreach sites in the camps and in 59 fixed sites (through NGOs). Health facility utilization is within the normal range of 2-4 consultations but access to 24/7 services remains a challenge. Adherence to the minimum standard of services and staffing is a concern and the quality of services need to be improved. Scaling up Prevention of Mother to Child Transmission service in the selected health facilities in the camps, in two Upazila health complexes and district hospital. Access to high-quality Newborn Care remains constrained in Rohingya refugee camps, UNICEF has established NSU in the UHCs of Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazila Health Complexes but referrals of sick new borns are not always finalized to these. UNICEF will support sick newborn care in supported health facilities and strengthen referral linkages, especially at night to secondary care in field hospital and upazila health complex to reduce burden in District Hospital. Maternal and neonatal death surveillance needs to be expanded to help identify and address avoidable mortality triggering factors. There are unmet needs to address vulnerable populations particularly newborns and their mothers as over 70 percent of deliveries occur at home without trained assistance and antenatal care is infrequent and remains sub-standard. Children under 5 of age are at risk for common childhood illnesses such as pneumonia, diarrhea, and malnutrition, some of which stem from difficulties ensuring exclusive breastfeeding for children under six months of age. There is inadequate access to information on services particularly for women and adolescent girls and boys, with men predominantly holding the decision-making power in Rohingya households. GBV and domestic violence affect women and girls disproportionately. UNICEF and its partners ensured the full involvement of females as community health workers (CHWs) - currently 85 percent - who are better able to engage with mothers and families as well as on GBV issues under SRHR activities. The household visits increase the possibility for women and girls to access care and information since their movement is sometimes limited. However, community engagement will involve not only the women and female caregivers but also men and male caregivers. This will continue to be vital for improving health-seeking behavior and linking communities with the health care system. Community outreach activities need to be harmonized, cross-linked with different working groups, and monitored so it can be improved. Health information and management systems do exist, however, government platform DHIS2 needs to be strengthened adjusting the technicalities of interoperability with other existing systems. Acute watery diarrhea and other infectious diseases need to be prevented and treated and considering the risk in the overcrowded makeshift settlement and host communities, it needs robust surveillance systems. Only 33,386 out of 902,947 individuals residing in the Rohingya camps have received 2 doses of the COVID-19 vaccine, and yet a large population remains unvaccinated. UNICEF will advocate for increased COVID-19 vaccine availability for the Rohingya refugees.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Under the leadership of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and coordination with the health sector in implementing routine immunization for children 0-11 months of age and pregnant women, and Supplementary Immunization Activities (SIAs) activities, when needed, for Rohingya and host population in Cox's Bazar.Activity 2: Under the leadership of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and coordination with health sector support in providing quality and equitable maternal, newborn, child health, adolescent health services, sexual and reproductive health services, and counseling and referral for GBV and support to pregnant women and children with HIV, with attention to people with disabilities. Activity 3: Under Leadership of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and coordination with the health sector provide support in strengthening Prevention of Mother to Child Transmission (PMTCT) services for Rohingya and host communitiesActivity 4: Under Leadership of Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and coordination health sector in preventing and managing Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) outbreak including management of 3 Diarrhea Treatment Centers - 1 in Leda and 2 hibernateActivity 5: Under Leadership of Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and coordination with the health sector in preparing monsoon and cyclone contingency planActivity 6: Under Leadership of Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and coordination with health sector support in maintaining the Severe Acute Respiratory Infection Isolation and Treatment Unit (SARI-ITC) in Teknaf with collaboration with icddr,bActivity 7: Under the leadership of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and coordination with the health sector in relation to risk communication and awareness creation to ensure rapid uptake of COVID-19 vaccines among target population refugees and host communities.Activity 8: Under Leadership of Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and coordination with the health sector in strengthening community health mechanisms to enhance community-based Maternal, Neonatal, Child, and Adolescent Health services including health promotion, and community outreach services. Community Health Workers are part of primary health care services in order to improve health-seeking behavior and link communities with the health care systemActivity 9: Under Leadership of Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW) and coordination with the health sector in strengthening Health Management Information System, specifically District Health Information System 2 (DHIS 2) including Maternal, Neonatal, Child Health Services, Immunization, Community Health and Non-Communicable Diseases data tracking, data analysis, and evidence-generation to inform strategic policy decisions and guidance through HMIS ConsultantsActivity 10: Under Leadership of Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoH&FW), coordination with WHO and health sector in strengthening coordination mechanism at district and field level.
