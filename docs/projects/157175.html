Project Summary

Leveraging our organization's existing presence in the targeted areas, excellent contextual understanding and over three years operational experience delivering WASH program activities in northern Syria, the proposed action will directly support implementation of the 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) through addressing the critical WASH needs of IDP and host communities, and establishing community-based mechanisms to safeguard ongoing water provision through cost recovery. We planned programme of activities under the WASH sector will increase and safeguard access to safe and adequate water for approximately 126,071 conflict-affected people. Areas have been selected based on People in Need (PIN) and Severity of Need as presented in the draft Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2019, in addition to our understanding of targeted systems, well-established operations and high levels of acceptance in targeted areas.
Targeted water stations are in urgent need of essential rehabilitation and support to maintain operations, essential for safe water provision to highly vulnerable populations in areas with high severity of need. The proposed action will deliver an evidence-based program of assistance to targeted water units to immediately increase access to safe water, while also safeguarding WASH service provision beyond the implementation period through working in coordination with Local Councils and water units to establish cost recovery mechanisms.
Targeted water systems arebelow:
Andan water station (Haritan sub-district, Aleppo) and Mseibin water station (Ariha sub-district, Idleb). Fully funded by the Turkey Humanitarian Fund (THF) from 01 January - 06 February 2019, reaching 31,239 direct beneficiaries, inclusive of approximately 10,121 IDPs in addition to a host population of 21,118 people.
Al Mastumeh water station, Ariha, Idleb: Total 99,157 beneficiaries, of which 16,408 IDP and 82,749 host (funding not secured)
Haritan water station, Haritan sub-district, Aleppo: Total 12,756 beneficiaries, of which 2,000 IDP and 10,756 host (funding not secured)
Bsarton water station, Daret Azza sub-district, Aleppo: Total 7,098 beneficiaries of which 4,538 IDP and 2,380 host (funding not secured)
Jandairis water station, Jandaris sub-district, Aleppo: Total 7,060 beneficiaries, the majority of whom are returnees with an estimated 405 IDP (funding not secured)
Planned activities will build on our WASH expertise gained over three years, specifically Al Mastumah water station from December 2015 through April 2018. As referenced in the needs assessment below, in April 2018 (when our last project supporting the Al Mastumah water station finished) the number of Waterborne Disease (WBD) cases in our area of operation was only 1 - as reported by EWARN. In August 2018, four months after our assistance to the station ceased, this number raised to 221 cases. By October 2018, an additional 134 cases were reported. These numbers emphasize the urgent, proven need for improved water treatment in the area. From 2015 through 2018, we developed excellent working relationships with the Idleb Water Directorate in addition to a highly nuanced technical understanding of the Al Mastumah water system, which will directly benefit planned activities proposed for 2019.
More recently, we have expanded a successful model of station support to increase access to safe water for catchment populations of Andan and Mseibin water stations in Haritan and Ariha sub-districts respectively. This has included successfully establishing Cost Recovery Mechanisms to safeguard access to affordable, safe water after grant completion. Building on this experience, we will work with the four new stations targeted during 2019 to also establish Cost Recovery Mechanisms which are informed by and responsive to the needs of conflict affected populations in the station catchment areas.

Needs

Several concurrent assessment sources emphasize the need to address water access and water quality provision in the proposed area of operation. Comprehensive WASH assessments were conducted by HNAP in mid-2018 to inform the 2019 HNO. These included a representative, individual-level HH survey clustered at the sub-district level as well as a stratified HH survey that focused specifically on IDP camps and informal settlements in northwest and northeast Syria (both assessments included water sampling and free chlorine (FRC) testing at the end-user side). The results indicate a slight increase in the overall water share received from the water distribution networks in 2018 compared to previous years (HNAP, 2018). However, the results also indicate that the water chlorination level remains largely insufficient, and that many people are still forced to complement the water they receive from the network with other sources or to solely rely on these alternative sources (HNAP 2018). For example, in Idleb Governorate this year, only 13.5% of the population could depend on the network for the entirety of their water consumption, while 37.4% had to complement their water resources with alternate sources and 48.8% reported that they needed to rely entirely on these alternative sources (HNAP, 2018). Results from the 2018 HNAP assessments also show that limitations in regular operations, as well as delays in maintenance and repairs have had a significant impact on the regularity of water supply and have significantly contributed to a higher risk of contamination.According to HNAP assessment of PIN’s WASH activities in the area in Jun 2018, 83% of HHs in Ariha dist are currently drinking high-risk water, and the percentage raises to more than 90% of HHs in Haritan and 85% of households in Darret Azza. The WASH survey prepared for draft HNO 2019 similarly shows that in terms of access to potable water the number of people in need in Ariha district is approx 112,000, with around 34,000 people classified as acutely in need (OCHA, 2018). The corresponding numbers of people in need in Haritan is 18,454, of which 6,078 are in acute need (OCHA, 2018) In Darret Azza, the severity scale in terms of needs for WASH services has even reached level 4 out 6 which is considered extreme. Likewise, in Jandairis, approximately 91% of the population is considered in need, of which 30% are considered acutely in need (WASH Cluster Dashboard, Jan 2019). In this sub-district, the severity scale in terms of need for WASH has also reached level 5 of 6, further highlighting the urgency of addressing WASH issues in this area (OCHA 2018). Better water quality and access would also decrease significantly the risk of Water Borne Diseases (WBD). In Apr 2018 (when our last project supporting the Al Mastumah water station ended) the number of WBD cases in our AoO was only 1 - as reported by EWARN. But by Aug 2018, this number had raised to 221 cases, and by October 2018 an additional 134 cases had been reported (EWARN, August and October 2018). These numbers further emphasize the importance of water treatment in these areas.Our WASH team conducted specific needs assessments in the proposed stations areas during Nov 2018, including a technical assessment for each station. Results show that these stations need rehabilitation, and that the planned works could raise capacity from 25% to more than 90%. Surveys also show that around 90% of potential beneficiaries in the catchment areas are currently using water from private alternative sources, without this water receiving any treatment. On average, these HHs spend more than 40% of their monthly income on water. As such, it underlines the urgent need to provide them with access to safe, affordable water.

Implementation of activities

For all targeted water stations, we will deliver a comprehensive package of assistance including essential rehabilitation to ensure stations can operate at full capacity in addition to initial support to maintain operations (including fuel, generator filters etc) while Cost Recovery Mechanisms (CRMs) are initiated, and rehabilitation works completed. CRMs will ensure access to affordable, safe water is safeguarded and maintained once grant funding ends. As reference above, we have successfully established CRMs with two water stations and will apply a proven approach and tools (including training, community sensitization and monitoring) to support cost recovery in the four, new stations targeted during 2019. This will include Al Mastumah. The process proposed by us to establish CRMs is summarized below: Collecting extensive user / beneficiary data, including average household income and resident households / users with particular vulnerabilities. Working in consultation with targeted communities, develop a CRM which is responsive to and informed by their needs, in addition to ability to pay. This will include subsidising water provision for the most vulnerable whenever possible. Extensive training for cost recovery staff and sensitization / awareness raising across the communityStation rehabilitation and provision of support for operations and maintenance (100%) for three months. Phased withdrawal of support for Operations & Maintenance as CRM start to function, reducing from 100% to 75%, from 75% to 50% and from 50% to 25% over a period of three to four months. In the final month of the action, we will monitor and support management of the CRM but will provide no support for other operations and maintenance costs which will be covered by the CRMs established. Based on a detailed technical assessment completed by our staff during early 2019 and with specific reference to Al Mastumah station, pumping of treated water can commence as soon as funding secured. The station will only be operating at a quarter of capacity initially and until identified rehabilitation works are completed. These works will be completed during the first three months and initiated as soon as funding is secured, noting we have already completed a technical assessment. During these initial three months, we will also complete data collection to inform the design and roll-out of the CRM. The CRM Manual will be developed (informed by the needs of the catchment population) in coordination with the water station and Local Councils, water unit and CRM staff will be training on operating and managing the CRM and extensive awareness raising sessions will be held in all communities within the station catchment area. As summarised above, the CRM will gradually be rolled out and support for operations and maintenance reduced from month six of the project, once rehabilitation works are completed and the station is operating at full capacity. A similar process is proposed with the other targeted stations, noting that these stations are not currently functional and urgent rehabilitation works are required before water pumping can begin. In addition to these activities, we will provide technical capacity building to water station staff including water testing / treatment, CRM, Water Safety Plans and station management.

Protection Risk Analysis

The 2019 Protection Risk Analysis for WASH highlights areas of risk for which we have already established effective mitigation measures. These relate specifically to the risks associated with the support to water supply systems, namely:-Unequal distribution of services inadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors;-Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities;-Improper storage of hazardous supplies (chlorine and other) materials) - Possibility of explosion, human casualty and asset destruction;-Risk of theft of chemicals such as chlorine/Sodium Hypochlorite which may be attractive to armed groups;-Potential exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/ rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure (water stations, towers, distribution networks);-Health risk associated with construction works and exposure to chemicals. We will adhere to all of the mitigating measures outlined in the matrix. The previous experience that we had in working with water units and community stakeholders will allow us to ensure that all mitigation measures are delivered. We have strong relationships with communities and authorities in the area of intervention, MOU templates that are accepted, as well as established warehousing SOPs and guidance which all staff adhere to for the use of personal protection equipment. This combined with the mechanisms below will ensure that protection risks are minimised for this project. The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable populations in the face of conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries in Syria will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cyclewherever possible – for example, needs and resource identification, design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. Moreover, by applying lessons learned from previous interventions, wewill engage with local councils, relief committees and other key actors to mitigate risks faced by women and children in particular, and to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights, though we will keep in mind the security environment within Syria and the safety of its potential beneficiaries at all times.We also ensure that all staff sign the organisation’s code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report abuse by staff or beneficiaries, including misuse of assets or funds. We have strict protocols against any sexual relations with minors or beneficiaries of any age. Additionally, we will ensure that assessments and beneficiary selection will assess women’s and girls’ unique needs in addition to othervulnerable groups. Inclusion of vulnerable groups within the implementation decisions will ensure that interventions are effective for the largest possible amount of affected people.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

We are continually updating its contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in NWS, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Risks beyond our control could include: shifts in administrative powers; heightened insecurity restricting access; targeting of aid organisations or staff. Our organization has dedicated Security staff that closely monitor and advise on the dynamic, continually evolving security situation. Consideration of the highly volatile situation within Syria has been built into program design. Real-time analysis of the security situation will continue to allow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary. During temporary, security-related suspensions, our field teams employ various techniques to maintain communication and support to project stakeholders and beneficiaries. With specific reference to WASH, should security deteriorate, day-to-day operations will be managed by water unit staff under the remote management of our WASH staff. Well-established procedures are in place to allow for remote management and continuous monitoring of implementation should security prevent our staff from physically accessing stations. This strategy is reinforced by an established training programme to build the technical capacity of water station staff, in addition to a well-developed range of remote monitoring mechanisms which will immediately be employed should our staff be unable to physically access targeted stations.Planned WASH activities for 2019 have all been informed by recent need assessments completed by our staff, and are therefore evidence-based, feasible and accurately budgeted. As referenced above, we have operated in targeted areas for many years and enjoys high levels of community acceptance and contextual understanding which will facilitate implementation of activities. Planned activities will be implemented in areas where we have complementary FSL, SNFI and/or Education programming which further increases acceptance, in addition to operational expertise and knowledge. We also have well-established working relationships with the relevant Water Directorates and Water Units, which will again facilitate implementation - in particular planned activities to establish CRMs. Based on our recent experience rolling out CRM with Mseibin and Andan stations, the risk of beneficiaries being unwilling to pay for water services can be successfully mitigated against through effective community sensitization and ensuring the CRM is informed by the unique needs and demographics within the catchment population. Costs are effectively being recovered for both stations targeted by us under the current OCHA action and the successful program of mapping, training, consultation and sensitization will be applied to Cost Recovery activities for stations targeted during 2019.
