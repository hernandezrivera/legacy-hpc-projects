Project Summary

This intervention aims to link to the strategic objectives of the 2022 HRP by providing a life-saving humanitarian intervention through an integrated health, nutrition and WASH package for displaced population, their host communities, returnees and other vulnerable groups in West Darfur, West Kordofan, South Kordofan, and Gedaref States. The total direct target for integrated services: 243,430 beneficiaries across West Darfur, West and South Kordofan and Gadarif State.
The project will focus on addressing protracted health, nutrition and WASH needs of most vulnerable target population, and enhancing basic health and nutrition service through static and mobile outreach service in the targeted localities, alongside improving institutional WASH component.
Concern has been operating in the targeted locations and the proposed plan is based on projection through BHA, EU, ECHO, Irish Aid and private funding.

1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian crisis in Sudan continues impacting the already fragile health system and reducing the capacity to provide basic health services / responding to the multiple emergencies affecting the country. The annual cycles of floods, disease outbreaks, civil unrest, border conflicts, and ongoing economic crisis with the extremely limited resources of the MoH to response for the needs, have further impacted the accessibility to health services. Health: Weak capacity of MoH to provide PHC packages across the country increases the pressure on INGOs and UN to fill the gap such as provision of support for the medicines pipeline system. Lack of health staff in isolated, remote facilities and their limited knowledge and technical skills contributes to weak services. PHC buildings don't meet minimum standard e.g. lack of storage facilities for medicines, lack of operation rooms. Malaria is one of the most critical problems in the rural communities and accessing medicines and treatment is limited. According to the 2021 multi sectorial needs assessment MSNA, only 13.4 % of women across the country had their deliveries in health facilities with 40% of the home deliveries not attended by a qualified healthcare person. Clinical management and psychological support to gender-based violence survivors, including clinical management of rape, is still burdened by a weak referral system and lack of qualified staff both at the facility and community level. COVID-19 keeps on disrupting health and nutrition service delivery countrywide, with case management capacities from health care providers, low vaccination pace and community denial. Nutrition: GAM levels (13.6%) is projected to remain at high level in 2022 across Sudan. The situation is more critical in 64 localities that are classified with very high prevalence of 15% and above acute malnutrition as per WHO emergency levels classification. Out of those, 9 localities fall into catastrophic levels i.e. GAM 30% and above. The range of above described protracted crises are compounding the negative nutritional outcomes of most vulnerable groups countrywide. Latest sector analysis reveals 36% stunting and micronutrient deficiency affecting over 48% of U5 suffering from anaemia. GAM rate is high in Concern’s following areas of operations: 1. West Darfur state (GAM rate 8.4 at state level), with critical locations Jabel Moon (10.5), Kulbus Kereik (12.6), and El Geneina (9.2) localities. Target: 35 HFs/OTP in three locations (Jabel Moon, Kulbus and El Geneina) and catchment HFs/OTPs. 2. West Kordofan (GAM rate 12.7 at state level) in Abyei-Muglad locality Al Sunut, El Nehoud , El Odaya, El Salam, Abuzabad Ghubaysh Keilak, Lagawa Target: 22HF/OTPs in eight localities. 3. South Kordofan (GAM rate 9.5 at state level) Abu Jubaiha, Leri, Tadamon,Gedr and Talodi. Target: 21 HF/OTPs in 5 localities. 4. Gadare in Western Galabat locality (GAM 19.78 at state level). Target: 4 HF/OPT.WASH: Sudan is additionally burdened with poor hygiene practices and lack of potable water which direct contributes to high infection rates, spread of waterborne diseases, acute malnutrition not only in HH level but even in facilities. In Concern's operation areas, institutional WASH is one of the most critical needs besides health and nutrition support. (lack of latrines, potable water, waste management). The health and nutrition facilities do not have basic WASH infrastructures in place contribute to weak health and nutrition services, with a high risk of disease outbreak especially during the C19 time.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

During the project design and implementation, climate change adaptation approaches will be considered particularly for the renovation and management of health/nutrition facilities / water sources. During the technical assessment and design of the renovation work, the reduction of water waste will be one of the most critical elements to consider, as well as solar systems. Nutrition and health activities will also consider the environmental footprint by using environmentally manageable materials for building renovation. Waste management (debris) will be organized during and after the renovation. Capacity building will conducted on waste management (particularly medical waste management, by means of appropriate storing and incinerating). Natural and locally available resources will be considered as much as possible to reduce the environmental footprint.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. Among other vulnerable individuals, mothers who walk many hours to reach the centers will be oriented about ways to protect themselves on the way. Each HF, where the mobile services will refer to, will maintain the availability of a protection focal person and the communities will be informed about the role of this person. Traditional healers will be targeted by behaviour change activities at community level. Focal persons in each HF (one male and one female) will be responsible for dealing with GBV cases and related protection issues. Mobile services (nutrition/health) will also have one-one protection focal point (possibly female) for dealing with sensitive issues. Children will be in high focus, with field staff duly reporting on safeguarding. Beneficiaries will be oriented about their rights regarding the project objectives (e.g. access to information, access to services). In order to increase equal access for the most vulnerable from different ethical groups and backgrounds (IDPs, refugees, and host communities), information sharing about project implementation, achievements, selection of beneficiaries, will be provided continuously through a selected focal person in each group.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Concern WW is a Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) member and this intervention will be implemented based on CHS principles. The targeted communities will be part of the entire project cycle through constant meetings, dialogues, focus group discussions (FGDs) and monitoring. At the beginning of the project, beneficiaries will be informed about the project objectives, deliverables and their expected contribution and entitlements. They will also be part of the assessment process and their recommendations will be recorded and considered for the project implementation plan. Continuous information sharing in local language through e.g. meeting with field staff, monthly coordination meeting with key leaders in the targeted communities, and partners, will be part of the information management during the project implementation. M&E activities include participants from the targeted activities to ensure appropriate feedback for improvement by way of HH visits and interviews and community FGDs. Beneficiary targeting will be guided by needs observed on the ground, humanitarian and “Do No Harm” principles, international methodologies and Concern’s specific policies targeting the poorest among affected population. Concern has an active Complaints Response Mechanisms (CRM) in place that will be utilized for the project. There will be various tools applied for getting information e.g., CRM hotlines, feedback boxes, focus group discussions, one-to-one discussions to record the community/individual feedback coordinated by MEAL team.
