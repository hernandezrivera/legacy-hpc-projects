Project Summary

Zimbabwe has been faced by a multiple of socio-economic challenges, which have been further exacerbated by 2019 hyperinflation and by the El Niño-induced droughts during two consecutive agricultural seasons (2018/19 and 2019/2020). The country has been left with a soaring inflation estimated at 300% in August 2019 according to International Monetary Fund and with a national cereal deficit estimated at over 800,000 MT for the 2019/20 lean season. This has resulted in households being faced with deteriorating purchasing power amidst shortages of basic commodities such as bread, cooking oil and fuel. It is anticipated that from October 2019 to January 2020, the food security situation will worsen with an estimated 3.58 million people (38% of the rural population) in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worst who will require urgent action to reduce food consumptions gaps and save livelihoods. Among these, an estimated 2.47 million people will likely face Crisis conditions (IPC Phase 3) whilst 1.10 million will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Nine (9) districts including Chivi district in the Province of Masvingo, were classified to be in IPC phase 4 (Emergency) for this period.
The 2019/20 rainfall season is forecasted to be below average characterized by a late start and erratic rainfall. This combined with the anticipated widespread poor access to agricultural inputs, will likely lead to below average cropped area. A third consecutive below-average season is expected to result in lower than normal livelihood opportunities, household incomes, and 2020 harvest, driving an atypically high assistance needs.
It is against this background that Nutrition Action Zimbabwe is planning to respond to food insecurity situation in Chipinge, Chiredzi, Mutasa and Murehwa. The intervention will target both urban and rural communities with urban receiving support through mobile money transfer while the rural communities will be targeted with direct with vouchers or other suitable modality. Early recovery agricultural packs in the form of seeds, tools, fertilizers and small livestock will be distributed to targeted vulnerable rural beneficiaries will urban beneficiaries will be targeted with Internal savings and lending schemes as an exit strategy.
Community self ranking criteria will be used to identify the most vulnerable beneficiaries . Further assessments will help to determine the specific interventions in terms of livelihoods support. Gender mainstreaming will be a key component of the interventions to ensure that gender groups are consulted at each intervention stage to ensure that their concerns, interests and preferences are incorporated in the project design, implementation and monitoring process. Community based volunteers and local leadership will be used as the key drivers of the program to ensure that the most vulnerable including the marginalised are reached by the program. A robust feedback mechanisms through a dedicated tool free line, suggestion boxes and whistle blower policy will be used to receive information from beneficiaries, staff and stakeholders. This will help to capture key information on any mal-practices for review, and also some complements on positive program issues.

1 - Activities

The project includes distribution of Unconditional cash for the targeted households. The cash transfer will occur through mobile money distribution. A total of 6 cycles of mobile money distributions will occur in the selected districts. The market assessment by WFP and partners indicated that prices in USD have remained constant despite the fluctuations with the local currency. In light of this the value of each distribution was determined using the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) currently pegged at USD 16 equivalent. Distributions will be made in ZWL currency at the prevailing bank rate at the time of distribution, through mobile money transfers via a mobile network platform with a wider coverage. Each HH will be targeted up to the actual number of people in the HH, but capped to a maximum of 5 members. The distributed cash will enable the targeted population to improve its immediate food security, coping capacity and recovery ability (e.g. through purchase of livelihood implements).The vulnerable rural population will also receive season sensitive agricultural inputs as an exit strategy while internal Savings and Lending Schemes will be piloted for both urban and rural targeted communities. These activities will be implemented to mitigate against the impact of the drought; 1. Provide monthly food baskets through cash-based transfers to acutely food insecure population in rural, urban and peri-urban areas. 2. Provide protective nutritional rations to increase the nutritional value of the food baskets provided for the most vulnerable households NAZ has in the past implemented food security and livelihoods programs through Mobile money transfer modalities and direct distributions and lessons learnt will be used to improve on the programming of this action. The mobile money will be delivered through a preferred network provider with capacity and experience in similar activities and willing to expand network coverage should targeted communities be out of network areas. Through community engagements, all targeted beneficiaries will be mobilized to have National Identification cards and where not possible, at least the targeted household should have an alternative person with the National ID. The targeted beneficiaries will be encouraged to procure standard mobile handsets if the household does not have a handset before hand to facilitate the delivery of the project. Owning a phone increases convenience, privacy and control over one’s account and eventually reduce problems on the use of the transferred money. Women will be encouraged to access mobile phones to address other possible gender-specific constraints. Though the Post and Telecommunication Regulatory Authority (POTRAZ, 2016) noted that the mobile phone penetration was nearly 85% for adults, a provision will be made within the action budget to facilitate procurement and registration of sim cards to facilitate mobile money transfer for the most vulnerable beneficiaries. As a lesson learnt from the previous interventions, the preferred network provider will be encouraged to utilize local agents who are trusted by the local community members. This confidence is critical as beneficiaries will rely on them to conduct transactions, resetting of blocked pin codes and continuous support. NAZ will operate a Toll Free communication system which will be used by the beneficiaries for trouble shooting. Agriculture seed packs will be distributed through a seed voucher program where prior arrangements will be made with local inputs suppliers. The beneficiaries will be provided with vouchers redeemable at any of the selected and participating agro-dealers. The beneficiaries are given an opportunity to make choices on the inputs they want and Agritex extension officers on the ground will give further technical guidance

2 - Indicators

Monitoring & Evaluation: A complete M&E plan will be put in place before project start-up by the project’s MEAL team to provide the monitoring framework, conduct surveys to provide information for evidenced based decision making. Context monitoring –This will monitor the price and availability of food commodities through an agreed food basket, market locations, and the frequency. The monitoring will be done in collaboration with other food assistance partners within the Food Security Cluster. Post distribution monitoring – This will track utilization of household food assistance, household food consumption, timeliness of the assistance, participants’ perception about gender and protection considerations, safety and security, vendor feedback, access to and effectiveness of participant feedback loops and other factors associated with the transfer of the entitlement. Project activities will be monitored at community level; targeting and selection of beneficiaries, verification process, distribution center, market level, health facility and at household level. The project staff will collect data on; people receiving support through Mobile Cash Transfer, nutrition awareness-raising sessions and group discussion carried out. Specific registers will be put in place to track on people adopting promoted behaviours as a result of the intervention, number of people adopting specific behaviours such as dietary diversity, exclusive breastfeeding. The following indicators will be monitored during the implementation of the project:Indicators: Number of households benefitting from Cash Based ransfers in rural areas; • Number of households benefitting from Cash Based Transfers in urban and peri-urban areas • Number of individuals benefitting from protection rations • Number of households receiving season-sensitive emergency crop and livestock input assistance. • Number of critical community assets for crop and livestock production rehabilitated and built. • Number of vulnerable small holder farmers receiving extension and advisory services to improve yields, manage crop pests and livestock diseases.
