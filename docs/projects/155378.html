Project Summary

The targeted districts of Chipinge and Buhera of Manicaland Province are districts that have been identified in need of support for the upcoming El Nino season. According to the National Nutrition Survey conducted in 2018, Manicaland Province had the highest prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition at (0.53%) and the Minimum Acceptable Diet was at 4%.
GOAL ZW is currently operational in Chipinge district implementing a three- year livelihoods/Nutrition intervention and will also re-enter Buhera district for a three- year livelihoods and nutrition program as of January 2019.
Water access remains low in both districts with approximately 40% of the boreholes in Buhera and 60% of boreholes in Chipinge not being functional.
The proposed intervention will ensure a holistic approach and complementarity to GOAL’s ongoing livelihoods and nutrition programs, targeted communities will be able to build their resilience and mitigate the impact of the expected El Nino, as well as be better equipped to protect themselves from the current and future Health related outbreaks, through their increased knowledge of Hygiene and Sanitation and Water access. Chipinge and Buhera Districts are livestock dependent, repairing of 78 boreholes and construction of 78 troughs through this action will not only provide targeted communities with water but will also contribute towards mitigating the impact of El Nino on the livestock. Each borehole will benefit of a Water point committee being established or strengthened to ensure maintenance and sustainability. The care group mothers from the nutrition intervention will be able to access water for their nutritional gardens thus contributing to diet diversification, increased water access will also ensure a level of hygiene in the household which will contribute towards their overall well-being. Reducing distances for mothers and young girls to fetch water will also enable mothers to spend more time at home with their children caring for them, it will also enable young girls to spend less time on the road thus addressing safeguarding issues.

Needs

According to 2018 ZimVAC report 28% of households in Manicaland are drinking from unprotected water sources. According to statistics from Rural Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Information Management System (RWIMS) there is very low coverage of hygiene enabling practices in particular hand washing facilities, the coverage currently stands at 15.2% in Chipinge and 5% in Buhera. The combination of these challenges has led to exposure of these communities to water borne disease like diarrhoea, typhoid and cholera.Approximately 40% of the boreholes in Buhera and 60% of boreholes in Chipinge are not functional . Women and girls are particularly affected in view of their role of fetching water and having to travel longer distances, further exacerbating issues related to safeguarding. Targeted communities by this action are resorting to alternative unsafe water sources like open wells, river beds and dams for consumption. In view of the anticipated El Niño which is likely to cause draught in 2019, access to clean and safe water will worsen leading to water borne diseases like diarrhoea, typhoid and cholera. The el nino phenomenon causes below normal rain and above normal temperature patterns and often brings drought or famine. These adverse patterns affect both crop and livestock activities from which 70% the rural populace derives their livelihoods from. According to 2018 GOAL preliminary contextual assessment in Buhera 64 cholera cases of which 10 have been confirmed and six (6) individuals have died in the community.

Activities

Through this action GOAL proposes to facilitate the rehabilitation of 78 boreholes (48 Buhera and 30 in Chipinge). A total of 78 Water point committees will be strengthened/and or established through trainings on maintenance and on community based management of water points. To ensure sustainability long after the project will have ended. The targeted communities will receive trainings on participatory health and hygiene promotion, the trainings will primarily focus on safe hygiene and sanitation practices, Participatory Health and Hygiene Education (PHHE) and water conservation. The trainings will include training of EHTs/Extension workers and village health workers on PHHE messages and hygiene practices. IEC materials will be developed and/or reproduced to disseminate health and hygiene information to communities with a particular focus on institutions.In complementing current GOAL livelihoods activities in Buhera and Chipinge, 78 livestock drinking troughs will be constructed at each rehabilitated borehole, providing access to approximately 28,000 livestock. Each borehole will provide clean safe water to an average of 60 households, therefore the project will benefit 4,680 households (approximately 23,400 community members). Monitoring Indicators and Targets• Number of people (disaggregated M/F, children) having access to at least 7.5 – 15l of safe water per day • Number of people (disaggregated M/F, children) reached with key health and hygiene messages• Number of rehabilitated boreholes• Number of functional water point committees formed• Number of livestock drinking troughs constructed at water points• Number of volunteers trained
