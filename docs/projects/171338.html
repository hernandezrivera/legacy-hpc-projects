Project Summary

This project is a WASH intervention by SIF, in partnership with ESDC, in schools across four governorates of the West Bank. The intervention is meant to improve access to WASH facilities and to increase hygiene awareness during the COVID-19 pandemic for students and teachers. At least 178 schools have been identified to be suffering from insufficient WASH facilities in Palestine. Schools suffer from weak infrastructure, insufficient sanitation units, insufficient drinking water fountains, environmental hazards, and a lack of hygiene supplies. Specific key challenges in schools that are identified in this project include: a lack of sanitary units, a lack of water tanks, lack of WASH access for people with disabilities, and overall poor hygiene practices. The COVID-19 pandemic has made these issues even more problematic and there is an increased need to improve sanitation and hygiene.
This project will target students and teachers in 20 schools, including 4 schools in East Jerusalem, 2 schools in Nablus, 9 schools in Ramallah & Al Bireh, and 5 schools in Salfit. Through this project, SIF will improve access to WASH services in these schools by constructing sanitary units (bathrooms) (quantity: 1), constructing sanitary units for people with disabilities (quantity: 5), rehabilitating sanitary units (quantity: 8), rehabilitating sanitary units for people with disabilities (quantity: 2), and constructing drinking water fountains (quantity: 9). The number of units have been determined based on a completed needs-assessment.
SIF will also increase awareness of COVID-19 and necessary hygiene practices, and provide the necessary inputs to improve hygiene in schools. This will include the production and installation of awareness signs at schools; the production and distribution of awareness materials; holding small group awareness sessions for teachers; providing hygiene kits for students; and providing hygiene kits for overall use at the schools.

1 - Needs

This project is mainly responding to HRP Strategic Objective 2: "The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL," and WASH Cluster Objective 2: "Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians." Schools across Palestine suffer from weak infrastructure, insufficient sanitation units / drinking water fountains, and deteriorating school environments, including environmental hazards and lack of hygiene supplies (Unicef Schools Survey for WASH, 2018). This has resulted in a challenging educational environment and increased health risks for teachers and students, further exacerbated by COVID-19. As of 20 September 2020, all children across the West Bank have returned to schools; since the start of the school year, about 90 schools were either temporarily or fully closed for a period of one to 14 days, following confirmed COVID-19 cases among pupils or school staff (OCHA, September 2020). Children face various protection risks in general, but during COVID19 pandemic, some of the risks have increased including physical and emotional risks and the current situation is placing additional challenges to ensure face-to-face education. The Palestinian Ministry of Education (MoE), with its limited available financial resources, struggles to implement COVID-19 safety interventions including distance learning, MHPSS, IPC measures, provision of hygiene kits, and the rehabilitation of water and sanitation facilities. Thus, there is an urgent need to secure a healthy and safe environment for students and teachers. SIF has been in contact with the Ministry of Education (MoE) - including the Director General of School Health - since the start of the pandemic to discuss needs in schools. The MoE has shared several documents with SIF, focusing on WASH-related needs in schools, the MoE COVID-19 preparedness and response plan, and studies conducted by partners working in the sector. Based on these documents and several meetings with MoE, WASH-related challenges in schools can be summarized as follows: 1. Lack of of Sanitary units: A UNICEF school survey shows that a toilet is available for an average of 49.5 students in West Bank and Gaza schools, while the MoE recommends to provide at least 1 toilet unit for each 25 elementary school students and at least 1 toilet unit for each 30 high school students. International standards (WHO) recommend providing at least 1 toilet unit for each 25 female students and 1 toilet unit for each 50 or 60 male students. 2. Lack of Water Tanks: 13% of schools in rural areas and Area C lack connectivity to water networks, and depend on unreliable water sources or private tankers. Surveys show that only 79% of schools have water storage tanks at all. 3. Poor Hygiene Practices: Student’s hygiene is affected by lack of soap and hygiene kits, as well as by dirty sanitation units and common unhealthy practices. As per the Unicef study, only 67.9% of students stated that they clean their hands after using toilets, after playing outside, or before eating. Additional reasons for not using sanitary units were: they are broken, lack of privacy, or lack of hygiene supplies. 82.9% of sanitation units in girls’ schools and 86.7% of sanitation units in boys schools lack toilet paper; while only 54.3% of units in girls schools and only 29.8% of units in boys schools have soap.4. Gender mainstreaming: gender aspects are not fully considered in the designing of sanitary units at schools in terms of number of available toilets with respect to the number of students which should be different at girl's schools as mentioned above. Also, 44% of West Bank students - especially girls and those in higher grades - refuse to use the sanitation units and prefer to wait until they go home because they believe that school sanitation units are dirty and unhealthy.5. Lack of Access for People with Disabilities: The WHO indicates that having inaccessible toilets leads to a disabled child not eating or drinking all day in order to avoid using the toilet, leading to health problems and sometimes to their dropping out of school altogether. Only 54.6% of schools in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, including UNRWA schools, have adaptations for students with disabilities in terms of appropriate toilets. --- At the launch of the HRP, SIF coordinated with the WASH Cluster to obtain the latest schools’ needs assessment provided by the MoE. SIF looked closely at schools in Salfit, South Nablus and Ramallah and Al Bireh Governorates, as it has implemented several water projects over the past years in these three governorates. SIF has also included schools in Jerusalem Governorate as SIF has been approached by the Jerusalem Education Directorate for support. Upon selection of governorates, SIF coordinated with the directorate in each governorate to validate the needs.

2 - Activities

As identified in the HNO 2021, at least 178 Palestinian schools suffer from insufficient WASH facilities. This project is designed to improve WASH services in schools and increase awareness for students and teachers to better manage the COVID-19 pandemic. Result 1: 20 Schools have better access to WASH services. Activities under this result will be led by SIF, which has a staff of well-experienced engineers who have been successfully implementing and monitoring WASH activities in the West Bank since 2010. As a member of the WASH Cluster and AIDA, SIF is regularly updated with sector-needs and plays an active and complementary role with partners. Activities under this result include: 1. Construction of sanitary units (bathrooms): number of toilet seats/sinks to be calculated based on number of students as per international standards; will follow MoE designs and specifications. Plastic storage tanks of 1.5m3 capacity will be provided. Designs will consider gender aspects. 2. Rehabilitation of sanitary units (bathrooms): works include replacing toilet seats, sinks, faucets, pipe connections, doors, tiles; painting of walls; and supplying water tanks when needed. 3. Construction of drinking water fountains: containing 8 faucets built with blocks covered with tiles; in addition to all pipe connections as needed. 4. Rehabilitation of sanitary units (bathrooms) for people with disabilities: includes level or ramped access; a wide door and sufficient space for wheelchair user; and provision of support structures such as a handrail and toilet seat. 5. Construction of sanitary units (bathrooms) for people with disabilities: Same as above, focusing on construction instead of rehabilitation. --- Designs will follow the Palestinian Construction Guide and will be implemented in close coordination with the Infrastructure Department at Education Directorates, and gender aspects will be considered in designing of sanitary units. . SIF will conduct technical field visits to each school to confirm exact needs and develop designs, BoQ, and tender documents. All safety measures at the construction site will be included in contracts and will be the responsibility of the contractor and monitored by SIF engineers. The directorates will be a main partner in the tender opening sessions, follow up field visits, and final handover. Result 2: People at 20 Schools are better aware about COVID-19 and related hygiene practices. Activities under this result will be led by ESDC, which has been working in the sector since 2003 and is an active member in the WASH cluster. ESDC has implemented several COVID-response interventions, targeting women, children, and youth, including awareness sessions, distribution of hygiene kits, and production of media/material for children. The project will build on what partners are doing in the sector to raise awareness on COVID-19; the project will refer to the Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE) plan and benefit from the wide material developed by UNICEF, WHO, and Palestinian government organizations. Activities under this result include: 1. Production and installation of awareness signs: best hygiene practices, do’s and don’ts, etc. 2. Production and distribution of awareness material: potentially stories, posters, brochures, etc., based on student ages. 3. Small group awareness sessions for teachers: regarding their role in guidance and monitoring and their role in raising children’s awareness against Covid-stigmatization; provide awareness materials for classrooms. Will target an estimated 8 teachers per school. 4. Hygiene kits for students: will provide individual hygiene kits for all students.. 5. Hygiene kits for schools: will provide hygiene supplies sufficient for 3 months. --- SIF will be indicating a DO NO HARM principle: Both organizations will analyse and anticipate the range of implications actions may have on the protection of different groups and their environment. The participation of vulnerable groups in project planning, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation will help ensure the project is responsive to protection concerns. SIF had developed a new Child Safeguarding Policy in 2020, and will abide by it throughout this project.

Regarding the evaluation aspects, several specific tools, taking into consideration COVID-19 safety measurements will be used in order to ensure an efficient and prompt evaluation of the activities during their implementation and after their achievement, detailed as follows:1. Random field visits to the schools by the MEAL department. 2. After the completion of construction/ rehabilitation activities of the WASH facilities, all infrastructure Departments within the Education Directorate in each of the targeted governorate will be requested to submit a stamped document to certify what had been done, as well as to certify that it was done as per the approved specifications. 3. After the completion of hygiene kits distribution to the schools and students, schools’ headmasters will be requested to submit a stamped document to certify that all school students have received the hygiene kit, as well as, that the school received the general hygiene kit for school use.4. A key informative interview (KII) using KOBO will be conducted with headmasters of the targeted school, in order to collect their feedback and measure their satisfaction on the project’s activities implemented in their respective schools.5. Remote Post-Assessment using KOBO will be conducted with a representative sample of the teachers who attended the awareness sessions through phone calls, in order to collect the attendees' feedback.Moreover, SIF’s complaint number will be provided to the schools’ principles in order to provide any complaints and/or feedback during the project implementation. All feedback and complaints received will be recorded, processed/studied by the MEAL department, and shared with the relevant team for follow-up on the recommendation/ actions as needed.Lastly, a lessons learnt session will be organized after the completion of the project’s activities in order to capitalize on the project, identify its successes and challenges, determine necessary improvements, and promote best practices.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

All safety measurements in regards to COVID-19 outbreak will be taken into consideration while implementing the project's activities. All construction work will be done outside of school working hours, and the material and equipment of the construction will be kept in a safe storage area, in order to avoid disturbing the students, as well as to ensure their safety. SIF interventions in the Palestinian Territory have a principal approach of individual and group physical and psychological integrity, self-respect, and empowerment. SIF interventions adhere to the Core Humanitarian Standard’s (CHS) framework as part of the foundation of its projects. The standards of quality, accountability, and commitment are at the principal values of the initiative.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

All students in the targeted schools will receive the same assistance in regards to awareness and the hygiene kits. Additionally, for the construction and rehabilitation of WASH facilities within the targeted schools, the work will be done in line with gender mainstreaming and will take into consideration people with disabilities.
