Project Summary

UNICEF CP will be in line with the CP AOR strategy for Syria as well as with the “No Lost Generation” framework, targeting 161 high severity (3 to 5) and 96 low severity scale sub-districts. The geographical prioritization based on communities
in sub-districts with medium to high severity and large number of people in need; UNICEF’s Child Protection strategy for Syria in 2021 will follow its 3 pillar structure in its prevention and response interventions:1) Improving the quality of community-based
CP through behaviour change and awareness raising on specific child protection risks, and structured psychosocial support and parenting programmes for boys and girls and parents/caregivers. 2) Expand the reach & improve the quality of child protection specialized
services for survivors of violence, exploitation, neglect & abuse through case management to address those critical needs & restore normalcy and wellbeing to the lives of children 3) Sustained capacity building of frontline CP responders to build sustainable
CP workforce ensuring minimum standards.UNICEF shall contribute to expanding the availability of quality CP services for girls, boys, women & men in targeted locations, focusing on children & women, internally displaced, host communities & refugees in camps/
settlements where possible as well as adapted delivery modalities for CP during the COVID-19 pandemic.Based on 2021 HNO MSNA, approximately one in two people are at risk from explosive contamination, and a third of the communities are potentially contaminated
by explosive ordnance. Contamination severely impacts lives, livelihoods and amplifies social and economic crisis aggravated by COVID-19 pandemic.Based on Joint Mine Action and CP inter-sectorial analysis nearly 1 out of 4 recorded victims is a child. RE
messages shall be provided to children and adults through both humanitarian & public service using both static facilities & mobile teams as well as adapted delivery modalities during COVID19 pandemic to reach affected populations. UNICEF will build capacity
of RE partners to provide quality EORE -age, gender & disability appropriate, also provide child victims assistance to mitigate negative impacts on their life & wellbeing.UNICEF in addressing Gender Based Violence (GBV) shall adopt holistic approach in GBV
services, including prevention, empowerment and response, to affected population through Community Centres, Women and Girls Safe Spaces and Child Friendly Centres.Adapted delivery modalities will be utilized during the COVID-19 pandemic. Support will be provided
for the PSEA mechanisms to protect beneficiaries & build capacity humanitarian actors to address needs of the population in efficient, transparent and accountable manner. The programme shall be implemented in coordination with other actors (Child Protection,
GBV and Mine Action) strategies & results framework.As part of Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), protection needs assessment through MSNA are conducted through engagement with the affected population at household level and with the key informant
at community level to accurately capture the conditions affecting the affected populations. UNICEF, together with the partners, will ensure that AAP agenda will be included throughout the programme cycle by monitoring and consultation with beneficiaries on
the quality of service.
The project will continue to advocate for and improve collaboration with other sectors to address key CP concerns e.g. child labour with ERL and violence in schools with education.

UNCEF Syria has a comprehensive Monitoring and Evaluation system in place with the required capacities and procedures to regularly monitor progress of child protection interventions towards planned results.
UNCEF Syria has a comprehensive Monitoring and Evaluation system in place with the required capacities and procedures to regularly monitor progress of child protection interventions towards planned results. This includes physical verification to ensure that any bottlenecks are quickly identified, and adjustments are made in real time. Program monitoring focuses on:
• Assessing the implementation of programmes (progress vis-à-vis targets) according to signed programme cooperation agreements with implementing partners;
• Verification of supplies delivered, bottlenecks and required follow-up actions;
• Monitoring of programmes and supplies to assess beneficiaries’ feedback in terms of the relevance, quality, timeliness and impact of specific programmes or supplies delivered.
Overall, monitoring is conducted through the following three modalities:
1) UNICEF staff: In areas that are accessible, UNICEF staff based at the Field Offices and in Damascus conduct regular programme monitoring visits to assess the abovementioned focus areas. Field Offices are located in Aleppo, Homs, Tartous, Qamishli, Deir-Ez Zor and Damascus (covering the five southern governorates) as well as cross-border hubs in Gaziantep and Amman;
2) Implementing partners: Through UNICEF’s cooperation agreements with partners, regular progress reports are submitted by partners according to UNICEF corporate reporting standards and requirements. All partners conduct regular programme monitoring visits to assess the implementation of their programmes. Partners submit monthly progress reports on the 4Ws (Who does What Where and When) including how many beneficiaries are reached in different areas. Reports from partners are verified by triangulating evidence from various sources. The 4Ws data is used for the monthly Situation Reports on the Syria crisis;
3) Third-party monitors: In areas where access is difficult or restricted, UNICEF uses a large network of local third-party monitors - called ‘facilitators’ - for regular situation monitoring as well as programme monitoring and post-distribution monitoring of supplies. All facilitators are deployed from their respective areas of origin and have specific expertise, experience and a thorough knowledge of the area in which they operate.

4 - Needs

Based on HNO2021, children comprise almost half of the affected population in Syria with 5,913 million children in need of child protection services and 95.4% living in areas ranked as high severity locations. Grave child rights violations remain a significant concern, with children at risk of being exposed to CP risks. In 2020, the UN verified 2,388 of violations against children in 12 out of 14 governorates in Syria.Deepening poverty continues to fuel harmful coping mechanisms and strain the capacities of families and communities to protect children, particularly adolescent girls and boys: child labour, including its worst forms is reported in all governorates and in 22% of assessed communities as frequently occurring[1].Separation from caregivers is a persistent issue across Syria. Death of a caregiver (15 % for boys and girls), economic need (25% for boys, 13% for girls), child marriage (11% for boys, 44% for girls) and child recruitment (10% for boys) are among the top cited reasons for family separations.[2] Recruitment of children by parties to the conflict is a practice in some parts of Syria. In 2020, the UN verified 813 incidents of recruitment and use of children (96% boys), with 99% used in combat roles, and 7% under the age of 15.[3]Psychosocial distress among children is reported in 23% of surveyed households[4] .Nearly 1 out of 4 recorded victims is a child: on average 4 out of 10 are killed and 6 out of 10 are injured. Economic deprivation, lack of livelihood opportunities, have led to the continued normalisation of GBV and severely impacted the psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of girls and women’s rights including those with disabilities. [5]COVID-19 has increased CP risk factors.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The risk of intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighbouring community not served. Protection risk faced by girls & boys may persist if community members do not play an active role in the delivery of protection services. Risk of existing traditional & social religious practices increasing due to limited engagement or acknowledgement of certain religious practices as a protection concern. Risk of girls & boys’ exposure to protection risk due to the limited initiatives to enhance participation in their own protection. Lack of a sustainable intervention due to limited involvement of national stakeholder in CP service delivery. Risk likelihood is Medium with High impact. Mitigation: i. Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation & Communication with communities can be proactive and informed. Ensure consultation with communities and neighbouring communities related to location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities. iii.Liaise with "neutral" parties as much as possible and triangulate all information from all sources prior to decision making. iv.Utilize sector agreed key messages to ensure consistency and inform sector of discrepancies or if new messaging. v.Linking protection intervention with existing services within targeted communities to enhance the system approach. vi.Support national actors to ensure sustainability of protection intervention. vii. Established community-based groups/committees to enhance community-based protection mechanisms. viii. Ensure information sharing with community

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Identified risks include a) limited access to affected population due to security situation; b) direct exposure of children &families to violent attacks while accessing service provision. C)risks of exploitation or abuse by service providers & d) services causing unintended harm. Assumptions: In carrying out community-based protection services,protection actors need to devote attention on locations choices &targeting of beneficiaries, avoid perceptions that assistance is delivered in a discriminatory/non-equitable manner. As risk mitigation measure, protection actors be guided by the sector severity analysis; analysis of exiting services through sector mapping tools, maintaining a transparent, inclusive & adequate communication with communities on available services; consult communities in planning, selection of services, feedback after service provision. GBV actors may need overcome cultural resistance from parts of communities to engage & accept direct engagement /provision of services to women & girls with limited access to facilities. Mobile approach be used. Agender-balanced strong overcome cultural barriers during implementation. Provide integrated services including RH service for easy entry points for survivors addressing community stigma. Sectors coordination remains critical (Health). Manage expectations with clear communications, referrals pathways. Quality of service provision may be impacted by staff turnover, lack of adequate technical capacities, particularly in specialized protection interventions. Mitigation measures include: 1) working through community-based modalities including mobile services; 2) consideration conflict patterns to inform services delivery. 3)diversification of delivery channels
