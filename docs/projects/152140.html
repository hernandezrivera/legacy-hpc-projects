Project Summary

The project aims to enhance Conflict affected populations' access to WASH services, including emergency response, thereby reducing their exposure to WASH related illnesses. This project will mainly Local Government Areas in Borno state where NRC has consolidated it's operations. Activities under this project will include construction or rehabilitation of boreholes, water points and temporary water storage facilities to ensure affected communities have access to safe water for drinking and addressing hygiene needs; construction of latrines, both shared household and communal latrines to curb the practice of open defecation; establishment of bathing shelters to compliment hygiene promotion initiatives that include provision of hygiene-related items , including the use of cash or vouchers for fostering good hygiene practices in order to mitigate the risk of waterborne disease outbreaks. The project will include a component of emergency response to epidemics such as cholera and other water and sanitation related diseases.

Needs

Approximately 75% of the WASH facilities in northeast Nigeria have been destroyed because of the ongoing conflict. This has worsened due to massive movements of populations into areas that have been considered safe but with inadequate WASH infrastructure to meet the demands for the increased total population. The WASH Sector Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) for 2018 approximates a total of 3.4 Million People in Need of WASH services across the three states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe. Poor sanitary living conditions have a direct impact on people’s health, their economic situation, and dignity. Inadequate sanitation and lack of safe drinking water are closely linked to malnutrition especially for children. Diarrhea, often associated with poor hygiene, inhibits food absorption causing under nutrition and stunting in children. A large number of people in the northeast still access water from open sources, which are a known risk factor for Cholera epidemics, especially in northern Nigeria. Nigeria has a high prevalence of Cholera and in the last ten years, multiple large-scale outbreaks have been experienced in the area. Areas bordering the Lake Chad are particularly prone to these outbreaks. According to the HNO figures, in 2018 alone,the epidemic has been reported from at least 18 LGAs with over 4,000 cases reported. More than one million people are living in LGAs are considered to be at risk of contacting cholera. Concrete measures needs to be put in place. The ongoing Cholera outbreak in Nigeria is also therefore linked to inadequate WASH services. Low WASH coverage, coupled with the practice of open defection heightens the risk of water-borne disease and worsens malnutrition. This is further exacerbated by a lack of knowledge and poor hygiene and sanitation practices.Sanitation remains a serious issue among communities in these new informal settlements, where new arrivals are settling in. New arrivals have limited or no access to sanitation facilities, which leads many to defecate in the open air and increase protection and health risks. The situation is exacerbated by the lack of money to buy basic hygiene items. 60% of the new arrivals are women and children, and 59% are below the age of 18 (28% boys and 31% girls) . In conflicts and displacements, women and girls are the most affected since they remain at high risk to various types of protection concerns. With limited water and sanitation infrastructure, women and girls are exposed to risks of sexual violence and harassment at water points and latrine facilities, as a number of safety audits revealed. Few latrines exist in Gajiram and those available are unlabeled, escalating the risk of sexual violence and harassment for women and girls . 56% of the affected IDP population is children below the age of 17 (45% M; 55% F) while 54% are women . The conditions are also unsanitary and unsafe, and often lead to Cholera outbreaks. The lack of proper lighting in latrines and lack of gender segregation and de-congestion at the water collection points expose girls and women to harassment or increases their risk to gender-based violence. Without accessible latrines, women and girls use the edges of the camps in the evenings in hopes of privacy. Communities have also reported tensions over water sources, sometimes based on ethnicity.
