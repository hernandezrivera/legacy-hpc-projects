Project Summary

In 2024, South Sudan continued to face severe protection challenges, particularly in regions impacted by displacement, conflict, and human rights violations. Key areas such as Renk in Upper Nile State, Aweil East in Northern Bahr el Ghazal, and several locations in Eastern Equatoria, including Torit, Magwi, Kapoeta, and Ikotos, are experiencing alarmingly high protection risks, especially for women and children. These regions are characterized by high levels of violence, exploitation, and neglect, compounded by a lack of access to essential protection services like legal aid, safe spaces, and psychosocial support. The instability caused by the ongoing conflict in Sudan has worsened protection needs, with hundreds of thousands of refugees and returnees crossing into South Sudan, many reporting serious human rights violations and trauma during their journey.
The beneficiaries in these regions are significant, with approximately 572,548 individuals in need across all locations, including the 550,000 individuals who fled Sudan through Renk and Aweil East, and smaller populations in Eastern Equatoria (i.e. 22,548 individuals located across; Torit, Magwi, Kapoeta, and Ikotos). Acted’s protection response aligns with the Protection Cluster objectives, focusing on the mobile and static provision of critical protection services tailored to specific needs, including psychosocial support, legal assistance, and establishment of safe spaces. As such, the proposed response is divided into two pillars, the first providing critical frontline emergency services at border points (Renk and Aweil East) targeting those in urgent need of protection assistance after fleeing the conflict in Sudan. This is provided through both static services located at formal border crossings and through a mobile modality able to mobilise to identify and respond to populations crossing via ad-hoc and informal crossing points. Services will include but are not limited to; protection referrals, integrated protection assistance, provision of in-kind support (MHM/Dignity kits) and MHPSS (including case management) support services.
Acted’s approach prioritizes the centrality of protection in all its sectors of response, working closely with local partners to strengthen coordination mechanisms to enhance referral pathways and address protection gaps through monitoring and dialogues. This will ensure that protection risks are reduced and the needs of the most vulnerable are met. Acted will also promote a nexus to development, bridging emergency humanitarian assistance with long-term solutions, particularly in Eastern Equatoria, by empowering local communities and actors to address systemic vulnerabilities, particularly those affecting women, girls, and persons with disabilities. This forms the basis of the second pillar whereby Acted’s response will include strengthening community-based protection mechanisms, raising awareness on key protection issues like child protection, and providing legal aid and housing, land, and property (HLP) rights support. Acted will also enhance local capacities through training and ensure the distribution of non-food items to support vulnerable populations.
This protection programming aligns with the Cluster Objectives CLPRO/CO1: Ensure Mobile or Static Provision of Critical Protection Assistance and Services; CLPRO/CO2: Strengthen Coordination and Promote Human Rights; CLPRO/CO3: Promote Nexus to Development and Solutions. Protections services will complement to Acted’s multi-sectorial approach, integrating CCCM (Camp Coordination and Camp Management) and WASH (Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene) interventions where relevant. By providing a comprehensive response that addresses both immediate and long-term protection needs, Acted aims to improve the safety, dignity, and well-being of the most vulnerable populations in South Sudan.

1. Needs

In 2024, the protection needs in South Sudan remain exceptionally high, especially in key areas affected by significant refugees and returnees' influx, such as Renk, in upper Nile state, and Aweil East in NBeG. Meanwhile, areas historically affected by a high presence of returnees such as the Eastern Equatoria State more specifically; Torit, Magwi and Ikotos counties continue to report and challenges pertaining to HLP. In these areas persistent conflict, displacement, and human rights violations have created significant vulnerabilities. Across these regions, the situation for women and children is particularly alarming. Lack of infrastructure, weak justice systems, and insufficient support for survivors means that many protection needs remain unmet. The absence of safe spaces, legal support, and access to basic services further intensifies vulnerabilities, leaving large segments of the population exposed to ongoing violence and trauma. The regional instability caused by the ongoing conflict in Sudan has further exacerbated the protection situation in South Sudan, with hundreds of thousands of individuals fleeing Sudan and crossing into South Sudan. These new arrivals, many of whom enter South Sudan through already overstretched entry points like Renk and Joda, report instances of violence during their journeys, adding to the overwhelming protection needs within South Sudan. Since April 2023, over 753,939 individuals have crossed into Upper Nile state, the majority of them South Sudanese returnees (83%). Many of these individuals report high levels of trauma, depression, and stress as they face the compounded challenges of displacement and insecurity. Protection needs such as psychosocial support, legal assistance, and access to safe spaces in Upper Nile remain catastrophic, with significant gaps in services across the region. Aweil East in Northern Bahr el Ghazal also faces acute protection needs, particularly among IDPs and returnees who have been displaced due to the Sudan crisis as well as long-standing inter-communal clashes and conflict-related violence within the state. The area faces significant gaps in the provision of basic protection services, especially at crossing points and in arrival areas and settlements, further exacerbating the vulnerabilities of the displaced and host populations. These gaps in services leave large portions of the population at high risk of exploitation, violence, and neglect. In addition to the challenges posed by the Sudan crisis, South Sudan is experiencing a protracted IDP crisis. In Torit, Eastern Equatoria, which has been a focal point for displacement, communities face significant risks and the lack of access to essential protection services. The situation is compounded by a lack of adequate psychosocial support and legal assistance, leaving many vulnerable individuals without recourse for their protection needs. Protection needs in Torit are rated as catastrophic, with high levels of trauma and displacement further exacerbating the overall vulnerability of both IDPs and host communities. Other areas in Eastern Equatoria, such as Magwi, Kapoeta and Ikotos, also continue to experience significant protection challenges, particularly related to access to essential services, legal support, and psychosocial assistance. As with Torit, these areas are marked by the ongoing presence of IDPs, community violence, and significant risks of gender-based violence. To address these urgent protection gaps, humanitarian actors in South Sudan must scale-up their response efforts, focusing on increasing access to psychosocial support, legal aid, and the creation of safe spaces for women and children.

2. Project activities

In line with the Protection Cluster strategy for 2024, Acted will enhance the provision of protection services through their integration at border points in Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, and Northern Bahr el Ghazal, as well as in locations with emergency needs identified by the Acted mobile team. Acted will partner with local organizations to reach host communities, vulnerable host communities, IDPs, and returnees in these areas where there is a significant assistance gap in HLP rights, and general protection. Local partners will be integral to the success of the response, given their deep understanding of the local context and capacity to provide effective protection services. Acted will focus on addressing HLP needs in areas of concern, where land disputes and unlawful occupation are significant issues for IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable populations. Protection services related to HLP will be provided through mobile teams and local partners, offering legal assistance and dispute settlement mechanisms regarding land rights. Acted will also provide cash and voucher assistance to support vulnerable households in securing land and property and to meet shelter needs, especially for those facing particularly high protection risks. Through protection scoping assessments, including safety audits and focused group discussions, Acted will identify the protection risks faced by communities in areas such as Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, and Aweil East. In locations with emergency needs, Acted will conduct awareness-raising workshops on topics like PSEA, early child and forced marriages, and the safety of women, children, and girls. Acted will also strengthen community-based protection mechanisms (CBPMs) and networks of Protection Focal Points in areas of high need, particularly in Eastern Equatoria. These networks will provide the primary protection assistance and serve as referral points for individuals in need of specialized protection services. Protection Focal Points will be trained to identify and address urgent needs, including referrals for psychosocial support, legal assistance, and safe spaces for survivors of violence. At border points in Upper Nile and Aweil East, where protection needs are heightened due to the ongoing influx of refugees and returnees, Acted will provide individual case management for at-risk individuals, including men and boys, women, and girls, particularly survivors of violence. This case management will include psychosocial support and, where needed, the provision of dignity/MHM kits to vulnerable women and girls in reproductive age. In Upper Nile, where there are no established safe spaces, Acted will work towards setting up such centers to facilitate centralized access to protection services for women, children, and survivors of violence. In Eastern Equatoria, where community centers already exist, Acted will work closely with local partners and CBPMs to enhance the capacity of these centers to provide case management services, including referrals and awareness-raising sessions to assist at-risk individuals. Acted will also continue to build the capacity of national partners through training programs for caseworkers to ensure that they are able to respond in a timely and efficient manner to the protection needs of children and other vulnerable individuals in IDP, returnee, and host communities. In addition to these efforts, Acted will continue to provide essential NFIs for protection cases, ensuring that vulnerable individuals and those facing acute protection risks, have access to the basic items they need for dignity and survival. This includes providing hygiene kits, clothing, and other essential materials to protect vulnerable groups.
