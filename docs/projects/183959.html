Project Summary

The NRC's Livelihoods and Food Security (LFS) core competence will aim at provide an holistic response for displacement affected population including IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities, utilizing a people cantered approach paired with an evidence-based decision making process ensuring adequate response analysis in the different Area offices. NRC currently has LFS outreach in 3 different States in Sudan (i.e. Khartoum, Gadaref and South Kordofan).
The LFS core competency, towards 2022, will implement activities to ensure emergency access to food in areas with IPC level 3 and above, will support rural and agricultural systems through activities focused to enhance: small food production, smallholders' access to markets and improve natural resources management. Moreover, the core competence will support access to income generating activities and employment fostering, in particular, youth entrepreneurship and youth's labour market penetration.
In delivering its response the NRC's LFS core competency will implement its activities through a market-based approach ensuring sound response analysis and decision making; foundation of the LFS response will also be inclusive and safe programming ensuring safe participation of beneficiaries and adequate protection concepts mainstreaming.

1 - Needs

Sudan has been prone to different protracted and recurrent crises triggered by conflicts and climate induced natural hazards in its recent history. Recurrent displacement, populations’ movements, food insecurity, livelihoods disruption and increasing protection issues for host communities, IDPs and refugees are the most concerning consequences that the country is facing in the middle of political and economic turmoil and meltdown. The highly instable and unpredictable macroeconomic condition of the country is reflected in the local markets where the prices of primary goods (i.e. staple food items, non-food items, fuel, gas) and services (healthcare, transportation) soared in the last part of the 2021. Prices of staple cereals such as sorghum and millet reached highest pick in August 2020 with an increments between the200 – 230% for the sorghum and 180% for the millet in at wholesale level, with consequent dramatic impact on the households’ expenditures and access to food. The last update of the IPC report foresee 4,631,000 million people expected to face level of acute food security (IPC 3 and above); approximately 1.3 million will likely remain in IPC phase 4 (Emergency) with additional 15 million people in IPC phase 2 (Stressed) across all the states in the county. The WFP Comprehensive Food Security & Vulnerability Assessment(CSFVA) indicated that households (9% in Khartoum state, 12% in South Kordofan and 17%in White Nile) tend to adopt crisis coping strategies to cover their basic needs, including selling productive assets(houses, land, animals), reducing non-food expenses on health, withdrawing children from school, or begging.According to SKBN coordination unit humanitarian update, a shortage of seeds is also reported in South Kordofan by host communities and returnees. Closure of crossline markets and borders as COVID-19 containment measures have a detrimental effect on fuel availability and inputs. Livestock is difficult to be sold with the current markets closure and, in some South Kordofan counties, this is having a severe effect and triggering population displacement to government-controlled areas in search for food. NRC rapid needs assessment in Open Areas in Khartoum shows that more than 75% of the refugee population struggle to access enough food to cover the needs of the family. The livelihoods and well-being in the community is worse now compared to last year, with daily labourers and those working in the construction sector being the most affected because of COVID-19 lockdown and movement restrictions. In times of crisis and in humanitarian settings women and girls and, more specifically, female-headed households, are more likely to reduce the quality and quantity of food consumption and adopt negative coping strategies increasing the risk of malnutrition. The COVID-19 socio-economic impact has disproportionately affected women as caregivers, while the nightly curfew has increased their risk of experiencing gender-based violence at home.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The LFS core competency, towards 2022, will implement activities to ensure (i) emergency access to food in areas with IPC level 3 and above, (ii) will support rural and agricultural systems through activities focused to enhance: small food production, smallholders' access to markets and improve natural resources management through the provision of commodity vouchers. Moreover, the core competence will support access to income generating activities and employment fostering, in particular, (iii) youth entrepreneurship and youth's labour market penetration. In delivering its response the NRC's LFS core competency will implement its activities through a market-based approach ensuring sound response analysis and decision making; foundation of the LFS response will also be inclusive and safe programming ensuring safe participation of beneficiaries and adequate protection concepts mainstreaming.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Assessments in South Kordofan, Khartoum and White Nile states, conducted by NRC indicated that communities already depend on natural resources for energy, income (charcoal and firewood), wild fruits, farming and grazing of the livestock. The FSL project plans to engage targeted groups in food production, mainly agricultural activities. These activities if unchecked may have negative impacts on theenvironment and continue to affect climate change. The project will promote climate smart agricultural practices through its collaboration with the extension staff from the State Ministry of Production and Economic Resources. By analysing the baseline practices that have an impact on the environment through the various assessments, the project has understood practices that require mitigation measures to reduce their impact. The project will work with women, men, girls and boys, local leadership and authorities, engaging them in the identification of the problems and designing the mitigation challenges. NRC held initial meetings with the Reducing Emissions from Deforestation and Forest Degradation (REDD+) unit under the Forest National Cooperation (FNC) which is part of the federal ministry of Agriculture and Natural Resources. The meetings aimed at identifying areas of collaboration to promote community-based forest management initiatives. The project will additionallyprovide inputs for community mitigation measures such as reforestation, gully reclamation, and planting of fruit trees after consultations with the target communities and authorities. Solar powered systems will be prioritized for water facilities, energy elements both at household and communal levels and infrastructure in general (schools). The project will additionally explore environmentally friendly cooking and fuel options.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

NRC practices safe programming across all its projects through training of project staff to be able to engage with communities in identifying protection issues for the different gender and age groups. Awareness raising within the communities is carried out to enable them to understand protection issues that can affect the different people by gender, age and displacement status and reporting mechanisms. Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) training is mandatory for all NRC staff and thisis cascaded to casual labourers, incentive staff and project management committees. Basic do no harm principle and accountability to affected populations will be upheld to ensure all aspects of the project take into consideration the needs of the elderly, the sick, people with disabilities, women, youth, men and children. The protection environment is not sufficiently safe or stable. The project has therefore taken into consideration risk analysis and mitigation measures in order to reduce the impact.Target communities were involved in identification of protection and security risks in their locations and this analysis has been captured in NRC's area and country strategies. These risk assessments remain living documents as they are continuously updated during specific periods of the year and when certain events take place in an area. The project will set up complaints and feedback mechanisms in collaboration with the target groups in order to enable them report on issues including PSEA and otherprotection concerns. Two-way communication will be established between NRC and the target communities using diverse methods in order to capture issues from all groups. The project will additionally be be compliant with humanitarian standards.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will conduct community mobilization and sensitization to inform the target communities about the assistance that will be provided. Feedback on how target beneficiaries prefer to receive theassistance will be recorded and used to inform the modalities and response strategies. The project has a complaints and feedback handling mechanism that include different ways of collecting information from target community members including people living with disabilities, those who are illiterate, women and girls. The mechanism provides for anonymous complaints and feedback to be captured and uses different media and methods that the affected can choose to use. The feedback mechanismwill allow the project to adapt project assistance based on information received. Monitoring and evaluation activities will measure if beneficiaries felt well-informed and respected during project delivery and their overall satisfaction with the project. The evaluation will reflect how the project tracked and responded to beneficiary complaints and how the feedback shaped the project delivery. NRC will establish a mass communication platform using SMS text and voice messages. The platform will allowtwo-way communication with target beneficiaries.
