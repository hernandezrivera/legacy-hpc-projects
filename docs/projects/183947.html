Project Summary

As of August 2021, Sudan hosts an estimated 1,111,329 refugees and asylum-seekers from the Central African Republic, Chad, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Somalia, South Sudan, Syria, Yemen, among others. The main refugee-hosting states are Khartoum, White Nile, Kassala, South and East Darfur, as well as West and South Kordofan. South Sudanese refugees are the largest refugee population, with a recorded number of 786,524(70.8%) refugees living across all states. Most of the current overall refugee population (70%) are living outside of official camps. Out-of-camp settlements include large collective self-settlements, communities that are integrated with the host community, and urban areas. Many out-of-camp settlements are in remote and underdeveloped areas where resources, infrastructure, and basic services are extremely limited. In the camps, the assistance provided is modest and, in some cases, does not meet minimum living standards.
Basic Vulnerability Index (BVI) analysis conducted by UNHCR show that at least one-third of refugees had high levels of overall vulnerability in all states, except for Sennar Most refugees in Sudan have moderate to high levels of overall vulnerability, with at least one third in every state experiencing high levels of overall vulnerability. In Kassala, White Nile, East Darfur, and West Kordofan, the majority of both refugees and host communities experience high overall vulnerability. Meanwhile, in Blue Nile, North Darfur, South Darfur, and North Kordofan refugees are more likely to experience higher overall vulnerability. • Overall, refugees in-camp/camp-like situations experience higher levels of overall vulnerability compared to those settled out-of-camp. The highest proportion of refugees in camp/camp-like situations with high to severe levels of vulnerability are identified in Blue Nile, Khartoum, North Darfur, South Darfur, and North Kordofan.. Based on BVI analysis, WASH, Shelter & Energy, and protection vulnerability is higher while Health and Nutrition, Education, and Food security are moderate however, still support is required. Across all assessed states, West Kordofan and East Darfur had the highest level of vulnerability for both refugees and the host community. This means that the overall population in these states had high levels of vulnerability across the assessed sectors and sub-indicators. Refuges are highly vulnerable and need multi sectoral interventions to address their vulnerabilities, and to maintain and fulfil their rights. They are mostly coming to Sudan with poor health conditions with little to no personnel belongings with them and who are in urgent need of protection, WASH, nutrition, shelter & health support.
The majority of the affected & target population are women & children. 53% of the overall refuges are women & children who are arriving in poor health conditions after walking days to cross in to Sudan and have little to no personnel belongings with them. Refuges are in urgent need of protection, WASH, nutrition, shelter & health support.
The project will seek to provide live saving interventions & sustain services of WASH, Health & Nutrition through safe, clean water & adequate sanitation facilities, quality primary health care, preventive & curative nutritional intervention . The services will be provided in a holistic & collective manner to enhance peaceful coexistence between refuges & host community & improving the peoples’ wellbeing. Gender, protection & accountability will be mainstreamed throughout the implementation. CIS with national partners will work in coordinated manner to provide the needed support, staff will participate & attend coordination meetings at different levels to provide assistance in coordinated & collaborative manner

1 - Needs

Sudan host over 1,111,329 refugees & asylums (protracted and new arrivals). CARE target areas host big numbers of refuges including Khartoum, South Kordofan, East Darfur, Kassala and Gadaref States. Refuges are highly vulnerable and need urgent live saving assistance to address their vulnerabilities, needs and unmet gaps. 53% of the registered households are female headed and 1% are child headed. Despite progress made significant gaps remain & exacerbated by funding gaps, economic crises including shortages of fuel, cash, medicines and fragile health system. Over 30% of the refuges are living in camps where they relay on assistance provided through humanitarian partners. Although progress has been made to achieve minimum assistance standards in camps, gaps remain across sectors. The majority of refuges children remain out of schools, water availability and latrines are below standards with high open defecation reported and access to health services is inconsistence associated with supply gaps in basic medicines and lack of qualified staff. There is a high food insecurity, this compounded by limiting access to income generation opportunities and prevalence of child labor, early marriage and other child protection issues as well as high risks of SGBV. Most refugees live in out-of-camp settlements, host communities and urban areas, while 33% population stay in 24 camps (10 at the East Sudan, 1 at Blue Nile State 10 at White Nile State, 2 at East Darfur State and 1 at the Central Darfur State) in protracted refugee situation and they need humanitarian assistance. They lack access to sufficient services and infrastructure, with WASH, Health, Education facilitations need urgent rehabilitation and expansion. They are expose to protection risks and exploitation including SGBV by the criminal networks who are active in Eastern Sudan and involved in human smuggling and people trafficking. Also, in other parts of the country (Khartoum, Darfur) thousands of refugees are living in poor situation, seeking for livelihood opportunities and humanitarian assistance. New arrivals continued to arrive through different crossing points along the borders of South Sudan, Eritrea, Ethiopia, CAR etc. In 2021 around 92,087 refugees arrive from neighboring countries and need humanitarian assistance. In 2022, this number is expected to be increased significantly. In Early November 2020 armed conflict erupted in Ethiopia in Tigray’s region. Accordingly, Sudan received big influxes of refuges who arrived in poor health situation, non or less personal possessions and frighten seeking safety, security and live saving humanitarian assistance. There is gap in all sectors and there is high need to provide humanitarian live saving assistance of all sectors and in all areas including entry points, transits locations, reception centers and settlements. (For more information please refer to the Refugee Response Plan Chapter and the Ethiopian Response Plan). CARE will provide refugees with needed assistance in East Darfur, South Kordofan, Kassala and Gedaref States.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

CARE recognizes that water and sanitation, health and nutrition response programs and livelihoods diverse action interventions do have impact on the health of the public, environment and natural resources. Interventions to preserve and protect the environment will be included from the analysis, design phase, implementation, monitoring & evaluation of each program intervention. The mitigation measures could include but not limited to: analysis of the potential impact of key interventions and discussion with community leaders and sector experts; regular joint water and sanitation facilities monitoring and supervision; emphasis on environmental education and capacity building for increased environmental awareness among beneficiaries and implementing partners; solid and liquid waste management; actions to avoid water pollution due to excavation, backfilling and concreting, hauling and damping of earth materials; controlling formation of stagnant water pool to avoid soil erosion and breeding mosquitoes, use sustainably energy sources particularly Solar system that last long. Health and nutrition facilities measures include cleaning campaigns, quality and monitored incinerators, and refresher training to health professionals, WASH experts and community volunteers. Youth, women, men and others will be organized into water user’s committees, hygiene promoters, management groups, health workers, committees & volunteers, nutrition volunteers, Livelihood committees and groups where appropriate and tailored training will be provided to contribute positively to the management of natural resources and peaceful coexistence. Environmental impact assessment will be carried out if interventions require to do so and will be decided with sector ministries

6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE will take extra caution in analyzing the protection of at risk groups, including children and girls. Efforts will include: no creation of new risks, address discrimination, analysis of the most vulnerable, safety and dignity in service delivery, orientation and familiarization on humanitarian principles and rights, and promoting empowerment. Furthermore, CARE and partners will exercise principles of protection mainstreaming: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of the communities about the best way to deliver the services and considerations for vulnerable groups, accountability mechanisms and Field visits. CARE and partners will empower communities by training & capacity building & awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consulting them in decision making process, identifying volunteers and establishing and training community committees. Strengthening community protection mechanisms and improving access to services through information dissemination on available services, good selection criteria and identified action of the most vulnerable and referrals will be prioritized. Do no harm analysis will be carried for each sector and the activities will be screened not to enhance risks for all especially women, girls and children. Facilities such as latrines and water points, income generating activities and trainings will be analyzed and discussed with at risk groups to ensure that the beneficiaries are protected and that the services are easily accessible and safely usable by all. Protection risks faced by women and girls, and in particular S/GBV risks, will be the specific focus of capacity building for staff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. Staff will also be oriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm and that they are able to respond safely and appropriately to protection cases when they are reported, using a confidential and survivor centered approach to disclosures of violence. An internal mechanism for reporting complaints and ensuring that required measure are in place will be promoted throughout the implementation period.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARE and its partners will take into consideration information sharing of the project and will get feedback of beneficiaries in the design of the activities and make deliberate efforts to incorporate their views and concerns throughout the programming cycle. To ensure accountability within the project, the staff and local government experts working under each proposed sector will be trained on accountability to program participants and to community leaders. CARE’s recent efforts to employ different accountability mechanisms such as a toll free number, rapid community review and reflection, and community score card represent important steps towards the project’s success as the beneficiaries will be able to provide direct feedback to service providers and suggest solutions to CARE and partners staff. The communities will receive orientation and familiarization sessions on CFRM in general, and on community meetings and forums such as women’s groups, men’s groups, Water User Committees, other project structures, Community Committees, nutrition and health workers’ meetings. Local communities and beneficiaries will be consulted on the design, implementation and monitoring of complaints-handling processes. Local committees in each project location will have two CFRM focal points (a woman and a man) to lead the initiative. This will enable beneficiaries in the targeted areas to safely report any concerns or provide feedback on the project. The participation of affected populations throughout the project period is fundamental in the programmatic approach.
