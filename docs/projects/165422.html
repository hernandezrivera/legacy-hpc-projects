Project Summary

QRCS aims to continue integrating nutrition-related activities into the primary & secondary healthcare facilities and community levels are currently operating. The intervention is intended to support the nutritional status for children under 5 years of age as well pregnant and lactating women across northwest Syria through the integration of the preventive and curative nutrition-related activities into primary and secondary healthcare facilities across NW Syria that are operated by QRCS in Idleb governorate (Darkosh, Zardana, Janudeya, Fos-Zuf, and Salqin), and rural Aleppo (Al-Bab, Darat Azza, Anjara). The service package entails:
1- Facility-based and outreach messaging about infant and young child feeding IYCF practices.
2- Nutritional assessment for pregnant and lactating women and children under 5 years of age (Application of mid-upper arm circumference MUAC and Z-scores among other assessment tools) and referral of undernourished cases to the appropriate facility as needed.
3- nutrition counseling for pregnant and lactating women, in terms of the infant and young child feeding practices for children 0-23 months.
5- Outpatient and inpatient management of moderate and severe acute malnutrition cases of children under 5 years of age.
6- Deployment of rapid response teams and nutrition mobile clinics to detect malnutrition cases and provide treatment.

4 - Needs

According to the Northwest Syria humanitarian readiness and response plan (31 Jan.2020), around 800,000 people have been displaced since May 2019 and over 2.8 million people in northwest Syria require humanitarian assistance. Some 520,000 people – around 80% of whom are women and children- have been displaced since the 1st of December 2019 and 280,000 at risk of displacement from areas around the M4 and M5 highways. Outbreaks of diseases, lack of health services and an increase in underlying acute and chronic malnutrition cause and concerns are the main potential risk will be affected IDPs and host communities in targeted areas. As per the drafted 2020 HNO report for Syria, there is insufficient access to curative and preventive nutrition services, requiring interventions to be delivered at scale, especially skilled maternal, infant and young child nutrition support. The coverage of nutrition services continues to be insufficient due to limited available resources and need to be immediately scaled up, especially infant and young child feeding and maternal nutrition interventions. Lack of access to health services and harmful coping strategies such as meal reduction are some of the causes linked to deteriorating maternal nutrition. Infant and young child feeding practices remain poor in Syria, with exclusive breastfeeding rates below pre-crisis levels at 24 percent in 11 governorates. Barriers to appropriate feeding of young children include breastfeeding difficulties, illness of the mother, affordability, and lack of support from family. Coverage of IYCF services are largely awareness-raising messages rather than the needed specialized services such as one-to-one counseling to better support pregnant women and caregivers on maternal nutrition and to safely feed their young children during the critical window of 0-24 months of age. The capacity of health and nutrition practitioners in specialized IYCF skills needs to be strengthened on a national scale.

5 - Implementation of activities

Outcome: IDPs and host communities have access to life-saving essential nutrition services to reduce malnutrition-related mortality and morbidity and create positive behavior change at the community level.Output: Support One SC, 6 OTP, and 3 nutrition mobile clinics and 24 community outreach teams will be provided the life-saving and essential nutrition services integrated with health services for IDPs (pregnant and lactating women, and children boys and girls) under five years who will receive the nutation supplementations.Activities: 1-Recruitment SC, OTP staff and 160 CHWs with concentrating on women staff2- Training SC, OTP, mobile clinics staff and CHWs based on nutrition cluster recommendations, and guidelines 3- Coordination with nutrition cluster for locations of nutrition activities and intervention, also to request for nutrition supplies and commodities.3- Delivery of nutrition supplies, such as HEB, Plumpy Doz, micronutrient supplementations, RUTF and RUSF.4- Nutrition screening of children under five (5) boys and girls and pregnant and lactating women and referral of acutely malnourished cases for treatment to CMAM centers, and analyses the screening data for programmatic decision making.5- Blanket Supplementary Feeding for children girls and boys from 6-59 months to prevent acute malnutrition and micronutrient deficiencies. 6- Micronutrient Intervention targeting Children 6-59 months (boys and girls)and PLW through the distribution of micronutrient powder and tablet in collaboration with the health sector.7- Provision of CMAM interventions through OTP, mobile clinics and complicated SAM cases at SC in Al-Bab maternal and child hospital.8- Support for infant and young child feeding including breastfeeding promotion, mainly through counseling and outreach nutrition awareness sessions.9- Monitor the quality of the nutrition services provided in the targeted health facilities usage of nutritional supplies and perform routine inventory checks.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Throughout the course of the Syria crisis, provision of life-saving activities by the health actors across Syria has been continuously confronted by a wide array of protection challenges that hindered their effort and extending beyond the scope of direct attacks on health personnel and health facilities.- Gender Based Violence GBV:Mitigating measures:- Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package. - Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services. - Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA:Mitigating measures:- A PSEA policy describes standard of behavior for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. - Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. - Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. - Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption #1 Cross-border operations and humanitarian space are sufficiently preserved to allow implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. Risk/s: Security incidents impacting border-crossings, terror attacks, cross-border shelling or clashes on Turkish soil. Consequences: Possible damage to relations with communities. Complications with assists transfer and delivery including medical consumables and pharmaceutical items Delays or stoppage of programming in multiple areas Mitigations: Options for procuring the needed supplies from inside Syria. Explore alternate official border crossings. Maintain good relations with local communities’ Secure stock in QRCS warehouses for emergency measures. Assumption #2 The safety and security situation remained relatively stable in the project implementation areas in a way that allows staff to travel to and operate sufficiently. Risk/s: Increased suspicion/tension or armed clashes between the parties to the conflict. Airstrike raids or shelling bombardment. Consequences: Death or serious injury to the staff or the beneficiaries. Mitigations: Delays/stoppage of programming Reduce exposure to areas that are known to be targeted Ensure contingency plans are in place for cases of aerial bombardment and indirect fire Ensure that the facility has a safe room equipped as a shelter for the staff in case of conflict-related incidents. Assumption #3 Targeted populations maintain adequate levels of cooperation, commitment and willingness to get engaged in the project activities. Risk/s: Rise of anti-NGO activities and loss of acceptance within the targeted communities. Mitigations: Ensure that feedback and accountability mechanisms are in place Ensure stronger communications and relations with the local civil authorities.
