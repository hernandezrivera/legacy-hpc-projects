Project Summary

In line with the South Sudan HRP and cluster objectives 2 and 3, the project will reach 33,000 (6,717 Men, 8,703 Women, 7,911 Boys and 9,669 Girls) conflict and natural disaster affected people in Fashoda County of Upper Nile state and Magwi county of Eastern Equatoria state. Activities in Fashoda will be done in coordination with WFP to complement the food assistance with activities that focus to protect livelihoods and reduce the household level food consumption gaps of the people. In Magwi, the activities will focus on resilience building support and disaster risk reduction strategies to mitigate the effects of climate change such as long dry spells and some actions to protect livelihoods and reduce household level food consumption gaps of the people. Assessments have shown that women (incl. PLW), children (incl. children under 5), PWD, elderly and chronically ill are impacted differently and are bearing the brunt of conflict droughts/floods and rise in food prices worsening food insecurity. The project will therefore target these most vulnerable groups through inclusive assistance, and will ensure that protection, gender and conflict sensitivity is mainstreamed in the proposed activities. Protection will be mainstreamed throughout the project cycle to ensure beneficiary safety & dignity, avoiding doing harm as result of the intervention, providing meaningful access to all sets of beneficiaries and ensuring all groups are actively involved and participate in the project in an inclusive manner.
Cordaid has been responding to the increasing humanitarian needs in the 2 areas providing WASH, FSL and Health assistance and is fully operational in the 2 areas with offices in Nimule, Magwi and Kodok, Fashoda.

1. Needs

Nearly, 300,000 people, mostly South Sudanese have returned to South Sudan since the Sudan crises started in April 2023. More people are expected to arrive as the fighting continues. South Sudan refugees in Uganda have continued to return to South Sudan since the start of this year into boarder locations such as Magwi. The economic woe of the country continues to bite the citizens with dramatic increase in food prices as result of disrupted markets and trade routes caused by the crises in Sudan and low productivity due to climate change issues such as flooding and long dry spells, thereby worsening food insecurity. According to the recently released IPC analysis, 5.83 million people (about 46% of the total population) are experiencing high levels of food insecurity classified as IPC 3 or above (crises or worse), with 1.64 million people in IPC phase 4 (emergency). An estimated 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the on-going conflict in Sudan are classified in IPC phase 5 (catastrophe). Magwi has witnessed an influx of refugee returnees from Uganda where over 15,000 people have been registered in the past months. The long dry spells experienced in some parts of Magwi has resulted into crop failure low crop production yet, some parts experienced heavy rainfalls which destroyed their farmlands and properties (Rapid assessment, June 2023). According to the updated FSL severity, Magwi county is in a severe intersectoral phase. The IPC analysis shows that during harvest/post-harvest period between December 2023 to March 2024, the people in Magwi will face stressed (IPC phase 2) acute food insecurity situation which will further deteriorate during the peak of the lean season between April to July 2024 to crises (IPC phase 3) food insecurity. These therefore requires resilience building support and disaster risk reduction strategies to mitigate the effects of climate change like drought and action to protect livelihoods and reduce household level food consumption gaps for the vulnerable households and the refugee returnees.Meanwhile, the protracted negative impacts of conflict, expected below to significantly below average harvest, and the large-scale influx of South Sudanese returnees as result of the on-going conflict in Sudan will remain the greatest concern in Fashoda. The arrival of the returnees has driven up food assistance needs, increased competition over the limited resources and has contributed to an increase in households facing large food consumption gaps. The IPC analysis report project Fashoda in Emergency (IPC phase 4) food insecurity situation between December 2023 to March 2024 during the harvest/post-harvest period and will remain in the IPC phase 4 classification during the peak of the lean season between April to July 2024. The situation is further exacerbated by flooding which displaced over 2,800 people destroying houses and properties. The FSL severity classifies Fashoda as priority phase 1 of extreme intersectoral severity. These therefore requires urgent food security interventions to protect their livelihoods and reduce the household level consumption gaps including food assistance and multi-sectoral response to impede the collapse of people's livelihoods in the county.Women, children, the elderly, and Persons with special needs will particularly be targeted in this project because they are disproportionately affected during crises such as floods, droughts (long dry spells), and conflicts which have been proven to have great linkage to food insecurity. Gender also plays a significant role in the food security status of households with women headed households slightly more food insecure across South Sudan. Cordaid and her partners have presence in the project locations. In Magwi, Cordaid has offices in Nimule and Magwi as well as ACH. In Fashoda, Cordaid has offices whereas ADA will implement activities in Fashoda through their offices in Malakal and Renk.

2. Project activities

In line with the FSLC 2024-2025 overall HRP objective, the project will prioritize cluster objective 2 (CO2) and cluster objective 3 (CO3) to contribute to the HRP strategic objective 2 and 3 respectively. Gender, age and Persons with Disability (PWDs) will be prioritized in the response while working in a coordination especially with WFP in Fashoda to complement the food assistance with actions that protect livelihoods because during crises, different genders, age groups and PWDs are disproportionately affected.
