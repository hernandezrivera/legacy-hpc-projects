Project Summary

Needs: The intersectoral severity of needs according to the HNO 2024 is extreme (level 4) for almost all districts in NWS. The top five priority needs reported by households were food, winterization support, household NFIs, access to health care, and hygiene items, linked to sharp price increases for food, rent, and water.
Activities: Implementation of a multi-sectoral emergency response in NW Syria for IDPs and host communities. Results: 1) Shelter and Settlement, incl. rehabilitation, winterization cash, and community projects for ca. 5000 HH; 2) Emergency IGA and Livelihood, WHH, SBG, and community projects related to agriculture and food supply chains. Protection is mainstreamed, including. Community based protection committees to carry our protection mainstreaming for the beneficiaries of the related result. Total beneficiaries: 24.860 individuals. PWD will be consulted in shelter rehab and community projects.
PRA, incl. PSEAH; R1: Violation of PSEAH or CSG, CoC principles by staff. M1. Anti-corruption policies in place; CoC policies and training provided; Complaint and investigation mechanisms are operational; Presence of PSEAH FPs, trainings, visibility materials. R2) Risk of not reaching the most impacted members of the target population M2)clearly set eligibility criteria and tools and have adequate MoV;CBP committees as entry points, also for referrals; CRM mechanisms operational for BNF to contact the organization; Targeting those at heightened risk (intersectionality, vulnerability);Distribution systems are consistent, accessible by all groups and knownR3) Risk that BNF are not reaching durable solutions and can become vulnerable again M3) Linking BNF with livelihoods opportunities; Exit strategies in place. R4) lack of acceptable documentation and lack in means of obtaining them among BNF M4) Safe and informed referrals are made to legal actors. R5) increased tensions and GBV incidents M5) Women and other at-risk groups are involved in the design of programming; ensure at-risk groups have the same access to services to avoid tensions in the community; capacitated staff. R7) Certain groups can’t access the distribution/redemption centers/locations of sessions due to barriers M7)Venues are evaluated to be central and physically accessible; Staff trained on inclusion principles. R8) Due to deterioration of the economic and/or security situation increase in forced evictions M8) Linking those affected with reception centers;Provision of cash/voucher for eligible cases. R9) Psychological distress due to conflict and worsened living conditions M9) Staff trained to refer BNFs with additional PSS needs M9) Provision of PSS groups in communities M9) Psychoeducation sessions provided
Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating R1)Civil unrest: M1) 1. Hiring locals who are fully aware of the context. 2. acceptance in the targeted community. 3. Maintain sharing information. 4. Movement restrictions. R2)Access prevention by the local authorities: local authorities are dissatisfied or consider I/NGO a risk activities M1) 1. coordination with local authorities (including AFAD) 2. Community engagement R3)Border closed due to acts of war. M3) Measures are partly carried out by vouchers, during border closure, only in-kind measures with procurement outside Syria affected. R4) Military actions of the civil war parties force partners to cease their activities. M4) existence of appropriate protocols and shifting to other locations R5)Strong fluctuations of the SYP and TRY price in relation to EUR/USD. M5)Procurement within the TUR, including lines in the contract to allow price negotiations with the suppliers R6) Aid diversion/ Authorities’ intervention: M6) 1. Strict commitment to the humanitarian principles and closely following Joint Operating Principles including suspension of activities when an entity tries to intervene to manipulate the activities. 2. Strong procurement procedures incl. vetting of all staff and vendors 3. CRM system
