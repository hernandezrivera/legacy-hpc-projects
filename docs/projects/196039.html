Project Summary

This proposed project is designed to address the shelter and NFI vulnerability in the Rohingya makeshifts and the host community. IRB will assist to Rohingyas community as well as the host community by providing durable and sustainable shelter materials and will build some shelters for EVI households. Optimum shelter locations in the camps will be selected in coordination with the CIC and shelter sector. Based on the gap analysis and subsequent discussions with the sector and other actors IRB is planning to implement the shelter and NFI support activities in camps 3, 4, and 7. Host community activities will be implemented in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilla. To address the needs of vulnerable populations, IRB will continue providing shelter and NFI materials to refugee/ Rohingya households in Cox’s Bazar. Shelter support will include improved shelter designs with a focus on Disaster Risk and options for durable materials. IRB will support Transitional Shelter (TSA) to increase the shelter's durability and minimize the risk of shelter damage from extreme weather. IRB will organize training /orientation for the beneficiaries before distributing the TSA materials. Training will focus on elevating treated bamboo columns off the ground and proper metal footing installation. Training will emphasize DRR measures such as building household-level drainage, bracing corners, installing tie-down kits, and fire protection in cooking areas. As Shelter repairing & Maintenance with shelter materials distributions, IRB will provide Porter support and construction labour for verified HHs (PwD, elderly, pregnant, widow/female-headed HHs). The diverse group of people who do not have the ability to construct/repair/maintain their own houses will be included for this porter and labour support. In addition, skilled volunteers trained in TSA 1 will monitor households daily and provide technical advice to encourage beneficiaries for using the materials as intended. 1500 Transitional Shelter (TSA) will be built for the most vulnerable families from Rohingya camps. Among them 800 TSA will be built in camp 3, 400 TSA will be built in camp 4 and 300 TSA will be built in camp 7.
IRB will provide Care and Maintenance materials to 4000 households in three camps (2000 HHs in camp 3, 1000 HHs in camp 4, and 1000 HHs in camp 7). Shelters that are not targeted for TSA 1 distributions, will receive Care and Maintenance materials and will be jointly assessed by Shelter/NFI Sector, UNHCR, IRB staff, and volunteers. Besides this, IRB will provide 100 Mid-term shelter assistance in camp 3. Households will be identified and verified by SMS, and then technically assessed by IRB.
The project will build 65 permanent shelters for the most vulnerable families in the host community areas in Ukhia and Teknaf Upazila. Among the 65 HC shelters, 45 shelters will be constructed in Ukhia Upazila (Haldia Palong union-15, Ratna Palong union-15 & Raja Palong Union-15) and 20 shelters will be constructed in Whykong Union under Teknaf Upazila. In coordination with UNHCR, NFI distribution will be completed in UNHCR camps according to sector’s guidance, recommendation and needs. IRB has a plan for NFIs distribution among 1750 (800 in camp-3, 600 in camp-4 and 350 in camp 7) extremely vulnerable Rohingya families living in respective camps and another 1250 (800 HHs in Ukhia and 450 HHs in Teknaf HC area) NFI packages for the vulnerable host households in the host communities in Cox’s Bazar district. NFI distribution will be coordinated with UNHCR. This proposed project is initially designed for JRP -2023 and continuity will be dependent on the availability of donor funds for 2024-2025.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Following the gap analysis, Islamic Relief Bangladesh will assess the needs in the respective camps and deliver assistance in collaboration with the sector and camp management. IRB will continue to work toward temporary shelter solutions to ensure protection and reduce fire risks as well as impacts on the environment. IRB will implement three types of activities 1. Shelter improvement, 2. Regular repair and maintenance support and 3. Household-level site development in Camp 03, 04, and 07.Improved shelter activities will include transitional shelter, Mid-Term Shelter, Steel frame Shelter, and Shelter with TSA support. As different materials are being used for the different types of improved shelter, the quality also differs and most of them still require regular repair and maintenance. After getting gap analysis from the Shelter/NFI sector, current gaps are found in the three camps (Camp 3, camp 4 and camp 7). For JRP-2023, 8183 shelters need to be improved in camp 03, 6308 shelters need to be improved in camp 04 and 7921 shelters need to be improved in camp 07. Here “Shelters need to be improved” is considered as the GAP in the camp.Regular Shelter assistance is needed for these three camps. UNHCR is providing assistance for these camps but they could not cover a blanket assessment and distribution due to lack of funding. For JRP-2023, 4873 shelters required regular repair and maintenance Support in camp 03, 6659 shelters required regular repair and maintenance Support in camp 04 and 5010 shelters required regular repair and maintenance Support in camp 07. Household level Site Development work is also needed for camp 03, camp 04 and camp 07. While providing Improved Shelter Materials, there is a need for Site Development in every household. “Shelters need to be improved” is considered as the GAP in the camp for household-level Site Development activities. These activities include slope protection, footpaths, and drains among others.

2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Following the JRP and Joint Need Assessment, Islamic Relief Bangladesh will assess the needs of the target population for JRP 2023 and deliver assistance in collaboration with the Shelter/NFI sector, site development and government. Islamic Relief Bangladesh (IRB) will continue to work toward temporary shelter solutions to ensure protection and reduce fire risks as well as impacts on the environment. To address the needs of vulnerable populations, IRB will continue providing shelter and NFI materials to refugee/ Rohingya households in Cox’s Bazar. Shelter support will include improved shelter designs with a focus on Disaster Risk and options for durable materials. The modalities chosen in this proposed project, are developed and approved at the Shelter/NFI Sector level and ISCG. IRB shall be able to mobilize/secure funding beyond 2024 for the continuation of the activities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Cross-cutting issues like Protection, gender equality, and social inclusion have been considered in this project. The Human rights-based approach will be mainstreamed with guiding principles of Non-discrimination, Participation, inclusiveness Protection etc. The best interest of children, women, and persons with disabilities will be ensured duly along with their active participation during the planning, implementation, and monitoring phases. Community-based project implementation mechanisms will be developed and strengthened. This project will ensure adequate protection measures, such as providing shelter materials and locks for homes and providing safe paths to services. Consult adolescent girls in the design, security, and management of camps and shelters. Inclusive shelter and settlement programming will enable persons with disabilities to contribute more to their communities, participate more in consultations and decision-making, and facilitate their protection.

3.2 - Gender

IRB will focus on gender equality for improving the quality of shelter/NFI facilities. Sector and Camp Management provide key guidelines and recommendations to prevent Violence against Women and girls through shelter planning and camp management will be followed accordingly. According to Camp Site Management, 47.5% of the population in Camp 3, 51% in Camp 4, and 51% in Camp 7 are female (OCHA, 2022). The aforementioned groups are particularly vulnerable to risks of abuse, exploitation, and gender-based violence. As part of shelter and site planning, it is considered the specific risks faced by women, girls, and persons with disabilities. Inclusive shelter and settlement programming will be ensured for Persons with disabilities, considering their community contributions and participation, as well as decision-making. In addition, women and girls accessibility to distribution points, as well as their mobility and access to other essential services will be ensured.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

This proposed project will consider child safeguarding issues and assess their vulnerability with special attention within the family and community such as child-headed households, separated children or children at risk in need of shelter, single-parent, large families, etc. This project will ensure the safety and security of adolescent girls and children by providing shelter assistance with separate rooms by partition fence, door locks, and keys to reduce further harm or violence. IRB strictly prohibits engaging any child in any construction work as casual labor & volunteer for cash for work purposes. To ensure equity & inclusion, disabled & elderly people, pregnant females, child-headed families/orphan child families will get priority during the NFI distribution and shelter kits distribution because of their vulnerabilities. Furthermore, considering labor and porter cash for work engagement, IRB will follow the standard guidelines to engage people aged between 18 to 59 years.

3.4 - Diversity

IRB’s shelter program will incorporate a gender diversity perspective into the design and implementation of the project. Men and women are not uniform groups of people; therefore, it is necessary to disaggregate between different groups of men, women, LGBTQ, and people with disabilities in terms of their diversity, their needs, and their vulnerabilities in the Rohingya crisis. IRB will include diversity in the project’s beneficiary selection, cash for worker selection, and shelter & NFI distribution activities. Porter and labor support will be provided to HHs based on the SNFI Sector EVHH criteria regardless of ethnic, social, gender status. IRB is a step closer to gender equality and diversity when the rights, responsibilities, treatment, and opportunities of men, women, boys, and girls no longer depend on their sex or their perceived gender role. IRB is committed to enhancing diversity and implementing the GAM as one tool to promote gender equality programming in the shelter sector.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) mainstreaming will be followed during this project implementation. To ensure transparency and accountability of relevant stakeholders, particularly beneficiaries, the project will apply the Complaints Feedback Mechanism (CFM). The complaint box and festoon with Cell Phone numbers will be set in the targeted communities, sites, and public safer places in their language. This mechanism will create opportunities for the beneficiaries/ stakeholders to come up with their concerns/complaints which will be considered by the project during/ before finalizing the selection of appropriate beneficiaries by maintaining an organizational complaint-resolving process. For effective CFM, a CFM box will be established at the CIC office, Distribution point, and other important places. Besides, at the project office, it will use the local CFM register book. Moreover, an orientation session will be provided to the selected beneficiaries on effective CFM.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, this project has considered DRR elements & environmental issues during the project design. During shelter construction, IRB will follow MTS guidelines including shelter-strengthening techniques such as providing metal footing, cross-bracing, tie-down etc. Provision of the HH level site improvement through this project will increase beneficiary’s resilience. Reinforcing walls and drainage around the shelter will contribute to the safety of beneficiaries.
