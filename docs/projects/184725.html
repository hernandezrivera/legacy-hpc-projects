Project Summary

Somalia is facing prolonged effects of climatic shocks, conflicts, political unrest and impact of Covid-19 that has affected people’s livelihoods. According to FSNAU post Gu assessment released in Sep 2021, there were below average rains in the previous season and projections indicate that Deyr rains will be below average in most areas of Somalia. 7.7 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance including 6.5M that require health related services. According to HNO 2022, overall health needs are likely to remain insufficiently addressed. Health services have eroded to bare minimum levels in many locations as health partners face severe funding gaps. Health service delivery in Somalia remains dependent on humanitarian partners who finance and deliver a large proportion of health care to the population. In the absence of sufficient funding there is a danger of erosion of minimum standards of care of primary health care services.
Buurhakaba is among the hard-to-reach areas with population of 172,800. The access is very minimal with limited humanitarian partners working in it. QC has been working in it, providing essential and reproductive health services. Hakaba referral health center has been functioning under the support of QC since 2018, providing health services to the people in the district. The overall humanitarian outlook in the district is alarming in which HNO estimated that people in need (PIN) is 92,281 persons. Among this, 52,050 people are expected to be in (IPC3&4) at the end of Dec 2021. As FSNU reported, total acute malnutrition burden in Buurhakaba shows 3.1 % of Children had severe acute malnutrition and 19.1% had moderate acute malnutrition.
QC will implement a life-saving integrated health intervention in Buurhakaba through ONE static and 3 mobile Health clinics to increase access to basic health services. QC will provide primary and referral health care with special attention to maternal and child health, including (BEmONC) and (CEmONC) and expanded Program on Immunization. The project will further work to push the demand factor for health services through mobilization campaign and awareness building sessions which will be conducted by HF team and trained CHW.
The project aims to cover the health needs of crises affected people in the target location by providing curative and preventive lifesaving health services. The project will provide ANC/PNC and maternity services to the PLW and under 5 children. Outreach team will be established to reach villages far away from the HF. The team will routinely visit villages to identify, screen and assist people in need of health and nutrition support. Routine immunization will be conducted. Project will reduce morbidity and mortality caused by malnutrition through identification, referral and treatment of the children suffering. Project staff will be trained and refreshed on EENC, KMC, BEmONC, Clinical management of rape, GBV core concepts, safe and ethical referral and IMAM guidelines. Project will also work to build the capacity of CHW to promote behavior change on IYCF. Covid-19 prevention and control protocols will be trained for HF staff, health authorities and CHW.
The project will identify and assess GBV risks and identify affected persons who are most vulnerable or unable to access health services and will work to raise awareness among staff on basics of GBV, gender and women’s rights to ensure that GBV survivors can report risks and access health care in a way that does not pose a safety or ethical risk to survivors.
The project will engage people living with disability (PLWD) in an effort to include them in the health interventions and ensure equal access for all and benefit the services being provided by the health facility. The project team will identify barriers existing that prevent access and promote opportunities for meaningful participation and will work ways that encourage accessibility and inclusion and address disability-related concerns.

1. - Projects activities

1.1 Improve equitable access to quality emergency and essential lifesaving health services for the crisis affected populations living in Buurhakaba. QC will continue to expand Hakaba referral health center services and includes additional 3 mobile health teams visiting outside villages and settlements and seek to increase access to basic health services, contain recurrent disease outbreaks. QC will provide primary and referral health care with attention paid to maternal and child health, including basic emergency obstetric care (BEMONC and CEMOC). 47260 beneficiaries will be targeted during the life time of the project. The Referral Health center and mobile clinics will provide OPD consultations and treatment of endemic diseases like pneumonia, anaemia, acute watery diarrhoea and ARI, ANT, PNT, BEMONC and CEMONC, immunization services and referral pathways. 1.2 Preposition of essential medicines, medical supplies and equipment in Buurhakaba hub which will supply to both fixed and mobile teams. The hubs will always carry minimum buffer stock of three months, enabling immediate and fluid response to health emergencies in the targeted areas. 1.3 Conduct training and capacity building for health workers working in the project as well as other health officials in the district. Topic to cover on trainings are disease outbreak response, national treatment guidelines, SGBV, IPC, trauma and emergency management, maternal child healthcare, immunization, obstetric care, Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) and referral mechanism, travel health, Covid-19 prevention and management, and health promotion and education approaches. 60 health staff will be trained. 1.4 Project will work to strengthen public health through conducting health education at Hakaba referral health center and mobile outreach services at the community level. The aim is to affect behavioural change in health and hygiene practices that contribute to reduce excess morbidity and mortality, and to increase demand for preventative services.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

QC has already been in Buurkabaha providing integrated health services and links with the crisis affected people was established which will strengthen the QC capacity to extend the provision of basic health service and continue serving the vulnerable communities. QC will enforce risk mitigation polices in place to mitigate the likelihood of violence and sexual exploitation to vulnerable groups including women and girls and will organize consultations meeting with the beneficiaries to sensitize the intervention components and include them in the decision making process to familiarize protection mainstreaming mechanisms available and capture their recommendation on the best way the health services can be delivered. Complaint and feedback tools will continue to be activated during the project including hotline and staff numbers where beneficiaries can direct their concerns and QC will ensure timely and immediate responses are provided to beneficiaries when a complaint is received to enhance accountability and reduce delays. Additionally, Female staff will be assigned to work closely with female beneficiates coming to the centre and identify their health needs while maintaining highest standards of privacy and confidentiality. The female staff will also give referral services to the female survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, and explosive hazards). The project will provide psychosocial counselling and referral services to the survivors of sexual violence and will transfer such cases to GBV handling centres with confidentiality. QC team working in the health facility will be trained on protection needs of the target communities to ensure that information of the beneficiaries is kept in appropriate and confidential channels. To stress the service is delivered in a dignified way; QC will work to sensitize the health staff about the core of conduct during the duty and any misconduct or misbehaviour against the beneficiaries including sexual exploitation will have zero tolerance.To ensure that everyone is included in these health intervention, QC will engage with people with disabilities in the decision making processes (assessment, implementation and monitoring) to further identify the best way the project can reach and include them in the project activities .Also, a mobile team will be trained and they will periodically visit the patients living in remote villages who cannot access and reach the health facility for the purpose of extending the health provision to all in need and ease barriers that can potentially hamper access to the interventions.
