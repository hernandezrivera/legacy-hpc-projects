Project Summary

In Syria, the public health system has been exhausted by ten years of conflict, not only because of the destruction and damage of many health facilities, but also due to the displacement and migration of professional health workers.
With the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, rising patient numbers are adding pressure to the fragile health system. Healthcare workers are particularly exposed to COVID-19, underscoring the risk of the already-overstretched healthcare capacity to be further compromised.
Within such context, the 438,000 Palestine Refugees (PR) who remain in Syria have become increasingly reliant on UNRWA for the provision of primary healthcare services and for subsidizing access to secondary and tertiary healthcare.
In 2021, while adapting its services to the COVID-19 preventive measures in place to protect both beneficiaries and staff, UNRWA will provide over 810,000 primary health care consultations through 25 health facilities, including 2 mobile clinics to sustain the provision of medical and dental services to refugees in areas where facilities are not fully operational. Besides, UNRWA will continue to subsidize access to secondary and tertiary healthcare services (including COVID-19 treatment) for around 12,000 cases through a network of partnered hospitals and private laboratories. UNWA will also subsidize COVID-19 testing and treatment for Palestine refugees.
With regard to COVID-19 preventive measures, UNRWA will continue operating a triage system at every health center, by which patients with fever and / or respiratory symptoms are quickly identified and supported by a dedicated medical team equipped with PPE. For COVID-19 suspected cases, laboratory tests (not PCR tests) are conducted at the health center and the patient is then transferred to a public hospital for further analysis.
Beside, telemedicine will continue to be provided through a hotline set at each health center and managed by a medical officer in charge of addressing patients' medical queries. Another hotline is managed by clerks to book appointments in order to avoid overcrowding at the center. UNRWA is also regularly reaching out to the community to raise awareness about the importance of having an appointment booked before coming to the health center.
All patients visiting the health centers are required to use disinfection liquid at the entrance and to keep physical distance with one another in the center's premises. This is reminded by all health staff as well as billboards and advertisements set on the building's walls.

4 - Needs

In Syria, the public health system has been exhausted by ten years of conflict, not only because of the destruction and damage of many health facilities, but also due to the displacement and migration of professional health workers. Hence, since 2011, the ability of public and private hospitals and clinics to respond to the needs of the population has decreased, while the general cost of living, including of health services, has dramatically increased. This trend is particularly affecting vulnerable people, including most of the 438,000 Palestine Refugees (PR) who remain in Syria. Indeed, an assessment conducted in August 2020 by UNRWA on the socioeconomic impact of COVID-19 indicated that close to 40 per cent of PR households stated they or their family members had faced health challenges due to COVID-19 measures implemented in Syria, out of which the majority stated it was due to high prices of the medicines (79 per cent). Within such context, the 438,000 Palestine Refugees (PR) who remain in Syria have become increasingly reliant on UNRWA for the provision of primary healthcare services and for subsidizing access to secondary and tertiary healthcare. Indeed, an UNRWA vulnerability assessment conducted in December 2017 found that 96.3 per cent of PR do not have any kind of medical insurance other than UNRWA services. Yet, access to humanitarian life-saving reproductive, maternal, newborn and child health services are particularly critical for vulnerable groups, as pregnant women, newborns, and those under GBV risk.

5 - Implementation of activities

UNRWA will provide over 810,000 primary health care consultations through 25 health facilities including:- 23 health clinics and points located in Palestine refugee camps and gathering areas across Syria.- 2 mobile clinics to areas facing movement restrictions, affected by conflict or localized tensions, and/or locations where access to nearby clinics and health facilities is compromised. UNRWA will continue to subsidize access to secondary and tertiary healthcare services (including COVID-19 treatment) for around 12,000 cases through a network of partnered hospitals and private laboratories. The coverage provided by UNRWA will cover between 75-95 per cent of the costs, depending on the level of vulnerability and availability of funds. UNWA will also subsidize COVID-19 testing and treatment for Palestine refugees. This intervention will be extended to UNRWA staff (health and non-health) to ensure their safety and the safety of beneficiaries by containing the spread of the virus. With regards to COVID-19 preventive measures, UNRWA will ensure that health centres are equipped with Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) to protect patients and frontline staff, and that disinfectants and sanitization materials are available at all facilities. UNRWA will also sustain the home delivery of medicines to vulnerable non-communicable disease (NCD) patients, operate telemedicine lines and maintain a triage system in all its clinics for people to safely screen patients with respiratory symptoms. To avoid overcrowding at health facilities, primary healthcare consultations will also be provided through telemedicine calls, when necessary.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Exposure to Explosive remnants of war as Palestine refugees access UNRWA Health centres. Mitigating measures: 1.Training and awareness-sessions to UNRWA staff and community members is conducted; large-scale distribution of ERW awareness raising fliers is organized to enhance awareness among the community, particularly in areas with high levels of destruction. 2. Mobile health clinics and health points have been established, in close proximity to the PR communities, minimizing refugees’ movement to access the health services; immunization campaigns limit unnecessary movement. 3. Telehealth services are provided for patients which helps limit their exposure. 4. UNRWA delivers regular medication of NCD patients to their homes and can also provide them by delegation through relatives or friends.2) Safety, dignity and privacy are eroded for GBV survivors and vulnerable groups when attending health clinics due to overcrowding. Mitigating measures: 1. UNRWA is using telemedicine to reduce the number of in-person consultations at the health clinics. 2. NCD patients are receiving long term supplies of their medication to reduce the number of visits made to the health clinics. 3..Ensure numbering system and prioritization of most vulnerable; 4.Ensure private spaces are made available where possible 5.Train staff on GBV. 3) The risk of SEA of beneﬁciaries persists. Mitigation measures: 1. Enforce UNRWA PSEA policy and raise awareness about its zero-tolerance 2.Ensure community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance 3.Train staff on code of conduct, and PSEA 4.Regular reinforcement of the zero-tolerance policy as well as UNRWA staff standards of conduct, reporting obligations and channels for SEA.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1. Humanitarian needs of of PRs in Syria remain relatively stable 2. Sustained commitment of donors to support UNRWA financially 3. Security situation and active violence do not deteriorate any further. 4. COVID-19 pandemic challenges remain within the scope of the Agency's capacity to respond financially and operationally. Risk UNRWA is unable to continue providing services and respond to PR needs because of lack of funding or due to increased humanitarian needs. Last year, UNRWA received only around 30% of its requirements for Syria. Mitigation: 1. continue to monitor the situation and to adjust plans prioritizing most pressing needs 2. Regularly engage with donors and partners to leverage funding opportunities 3. Ensure robust financial, planning and management systems 4. Maintain communication with stakeholders on status of funding. 5. Continue to monitor its interventions through a results-based monitoring framework. The RBM approach to programming ensures evidence-based decision-making and accountability. Risks are also mitigated by ensuring a sound and solid monitoring of implementation. Risk UNRWA staff and PR are exposed to violence and security threats. Mitigation: 1. Monitor security updates and conduct security assessments for facilities and missions 2. Train staff and beneficiaries on ERW, and personal safety. Risk: COVID-19 pandemic situation persists/worsens, leading to further operational challenges. Mitigation: 1. UNRWA will adapt its programme modality to ensure sustained delivery of services while prioritizing the safety of staff and beneficiaries 2. PPEs will be provided to frontline staff and sanitization materials will be made available at offices and distribution centres.
