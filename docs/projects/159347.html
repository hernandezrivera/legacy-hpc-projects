Project Summary

According to August 2019 IPC Phase report, 6.35m people (54% of population) in South Sudan are classified to be IPC Phase 3 or worst acute food insecurity. An estimated 145,000 women, men and children in Northern Bahr el Ghazal will lack access to food and livelihoods in 2020 (IPC Phase 4). CEF plans to deliver a 12 months emergency food assistance and livelihoods activities to critically vulnerable households through a provision of emergency food relief and livelihoods resilience program for crisis affected people in Aweil South.
In line with the FSL cluster CO1, CO2, and CO3 and the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) SO1, SO2, and SO3, CEF is proposing to scale up response in Aweil South through distribution of dry season kits (vegetable and fishing kits), livestock vaccination, training of CAHWs, cash transfers, business creation and development, and assets creation
The overall objective of this project is to improve the food security and livelihood status of vulnerable households (returnees and residents) in Aweil South. This project is designed to reduce vulnerabilities and risk associated with the current food insecurity and poor economic conditions as a result of climatic shocks (flooding), increased instances of inter-communal violence, unfavorable terms of trade, and reduced market access due to the worsening road conditions. A total of 28,000 beneficiaries comprising of returnees and members of resident communities who may not receive assistance from WFP and FAO funded project will be targeted directly with lifesaving livelihoods and food security interventions. The project will require $288,000 to provide the intended services and activities throughout the intervention period.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is among the world countries with extreme poverty though it is blessed with variety of natural resources. With low level of agricultural productions, high percentage of the people of South Sudan live in extreme poverty compared with the people from neighboring countries. Children faces acute and severe malnutrition, the major cause of child mortality in the country. Since before independent from Sudan, people of South Sudan has been relying on food aid from WFP and other humanitarian agencies to save lives. Though the objective of saving lives is still paramount, there is a need to strengthen grassroot resilience and recovery process to build long term solution to food shortage, high morbidity and mortality, chronic poverty, and under development in South Sudan. To achieve this long-term solution, there is a need to invest resources on sustainable food security, nutrition, and sustainable community resilience to crises caused by war, diseases, and natural disasters. Though Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) remain relatively calm during the last 6 years of civil war which started in 2013, the population of NBeG according to August 2019 IPC Phase Classification report is classified at IPC Phase 4. Though projected IPC Phase classification for January 2020 to April 2020 varied from county to county, 450,000 people in NBeG will be in food crisis (IPC Phase 3) and 145,000 people will be in emergency (IPC Phase 4). Majority of people in Aweil South have agriculture-based livelihoods which was affected by climatic shocks (flooding), increased instances of inter-communal violence, unfavorable terms of trade for majority of the households, and reduced market access due to the worsening road conditions. Additionally, GAM prevalence deteriorated due to the limited access to and availability of food during this period coupled with seasonal increase in morbidity –such as malaria, and lack of adequate access to health services. Immediate action to maintain and save livelihoods in Aweil South with long lasting impact can be achieved by improving access to farming, fishing, and livestock-safeguarding. CEF will support development of local businesses, agriculture, livestock, and fisheries in Aweil South to strengthen food security in the county.

2 - Activities

1) Distribution of seeds, tools, fishing kits and quick maturing vegetable seeds. 2) Capacity building and training on demonstration fields on best management practices such as disease management, post-harvest handling, value addition, and marketing. 3) Food assistance for the vulnerable and affected population. 4) Formation of women or producer groups to be served and trained with ox plough to expand agricultural land. 6) Cash allocations for the most vulnerable people, the elderly, widows etc. 7) Procuring, operating and maintaining the cold chain for safe storage of livestock drugs. 8) Livestock vaccination and treatment on common diseases. 9) Provision of drugs and training of Community Animal Health Workers (CAHWs) on disease investigation, vaccination and treatment and best management practices to increase output. 10) training 10 groups (25 each; 75% women) on income-generating activities. 11) Provide business startup kits for each group. 12) Train community agricultural extension officers on crop production

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The project consultation meeting with beneficiaries will consider the attendance to have 50% female, 50% youth, and 15% persons with a disability during consultation and decision-making processes. Beneficiaries will be informed about this criterion to ensure voices of female, youth, and persons with disability are heard in relation to project design and implementation. The beneficiaries will be provided with information about program activities before the commencement of the project to involve them in project management, create awareness and sense of ownership, and provide accountability mechanism. CEF will ensure staff are trained on protection of persons of all age including child protection and GBV risks. CEF recognizes the community engagement and therefore will ensure participation of community members in activities at community level hence championing community protection mechanisms. CEF will train community existing protection structures such as church leaders, school administrations, and local authorities on protection principles including GBV risk and referral pathway existing in the community.CEF will work closely with local leaders and authorities to create community awareness on the targeting and selection criteria. CEF will engage some resident community and returnees’ members during the identification of project beneficiaries in order to build a strong relationship between returnees and resident community members. CEF will consider all age group and gender in the project implementation.The confidential complain mechanism will be establish and all the issues that may arise will be addressed without discrimination. CEF will position a complain desk and box in a visible area and place accountability to the affected population officer to receive complains. Complaints received will go through the case management process to generate appropriate solutions and feedback.
