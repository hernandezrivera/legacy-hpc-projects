AKF proposes a community-based approach to early childhood programming to ensure children, especially the most marginalized in Syria, have a good start in life. The purpose of the project is to scale up community-based ECD as a complementary programmatic vehicle to traditional KG models – while at the same time strengthening the institutional measures (within the UN system and government) required to coordinate and support community-based ECD. The specific objective of the project is to increase equitable access to quality education opportunities, with a special focus on quality and inclusive early childhood development for children 0-6. To achieve this, AKF will increase the capacity of communities, caregivers, and families through the establishment of community-driven ECD models to be reviewed by ALaaty, and implementation of a national system strengthening programme. Through this project, 3900 girls and boys (aged 0 to 6) from Damascus, Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Hama, Homs, Tartous, Lattakia, AlHasakeh, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-Ez-Zor, Quneitra, Dar'a, and As-Sweida, are expected to benefit from equitable access to quality early learning and essential protection services that address their developmental needs.
The project also aims to increase opportunities for 960 adolescents between the ages of 10 to 18 years (out of school or at risk of dropping out), with a particular focus on girls, PwDs and IDPs, to pursue education in a safe environment in Salamieh City. The project will work with local teachers and community groups to establish safe learning spaces to deliver non-formal education (NFE) for out of school children or those who are at risk of dropping out; develop and deliver education gender-sensitive curriculum, designed for target students, that integrates child protection, psychosocial support, special education and self-learning; and conduct awareness-raising campaigns to parents and communities about the role of NFE in providing a pathway for children to continue their education. AKF believes in the importance of work on authorities' levels and continuing professional training for teachers. Thus, the proposed intervention in Formal education for 2021 will be continued in previously proposed activities. During the last two years, AKF trained more than 170 supervisors and heads of sectors in (Hama, Idlib, Ar-Raqqa, Damascus, Rural Damascus and Tartous) and built their capacity to be professional trainers. Then the trained supervisors transferred the training on their turn to more than 2000 teachers and primary and secondary school's principals. To ensure sustainability, AKF will follow up with them through Community of practice CoP sessions and continue the professional development training for 500 male and female supervisors and teachers on three online courses (Creating an Inclusive Learning Environment, Developmental Dialogues, Facilitating an Inclusive Learning Environment Workshop)which focus on the equity, teachers' sensitivity, Classroom, Agreement, Differentiation, Individual collaborative learning, Effective facilitation, Learning objectives & sequencing, besides 5 face to face training days about blended learning and basics of IT skills.

4 - Needs

Syria is experiencing a catastrophic impact on the availability and quality of educational opportunities and weakened the education system as a whole. Children and youth are left unprotected, disconnected, and often with no access to education. One of three schools is out of service (UNICEF factsheet, March 2021), because it is damaged, destroyed or sheltering IDPs, while functioning schools are poor and crowded. Economic pressure on crisis-affected families led them to adopt negative coping mechanisms increasing out of school children and youth. Child labour and child marriage reported as frequently occurring in 22% and 18% of assessed communities, respectively (HNO 2021).COVID-19 preventive measures including closing schools and shortening the semesters left school-aged children with a gap in their knowledge compared to the original Ministry of Education plan. Besides, COVID-19 lockdown increased economic pressure leading to many children leaving schools to work to support their families.In Salamieh, according to AKF’s assessments (2020), the rate of out-of-school-children is 14.6% (6 to 8 years). Dropouts are driven by low household's economy and children disability preventing them from accessing services. 72,800 children (ages 6>18) enrolled in 121 active schools within the District. Overcrowding, poor educational support, variable teaching quality and teachers’ low salary are contributing to poor education efficiency. HHs with economic means seek auxiliary educational support which is not accessible for many due to cost barriers. Non-compulsory education in preparatory and secondary levels and a weak follow-up system on the status of drop out children worsens the problem.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims to contribute to the specific objective of improved access to basic services, electricity, water, education and health, and Sector objective of enhanced quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth through the following:1- Scaling up community-based ECD platforms for the most marginalized children in Damascus, Rural Damascus, Aleppo, Hama, Homs, Tartous, Lattakia, AlHasakeh, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-Ez-Zor, Quneitra, Dar'a, and As-Sweida through the following key activities: (1) establishment of 20 Family Centers (FCs) to offer a basic package of child development programmes; (2) establishment of 15 Community Based Child Centre (CBCC) to provide enhanced non-formal education.2- Enhancing a community-based NFE platforms in Salamieh District as follows: i) Develop a curriculum for out of school children or at risk of dropping out which is gender-sensitive and based on UNICEF’s standard operating procedure for Non-Formal Education (NFE); ii) Establish Community Consultative Group (CCG) (50% F) who will select 8 (4F) Lead Teachers (LTs) and establish 4 NFE centres. Each centre will have a centre management committee (CMCs) with 10–12 members (50% F) who will be trained on soft skills, GBV, and gender equality. CCG and CMC will select 120 non-formal teachers (50% F) for the centres; iii) Train and mentor non-formal teachers and school leaders to deliver this curriculum. Parents will receive awareness on Psycho-Social Support (PSS) for children. Promotional materials will be developed for distribution among communities.Process and outcome monitoring activities will include baseline and end-line assessments of the target group, feedback with project beneficiaries and FGDs with project stakeholders

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Insufficient water and sanitation facilities; consequently, inability to put in place and maintain Covid-19 safety measures including positive behavioral changes. Mitigation measures: Learning facilities will be improved to ensure sufficient water and sanitation facilities will be provided and maintained. WASH facilities will be sex and age-segregated and accessible to people with disabilities. Students will be trained on proper hygiene practices. All mentioned procedures will be in cooperation with health authorities. Learning facilities will have Covid-19 safety protocols in place. Protocols will include identification, prevention measures, awareness-raising and a referral system between the learning facility and the relevant health centre Safety and Security concerns about male and female youth exposure to physical or verbal harassment on their way to the centres. Mitigation Measures: AKF will ensure a safe and secure environment through careful selection of centre locations in safe and easy-to-access areas, along with providing transportation CBCC facilitators selection committee might face pressure or even might be criticized or accused of bias in selecting the facilitators. Mitigation Measures: AKF will start with mobilizing communities to consider both qualification and social representation as selection criteria for committees. AKF will expand reach to communities as much as possible, to increase the probability of qualified and agreed upon facilitators to lead the CBCC, these committees will be responsible for parents’ engagement in Centres management through follow up meetings and sharing feedback.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Lack of interest and participation of the community in ECD. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigation: AKF will conduct a number of community mobilization and awareness raising activities such as SMS, campaigns and community meetings.Risk 1: Some teachers might be unable to attend morning classes due to their jobs. Likelihood: Low. Impact: high. Mitigation: AKF will mitigate this risk through designing the classes to be implemented on weekends and afternoons.Risk 3: Involved teachers and adolescents might be unable to attend due to the cost of transportation to the centers. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: AKF will provide transportation for both teachers and adolescents.Risk 4: Exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitor exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the project budget.Risk 5: COVID-19 health related risks to staff, beneficiaries, operators and service providers.Mitigation: In order to mitigate this risk, AKF will provide personal protection equipment and train the project team on COVID-19 related prevention and protection measures.Assumptions:If Community leaders and stakeholders were properly engaged, they will be able to mobilize communities to actively participate in project activities. AKF will coordinate with other key stakeholders through regular meetings. Accountability to local populations will be achieved through consultation with parents. Community feedback will be collected through AKF already established PTAs and FGD with local communityIf COVID-19 safety measures are taken, the potential spread of infections will be prevented/minimized.
