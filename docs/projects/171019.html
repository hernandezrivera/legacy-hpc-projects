Project Summary

Action Against Hunger (AAH), together with the Union of Agricultural Work Committees (UAWC) and Gaza Urban & pre-Urban Agricultural Platform (GUPAP) propose to provide immediate relief responses to support vulnerable households across 3 governorates of the Gaza Strip (Rafah, Khan Younis and Middle Area) to cope with shocks that affect their food security status; enable to protect their livelihoods, increase productivity and improve their food and nutrition security status. This includes households who base their income on agriculture activities and cannot maintain or rehabilitate their key productive assets, and farmers who are at risk of food insecurity, whose livelihoods were affected by the current situation, with family members unable to work (with physical or mental disabilities, medical cases), with a high number of dependents and do not have a stable source of income. This project has been developed to contribute to the achievement of the three Food Security Sector (FSS) objectives of the oPt HRP: (O1) Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced, (O2) Households suffering from limited of access to food and to income opportunities are able to meet their basic food needs, and (O3) FSS activities, including advocacy and awareness, are planned and implemented in a coordinated manner, and partnership with key national ministries is strengthened, and to align with strategic priorities of the Ministry of Agriculture, and has been coordinated with similar interventions within the target area. Firstly, AAH will focus on rehabilitating productive
assets, such as damaged greenhouses, water ponds and installation of new solar energy units for irrigation pumps, partially done using the cash for work (CfW) modality and with the support of UAWC. This will enable 558 households to increase their resilience to man-made, covid-19 restriction measures and natural shocks, make use of the available natural resources for their agricultural activities and reduce the costs associated while gaining an income for those employed in cash for work activities. Secondly, 60 female headed households will be supported by AAH through enhancing existing home gardening and increased knowledge of home garden techniques. This will contribute to food security at household level while increasing the dignity of the women targeted. Finally, GUPAP and AAH will organize awareness sessions at community level to include food security issues, including barriers faced by small producers and women producers to promote local production. These will result in advocacy materials being developed for national and international audiences. The project will reduce the food insecurity and improve livelihoods of an estimated 3,399 people (745 men, 949 women, 805 boys, and 900 girls) and provide them with the means to show resilience in the face of these challenges. Small scale farmers, who have accumulated levels of debts and decreased income from recent restrictions and inability to restore productive assets will be prioritized in the first activity. Female headed households with high dependency ratio with existing home-gardens will be prioritized to be supported further in improving their food security (activity 2). Accountability and participation will be ensured through a detailed MEAL framework specifically developed to ensure that beneficiary selection is conducted in a transparent and accountable manner and through applying AAH’s thorough Feedback and Complaint Mechanism to guarantee the inclusion of communities and individuals’ suggestions into the project. AAH seeks to achieve equal and impartial access to vulnerable populations, aiming for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible including through community consultations to further achieve beneficiaries’ empowerment and sustainability beyond the project’s lifetime

1 - Needs

According to the oPt Humanitarian Need Overview 2021, the overall number of Palestinians in need 2021 will be 2.45 million (nearly half of the oPt population). The World Bank (2020) forecasts that by end of 2020 the poverty rate in the Gaza Strip will increase from 53% to 64%, or the equivalent to 37,300 new poor families. There, 70% of the population is severely in need of humanitarian assistance, considering that about 72% of the total population is in need for food security assistance, out of which 75.3% are severely affected and 24.7% at stress levels of food insecurity. Between 2020 and 2021, an increase of 18% of food insecure people is predicted. Historically, the agriculture sector was the backbone of the Gaza Strip economy: agriculture played a large role in providing employment opportunities, especially in times of crisis when it was difficult to work in other sectors. These characteristics enhance the role of agriculture in strengthening the resilience of Palestinians and increasing their capacities to adapt. To maintain food supply chains and make producers more resilient to shocks regularly faced by farmers in the Gaza Strip, urgent rehabilitation of key agricultural productive assets is needed. In addition to recurrent climate risks, violence from the Israeli army and blockade, the COVID-19 pandemic has created an additional layer of vulnerability on farming households also crippled by heavy financial debts. The main constraints faced by farmers resulted from measures applied through the 13-year-long blockade as well as from manmade destruction of land and crops (bulldozing and spraying) by the Israeli army and losses from the “Great March of Return” demonstrations between 2018 and 2019. High salinity of water (Gaza only relies on one aquifer source), fragmented land ownership and limited power supply, constitute further structural challenges that the sector and farmers have to face daily. As reported by a Care Policy Brief, data collected by PUI (September 2020) 97% of farmers reported a decrease in their agricultural income by about 50% during COVID-19. The latest findings from FAO bi-weekly survey (3 October 2020) indicate that farmers reported inputs largely available in warehouses, nonetheless, remaining largely inaccessible due to movement restrictions. In addition to the recurrent cash liquidity shortage, these restrictions have pushed suppliers to request payments of cash up front and therefore increasing even more greatly the level of debts of farmers households. As reported by PNGO (September 2020), closure of crossings and markets along with the temporary suspension of vegetables exports in March 2020 damaged crops to be exported and led to a great decline of prices on local markets. Restriction measures linked to the pandemic have also affected daily workers working in the agricultural sector, as IRPAL (October 2020) reports, 80% of the 7000 workers registered are now without income. The regular daily wage was of ILS 30 and disappeared after the pandemic started, pushing workers to accept lower wages at only ILS 10/day. Women in agriculture are particularly disadvantaged, despite being a significant unregistered portion of the workers (22.5%). This is reflected in limited economic opportunities, restricted mobility and limited technical skills, hence marginalizing their role in the agricultural production process. In Gaza, at least, 7.1% of female-headed households identify that 50% of their income comes from agricultural activities. These households are particularly impacted by the limited services available in the sector (Oxfam 2020). According to a study conducted by AWRAD (2020), 71% of respondents had fully or partially lost their family income, among whom women were the most affected with 76% of female against 66% of male. As urban crawling has taken over lands in the Gaza Strip as well, urban forms of home-gardening offer alternatives to sustainable source of food if applying safe and chemical free practices (AAH, 2019). From previous AAH activities (2019), households supported with similar interventions were able to produce on average 40 Kg of tomato, 35 Kg of pepper, 18 Kg of onion, 22 Kg of potato and cucumber and 15 Kg of eggplant per season. The main barrier to food security is financial access to food. A recent assessment conducted by phone by Action Against Hunger monitoring the impact of COVID-19 on beneficiary households, including active in cash-for-work activities in the agricultural sector, highlighted that 49% reported being negatively affected by the crisis in September 2020 compared to 28% who reported being affected in April 2020. Average income decreased from 591 ILS before the pandemic, to 253 ILS in April 2020 and finally to 237 ILS in September 2020.

2 - Activities

A1: Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs for farmers-Immediate relief actions to rehabilitate existing and not fully functioning key productive assets. Under this activity 558 households (3169 individuals – 694 men, 884 women, 751 boys and 840 girls) who depend on agricultural activities for their livelihood will be supported to restore their livelihoods activities and enhance their resilience to shocks and crises. A prior coordination was done with FSS and Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) to identify the targets and the priorities.* The activity will support 300 small scale farmers, prioritizing women farmers and households with high dependency ratio, who have greenhouses (1-2 donums area) to rehabilitate their partially damaged greenhouses. ((120 farmers will be targeted in priority in Deir el Balah, Az Zawayda and Nusseirat, 90 in Al Qarara, Qizan an Najjar, and Abasan Al Kabira (Khan Yunis), and 90 in Rafah (Mosabeh),, Shokat as Sufi and Al Mawasi (Rafah))). The intervention will enable farmers resume agricultural activities, reduce their debts and the impact of natural hazards and enable them reaching production capacity to levels before the damage, secure 40-55% of the needed agricultural production costs through the received assistance.* The activity also covers the rehabilitation of 120 rainwater harvesting ponds (average 100m3 capacity per pond) (60 in Al Qarara, Qizan an Najjar,and Abasan Al Kabira (Khan Yunis), and 60 in Rafah (Mosabeh), Shokat as Sufi and Al Mawasi (Rafah). This activity aims to improve the availability and the quality of water, and therefore adequate for watering a wider variety of crops that cannot be irrigated with high salinity groundwater. Cash for work modality will be used to implement the above activities, 78 heads of households or workers from women-headed households from the most vulnerable groups in the targeted areas who have the physical capacity (young adults and adults) and experience working in the agricultural sector, will be provided with short term job opportunities to rehabilitate the agricultural assets, that contribute in addressing immediate food and basic needs, preventing negative coping mechanisms and increasing their feeling of dignity.60 households of small-scale farmers, prioritizing women farmers, who face difficulties to cultivate their lands and greenhouses, due to lack of access of electricity and irrigation water, will be supported by installing new solar energy units to their irrigation pumps (30 in Qizan an Najjar,and Abasan al Kabira (Khan Yunis), and 30 in Shokat as Sufi and Al Mawasi (Rafah). This activity aims to enhance the farmers resilience to cope with the electricity shortage crisis, improve the productivity of the agricultural lands and reduce the operational cost though providing substantial increase of water availability with lower energy costs. Localities of intervention were coordinated with FAO’s interventions.A2: Supporting vulnerable female headed households in the target area to increase their access to food of sufficient quantity and quality through home gardening in Deir el Balah, Az Zawayda and An Nuseirat (Middle Area). Under this activity, the project will enhance access to and the utilisation of fresh food for 60 households (330 individuals – 72 men, 92 women, 78 boys and 88 girls) by rehabilitating their home gardening and providing them with range of agricultural inputs to improve the efficiency and the productivity of their home gardens (average 100m2). The activity will target economically poor women headed-households in priority with high levels of debt and limited access to sufficient quantity and quality of food and limited purchasing power, particularly encouraging the involvement and ownership of young adults, adult and elderly women, thereby increasing dignity. The activity aims to improve the food security of the targeted households, increase their purchasing power from savings on food costs and income from sales of garden surplus products to their neighbours.A3: Advocacy for promotion and inclusion of food security issues. GUPAP will organize 3 awareness workshops with groups of farmers and women headed households targeted (young adults to elderly) to contribute to ongoing policy dialogues to raise sensitivity on barriers faced by small producers and women producers to apply agroecological practices by procuring adequate inputs, promoting local production over imported products and regulation policies on chemical inputs use through the FSS and PNGO networks will be organized. Participation of young and adult women will be facilitated within decision-making spheres within the agricultural sector to better represent their priorities and challenges. Factsheets on same topics will be produced to reach a wide number of actors nationally and internationally.

3 - Indicators

AAH will develop a comprehensive M&E Framework for the action. AAH will supervise UAWC and GUPAP staff responsible for project monitoring to ensure the monitoring system relating to impartial beneficiary selection and on-going project implementation is as effective as possible. Joint MEAL planning through the development of project tracking tool will be conducted through participatory approach including UAWC and GUPAP. AAH technical and MEAL teams will conduct regular field visits (if the context allows) and meetings with GUPAP and UAWC to follow up the progress and ensure activities are implemented according to the project workplan. AAH MEAL team will collect disaggregated data (sex, age, disability) and monitor the desired outcomes and impact of the activities on farmers’ ability to restore their livelihoods and face shocks, as well as women headed households and the improved food consumption through home gardens. Female field staff and social workers conducting visits and data collection will be prioritized to communicate with women beneficiaries. The MEAL plan will monitor awareness raising at community level and track feedback and application related to advocacy activities within the communities. MEAL activities: Develop project tracking tool to provide timely evidence on key results achieved using planned indicators, timely monitoring of the assumptions and external risks Carry baseline/ end line exercises to assess the provision of support to the farmers and CFW activities led by AAH and UAWC, carry baseline/end line assessments with the women headed households under Activity 2 will use of gender sensitive tools and mixed method approach to assess in depth outcomes on women access to quality and sufficient quantity of food. Baseline and end line reports will be illustrate key findings and progress achieved through all activities, aiming at capturing progress but also addressing key recommendations for the agriculture sector and programmatic learning. Close coordination will take place with relevant ministries and NGOs through the FSS concerning the verification of the applicants’ data and access to the cross checking to avoid overlapping. The expected results are the following: Improved production and productivity capacity for vulnerable farmers; rehabilitation of agricultural open lands, water ponds, installation of alternative source of electricity, provision of an emergency package of agricultural inputs; Increased capacity of families, with particular focus on women to prevent the adoption of negative coping mechanisms; Cash assistance in order to support economically vulnerable households through CfW opportunities to support households who had recently lost their income or saw their income decreased to improve households’ food security and reduce negative coping mechanisms. Through FSS and local partners, the project will establish a communication channel to ensure full coordination and with the relevant contact in the MoA. AAH will contribute to the exchange of information and lessons learned, factsheets and stories among the cluster community to be used for advocacy purposes, which will be shared with the target communities.Expected indicators and targets:300 dunums made productive or with improved production capacity (full and partial land rehabilitation, piping and irrigation system, seeds and seedlings distribution, etc)12,000 m3 of water storage rehabilitated or provided162 kW of renewable power supply systems installed480 HHs (headed by farmers, herders, breeders, receiving inputs and services6,000 m2 of home garden rehabilitated or established;78 individuals benefiting from Cash for Work;100 people reached by non-technical awareness sessions. This includes measuring the improvement in the number of women who did not resort to negative coping mechanisms as a result of the initiative. Proxy indicators to measure food security and use of coping mechanisms will be measured:(i) Coping Strategy Index (CSI),(ii) HH Dietary Diversity Score (HDDS),(iii) Food Consumption Score (FCS).

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Through the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming is guided by protection principles of Do No Harm, respecting human rights and IHL principles. AAH considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. Up to date AAH has made use of cluster specific Protection and Gender Mainstreaming Tip Sheets, along with learnings from various interventions and external evaluations. To inform its assessments and implementation AAH also continuously works on understanding gender roles and how its interventions might impact them without aggravating existing power relations between genders. By understanding gender roles and household dynamics, AAH believes that the organisation mitigates the risks of doing harm. In 2019, AAH conducted a masculinity study in both Gaza and the West Bank, focused on identities/roles of men in order to fully understand men, women, girls and boys and gender dynamics. Focus group discussion with men and women benefitting from the intervention will be separately organized to assess better the interaction of the assistance received with existing intra-household relations and decision-making processes between members according to age and gender. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All AAH staff has the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct. AAH has in place focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. Moreover, majority of AAH staff received protection cluster facilitated trainings with the aim to know how to detect and refer potential cases of GBV and other protection concerns. Identified potential protection cases will be referred to the relevant clusters and working groups. As part of AAH accountability to affected populations, a feedback mechanism emphasizing fluid and transparent communication will be in place. It serves as a mechanism for project staff, beneficiaries, host communities, local authorities, and other key stakeholders to comment about project performance, share data and information.. The information collected will be regularly fed into our programming to ensure no harm is done and to guarantee continuous improvements. The mechanism will consist of a mixture of channels: SMS/phone numbers, email addresses, and feedback and complaint desks when possible, accessible to people with disability. AAH will reach out to beneficiaries through regular consultation processes (assessments, FGDs, community meetings, PDM survey on a randomly selected sample, etc.). During the assessment at the inception phase, beneficiaries from different ages/genders/disabilities will be consulted to identify their preferences in terms of providing feedback. The mechanism takes into account the perception or possible reservations that the target population might have in relation to their use, especially for complaint mechanisms. Vulnerable groups will receive additional sensitization to make sure they feel comfortable in using these mechanisms. Data will be sex-and-age disaggregated to show possible trends and understand barriers that might need to be addressed. Emphasis will be placed on closing the feedback loop, meaning that the person who raised a complaint will always receive a response on their case and beneficiary data (keeping confidentiality) will be used to inform programmatic decision-making. To further ensure protection is mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, all beneficiaries are informed about and are requested to sign a data protection form, communicating their preference about how AAH and partners will treat their personal data. Tthe indicator “% of communities/project beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” will be measured through an endline survey of the beneficiaries.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

AAH upholds the humanitarian principles of Humanity, Impartiality, Neutrality and Independence. AAH is committed to operate in an ethical manner and on a programmatic risk mitigation strategy for risk of fraud, waste, and abuse within the proposed activities. As a global frame, AAH relies on the organizational Policy on Anti-Bribery, Corruption and Abuse of Power and Code of Conduct. Organizational whistle blowing mechanisms and procedure are in place, `ZERO TOLERANCE´ towards fraud, bribery or corruption is applied. Reporting mechanisms have also been implemented to report these behaviors to headquarters. Ethical Risks related to any kind of abuse (sexual, power, etc...) are mitigated by providing compulsory briefing to each new staff member on ethical policies. AAH Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. AAH has a Policy for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and two referent persons against Harassment in Gaza. Fraud Risks in activities such as cash assistance, is continuously monitored during activity implementation and post distribution monitoring. In 2019, AAH has conducted in its Middle East country offices a Protection Mainstreaming Internal Evaluation for which the consultant spent the month of March in oPt. Particular attention was paid to the way AAH includes vulnerable groups in its work, for instance through inclusive participatory assessments. As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focuses on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. Beneficiaries’ selection will be partly supported by the involvement of local committees and existing women’s group in coordination with AAH and partners. UAWC works closely with 4 agriculture committees in Rafah, 8 committees in Khan Younes and 6 committees in Middle Area. Women participation to these committees, including young women will be facilitated through the existing networks of UAWC and GUPAP, the latter supporting the Women Agripreneur platform. AAH is reporting under the HRP disaggregated data by sex and age; though pending the reporting format, AAH usually includes further disaggregation on vulnerability such as data on people with disabilities. In addition, AAH started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs. Through the implementation of beneficiary feedback mechanism, beneficiaries and members of the communities where AAH works can report irregularities in the beneficiary selection, cash or other assistance distribution process. The project will develop specific SOPs including a set of eligibility and selection criteria, as well as a score system. Potential beneficiaries - pre-identified by collecting lists from medical organizations, clusters (FSS, Protection, Health) and working groups (Disabilities working group), will go through a verification process jointly carried out by the technical team and social workers. Random checks will be carried out by the MEAL team members to ensure transparency, neutrality and objectivity and spot potential bias or incongruency.
