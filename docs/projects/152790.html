Project Summary

The overall aim of the project is to contribute to strengthening prevention, mitigation and response to Child Protection issues as well as strengthening quality of care for vulnerable children. This will build on the already gained milestones from the 2018 that has already enabled AYA to provide community based child friendly spaces with support from UNICEF through IsraAID in Mundri Payam, Kotobi Payam in Mundri West a and Lui in Mundri East. Also boost and strengthen the family tracing and reunification exercise in Mundri Counties. 6000 beneficiaries are expected to be reached. 60% children and 40% adults.
AYA intends to continue run and equip two child friendly spaces in Mundri Payam and establish three additional child friendly spaces in Lui, Kedi’ba and Kotobi Payams. The center will act as venue for identification of vulnerable children to be placed under case management and also identification of separated and unaccompanied children who needed to be reunified with their parents.
The project will establish and train child protection committees in both Mundri East and Mundri West, conduct training to members of the committees and provide continuous mentoring to the committee. This mentoring will help the committee to identify and handle child protection concerns.
The organization will support conducting FTR and there will be constant awareness on general child protection issues identified in the community in both Mundri East and Mundri West.
AYA is a National NGO that implement child protection project funded by UNICEF through IsraAID. The organization is the implementing partner to IsraAID since 2015 under UNICEF funded projects of GBV and Child Protection.
The organization has strong history of protection activities. AYA is HRP partner for 2018 under GBV Sub-cluster. It secured first round standard allocation and also second round standard allocation for both Mundri East and West. AYA participated in Localization training supported by Protection Cluster and now is the organization co-chairing the GBV Working Group in Mundri which has seven partners under its coordination (gbvsubclustersouthsudan@gmail.com). AYA is also alternate Focal Point for Deep Field Coordination appointed by UNOCHA. This makes the organization to convene partner meeting in Mundri for all the humanitarian partners operating on the ground (mukhwana@un.org). AYA also implement GBV Prevention project supported by IOM in Juba. This enabled the organization to run four economic empowerment groups in Juba (eletiyo@iom.intl).
Therefore, the organization has installations in place including sustainable internet, vehicles, motorbikes and available skilled and staffs to conduct the project to the maximum.

Needs

South Sudan is first and foremost gradually becoming protracted protection crisis. Since 2013, the population has been exposed to repeated deliberate attacks on civilians and other violations of international humanitarian and human rights law (UN OCHA HRP “2018). Though of the signing cessation of hostilities agreement was conducted in December 2017 and signing of revitalized peace agreement was conducted on 12th September 2018, the fighting in parts of South Sudan continued. A country of more than 11 million people has faced numerous, overwhelming challenges since conflict broke out in December 2013. During the fighting, homes were reportedly looted and burned, children were reportedly killed and forcefully recruited in the military ranks, others were reported missing or separated, and cases of GBV were reported in an unprecedented high figure. The majority of people displaced by the fighting were women, children and the elderly, including persons with specific needs. Fighting in Kedi’ba headquarters of Mundri East County in December 2016 displaced nearly 5000 individuals who short refuge in Mundri IDP camp. These people in the last two years depended on humanitarian assistance since the security could not permit them to cultivate outside Mundri. In Bangolo, Medewu and Kotobi, communities faced tremendous challenges due to lack of available services. This categorically forced women, children and elderly people to seek refuge in Mundri town (AYA FGD- April 2018). 8000 IDPs migrated to Mundri town. Conditions of IDPs especially children deteriorate on everyday basis. This left many traumatized since the only source of livelihood was left back home. Rain washed children and pregnant mothers and elderly people and persons living with disabilities were left behind in bushes (May 2018 Assessment). There are only two child friendly spaces to support children cope with this difficult situation which is not proportional to the number of vulnerable children in-need. The 27 identified separated and unaccompanied children have not been reunified with their parents; no support was given to them. This leaves them to look more vulnerable. According to the series of assessment reports, displaced populations reported incidents of forced recruitment and child neglect, child labour and economic violence. Some female child headed households resorted to survival sex due to lack of basic services such as food and even the emotional support. Young girls abort their babies and throw them in pit-latrine, there has been no child protection network to support the community identify and advocate for child protection concerns, rate of conflict induced criminality among the children is on rise especially in Mundri town and nearby villages. Children ended up involved in smoking of opium, drinking alcohol as coping mechanism to withstand the effect of the conflict. 5 children whose father and mother were killed the same day remained ruined and vulnerable as result of conflict. They are real orphans but without any support. Though many women had lost their husbands during conflict. Raising the number of registered orphans to over 200. AYA supported child friendly spaces have numerous challenges. There are no child friendly space materials since 2016 where UNICEF supplied the materials. Children only depend on traditional games. Identified vulnerable children have no support. Therefore, it is prudent for AYA to strengthen the community structure, strengthen FTR system and conduct psycho-social support and case management for vulnerable children that suffer as result of this conflict.

Activities

Provide structural community psycho-social support. Create 3 community based child friendly spaces (1 in Mundri West- Kotobi Payam, 2 in Mundri East- Kedi'ba and Lui Payams). Support existing two child friendly spaces in Mundri West. Conduct child friendly activities in the established child friendly spaces. Provide case management services to girls and boys with protection concerns. This will be done through child friendly spaces, community based protection committees, schools and other community centers. Recruit and train CFS Animators in locations where new child friendly spaces will be established. Train additional CFS Animators in CFS Centers in Mundri. 20 CFS Animators identified and trained. Create and support community based child protection committees. Six committees created in six schools (3 in Mundri East and 3 in Mundri West). Three other committees to be created in community (1 in Mundri East and 2 in Mundri West). Train 81 members of child protection committees (network) and provide mentoring sessions to members on weekly basis. Provide training to service providers on Child Protection issues, guiding principles, PSS and referral pathways for child survivors. The personnel to be trained include 12 AYA Social Workers, 6 Police Officers, the CMR personnel in health facilities. Conduct family tracing and reunification to separated and unaccompanied children. This will follow massive awareness and identification processes. Develop IEC materials in local languages and disseminate it to wider community to prevent separation during emergency and respond when it occurs. Create awareness to general community on child protection issues in the community. 30 music dance and drama conducted on information on services available, messages on protection issues affecting children in Mundri East and West. 12 information dissemination meetings to be conducted in community of the project area. Establishment, training and continuous mentoring activities with peace building clubs in the six schools where Child Protection Committee will be established.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

The project intends to continuously and timely provide beneficiaries with information regarding assistance to be provided by the project and others that require referral. There will be monthly meetings to inform the beneficiaries. Information can also be made available through beneficiary groups like women groups, use of announcements in social functions, schools and health centers to reach many beneficiaries. Referral pathways existing will also be used for disseminating about the services to the children of abuse. Posters, T-shirts and billboards will be used for information dissemination about the critical services. Volunteers will visit homes for protection concern and pass prevention messages to the community and carry community sensitization of child protection. Coordination with other CP partners will be promoted. AYA is the Co-lead of GBV Working Group and also the only current CP actor on ground. It will use the strength from the coordination to share information and identify needs to avoid duplication of efforts.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages to specific population groups will be tailored for example adolescent girls, pictures will be used to disseminate information about available services. Some group of population may need discussion posters and it will be designed specifically to suit their situation. Sensitivity of local cultures, religion and norms will be taken into consideration before sharing the information. Staffs to be taken to specific location will be drawn from the local people who knows the back ground. This is especial when working in child friendly spaces.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

AYA is organization trained and has Focal Point for PSEA. Therefore, all the staff have signed the code of conduct. The information is shared with the community in general. AYA has robust policy on prevention of sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. No staff, partner or volunteer is allowed to engage in sexual exploitation and sexual abuses of any kind. Though sexual activity with a minor, beneficiaries, exchange of sex for aid among others. These policies will also be mainstreamed to other partners operating in the area.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Over last few months, AYA has been implementing child protection through establishment of three child friendly spaces. The funding came from UNICEF through IsraAD. Therefore, AYA was fully involved in consulting with children who will directly benefit from the services, parents whose children are targeted and community leaders. Therefore, full recommendation of implementing the activity was obtained from the community. There was also participatory approach used through focus group discussion to generate needs and prioritize the development of concept note. Therefore, all have been fully consulted and engaged in the process.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

There is always complaint desk set in each place of activity. This will enable the staff to collect the complaint and feedback generated to the community in question. AYA considers feedback from community and especially its beneficiaries. All beneficiaries form important part of giving feedback concerning the quality of programming and how it should be done to benefit the intended group of people. A group of beneficiaries will be selected to have face to face discussion sessions as focus group discussion to generate feedback and use the feedback to improve the response in the field. Monitoring and evaluation team of AYA will be made available to collect feedback and communicate it in terms of reports to improve the work of organization and offer the response in more dignified manner.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is based on protection risk analysis taking into account specific needs of children (boys and girls) affected by the conflict and other vulnerable groups. It puts efforts on identification of specific risks to specific target groups for example PSEA against children and other beneficiary groups. This explains why AYA has strict code of conduct and all staffs are oriented on the code. The project also identified some possible risks to staffs such as ambushes and worked on how to mitigate such risks from happening. This will be through daily security analysis and orienting staffs on security protocols. AYA has safety policies put in place to mitigate such risks from occurring. IOM trained the staffs on risk mitigation aspect.

This project has identified number of barriers to access.
