Project Summary

A period of over 9 months has passed since the conflict between the RSF and SAF broke out, 7.5 million people have been displaced, with 6 million being internally displaced (According to the OCHA’s Sudan: Nine months of conflict - Key Facts and Figures (15 January 2024)). The humanitarian needs in Sudan have been consistently increasing across Sudan where no states are safe in the current situation. Sudanese people are facing increased needs for food support in areas where IDP influx is high which includes East, North, and South Darfur and Gedaref States. On the 15th of December, the RSF attacked Al Jazerah state resulting in displacing the IDPs who came previously to seek refuge in the state in addition to the state residents. According to IOM DTM, most of the IDPs are seeking refuge with family members. The pressure on the host community is increasing as one family could host over 3 families at a time with limited income. According to OCHA 4.9 million 10% of the population are IPC PHASE 4, while 12 % 12.8 million people are IPC phase 3. This indicates a possible famine if we don’t act immediately.
This project aims to tackle food security crises in Sudan. This is done through two main activities: Firstly supporting 6986 IDPs and 1233 individuals from the host communities with cash and voucher assistance. This will be done in 6 localities (Al Mafaza, Madeinat Al Gedaref, Wasat Al Gedaref, Nayala Shimal, Nayala Janoub, and Ad Du’ayn) in 3 states (Gedaref, South Darfur, and East Darfur). Secondly support 200 individuals (170 IDP, and 30 host communities) through providing livestock assets (Goats) in Beliel locality in South Darfur. The project will also conduct assessments to ensure the appropriateness of the items provided and to ensure to prioritize and address the needs of vulnerable groups such as women, people with disabilities, and the elderly.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project is designed based on the pronounced and observed needs of the project target groups. We will ensure that the needs of IDPs, returnees, and vulnerable residents will be addressed while protecting their dignity, and safety while creating access. The project is designed to put community participation as the main pillar. Thus, the project will not be implemented and monitored by ZOA staff only, but rather with the participation of beneficiaries and community members to ensure inclusion, relevancy to the needs of the people, and success of the project. In this manner, ZOA will ensure that people from different groups are part of the community consultations when it comes to determining priorities. Additionally, they would be included in setting criteria for the beneficiary’s selection and reviewing the selected lists. In line with protection principles, the project beneficiaries will be targeted by an inclusive committee representative of all the community groups men, women, youth, elders, disabled persons, etc. We will also work through and closely with protection actors in the targeted areas and make referrals where necessary. During registration and distribution of assistance, we will ensure that people with special needs have very short queue times and separate lines for women, men, and people with special needs.

4 - AAP & PSEA
4.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback? Please select as many as relevant.

Face to face with group consultations with aid workers

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project team conducts a needs assessment at the beginning of the project. In this assessment, the team will conduct FGDs, interviews, and more with different groups including women, men, men with disability women with disability from different age groups. With attention to localization, the local community is asked to identify a committee that supports the project implementation, monitoring, and evaluation. This committee should include members from all genders, ethnic, ages, and disability groups and they will be the focal people for their different groups. The project will also ensure that the community monitors the performance of the committees and ensures there are safe and open complaint channels. While also keeping the space open for the committee and community to give their complaints and suggestions regarding the project and how it addresses the different needs. These complaints and suggestions are to be seriously taken into consideration during the implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of the project. This is how the project is planned to include all groups and ensure their different needs are being met.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The project will also use interagency approaches through working with, UN agencies, HAC, and sector working groups to take best practices and discuss possible challenges. ZOA has a well-designed reporting mechanism internally and externally where it allows beneficiaries to be able to report their complaints or exploitation cases directly from community committees, then our field staff and area managers to the Netherlands government. Complaints are taken seriously and there is a dedicated staff for feedback and complaints in each office, who will ensure proper and confidential investigation and dress and escalate the case according to the given situation.
