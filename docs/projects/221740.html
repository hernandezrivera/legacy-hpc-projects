Project Summary

This proposed HRP interventions by Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) will be implemented in Pibor, Bor South, Aweil Center, Kapoeta South, and Ibba counties of South Sudan in response to sexually gender based violence (SGBV) and to address multiple GBV protection risk factors faced by women, girls and and men. CRSF’s tailored GBV response is targeted to reach 6,250 (3,250 Women, 800 Men, 1,200 girls and 1000 boys) vulnerable population.
CRSF will aim to implement a comprehensive approach to GBV prevention, response, and risk mitigation in line with the GBV AoR cluster objectives PCO1 and PCO3 to ensure; Women, men, boys and girls are provided with awareness-raising sessions on GBV risks, available response services and timely reporting; reached through GBV prevention initiatives (e.g. SASA, EMAP, community cares programming, supported with specialized GBV response services (GBV case management, referrals, legal aid, safe houses & WGFS programming; reached with group PSS services, including recreational and social activities; women and adolescent girls are provided dignity kits; men, women, girls, and boys are linked and supported with cash and voucher assistance; supported with GBV risk mitigation interventions (Income generating activities, fuel efficient stoves, intergradation of GBV by other clusters and humanitarian actors; GBV referral pathways established and functional at county level; and women and men (community leaders or humanitarian, GBV partners and government social welfare staff) trained on GBV
Child Relief and Support Fund is proposing a $200,000 grant for 6,250 vulnerable beneficiaries to deliver the proposed GBV response including; Strengthening Psychosocial Support services. To ensure efficient coordination, CRSF will liase with other partners in other sectors and GBV AoR sub cluster to avoid duplication of resources and activities and ensure that beneficiaries are linked to a whole set of interventions. Further, CRSF will work with health partners on ground to ensure that SGBV survivors for instance rape cases are managed.
To ensure sustainability of the GBV intervention response, CRSF will work closely with community structures including community leaders, Payam administrators and camp leadership so that they are trained will be trained on GBV key concepts, PSEA, Psychosocial Fist Aid (PFA) and safe referrals. CRSF will conduct similar training for other key stakeholders; county department and state ministry of Gender Child, and Social Welfare (MoGCSW), mother support groups, youth and women Association, local leaders (Sultans) and Police in the target project locations. CRSF will further coordinate with other partners to ensure targeted capacity building of their staff and teams on GBV risk mitigation upon capacity gap assessment. To ensure a clear tracking of status of implementation of legal aid interventions coherent with GBV AoR cluster, CRSF will ensure regular and effective reporting of the GBV using the recommended GBV AoR Cluster 5Ws tools through Activity info and report mobilized resources using Financial Tracking Services or Financial Tracking System (fts).

1. Needs

In South Sudan, 9.18 million people (81% of the affected population) are exposed to at least one of the top five protection risks. Further analysis indicates that 98% (840,000 people) of returnees are the most protection risk-exposed population group, followed by IDPs at 81% 1.5million people . The impact of severe food insecurity with most households in IPC phase 3+, economic down turn, floods and subnational violence causing displacements of local and vulnerable populations exacerbated by the Khartoum/sudan conflict/crises which resulted into high influx of South Sudanese returnees, refugees and IDPs continue driving GBV against the vulnerable women and girls in South Sudan. According to IPC Report (Nov 18th, 2024), 5.83 M people (46.3%) of the country’s population face high levels of acute food insecurity and classified in IPC 3 or above (Crisis or worse). The most food insecure populations in counties including Pibor, Bor South, Aweil Centre, and Kapoeta South have been significantly affected by frequent climatic shocks, economic crisis, conflict and insecurity including the spillover effects of the conflict in Sudan causing forced displacement and low food production. Subsequently, these factors expose vulnerable population groups of women and girls to GBV protection risk. Looking at protection cluster, gender-based violence (GBV) is rampant and a key protection risk driven by harmful social norms and systemic gender inequality, severely impacting the safety and rights of women and girls. The Protection Risk Monitoring System reported GBV as the main protection risk in 38% of the 2,497 communities surveyed in 2024. The ISNA revealed that 36% of women and girls avoid certain areas due to safety concerns, while 53% of households face barriers in accessing vital services. Subsequently, the ripple effect of displacement crisis has led to elevated levels of conflict-related sexual violence and gender-based violence, and thus making South Sudan one of the most severe protection crises globally. According to GBViMS statistics (2024), there has been a notable increase in GBV cases with about 97% of reported cases in South Sudan affecting mainly; women and girls of which 79% were adolescent girls.An abstract and in-depth assessment conducted by child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) in September 2024 in Pibor, Rubkona and Aweil center revealed a stark and distressing Gender-Based Violence (GBV) situation exacerbated by high cases of sexually gender based violence mong school girls and women. Moreover, a survey conducted by GBV experts revealed a limited availability of core GBV services in 35% of counties with in South Sudan while a multilayer data analysis estimates that 2.75 million people will need GBV interventions in 2025. Therefore, there is need to enhance protection of women and girls against sexually gender based violence and comprehensively address risks root causes of GBV so as to restore community morals and values, dignity of women and girls, break the cycle of violence, ensure justice, and foster a more inclusive and equal south Sudanese society. With Operational presence in the highlighted target counties, Child Relief and Support Fund (CRSF) intents to respond to the GBV protection issues at hand. Currently with funding from UNDP, Rodenberry foundation, FAO and Recreation protection projects, CRSF in coordination with GBV AoR sub cluster envisages to mobilize more financial resources to address the GBV protection drivers in south sudan under this project plan.

2. Project activities

CRSF is proposing its GBV response activities in line with GBV AoR sub cluster objective and 2025 HRP Strategic objectives as follows;Activity 1: GBV Prevention risk mitigation and response strategies awareness raising on GBV and PSEA.Activity 2: Case management for women and girls Activity 3: Strengthen Psychosocial Support services and collaboration with MHPSS working group Activity 4: Strengthening Engagement of Men in Accountable Practices EMAP, Activity 5: Strengthening the implementation of primary GBV preventions interventions, Activity 6: Strengthening coverage of dignity kit distribution and programming as part of risk mitigation, Activity 7: GBV preparedness and contingency planning and provision of livelihood support, through the provision of need-based capacity building trainings to GBV actors and frontline service providers, Activity 8: Construction of WGFS using acceptable standards Activity 9: strengthening GBV case management and referral pathways for GBV survivorsActivity 10: Strengthening legal aid, safety and protection while enhancing the availability of safe houses for SGBV survivorsActivity 11: Support women and girls through economic empowerment in cash assistance to initiate small scale business in CRSF project target locations/counties
