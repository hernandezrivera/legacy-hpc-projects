Provision of equitable access to gender and disability inclusive education for IDPs, Returnees and Host Communities children aged 3-18 years in Panyinjiar, Mayendit and Guit Counties of Unity State

Basic Info

Project Name

Start Date

01/01/2024

End Date

31/12/2024

Project Summary

The project will be primarily and directly targeting the enrollment of 6,638 children (3,452 boys and 3,186 girls) primary school from IDPs, Returnees and Host Community population. Among the direct beneficiaries are 332 children (173 boys and 159 girls) Children Living with Disabilities (CLwDs). Other secondary beneficiaries are 160 teachers (132 men and 28 women) supported with training and incentives, strengthening the capacity of 190 SMC members (90 men and 100 women). The project will be implemented in coordination with Health, Nutrition and WASH service providers for school-based health, nutrition and hygiene programming. The project will also mainstream protection for PSEA, schools safety audit, distribution of dignity kits and MHM training. The project will also involve FSL partners for school-based vegetable demonstration gardening for skill learning especially for adolescent girls. To increase community participation, the project will support monthly AAP community meetings for feedback responses and collective plans of actions and implementation.
The project will establish 12 temporary learning spaces and classrooms including establishing 24 gender segregated latrines with water points for hand washing and distribute or provide sanitation and hygiene materials (hand washing soap, sanitizers, masks, etc.) and PPE for students and teachers. The project will support the distribution of recreational materials from the cluster pipeline to the 12 target schools and provide teaching and learning materials for ECD, primary, and secondary school education. Capacity strengthening will be provided by training of 160 teachers (132 men and 28 women) on EiE pedagogical skills and child-centered methodologies to improve teaching and learning process for emergency-affected children and train and update teachers on PSS, CP, and referral mechanisms including CP/GBV, health and nutrition including training of 190 SMCs/PTAs members (90 men and 100 women) on school management, governance, psychosocial support (PSS) and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR)/ climate change. Other activities will include provision of life-skills and basic vocational training to youth and adolescents, support prevention and mitigation of communicable diseases through training school communities by providing appropriate Information and Education and Communication (IEC) materials (i.e. posters, leaflets), conduct back to learning campaigns and Post Distribution Monitoring to document how supplies are used.

1. Needs

South Sudan continues to face overlapping crises, including floods, conflict, dry spells and food insecurity all of which adversely affect the resilience and vulnerability of people across the country. The ongoing Sudan crisis has had a dramatic negative impact on the already fragile humanitarian situation. According South Sudan FEWS NET Food Security Outlook June 2023 - January 2024, warns that South Sudan will continue to face extremely high levels of acute food insecurity, with over 60% of the population anticipated to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes. The South Sudan Humanitarian Snapshot September 2023 shows that 9.4 million people are in dire need of humanitarian aid, 2 million people are living as IDPs in the country. About 293,000 persons have cross into the country along the borders with Sudan and additional 30,000 people have also returned from Ethiopia into the country. According to the South Sudan IPC Acute Food Insecurity Phase Classification Second Projected Situation Map and Population Table (Apr - Jul 2023) forecast that Panyinjiar, Mayendit and Guit Counties were projected to be in IPC Level 4 (Emergency). From the Inter-Agency Assessment Report, Panyijiar County, Unity State, January 2023 and Interagency Rapid Needs Assessment Report: Mayendit county, Unity State 03 - 08 September 2022 shows that flood has directly affected settlements by displacing the population from their houses, gardens with growing crops, livestock grazing grounds, clean water sources, essential services points (health units, markets). Women face difficulties collecting firewood. Infrastructures such as schools, nutrition and health facilities has been destroyed. An estimated 78,752 people have been affected by the food insecurity crisis in Panyjiar (i.e. 47,725 and 31,027 people in the Greater Ganyliel and Nyal respectively), about 82,572 people have been affected in Mayendit County and 48,295 have been affected in Guit County. Majority of people especially girls and women in Panyinjiar, Mayendit and Guit Counties of Unity State drops out of school in early primary-level due to cultural related factors such as early marriages, early pregnancies and demand for bride wealth. Existing community infrastructures such as school serves as a unique entry point for protection of children and other lifesaving services, including access to safe drinking water, food, AWD/cholera prevention, and protection mechanisms reaching one of the most vulnerable groups - children. This project will be targeting children between the ages of 3-18 years for ECD, primary school and the ALP. It will involve provision of school materials, support to teachers for incentives, induction of teachers, Training and support to PTAs and SMCs, establishment and support to school clubs, renovation of classrooms and construction of TLSs, plus sanitation facilities (Latrines).
