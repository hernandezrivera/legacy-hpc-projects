Project Summary

The project aims to improve access to food and contribute to improving social cohesion and supporting early recovery and building resilience of vulnerable and food-insecure communities affected by the Boko Haram crisis and violence in the Far North region. This project will address the immediate food needs of 40,000 vulnerable people, including refugees, internally displaced persons, returnees, and local populations who are severely affected by the 03 crises mentioned above, disrupting economic, agricultural, and pastoral activities in these regions. The 40,000 beneficiaries will be distributed as follows: 30,000 people to be assisted under the FFA for early recovery and 10,000 people by the FFA for Resilience. The implementation of livelihood activities, community participatory planning (CBPP) sessions are conducted in collaboration with local community members (women and men) to identify the real needs and priorities of local communities that will help affected populations develop their own response strategies and build resilience. This people-centred approach ensures the provision of effective solutions with beneficiaries and reduces reliance on relief.
Assistance through for FFA, will be implemented using cash modality and in-kind Food for three months under the FFA-early recovery, and six months for the FFA-Resilience. Therefore, the cash transfer value is aligned with the in-kind food ration. It considers beneficiaries' food preferences for the commodities and prices on the local market. The transfer value is calculated based on the identified food gap and is designed to assist beneficiaries to reach the recommended minimum calories intake of 2,100 Kcal per person per day. The use of cash-based transfers is expected to improve the cost-effectiveness of WFP's operations, while seizing the economic opportunities of local markets, with particular consideration for the beneficiaries’ choice and dignity.
WFP will collaborate closely with Government technical ministries, cooperating partners and communities to ensure project implementation and sustainability. Nutrition, Gender and protection issues are taken into consideration at all the stages of the project elaboration, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Gender and protection risk analysis will be carried out and the most vulnerable will be targeted, including female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities.
Project sites will be in secure areas and at convenient distance from the homes of the beneficiaries.
To support smooth project implementation and ensure sustainability, capacity strengthening sessions will be organized for all stakeholders on gender and protection policy, project management, monitoring and nutrition.

1 - Besoins / Needs

WFP Cameroon under the framework of the Country Strategic Plan (CSP) 2022 - 2026 will implementing a series of activities with the support of partners, to deliver food assistance to refugees and internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and local vulnerable population as well as to improve the resilience of vulnerable communities. Early recovery and resilience activities are geared towards the implementation of strategic objective (SO) 1 & 3 of the CSP which ultimately aims to achieve the Sustainable Development Goal 2 of ‘Zero Hunger’. The goal of these activities is to address immediate food needs of vulnerable individuals and households including refugees, returnees and local communities, while helping them to reinforce their livelihoods, to reduce the risk to natural disaster and to build resilience to present and future shocks. All these activities include the provision of food assistance to vulnerable persons where general food distributions have considerably reduced or stopped.
