Project Summary

Due to the protracted and complex humanitarian crises facing communities in the NW SW regions and the protection needs and gaps identified by IRC and partners, IRC is proposing a 12-month holistic project to respond to humanitarian needs in 3 protection domains : child protection, women’s protection and general protection (rule of law).
Focusing on hard-to-reach areas within both the NW and SW, IRC will support survivors of human rights violations (adults, women and children ) and survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) through protection monitoring and advocacy activities, tailored case management services for adults, women and children including survivors of GBV, IGA for GBV survivors to support to promote resilience, specialised and non-specialised PSS services, capacity-building and awareness-raising targeting different audiences (community and government actors) on protection issues to improve the protection environment and also to enhance sustainability of the intervention.
IRC expects a deterioration in the security and access environment during the run-up to the 2025 presidential election – something we’ve already started to see in the hardening of positions from both NSAGs and the military and civil authorities in the NWSW in the last six months1. This will require flexibility and adaptations to service delivery to meet new needs as the crisis evolves.
Building on the experience gained through IRC’s implementation and community acceptance in the NW and SW regions IRC/Cameroun has the tools and the experience to work with affected communities and partners on reviewing and adapting activities to to the changing contexts.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The humanitarian context in the Northwest and Southwest regions of Cameroon (NWSW) is becoming increasingly complex. The HNO/HRP show that although the number of persons in need in the NWSW has stayed on the same level (1.2 million) from 2022 to 2023 (HNO 2023), however, the severity of their needs is increasing. As the crisis continues, with no political solution in sight, peoples’ coping strategies are worsening. The humanitarian needs are acute in city centres, but even more so in rural hard-to-reach areas which are controlled by numerous Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs). Lockdowns and roadblocks set up by NSAGs and military significantly populations’ freedom of movement and therefore access to basic services. The action is informed by the following needs and gaps : The protection environment in the NWSW remains particularly volatile. Protection issues are on the rise. The most common protection issues identified and documented by IRC and other protection actors include movement restrictions, theft, destruction of property, physical attacks, extortion, murder, kidnapping for ransom, unlawful detention, and threats of violence. Between January and July 2023, IRC identified and documented 804 protection incidents. These incidents include 21% extorsion of property and illegal taxes, 17% torture and inhuman treatment, 10% GBV, 11% of properties destruction, 10,5% of killing/murder, 8% physical assaults and 6% arbitrary arrests. Protection incidents recorded by status shows that 36% of host community members suffered from rights violation compared to 29% of IDP and 35% of returnees (Monthly Protection Monitoring Reports, IRC). Lack of civil documentation has been identified as a key element which increases exposure to arbitrary arrest and detention, or illegal taxation at checkpoints, especially for boys and men, since lack of civil documentation is interpreted by regular forces as a perceived sign of supporting armed groups. In addition to victims of human rights violation, people living with disabilities are more likely to be exposed to danger from attacks, due to barriers to escaping and staying out of harm’s way, and because of the degradation of the support systems which existed before the crisis. The protection envirnment has been worsen also by disruptions to education, skills and self-employment opportunities which often leads youth to gravitate towards recruitment by NSAGs. An estimated 24% of youth in NW & SW have lost their sources of income because of conflict-related insecurity and displacement (ILO, 2022). Despite the activation of the education cluster (2018), and the provision of emergency education support by educational partners – although often concentrated in easier-to-reach urban areas; the on-going conflict and imposed lockdowns have continued to worsen the overall education and protection environment especially for children and youth, and protection and reintegration needs (notably education) remain high. For example lockdowns and displacement: Lockdowns and no-school policy by NSAGs forced 68% of schools in NW and 38% in NW to close-down (2021-2022 academic year) leaving thousand of children without education (OCHA Sept, 2023). Grave forms of child rights violations have continued to be reported as well as attacks on education and educational personnel and teachers have left the profession due to increasing insecurity and loss of livelihoods due to displacement and school closures. The Overall Goal of the project is to ensure that communities most affected by the crisis - especially survivors of protection incidents have safe and continuous access to high-quality services. This goal will be achieved through the following expected result: Expected Result : Protection/Safety: Crisis-affected population including school age girls and boys, women and girls, and victims of human rights violations in the NW & SW regions of Cameroon have their most urgent protection needs met in a safe environment.
