Project Summary

The current context in the oPt is marked by imposed closures and the stop of peace negotiations between the Israelis and Palestinians. The West Bank is undergoing an acute crisis and continuous demonstrations and clashes. In the Gaza Strip, blockades and political divide continue to marginalize the population. Salary cuts and early retirement have directly impacted most sectors in general, but particularly the education sector. In the 2019 HNO, the Education cluster estimates that children with disabilities are the most affected by the protracted and acute crisis: they lack basic assistive devices that would facilitate their learning in the classroom, and are in need of Psychosocial Support (PSS) and remedial education. Since 2014, HI has strived to improve the access of children with disabilities to education in Gaza. HI also supports 3 special schools to develop child friendly and accessible education environments, to improve the delivery of adapted education services responding to children’s individual needs. In Gaza, the Middle Area and Rafah, HI develops emergency response plans provides PSS and organizes inclusive summer camps in Gaza. Furthermore, HI closely collaborates with mainstream education service providers to build teachers’ capacities to adopt inclusive education strategies. In 2017, HI started directly supporting the capacity-building of educational staff and implemented activities aiming to make schools more accessible. HI also provided specialized education materials and developed technical guidelines on the inclusion of children with disabilities in education. The project’s overall objective is to enhance the access of children with disabilities to inclusive education services in emergencies. This project falls under HRP strategic objectives 2 and 3 and under Education Objectives 1) Increase equitable and safe access to inclusive, quality educational services for vulnerable children and 2) Most vulnerable schools are supported in responding to emergencies through preventive measures, remedial programs and school-based psychosocial services. During this project, HI will work with and technically support 3 partners. The project will respond to the education cluster objectives through the provision of personal support for children with disabilities: enhance their mobility, provide visual and hearing assistive devices, transportation, school kits, education materials and stationaries, school-based structured individual and group psychosocial services. HI will also support the provision of holistic services through referrals to other stakeholders. Furthermore, HI will aim to develop an enabling educational environment through the provision of technical support for mainstream and special education service providers. These will be supported to respond to the needs of affected children through the development of adapted processes and tools benefiting both children with and without disabilities. HI will further provide educational materials, tools and resources and create a welcoming and accessible educational environment for children with and without disabilities. The project will be implemented through partnerships with 3 local organizations in Gaza.

Needs

In the oPt, and based on the education cluster 2019 HNO, around 0.5 million children across the oPt face challenges in accessing quality education in a safe, child-friendly environment, The ongoing restrictions on the movement of people and goods, and recurrent conflict in the Gaza Strip have affected 480,013 basic, secondary and kindergarten (KG) students and teachers. In Gaza, the education sector is experiencing the worst year due to UNRWA and the Ministry of Education and Higher Education’s significant budget cuts. The UNRWA financial crisis and internal divide in the oPt resulted in huge needs for stationary and educational materials, a lack of budget for teachers’ capacity building, and a down scaling of mental health programs. Furthermore, the average number of students in classrooms has considerably increased, classrooms overcrowded and schools are now operating in a double and some in triple shifting. Since Great March of Return on March 30th 2018, 191 people have been killed and 20,833 injured by the Israeli forces . In May 2018, HI started responding to the needs of injured persons. In September 2018, HI’s database indicated that among 214 injured children of school age, 23% are expected to develop permanent disabilities. The 2017 census of the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics indicated that 35,675 children with disabilities live in the oPt, 27.4% of which have never accessed education. According to UNICEF OoSC report published in 2018, 1 in 3 children with disabilities aged 6 to 9 and about 1 in 2 children with multiple disabilities of the same age do not attend school. In addition, 32.5 % of children with disabilities are out of school. Girls with disabilities aged 10-15 are out of school at higher rates (36.6 %) than boys (26.3%). Access of children with disabilities to pre-schools is very limited due to the time required for families to nd medical solutions for their children and due to the lack of knowledge of pre-school staff about how to include children with disabilities and meet their needs. This study also shows that those enrolled are likely to drop-out of school as their education progresses, especially children with disabilities aged 15-18 (48.5%) and those aged 12-14 (29.1%). Girls with disabilities face additional barriers due to the cultural and traditional norms in their communities, which result in them being mostly hidden. In Gaza, a rapid survey conducted by HI in 3 special schools indicated that the dropout rate increased by 5% between March and May 2017, while another 32 children with disabilities faced an increased risk of dropping out. Furthermore, HI observed a lack of referral pathways and transition mechanism for children with disabilities between special and/or mainstream pre-schools. In July 2018, PACF, a special school for children with multiple disabilities, was damaged by an airstrike near Al-Kateba. The school year started without rehabilitation of the school premises, which emotionally affected students.

Activities

All activities will be implemented in line with HI’s Do No Harm and child protections policies and the intervention areas of the Joint Protection and advocacy Strategy. These include PSS, school transportation, and adaptations to infrastructures. The action includes two components to reduce the vulnerability and risks behind the exclusion of children with disabilities from accessing services. The first component aims to ensure that children with disabilities access inclusive education services. This will be achieved through the provision of assistive devices, financial support for transportation, the provision of educational materials and stationary, school based individual and group PSS, recreational activities and external referrals to other service providers. The second component will aim to build the capacities of mainstream and special education service providers to respond to the needs of affected children. This will be done through the development of adapted processes and tools benefiting children with and without disabilities, the provision of educational materials, tools and resources and the creation of a welcoming and accessible environment for children with and without disabilities. ER1: Children with disabilities have an enhanced access to a functional and productive learning environment 1.1 Installation of solar power system in 2 special schools in Gaza in order to ensure that the education environment is functional and productive for both boys and girls with disabilities (Responsive) 1.2 Provision of safe transportation for vulnerable boys and girls with disabilities to enable their access to schools and reduce their risk to dropout from schools (Remedial) 1.3 Provision of educational and stationary materials to girls and boys of poor and vulnerable households to maintain their access to education (Responsive)1.4 Provision of mobility, hearing, visual and educational assistive devices for boys and girls with disabilities in need in Gaza to overcome environmental barriers preventing equitable access to education (Remedial) 1.5 Provision of psycho-social, inclusive open days and recreational activities for boys and girls with/without disabilities and their female caregivers, a particular focus on girls and women due to emotional, cultural and social barriers they are facing, caregivers and teachers (Responsive) 1.6 Provision of educational counselling support for boys and girls children with disabilities and male and female teachers to respond to the individual needs of children (Responsive) 1.7 Support referral pathways to identified boys and girls with disabilities to specialized rehabilitation services, special education services, and mainstreaming schools in collaboration with the existing referral networks of HI partners, that will improve their inclusion in the regular schools (Remedial). ER2: Mainstream education stakeholders provide inclusive responses to address the needs of children with disabilities 2.1 Identify the barriers and potentials of children with disabilities in particular girls and adolescent girls with disabilities to access mainstream schools in the Gaza Strip (Environmental building) 2.2 Support preschools, mainstream and special schools to develop an adapted and accessible environment for boys and girls children with/without disabilities (Remedial) 2.3 Provision technical support to male and female the staff and teachers of preschools in Gaza and schools in Gaza to respond to the personalized individual needs of boys and girls children with disabilities (Remedial) 2.4 Establish and test the coordination and monitoring mechanisms between preschools, mainstream and special schools through inclusion units, referral networks and pathways that will support the inclusion of boys and girls with disabilities in education (Remedial) 2.5 Provision of technical and financial support to schools in the targeted areas through awareness raising. 2.6 Provide technical support to Gaza sub-cluster members

Indicators

According to HI’s Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation (PME) policy, the teams are applying a project quality framework which commits to 12 quality criteria. In line with the policy, the following system will be established: - Planning: set up of project steering committees, project steering tools, and quarterly risk assessments. - Monitoring: project quarterly reviews, monthly data collection, beneficiary database, administrative and financial tools, and regular self-assessments. - Evaluation: satisfaction monitoring visits, and final participatory evaluations. Indicators of Expected Result 1 - 902 (420 boys and 365 girls) students and (36 males and 81 females)teachers in Gaza benefit from the provision of material support, the optimization of existing learning spaces and teaching/learning supplies. - 981 (253 boys and 199 girls) of students with disabilities, (64 men and 465 women) parents and school staff in Gaza benefit from school-based psychosocial support services. - 702 (324 girls and 378 boys)children with and without disabilities in Gaza participate and benefit from inclusive open days and extra-curricular activities. - 150 (45% girls and 55% boys) children with disabilities in Gaza benefit from assistive devices. - 45 (25 boys and 20 girls) children with disabilities benefit from inclusive education. - 70% of identified out-of-school children successfully referred to other special and mainstream service providers based on their specific needs. Indicators of Expected Result 2 - 1 situational assessment on the barriers and potentials of children with disabilities in particular girls and adolescent girls with disabilities to access mainstream schools in the Gaza Strip developed. - 452 (199 girls and 253 boys) children with disabilities and 77 (63 females and 14 males) educational staff in Gaza benefit from accessibility works in pre-school and schools . - 77 (63 females and 14 males) pre-school and school staff improve their capacities to respond to the personalized psychosocial and educational needs of children with disabilities. - ER 2 OVI 4: 1200 (600 women and 600 men) will be exposed to awareness raising sessions on rights of boys and girls with disabilities to access inclusive education. - 20 (10 females and 10 males) cluster members improve their capacities to plan, program and mainstream inclusive education for children with disabilities.
