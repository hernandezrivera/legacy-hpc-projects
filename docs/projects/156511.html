Project Summary

With the deterioration of the living conditions of the camps’ residents, and lack of funds to maintain an adequate shelter. IYD conducted a rapid needs assessment in the Kherbet Eljoz and Atmeh camps located in northwest Syria, as the result, IYD designed this project, to improve the tents grounds isolation by raising 11000 tents 20 cm up the ground by fixing bricks around the tent and fill the middle space by graveling and soft sand to be compacted manually. In this way, tents will be protected from flooding, a layer of gravelling will be laying around the tents from outside to forcing the water passing without entering the tents. The main activity will provide shelter services for the IDPs through the following steps:
. install bricks around the tents.
. fill the space with gravel and soft sand.
. laying and squeezing the gravel and sand in the middle of the tents to make a fixed base to the tents.
The technical team during the project cycle life will take responsibility for the qualitative and quantitative for delivered goods and daily observing for the activities and provide a daily report about the progress of the activities in the field.
IYD is considered the environmental impact as a key factor, and follow the cluster's standards to avoid any negative impact on the environment, therefore the proposed activities were planned to have no environmental impact on the project area, so the insulation ground that we are going to install is removable, and has zero impact on the land and soil, which means there will be no external effects on the project environmental features.
Throughout the implementation of this project, IYD will mainly work on the following activities :
* Conduct needs assessment in different IDP camps to confirm the exact number of tents for insulation.
*Preparing the beneficiaries list via selection criteria in coordination with camps management.
*Preparing the technical specification of the materials and tender documentation to launch the announcement for contracting with a vendor.
*Contracting with the vendor and start the tents ground insulation.
*Supervision the implementing in the project areas.
*Receiving the insulation materials.
*Storage of the materials in the warehouse to be a distribution to the beneficiaries in time.
*Conduct post assessments using mobile data collection applications.

Needs

According to HNO 2019, Of the estimated 6.2 million internally displaced persons in Syria, an estimated 871,150, or 14 per cent of all IDPs, are living in IDP sites where they receive humanitarian assistance and services. Of these, almost half (420,103 IDPs) are in the northwest of the country, the majority of whom are located in Idleb Governorate. Between September 2017, and August 2018, the CCCM cluster has tracked almost 1.1 million population movements across northern Syria – a significant increase from the 804,300 reported in the 2018 HNO. Approximately 25 and 26 per cent of the population in sites do not receive adequate shelter and NFI assistanceKherbet Eljoz located at the western part of Idlib governorate in the countryside, 25 km far away from Jisr Ash Shugur city. According to the quick study done by IYD team in Kherbet Eljoz , there are 51 camps contain 4000 tents spread over the mountain, valley and the plain, forming mountain slope terraced, near to the Turkish border. The 51 camps divided into three sectors, the first in Zof village contains 11 groups, the second in the south part of Kherbet Eljoz contains 11 groups, the third sector in Kherbet Eljoz contains 13 groups. 60% of the tents in the camps was isolated by a thin layer of cement (5 cm in maximum), this thin layer now has a lot of cracks allow water to pass into the surface, all camps suffer from this problem. The neediest thing to be done there is to isolate the tents of Kherbet Eljoz sector because of the nature of the land it is moist soil (clay land), more accurately, Salah Aldeen camps consist of 550 tents need to isolation. The information from the field reported that Saed charity Organization will intervene in Zof sector and Syria relief will intervene in the south sector. In Darkoush sub-district there are three camps Aleppo 1, 2, and 3 contain 150 tents based on clay land near Al-Asi river need to isolation.Atmeh camps are located near the Turkish border consist of two parts; the north sector and the south sector with uncovered sanitation line separates between them. The north sector located in a hill and all tents in good condition, the south sector located on farmlands with red soil, all tents had exposed to flood during the last rainstorm, 60% of the tents need isolation. Based on that, it is crucial and important to target the above-mentioned camps with ground insulation to prevent the risks stemming from the flood, soil sliding and absorption, as well as improving the shelter conditions of the families residing in these tents.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions :-112560 beneficiaries can benefit from the tent insulation providing that the security situation and access will be conducive for IYD to deliver the assistance. -BAH and BAS borders will continue to remain open, but temporary closure might delay deliveries.- Partners will elaborate on different plans for deliveries allowing for flexible dispatching of goods when certain areas are inaccessible.- Contingency areas in other locations will be pre-identified with the CCCM cluster during project design.- The weather conditions will not affect project implementation.Risks and Mitigation-IDPs remove insulation for possible resale in local markets in order to gain cash liquidity. Although the removal and sale of insulation materials will potentially have a high impact, the risk is relatively low based on past experience, and IYD will mitigate this risk by fastening insulation within the tent using the toolkits (best practice that is now programmed), as well as raising the beneficiaries' awareness about the benefits of the insulation such as reducing fuel costs, fire risks, etc.-HLP issues might be found within the camps we intend to target. IYD will mitigate this risk by making both the camps’ managers and the landlords sign consent forms which expressly provide the permission of IYD to conduct the project activities. In addition, IYD will consult the HLP group and CCCM.
