Project Summary

The project aims to increase the accessibility to GBV services by women and girls with the assistance of WGSS community centers in selected project areas. Orange will provide improved and specialized GBV services through 3 WGSS located in Idleb City Center, Maaret Al Numan and Kafr Nobol. Orange will establish new 3 WGSS in mentioned areas. The project activities will include supporting staff with core GBV terminology, essential GBV training, communication skills, referral pathways, PFA, PSEA in global standards, and providing case management, GBV prevention activities and information, livelihood and life skills training, psychosocial support, individual/group counselling sessions, awareness sessions to the GBV survivors at the same time. The project will supervise for reduce the risk and ensure safety for the GBV survivors with regular safety audits, FGDs feedback and its action taking mechanisms, and community mapping activities.

Needs

Assessments have indicated that gender-based violence, particularly sexual harassment, early/forced marriage and domestic violence (including intimate partner violence and other forms of family violence against women and girls) continue to pervade the lives of women and girls in Syria inside and outside the home, resulting in very few spaces where women and girls feel safe. The length of the crisis and deep-rooted social norms, in conjunction with the mounting lawlessness in some areas, are normalizing this violence, resulting in the detrimental psychosocial wellbeing and continued erosion of women’s and girls’ rights. The fear of sexual harassment (on the way to school, in the market, at distribution sites, etc.) as well as sexual violence, often associated with kidnapping, is a concern raised by consulted women and girls and contributes to psychosocial stress and limits their movements. Girls and boys of all ages still face violence in multiple life situations: at home, at school, at work and in the community. Adolescence, in particular, is a time when exposure to new forms of violence increases. Adolescent boys are more likely to be killed and injured, detained and recruited by parties to the conflict or to be involved in child labor, while girls are increasingly at risk of being recruited and used by parties to the conflict in some locations. Adolescent girls are particularly at risk of early marriage and other forms of gender-based violence, including sexual violence. As is the case globally, women and girls are disproportionately affected by all forms of gender-based violence in Syria, in both the public and private spheres. In addition, women who have experienced divorce and widowhood and women and girls with disabilities continue to be exposed to stigmatization and discrimination, as well as forced marriages. They also face specific challenges in accessing assistance (feelings of shame and risks of sexual harassment) (HNO,2019 Draft). FGD participants in Idleb, especially women and girls identified various consequences of violence such as; lack of safety thus movement restriction, need to be accompanied by a chaperon when leaving the home by adolescent girls,fear of kidnapping, isolation, depression, aggression and perpetual fear. Additionally, some FGD participants discussed nervous breakdowns and suicide. There are data from GBV experts show that although the access to the GBV services increased in Idlib Governorate, GBV survivors encounter barriers to access them for several reasons such as intimate family do not allow them to access services as it considered as disclosing marital secrets, husband's refusal, stigma and gossip. (GBV Voices from Syria, 2018)

Implementation of activities

Objective: Survivors have access to quality specialized GBV services across project areas through the mobile teams and static facilities. Increase measures to prevent and mitigate risks of GBV The project intends to increase the availability and accessibility of GBV services. Orange will support female and male headed households, adolescent girls, divorcees, widows, and female headed women living with disabilities. Orange will show special attention to GBV survivors living with disabilities on the purpose of inclusion of GBV survivors to GBV services. Orange will lead the mechanisms of GBV activities and ensure the quality of the GBV response through strengthening the referral mechanisms (including mental health), capacity building of non- protection and protection staff and GBV actors, training of staff on GBV concepts, essentials and prevention, GBV prevention activities, awareness sessions, individual/group counselling sessions, psychosocial support, livelihood and life skills training, and case management. Orange will also deliver dignity kits to women and adolescent girls. The following main activities will be conducted as a part of the project: 3.1.2 Provide specialized GBV services to survivors3.1.3 Increase the capacity of GBV actors to respond to GBV through a survivor-centred approach and inclusive approaches3.2.1 Provide activities empowering women and girls and GBV prevention activities3.2.2 Provide safe spaces for women and girls3.2.3 Provide livelihood opportunities to empower women and girls3.2.4 Provide GBV programming to adolescent girls3.3.1 Engage and train humanitarian actors (non-specialist) to reduce risks of GBV and refer to specialised services3.3.2 Increase (field and national level) advocacy to reduce risk of GBV and increase mitigation strategies MEAL planning and implementation is based on procedures and quality standards defined in internal Orange MEAL Policy and guidelines. Each project has a MEAL Plan developed between the project and MEAL staff as part of project cycle management. Initial MEAL planning is completed before commencing the implementation to ensure that MEAL activities are well incorporated into the work schedule and that the MEAL budget is fully reconciled with the project budget. Data quality is assured by triangulation of multiple quantitative and qualitative sources, and use of electronic data collection software to minimize errors and allow for real-time data quality checks.Orange M&E staff will be in charge to execute the M&E plan for this project, which will include the following elements.- Monitoring the outputs mentioned in the logical framework.- Preparing the M&E reports for each activity on monthly basis supported with photos and the means of verification mentioned in the indicators. - Following up the financial reports and ensure that all the processes are compatible with Orange financial policies.- Interim narrative and financial reports.- Making sure that all the activities have no harm or bad impact on the local community. On the other hand, Orange will ensure the community engagement in monitoring the activities by setting a complaints and suggestions box to get the direct feedback from them in transparent way, those complaints and suggestions will be addressed from the Turkey offices of Orange and will be taken in consideration to correct any deviation might occur.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk 1: Lack of quality services and referral expose survivors to harm, enhancing risk of stigmatization reprisal and further trauma. Lack of qualified services lead to fewer disclosures and lack of trust in services. Risk of GBV service providers safety and security because of their role. Privacy worries along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women and girls to access GBV response services in the WGSSMitigation Measure 1: Service mapping including only those that can provide survivor centered services. Promotion of SOPs roll out for all staff of organizations involved in the referral Traditional legal systems put women more at risk, message to be mainstreamed Self-care strategies as part of programmes and organization HR policy GBV case management service providers ensure confidentiality principles are in line with global standards and the GBV SOPs, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. Protection Risk 2: Resistance or backlash from sections of the community and community leaders on GBV messaging. Backlash from family or community on women participating in recreational, vocational or empowerment. Lack of support from family members to allow women and girls to travel to the WGSS and access services. Female headed beneficiaries with disability have difficult accessing GBV services and the WGSSMitigation Measure 2: Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders Ensure the support of male family members by engaging them in awareness-raising activities Adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure sensitivity to the context and culture Thorough gender analysis prior to start of programming Equip all WGSS with child-care servicesProvide transportation support for persons with disability Include persons with disability in all conducted assessments at the level of WGSS and/or community to understand their needs and gaps in their access to servicesEnsure all WGSS staff are properly trained on how to identify persons with disability in need of treatment for a timely referral to relevant services. Protection Risk 3: Resistance or backlash from community for a woman only space.Mitigation Measure 3: Community engagement from onset of project, from assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders Protection Risk 4: Livelihood opportunities may increase risks of GBV. Income expectations may be unrealistic and might create dependency if so problemsExploitation by service provider or by other actor in the communityPressure on the service provider from the local authorities on who should or should not receive the service and favouritismMitigation Measure 4: Consult women and men, boys and girls in the design of the programme.Ensure beneficiaries know services where to disclose if violence occurs. Raise awareness on PSEA and other types of exploitation and where to report. Establish good relationship with local authorities, clarify humanitarian principles and stick to them.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: Unforeseen changes in the conflict cause not safely access to the project locations and the project staff get besieged in one area of control or operational assets are lost because of conflict changesMitigation Measure 1: Continue to gather information from range of sourcesDevelop contingency plan to work in alternative location if conflict continues. Continuous efforts by the access teams to gain new assurances and connections to secure humanitarian space. Risk 2: Low quality of programming includes limitations on direct monitoring, statistical data, percentages, samples and tight data related to gender, age or protection issues etc. This is the setting for a decline in program quality unless remote management tools are accurately employed.Mitigation Measure 2: Have monitoring and data collection staff from the same community and start the hiring process prior to kicking off the project implementation of activities. Deterioration of the security situation. Risk 3: Regarding to money laundering risk by money transfer agents, Orange Organization have no way to assess the specific risks. Given the comparative resources to undertake such research it seems like that this risk could best be checked by government institutions rather than NGOs. In the current situation, the use of the money transfer (hawala) system as the “least risk” option, as we can neither access the banking system or formal money traders in Syria.Mitigation Measure 3: Dealing with Hawala agents, which are pre-verified agents by other NGOs operating in the area. Risk 4: Threat of bribes and payments elevates in areas where law and order is low and non-regimented or non-disciplined forces administer an area. It is frequently seen at checkpoints. In a conflict situation, it is common that aid is used to enable a position of power and we can expect that individuals may misuse their position.Mitigation Measure 4: The staff sign code of conduct and conflict of interest protocols. Additionally, the staff are trained on Humanitarian Principles along with finance, logistics and compliance procedures. Risk 5: Detention or arrest has been one of the most reoccurring security incidents in Syria since 2011. It occurred in regime, opposition and ISIS controlled areas. This also includes Syrian staff in Lebanon and potentially in Turkey. Detentions can be for any reason or duration. All must be managed on a case-by-case basis. Detentions can be both individuals and materials. The mistreatment of detainees is a real threat.Mitigation Measure 5: Staff tracking and daily check-ins. Actor mapping and connections established in all areas. Staff training on visibility and communications practices. Hiring project staff from the same community to avoid any type of conflict.
