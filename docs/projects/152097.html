Project Summary

This project will support 3,000 women and youth (especially female youth) affected by the conflict in Borno and Yobe States to acquire contemporary innovative and context-demand skills that enhance their economic recovery and facilitate their leadership and participation in peace processes. It will provide access to acquisition of vocational skills, rice and vegetable farming, including processing of products for value chain; as well as other income generating activities (IGAs) and livelihoods options. Similarly, the project will support adolescent girls and young women who have never attended any formal education to access functional literacy/basic education and market-relevant technical skills to increase employment, livelihood and entrepreneurial opportunities and self-reliance. It will further strengthen community social-cohesion through strengthening community structures to promote empowerment of adolescent girls and young women's access to basic services that further build their resilience. The project will also influence socio-cultural transformation to reduce barriers women and girls face in accessing basic services, support improvement of policies, institutional capacity and financing framework to facilitate access to market-relevant vocational skills for conflict-affected young women and girls. A significant part of the project will build on existing mechanisms to ensure long-term continuity and sustainability. Existing vocational training centres will be identified and strengthened to provide training to adolescent girls and young women.
With regards engagement in peace processes, the project will train beneficiaries on conflict resolution, reconciliation and peacebuilding, as well as engage communities (including men and boys as gender champions) to create an enabling environment for the active participation of women and youth in peacebuilding.

Needs

The violence and destruction in North-East Nigeria as a result of the conflict, has devastated social and economic stability and displaced about 26% of conflict-affected households. An estimated 28% of these conflict-affected households have lost economic opportunities and sources of livelihood. According to UNHCR Vulnerability Screening (2017), 63% of vulnerable households within the most conflict-affected areas in the North-East are female-headed. Also, many women who were previously engaged in the agriculture sector have lost sources of livelihoods as a result of displacement. With limited skills for employability and carrying the burden of child care often single-handedly, many women are at high risk of adopting negative coping mechanisms including survival sex, to gain financial resources to support themselves and their families. As the security situation relatively improves, some Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) are now returning to their communities, whilst others are still staying in host communities with the hope of returning later to their communities of origin or integrating into host communities. In 2018, about one million IDPs remain in host communities while 1.6 million have returned to their LGAs of origin in Borno (Damboa, Konduga, Monguno) Adamawa (Madagali, Mubi North, Hong) and Yobe (Gujba, Potiskum, Fika) States. Even though these communities are not experiencing active fighting and therefore considered safe for returns, there are limited access to basic services, limited income and livelihood opportunities, and increased resource demands which prevent voluntary returns and also cause societal tensions among IDPs, returnees and host communities (Source: Multi-sector needs assessments and Livelihoods and Economic Recovery Assessments). The current transitioning of the conflict from a purely humanitarian to that of a recovery phase has seen an expansion of development actors in the operational context, as the humanitarian-development nexus approach takes hold. Conflict-affected women and youth need to be provided with vocational skills training, livelihoods and economic empowerment options to curtail negative coping mechanisms and reduce their vulnerability. Empowering them will also restore their self-confidence and dignity and build their resilience for recovery, reintegration and rehabilitation which correspond to humanitarian-development nexus requirements. Also, the participation of women and youth is key in conflict resolution, reconciliation and peacebuilding efforts, as communities have been socially divided and there is high mistrust, especially towards those suspected of association with non-state armed groups. These interventions are crucial for restoring social cohesion and creating durable solutions in affected communities. It is also important to note that the changing trends of the crisis have generated the need for a more focused intervention on recovery and transformative conflict resolution and peacebuilding efforts. This has opened up new opportunities to engage women and youth and build their leadership to contribute to peacebuilding and community reconciliation efforts.

Activities

The project activities are divided into two main components: 1. Economic Empowerment: Skills training and Income-Generation Activities/Livelihoods throughProviding vocational skills-training for women and youth; creating access to IGAs to boost their social-economic capital and self-reliance. Assessments will be undertaken to determine preferred and innovative context-demand-driven vocational and income generating activities for women and youth, including non-traditional skills normally available at vocational centres. Upon completion of training, beneficiaries will be supported to form cooperatives; provided with start-up kits/small grants for the establishment of micro/small-scale enterprises. In addition, drip irrigation technology will be provided to women to support vegetable production during dry season for increased profit. Women and young mothers will be supported with alternative child care options to provide them time for their training/businesses. Activities include:• Undertake an assessment on preferred vocational skills and IGAs/livelihood options by women and youth and existing vocational skills centres as well as potential employers• Equip 800 female youth and 200 male youth with vocational skills • Strengthen existing vocational training program delivery mechanisms • Train 1200 women/female youth and 200 boys/young men on literacy and numeracy skills• Train 1,000 women on marketable income generation skills of their choice• Train 1,000 women on entrepreneurship, value chain and access to market strategies• Organize women into 50 groups/cooperatives (40 members per group) and provide them with start-up funds/kits• Train 600 women on improved parboiling techniques (IPT) • Organize women trained on parboiling skills into 10 cooperatives (30 per group) and provide them with basic parboiling equipment/start-up funds• Provide 20 rice milling destoning machines to the cooperatives of women trained on IPT (for value-added processing) • Support 400 women farmers with climate-smart and labor-saving technology, including Drip irrigation for production of high-value vegetables• Support young mothers with context-specific alternative child care facilities (Target to be determined by number of beneficiaries with young children)• Establish a mentoring and coaching system involving young women mentors. 2. Engagement and participation of women and youth in peace processes This component will support the implementation of National and State Action Plans on Resolution 1325 developed in Borno and Yobe States. Women and youth will be trained on mediation and peacebuilding techniques to enhance their capabilities for active participation in these processes at community, state and national levels, including through the media. “Peace Huts” will be established to provide a space for community women and youth to discuss issues of peace, including prevention of gender-based violence (GBV). The project will support women to work in partnership with the police to strengthen community policing, and provide an alternative justice system for rural women, through mediation exercises that adhere to national and international human rights standards. Activities include:• Train 400 women, 200 female youth and 100 male youth on mediation and peacebuilding techniques/recovery processes • Organize advocacy meetings and facilitate the representation/ participation of women and youth in entrepreneurship and vocational learning, community/state peace and recovery structures• Link trained women and youth with media organizations, potential employers and support their participation in economic recovery discussions and the dissemination of peace and reconciliation messages• Establish 8 ‘Peace Huts’ in communities and support their operationalization• Create/consolidate women’s and youth peace networks and facilitate their linkage at various levels • Support women/youth networks to undertake peace missions and exchange visits
