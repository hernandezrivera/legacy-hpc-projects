Project Summary

In early 2020, the humanitarian situation in many parts of North East Syria remains dire with approximately 1.4 million people in need of assistance including an estimated 390,000 people who remain long-term-displaced. Lack of access to safe water and sanitation services continues to affect the health of hundreds of thousands of households. The program seeks to improve WASH services by rehabilitating water stations, networks and sewage systems, undertake vector control, chlorination mapping, emergency WASH in camps and settlements (water trucking, installation of latrines and water distribution points), and sustainable water supply solutions for camps in the form of a water network. There will be a particular focus on Der Ez Zor where it is estimated that around 76,300 individuals are currently displaced and where following the territorial defeat of IS there have been a large number of spontaneous returns to hard-to-reach areas, and where services such as electricity or water supply are very damaged after years of IS rule.

4 - Needs

Findings from the 2019 HNO indicate that 14.9 million people are in need of water and sanitation services throughout Syria. REACH reported that in Deir ez Zor around 97% (in the North line) and 94% (in the East line) of the communities rely on water trucks as the primarily source of drinking water. The cost of monthly water trucking is an estimated USD 20 to 40 per month for a family of five using 60L of water/person/day. Also, Deir ez Zor governorate registered an increasing number of issues with drinking water quality in 88% of the assessed households along the West and the North line, while 72% of the household in the East line reported that the cost represents a barrier to the access to water due mainly to the high cost of transportation from the filling point to the farthest communities. The limited availability of electricity and limited technical and financial capacity of local councils to operate and maintain water supply systems has resulted in a reduced productive capacity of water pumping stations and water supply networks. Many water supply systems in Ar Raqqa and Deir ez Zor governorates along the Euphrates River have been damaged due to the conflict or have stopped working due to lacking maintenance or staff. Many houses are still receiving insufficient, and more alarmingly, unchlorinated and unsafe water. Furthermore, at least 70% of sewage systems are untreated and solid waste removal services from urban areas are disrupted, posing a significant environmental health risk that could result in vectorborne diseases. An assessment by this NGO’s team in 35 villages in Deir ez Zor in early 2019 in the Kisreh sub-district found 2,217 cases of leishmaniasis with children disproportionately affected.

5 - Implementation of activities

This NGO will rehabilitate and re-commission 20 water supply systems and 12 water networks focusing specially in Deir Ez Zour, but working as well in Raqqa and Aleppo governorates. These stations will be rehabilitated (were possible) to meet the designed capacity. To increase the water quality/safety, a total of 30 chlorination systems will be installed in water station lacking chlorination systems or non-functional ones. Chlorine will be provided to these water stations, and to rehabilitated stations as well for the lifetime of the project. Other infrastructure to be rehabilitated will be sewage systems, for a total quantity of 5. In order to detect the most urgent needs this NGO will engage with authorities and the cluster, and will as well prepare exhaustive assessments to clearly identify the needs.The rehabilitation of WASH facilities will ensure that IDPs have access to gender segregated latrines including hand washing stations and that the ratio of people per latrine does not exceed 20 as per SPHERE standards in the Syrian context. Vector Control activities to control the spread of vector borne diseases, specifically Leshmaniasis, will target 30 communities in Deir Ez Zor and Membij, with each community on average comprising 3,000 individuals. This NGO will also distribute 5,000 hygiene kits to to IDP HH in informal IDP camps and collective centers. Hygiene promotion activities will be conducted in these same areas where hygiene kits are distributed. Distributions of NFI/hygiene/shelter kits and food rations/vouchers/cash, plus emergency WASH activities will target all new IDPs displaced within the previous month according to their needs and in coordination with other organizations.The NGO will work with the NES WWG to improve data collection/response mapping by submitting water system technical information to feed into the WWG infrastructure rehabilitation priority list.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Unaccompanied minors as well as female headed households are currently at the greatest risk of protection related issues. They are exposed to a range of different forms of harm, from SGBV, to conflict related trauma and early marriage. Related to WaSH, the design of latrines include, in the blocks for women, privacy walls with basins inside the wall space to provide privacy. All blocks of latrines, not only women’s but men’s as well, have solar lights to reduce the risks of GBV (the lights are included as well in men’s latrines as well to avoid creating a social hub in the women’s latrines by night). This NGO is improving its designs and will include a specific latrine in each block to be used by children. The water distribution points are designed to be used by children with taps at a comfortable height. Accessible latrines and wash facilities are also installed. Any vulnerable protection cases identified by this NGO WASH team in the course of implementing the activities listed above will be referred to other service providers in NES. To aid this, in-house protection specialists will provide comprehensive training to all staff and partners on the referral mechanism and pathways. These protection specialists will also join different field teams (WASH, FSL etc) on a regular basis to provide protection advice and on-the-job mentoring to other staff. This NGO has a specific policy in place that all staff and contractors have to sign and abide, that clearly states the expected behaviour to avoid abuse and exploitation, and any other damage to beneficiaries and program participants.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk - There is a broader risk of more widespread resumption of conflict between the SDF, GOS, TAF and FSA, especially in border districts such as Manbij, Kobane and Tabqa. In 2019, peaking in October, the Turkish Military incursion resulted in the establishment of a “Buffer zone” from Talabied to Ras Al Ain. Further escalation of the conflict, or deterioration in the security situation, in Der Ez Zor as a result of any of the following: IS resurgence particularly in the wake of reduction in support from coalition or redirection of military efforts to other conflict areas; tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict; movement of GoS troops into the area. Assumptions: The security situation will continue to improve in Der Ez Zor, enabling the project to expand activities from the West line to the North Line and East Line. - The self-administration/SDC retains control over key locations west and south of the Euphrates, specifically Manbij and Tabqa. – This project will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly Mitigating Measures this organization has developed, in coordination with other NGO actors and the NGO forum, its own scenario analysis and contingency-planning. In the event of wider political instability, insecurity or resumption of conflict this organizations response would depend on the severity of the scenario. In the event of localized insecurity/access restrictions/power-changes in one or several locations, This organization would likely suspend operations in these areas and shift its resources to its nearest other functional field offices to assist those newly displaced and affected by these events. If international staff had to be scaled down or pulled out completely (due to border access issues, loss of emergency evacuation options), this organization would remotely manage staff and the project
