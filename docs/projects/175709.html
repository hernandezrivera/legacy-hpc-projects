Project Summary

In line with the South Sudan HRP and the FSL Cluster priorities, NPA will support vulnerable and food-insecure Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees and vulnerable host communities in IPC 4 locations in Canal/Pigi, Fangak, Pochalla & Bor South Counties of Jonglei State, Rumbek East, Rumbek Center, Rumbek North, Cueibet, Yirol East, Yirol West and Awerial Counties of Lakes State, Terekeka County of Central Equatoria State and Kapoeta North County of Eastern Equatoria State. The project aims to improve their food security and livelihoods through provision of timely and appropriate main season inputs/kits (Crop, vegetable and fishing), training in improved agronomic and fishing practices. NPA will provide emergency assistance and livelihoods support to save lives and avert total collapse of livelihoods in the target Counties. The provision of agricultural and fishing inputs/kits, training in agricultural production and fishing is expected to improve the food production and in turn the food security of the target households (HHs) thus supporting them to recover from crisis and placing them on an accelerated path to early recovery and resilience to acute shocks.

NPA seeks to support 93,714 HHs (562,285 individuals) in the targeted Counties with agricultural and livelihood kits. Some of the kits will be provided by FAO and some will be procured and distributed directly by NPA. Training on improved agronomic and fishing practices, marketing and group dynamics will be done through model farmers and fisher folk who will undergo a Training of Trainers (ToT) and subsequently train the farmers and fisher folk in their communities. The knowledge gained from these trainings will improve the agricultural and fishing practices of the target HHs thus improving food production, addressing their immediate food security needs and building their resilience to future shocks. The provision of agricultural inputs and fishing kits and subsequent enhancement of agricultural production and fishing is expected to contribute to improvement of the Household Diversity Score (HDDS) and Food Consumption Score (FCS) of the target HHs. The proposed interventions are aligned to the FSL cluster objective 2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support.
NPA will employ a community-based beneficiary selection approach which will entail establishing Project Management Committees (PMC’s) to lead the selection of eligible beneficiaries based on the beneficiary selection criteria. Vulnerable HHs with severe food insecurity including female, child, disabled/chronically ill headed HHs; persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS and survivors of Gender-Based Violence (GBV) will be targeted in this project.
The target communities will be the prime movers of the implementation process through their continuous engagement in the project. NPA will hold regular meetings with the PMC’s and community members and conduct Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) to get their feedback on the project implementation. NPA will ensure that their feedback is incorporated in the implementation of this project and design of future projects. Further, to promote accountability to the affected populations, NPA will use the community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns.

1 - Needs

In 2020, communities in South Sudan were hit hard by the triple shock of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19. According to the South Sudan IPC key messages for the period of October 2020 to July 2021, an estimated 7.24 million people (60% of the population) are likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. During this period, an estimated 2.47 million are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) i.e. acute food insecurity. During the April to July 2021 period, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate further throughout the entire Jonglei State, with an estimated 1.68 million people (84.7% of the population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 876,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity and 792,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. During this period, the entire State is projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity because of further deterioration of the conditions causing food insecurity. All the eleven target counites will likely experience a further deterioration in food insecurity during the April to July 2021 period. During the lean season of April to July 2021, Cueibet, Rumbek Center, Rumbek East, Rumbek North and Yirol East Counties of Lakes State are projected to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, whereas Awerial and Yirol West counties are projected to ne in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. During the same period, Terekeka County in Central Equatoria State and Kapoeta North County in Eastern Equatoria State are projected to be in emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. As per the IPC report, the key drivers of food insecurity include reduced food availability due to flooding, dry spells, crop pests and diseases, insecurity and displacement of farming households which affected most agricultural activities. The ongoing economic crisis and the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic continue to make it difficult for many households to access food from markets because of loss of sources of livelihoods, reduced income, high food prices coupled with high inflation rates in the country. The seasonal deterioration of road infrastructure during the rainy season is expected to affect market functionality by disrupting the timely restocking of markets. The effects of insecurity in parts of the country may to displacement, depletion or loss of assets, and disruption of livelihoods, further contributing to reduced income for purchasing food and essential needs; and further impacts of food utilization that not only affects the health of the population, but also negatively affects availability of labour and leads to reduced income at household level. Therefore, immediate scale-up of multi-sectoral humanitarian assistance to save lives and avert total collapse of livelihoods in the affected counties, particularly those with populations in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4). Furthermore, urgent action is required for populations in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) to protect livelihoods and reduce food consumption gaps. As per the Food Security & Livelihoods severity ranking and people in need for the depth of the lean season May - July 2021, NPA proposes to target areas that fall under IPC 3 and IPC 4 and are considered between priority 1 to 3 of severity ranking.

2 - Project activities

Activity 1 - Project launch and community mobilization: NPA will conduct various meetings with project staff, local authorities, key stakeholders and community representatives to discuss the project objectives, strategies implementation plan, distribution plan and community participation. NPA will furthermore strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria, and how to provide feedback or complains to NPA. Activity 2 - Formation and training of PMCs: NPA will facilitate the formation of the Boma-level PMC’s. Each PMC will comprise of 10 members (5 women, 5 men) who will be trained and empowered to be a voice to and of the targeted beneficiaries as well as participate in beneficiary selection and monitoring of the project implementation. Activity 3 - Beneficiary selection: NPA adopts participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches in identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries through the PMC’s. Under the guidance of NPA, the PMC’s will identify and register the beneficiaries according to NPA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS, Survivors of GBV etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. Activity 4 - Distribution of complementary cropping and livelihoods inputs: NPA will deliver and distribute main season cropping and livelihood kits in the targeted locations. NPA will distribute Crop kits (Maize, Sorghum and Cowpea), vegetable kits and tools (Amaranth, Collard, Okra, Tomato, Onion, Watermelon, Pumpkin and Maloda) and Fishing kits (hooks, monofilament and spools of twine) to the 93,714 targeted HHs (562,285 individuals). 55% of the target HHs will be female-headed. NPA will ensure that gender and protection are mainstreamed throughout the project activities. Provision of complementary cropping and livelihoods inputs is aimed improving production of vegetables, crops and fish hence contributing towards addressing food insecurity and reducing malnutrition rates. The vegetable, crop and fish products will go a long way in restoring the nutritional status of the affected population, through increased food consumption and widened dietary diversity options. Activity 5 - Formation and training of farmers and fisher folk groups: NPA will encourage and facilitate the formation of farmer and fisher folk groups to encourage team work and peer to peer exchange. ToT training on improved agronomic practices, fish preservation, marketing and group dynamics will be provided to 9,373 model farmers and fisher folk. The trained farmers and fisher folk will then sensitize and build the capacity of the other farmers and fisher folk in their communities. Activity 6 - Monitoring and reporting: NPA will conduct routine monitoring activities to gather data on the progress of the project and submit the required reports. NPA will also conduct post distribution monitoring after every distribution to ascertain the appropriateness of the response. Outcome monitoring will be done periodically to assess the changes in the target communities as a result of the project. Feedback from the beneficiaries, lessons learnt and best practices will be used to inform subsequent project activities as well as design of future projects.

3 - Project indicators

NPA South Sudan has a strong in-country monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning unit led by a Monitoring and Evaluation Coordinator. The project's success will be measured through monitoring activities which are strongly linked to program implementation activities under the existing country monitoring and evaluation structure. M&E will be a continuous and collaborative process conducted by NPA staff, communities and other relevant stakeholders. The purpose of the M&E activities will be to track progress against the project indicators and inform managerial and strategic decision-making in order to ensure maximum sustainable impact. In addition, monitoring will assess the project’s progress against output and outcome results to establish how and why desired changes have happened and overall, how the project worked. M&E activities will be guided by the project’s M&E plan and logical framework. A mix of qualitative and quantitative monitoring techniques will be employed in this project to ensure that accurate data is collected and triangulated. Given the fluidity of the situation and the varied activities being implemented, monitoring will be conducted on a daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly basis by program staff, PMC’s, relevant stakeholders depending on the specific activity, output and indicator. The data will then be submitted to the M&E unit for data entry, verification, analysis and management. The data will be used to inform program implementation as well as measuring the level of attainment of the indicators. Periodic meetings involving the relevant stakeholders will be conducted to review the findings of the monitoring activities. The following indicators will be monitored in this project:• Number of HHs (female, child & male headed) receiving agricultural inputs/ vegetable & fishing kits• Quantity and variety of seeds distributed• Quantity and variety of crops produced (outcome)• % women receiving livelihood kits (crop/ veg/ fish);

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

During the community mobilization and sensitization meetings, NPA will provide beneficiaries with information on how to safely and confidentially report SGBV/SEA if and when it occurs. NPA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues of concerns and feedback on the project activities. In line with NPA's Safeguarding Policy on PSEA, NPA will inform the target communities about NPA's staff responsibilities and the expected code of conduct. At the start of the project, NPA will develop a plan on how information about PSEA will be disseminated. In all it's communication activities, NPA will re-emphasize that all the beneficiary entitlements are provided freely and that they should immediately report if and when they’re asked to do or provide anything in exchange of the project’s entitlements. NPA will work closely with community leaders and stakeholders and will sensitize them on how to report any SEA cases that they come across.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

NPA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries voice any issues of concerns and feedback on the project activities. NPA will set up help desks and encourage vulnerable community members to share any feedback they have on the project through face to face meetings. The most vulnerable members will form part of the PDM sample to ensure that their voice is heard.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Through community mobilization and sensitization activities, NPA will endeavor to provide accurate, timely and accessible information to beneficiaries about every aspect of the project including the planned activities, beneficiary entitlements, timelines and how to share feedback or report complains to NPA. Community mobilization will take the form of regular community and bilateral meetings. NPA will also place banners and signposts with project details at strategic points in the community. NPA will actively involve beneficiaries in identifying their priorities and solutions to their challenges to ensure that the voices and concerns of all groups are considered during the project’s design, planning and implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

NPA will maintain a flexible and adaptive approach throughout the project implementation to ensure that the project remains relevant in the target communities. Project adaptations and adjustments will be informed by beneficiaries’ feedback and will be done in consultation with the target communities and other relevant stakeholders.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

While NPA has recently conducted a country-wide operational, security and programmatic analysis, NPA will conduct a further in-depth risk analysis to ascertain the operational, programmatic and security related risks alongside the corresponding mitigation measures before commencement of the project activities. NPA has identified the following protection related risks and analysis.; 8.1: Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services (Likelihood is low and impact is high). To mitigate this risk, NPA will ensure that registration is conducted and assistance provided as close to the beneficiaries as possible. NPA will work with existing community structures and local authorities to ensure that appropriate and locations are identified for project activities so that beneficiaries do not walk for long distances for registration or to receive assistance. PMC's will assist in crowd control during project activities. Activities will be implemented in line with the seasonal calendar to ensure accessibility to project locations. Inputs will be distributed and trainings conducted prior to the rainy season during which some locations are either cut off or inaccessible. 8.5: Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities (likelihood is low, impact is high). As a mitigation measure, NPA will adopt participatory, vulnerability and rights-based approaches throughout the project life-cycle from the onset of the project, consultations, identification, verification and registration of target beneficiaries. Under the guidance of NPA, the PMC’s will identify and register the beneficiaries according to NPA’s vulnerability criteria for targeting food-insecure women/child/chronically ill-headed HHs, People with disabilities, widows, elderly, pregnant/lactating women, orphaned, Persons living with or at risk of HIV/AIDS, Survivors of GBV etc. This will ensure the prioritization of assistance towards those who need it most. 8.6: Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms (likelihood is low, impact is medium). To mitigate this risk, NPA will use a community-based complaints and feedback mechanism to provide a platform for beneficiaries to voice any issues or concerns. NPA will strengthen community communication to ensure beneficiary and non-beneficiary communities are well informed about the project, beneficiary criteria and how to provide feedback or complains to NPA. This will be done through regular meetings with the target communities and where possible use of IEC materials to disseminate information in the target communities about the CRM. 8.8: Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources (likelihood is low, impact is high). NPA's mitigation measure for this risk entails developing and sensitizing the local authorities, PMC's on the beneficiary selection criteria. The beneficiary selection process will be monitored by NPA staff to ensure objectivity and impartiality. PDM's will be conducted to ascertain the objectivity of the beneficiary selection process and appropriate corrective measures will be implemented in case of any issues identified.
