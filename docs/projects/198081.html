Project Summary

This project will provide integrated education and protection support to 6,720 IDP children in urban Yaounde and Douala, improving child development and wellbeing by promoting access to quality, equitable and safe education and strengthening the system to respond to and prevent child abuse, neglect, and exploitation.
This will be achieved through a holistic approach which seeks to:
1) holistically provide access to inclusive, quality education through community-based learning provision and cash for education support
2) provide quality child protection services through child friendly spaces and specialized case management support for acutely at-risk cases such as unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and improved government case management capacity
3) strengthen community-based mechanisms for child protection and education, including through training, coaching and strengthening of referral pathways
This project has been designed on the basis of a recent integrated education-child protection needs assessment conducted by Street Child in Douala, and seeks to directly respond to key issues identified through the community consultations conducted for this assessment. It has also been designed taking into account UNHCR’s strategy of community empowerment and emphasis on exit strategies for programming in Yaounde and Douala, seeking to contribute to strengthening NGO partner, community and government systems to build stakeholders’ capacity to be able to meet the needs of IDP and refugee children independently and at scale in the future.

1 - Besoins / Needs

Since establishing in 2008, Street Child has expanded its operations from Sierra Leone to 16 countries, helping over 520,000 of the hardest to reach children. Street Child’s purpose is to ensure that children are safe, in school and learning. Street Child are specialists in developing solutions for education and protection provision in low-resource environments and emergencies, such as in refugee settlements or in highly impoverished regions, as well as in response to crises, including the COVID-19 pandemic and other protracted conflicts. In complex contexts such as the NE Nigerian protection and displacement crisis and Ugandan refugee settlements, Street Child is one of the leading education and child protection actors delivering efficient, sustainable, integrated programming in close collaboration with local partners and government stakeholders. Our unique partnership model maximizes impact while keeping costs low, and we are recognized as a radical leader in localisation in child protection and education focused projects - trusted by donors such as UNICEF, FCDO, and Education Cannot Wait to spearhead efforts to increase the access, capacities, and inclusion of local actors in international humanitarian efforts. In Cameroon, Street Child has become a leading education in emergencies and child protection in emergencies actor in the North West and South West (NWSW) regions, implementing a $2.1m multi-sectoral UNICEF supported project targeting over 20,000 individuals across 6 divisions and a UNICEF supported child protection case-management project reaching 4,500 children in 2021/2021. Street Child also became one of the first INGOs to implement non-formal education programming in the NWSW in 2021. Our proposed integrated education and child protection project has been designed based on Street Child’s recent assessment of the critical and under supported education and child protection needs amongst IDP communities in Douala (report can be shared on request). Our assessment found that only 31% of IDP children are attending school regularly, and that key barriers to enrolment include lack of identification documents and lack of capacity of bilingual schools to absorb the influx of displaced learners. Household poverty and the costs associated with education are also noted as a significant barrier facing IDP children in the 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO). Street Child’s assessment also found that IDP children, particularly those who are out of school, face significant protection risks with GBV, early pregnancy, child labour, prostitution and arbitrary detainment reported as the most prevalent risks in the communities assessed. For girls, GBV and early/unwanted pregnancy was reported by 55% of respondents, and for boys 73% of respondents identified arbitrary detainment and 71% identified child labour as high risks. There are also high numbers of unaccompanied and separated children and cases of child-headed households, as children displaced to cities alone to find education and income-generating activities. The same barriers to education and protection for IDP children are reported in Yaoundé; financial barriers to education for IDPs, overcrowding in classrooms and high rates of IDPs without documentation are reported (HNO 2021).
