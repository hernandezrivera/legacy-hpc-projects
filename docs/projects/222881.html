Project Summary

The S-CCCM project in Cox’s Bazar will enhance resilience for 186,510 Rohingya refugees and 2,795 host community members. This project will be addressing needs in Camps 1E, 1W, 2E, 2W, 3, 4, 4 Ext, 5, 6, 7, 17, 21, Kutupalong RC, and the host communities of Rajapalong, Haldia Palong, and Jalia Palong. Core initiatives include service coordination, resilient shelter construction, and site development through a properly site planned approach along with Disaster Risk Management (DRM) integration. Through collaboration with ISCG and multiple stakeholders, BRAC will foster social cohesion and resilience.
Aligned with the S-CCCM strategy, BRAC will apply gender-sensitive and disability-inclusive practices. Support will focus on approximately 3.1% of individuals with disabilities, 4.20% of the elderly, 24% of adult women, and 52.13% of children of the total population. Women and youth will play active roles in community initiatives, while BRAC will enhance specific efforts to bridge gaps by addressing Complaints, Feedback and Response to strengthen community resilience. Through durable materials support, BRAC will promote sustainability and an accessible site development approach to prioritize the immediate needs of vulnerable populations. Tailored protection mainstreaming services, inclusion of youth and women engagement and emergency response training for volunteers will ensure comprehensive support, including assistance to 8,997 newly arrived individuals.
BRAC will emphasize community participation and accountability through inclusive decision-making in neighborhood and thematic groups. Real-time needs assessment will be facilitated through CFRM to address 52.11% of unresolved complaints from ISNA 2024. A web-based integrated camp management portal will be developed to support Camp Coordination Meetings and Disaster Management Committee (DMC) meetings, enhancing coordination between government bodies, humanitarian organizations, and camp communities. A compendium will also document best practices and key lessons from organizations working on DRM in Cox’s Bazar, serving as a valuable repository for strategies, innovations, and solutions. To engage both Rohingya and host communities in risk reduction, International and National Disaster Risk Reduction Days will be observed, fostering awareness and collaboration.
BRAC Shelter support activity involves new improve shelter construction for 670 (500 for camps and 170 for host community) households. A Bamboo Treatment Facility (BTF) will enhance the durability of Borak bamboo. Regular shelter needs will be identified and addressed through CFRM, benefiting 3,000 HHs. While emergency shelters will support an additional 500 HHs affected by extreme weather. Labour and porter support will be provided to 500 Extremely Vulnerable Individuals (EVI) HHs. After the distribution of the shelter materials, the BRAC team will ensure Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM). In addition, 390 beneficiaries will receive technical training on shelter construction. Furthermore, infrastructure improvements will be constructed, including 3,300m of drain, 800m of pathways, 1,900m of roads, 1,400m of stairs, and 800m of bridges, alongside tree plantations to enhance environmental resilience.
BRAC’s Emergency Preparedness and Response initiatives aim to build resilience through disaster preparedness and environmental sustainability. Key actions include establishing community based Early Warning Systems with established block-level Disaster Risk Management Committees, deploying Mobile Firefighting Units, and providing protective gear and safety training to volunteers. Environmental resilience will be strengthened through Integrated Water Resource Management, including rainwater harvesting, hydro electricity, and ecosystem conservation projects. Additionally, Climate Schools will engage youth, and fire response support will be expanded, all to promote community-led risk mitigation.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

Cox’s Bazar, designated a ‘multi-hazard zone’ within the cyclone belt, is one of the most vulnerable regions to natural hazards. The arrival of nearly one million Rohingya refugees since 2017 has amplified these vulnerabilities, especially among women and children, straining local resilience and the environment. Geographic location, climate, topography, and human activity have heightened the susceptibility of both refugee camps and host communities to extreme weather events.According to the SCCCM daily incident dashboard, natural and man-made hazards, including cyclones, heavy rains, flash floods, fires, slope failure, and landslides, impacted 208,858 individuals as of October 2024, with 24,504 partial and 1,672 fully damaged shelters reported in the camps. Host and local communities in Cox’s Bazar have similarly suffered. For instance, heavy monsoon rains in June 2024 tragically resulted in the deaths of 10 individuals, including three host community members and seven Rohingya refugees, with 7,794 refugees affected across the 33 camps and 1,191 shelters damaged or destroyed. The recent Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment (MSNA) (2023) and Inter Sectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA) (2024) have identified critical gaps in the Shelter, Camp Coordination, and Camp Management sector, particularly within refugee camps.The ISNA 2024 highlights that 96.71% of shelters are makeshift, constructed quickly with materials like tarps, plastic sheeting, wood, bamboo, or scrap metal, and require frequent maintenance. Women-headed households face heightened vulnerabilities due to inadequate shelter and resources. According to the Refugee Influx Emergency Vulnerability Assessment (REVA) by UN agencies, 95% of Rohingya households are moderately to highly vulnerable, relying heavily on humanitarian assistance for their basic needs. Only 0.84% of these households have access to permanent housing, emphasizing the need for durable shelter solutions. Furthermore, 15% of households expressed dissatisfaction with shelter assistance, and 41% reported that support was insufficient to meet their needs. ISNA 2024 also shows that 41.13% of respondents received shelter or non-food item (NFI) support within the last 6-12 months, underscoring the frequent need for shelter maintenance.Challenges are compounded as 4.70% of households lack cooking spaces and facilities, while an equal percentage reported inadequate sleeping space. These conditions necessitate a "Build Back Better" approach to improve the living standards of Rohingya households. The ISNA 2024 further highlights gaps in the Complaint Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM), with 51% of households not submitting complaints and 31% citing a lack of follow-up. Of those who submitted complaints, only 37.24% reported action taken, and 10.53% found responses unsatisfactory. These findings underscore the need for improved CFRM accessibility, timely follow-ups, and trust-building measures to ensure effective complaint resolution and community confidence.Host communities in Cox’s Bazar also face significant challenges related to income-generating opportunities, healthcare, and adequate shelter. According to the 2021 J-MSNA, 65% of respondents identified food access as their primary need. However, the 2023 J-MSNA showed a shift in priorities: food remained a top concern at 33%, but shelter materials (14%) and access to water (10%) also emerged as critical. Temporary shelters and poor living conditions heighten vulnerability to extreme weather events for both refugees and host communities, as demonstrated by Cyclones Mocha and Hamoon in 2023. The J-MSNA 2023 data highlights that in Jalia Palong Union, 57% of households reported lacking functional domestic spaces, with this figure rising to 80% in Haldia Palong, underscoring the pressing need to improve resilience among Rohingya refugees and host populations.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

The project aims to mainstream protection policy by emphasizing safeguarding, inclusion, and accountability to ensure safety and dignity for all participants. BRAC’s community-led approach prioritizes the needs of vulnerable groups, including female-headed households, people with disabilities, and Indigenous communities, promoting a culture of diversity, equity, and inclusion across all programs. As part of the PSEA network, the project upholds ‘Do No Harm’ principles and practices a ‘Zero Tolerance’ policy against abuse, embedding preventive measures such as staff training, reporting channels, and a comprehensive safeguarding policy conducting thorough risk assessments with partners and stakeholders to identify and reduce protection risks linked to activities. Furthermore, the project’s approach includes community engagement to integrate beneficiary input, raise awareness, reduce stigma, and address local challenges. In terms of Cash for Work, the project participants will receive an awareness session on PSEA and safeguarding to reduce any protection-related concerns. To create safe environments for women and girls and address gender-sensitive issues, each newly constructed shelter will incorporate secure, lockable doors and internal partitions to enhance privacy. The project aims to tailor interventions to community needs while supporting women’s leadership in decision-making processes. A Complaint Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) will uphold accountability, allowing community members to submit feedback or report issues through suggestion boxes, hotlines, and email, with all complaints addressed following a standardized procedure. Site development will ensure accessibility through disability-friendly designs. This policy aligns with international protection standards, ensuring that programs are inclusive, safe, and responsive to the diverse needs of the community.

2.3 - Age & Disability

The project will focus on age and disability inclusion and child safeguarding by ensuring that camp infrastructure, including shelters and facilities, is accessible and accommodating for people with disabilities and aged people. In line with the commitment, the project has set a target of approximately 3.05% for individuals with disabilities, the elderly 4.21%, adult female 24% and children 52.24% for promoting equity and accessibility.Furthermore, this project will prepare a gender and inclusion plan and will ensure that project activity is tailored to different age and disability group needs for ensuring active participation and inclusion of the elderly, children, youth, and persons with disabilities throughout the project. All assessment, monitoring, and reporting systems will include sex, age, and disability disaggregated data. Moreover, during any emergencies, the vulnerable groups will be prioritized for evacuation to safe places and provide assistance. A referral mechanism will be established to ensure rehabilitation services for every person with disabilities. This streamlined process ensures that individuals with disabilities receive the necessary support and services to enhance their overall well-being.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Accountability to the affected population (AAP) involves responsibly wielding power and being answerable to those we seek to assist. BRAC ensures that project participants from both host and Rohingya communities have a meaningful voice in decision-making through inclusive structures. The project fosters transparent, two-way communication, even during crises, to better understand and address the information needs of the community. BRAC ensures beneficiaries' meaningful access, participation, and empowerment, with accountability woven into every level of program design, planning, and execution through the following process:1. Regular and diverse community engagement through FGDs, interviews, courtyard sessions, meetings, and other feedback mechanisms with various target population groups, as well as through bilateral dialogues and consultations with government officials and community leaders.2. Accessible feedback channels such as Complaint and Feedback boxes in all CFRM hubs and as well as hotlines for PSEA complaints.3. Through neighbourhood and thematic group sessions, the feedback from the community is captured and addressed by CFRM focal and share with the relevant stakeholders for evidence generation for informed programming and decision-making.BRAC will conduct regular awareness-raising sessions among the community about these channels and mechanisms. Hotline of UNHCR and BRAC Safeguarding Focal will be visible through different visibility materials (poster, flyer, banners, stickers, etc.). Moreover, during the emergency situation, volunteers use the loud speakers and hand mikes to sensitize community people for safe relocation, tie-down, etc. BRAC values the perspectives of all groups, including children, men, women, the elderly, minority cultural groups, and persons with disabilities, ensuring their active participation throughout all phases of the project.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

BRAC is dedicated to embedding PSEA principles throughout its project’s culture, operations, and management by prioritizing PSHEA in organizational policies, procedures, and practices. This commitment upholds a zero-tolerance stance against inaction and retaliation in SEA cases, engaging all levels from frontline staff to senior management. Preventative measures include regular SEA risk assessments, mandatory Safeguarding and PSEA training for all project staff and personnel, and adherence to policies (PSHEA policy, Child and adolescent protection policy, Whistle blowing policy Prevention of Workplace Bullying and Violence policy and Adult with special needs policy and the Code of Conduct). These are reinforced by SEA reporting mechanisms, collaboration with government and Protection sectors, and child-friendly PSEA materials visible to beneficiaries. To address the gaps regarding PSEA awareness, informal reporting among CICs and Majhees as identified in the ISNA 2024, BRAC already has Shurokkha Bondhus (camp based focal points), who will actively be working in the camp to provide necessary support for staff, volunteers, vendors, and stakeholders, along with training on gender-sensitive complaint mechanisms and GBV referral processes to ensure compassionate responses.BRAC will disseminate communication materials posters and stickers on safe reporting, and establish a “Community Feedback Line” for complaints and feedback. Safeguarding and PSEA will further be strengthened through diverse reporting and feedback mechanisms, such as complaint boxes at service points, 24-hour hotline numbers of PSEA focal persons, and sensitisation on rights-based assistance.In case of SEA allegations, BRAC will ensure all cases are handled sensitively, timely, and fairly, prioritizing the survivors' safety and rights. All SEA incidents, including those involving external entities, will be reported.

2.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

This project will integrate Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and environmental resilience, addressing climate change impacts to build sustainable, adaptive communities in disaster-prone areas. A comprehensive compendium of best practices in multi-stakeholder Disaster Risk Management (DRM) will be developed, drawing on insights from government, NGOs, community groups, and the private sector. This resource will outline collaborative strategies to strengthen disaster preparedness and response, enhancing resilience and coordination.Key project activities will focus on climate-resilient and environmentally sustainable designs, such as resilient shelter models and improved construction materials and techniques that withstand extreme weather. Context-specific training sessions for camp volunteers and Cyclone Preparedness Programme (CPP) volunteers will reinforce localized disaster response capacities. The Climate Schools initiatives will develop the capacities of women and youth groups, enhancing local disaster response capabilities. Environmental resilience will be strengthened through ecosystem conservation efforts, including microclimate initiatives and buffer zones created by plantation development. These microclimate initiatives will support climate change adaptation by creating localized environmental benefits, reducing heat, and improving soil and water retention. Together, these initiatives promote sustainable adaptation, raise risk awareness, and contribute to long-term resilience against climate challenges.

2.7 Does the project have EPR measures embedded? (e.g. business continuity plan, emergency response standard operation procedures, and contingency funds) If Yes, briefly describe.

This project is equipped with a Business Continuity Plan (BCP) to respond swiftly and effectively to sudden-onset emergencies including contingency funds and stockpiles across sectors, which can be immediately mobilized during crisis to ensure rapid response and timely support for vulnerable populations.Additionally, fire is becoming an emerging man-made disaster in the Rohingya camps. From ISNA 2024, the evidence suggests that the use of LPG is increasing widely, which has been causing frequent fire incidents in cooking spaces. Based on that, BRAC will actively provide fire safety mitigation and response training, including the use of fire safety tools, conducting regular fire drills including safe cooking simulation, electrical/solar energy inspections, and maintenance of fire safety equipment. BRAC S-CCCM team will ensure that all sectoral infrastructures and facilities are equipped with essential fire safety equipment.On the other hand, to enhance the community-level Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) system, BRAC will conduct cyclone preparedness drills involving youth and female thematic group members, volunteers, and adjacent host communities through multi-partner collaboration. Quarterly Disaster Management Committee (DMC) meetings will also be held to ensure coordination among all stakeholders. In addition, during emergency situation, tie down kits will be provided among the vulnerable households. Furthermore, early warning systems and a Multi-Hazard Response Plan that addresses landslides, cyclones, flash floods, and fires are key components in ensuring a prompt and effective response.In an emergency, BRAC will conduct immediate door-to-door shelter and infrastructure assessments to enable proper referrals within 24 hours and implement a 72-hour shelter response to meet critical needs. During floods or monsoons, BRAC will help community people move to safe shelters and support them in returning home when it’s safe as well.

2.8 Does your project include or engage youth (i.e. 15-24 year-olds)? If Yes, briefly describe.

The project will strategically engage youth by offering capacity development training for young camp volunteers in disaster preparedness and response, empowering them to assume active roles in disaster risk management, thereby promoting a culture of resilience and community involvement. Through participation in community-driven assessments, youth will acquire a comprehensive understanding of local hazards and vulnerabilities, enabling them to make meaningful contributions to informed and effective Disaster Risk Management (DRM) strategies.Youth Thematic Group members will play a critical role in multi-hazard preparedness by assisting in the relocation of vulnerable families from high-risk zones and ensuring their safe return after a crisis. Through Thematic Meetings, they will address specific camp challenges, contribute to Community-Led Initiatives (CLI) to promote a spirit of volunteerism, and participate in Camp Cleaning Campaigns to improve living conditions. During emergencies, they will lead preparedness sessions, mobilize aid, and implement safety protocols, thereby enhancing community resilience and supporting secure, adaptive responses within the camps.

4. Development linkages
4.1 Does your project promote self-reliance and resilience for refugees? If yes, briefly describe.

The project will enhance resilience among refugees by integrating a community-centered approach to Disaster Risk Management with tools, training, and structural support. By providing shelter kits and delivering capacity-building training for camp volunteers, the project equips refugees to effectively manage and respond to disasters. Shelter kits, volunteer training, and community involvement in infrastructure projects enhance disaster preparedness, while cash for work programs will promote self-reliance. Additionally, through this project social cohesion will be enhanced through multi-sectoral collaboration, aligning DRM principles with community-driven efforts. These initiatives will equip refugee communities with a solid foundation for resilience and preparedness against future challenges.

4.2 Do project activities in the host community link to development initiatives, and/or align with development priorities in the Cox’s Bazar district? If yes, briefly describe.If yes, briefly describe.

In line with the 8th-Five Year-Plan (8FYP) and larger Vision 2041, considering the challenges faced by Cox’s Bazar district, the construction of wooden bridge, road, and community shelters in Ukhiya Upazila will represent a strategic enhancement of infrastructure and connectivity within the host community. This initiative will facilitate improved access to essential services, safe spaces and economic opportunities, which are crucial for local livelihoods impacted by the influx of Rohingya refugees, in direct alignment with multi-sectoral District Development and Growth Priorities (DDGP) 2022 of Cox’s Bazar as well.
