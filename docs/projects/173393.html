Project Summary

The overall objective of the NRC education programme in Bangladesh is to ensure that displacement-affected Rohingya and host community children and youth enjoy quality education and life-long learning opportunities that will ensure they are able to live and work in dignity. NRC’s education programme will target adolescents and youth (14-24 years old) including Rohingya refugees displaced by the conflict in Myanmar, host communities and Bangladeshis affected by climate displacement. Out of school Rohingya refugee and host community adolescents and youth will be supported to access relevant, quality learning opportunities. Host community children and youth at risk of dropping out will be targeted through retention programme that will be implemented in the public schools. Climate internally displaced children and youth will be targeted through an emergency programme that will provide a safe space for them to connect and share experiences. Teachers, learning facilitators, trainers and education personnel will be targeted through continuous professional development. NRC will focus on intensive professional development programmes for females in order to overcome the barrier of the female participation in the programme. NRC will consolidate and expand its youth programming. NRC will respond to the diverse problems and needs faced by vulnerable children and youth in Bangladesh through the delivery of various programmes that will create different learning pathways. Education team will provide the most vulnerable children and youth with basic skills, including literacy and numeracy, remedial classes, life skills and vocational skills. The team will also work on strengthening existing systems — via teacher professional development, asset provision to schools, supporting school governance structures, and more to support the retention and improve the wellbeing of students in schools. The team will ensure first-phase education response to climate displacement by strengthening protection, social connectedness, and learning. Education will continue to develop alternative methods to reach children and youth with learning opportunities such as self-learning and e-learning modules if access is restricted as during COVID. NRC will construct temporary learning centres, the TLCs are equipped with WASH facilities and water source, disability access, solar power for ventilation and lighting, fencing etc. All possible in the camp setting disability-inclusiveness measures will be taken - including physical measures as well as training of the learning facilitators. Every learning facility is managed by an education committee that consists of 7 community members consisting of at least 2 women, parents of the learners, influential members of the community and the Host teacher. They committee meets biweekly to discuss the learning process and give feedback. NRC will train learning facilitators on identification and referral of possible protection cases. Referral pathways will be identified for each TLC and HBLC; both staff at learning centres and NRC staff trained on the use of the referral pathways. NRC does not take responsibility of management of the cases, and only refers them to identified protections services.

1 - Identified Needs

Entering the fourth year of the humanitarian response, the situation has gradually begun to stabilise: basic assistance has been provided, living conditions in the camps have improved and basic disaster risk mitigation measures are in place. However, despite the progress, the Rohingya remain in an extremely precarious situation with an uncertain future and acutely vulnerable to the effects of climate change through violent tropical storms, cyclones, severe flooding and landslides. Furthermore, the Refugees face gender-based violence, inadequate WASH, minimal education opportunities and restricted from participating in livelihood activities, meaning they remain highly aid dependent. Access to quality, inclusive, diverse and age appropriate education for both Rohingya and host community children and youth have been limited. According to 2021 JRP PIN, there’s around 715,000 children and youth age 3 to 24 in need of support. The only learning opportunities available to refugees are provided by education organisations and informal education networks because the Government of Bangladesh does not allow refugees to enrol in formal education or partake in livelihoods activities outside of the camps. The efforts to provide education services to refugees have been constrained by a number of factors, including a lack of space for facilities in camps that are already heavily congested, the lack of qualified teaching personnel specially females and the governments constraints pre and during COVID. Based on education sector gap analysis, the Education Sector partners currently provide services through several learning centres and home-based learning centres spread throughout the camps. Services are available primarily to children aged 3-14, with minimal educational opportunities currently available to adolescents and youth aged 15 and over. From a closer look at this age group, around 40,000 adolescents and youth from 15 to 24 years do not have access to any kind of learning opportunities. While most education actors work in camps, the host community have been left with little assistance. During the JRP consultation meetings with the authorities, they indicated the gap in response to the high drop-out rate among youth due to income generation activities, early marriage, and parents’ disinterest in education. Across Bangladesh, the impacts of climate change and environmental degradation contribute to more frequent extreme weather events and severe disasters. The humanitarian emergency worsened at the onset of the monsoon season in June 2020, with heavy rain, high winds, flooding and landslides damaging not only the refugees’ shelters but a reality for millions living along Bangladesh’s coast to displacement and forced migration.
