Project Summary

The North West and South West regions of Cameroon are affected by a complex humanitarian crisis that has resulted in system wide breakdown of infrastructure and services especially in the health sector. MSNA data from August 2020 estimate that 269 out of 933 (29 percent) health centers are no longer functional. Likewise, with attacks targeting health personnel and destructing medical equipment, the technical capacity and availability of staff to work in these areas is very low. The two regions lack emergency medical and psychological units, necessary to provide emergency surgical management of those wounded and to provide psychiatric and psychosocial support to those traumatized by the violence. Finally, the incidence of rape increased dramatically in North-West and South-West regions with 490 cases reported in 2020 compared to 17 in 2019. This situation has been worsened by the COVID-19 Pandemic which has increased the burden on a health system that has already been fragilized by the humanitarian crisis. In the most of affected areas, health services are not available while less affected areas have health services of poor quality. The present project aims to reduce excess morbidity and mortality of internal displaced population (IDP) in the North West and South West regions of Cameroon affected by the humanitarian crisis by ensuring a timely and equitable access to health care services. The project will target 473,069 IDPs members of host communities (51% of this target are women, 16% are children under 5, and 11% are people over 60 years old). The areas of intervention will be Fako, Meme, Manyu, Ndian and Kupe - Muanenguba divisions in the South-West, Mezam, Momo, Bui, Ngo-ketunjia and Boyo in the North-West region, Mungo and Wouri in the Littoral region and in the West region, Menoua, Mifi, Ndé and Noun divisions. The project will emphasize on timely access to equitable health care for men and women (taking into consideration the distance to the closest health facilities for women, children under 5 and people above 60) and quality of interventions (assistance by skilled attendant, referral of cases).

1 - Besoins / Needs

The situation in the North West and South West has remained unpredictable and volatile for the past 4 years since the onset of the crisis. The combined effect of COVID-19 and the crisis has had a huge impact on the fragile health system. Access to essential health care remains a challenge to most of the affected communities due to the complete or partial shutdown of health facilities, the distance to the closest functional health facility and the inability to pay the cost of health care. The Multisectoral Needs Assessment carried out by IOM in August 2020 showed that 29% of health facilities are closed in both regions and over 300,000 people were still displaced from their homes. The persistence of insecurity is causing additional displacements in these regions. There is an urgent need to improve epidemiological surveillance in these regions as the risk of outbreaks of diseases like Cholera, Measles, yellow fever and Monkey Pox remain high due to the poor living conditions of affected populations and poor vaccination coverage for routine vaccines. A total of 5 out of 18 health districts in the South West region have reported cases cholera in the past year, while 10 out of 17 health districts of these regions have reported at least 1 case of COVID-19. The proximity of some of these health districts to parts of Nigeria under epidemics of laser fever is of great concern given that movement between Cameroon and Nigeria is difficult to control, it is important to strengthen the capacity of the health districts carryout response activities during outbreaks and community-based surveillance for early warning on public health events. Persons living with disabilities, pregnant women and teenagers are most vulnerable and hence they need assistance to access quality health care. Given that most of the affected populations are living under poor living conditions, they are exposed to many pathogens that increases the likelihood illnesses. The brutal nature of the crisis has traumatized a good number of people who need mental health assistance. The project will have a mental health component to take care of the mental health needs of the affected populations
