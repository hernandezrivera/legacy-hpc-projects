Project Summary

Goal: Provide direct assistance to the most vulnerable population of 3 conflict affected neighborhood of Tripoli through a multi-sectorial approach and a strengthened referral system.
Specific Objective: To contribute in alleviating the suffering of vulnerable refugees, IDPs, migrants and host communities in Tripoli, with particular focus on children and unaccompanied and separated children, through the delivery of primary health care and protection services.
Result 1: Vulnerable children and community members of the most vulnerable neighborhoods have access to protection and basic health services.

1 - Needs

Child Protection/Education: Displacement has led to overcrowding in many schools and the use of the facilities as shelters, particularly in urban settings. This led to the closure of 13 schools across the city (areas such as Qasr Ben Ghashir, Ain Zara, Tajoura, Salah Eddin have specifically been affected) and their reconversion in temporary shelters, while the high levels of violence in Western Libya led to the suspension of the academic school year for 122,088 children around Tripoli. Amidst violence and conflict, children are affected the most: both at home and in schools, they suffer the repercussion of the conflict, ranging from displacement to the limited availability and variety of food provided (overcrowded schools do not cope with the increased demand in food, already hampered by the cash crisis). In particular, refugees and migrants children have little or no access to education: children without official civil documentation and certificates are not permitted to join Libyan schools and sub-Saharan children face particular discrimination due to language barriers. Very few trainings or learning opportunities exist for those who cannot integrate into formal schools, and nonformal education is almost non-existent. The violent context and the lack of educational and recreational opportunities exposes children to the risk of domestic violence, recruitment by armed groups, sexual and gender-based violence, abduction, unlawful detention, torture and other forms of ill-treatment, as well as killing or maiming due to presence of unexploded ordnance. Protection/Health: The depletion of services provided by the public hospitals and healthcare centers, decreased dramatically the access to affordable and quality care of the most vulnerable niches of populations. Furthermore, several health centres have already been closed due to proximity to the conflict, with staff and equipment being relocated and inhabitants and newcomers (IDPs) lack of appropriate information of the available services. Furthermore, migrants and refugees, devoid of Libyan documentation, are denied access to care as the priority is given to nationals and must rely to the expensive private system. Therefore, health problems are often neglected or improperly treated with homemade solution.

2 - Activities

Activity 0: Setting up of a mobile unitThe mobile team will be responsible of identifying (i) the most vulnerable households in needs (ii) the children in need of psycho-social support and the parents to be included in the regular program of the Baity Center and (iii) the beneficiaries in needs of healthcare. Activity 1.1 Awareness raising campaign in schools and communitiesThe mobile team will visit the 2 schools once a week, promoting awareness activities for children on the topics of “healthy nutrition”, health, hygiene and sanitation, while at the same time conducting free health screening, followed by referral to more specialized services should it be needed. Activity 1.2 Community protection outreach activities: Community engagement and awareness raising on protection and health activities will accompany the project throughout its implementation, directed at the migrants schools’ staff, the families of the children attending the identified schools, as well as the communities at large and the local authorities of Suq al-Jumaa, Al Seiyahia and Gut Al Shaal.

5 - Mitigation measures

INTERSOS is registered in Libya and access to Libya is granted. Anyway, INTERSOS can count on local staff with solid experience. The program would not stop in case of restricted access to Libya as most of the works, goods and services are purchased locally and local staff is trained and used to remote management. The area of intervention is in the urban perimeter of Tripoli and movements are done constantly. Movement are constantly monitored by a security agency. The support of Municipalities and Schools Authorities ease the negotiation with the authorities to grant the access. The conflict situation is closely monitored by a security agency. Security alert system in place prevents risk and different routes or timing are adopted to assure the operations.In case of prolonged period of instability, activities will be stand by and information released to SV.INTERSOS ensures a continuous coordination with local partners and international specialized agencies (security agency) to monitor the situation. INTERSOS adopts a comprehensive Planning Process (CPP) including security plans and SOPs in case of any major accident. INTERSOS ensures good relationship with authorities in the area, informing them of the planned activities. It ensures a transparent and accountable way of working. Plans of intervention are shared and discussed to increase acceptance. The liaison officer will ensure the timely and constant contacts with the authorities. INTERSOS ensures directly the monitoring and evaluation of the activities. Performance of staff and contractors will be monitored by INTERSOS staff: any breach will result in disciplinary actions.

6.1 - Other accountability mechanisms

INTERSOS has and will involve the target communities and beneficiaries at different stages of implementation of the project to maintain its relevance and adjust its programming to the needs of the beneficiaries. The community will be included in the implementation: on the one hand, the awareness raising campaign will target children, members of the community, relatives and caregivers; on the other hand, community members will be selected as community mobilizers. The intervention is designed upon previous experiences considering the needs of the different gender (in particular girls) and age (children in general) groups. Their participation during the action will be ensured through unstructured key informants interviews and continuous monitoring.To involve the target communities in the implementation, beneficiaries will be engaged in providing feedback and inputs on the action, to ensure its relevance. This includes gathering inputs from the target group through focus group discussion and inception meetings (mostly in the activities in the schools). Based on the feedback, INTERSOS will determine if the activities contributing to the action need to be revised/adjusted.The M&E will assess and evaluate the adherence of the project to the commitment of mainstreaming protection; therefore, ad hoc indicators will be set to monitor if the project (i) prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm (ii) ensure a meaningful access to service to beneficiaries (iii) Guarantee accountability (iv) ensure participation and empowerment of right-holders.INTERSOS will use hotlines and complaint boxes to receive feedback as well.
