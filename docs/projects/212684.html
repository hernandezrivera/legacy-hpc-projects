Project Summary

Human Appeal (HA) is dedicated to establishing a secure and safe environment for 3768 internally displaced children (IDPs), comprising 2286 boys and 1482 girls, aged 6 to 17 years. These children are enrolled in 7 schools located in Ariha, Ehsem, Mhambal, Qourqeena, Atareb, and Maaret Tamsrin. The educational levels offered by these schools span both primary and secondary education.
The program’s overarching goal is to ensure uninterrupted access to both formal and non-formal education, while strictly adhering to the INEE minimum standards for Education in Emergencies (EiE). Additionally, the program provides winterization support to safeguard educational continuity during adverse weather conditions.
These 7 schools face significant challenges due to urgent gaps resulting from insufficient support and funding. To address these critical needs, HA will undertake the following initiatives:
- Light care and maintenance: Ensuring the schools remain operational and safe.
- Covering running costs: Supporting essential operational expenses.
- Supplying heating fuel: To maintain a conducive learning environment during cold weather.
- Incentivizing teachers: Providing incentives for 100 teachers across all 7 schools.
Furthermore, the project aims to foster resilience within the affected communities, aligning with the Edu’s objective of promoting resilience. It takes into account cluster-specific objectives and priorities. The project strives to achieve uninterrupted access to both formal and non-formal education, in alignment with the INEE minimum standards for EiE. Additionally, winterization support ensures that educational continuity remains intact.
HA key educational activities:
- Quality education for IDPs: This includes 3768 children (2286 boys and 1482 girls), with 84 children with disabilities (CwDs), across the 7 schools in the specified sub-districts.
- Formal education: 2823 students (1012 girls and 1811 boys) will receive formal education in Ariha, Ehsem, Mhambal, Qourqeena, and Atareb.
- Non-formal education (NFE): For 945 students (462 girls and 483 boys) in Maaret Tamsrin (AlZohoor Camp - Human Appeal Camp), until a formal school is constructed within the camp by Human Appeal (HA UK Secured Fund). The NFE curriculum follows UNICEF guidelines and UNICEF curriculum B.
A post-test will assess the equivalency of formal education levels at the end of catch-up classes and Accelerated Learning Programs (ALP). Results will guide educators in determining students’ eligibility for enrollment in formal education (FE) curriculum classes.
Regarding Monitoring and Supervision:
-Competent MEAL (Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning) and specialized program staff will monitor and supervise proposed activities to ensure high-quality results and efficient progress.
-Regular visits by M&E staff will assess progress and provide ongoing assessments and surveys.
Moreover, Protection Risk Analysis Risk Assumptions & Mitigation will be periodically developed and updated to mitigate potential risks. Close coordination with the protection sector will be maintained, following their guidance.
With regard to Inclusion and Accessibility, Human Appeal will strive to achieve this through;
- Providing tools for children with disabilities ensures equal access to services.
- Efforts will focus on including children with disabilities in various activities.
- Rehabilitation works and staff training will be tailored to meet their specific needs.
- Tools such as ramps and private toilets will enhance accessibility in the 7 schools.
Therefore, through this program, Human Appeal is committed to creating an inclusive and supportive environment for all beneficiaries in NWS.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project aligns closely with the objectives of the Education sector, specifically focusing on “Objective 2: Enhancing the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth.” Referring to People in Need Calculation Methodology- Whole of Syria Education Sector, Humanitarian Needs Overview 2024, the numbers and percentages reflect the urgent need for educational services. Humanitarian Appeal will also conduct a need assessment that accurately reflects the need, gaps, and numbers. The initiative aims to create a safe environment and improve access to education for 3,768 internally displaced children (comprising 2,286 boys and 1,482 girls), including 84 children with disabilities. These children are spread across seven schools in the areas of Ariha, Ehsem, Mhambal, Qourqeena, Atareb, and Maaret Tamsrin. The primary goal is to ensure uninterrupted access to both formal and non-formal education, in line with the INEE minimum standards for Education in Emergencies (EiE). Additionally, the project provides winterization support to safeguard educational continuity during harsh weather conditions. These seven schools face urgent challenges due to insufficient support and funding. The project aims to activate services within the minimum standards, addressing minor rehabilitation, operational needs, and winterization requirements. Key interventions include: - Signing a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the Education Directorate to launch activities.- Providing light care and maintenance for the schools.- Covering running costs and supplying heating fuel.- Offering incentives for teachers.- Equipping schools with tools to support children with disabilities, ensuring equal opportunities for all students.- Regular school visits by the project team to monitor educational processes, provide necessary support, and enhance teacher performance. The project leverages technical expertise, administrative skills, and a well-trained team to address the complexities of this context. It prioritizes urgent needs, considering cross-cutting elements such as gender, protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA), and accountability to affected populations (AAP).
