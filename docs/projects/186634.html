Project Summary

This project will take an integrated approach towards ensuring healthy lives and promoting well-being and the three strategic priorities of achieving UHC, addressing health emergencies and promoting healthier populations, while optimizing synergies between one another. This project will focus on strengthening the capacity of management of acute malnutrition across the BAY states. It will strengthen the capacity of existing government health workers in provision of quality nutrition services 10,250 MAM children out of the estimated MAM children expected to develop medical complications across the accessible worst affected LGAs in the BAY states. LABDI will also support the lead mothers in smooth running of the facilities to ensure uninterrupted supply of commodities (tom brown) to the implementing beneficiaries. This will be achieved via production and distribution of 2.5Kg of the tom brown solution to the children screened. LABDI will also train 120 health workers across the BAY states in the management of MAM. The limited capacity of health care workers which is worsened by high staff turnovers (attrition rate) in especially the security compromised areas is an additional justification to sustain these training sessions. The training in 2022 will focus on T.O.T (training of trainers) which will focus more on resident health care professionals to ensure sustainability.
The project will also facilitate access to life-saving nutrition services through early detection and referral of malnourished children especially from Hard-To-Reach areas to nutrition treatment centres for timely intervention. It will complement the humanitarian nutrition response of the sector in general and LABDI’s efforts in particular by addressing unmet nutrition and health needs. It will also support the State MoHs by providing integrated life-saving nutrition services to the under-5 children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in the priority areas. The services will be provided through 18 mobile Nutrition Volunteer teams positioned in hard-to-Reach communities in the states by enhancing their capacity to strengthen community mobilization activities for timely detection of MAM children through routine screening for malnutrition and subsequent referral to the nearest treatment centres at the same time promoting IYCF practices.

1 - Needs

According to the 2021 HRP, 1.5 million people, including over 1 million children under five and half a million women of reproductive age within the BAY states, need life-saving nutrition services. Of these, 15 per cent are IDPs; 15 per cent returnees and 70 per cent are in host communities. Women and children are the most affected and those living at the reception centers are extremely vulnerable as they have not accessed nutrition services prior to their arrival at the reception sites. Children who are severely malnourished are 12 times more likely to die (all-cause of mortality) compared to a well-nourished child. A non- breastfed infant under 6 months is 14 times more likely to die from pneumonia/diarrhea than an exclusively breastfed infant <6months. According to the Nutrition and Food Security Surveillance Round 9 (NFSS IX) and the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification Acute Malnutrition analysis 2020 (IPC AMN), the nutrition situation in Borno and Yobe State is classified as Phase 3 (Serious) and Adamawa at Phase 2 (Alert) level, with GAM levels of 13.6% in Yobe, 10.7% in Borno and 7.5% in Adamawa. The nutritional situation is also expected to deteriorate further due to worsening of the immediate and underlying issues including increased displacement and the impact of COVID on the socioeconomic status. Based on the NFSS IX, approximately 809,814 children under five will suffer from acute malnutrition including 295,288 and 514,526 with MAM. Approximately only 54% of infants less than 6 months are exclusively breastfed in the BAY States. A total of 720,739 PLW women will require contextualized and skilled IYCF counselling both at facility and community levels with the aim of improving the exclusive breastfeeding rates. Children and women in hard-to-reach areas e.g. Magumeri, Kala/Balge, Gubio, Mafa, host community areas with low humanitarian activities/presence and poor infrastructure development e.g. Yobe State and newly arrivals from inaccessible areas are more likely to suffer from acute malnutrition. Women in rural setting are less likely to exclusively breastfeed their infants less 6 months due to increased workload/labour and lack of access to skilled IYCF counselling at health facilities. To reduce morbidity and mortality associated with global acute malnutrition, there is need to urgently increase the capacity to treat SAM cases with medical complications by establishing additional inpatient care facilities in Yobe and Adamawa States and in LGAs near to inaccessible areas in Borno State. There is also need to scale-up the identification, referral and treatment of children from hard to reach areas and in the newly liberated or return locations and other areas planned for the returns.

2 - Activities

Since 2016, the provision of life-saving services to the affected communities is a joint effort between the Government of Nigeria and the humanitarian community. This project will be implemented in line with the World Health Assembly’s Global Programme which is focused on 3 interconnected strategic priorities to ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages: achieving universal health coverage (UHC), addressing health emergencies and promoting healthier populations. Close collaboration with sector partners including SMoH, Hospital management board, nutrition sector, health sector, and partner organizations will be maintained to review needs, assess progress and devise a strategy for strengthening. The key activities are: i. Strengthen government capacity to provide nutrition services. ii. Organize a TOT for a pool of MoH master trainers on Management of MAM. iii. Promote an integrated approach for the management of MAM as a minimum package of MNCH services; this will be achieved through the production and distribution of 2.5Kg of tom brown services to the beneficiaries of MAM cases. This will go a long way in facilitating MAM cases with medical complications. The present collaboration with government will be strengthened. Strengthen Government capacity on regular joint supportive supervision/monitoring of nutrition services; iv. Support the systematic implementation of the Nurturing Care Framework in Health Facilities to ensure that children are developmentally on track.v. Providing access to comprehensive and high impact health and nutrition services for people in areas with low/no health service coverage in the BAY states. This will be through systematic screening for malnutrition and referral of all identified cases to treatment centres. While those at risk will be counselled through IYCF in addition to treatment for minor illness, vaccination, vitamin A supplementation and deworming to children.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

The project is designed to prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of accessing health interventions. In addition, LABDI treatment standards will be followed to ensure interventions achieve the desired effect and not cause harm. There will be no discrimination and anyone within the targeted areas can access the services. Patients seeking treatment will be treated with respect and dignity. Also, training on GBV and PSEA policy/standards of conduct will be integrated into all LABDI trainings sessions.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

LABDI’s Community Mentors (CMs) will be engaged to continuously interact with the community members to inform them about the projects and services obtainable at the SCs, they will also support community mobilization as well as nutrition screening and referrals. This project will ensure that beneficiaries are not harmed. LABDI will periodically solicit opinions and thoughts of beneficiaries (including of the vulnerable groups and those with special needs- including people living with disability) through community engagement sessions. Feedbacks from the community will serve to guide the project implementation. Through established network of LGA mentors, the beneficiaries will be able to give feedback on the quality of service delivered and how well the activities have addressed their needs. This feedback will be used to modify services and activities implementation to the vulnerable population.
