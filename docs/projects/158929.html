Project Summary

The space for individual Human Rights Defenders (HRDs) and civil society organizations (CSOs) in the occupied Palestinian territories (OPT), speciﬁcally those working to end the Israeli occupation in Palestine or to promote human rights (HRs) in the OPT, is increasingly shrinking. The project responds to an increasingly dangerous environment for HRDs in the OPT. The vision of this project is that HRDs will be free to carry out their peaceful and legitimate work and to promote human rights and justice. Our goal is defending HRDs and minimizing the “chilling effect” that targeted harassment has on their work. We intervene by building resilience of local HR communities to withstand the persecution they face.
Proposed Outputs/target Include:
Direct Services for individual HRDs (men and women and, where applicable, children) and organizations at risk, aimed at mitigating these risks (legal, ﬁnancial or others) to their body, person or work. These services focus on coordination—via a 24-hrs hotline—and ﬁnancing of legal counselling and representation, in order to increase likelihood of early release from detention/arrest, and avoid indictment and/lawsuits. Other services include: capacity-building training and media support for HRDs (men and women) and organizations. Advocacy activities that address the root causes of persecution, and develop strategic action to prevent and minimize it, and by outreach to the international community in order to leverage its capacity to enforce international HR law.

1 - Needs

This project responds to the need of protection for individual human rights defenders (HRDs) and human rights organizations, mitigating threats through direct legal support, resilience building and advocacy. In recent years we are witnessing a growing systematic attack on HR organizations and individual HRDs (men, women and also children) who peaceful protesters, intended to delegitimize and deter them from working, and silence criticism over Israel’s violations of human rights. Concerted campaigns and incitement by the Israeli government and non-state actors against HRDs is endangering the space for opposition human rights violations. This project also will have different challenges that will include the following: (1) Legal Risks: Israel violates HRDs’ rights and freedoms by issuing arbitrary military/police orders, using police/military force, and surmounting legal procedures - criminal or administrative. Men, women as well as children are affected. Palestinian HRDs in the OPT face greater risk since they are subjected to a military regime with little access to justice within the conﬁnes of the military court system. HRDs and organizations are not equipped to legally defend themselves, and the costly defense demands resources that could otherwise be used for activities. (2) Public Targeting and incitement: Coordinated efforts to discredit organizations who oppose the occupation including attempts to restrict their ability to fundraise and label CSOs as ‘foreign agents’ or ‘state enemies’. Without rapid professional response to counter attacks, such allegations may actually turn into restrictive legislation or initiatives endangering HRD's funding sources. (3) ‘Chilling Effect’: the intimidation resulting from the targeting leaves activists (men, women and at times children) in a state of emotional ‘burnout’; without investment in activists’ wellbeing, this leads to a ‘chilling effect’ against HR activism. The work of women human rights defenders (WHRDs) underlies not only the power dynamics between Human Rights Defenders and state representatives, but on top of this, women face the pressure of gender dynamics of society in general, creating a two-fold burden. Incrimination, arrests, investigations, incarceration, legal proceedings and trials create a complex reality that is different for women to cope with. Many women human rights activists have testiﬁed to being verbally, sexually and/or physically harassed by soldiers/police during protests, arrests or investigations, as well as being subjected to humiliating treatment based on gender, sexuality and appearance. The HRDF constantly considers special needs of female HRDFs in its work, including in this project. (http://hrdf.org.il/women-human-rightsdefenders/).The HRDF works in close partnership with the Coalition for Women for Peace to assess the needs of WHRDs and is constantly advising its lawyers (most of them are women) to conduct needs assessment.

2 - Activities

Activity cluster 1 –Risk Reduction 1.1. Legal Aid including direct representation, advice and counsel to HRDs and organizations. Legal representation will be provided in cases of criminal prosecution (detention/arrest, interrogation, indictments) for men, women and children HRDs and organizations. Aid is rendered by contracted top attorneys (mostly women, but also men). 1.2. Urgent aid will be facilitated through 24-hour phone hotline to HRDs (men and women) and organizations, in order to provide timely and effective legal counsel. Activity cluster 2 - Resilience Building 2.1. Training workshops will inform HRDs (men/women) on their special rights and provide them with tools to better prepare activities in order to minimize risks and to defend against different threats and considering speciﬁc gender needs. Training workshops are designed together with expert lawyers and activists (men and women). 2.2. Media crisis management for HRDs who are victims of vilifying public smear campaigns or of incitement to violence. The HRDF contracts media experts to provide consultations to HRDs (men and women), and to push to include the voices and perspectives of targeted HRDs and HR organizations in mainstream media. Activity cluster 3 – Advocacy 3.1. Outreach activities to disseminate information to international community members through meetings, briefs, regular newsletter and urgent updates and website maintenance. 3.2. Using mechanisms of international monitoring and action, such as ﬁling formal “urgent complaints”, issuing reports, submitting opinions to the UN state review, and so on.

3 - Indicators

The success of the proposed project will be measured through reaching the following indicators: Activity Cluster 1: • Direct legal aid to around 50 legal cases HRDs (men and women; children if necessary); hotline calls answered to HRDs or human rights organizations per year.• Overall good legal results for the large majority of legal aid beneﬁciaries, such as: - early release from detention/arrest (after no more than 2 arraignment hearings); - reduced and/ dropped charges from the indictment; - lighter sentences/punishments. • Dozens of activists’ groups will contact the HRDF prior to organizing actions, in order to get legal advice or counsel and/ to arrange to have standby lawyers in advance. Activity Cluster 2: • About 1-2 trainings will be held for an average of 10 HRDs per training. • Media discussion of HRDs and HR organizations will include the voice of at risk HRDs and/ their lawyers. • Public discussion will include perspectives that counter-act the targeting of HR community. Activity Cluster 3: • At least 2-3 brieﬁngs held to international diplomats; newsletters sent to over 600 recipients, in addition to providing legal updates about different cases of HRDs. • Conducting advocacy work and activities to mobilize the International mission representatives and international community to attend trial hearings of local HRDs, in order to be more informed about the situation on the ground, and show more support for HRDs.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

The feedback and complaints collected will help us to learn from beneficiaries and communities targeted in this project, and be responsive to their views, opinions, and complaints, so that we will be able to improve the quality and direct impact of our work in the field. Also, the feedback will help us to identify problems, protect the safety and dignity of those who made the complaint, in addition to sharing information through the process of receiving and responding to feedback and complaints. The feedback and complaints gathered will be used to inform decision-making counterparts as it will safely voice the complaints and grievance of beneficiaries, to receive their rights and gain protection especially human rights defenders, under the human rights and international humanitarian laws, and to hold decision-making figures to be accountable to imply human rights laws and abide to international conventions and treaties.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

This project contributes to addressing the needs of human rights defenders in the West Bank including East Jerusalem. The safety and dignity of the beneficiaries have been prioritized and throughout the work the principles of do no harm and always considered. Through this project we are aiming to bring social change to the lives of human rights defenders, throughout our different activities including awareness raising and media crisis management. Continuously, the aim is to protect the beneficiaries and do no harm against them, other stakeholders or any other counterparts in this project. Therefore, there is a strong link between the desired change and do no harm, as there won’t be no social change without a confrontation on some levels between the objectives of this change and its potentially negative effects for the projects’ beneficiaries. The monitoring process for this project will include regular meeting with the HRDs and random calls to check if they are satisfied with the services provided to them.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The project enables access to assistance and services, and targeting of vulnerable groups. This will mainly focus on our priority of work which is providing assistance and services to HRDs. To add, the protection mainstreaming that the project is highlighting, comprises the principles of prioritizing safety, dignity, avoid causing harm, participation, and empowerment. The application of the above mentioned principles will assist us in providing assistance and services. This project is creating equal and impartial access to aid through the concept of inclusion, thus our main focus for this project is targeting human rights defenders including vulnerable groups, activists, human rights organizations, men, women, children, and others.
