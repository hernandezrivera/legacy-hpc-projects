Project Summary

The INGOs forum in Cameroon (CHINGO) was established in June 2019 with the objective to enhance collaboration and coordination between INGOs and between INGOs and external stakeholders (line ministries, UN agencies, donors, civil society organizations) engaged in the humanitarian response to populations affected by the three on-going crises in Cameroon.
Under the strategic guidance of its Steering Committee and the leadership of its coordinator, the INGO forum will specifically aim at enhancing information-sharing between INGOs, notably to build common positions, improving representation of INGOs and joint advocacy and contributing to the overall humanitarian coordination architecture. The project includes various services to INGOs, including trainings for INGOs and partners’ staff, sharing of experience and lessons learnt, and facilitation and support on issues of common interest. CHINGO also engages with external stakeholders and provides a collective voice for INGOs as well as an entry point for requests to INGOs. Advocacy and communication activities aim to support better understanding of the assistance provided by INGOs in Cameroon, identify and propose solutions to challenges faced by INGOs in humanitarian operations and increase the visibility of the crises in Cameroon with the overall objective to better respond to humanitarian needs of populations affected by displacement.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The constant rise of humanitarian needs in Cameroon highlights the importance of robust coordination mechanisms aiming at delivering a multifaceted emergency response in line with humanitarian principles. The Humanitarian Country Team under the leadership of the Humanitarian Coordinator is providing strategic guidance to humanitarian actors. In the Far North and in the North-West and South-West regions, coordination is led by OCHA while in the eastern part of the country, UNHCR is leading on the coordination of the assistance to be provided to the CAR refugee caseload. International NGOs are crucial participants in these coordination mechanisms and key contributors to the delivery of an efficient and coordinated humanitarian response to crisis-affected population. INGOs also intervene in recovery and development actions and as such are engaged in the roll-out of the Humanitarian-Development-Peace Nexus approach in Cameroon.INGOs operate in a very challenging environment and several constraints have undermined or limited INGO action in all the active crises in Cameroon.Challenges related to humanitarian access have limited the ability of INGOs to deliver assistance to populations in need, especially in hard-to-reach areas. More acutely in the South-West and North-West regions, frequent clashes between governmental forces and Non-State armed groups, delays at roadblocks, days of lockdown and the harassment and intimidation of aid workers continue to prevent humanitarian agencies to fully access rural villages or families displaced to remote bushland areas. Increased insecurity for humanitarian workers, evidenced by the rise in attacks against humanitarian workers (three humanitarian workers killed since the beginning of 2020) and the number of days of lockdowns, from which humanitarian workers are not exempted, result in delays to humanitarian programming.In the Far North, increased attacks at the Cameroon-Nigeria border areas and direct threats from Boko Haram against humanitarian workers have negatively impacted the capacity of INGOs to reach people in need. This growing insecurity also results in increased humanitarian needs, due to repeated waves of displacement of civilian populations, leaving everything they own behind them. This comes in addition to a serious reduction of physical access due to the suspension of UNHAS flights – which resumed in September 2020 but without full capacity to access the department of Logone-et-Chari – and of large-scale floods. INGOs also sometimes face access restrictions from local authorities in the form of lengthy authorization to be obtained to carry out specific activities or temporary suspension of humanitarian aid. There is a need to engage more with local authorities to ensure INGOs’ work is understood by all relevant actors and to facilitate the delivery of humanitarian assistance to refugees, IDPs and host communities in need.Administrative hurdles: There are major administrative issues which impede the action of INGOs, namely: lack of tax waivers for INGOs, high custom fees for imported goods, no fast-track procedure for visa for international staff, especially since the outbreak of COVID-19. Ultimately INGOs are subjected to pay tax on all procurements, which has an implication on the quantity and quality of services they are able to provide. Moreover, registration of new INGOs is increasingly being met with cumbersome and lengthy registration processes. Further collective engagement is needed from the INGOs to achieve better recognition from central authorities, ease some of the processes and improve the delivery of humanitarian assistance.Limited visibility of Cameroon crises and insufficient funding: Despite the urgent humanitarian needs and the complexity of the crises in Cameroon, the country is still perceived as a stable country with an acceptable level of development.
