Project Summary

This project aims to provide life-saving assistance to internally displaced persons (IDPs) and host community households in sub-districts of Aleppo and Idlib governorates, targeting a total of 36,000 households, equivalent to 180,000 individuals.
The assistance will be provided through the following activities:
1. Rehabilitation of Armanaz - Sheikh Bahr Quarry Junction road, with a length of 4,50 KM. The road serves about 10 camps in addition to 10 other villages adjacent to the road, and the number of beneficiaries is estimated at about 100,000 people. The road needs to scrape off the old asphalt along with exposure and adding layers of gravel and asphalt
The road is considered one of the important vital service roads, as it connects the regions of Armanaz and Maarat Misrin, passing through some villages, camps, and the junctions of some of the villages that are located on the road. It is a link that shortens a large distance for cars connecting the two regions, especially ambulances, which currently travel a distance of more than ten kilometers. Due to the difficulty of passing through this road .
2. Rehabilitation of of Kafr Deryan road: The rehabilitation works will be with a length of 3.00 km (5.000 m2). The significance of the road lies in its role as a crucial link between Kafr Deryan and Sarmada. Moreover, it serves as the sole essential road connecting the camps situated to the south of Kafr Drian, extending to the village of Kafr Arouq. Additionally, the road plays a pivotal role in connecting the region to health facilities, schools, and camps distributed on both sides of its path.
3. Rehabilitation of Babsqa road: The rehabilitation works will be with a length of 6.50 km (10.000 m2). This road linking the Bab al-Hawa crossing through the villages of Babsqa and Al-Kafir to Ras Al-Hosn, this road holds significant importance. It plays a crucial role in enhancing accessibility between Ras Al-Hosn, Al-Kafir, Babsqa, and the camps situated on either side of the road, connecting them to health facilities and schools. Furthermore, it is recognized as the primary route for ambulances due to its proximity and vital importance.
4. Rehabilitation of Harim-Sarmada road: The rehabilitation works will be with a length of 1.00 km (3.000 m2). This road has been rehabilitated before with this project, but due to the recently floods there are some new areas has damaged and need to be rehabilitate. This road serves as a crucial link between Harem and Sarmada. Its significance lies in connecting the Harem region to health facilities in Sarmada and commercial markets. The road plays a vital role in providing access to numerous camps on both sides of its route and facilitates the commute of teachers and students to their respective schools.
5. Creating short-term employment opportunities to engage local community members in direct contributions to preventive rehabilitation work in Harim and benefit from it.
Individuals will be employed in road rehabilitation work (road edge paving, road marking, installation of signage) for a duration of 80 working days.
6. Support small projects for 200 individuals, with equal distribution of support between genders. 50% of the beneficiaries are disabled men, and 50% are women who are the breadwinners.
These individuals have the experience to establish small income-generating projects that are suitable for their capabilities in five different locations (Harem, Kafr Takharim, Salqin, Armanaz, Azmarin Sarmda).
Training will be provided to these individuals in project management for a duration of 10 days. Following that, the Sham Humanitarian Foundation will purchase the necessary materials for the projects managed by the beneficiaries.
The cost of each grant is $750 USD.
Additionally, the project team will conduct regular visits to the beneficiaries to provide support and monitor the progress of their projects.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The project is scheduled to last for 12 months, spanning from January 2024 to December 2024, and will focus on sub-districts in Idlib and Aleppo where AHF aims to restore vital roads. Small grant initiatives will prioritize households from both internally displaced persons (IDP) and host communities who lack any source of income. The road rehabilitation efforts will entail several steps, including the removal of existing asphalt layers, repair or replacement of damaged sub-base and base coarse layers, spreading gravel of various diameters to a thickness of 50-55 cm and compacting it to meet required standards. Additionally, the road will be sprayed and painted with MC0 material at a rate of 1.5 kg per square meter. An average of 6 cm of asphalt will be applied, tailored to accommodate different types of vehicles and conforming to local and international standards. Quality tests for all completed work will be conducted in a certified laboratory, including Atterberg limits, Los Angeles abrasion test, Proctor compaction test, Marshal test, California bearing ratio, Asphalt Tests, Sieve Analysis Test, Field Density Test, and Sand Equivalent Test. Safety measures will be implemented, including temporary detours, road markings, traffic signage, speed bumps, and pedestrian crossing points. High visibility paint and tactile painting will be used to enhance accessibility for individuals with disabilities, removing environmental barriers. AHF will ensure that all project activities adhere to established standards and guidelines, such as those outlined in the Sphere Project and ERL Cluster Guidance. Staff will undergo training on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and collaborate closely with the Protection team to address any protection concerns. *Consulting people with disabilities (PWDs) during the design and implementation of project*s is crucial to ensure that their needs, perspectives, and experiences are adequately considered. Here are some ways in which PWDs can be consulted: Establishing Advisory Groups: Forming advisory groups composed of individuals with various disabilities who can provide insights, feedback, and recommendations throughout the project lifecycle. Focus Groups: Organizing focus groups with PWDs to delve deeper into specific topics, gather qualitative data, and explore potential solutions. Continuous Engagement: Maintaining ongoing communication and engagement with PWDs throughout the project lifecycle to address evolving needs and incorporate feedback iteratively. By adopting these approaches, project teams can ensure that the voices of PWDs are heard, respected, and integrated into the design and implementation processes, ultimately leading to more inclusive and effective outcomes. In summary, AHF possesses extensive experience in road rehabilitation, ensuring effective program management and ongoing learning to enhance the implementation of future projects.
