Project Summary

Child Protection and Education sectors are working to integrate their projects to ensure that children get better and improved services and that long term effective solutions are attained for long term education. In this regard, this project presents an opportunity to implement a program that looks keenly into protection and education needs of the children in the localities of Al Abassya, AbuJubeiha, Kadugli and Abukarshwla (South Kordofan), Elnehoud and Wad Banda (West Kordofan), Aldeain, Adila and Yassin (East Darfur), Kutum and Saraf Omra (North Darfur), Geissan and Bau (Blue Nile) beside in Abyei PCA. The need for this project is embedded in the fact that a myriad of assessments conducted in these areas show a huge gap of children out of school and increasing access without working on protection issues that ensure retention and transition will not resolve the issues. The project will be implemented in the targeted areas through an integrated Approach that looks at Child Protection as a key element of Education and education as a fundamental right of the child. This project will ensure that children in the target localities attend and stay in school through innovative community based mechanisms that are easy to replicate and sustain. GAH will work with state and localities based family tracing and reunification FTR systems, Moreover, the action will strengthen the capacity of MOSW/MOE on case management and referral of children with speciﬁc needs. GAH will work with community based education and child existing community structures (PTA, CBCPN, Committees), supporting exciting child protection facilities (CFS) to expand as child/adolescent centers and integrate education and child protection in both child/adolescent center and schools, establishment of a very tight link between the social workers and surrounding schools and train the CBCPN and link them with PTA to complement their efforts. The project will also target rehabilitation of class rooms which pose risks for the students and construction of new class rooms to facilitate hosting the huge number of school children. GAH is planning to rehabilitate and construct of school latrines especially for girls schools and advocate with the local community for school fencing and make the schools child friendly schools. To ensure the sustainable result, GAH will improve the capacity of teachers, PTA, children and parents and will sensitize them on child/adolescent centers approaches. Project will target children on the move and enroll them in schools to reduce further movement that may lead to child abuse, neglect including child traﬃcking. A referral mechanism will be established/strengthened not just for education but with other sectors as well (Health, Nutrition and Livelihoods) The project would like to tackle girl child education through use of a peer to peer support group called the girl power clubs in schools where girls attending the same school are grouped together and sensitized to support each other. Child clubs and parent support groups will also be supported Special care will be given to children with disability through a well-planned disability friendly school facility improvement and parent support. The PTA will also develop SIP that will look into the immediate and future needs of the school and link the school to other community structures. At the end it is envisioned that the schools and children with their parents targeted by this project will have better access quality and attain a graded level of education while enjoying their fundamental human rights.

1 - Needs

In conﬂict affected communities, the most vulnerable suffer double tragedy facing difficulties to access services and suffer violations especially for those who suffer from protracted displacement or several displacements. According to HNO, inter-agency assessments, and focus group discussion/ consultation conducted by GAH in previous and ongoing projects. The total of affected children in South Kordofan are 42,309 IDPs& returnees- 23,704 HCs in (Alabassiya, Abujebha, Kadugli and Abu Karshola), in West Kordofan451 IDPS- 31,767 HCs in (Elnehoud, Wad Banda) , in Blue Nile (Aldamazeen, Bau), in East Darfur 25,373 IDPs& returnees – 11,724 HCs (Eddaein, Adila, Yassin), in North Darfur in ( Kutum, Saraf Omra) and 15,129 vulnerable HCs in Abyei PCA Area. the humanitarian situation is critical in all assessed locations and major educational and child protection concerns. 60% of the assessed population are women and children, the lack of protection networks, trained PSS staff and huge number of unaccompanied and Separated Children were reported in the assessments and consultations sessions. in addition, to cases of GBV (physically and mentally disorders children and adolescent, FGM and early marriage) are widely practiced. Also, child labor which expose children to even more GBV, SGBV, school drop off and child recruitment, high rates of child labor exposing the children to different types of child exploitation and abuse. The CBCPNs exist in some areas but are missing/weak in some localities, forced child marriage and other types of child abuse is among the concerns. In education high cost of education such as school fees, cost of textbooks, learning materials and school uniform besides cost of school meals prevents parents from sending their school age children to school , forced child marriage and other types of child abuse is among the concerns, Abyei remains completely under served with most areas inaccessible and very few players. GAH with IOM have worked on some projects before but currently education is missing in intervention. In 2018 GAH started a protection project but the needs are too huge compared to the gaps. The project would like to utilize the CBCPN to reach out school dropout is very high approx. 2000 school age child among the IDP children are out of school due to families unable to pay school & registration fees, buy school uniform and school feeding. Classrooms in existing schools are overcrowded with 70 to 100 students per class. Some children are working to support their families and most of the school is lacking school WASH facilities and 48 are in need for rehabilitation. school dropout among the nomadic children is very high according to the locality based MoE the rate is high among the locality children and significant high among the nomadic population.

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through protection mainstreaming, GAH interventions aim to ensure ease of access by the targeted population to Child/Adolescent centers and schools. This approach will be implemented throughout the project in a fair and just manner devoid of discrimination and will take into account challenges of mobility and access by intended target beneficiaries. Additionally, the enhancement of safe and reliable referral mechanisms and more specifically for persons with special needs will be given top priority. In the same breath, to tackle the challenging social cultural barriers affecting GBV survivors from accessing services and assistance, GAH intends to adopt intense and inclusive awareness raising sessions with the involvement of all stakeholders so as to bring to fore and sustain much needed advocacy necessary to addressing GBV related concerns within the communities. through involvement of local partners. Additionally, through integrated protection and education initiatives, the capacity of established community based structures including CBCPNs and PTAs will be strengthened. for the betterment of service delivery to target communities during sensitization and awareness raising campaigns. By focusing on activities including risk reduction, it is hoped that targeted communities’ level of resilience, awareness and capacity when faced with humanitarian hardships will be enhanced. As a strategy for sustainability, the inclusion of and focus on strengthening local partnerships and existing national systems also aims to boost mainstreaming of protection and GBV into essential services proposed for a more comprehensive, focused and sustainable result.Further, towards this effort, GAH also intends to prioritize safety and dignity and avoid causing harm by striving to tackle through proposed the unforeseen or unintended negative effects on the targeted population that may be a source of renewed exposure to physical and psycho-social risks. This will be achieved as mentioned through close collaboration with all actors undertaking protection and GBV related interventions.Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs) established in the target communities and seek to enhance beneficiaries’ rights to accountability

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

GAH is a Protection based organization and we anchor all our projects on the principles of offering services with dignity and conflict sensitivity. GAH will use our technical skills experience and tested tools to support this project. .While all agree that AAP is vital and necessary in programming it is not always the case that its principles are adhered to fully in the delivery of service more so in a context like Sudan. This project will continuously engage with respondents to ensure AAP is put in its rightful place. Since communities are fluid and conflicts are dynamic there will be a need for continues capacity building especially of the community to engage with the service providers and ensure quality is rendered and on time. Skills in communication with communities, use of tools like TTT for women and DTD for men plus P2P for youth will ensure we are able to communicate to the community. Information sharing through communication with communities (CwC) will be a key in provided the much needed checks and balances for quality. GAH uses a robust culturally appropriate accessible complaints and Feedback mechanism and this will be applied in this project. The clear and well defined complaints and feedback mechanisms will be monitored regularly. The voices of the vulnerable will be heard through such a mechanism especially in this context where there are multi layered barriers to accessing information and complaining. .The community based approach of implementation also ensure that the community has representation in the project and are part of its feedback mechanism. The committees created for Protection and PTAs will be used to not only identify respond and refer beneficiaries but also as sources of information and points of feedback and complaints. GAH will ensure that all project activities have banners with appropriate logos for visibility and that branded signboards are place in all facilities indicating services provided and which ones are free. In addition the staff hired for this project will be qualifies and a proper recruitment process will be followed for new comers. In as much as possible local residents will be given opportunities to work in the project especially for the low levels even in such cases proper hiring procedures will be followed. GAH has a clear policy on its delivery, staff code of conduct and a policy on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). this project will use one and check the application of these policies a bid to strengthen its use and reporting. We do as have policies on graft and abuse of office including allowing whistle blowing, Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFMs) established in the target communities and seek to enhance beneficiaries’ rights to accountability
