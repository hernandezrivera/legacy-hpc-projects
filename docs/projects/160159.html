Project Summary

CMC aims at providing integrated life-saving emergency shelter materials and life-sustaining non-food items to the most vulnerable populations, targeting the IDPs, non-displaced, returnees and vulnerable host communities in Torit and Magwi, Eastern Equatoria State through provision of 70% dignified cash-based solution to build resilience and copping mechanisms and 30% in-kind NFIs to the newly crises affected populations with no access to local market integrating CCCM, Protection, WASH, Nutrition, Education and FSL maintaining coordination through static response modality, contributing to enhancing reduction of protection risks and exposure to diseases due to poor shelter conditions and lack of non-food items. The project strives to support the existing coping mechanisms of the local community and building resilience towards other shocks due to disasters related vulnerability through Cash Based Interventions (CBIs) distributing cash vouchers where markets are functional, and enhancing self-supported solutions for shelter reconstruction to enhance resiliencies, sustainability and beneficiaries’ ownership of the project achieving Clusters objectives SO1,2&4 (SO1:1, 1:2, 2:1, and 2:2)
CMC’s intervention will be part of an integrated response mainstreaming S/NFIs through multi-sectoral intervention with other actors, with CMC providing shelter and NFI’s, while other partners provides food assistance, livelihoods, health, WASH and nutrition to support and rebuild dignified lives of the crises-affected population tackling 70% cash-based and 30% gift in-kind approaches throughout the intervention to alleviate human suffering in a safe and dignified manner, The project targets 10,000 (8,000 Direct, and 2,000 indirect) beneficiaries facing IPC3 and GAM ranging at 20.8% including; unaccompanied elderly, people with no links to the community, the separated children, women at risk (pregnant and lactating mothers), child-headed households, people living with disability and returnees populations. To enhance sustainability and resilience, CMC will build capacities of the communities to cope with current shocks and sustain livelihoods, linkages with development action to support resilience protection and accountability to affected people will be at the central point in this project, using communities centered approaches. CMC will ensure that services delivered are gender-sensitive, considering people with special needs and disables. CMC will establish strong feedback and complain mechanisms affective at all stages of the implementation, training of the AAP focal point consisting of the beneficiaries, the M&E team ensuring feedbacks are addressed. To ensure accountability to affected population, indicators will be established to track cooperation with communities, attaining to complaints and conducting feedback sessions to address GBV threats in S/NFIs, and ensuring services satisfies the needs of the beneficiaries, M&E team will work closely with the trained AAP focal points to note complains and feedback mechanisms and the ACTIVE citizenship, AAP score cards will be use to evaluate every activity during the implementation through a feedback boxes, media talk shows, and feedback from other actors on the ground.
PDM will be conducted to measure the appropriateness, coverage and effectiveness. Beneficiary selection criteria will be established and explained to the community before and during distribution. CMC acknowledged the rapid increasing cases of protection and Sexual Gender Based Violence proving protection and S/GBV as cross cutting thematic issue that need to be addressed across all projects and this project will contribute in addressing the gaps through inter-sector response. CMC protection team will be involve at all stages of designing, planning, implementation and evaluation of every proposed activities to ensure maximum protection mainstreaming and Matigation of GBV and protection threats together with other partners

1 - Needs

The Protracted conflict in South Sudan has caused erosion of physical and social infrastructure, death and displacement of hundreds of thousands of people in former Eastern Equatoria State. The revitalized peace agreement signed did not hold hope in 2016 as conflicts then echoed to tribal conflicts, cattle raiding, and several clashing with the armed groups including SPLA-IO, NAS and other unorganized forces in Eastern Equatoria at least 30,000 to 45,000 people displaced. Magwi and Torit in Eastern Equatoria State are among the most affected with recurrent attacks often causing more displacements, death and destruction of infrastructure. The newly signed peace in 2019 created little hopes as population begun to return in some of the areas in Magwi and Torit in search of resiliencies and rebuilding livelihood through agriculture. The national financial crises have worsened the situation. According to the World Bank, over half (51%) of the 12.3 million South Sudanese live below the national consumption poverty line attributing to the situation continuing to worsen resulting from more people displaced, livelihoods destroyed and more fighting being reported. Renewed inter-tribal fighting, cattle raiding, and political frictions as a results of the boundaries conflicts in the mentioned counties early June 2019 displaced an estimated at least 15,723 people in Magwi and population returns in Torit according to IOM-DTM report. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) ‘Key IPC Findings: January-July 2019’ and September-October 2019’ report highlighted Magwi and Torit counties in Eastern Equatoria state as one of the most food insecure crises affected counties in South Sudan with IPC3 and possible increment to IPC4 by March 2020. Additionally, the October 2018 Standardized Monitoring and Assessment of Relief and Transitions (SMART) survey indicated the prevalence of global acute malnutrition (GAM) was 9.9% and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) at 9%.2 3, Cattle raids and tribal conflicts, which previous REACH studies have linked to food insecurity, have become progressively more frequent, far ranging, and violent letting to luck of proper sheltering and NFIs among the displaced population in the counties. In order to further understand the worsening food insecurity, cattle raiding, tribal conflicts and the linkages between cattle migrations and deteriorating humanitarian conditions including the luck of shelter and NFIs, IRNA assessments in Magwi, and Torit Payam between May and August 2019 indicates population returning in hope of the revitalized peace agreement between the warren parties in South Sudan. The report indicates displaced population totally with no access or less access to water, sanitation, hygiene, health, defecation practices, hand washing, WASH NFI and Shelter/NFI distribution, laundry practices, menstruation, solid waste disposal the report indicated that, nearly 16,000 people were staying with host communities in Magwi and Torit; while more than 3,500 people returning home in hope of the Signed peace agreement. Inter-agencies assessments indicates IDPs and returnees are in dare needs for S/NFIs, Health, FSL, Nutrition, WASH and few to mention. Lack of these services are likely to increase the mortality and morbidity of the disease burden and human sufferings among the displaced population (IDPs). The displaced population are reported to have loss most of their belongings including food and non-food items, further increasing hunger and the risk of malnutrition among children under five years, pregnant and lactating women. According to survey conducted by CMC in early 2019 to the recent peak displacement, the GAM rate in Pajok, Magwi and Torit is at 21.6% with a very low immunization coverage of below 50%. The purpose of this project is to increase access to S/NFIs services to the internally displaced persons and the Host Communities in Eastern Equatoria state

2 - Activities

1. Conducting detailed Shelter NFI needs assessments for the in-kind NFIs and Market assessment for the CBI with focus on risk level, vulnerability and protection threats using mobile data collection method. Every intervention will be preceded with a detailed needs assessment conducted by the NFIs response team. Protection Officer will be part of the assessment team and will capture information linked to safety and dignity of the beneficiaries, and prioritize the assessed needs. Mobile data collection tool or paper tools will be used, depending on the context. All data collection tools will be used such as household interviews, focus group discussions, and key informant interviews will be used to collect data. 2. Conducting verification and registration of target beneficiaries for the in-kind NFIs and CBI with support from CMC’s protection team and focus on community copping mechanisms. Verification of needs and numbers of beneficiaries will be conducted with support of CMC’s protection team, local community and local enumerators will support the process. This activity is only recommended where needs have been identified and clear targeting criteria have been agreed on with the community. This activity proceeds a needs assessment. Verification report is shared with SFPs and cluster coordination team and approval to proceed is given. CMC’s protection team will conduct community awareness and sensitization sessions on reported protection risks, verifying vulnerable persons with specific needs (people with disability, elderly, unaccompanied minors). The protection staff do facilitation of referrals with during NFI intervention cycle. 3. Preparing for distribution through submitting the core pipeline request and pre-positioning of the items to intervention location by pipeline and logs cluster. This activity is only approved once a verification and registration report is approved by SFP and cluster coordination team. A pipeline request is prepared and sent to the SFP and cluster coordination team with an attachment of the assessment and registration reports. This is done for every intervention. Pre-positioning is done by delivery of the supplies to the location before mobilization of registered beneficiaries.4. Conducting trainings and capacity building of the beneficiaries; CMC project shall carryout the training of different groups within the beneficiaries including; Reliefs Distribution Committees (RDCs) the Local Shelter Experts (LSEs), the Volunteers, the AAP focal points, the community participatory monitoring and evaluation team (CPM&ET) to be part of resilience and sustainability since the group remains within the beneficiaries. CMC will ensure Protection and hygiene promotion packages are mainstreamed in the training with coordination from the other actors.5. Conducting distribution of the NFIs and shelter materials to only targeted most vulnerable affected beneficiaries with support of protection team Distribution is organized in a safe and accessible locations accepted by all beneficiaries. Distribution duration is determined by the caseload of the response. This is done in all interventions under this project. Distribution will have designate start and closing times, which is agreed upon by the beneficiaries. This will be conducted across all interventions. The protection staff will do facilitation of referrals during NFI intervention cycle. The NFIs ERT will conduct demonstration on the proper and sustainable use of the NFIs; they will also conduct sensitization environmental issues related to shelter materials collection and energy 5. Conducting rapid monitoring during interventions and Post Distribution Monitoring 30 days after distribution when applicable. Rapid monitoring will be conducted during all distributions by a MEAL officer this is to monitor the quality of the interventions and programming. PIM will be conducted three months after the intervention
