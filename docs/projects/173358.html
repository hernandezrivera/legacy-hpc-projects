Project Summary

In Borno state, situated in the northeast of Nigeria, is the state that has been the most affected by the Boko Haram insurgency and military counter-insurgency operations since 2009, and by extension is highly vulnerable and which have a high record of water borne diseases related deaths due partially to the insurgency in the region. It has been shown that appropriate water, hygiene, and sanitation interventions can decrease diarrhea incidence by 26% and mortality by 65%.
An estimated 2.8 million people across NE Nigeria remain in critical need of sustained, equitable access to clean water and dignified hygiene and sanitation services.
For Access to safe clean water; 1.02 million people receive less than 15 litres per person per day while about 1.06m people have intermittent water access with over 500meters walking distance and over 30 minutes queing time at water points.
In Borno state, an estimated 28 percent of internally displaced people, 34 percent of people living in host communities, and 32 percent of people who have returned to their communities do not have sufficient access to clean water for drinking, cooking, and bathing. As of April 2018, there were 638 reported cases of cholera in the state, including three deaths due to unsafe water consumption. we intend to directly reach 5,000 households, consisting of approximately 35,000 beneficiaries.
At the same time, the water and sanitation situation in the region is critical. The insurgency and counter insurgency has resulted in widespread forced displacement of people and disruption of lives leaving the northern states with a lack of necessities including water and sanitation
Insecurity in these areas occupied by the insurgents also makes planning and delivering essential interventions difficult. Resources in camps for IDPs have been overstretched, with humanitarian agencies providing most of the health services. In most cases the camps are overcrowded and poor sanitation and hygiene has made them potential spots for recurrent outbreaks of diseases such as cholera. This, in the long-run, increases cases of infant and maternal mortality.

2 - Activities

1. Increased access to basic drinking water in households and institutions: We proposed to use STSI water filters, These water filters are amazing and have been Tested and successful in over 46 countries. These water filters out over 100,000 gallons of water and removes 99.8%of bacteria mainly, -Salmonella, E-coli, Typhoid, Hepatitis A/E, Cholera.STSI would use two different filter: The bucket filter -attached to a tank and bucket. These filters does not require electricity. This bucket filter filters out 100,000 gallons of water and with good maintenance and handling it can last up 3 years. ►►Video link to the innovate filters: https://youtu.be/ebP1lIf0IAwWe will also conduct bucket chlorination using Calcium Hypochlorite (HTH chlorine) solution and Treatment with Aquatabs. 2. Operation and Maintenance of water facilities: We will work with water facility operators, technicians and attendants and local partners from the affected population and this aid in strengthening maintenance and building local ownership of facilities. 3. Adoption of key hygiene behaviors correctly and consistently and improved management of fecal waste: The use of community volunteers to perform house to house community hygiene promotion is one of the community engagement strategy to be use. Community mobilizers/volunteers will be strategically deployed to conduct community hygiene education from house to house, providing key messaging on handwashing with soap at critical times, keeping water sources clean, covering water, maintenance of water filters, The mobilizers are responsible for reporting any cases of diarrhea and other water borne ailment they came across and directing them to the nearest treatment centers. 4. Increased access to basic sanitation services in households and institutions:The absence of a clean environment affects human health. Sanitation aims to protect human health by providing a clean environment that prevents the transmission of disease, especially through the fecal-oral route. In Borno, pit/emergency latrines are the sanitation facilities in IDP camps and host communities. When pit latrines become full, it is critical that they are emptied in time; otherwise, the practice of open defecation proliferates, contaminating the environment with ready sources of disease. In the most densely populated camps, which were the main focus for the cholera outbreak, safe management of full pits and eradicating open defecation was critical to maintaining access to safe sanitation and a necessary measure to stop cholera transmission.Community-Led Total Sanitation Approach (CLTS) is a recent innovative and highly effective participatory approach for mobilizing rural communities to address their sanitation problems. This innovative method focuses on spontaneous hygiene and sanitation behavior change led by the entire local community themselves in order to break the focal-oral chain of transmission and stop open defecation totally. Improved management of fecal waste: We provide support by regular desludging and disposal of latrine waste and also partner with Borno State Environmental Protection agency (BOSEPA) is responsible for environmental sanitation and management of sludge disposal sites. Community volunteer’s workers recruited from the affected population will be responsible for cleaning and disinfecting latrines and garbage removal. 5. Capacity Building for Local partners and private sector to deliver WASH services: Capacity building training will be based on Train The Trainer (TOT) approach, where the trainees will be able to have the capacity to train others stakeholders and pass the knowledge down to achieve an hygienic communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

A feedback mechanism will be established and operationalised through stakeholder engagement meetings, anchored by project staff and community leaders, to provide and receive necessary feedback about the impact of the project, including aspects of the project that are going well, challenges, delivery methodology, context-appropriate solutions to problems, and opportunities to drive community capacity and ownership of the activities. Through the engagement of an external project evaluation expert, the aim is to ensure that objective feedback is received from support communities and beneficiaries about the quality, target and impact of the intervention on addressing the goals of the project. The feedback received at intervals during the implementation of the project will, along with the stakeholders’ meetings, influence any changes to the implementation of the project to ensure that it meets the needs of target beneficiaries.
