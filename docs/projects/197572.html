Project Summary

Reference to HNO report 2022, Rural Damascus governorate hosts 1,689,265 people in Health Need out of the total population of 3,325,679 according to health severity scale (updated May 2022), which constitutes the third highest location of PIHN after Aleppo and Idleb. According to HeRAMs Annual Report 2021- Public health centers; out of a total 195 public health centers in Rural Damascus, 31 are classified as Non-functioning, 42 Partially functioning and 122 fully functioning. However, the number of health staff (Doctors, nurses, and Midwives) per 10,000 populations given the benchmark is greater than 22 health staff per 10,000 populations (Inter-Agency Standing Committee (ISAC) Standards) is below this benchmark in public health centers in Rural Damascus.
In this context, Duma is averagely classified as 3 on health severity scale with an estimated number of people in health need of 56,578 out of the total population of 144,000. According to HeRAMs (Health Resource Availability and Mapping System) HCs Q2, Ash-Sharqi health center in Duma is classified as fully functioning while the Infectious Diseases center in not functioning. Nevertheless, same concern mentioned in HeRAMs Annual Report 2021- Public health centers related to availability of health staff (Doctors, nurses, and Midwives) per 10,000 population given the benchmark is greater than 22 health staff per 10,000 population (Inter-Agency Standing Committee (ISAC) Standards) applies in Duma fully functioning center.
Furthermore, According to Syrian Arab Republic: EWARS Weekly Epidemiological Bulletin EPI 2022 Week 23 (5 - 11 June 2022), the leading causes of morbidity in Rural Damascus among all age groups were still Influenza Like Illness (991 ILI / total cases 9808=10.10 %) and Acute Diarrhea (469 AD / total 8,626 =5.4%). While as of 11 June 2021, a total of (5,029) confirmed COVID-19 cases were recorded in Rural Damascus. The number of confirmed AWD cases in Rural Damascus is 37 (Syria Weekly Situation Report, AWD/CHOLERA, 27 October – 3 November 2022).
Due to the large number of vulnerable groups, displaced persons and returnees who have inadequate access to healthcare, LSA will facilitate, through this project, the delivery of needed health services. As the cost of health care services has become inordinately high for the aforementioned vulnerable groups and IDPs, LSA aims to support them in receiving the necessary health services on timely manner via its this project.

3 - Needs

After years of besiegement, a local agreement for Duma was reached on 8 April 2018. Civilian infrastructure in Duma was severely impacted by the hostilities and deterioration of the humanitarian situation inside the area is hugely noticed, with minimal access to basic commodities and services. This puts the vulnerable population at an increased risk of contracting communicable diseases at a time where medical supplies are hardly available as affirmed by OCHA in the Strategic Allocation Paper for First Standard Allocation 2020 which indicates that rural areas including main towns in rural of Damascus are considered in high severity of needs due to low availability of basic services while at the same time high levels of IDPs. Duma is classified as 3 on health severity scale with almost half of the population in health need (37,365). According to HeRAMs (Health Resource Availability and Mapping System), the public health center in Duma Al Jadid is non-functioning as well as the Specialist Rural Damascus public hospital is still not functioning which makes access to primary and secondary health care services of a persistent need. 4 - Implementation of activities

Diagnostic investigation as well as Secondary care will be supported on referral where the referral mechanism process starts with the doctors at the static medical point who initiate the referral requests, then the referral officer assess the criticality of the medical condition and confirm the need for the surgical intervention. The assessment of the medical condition and the outcome of the economic and social study that describes the vulnerability degree for each case are reviewed and the decision of prioritizing the cases to benefit from the services free of charge is made by the referral officer, project manager and one of the board of directors collectively, This study relies on several factors that determine the severity of each case and helps to determine the priorities in providing secondary health services to the most vulnerable people. Then, the patient is directed to the relevant health care facility to receive the needed service where Lamset Shifa association has a network of pre-assessed and contracted health facilities. Once the service is provided, the health facility will send back a copy of the authorized form with the claim that includes information about the supported beneficiary- usually a statement is sent on monthly basis-. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Lamset Shifa Association provides the services to all in need without ethnical, religious, gender and/or political discrimination. Having already existence in Duma through previous engagement with WHO, Lamset Shifa Association was able to interpret the identified health needs into the proposed activities. Moreover, Lamset Shifa Association will maintain open dialog with the communities through direct interviews with random beneficiaries taking into consideration gender / age, elderly, and people with disabilities conducted by the M&E officers on monthly basis, periodic surveys, and small focused group discussions within all precautionary measures. In addition to hotlines displayed in the SMP with transparent and well documented feedback mechanism with taken corrective actions along the project implementation cycle so that their voices are well heard, and their concerns are properly addressed.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Lamset Shifa Association adopted an updated version of its internal Code of Conduct and PSEA policies after a previous assessment conducted by OCAH, whereby areas of clear definition, reporting mechanism, investigation confidentiality and feedback were enhanced. Moreover, Lamset Shifa Association enforces these policies not only by training its staff but also through obtaining their writing acknowledgement and commitment to adhere to these policies.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Lamset Shifa association will monitor day-to-day implementation of the activities where 1 Monitoring and Evaluation officer will be recruited and responsible for monitoring and evaluating project implementation. Reports showing the progress towards achieved results will be submitted on monthly basis. In addition, Small Focused Group Discussions and beneficiaries’ satisfaction surveys will be conducted periodically to ensure proper service provision. A registry will be maintained of all received complaints, actions taken, and the feedback received to ensure transparency and effective feedback mechanism. As well as NGO / HQ hot line numbers will be displayed in the SMP. Moreover, Lamset Shifa Association will welcome Monitoring and evaluation activities conducted by representatives either by field visits to project’s site to monitor the implementation of the project activities or through tele-assessments with direct beneficiaries as cross-check measures that health care services are provided to those most in need and free-of-charge.
