Project Summary

There are enormous child protection concerns created by the on-going crisis in Syria, which has attracted several child protection actors. There are currently more than 90 child protection organizations (comprised of Syrian NGOs, INGOs, SARC, UN agencies) involved in the on-going response, in the existing hubs. This has called for a more strengthened coordination in the Child Protection Area of Responsibility, under UNICEF leadership.
Continued investment in coordination is critical towards building a community of practice, monitoring and providing strategic oversight for a well-coordinated response for the protection of boys and girls in humanitarian settings across the country. Coordination is important to ensure a timely, equitable spread and appropriate interventions, which are efficient, predictable and effective.

4 - Needs

The Syria crisis is one of the most complex humanitarian crisis. Two UN-led coordination hubs and one INGO-led hub have been established in Damascus, Gaziantep and North-East Syria, as well as a Whole of Syria overall coordination system that ensures coherence across the child protection responses through common needs analysis and strategies. As a Sub-cluster Lead agency for Child Protection Area of Responsibility, UNICEF must ensure that sectoral coordination needs at Hubs and at Whole of Syria levels are met through appropriate support. This project presents accordingly the activities and related budget allowing to fulfill these coordination tasks to ensure following objectives • Predictable sectoral leadership and coordination • Increased predictability of geographic coverage and sectoral responsibility • Demonstrated accountability •Increased field level capacities to address gaps. • Maximized coordination and partnership and buy in from the major humanitarian actors to ensure a more strategic and coherent response in support of national led efforts. • Enhanced ability of the Strategic Steering Group to lead a more strategic and coherent response, and better influence how available resources are prioritized. UNICEF has dedicated coordination capacity in the Syria hub only. At the WOS level, as well as Turkey XB hubs UNICEF staff is double hatting as Child Protection Area of Responsibility Coordinators (50%) and technical programme staff responsible for cross-border child protection programming (50%). IM specialists support WOS and hub-based coordination and are also doublehatting with programming and/or education cluster. With the rapid changes in the areas of control there is a growing need to also strengthen sub-national coordination in the 5 field offices of Aleppo, Qamishli, Tartous/Lattakia; Homs/Hama and Rural Damascus throughout 2020. In each of these locations there is a need for additional investment in capacity to reflect the growing demands.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project covers both the staff costs related to the coordination function, including information management, as well as the costs of key strategic activities that are related to the six core coordination functions, namely: i) support service delivery based on needs; ii) inform strategic decision-making through needs assessments and gaps analysis; iii) plan and implement cluster strategies; iv) monitor and evaluate performance; v) build national capacities; and vi) support advocacy efforts. In 2020 the Child Protection AOR will continue to pursue a complementary strategic approach to reach girls and boys with the required flexibility within a rapidly changing operational environment. The coordination structures will build upon the investment of previous years to improve equitable access to quality child protection interventions through two main intervention priorities: 1) improving the quality of community-based child protection through support to community-based structures and psychosocial support interventions; and 2) expanding the reach and improving the quality of child protection services for children most at-risk and survivors of violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. Optimising child protection outcomes through multi-sector responses, strengthening the workforce and generating evidence on child protection issues to inform programming and advocacy withstakeholders are some of the key strategies that will be used by the Child Protection AOR to support these two intervention priorities. More specifically, activities under this project include: Coordination activities such as planning workshops and technical events. Capacity building to strengthen skills and knowledge of CP actors and service providers to provide quality child protection services. Knowledge/evidence generation to supporting the AOR with reliable evidence generation on core child protection issues).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risks• Absence of partners in some of the changing control, to offer the required services. • Low capacity of partners to ensure quality child protection in emergency services. • Limited access due government restrictions to adequately respond to the needs of women and children. • Change in dynamics due to more areas changing control. The challenge will be in managing the transition. • Physical safety of child protection actors if they are perceived to be impartial. • Inadequate data to informing child protection Area of Responsibility programming. • Low funding by donors to sustain coordination and activities.Mitigation measures • Develop a preparedness plans to address different scenarios including possibility of more areas changing control in the North West. The plan should ensure availability of partners, where there is need. • Develop a minimum training package for child protection actors and ensure all child Protection actors are trained on the vital services required. • Advocate with Humanitarian coordinator to lobby government on delivery of required child protection services • Train the Child Protection partners on humanitarian imperative, which requires staff to be impartial.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Increased insecurity, making it hard to deliver child protection services. Limited access to affected population due on-going fighting by parties to the conflict. Limited technical support due to few professional staff. The major assumption is that the limited access will be temporary. There will also be local capacity to ensure response. To mitigate the above risks; Advocate with parties to the conflict to ensure passage of humanitarian assistance, train emergency preparedness teams in some of the hot spots to ensure delivery of services. Invest in community structures to ensure continuity of services. Pre-position the necessary essential Child protection supplies, to ensure continuity of services.
