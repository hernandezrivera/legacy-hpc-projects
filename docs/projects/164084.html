Project Summary

The conflict has widely disrupted markets, agriculture and livelihoods, with negative consequences worsening year on year as the conflict ensues. Typical livelihood strategies – in particular agricultural systems – have been eroded or completely destroyed. Many families have been left without traditionally male breadwinners, leaving female heads of household to enter the labor market – in many cases for the first time, with lack of access to education, training and experience, and conservative social norms limiting opportunities. The main changes at HH level identified by participants were an increase in HH members contributing to HH income, including negative coping strategies engaged in by women and children.
PIN will support communities in both NES and NWS to enhance market systems and empower vulnerable, conflict-affected households to launch viable income-generating activities to meet their basic needs through activities including Cash for Work (CfW), vocational training, apprenticeships and business grants targeting at-risk households, including those resorting to negative coping strategies. Cash for work approaches empower beneficiaries to work for the cash assistance received, promoting resilience and dignity whilst supporting communities and stimulating local markets. PIN favors high-value CfW activities that support broader communities through the provision of services, infrastructure, and/or production of products for sale or donation. PIN’s cash for work approach covers the most vulnerable HH for a minimum of three months, supporting them to meet their basic needs whilst minimizing resort to negative coping strategies. Initiatives supported will include public infrastructure rehabilitation, and activities explicitly intended to support women, including education-oriented, knitting and sewing activities. Furthermore, PIN will implement an integrated package of Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET), Small Business Grants (SBGs) and Apprenticeship placements with local businesses. Target populations will include vulnerable male and female heads of household within host, IDP and returnee communities.
PIN aims to provide most needed assistance to such groups through: employing a wide spectrum of different activities to meet distinct needs, establishing beneficiary selection criteria to prioritise inclusion of those most vulnerable and adapting its assistance delivery to promote access for the most vulnerable people. PINs programmes are carried out in coordination with local authorities at various levels – from cross-border, to hub, to district and village level. PIN engages in regular dialogue with authorities to ensure transparency, accountability, and protection mainstreaming. This includes consultations and briefings on needs, humanitarian principles, PIN procedures, and beneficiary selection at planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation stages of projects.

4 - Needs

Recent PIN’s assessments across Northern Syria highlighted that works for infrastructure repairs remain a significant priority across all assessed communities, with all groups assessed likewise ranking Cash for Work (CfW) in their top 3 priority assistance modalities. In NES, during repeated meetings with LCs, municipalities and HAO, needs for minor rehabilitation of physical infrastructure including roads, sidewalks rehabilitation and lightening of streets were frequently mentioned. Women’s committees repeatedly stressed the necessity and usefulness of sewing and knitting activities for females as a socially-acceptably pathway into income generation. Recent Endline assessments from CfW activities in NWS highlighted an 88% decrease of beneficiaries in the top 25% of severe negative coping strategies and a 27% increase in the number of beneficiaries with acceptable food consumption scores at the Endline phase compared to baseline. PIN’s assessments from 2018, demonstrated that the main employment barriers were lack of employment opportunities, lack of skills, low wages for unskilled labor and high competition amongst unskilled laborers. Due to the destruction of both livelihoods themselves, as well as vocational training opportunities contingent upon large-scale employers, formal education services or factories, respondents saw little chances of acquiring well paid, skilled jobs. Women are facing additional challenges to participate in the job market because of household obligations and child-care. This problem is particularly severe in Raqqa, where there are high rates of widowed/divorced women, and FHH depend on support from the community or child labor to be able to survive. Pre-war livelihoods ranged from work in tailoring shops, working in factories or farming, or working in construction and retail shops. By contrast, post-conflict employment mentioned across all areas was daily work; affecting even those who used to work in agriculture or in large factories.

5 - Implementation of activities

PIN aims to Strengthen access to livelihood by creating income generating opportunities and by improving access to production and market infrastructure to restore local economy recovery. The following activities are foreseen: Cash for Work (CfW), Delivery of Technical Vocational Education and Training (TVET), Apprecentships, Small Business Grants (SBGs). CfW targets the most vulnerable through cash for work activities based on a combination of eligibility criteria and at least one vulnerability criteria. PIN will strive to ensure inclusion of PwDs. The workers will be employed during 12 weeks (approx. 60 days), 5 days/week and the daily amount will be adjusted based on the situation at the time of implementation accordingly, to be in line with local labor market trends as reported by PIN surveys or data from REACH or information provided by the Cluster respectively. PIN will provide temporary support for operational costs and/or staff incentives, as well as support the institutions/NGOs with the necessary training equipment. The training cycles will last for 3 months (up to 12 weeks), with the beneficiaries attending 4 hours a day, five days a week; an equivalent of a part-time job, allowing beneficiaries to maintain other income-generating activities and/or domestic or child care responsibilities. The most successful TVET graduates will be selected for SBGs. PIN will prioritize the business grants that thematically match the vocational trainings provided. A call for submission of business proposals will be announced and the best one selected by the Evaluation Committee. The value of SGBs has been set in line with the Northern Syria Food Security and Livelihoods cluster recommendations. The apprenticeship component is designed to partner workers with potential employers and foster employment opportunities of beneficiaries. At the end of the mentoring cycle, apprentices who successfully finish their apprenticeship will receive a start-up kit, where appropriate.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

As per PIN’s protection mainstreaming strategy for 2019-2021, PIN carries out regular protection risk assessments, focusing on all assistance modalities. These protection risk assessments feed into the development of protection risk and mitigation matrices. The following major risks and respective mitigation measures (MM) were identified: 1)R: Most vulnerable are not selected for assistance, 1)MM: targeting criteria communicated with the communities and LCs; advertisement in targeted public places; use of Washington groups Questions and advocacy for inclusion of PwDs by the local councils.;100% verifications of the beneficiaries; FGDs with representatives from a cross-section of community, wide advertisements and communication channels for PIN’s CFRM, 2)R:inter-groups tensions over assistance; 2)MM:Community stakeholder meetings, harmonization of the packages across partners, Provision of assistance to host communities and IDPs.3)R:PSEA 3)MM: Training of all staff and trainers; Gender balanced teams, Clear process to swiftly address PSEA case, Signs are posted throughout project sites stating that assistance is free. 4)R: Project sites are not accessible/safe, 4)MM: distribution of protective equipment for CfW, collaboration with Security and Access teams for timing and place of CfW activities, Accessibility adjustments of TVET centers to ensure access of PwDs.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

(A1/R1): The security situation in all target areas remains conducive to implementation; (MM1): Suspend/relocate activities to areas where access is upheld; and/or switch to remote management, potentially through pre-identified local partners. (A2/R2) PIN’s staff, offices and warehouses in Syria are not subjected to targeting/abductions/raiding by armed actors. (MM3): rigorous security monitoring and management mechanism. If specified risks occurred, PIN would temporarily suspend activities; carry out internal/external investigations; advocate for dialogue with relevant actors, inform relevant counter-terrorism agencies/authorities/partners of the incident; and seek to relocate the intervention;(A3/R3): LCs/others continue to collaborate in line with principles. (MM3): Temporarily suspend activities; engage in dialogue to re-sensitize on principles of engagement. If such action fails, PIN would relocate resources to other target areas.(A4/R4): Armed actors present in areas of operation facilitate safe and principled humanitarian access; (MM4): Suspend operations, engage in advocacy with local authorities and armed actors (jointly with others, including UNOCHA, NGO forums, and/or Access Working Groups) in an effort to restore free and unhindered access. If such efforts were unsuccessful, relocate activities to other target areas facing acute needs; (A5/R5): Local markets remain functional across target areas; (MM5): If border crossings to Syria were closed/restricted or PIN was prevented from transferring goods, PIN would suspend activities; advocate with UNOCHA/local authorities to facilitate access; purchase locally if possible; and shift funds to Cash-based programming. (A6/R6): PIN is able to retain registration in NES under the HAO, in Erbil under the KRI Administration and central Iraqi authorities, and in Turkey; (MM6): If PIN’s registration or visas in cross-border response hubs, PIN would adopt a phased approaches to withdrawal.
