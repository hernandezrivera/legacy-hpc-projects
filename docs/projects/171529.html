Project Summary

Somali has one of the highest and protracted internally displaced persons worldwide and every year communities are displaced by conflict and natural disasters like drought and flood. IDPs and marginalized communities including disabled persons have lower access to healthcare and social services than others. In 2020 COVID-19 pandemic has disrupted health system gains and further exacerbated population vulnerabilities. As of September 26th 2020, Somalia has confirmed 3,588 cases of COVID-19, including 187 health care worker infections; with a case fatality rate of 2.7 percent. With a medium attack rate for COVID-19 over the course of 2021, an estimated 20% of the Somali population (2.5 million) will need to be reached with some form of COVID-19 response action; from awareness, preventative to testing and treatment services. One in three people are or have been affected by mental health disorders in Somalia (WHO, 2016), a rate higher than other low-income and war-torn countries. Also the COVID-19 has added to the mental suffering of the affected population and aggravated gender and interpersonal violence (GBV/IPV). According to the Somalia health cluster, over 3.9 million Somali people require lifesaving essential healthcare and health protection services.
Sexual and reproductive health remains huge concern in Somalia. Over a Somali woman’s lifetime, she will face a one in 14 chance of dying from complications related to pregnancy or childbirth; facing the sixth highest lifetime maternal death risk (692 deaths per 100,000 live births) in the world (SHDS, 2020); the country has one of the world’s highest rates of under-five mortality at 177/1000 live births (UN IGME, 2020). despite most maternal deaths being preventable. The risk of childhood mortality is highest in the neonatal period, accounting for over 60 percent of deaths among children under age five (UN IGME, 2020). Women and children are particularly exposed to elevated health risks. Access to birth spacing services is limited, and up to 99 percent of women experience Female Genital Mutilation/Cutting (FGM/C) (SHDS, 2020). Survivors of gender-based violence (GBV), reported as 77 percent of IDPs and 20 percent from the host community, face fear of reprisals, stigmatization and difficulties accessing safe and appropriate services (JCMNA, 2019). Negative coping practices as a result of poverty, displacement and isolation due to the crisis, that have also been exacerbated by COVID-19, place the affected at increased risk of violence, abuse and marginalization; as well as negatively affecting their health care choices and access. Care for expectant mothers throughout their pregnancy remains particularly poor, with only 32 percent of births attended by skilled health personnel (SHDS, 2020); these gaps in reproductive health services along with appropriate referral care is essential to prevent the high incidence of maternal and infant death.
Thereafter, UNFPA and its partners will implement this project to respond those needs with the primary focus to provide life-saving quality reproductive and maternal health services for the vulnerable displaced and other crisis affected communities in Somalia. The following are the specific objectives of the intervention:
a) To ensure access to quality emergency obstetric and newborn care services for the IDPs & crises affected communities including COVID19 pandemic.
b) To provide promotive and preventive sexual and reproductive health care including maternal nutritional services among women of childbearing age in the affected population
c) Enhance the capacity of service providers for sexual and reproductive health including Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) and COVID-19 prevention and control.
d) Support the procurement and supply chain management for the sexual and reproductive health commodities including RH kits, PPEs, Infection Prevention & Control (IPC) and COVID-19 testing equipment and supplies.

Project Activities

The interventions will focus on provision of life-saving quality reproductive and maternal health services for the vulnerable displaced and crisis affected communities in Somalia including persons with disabilities. The health services will be delivered through static health facilities and mobile health clinics. UNFPA, through its implementing partners, will: OUTPUT 1: Improve access to quality emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) services for the IDPs and other affected population during the COVID19 Pandemic Activity 1: improve access to skilled birth attendant deliveries in BEmONC and CEmONC facilities in the affected areas and IDP settings during the COVID19 PandemicActivity 2: Ensure access to skilled care to all pregnant women with pregnancy related complications Activity 4: Support referrals of Obstetric and newborn emergencies from Primary Health facilities to referral health facilities Activity 4: Procurement and distribution of Emergency Reproductive Health (ERH) kits, PPEs and COVID19 testing equipment and supplies.Activity 5: Conduct training of 60 nurses, midwives, and doctors on infection prevention & control and minimal initial service package OUTPUT 2: Provide curative and preventive sexual and reproductive health care including maternal nutritional services among women of childbearing age in the affected population during the COVID19 targeting most vulnerable communities including IDPs and persons with disabilities. Activity 1: Improve access to modern contraceptive methods among women of child bearing ageActivity 2: Conduct nutrition screening and awareness for pregnant and lactating women and procure and distribute iron and folic acid Activity 3: Ensure clear, consistent risk communication and public health messaging on sexual reproductive health, maternal national awareness and COVID19 prevention. These interventions will be designed and implemented to strengthen maternal and reproductive health services, to protect health care workers, and to ensure testing capacity for COVID-19. Furthermore, once these services are in place and strengthened, especially in the static clinics and the integrated services for RH care, every effort will be made to ensure their continuity beyond the life of this project. This will be achieved by making them part of the national and government network of services, with human resources identified and placed for longer term service, as well being supported through Ministry of Health’s supply chain for medical commodities and supplies and other supervisory support.
