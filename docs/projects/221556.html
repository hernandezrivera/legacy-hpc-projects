Project Summary

South Sudan remains in the topmost food insecure countries globally, with needs expected to rise to record high levels heralding the risk of Famine. Currently, it is projected that 65-70% of the population, will require food aid beginning at the peak of the lean season (FEWSNET June 2024). The nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million U5 children projected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 0.65m cases of SAM. About 1.11 million PBW are also projected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. In 49 counties, acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15% (IPC AMN Nov 2024).
The situation is worse in counties such as Fangak and Nyirol which have suffered cyclical effects of floods and insecurity. As per the South Sudan NC updates of June 2024, between Jan to May 2024, Nyirol and Fangak had the highest OTP and PLW admissions respectively nationally. Hence according to the Intersectoral Priority (OCHA), Fangak and Nyirol have been categorized under priority 1 and 2 respectively. This calls for a concerted effort for urgent treatment and prevention interventions and this is the background upon which this project has been designed and proposed. Nutrition life-saving interventions under this project will target primarily U5 children, PBW, and adolescent girls. Whereas the NC target is 100,562 (Fangak 54,322 and Nyirol 46,240), this project will target 56,797 (Fangak 10,557 and Nyirol 46,240), beneficiaries out of the total PIN of nutrition assistance in 2025 from high vulnerability.
This project will be aligned to the Nutrition Cluster’s objectives and intervention prescriptions. To address these multiple drivers of chronic malnutrition and for results optimization, an Integrated Package of interventions will be adopted involving collaboration with key sectors namely FSL, WASH, Nutrition, Health and GBV Interventions for multiple services to vulnerable families. In the prevailing context of conflict and flooding, both static and mobile outreach services shall be adopted to reach the displaced with nutrition survival kits to ensure timely emergency response. To strengthen sustainable nutrition outcomes, the response activities will cover (1) case finding screening, (2) early detection and admission to OTP services for MAM U5 children including treatment of malaria, diarrhea and other high prevalence diseases through collaboration with the health sector (3) provide SC services for SAM children with medical complications; (4) deliver expanded MIYCN services to PBW and care-givers of children under 23 months through counselling of PBW, care-providers and MtoM support groups, including vitamin A supplements, de-worming and kitchen gardens.
AAP will be strengthened by training project staff on how to apply the QAAP approach, establishing QAAP committees and initiating community complain and feedback mechanism in the nutrition facilities preceded by sensitization through community awareness raising and having systems in place for representation of their different interests and need. Further there will be focus on raising awareness regarding beneficiaries’ rights and entitlement in all the nutrition sites including their involvement in the design, implementation, M and E on the planned nutrition project.
The current drivers of GBV will be addressed through mainstreaming protection and disability inclusion, emphasis on gender and age considerations, conducting safety audits and collaborating with the targeted communities in implementing protection practices to reduce risk of SGBV. To improve sustainability, this project will be integrated fully with health and use the BPHNS to guide capacity building targeting SMoH, CHDs, project staff and CNVs as well as other common interest groups such as MtoMSG and local authorities with a focus on job-relevant training including refresher courses.

1. Needs

South Sudan remains in the topmost food insecure countries globally, with needs expected to rise to record high levels and some areas facing a risk of Famine. It is grappling with a long-standing humanitarian crisis marked by chronic food and nutrition insecurity exacerbated by the ongoing conflict in Sudan, which has forced hundreds of thousands to flee into S. Sudan (WFP Situation report # 326). In its S. Sudan - Food Security Outlook for June 2024 to Jan 2025, FEWS NET projected that 65-70% of the population, will require food aid beginning at the peak of the lean season. An estimated 67% of the acute malnutrition burden is concentrated in the five states of Jonglei, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Upper Nile, Unity and Warrap. In the current analysis period (July to September 2024), 53 counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 3 or above (Serious or worse). According to the IPC AMN Snapshot for September 2024 - July 2025, of 18 November 2024, the nutrition situation has also deteriorated, with an estimated 2.1 million children aged 6-59 months suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition between July 2024 and June 2025, including 650,000 cases of SAM. About 1.11 million pregnant or breastfeeding women (PBW) are also suffering or expected to suffer elevated levels of acute malnutrition in the same period. In 49 counties, acute malnutrition rates exceed the emergency threshold of 15%. The situation is worse in counties such as Fangak and Nyirol which have suffered cyclical effects of floods and insecurity. Flooding has caused displacement, loss of food crops and damage to facilities causing service disruptions and access barrier for all those seeking nutrition services. Fangak and Nyirol, are among the worst food affected counties with areas such as Juaibor, Keew and Nyadin in Fangak, Pultruk, Chuil & Pading in Nyirol having experienced severe flooding for 4 consecutive years. According to the S. Sudan NC updates of June 2024, between Jan to May 2024, Nyirol and Fangak had the highest OTP and PLW admissions respectively nationally. Hence according to the Intersectoral Priority (OCHA), Fangak and Nyirol have been categorized under priority 1 and 2 respectively. The NC’s target of 100,562 (Fangak 54,322 and Nyirol 46,240) beneficiaries further explains the burden of malnutrition in the two targeted counties. Whereas the key drivers of Acute food insecurity have been identified as Economic crisis, Conflict and insecurity, Climatic shocks, Low agricultural production, on the other hand, Acute Malnutrition has been attributed to Poor childcare feeding practices, High disease burden, Limited access to sanitation facilities and Low program coverage. This poses a challenge to the universally recognized position that the sustained improvement in nutrition status can only be achieved when food security improves, when people have access to improved water and sanitation infrastructure, and adopt improved hygiene practices, and when people have access to prevention and treatments of malaria, diarrhea and other high prevalence diseases. The Sectoral Objective 4 underscores the need to strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women. Integration with health should ensure that critical services are offered e.g integration of SC in Health delivery, Prevention, testing and treatment of malaria, Prevention and management of diarrhea including provision of Zinc & ORS as well as EPI (Immunization). Whereas mainstreaming child protection and GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites is critical it is, important to note that this package can only be complete if the Nutrition program scales up the provision of MIYCN counselling, micronutrients, BSFP, detection and treatment of wasting as well as integration of child stimulation and ECD in nutrition sites.

2. Project activities

The project will scale up interventions in Nyirol and Fangak Counties to ensure that U5 girls and boys, adolescent girls, and women in South Sudan have increased and equitable access to quality nutrition services and adopt appropriate nutrition-related behaviors. The implementation will strengthen preventative actions that reduce the rate of malnutrition and improve the sustainability of treatments. The project will improve overall nutrition service delivery effectiveness by increasing community engagement, facility-based data collecting and analysis, and feedback information. Timely coordination and engagement with the Ministry of Health will be critical in building long-term solutions. This nutrition project will be integrated with other sectors at the county level for collaborative programming with co-located partners in FSL, Health (HSTP), Protection, and WASH.The activities planned to attain output targets are described below: Objective 1: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2025.To achieve the above objective, respective activities will include the following: 1.1 Prevention of malnutrition through promotion and provision of MIYCN counseling services and social behavior change , 1.2 Prevention of micronutrient deficiencies through micronutrient supplementation Objective 2: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PBW in prioritized locations by the end of 2025. For objective 2, the following activities will be prioritized: 2.1 Early detection and treatment of girls and boys under 5 years of age and PBWG with acute malnutrition, 2.2 Advocacy for integration of nutrition sites in the health facility service delivery ,2.3 Improving program quality by ensuring that SC,OTP and TSFP sites meet at least three Sphere standards in relation to the treatment of severe acute malnutrition ,2.4 Monitoring & Supportive supervision visits, 2.5 Scaling up capacity strengthening efforts through on job training (OJT)& mentoring. 2.6 Support CNVs to undertake home visits, 2.7 Strengthening AAP by establishing and sustaining community feedback mechanism at all nutrition sites.Objective 3: To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information by the end of 2025. Corresponding activities for objective 3 will include the following: 3.1 Support nutrition surveys and assessments, 3.2 Improve Nutrition Information Management SystemObjective 4: To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable children and women in prioritized locations by the end of 2025. Under objective 4 these activities will be undertaken: 4.1 Prevention, screening, and treatment for morbidities among PBWs and children under five years with acute malnutrition in nutrition sites, 4.2 Integration with WASH and provision of WASH kits to children with SAM in nutrition sites, 4.3 Establishing or maintaining demonstration kitchen garden at nutrition sites or in the vicinity, 4.4 Integration of child stimulation and ECD in the provision of care for children with SAM and MAM, 4.5 Mainstreaming of GBV risk mitigation at nutrition sites, 4.6 Collaborate with health in the management of diarrhea.
