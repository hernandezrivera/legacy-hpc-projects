Project Summary

CNSF would like to undertake the MPCA project in areas of Mosul and Anbar where there is a high number of returnees, vulnerable households and the correct conditions for a cash distribution.
We will target areas with a lack of livelihood opportunities and depletion of financial resources based on MCNA results and additional assessments. Multi-purpose cash assistance will help empower beneficiaries and support durable and sustainable returns by providing highly vulnerable families with cash to meet their basic needs according to the Minimum Expenditure Basket in Iraq. This will help to prevent negative coping mechanisms and secondary displacement. The Multi-cluster needs assessment (MCNA) indicates that the need for multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) has increased over 40 per cent.
The beneficiary selection will be based on socio-economic vulnerability using the standard CWG tools and scoring mechanism to assess beneficiaries for one or multi-month MPCA installments. We will undertake community consultations and assessments to identify the most appropriate method of cash transfer and to understand what their main basic needs are. This will be linked to a Market Assessment to identify if the main items needed are available locally and to understand the health of the market system.
This program will support sustainable and durable transition to return as the assessment data can link to the Cash Transfer Social Protection Program implemented by the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs (MoLSA) and link with national level governmental rather than creating a duplicate or alternate system.

Needs

The Multi-cluster needs assessment (MCNA) indicates that the need for multi-purpose cash assistance (MPCA) has increased over 40 per cent. Within the Old City the beneficiaries prefer the cash option as it allows them more freedom and flexibility to purchase items to meet their exact needs. Cash can support beneficiary purchasing power and respond to a range of assessed humanitarian needs. Additionally, cash has the added value of building resilience and assisting in the transition to recovery and development which is critical for the current context in Mosul and Anbar where returnees are settling back into their homes after periods of displacement and beginning to rebuild their lives. Cash can also support in the protection and restoration of livelihoods if the households should choose to use it for that purpose. CNSF has conducted initial market assessments in Bab Lagash and Bab Jaded markets Mosul and Al Khaldiya markets in Anbar and has found that the markets are open are functioning which makes them suitable areas for an MPCA project, however further more in-depth assessments will be conducted at the beginning of the project to ensure that the market systems are able to manage an increase in demand for the key items. We will coordinate with other actors such as ICRC who had done a blanket MPCA in the Old City but have recently shared areas which they have not been able to reach but have identified needs. This cash transfer project can help to further revitalize the economy by injecting money into the market and increasing the volume of trade and number of traders and, further down the line, a healthy market can create more labor jobs, thus supporting resilience and transition out of the humanitarian phase. The caseload for this project has been estimated using CWG guidance of 23.8% for 3 month transfer, 31.8% for 2 months transfer and 44.4% for a 1 month installment.

Activities

a) Assessments This project will begin with using MCNA data to identify areas with high needs and desk review to find out if Market Assessments and CWG assessments have already taken place in these areas, if so we will set up data sharing agreements to access the information, if not we will conduct our own assessments. The market assessment and community consultations are essential to ensuring the pre-conditions for a CTP are in place. We will conduct community consultations regarding the most appropriate cash transfer method to ensure that female headed households, people with disabilities and elderly can also take part and that the approach does not exclude those who for example, cannot attend a distribution site or do not have a mobile phone. b) Selection of Beneficiaries CNSF will use the Cash Working Groups (CWG) Vulnerability Assessment Tool to guide our selection of beneficiaries. The total number of beneficiaries will be determined based on their eligibility for 1, 2 or 3 months of assistance using the CWG vulnerability scoring tool. Once identified the beneficiaries will receive and information sheet explaining about the transfer mechanism, CNSF, the aims of this project, their rights and method of submitting a complaint or feedback. The Project Manager and Field Coordinator will be responsible for undertaking the selection and identification of beneficiaries, the Director and Program Manager will support with security and access monitoring and with technical support. c) Transfer methodology - The transfer methodology will be determined by the target populations as they can advise what the most appropriate and accessible approach will be. Based on this CNSF will put together a plan for the most feasible approach and complete a risk assessment and mitigation process to ensure that we can deliver securely. Examples of approaches include using the Hawala system which is commonly used in Mosul or direct cash. The Finance Manager and Oﬃcer will be key to this process. The Field Coordinator and Project Manager will conduct monitoring of the markets, liaison with local authorities when necessary and the complaints and feedback mechanism during this time. d) Protection Mainstreaming - A guiding principle of protection mainstreaming is meaningful access, therefore CNSF will do our best to ensure that the selected households are able to take part in the transfer by adapting our modality when necessary. As part of the initial assessments we will conduct a gender analysis to identify the barriers and risks women, people with disabilities and the elderly may face in taking part in this project and to develop mitigation measures and adapt to ensure they are not excluded from participation. When considering the principle of Do No Harm the MPCA could cause the beneficiaries to become targets and be put at risk of harm, theft or extortion if it is known that they have access to $400 that month. To mitigate this we will conduct an analysis of the local security environment to ensure the most suitable means of assistance and delivery is chosen. CNSF will not widely publicize the amount of cash or the location of the beneficiaries to help prevent risk of attacks or theft. To mitigate the risk of fraud, extortion and intervention by authorities we will ensure the time when, and the environment through which they must travel to reach the assistance, is safe for all people concerned. If safety is a problem we will make a special effort to move the point of delivery closer to beneficiaries homes or look at mobile money options. CNSF will set up a Complaints and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) to allow beneficiaries to report any problems or concerns they’re facing with the cash including extortion, threats or theft. The Program Manager will support with protection and gender mainstreaming will create an appropriate complaints and feedback mechanism based on our current mechanism and the communities advice on the most appropriate approach. We will conduct analysis of household and community dynamics regarding the use of cash as a modality of assistance to determine if there are risks specific to giving cash to women within a household or community. This analysis will consider whether the response will exacerbate protection risks particularly in terms of GBV. e) Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) - Following the 3-month distribution phase CNSF will conduct a PDM to assess the impact of the project and to identify any areas of concern or improvement. We will use the standard CWG PDM template and will conduct the questions in-person (when access and security allow) and over the phone. In the past we have used google forms to collect the information and to easily produce graphics, charts and comparisons to analyze the results. The Project Manager, Field Coordinator and Admin Manager will work together to create a PDM questionnaire that is suitable to judge the impact of the project.

Protection Risk Analysis

Security: The presence of cash in an unstable environment can make the beneficiaries a target for looting, theft and extortion. Diversion: If cash goes to unintended beneficiaries. Market risks: The increase of disposable cash into the local market has the potential to distort market norms, prices and items. This can cause the market to shift to meet the needs of the beneficiaries and may not be healthy for the market as a whole. Cash as a pull factor risk: The targeting of this area may pull other returnees to return to Mosul and Anbar and potentially to return to homes which are not safe or where there is presence of UXO’s. This pull factor can cause people to risk harm by moving back into unsafe environments. Beneficiary selection risk: As this project will identify 2000 households it is likely that they will be chosen from part of a larger neighborhood which might cause tensions within the community because not all households will receive the cash transfer. GBV: Giving cash to FHH can lead to conflict within the household or impact the gender dynamics which could lead to GBV as male family members could feel useless or uncomfortable with women taking part in this kind of project which is traditionally male activities

Mitigation Measures

Security Mitigation: A common form of transfer is through the Hawala system which reduces the amount of physical cash being moved around in one go. The distributions will be capped at 100 households per day of distribution. We will not publicize the dates of the cash transfer or identify the beneficiary’s location to protect them from being targeted for extortion or coming to harm. We will conduct a full risk assessment for each area and the methdology we choose to make the payments with. Diversion Mitigation: The beneficiary selection process is designed to target the most vulnerable and each household will need to register to receive the transfer using their ID cards or other forms of oﬃcial ID. This will help to ensure that the cash is going to the intended beneficiaries. Market risks Mitigation: The cash will be transferred in installments based on progress towards pre-agreed construction targets. This will distribute the impact on the market. In the assessment stage a market analysis will be conducted which will assess the availability of the required materials in the markets and the vendors capacity to scale up to meet higher demand for the time period of the project. The prices in the market will be monitored throughout the project to identify if there has been a change in prices and to analyse if that is a result of this project. Cash as a pull factor risk Mitigation: We will avoid a pull factor by controlling the visibility of the project and by working with the community to explain the vulnerability criteria and purpose of the project and the timeframe. With continued consultation and communication CNSF will ensure that the community understands the whole project and that returning to the area is not a guarantee of humanitarian assistance. Beneficiary selection risk Mitigation: CNSF will aim to target peri-urban, under-served neighborhoods and will conduct 3000 assessments to select 2000 beneficiary households. GBV Mitigation: We will minimise the risks to women and children by monitoring the project throughout and by conducting a gender analysis at the beginning to identify the kinds of barriers and risks women may face. We will select a low visibility transfer mechanism to keep the cash transfer discreet.

How is your project accountable to affected population

CNSF follows the commitments in the AAP Framework: 1) Leadership/Governance – we will ensure complaints and feedback mechanisms are integrated into our project design and all staff sign the code of conduct.2) Transparency – We will provide accessible and timely information to affected populations regarding CNSF as an organisation, the processes that affect them such as the vulnerability criteria and feedback mechanisms. We will ensure that they have the information to make informed decisions and will facilitate a dialogue. We often use information sheets, community consultations and focus group discussions, however, we are adaptable to the different needs and different contexts in each location.3) Complaints and Feedback – we will base out mechanism on community discussions and adapt it to ensure it is accessible to all the community. We actively seek the communities views from the project design phase and all through the implementation. 4) Participation – we will involve the affected populations though-out the project cycle and encourage their participation in decision making processes and will endeavor to include the most marginalized.5) Design, monitoring and evaluation – we will conduct PDM’s and ongoing monitoring to ensure that we can feedback learning back into the organisation and make evidence based decisions for the project. We are committed to communication with beneficiaries and promoting beneficiary feedback at all stages of the project cycle, from needs assessment to project evaluation.
