Project Summary

The project aims to enhance access to nutrition services for children under five and pregnant and lactating women in remote areas. It will be implemented in Tal Tamer sub-district in Al-Hasakeh governorate, and in Hajin and Susat sub-district in Abu-Kamal district, Der Ez Zor governorate. The catchment areas, as per OCHA, encompass:
• Hajin: 104,568 individuals.
• Susat: 39,081 individuals.
• Tal Tamer: 58,209 individuals.
Nutrition services will be provided by two mobile teams in Tal Tamer and by two mobile teams and two static teams in primary health care centers in Abu Kamal district (Hajin and Susat sub-districts). Each team will comprise four Community Health Workers (CHWs), one team leader, one nutritionist, and one data clerk, totalling six teams and 42 staff members. Community and settlement lists to be visited will be identified by each team, with the plan reviewed every three months.
Prior to commencing activities, all teams will undergo training on Community-Based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) and Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF) programs, as well as orientation on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and safeguarding issues.
The teams' main responsibilities include:
1. Conducting screening for children under five using Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) and z-score screening for malnutrition.
2. Screening Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLWs) using MUAC screening.
3. Provide treatment for moderate acute malnourished children.
4. Provide treatment for Sever acute malnourished children without complication.
5. Provide treatment for moderate acute malnourished PLWs.
6. Distributing blanket multivitamins to PLWs.
7. Conducting one-to-one counselling sessions for PLWs and caregivers on initiating breastfeeding, exclusive breastfeeding for up to six months, and introducing adequate and appropriate complementary foods for children aged 24-59 months.
8. Providing group counselling on the same topics.
9. Disseminating messages through Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials to raise awareness about IYCF and CMAM activities.
10. Following up with referred malnourished cases when possible.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

According to the 2024 HNO, 5.7 million people in Syria require life-saving nutrition services in 2024, recurrent disease outbreaks, waterborne diseases, water crises, vaccine-preventable illnesses, and food insecurity are contributing to rising mortality and morbidity, including increased rates of malnutrition and increased humanitarian needs. The 2024 SMART survey, conducted by NES partners, revealed significant nutrition challenges across Governorates in North East Syria, for instance, the prevalence of combined GAM rate in our project locations in DEZ and Alhsakeh, with 8.4% and 4.3% GAM rate respectively. The stunting increased dramatically in all NES, with a current rate of 26.6% in Alhasakeh and 33.4% in DEZ. Figures also show low percentages of proper nutrition practices, with Timely initiation of breastfeeding within the first hour of birth ranging from very low (6.7%) in DEZ to low (30.1%) in Aleppo.Exclusive breastfeeding rates were far below the ≥80% target in both Aleppo (27.8%) and DEZ (13.6%). and only 24.9% and 28.0% of the children met the minimum dietary diversity in Aleppo and DEZ respectively. IPV is planning to use the minimum service package defined by the nutrition WoS cluster, which will be addressed through 4 mobile units in Tal-Tamer subdistrict in Alhasakeh governorate, and Hajin subdistrict in DEZ, and 2 PHC centers which will have embedded nutrition activities in Hajin and Susat subdistrict, the teams will provide screening and treatment for both CU5 and PLWs. IPV will run OTP and TSFP programs in both PHCs, to treat MAM cases for CU5 and PLWs, and SAM without complication for CU5, and will use the NES CMAM guidline. Furthermore, micronutrients (multivitamins) will be provided for PLWs and adolescent girls in the selected locations.IYCF practices will also be enhanced, including community mobilization, IEC material distribution, one-to-one and group sessions, and proper counseling when needed, focusing on early initiation of breastfeeding, exclusive breastfeeding till 6 months, continuity breastfeeding till age of 2, and introduction of varied solid food at age of 6 months.Protection Risk Analysis: 1)The risk from SEA. Mitigation: -Community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance, and awareness-raising on PSEA amongst personnel, Provide information to communities about the NGO principles to which NGO adheres to. 2. GBV risk. Mitigation: health outreach services are the first to contact GBV survivors seeking assistance, and survivors' safe access to facilities. Train the staff on PSS and support the presence of female staff in all teams. 3)risk of violence against Healthcare workers. Mitigation: Set up centers in safe and accessible locations, Relocate centers in relatively safe areas, Reporting on attacks against healthcare. Addressing PSEA: Through PSEA training, Code of Conduct, reporting channels, community awareness, and ensures staff training and commitment to safeguarding women and children.
