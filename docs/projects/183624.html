Project Summary

The proposed response aims to alleviate poverty and food insecurity for the most marginalized farming households in Area C and focused on vulnerable Farming HHs with demonstrated protection and socio-economic vulnerabilities of 43 communities from 3 governorates (Qalqilya, Bethlehem and Hebron),with a particular focus on herders and livestock bearers.
The intervention is in line with strategic objective three (SOP3, SO2, and SO1), in addition to the FSS O1, O2 and O3 through its integrated approach to strengthen resilience of agricultural based livelihood by addressing community scale response components in conjunction with individual support of vulnerable farming households to deal with protracted livelihood and protection threats. Further the intervention has focused emergency assistance for FSS 2021 identified vulnerable herding southern WB communities at risk of livelihoods loss due to the settlement expansion plans and settler violence. The intervention will achieve the aforementioned through three main components that respond directly to the identified 2022 FSS priorities of
i) Rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs for farmers and herders including promotion of technical capacities using the BBB (Build-Back Better) approach for increased resilience. For HH level assistance through a CFW modality of labor and tailoring of assistance in particular for Female headed HHs.
ii)Increased access to Agricultural Resources through Rehabilitation of community agricultural infrastructure
iii) Building the Capacity of community members to deal and mitigate with protection risk ( Gender, Safeguarding- including SEA and protection), and address protection concerns of farmers prone to settler violence and land assets confiscation through legal aid support including a) provision of legal aid awareness sessions b) technical legal aid support in preparation of legal files and coordinated referral to legal aid
institutions Further the proposed response will ii) contribute to increased accountability for violations of IHL and IHRL through OXFAM by a) production of briefing note and facilitate advocacy campaigns including to advocate to take action to stop recurrent IHL and IHRL violations in coordination with HCT AWG, OCHA, AIDA and any other relevant stakeholders.
Overall the project will:
• Rehabilitation of 750 dunnums of lands, 70 GHs,15 km of agricultural roads, 20 water wells, 10 km of water carrier lines, 70 livestock sheds, Rehabilitation/ provision of 20 water storage
• Provision of diary production equipment for 70 HHs
• Provision of Emergency fodder distribution for 316 HHs for 2 months and distribution of feed crops seeds
• Conduct 35 technical trainings on Crop management and 15 trainings and livestock health management to build technical capacities of vulnerable agricultural HHs to deal with natural and manmade shocks to their livelihoods through the MoA Directorates technical staff.
• Conduct 35 technical trainings for females on farming management, quality control, market requirements and packaging to enhance their knowledge of the market
• Conduct 15 technical trainings to enhance women households’ knowledge in food processing and conservation, in cooperation with the Rural Development and Women Department-Ministry of Agriculture.
• Assisting needy and vulnerable households through cash transfers, including cash-for work for Beneficiaries benefiting from inputs for land rehab.
• Provide 50 legal awareness and legal support to target communities, in coordination with relevant stakeholders and prepare 400 legal files for farmers.
• Provide awareness and capacity building sessions to the Community Based Project Support Committees CBPSCs on PSEA including gender protection and safeguarding.
MA’AN, LRC and Oxfam have designed and proposed the above intervention to complement the current and foreseen work of partners and other FS aid actors in their interventions at the HH and community Levels.

1 - Needs

In September-October 2021 MA’AN Development Center conducted an overall needs assessment as part of the design of its Humanitarian Strategy in Area C, within which the presentproposal is framed. The needs assessment includes updated statistics from the Palestinian Authority (PA) Ministry of Agriculture, updated data from the DoAs, and collected data from conducted focus groups with the local communities and CBOs in addition to updates and additional information based on MA’AN’s direct engagement in the Food Security Sector. The assessment was further informed by MA’AN during the implementation the project funded by the Canadian Government in 2020-2022, which targets 24 locations within 6 governorates in the West Bank in addition to a rapid assessment conducted by MA’AN & partners where 75 localities in the governorates (14 Qalqilya, 6 Bethlehem, 55 Hebron and eastern slopes) FSS vulnerability . The need assessment included interviews, meetings, field visits and focus groups with beneficiaries, stakeholders and the FSS, The response builds on current interventions funded by the Canadian Government and implemented by MA’AN which aimed to alleviate poverty and food insecurity for the most marginalized farming households in Area C and focused on vulnerable Farming HHs with demonstrated protection and socio-economic vulnerabilities, with a particular focus on herders and livestock bearers.As of September 2021, 233 agriculture structure were demolished which represent more than 34% of the total structures demolished. In fact, vulnerable communities (Communities and Bedouins living in area C) are faced with limited access to land and water sources due to the Israeli restrictions and control over the water resources, settler violence and Israeli forces excessive use of force and lacking building permits, restrictions on access to farming and grazing land. As well as, trees uprooting, field burning, livestock poisoning or killing, waste and grey water from settlements discharged on Palestinian agricultural fields, demolition of agricultural structures and assets by settlers and IF and extreme weather conditions. Moreover, vulnerable households are suffering from poor access to nutritious and sufficient food and Lacking of economic access and coping capacities. Palestinian Bedouin and herding communities are disproportionately affected due to their location in sensitive areas and their reliance of agriculture-based livelihoods, as their sole or main source of income. Forced displacement as a result of a home demolition or the recurrent loss of assets has devastating socio-economic consequences, including increased food insecurity and dependence on humanitarian assistance). Upon a recent assessment conducted July 2021 by the FSS cluster for urgent needs of Herding communities, there are 15 communities in Hebron and Bethlehem governorates mostly impacted by settlers’ violence. Based on the FSS cluster’s recommendation, there is an urgent need for emergency fodder and seeds crops distribution as a humanitarian assistance in to support these communities.The needs of Vulnerable farming households in area C communities remain vast, and disproportionately addressed by aid actors Further,Quantitative data from the assessment reveal that there is need for support to farmers for rehabilitation of 1000 donums belonging to 500 HHs in 6 communities from the Qalqilya governorate with irrigation networks for sustained productivity of Trees ( not covered through HRP), and need for inputs of vulnerable farmers to sustain livelihoods for 1230 donums belonging to 992 vulnerable farming HHs (300 from 4 Qalqilya localities , 230 for 6 Bethlehem localities, 700 in 17 Hebron localities) with this regard it was found that farmers in vulnerable localities of Beit ummar, halhul, khilet al dar and qalqus could greatly benefit from provision of water storage for agricultural purposes to bypass reduced access to water. It was prevalent that Eastern slopes Bedouin farmers relying on livestock as main source of livelihood are in need of urgent assistance with rehabilitation of sheds, food processing equipment, the assessment identified 236 farming HHs from the Hebron governorate and Eastern slopes in need of assistance to enhance living conditions of livestock and technical know-how on livestock management. Further consultations with MoA directorates and farmers regarding community agricultural services revealed the need for rehabilitation of 31 km of agricultural roads (28 in 8 Hebron localities, 10 in 4 Qalqilya localities) to enhance access of vulnerable farmers to their lands. Qualitative findings of the assessment confirm the main cause of food insecurity in the oPt is limited economic access to food, which is driven by unemployment and poverty.The restraints in sustenance of livelihoods and needs include:Constrained productive capacities due to damages of vulnerable farmers Agricultural assets resulting from settler violence and other occupation regime and extreme weather conditions; • Shortage of water for agricultural purposes; • Lack of farmers’ access to agricultural lands due to inadequate agricultural roads and/or presence of land in “no-go” zones; • Further the COVID 19 pandemic caused significant increase in food and inputs prices,placing significant stress on all dimensions of the food system. • Disruption of producers access to basic agricultural inputs with foreseeable adverse impacts on agricultural productions. Several farming HHs have suffered from shortages and limited access to, competitiveness with Israelis and increased prices of agricultural inputs, particularly fodder • Poor knowledge of the market and deficiency in market information and requirements especially women on Bedouin communities who are in charge of certain processes.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Some communities targeted by the project are very risky locations (surrounded by settlements or under ICA threat of demolition), therefore MA’AN , in alignment with the “Do No Harm” Principles for humanitarian assistance depends approaches to reduce risks to beneficiaries as a result of intervention. MA’AN adopts key principles that serveto reduce unintended negative impact of the project; these principles include: Effective feedback mechanism:, strong linkages with beneficiary communities, with a feedback mechanism that has allowed targeted communities to participate actively in the design of the project, and will further allow them to participate in the beneficiary selection process, and determining needs per each beneficiary. The project staff will further hold regular meetings with villages’ representatives and will update them about the progress and workplan of the project. Further, MA’AN will implement the project in a transparent manner where beneficiary list will be made public and reason for any person not being selected will be stated. The community will have a period to object to the list. MA’AN will ensure that the assistance is provided in times where control is limited (at night, during week-ends) in coordinated times with the community committee and beneficiary to assess the security situation of the community. MA’AN mobilizes/employs local coordinators for its projects in Area C to mitigate access risk in locations where security incidents are likely to occur. And when possible in alignment with its procurement policy and guidelines procures from local sources.MA’AN has been working for many years in the target , including for support to livelihoods, and have a good relationship with the communities, so they will be made aware of any problem with ICA or IDF in the communities in real time. Aligning with the“Do No Harm” Principle, the implementation team will ensure all planned rehabilitation work by the beneficiaries through and project is completed with project closeout. In instances where HHs are unable to effectively conduct the land, GHs and sheds rehabilitation, MA’ANs technical team will directly supervise the rehabilitation work for disadvantaged households (female headed households , persons with disabilities …etc).MA’AN will conduct risks analysis aiming at identifying and mitigating possible risks including PSEA associated with the action. the risk identification will be done through assessment and community consultations(FGDs).further MA’AN employs female MEAL staff as focal points for the CRM which also handles PSEA. The MEAL officer contact info along with PSEA networks will be present on all project outreach materials. The program activities include gender and safeguarding including PSEA trainings for community members for clear PSEA messaging and awareness. On institutional level MA’AN and all it partners are required to be trained on PSEA policy, Staff code of Conduct and Child protection policy.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The intervention is designed to promote and help protect and the rights of people who have historically been marginalized or discriminated against, such as women, children, the elderly and PWDs practicing agriculture and herding in Communities in area C. For target HHs, the selection criteria is designed to be transparent and ensure Equal and more importantly equitable access to aid. The criteria will focus on the socio economic conditions of the households as well as on the vulnerability of the land and rehabilitation needs and the gender make up of the household. As women are responsible for the household in Palestinian society, the number of females in thehousehold will also be an important part of the application process. As women-headed households are disproportionately affected by poverty. The needs of both genders are addressed equally. The entire family, regardless of sex, age, and disabilities will equally benefit from the intervention. As a positive measure towards women, the project teamwill take all necessary measures to assure equal access of women farmers to benefit from this project. These measures will include but are not limited to ensuring women participation in the CBPSCs, project orientation session, and monitoring activities. All beneficiaries will be treated with dignity and respect. Considerations to the needs of the elderly and PWDs are taken and will ensure that special arrangements are made to rehabilitate or construct green houses and sheds to them. At the start of the project, the project will hold an orientation workshop in each location to explain the project and tentative time frame.In order to involve the affected population in the project implementation. Community Based Project Support Committees (CBPSCs) will be formulated to assist in the project implementation and to ensure accountability and transparency with beneficiaries as it is part of MA'ANs community integration and ownership approach to community development. The (CBPSCs) will consist of members of the targeted communities, ,Municipality representatives, representative from the Directorate of Agriculture –DoA and representatives from local CBOs and the project team. Women and people with disabilities will be encouraged to be part of the committee. The committee will assist in the beneficiary selection according to the set criteria and provide information to the project management as needed on any special cases or circumstances. The (CBPSCs) will supervise the public announcement of the project among the inhabitants of the targeted communities. Representatives of the communities will participate in the application process. Women of these communities will play an integral part in theselection of beneficiaries through participation in the application selection process. A comprehensive analysis of the candidates will be conducted to select final beneficiaries of the land rehabilitation components. (CBPSCs) and beneficiaries will beencouraged to provide feedback about the process of application, selecting beneficiaries by contacting the field coordinators and/or the project manager. All suggestions and complaints will be taken seriously and needed action will be taken accordingly. Furthermore, the (CBPSCs) will also be involved in monitoring the project through conducting regular visits with the project staff, monitoring materials delivery, and conveying feedback on progress of rehabilitation to the project team.
