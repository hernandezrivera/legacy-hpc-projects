Project Summary

The objective of the project is to meet the basic life-saving ES/NFI needs of affected populations due to conflict, violence or natural disasters within South Sudan. As part of an emergency response, the project can take place in any location at short notice. The target is to reach 45,000 individuals who are deemed the most vulnerable using both the cluster’s and Medair’s vulnerability criteria. Medair will target newly displaced persons, protracted displaced persons, returnees and vulnerable host communities. Within these groups, the most vulnerable will be defined as those sharing limited resources, over-burdened households (especially those contributing to COVID-19 transmission), people with disability, female-headed households, pregnant and lactating females. Medair places Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) and protection mainstreaming at the core of all programming to ensure meaningful access to services and to protect the safety and dignity of beneficiaries.
With continued violence throughout South Sudan, particularly at the sub-national level, Medair continues to see many people displaced and most having left their homes with no items. In some cases, homes have been looted and burned down. IDPs must then share resources with already vulnerable host households. This means multiple families will share one saucepan, one mosquito net, one blanket, etc. Medair aims to address this by responding to sudden onset displacement due to violence. Additionally, Medair responds to natural disasters, such as abnormal flooding, that adversely affect both IDPs and host communities resulting in the need for ES/NFI.
Medair will complete an effective analysis of the population’s needs using the cluster’s analysis tools. An analysis (using tools such as the stakeholder analysis, a protection risk analysis, research, observation, and Medair field team’s findings) takes place to then determine if an intervention is appropriate and what interventions are possible. The communities are then involved in discussions to help determine an intervention that is appropriate for their needs alongside Medair teams. Medair's ERT team is multi-sectoral and can supplement an ES/NFI intervention with WASH, Protection, Nutrition, and/or Health when necessary. (TRC comment #3).
Once an intervention is confirmed Medair works closely with the ES/NFI cluster and IOM to receive items needed for the distribution in a timely manner for beneficiaries. Medair sets up a community distribution committee made up of representative community members to discuss the appropriate and safe way to distribute to allow meaningful access. Guidelines are given on the selection criteria and this is disseminated to communities. The teams are available to answer questions on this. A safety audit is completed to make sure the distribution site is safe and accessible.
Throughout the distribution the Medair team employs representative casual labourers to ensure a clear and well-organised feedback mechanism that swiftly resolves any issues the same day. Following the distribution, the Medair team will complete exit interviews so they remain accountable to the beneficiaries, they can deal with any urgent issues whilst in the field, and they can identify any furthers gaps or needs to inform internal lessons learnt. If possible, Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be completed between four weeks and three months to fully assess the impact of the intervention, to learn if the community felt their most basic needs have been met, and to determine if this was completed in a safe and dignified manner.
So that Medair programmes remain accountable and transparent to all involved, the Medair team will then report to the cluster, donors and sometimes communities on findings from the intervention. If further needs have been identified that Medair are unable to respond to, then the team will share those needs with other partners and the cluster.

1 - Needs

Despite improved security, there is still significant risk that populations will continue to be displaced by armed groups, cattle-raiders, inter-communal fighting and natural disasters, specifically flooding. It is likely that the humanitarian situation will deteriorate over the next year as a result of the repercussions of the formation of the new government, a result of seven years of conflict and a continued reduction in access to basic services. Emergency Shelter and NFI assistance is still urgently needed to serve those who have fled their homes and left their belongings behind. An estimated 1.6 million people are currently displaced (HNO South Sudan, January 2021) and most are in desperate need of some form of ES/NFI assistance. The ES/NFI Cluster estimates 2,100,000 individuals currently need ES/NFI assistance within South Sudan (Draft HRP cluster response plan, February 2021). Medair teams continue to find multiple families sharing very limited and very basic resources with vulnerable hosts, IDPs and returnees who continue to flee violence and flooding with nothing. Looting and the burning of houses have caused the destruction of belongings, while for others, items have been washed away by flood waters. In particular, IDPs outside camps require basic household items, especially those who have relocated several times. In Medair’s response to natural disasters, such as abnormal flooding, and the COVID-19 pandemic that adversely affected both IDPs and host communities resulting in the need for ES/NFI, needs are once again seen. Generally, those who are newly displaced will have priority over protracted displaced populations. Women and children will remain particularly vulnerable to protection risks due to lack of shelter and in some instances host community members will also need support due to pressure on their limited resources to help IDPs. Men remain at risk of violence in some areas where they can be targets, and child abductions are an on-going concern. Since the signing of the R-ARCSS peace agreement in September 2018, there has being an increase in refugee and IDP returnees throughout South Sudan. According to round nine of the IOM DTM, 258,316 total returnees were recorded in the first nine months of 2020. More returnees are expected due to the establishment of the new government in 2020. Many IDPs, refugees, and returnees need ES/NFI support. Medair aims to address the needs of the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees particularly pregnant and lactating women, female-headed households, the elderly, and the disabled. In this specific context, Medair responds to recently displaced populations fleeing conflict. If warranted, Medair will also support some of the most vulnerable host community members in order to build their resilience and ease community tensions. This aligns with the cluster response for 2021 where targeting includes prioritising people unable to support themselves with life-saving shelter and non-food items (Draft HRP cluster response plan, February 2021). The goal is to support those in need by giving them life-saving ES/NFI items, thus supporting people without eroding their existing coping mechanisms. Medair will also be prioritising more multi-sectoral, multi-agency responses promoting a holistic approach to emergency response. These will target multiple lifesaving needs within interventions in coordination with partners and the cluster hubs, which meets the current cluster HRP plan for 2021.

2 - Project activities

Carry out ES/NFI needs analysis upon receiving cluster alerts • Respond with appropriate modality (flood kit, quick intervention, planned intervention, integrated response) providing ES/NFI items and training to meet the life-saving needs of communities. • Conduct PDMs, exit interviews and positive/negative feedback registration to ensure AAP. Implement feedback mechanisms before, during/after distribution to consider affected population’s input. • Coordinate with other partners by attending national- and state-level cluster meetings, networking with other agencies, and interacting with other agency partners on the ground. • Localize every intervention by partnering with local NGOs or coordinating a handover to local partners. Coordinate with Humanitarian Hubs in an emergency to allow for an efficient, timely response, reduce the likelihood of activity duplication, effectively use prepositioned supplies and contribute toward general coordination. • Recognise that the increase in returnees and ongoing high numbers of IDPs need an area-based approach covering multiple sectors to meet all the basic needs of a population. To understand the complex needs in a changing context, more time should be spent within a community and more collaboration with partners/specialists. Medair will spend as much time as possible with communities to ensure a full needs assessment has been completed, effective AAP is being implemented and the mechanisms are being used. With returnees and IDPs facing multiple needs, Medair will coordinate with other partners for a holistic approach to address multiple needs. Medair will engage HLP and protection specialists as necessary and coordinate with other actors to ensure no activity duplication. Medair ERT will coordinate with its sectors (WASH, Protection, Nutrition, Health) to deliver a multi-sectoral response where needed and possible. • Participate in the Strategic Advisory Group (SAG) if selected. • Mainstream Protection: “do no harm” analysis, ensure most vulnerable people have priority access to distributions, and serve the population according to Medair values and Code of Ethics, incorporating accepted AAP good practice, train staff in protection mainstreaming, utilize cluster protection tools during FDGs, KIIs, and enumerator training, etc. • Consider gender inclusion throughout project implementation through gender analysis in needs assessments, gender-sensitive activities (hire women enumerators/casual labourers, female community leaders as key informants) and gender-sensitive outcomes. • State Focal Point for Central Equatoria State: proactively visit field sites across state to understand the specific needs; network to build up key contacts and relationships across the state; organise monthly state coordination meetings; build partner capacity on emergency responses; engage and build relationships with key RRC/ROSS officials. • Monitor IDP and returnee movement in initial needs analysis to understand where IDPs plan to settle (helps determine response plan). Monitor returnee, both refugee and IDP, movement motivations (if they plan to stay) and help identify the number of people returning/planning to return. This information is shared with the cluster. • Contribute to community resilience through providing lifesaving NFIs, employing local casual labor, training enumerators to use digital data collection tools, and training beneficiaries to use NFIs or build shelters.

3 - Project indicators

In an effort to ensure robustness in process, Medair has a separate monitoring, evaluation and learning team which is responsible for measuring projects’ success using standard and custom monitoring tools. The SNFI team also plays a pivotal role in making sure all the critical project indicators are accurately traced, compiled, and reported to the respective donors, clusters and other stakeholders. At the conclusion of every intervention, the SNFI team will undertake exit interviews and when possible and feasible, a PDM will be carried out four weeks to three months after the intervention. The data obtained will be collected and analysed on a regular basis and reported to all stakeholders. Medair also will continue to keep all project related data in the project data sheet for further reference and future programming. SNFI distributions will be conducted after carefully registering beneficiaries on a house-to-house basis and names inputted on registration forms. The M&E team will continue to ensure all the collected data is accurate and without double counting and evaluates the project against the indicators. If gaps are identified and the project is not on track, corrective measures will be taken, and the project will be re-aligned. Of importance to mention, in order to protect the health of the community and staff and to curtail community transmission of COVID-19, these surveys will be conducted depending on the COVID-19 situation in South Sudan.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All Medair staff receive regular training in Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Medair staff remain vigilant in the field to provide casual labourers and beneficiaries at risk of exploitation the feedback mechanism to report any incidents. Enumerators are also trained in Protection Principles and Medair’s Code of Ethics before they start working

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The team will consult with the community to identify the most vulnerable in collaboration with specialist protection partners. Special assistance will be given to those who need it. The Medair team ensures there is a clear and well-organised feedback mechanism so that any issues or suggestions are considered and quickly resolved during the project implementation. While opportunities for written feedback are provided, beneficiaries can also provide verbal feedback to be recorded by enumerators. Additionally, Medair actively seek out vulnerable members for inclusion in the assessment FGDs and KIIs. Exit interviews will be conducted with the most vulnerable and their care givers. The Medair team also seeks out any at-risk individuals who are not connected or are outside the local communities to receive their feedback. Medair aim to work with protection partners that can also help identify any particularly vulnerable households that may otherwise be overlooked.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The Medair team closely consults with local authorities and the community through a thorough assessment that identifies beneficiaries’ preferred communication methods in addition to informing them about the project’s goals. Additionally, feedback mechanisms are provided throughout the intervention by means of FDGs, KIIs, a feedback desk, exit interviews and PDMs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The analysis, interventions and PDMs include input from local authorities, community leaders and elders, women and youth groups (teens, not children), church leaders, people with disabilities, and other NGOs. IDPs, returnees, and hosts are all consulted so different perspectives of the context are explored and understood by the field team as are the views of different local tribes. Prior to the distribution the Medair team make themselves available and accessible for any feedback the community wishes to give. If feedback indicates that a multi-sectoral response is required, Medair's ERT also cover WASH, Protection, Nutrition, and Health sectors. (TRC comment #3)

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Four most relevant:• Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and servicesMedair complete a protection risk/mitigation analysis and safety audit to assess any risks for beneficiaries attending activities. Medair then mitigates against these risks and where needed will act to make sure beneficiaries’ safety is a priority at all times. An example would be, where distance to humanitarian assistance is identified as a risk, Medair aims to mitigate this by establishing several distribution sites that will require less travel time to arrive at for beneficiaries. • Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and Older persons, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activitiesMedair actively seeks out marginalised groups, with the aid of local authorities and community leaders, to participate in every stage of the project. Their voices are included in the assessment and subsequent project design. Marginalised groups are also prioritised during verifications and registrations. When needed, extra measures are employed to ease the participation burden to marginalised groups such as older persons and those with disabilities. Additionally, women are actively recruited for casual labour roles used to implement the project. • Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resourcesThere are many external stakeholders that try to influence beneficiary selection for targeted distributions. The Medair team work hard to triangulate information received through multiple sources, and field teams verify the information in person before intervening. This reduces either missing the most vulnerable households or favouring one community/tribe over another which can exacerbate tensions and lead to conflict. Medair teams will always complete a stakeholder analysis before entering the field and will adjust on the ground as necessary. Medair also runs trainings in humanitarian principles where necessary and have clear selection criteria that are communicated to stakeholders. • Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project areaMedair continues to monitor land disputes and specifically asks questions on this in the analysis survey and FGDs to fully understand any HLP issues when assessing an area. If any major concerns are reported, then Medair would seek appropriate advice from partners who specialise in HLP. Additionally, the Medair team seek to mitigate beneficiaries' HLP risk by involving the host community and its leadership, state ministry of land and housing, and properly assessing the area to gain an understanding of traditional and cultural influence on HLP issues. (TRC comment #1)
