Project Summary

According to the 2020 post Gu technical release, over 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face crisis or worse through December. In riverine livelihoods of southern Somalia, devastating floods during the 2020 Gu season have destroyed farm land, planted crops, and population displacement. Moderate to heavy precipitations in the upper catchment of the Juba and Shebelle rivers in the eastern Ethiopian highlands and intensified rainfall within Somalia since mid-April have caused riverine flooding in Bardhere of Gedo region and some parts of Middle and Lower Ju-ba. This has further led to significant declines in crop harvest as well as loss of agricultural employment opportunities. As a consequence, a significant proportion of poor households in riverine livelihoods face moderate to large food consumption gaps both during the current and projection periods.
Additionally, in Lower Juba and Gedo regions that are low Potential Agropastoral livelihood zones, the loss of income and food stocks due to the desert locust upsurge is similarly expected to lead to food consumption gaps, as poor households in this livelihood zones have few livestock assets or other income sources to rely upon. The number of rural IDPs in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or Emergency (IPC 4) is also expected to radically increase. Most rural IDPs are pastoralists or agro-pastoralists that have become destitute and are living in rural, host community villages where they can access social/kinship support (including food and milk gifts) and access labor employment.
HAPEN will target these vulnerable households in L.Juba and Gedo. The project will: 1.provide temporary access to cash through conditional and unconditional transfer for a period of 3 months to improve access to the basic needs of the vulnerable households. HAPEN will undertake a baseline survey to identify the most vulnerable households who cannot be engaged in works (disabled, the old, pregnant women and/or lactating) to be enrolled for the unconditional cash transfer. The households benefitting through conditional transfers will be engaged through cash for work activities, with the objective of providing short term income support while improving communal assets. Communal infrastructures (Water catchments and canals) will be identified with the help of the local communities for rehabilitation/clearing. The cash will be delivered through the voucher systems. 2. Additionally the project will also aim at protecting and sustaining livelihoods through the provision of inputs (seeds, tools and training) for agriculture farming.
Community-based targeting will be conducted and eligible households identified and registered. Different age groups will be taken into account and specific vulnerabilities linked to the life cycle will be included in the vulnerability criteria.

Project Activities

1. Unconditional (300 HHs) and conditional cash transfers (1,000 HHs) activities targeting the vulnerable households in Gedo and Lower Juba for a period of 3 months.-Community awareness will be undertaken in the villages to inform on the project and how activities will be undertaken-Beneficiary profiling and registration in the target locations in consultation with the community elders-The criteria for targeting the beneficiaries will ensure that the most vulnerable are not left out. Emphasis for unconditional transfers will be given to the old, disabled and women headed household. -For CFW activities, the communal infrastructures selected for rehabilitation will be discussed prior to selection. Households that are vulnerable but are in a position to work will be selected, as well as households that have able bodied persons to work in the project. Household will be engaged on a weekly basis (6 days a week) and paid at the end of each week. -The transfer amount will be established based on the MEB at the time of implementation. It is expected that this values will range between 80-100% of the MEB.-Happen has implemented cash transfer activities before through the mobile money system in coordination with the Hawala. The use of mobile money transfer was more secure, effective and faster, however for the households that do not own a phone or will have no access to a phone, voucher system will be implemented. 2. Distribution of agriculture inputs and training 3,000 farmer households.-This activity will mainly support the poor agropastoral households to aid them recover their livelihoods. The beneficiaries will receive a package of agricultural seeds and tools. These kits will be aligned to the recommended packages outlined in the guidance note on the provision of agricultural inputs in Somalia provided by the Food Security Cluster. Happen will identify traders who can supply the inputs to the villages. In order to increase accountability, vouchers will be used to redeem the inputs. Training on post harvest and handling of grains will further be undertaken by extension officers.Monitoring of the activities will be frequently undertaken every 2-3 weeks to determine the impact of the cash transfers to the beneficiaries.

Project Indicators

-Improved access to food that will be measured through the number of target population with acceptable food consumption score, and these are the households mainly benefitting from the cash transfers (UCT-300 and CFW-1,000HHs)-Average score on beneficiaries household dietary diversity. This will start from the Baseline undertaken for approximately 200 HHs to determine the household dietary diversity score, then through the PDM to determine the expenditure patterns (what have households spent on food) and lastly mid and endline dietary diversity score.-For activity 2, Hapen will monitor the no of households assisted with agriculture inputs (3,000HHs) to identify the % of farmers who continuously cultivated their farms and the % of the yield increase in the subsequent season

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of cash to support vulnerable households gives them the choice to determine how to utilize the cash. Cash programmes are more feasible and can easily be implemented during a crisis to reach beneficiaries on time as compared to in kind. Markets in the target areas have remained functional.

Mitigation Measures

1.Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services. Hapen will ensure that the registration and distribution activities are undertaken at the field level where there is ease of mobility for all. During the awareness creation, communities will be sensitized on the complaint mechanism to ensure set standards of accountability are followed in order to meet the policy of zero tolerance. Where possible all cash payments will be undertaken through mobile transfers, in cases of use of vouchers, ensure that the hawala used can reached the beneficiaries at village level to avoid long distance travel 2. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities Hapen has an established vulnerability criteria methodology that will be utilized to ensure that all vulnerable groups are identified and targeted. Community awareness/campaign activities will be undertaken to ensure inclusion of the marginalized groups among the women so that they understand their rights in the project. The selected project committees will be representative of all groups within the community (ie. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) in order to ensure that they are diverse and not biased. Hapen will closely work with community elders to ensure that infrastructure selected for rehabilitation are strategically located to reduce mobility especially for women. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms Hapen will organize awareness raising sessions so that people understand the means their can use to channel their complains and feedback. A mechanism for submitting feedback will be established. This will not require beneficiaries exposing themselves to project staff. Respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. Mechanisms for collecting feedback should use a combination of channels. This will include active channels, where client perspectives are deliberately and systematically solicited (such as focus groups), reactive channels, where clients have more control over when and how they share perspectives (such as complaint lines) and open channels where feedback will be solicited in an ad hoc manner, through ongoing interaction during implementation. Consideration for a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors will also be undertaken in order to minimize confusion especially when one community is targeted by different partners.
