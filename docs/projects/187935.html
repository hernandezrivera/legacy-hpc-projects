Project Summary

The proposed project will contribute to achieve the SO1: To Scale-up and sustain minimum food consumption needs through lifesaving and life-sustaining food assistance through 2 phases, at each phase of project it will swiftly address food insecurity of 30,000 households (180,000 individuals), 25,000 households will be supported via three rounds of food distribution, in addition to distribute a bread bundles with a weight of 800 gr on daily basis to 5,000 HHs (30000 individuals) whom living in the camps in North of Syria for 6 months/6 days a week, each person will receive 250g of bread daily /based on the daily survival needs (250 g bread/person/day), and since the size of the families is different, we will identify the portion per family per day based on the number of the family’s members, every three members will receive a bundle with 800-gram weight, The estimated value of the bread to be distributed is 1170 Ton (30000 person * 250 g * 156 day)
After informing details of food distribution 3-7 days prior to the event, 25,000 households will receive food basket for 3 months through small and multiple distribution points at each area to avoid a high number of beneficiaries at the same place.
Ensuring the beneficiaries feedbacks and recommendations to inform the project implementation, feedback and complaint mechanisms will be circulated through printing on bread bags and leaflets distributing with food baskets, in addition to PSEA leaflets.
Through setting a selection criteria based on vulnerability analysis, the most vulnerable groups among the targeted communities will be supported and they can reduce their likeliness of engaging negative coping mechanism, and their life-risks related to food shortage to be mitigated which will be verified through post distribution monitoring activities.
The project is considered as a planned and will target two locations through two phases:
1- Aleppo governorate during 2022.
2- Idleb governorate during 2023.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, 14.6 million people need some form of food and agriculture assistance, assessment, this includes at least 13.2 million people are estimated to be food insecure, where protracted crisis in Syria has intensified needs over the past year through a complex and deepening socio-economic crisis, degradation of livelihoods, knock-on effects of COVID- 19, the ongoing conflict in the North of the country and major losses in the food production sector (due to conflict and climate change).prices of bread have also increased several times for a bundle of bread and only a small percentage of the population’s minimum bread needs were met.These have combined to severely compromise the national food security situation, where stated that the crisis brought devastating impact on the infrastructure including irrigation systems, accessing to the core agricultural inputs, and the economic effects on farmers' income. Many people were obliged to discontinue agricultural production, including crop and livestock production, because they did not have the means to afford through the crisis, despite the deterioration in farming activities across the country, agriculture still represents an important source of income and food in all assessed areas.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will be implemented directly by IAC staff, at each phase of the project will provide 25,000 HHs from the IDPs and Vulnerable Returnees with food baskets for 3 months, IAC will conduct the distribution through many small distribution points at each targeted area. Also IAC team will distribute wheat bread to 5000 HHs from the IDPs whom living in the camps on daily basis for 6 days a week along of 6 months of distribution.The main activities are: 1. Field team will start to collect the beneficiaries list. 2. Logistic team will announce for 3 public tender for procurement of Food baskets, and the wheat flour, and the 3rd one will be to bake the bread inside Syria.3. While the head office in Turkey is working on the procurement of the food baskets and wheat flour the field team will conduct home visits for verification of the beneficiaries. 4. Field team will conduct kick-off workshops with all of local councils and local authorities and camps managements in targeted areas to sign MOUs. 5. Distribution team will conduct the distribution of the food baskets for the selected beneficiaries for three rounds, and will conduct the daily bread distribution in the selected camps.6. Field MEAL team will conduct the PDM after each round of food baskets distribution and on monthly basis for the bread's one to measure the beneficiary's satisfaction regarding the quality of the distributed food baskets, the field team behaviors and the timing of the response.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

To prevent any PSEA issue from occurring during the implementation period of the project activities, especially the activities in which the project staff are in direct contact with the beneficiaries, such as the distribution team. All field activities have been reviewed and assessed for risk factors for PSEA with the use of the PSEA Network Field and Organizational Mitigation and Prevention Checklists and the following prevention and mitigation measures will be taken: 1- IAC will make sure that All staff, and volunteers understand and sign the Code of Conduct. 2- Field staff with direct contact with beneficiaries will receive PSEA training 3- IAC will review internal reporting mechanisms and ensure all staff know about reporting mechanisms 4- female staff will always be present at the distribution site and involved in the distribution process 5-Benefeciaries will be given clear information on timing and content of distribution 6- Beneficiaries will be given information that aid is free7- PSEA messaging and awareness raising will be done though pasting PSEA posters and placing PSEA banners at the distribution site or other service place.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:- Access to specific Sub-districts remains open.- The Food baskets arrived to our warehouses on time.- No drastic changes on the Bab-Alhawa border regulations. Risks:Risk 1: escalation the airstrikes in targeted areas. Mitigation 1: Conducting the distribution via many micro distribution points meaning don't have any high number of collected people in the same place in addition to cancel the day of distribution when mentioning any fighter jet hovering in the sky. Risk 2: Turkish Government decided to close the Bab Alhawa border for urgent issue. Mitigation 2: 2.1. put this issue on the contract with the supplier and put a condition that after signing the contract if Bab-Alhawa closed for any issue, the supplier will provide the same quantities of agreed items in Side Syria with the same specification from local resources. 2.2. Immediate contracting with multi suppliers inside Syria to provide the same food items with the same quantities. Risk 3: Delay from the supplier on delivering the Food Baskets to warehouse.Mitigation 3: Put a high penalty for the delay in delivering on the supplier in the signedcontract, so the supplier will be more focus on his commitment with the delivery date.
