Project Summary

19,500 people affected by armed conflict have protected and reliable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene facilities and a safe and healthy environment, contributing to reducing the risk of transmission for WASH related diseases.
FHA will directly address water, hygiene and sanitation issues in the targeted resettlement centers and host communities through the provision of inclusive and gender sensitive, life-saving water; sanitation; and hygiene services, items, and facilities for vulnerable populations. The project will rehabilitate inclusive water access points in the target areas. Per eadh rehabilitated water point, a WASH committee will be established / revitalized and trained for effective and sustainable management of the water points. Targeted households with also be supported with dome slabs for self-construction of latrines. Construction of new water points will be supported through coordination with UNICEF and SDPI. Point of use water treatment products (certeza) will be promoted and distributed in cholera prone communities to ensure safe drinking water at HH level. FHA will also promote good water conservation mechanisms such as use of buckets with lids and discouraging local practices such as putting tree leaves in water buckets which potentially contaminates drinking water. FHA will promote proper hygiene and sanitation practices through social behavior change communication. Community based volunteers / health promoters will be trained and support for continued sensitisation and promotion of hygiene and sanitation practices at household level.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Water supplyFH will ensure access to sustainable clean water at IDP centers and host communities in the district of Metuge in Cabo Delgado and the district of Rapale in Nampula province. The project target will rehabilitate 10 non-functioning water points to provide immediate and relief water access to at least 5,000 people in the targeted communities. In communities where there are gaps in water supply, FH will coordinate with Unicef and SDPI and propose for contruction of new water points. For each water point constructed / rehabilitated, a WASH committee will be established and trained in compliance with the National water policy to ensure effective management of the WASH facilities. Hygiene Promotion - 19,500 peopleThe project will disseminate key hygiene messages through community sensitization meetings / sessions that will be held in small gatherings of between 15 to 25 people. Sensitization for hygiene promotion will also be conducted at public places such as markets, water points, health facilities and schools using megaphones and or printed IEC material. Menstrual Hygiene Management will be promoted targeting women at active reproductive age as well as female adolescents of 11 – 19 years. Proper hygiene practices such as handwashing will be promoted for prevention of cholera and other water related diseases. FH will train community-based hygiene volunteers to ensure the day to day sensitization activities. Hand washing stations will be installed at strategic public places (water points, schools, communal latrines and market places). IEC materials (leaflets and posters) to be used for social beahivior change will be based on the MISAU and WASH cluster approved messages. Sanitation PromotionThe project will support resettled IDPs with dome slabs for self-construction of household latrines. Communities will also be engaged through sensitization sessions and discussions for promotion of proper use of latrines and essential sanitation practices. Beneficiaries will be encouraged open ans use garbage pits for solid waste management. Inclusive latrines will be constructed / rehabilitated at schools hosting IDP students. Emergency communal latrines will be constructed as a temporary sanitation solutions at sites receiving new IDPs and these will be replaced by household latrines. All WASH interventions will comply with the Sphere and WASH cluster Standards. Distribution of WASH NFIs – 3900 HHsThe project will distribute hygiene and dignity kits comprising of; water buckets, soap, reusable face masks, tooth paste, tooth brushes, multi-purpose cloth, women pants, menstrual hygiene pads and GBV IEC materials. The composition of hygiene and dignity kits will follow the recently approved WASH cluster NFIs distribution strategy

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Consultations will be held with affected populations, including discussions with specific target groups such as the elderly, disabled and women and girls on access and provision of WASH services and supplies. All voucher distributions will comply with the WASH cluster starndards. The placement of communal latrines and distribution sites, will include consultations with the elderly, the disabled and women and girls to ensure that these groups feel safe in accessing and using the facilities/sites. Continuous feedback will be sought from the IDPs through different feedback channels including regular community meetings with the IDPs, help desks, linha verde, and community committees. The project will also conduct a gender analysis during the first quanter of its implementation and results from this analysis will be used to improve on the implementation of the project.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

FH/M has ensured women participate in the project through purposeful targeting of at least 50% women headed HHs, having 50% representation by women in project steering committees and help-desk committees. The committees will not only be trained in management but also on the vital role of women in HH and community development. Gender awareness will be extended to the entire community at every point of interaction with the project. An in-depth gender analysis will be conducted by FH/M to guide project implementation and ensure sustainable empowerment of women beyond the life of the project. FH/M will promote shared power between men and women - including control of resources and decision-making - and support for women’s empowerment as a pathway to reducing gender inequalities. Women with babies will be prioritized during food voucher distributions and voucher redemptions to allow them ample time to travel back to their homes to feed their children and perform their other roles (reproductive, productive, and community managing activities). FH/M will engage both men and women as part of the project steering committees for meaningful involvement in decision making by both sexes. In this project, FH/M will reinforce gender empowerment and transformation by engaging women who will comprise at least 50% of beneficiaries and project committee members respectively. FH/M will ensure that distances from homes to voucher distribution points or vendor shops are within walking distances (less than three kilometers) of the beneficiaries. To ensure protection and inclusiveness, FH/M will ensure that voucher distribution is conducted during daylight and will provide space for nursing mothers. FH/M will take advantage of pre and post distribution speeches to convey key messages on issues such as gender and protection.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

FH will create or revitalize disability management committees to ensure identification and effective involvement of PWD and other vulnerable people in the project. Personalized support particularly in transportation of entitlements after distributions will be provided through community based management committees. The project will seek to strengthern the local social-networks to ensure continued support to PWD. Distribution sites will be set in consideration of the inclusion requirements.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

This project will have minimal negative environmental impact and FH/M will put in place measures to mitigate any potential negative environmental impact. The project will promote proper management and handling of solid waste to avoid environmental pollution and ensure safe living environment. Were risks of environmental pollution is high, FH will work with specialized service providers with experience and appropriate equipement for safe handling, transportation and treatment of fecal sludge where appropriate.Additional measures will include; avoiding crowds during distribution by serving project beneficiaries in small manageble groups at a time. WASH activities will include training and sensitization on proper management of waste management at household level. FH/M field staff will ensure garbage is collected after distributions and properly disposed of. Where vouchers will be used, vendors will attach all the vouchers to the invoice and send them to FH/M for payment. Therefore, the paper vouchers will not be disposed of by the vendor, which might result in poor disposal and thus impacting the local environment. An FH/M staff assigned to monitor voucher redemption will also monitor compliance to environmental protection by stakeholders.
