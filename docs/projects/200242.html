Project Summary

The proposed intervention aims to strengthen the provision of Child Protection (CP) services and Child Protection support to the children and families affected by the crisis among the Lebanese and Palestinian communities.
It will be built up on existing projects, benefiting from the long-lasting experience of TDH IT in preventing and responding to protection threats of the migrants and Lebanese children and from the experience of the organization working with Palestinian communities in Lebanon.
Targeted beneficiaries will be supported with the provision of full cycle quality CP prevention (Awareness, Community based PSS, Caregivers Program) and response services (Focused PSS and Case Management) in vulnerable localities particularly affected by the crisis in Beirut, Mount Lebanon and Baalbeck-Hermel Governorates, as well as in Palestinian refugee camps across Lebanon.
In line with sector request to prioritize the enrollment of extremely vulnerable children and those who are the hardest to reach, TDH IT has refined and reinforced its Needs assessment tool with additional criteria which have enabled the staff to identify and enroll children and caregivers facing multiple and complex protection vulnerabilities. The outreach process includes those in our waiting list, household/door-to-door visits, consultation with communities as well as internal/external referrals in coordination with other humanitarian actors based in the areas of intervention.
Thanks to TDH IT integrated programming, children will be referred from CP to EDU, and vice versa, according to their specific needs. Inclusion of children with disabilities represents one of the main pillars of the intervention and it will be ensured from the assessment and outreaching phases to the provision of services. All TDH and partners' staff will be trained on inclusive methodologies to actively involve children with disabilities, and to spread a culture of inclusion during the implementation of the activities. Children with disabilities will be also screened through the use of the Washington Group Questionnaire, which is already integrated in TDH IT multi-sectoral needs assessment tool.
The intervention will be implemented both through TDH IT direct implementation and through the involvement of local organizations, benefiting from their technical skills and added values in providing PSS through alternative modalities (e.g.: sports, arts, yoga). Such collaboration has been piloted by TDH IT through previous initiatives with successful achievements in terms of child protection mainstreaming, outreaching new typologies of beneficiaries and addressing related vulnerabilities.
Among targeted Palestinian camps, the intervention will be fully implemented through local organizations, already collaborating with TDH IT in the provision of NFE services to increase Child Protection in Education integrated approaches and mainstreaming.
Community engagement initiatives will be organized in all the locations mentioned to raise awareness on the main Child Protection threats (especially child labor, early marriage and violence against children) as well as on safe identification and referral (SIR) mechanisms in order to activate the related prevention and response intervention.
The modality of implementation will be face-to-face, unless additional issues will require to adapt to the emergency situation in the country and will require the return to remotely or blended modality, through the use of ad hoc tools (interactive sessions, videos and games, arts to be implemented via remote) and the use of online platforms (WhatsApp, Zoom, conference calls). Specific cholera and COVID-19 awareness sessions will be delivered throughout the intervention in line with the MoPH/health sector recommendations.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Focused Psychosocial Support (FPSS) will be provided by technical staff and animators to the most vulnerable children at medium to high risk of CP violations (child labor, child marriage, physical/emotional abuse), focusing on their emotional wellbeing and providing them with the needed skills to identify their emotions, manage their reactions, learn how to express and report any kind of abuse or violation they are exposed to. Children identified at medium/high risk will benefit from individual support provided by TDH IT Case Management team, together with their family. Beneficiaries will be identified both internally (during CBPSS and FPSS, and during educational activities implemented by TDH IT in the same areas through other funding) and through referral from external agencies or from the communities. Caregivers will be provided with peer-to-peer and ad hoc sessions, supporting them to release stress and enhance positive communication within their families.Community Based Psychosocial Support (CBPSS) will be provided through the use of inclusive structured recreational activities both in indoor/outdoor spaces. Community engagement initiatives will be organized through a wide range of activities to mainstream CP in local communities of reference, and will be implemented in the framework of the QUDWA plan, a national Social Behavioral Change & Communication plan to prevent child marriage, child labor and violence against children.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

The planned ERP intervention aims to respond to the compounded crises impacting Lebanon which are creating additional protection risks for women, adolescent girls, children, and marginalized groups. All staff, partners and service providers involved will receive trainings on TDH IT Code of Conduct, PSEAH policy, Child Safeguarding policy and Gender. TDH IT will have a fair representation of males and females in the provision of training/awareness initiatives. Collection and analysis of gender/age/disability/nationality disaggregated data will be ensured throughout the intervention. High coordination with the SGBV task force is ensured for case management services and referral mechanisms. Prioritizing the inclusion of children with disability in the programming, mainstreaming gender, increasing awareness on PSEAH policy, will pave the road to mitigate protection risks in the communities raising as a result of the multiple shocks affecting the country.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

TDH IT has two regional hotlines for CM reporting and Complaints boxes in its centres which are managed according to the TDH IT Whistleblowing Policy. TDH IT uses WhatsApp to capture feedback from beneficiaries on activities, with adaptations made on an ongoing basis. HH visits are also conducted, especially for CP cases or CwDs. Feedback is recorded during distributions with PDMs completed with recipients of distributed items to understand feedback on distribution quality, sufficiency, utilisation and effectiveness. TDH IT will ensure that selection criteria for assistance will be clearly communicated to avoid contributing to tension in the community. Referrals ensure that HHs receive the services they need, especially for CM and essential services. TDH IT also updates service mappings and coordinates to ensure integration of services and create synergies between sectors.
