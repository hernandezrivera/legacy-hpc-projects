Project Summary

The project will cover Protection and assistance with the overall aim of strengthening the protection of refugees, asylum seekers and internally displaced persons as well as host communities affected by the current situation in Libya through life-saving and essential services.
With regards to refugees and asylum seekers, UNHCR will continue providing urgent life-saving assistance upon disembarkation and in detention centres, advocating for release from and alternatives to detention of refugees and asylum-seekers intercepted or rescued on land or at sea by the authorities and undertaking registration and providing basic aid and information through community centres, outreach partners and hotlines. UNHCR will focus on identifying solutions for refugees and promoting alternatives to the use of smugglers, raising awareness on the dangers along the migration routes and within Libya and strengthening communication to get the messaging out to the right platforms, enhancing inter-state cooperation and government capacities to provide protection and strengthening partnerships to address the problems and deliver much needed responsive services to those affected. UNHCR will also engage in developing mechanisms to protect the most vulnerable persons with specific needs, including survivors of sexual and gender-based violence, victims of trafficking and UASC, or persons of concern to UNHCR in detention and urban settings in Libya.
UNHCR will expand the programme for IDP protection and assistance through a more enhanced Protection monitoring/assessment, coordination and/or provision of assistance but as well reinforcing assistance to host communities and Libyan civilians in need including individual and community based assistance. The support to IDPs and host communities will impact positively in this context where all communities are in need due to the country current situation. UNHCR will focus on advocacy and outreach to communities, including through information sharing mechanisms to reach IDPs and host communities and support response initiatives of the Libyan authorities. UNHCR’s key priorities in the IDP response is to identify and report critical protection risks, violations of human rights and international humanitarian law. Key activities will include protection monitoring, field missions and strengthened communications with communities. UNHCR will also focus on responding to such risks and violations through various means including establishing proper and confidential referral mechanisms to address immediate needs. As a means to support/enhance the recovery program in conflict-affected areas as well as areas burdening population movements, small-scale projects referred to as QIPs or community-based projects will be supported to create social cohesion between IDPs, returnees, migrants, refugees, asylum seekers and host communities in coordination or consultation with host communities and local authorities according to priorities; create rapid conditions for durable solutions towards and return/recovery, and lead to improvement of the protection situation of the people of concern.

Needs

According to the HNO and severity map 2019, as well as UNHCR’s assessments’ findings, weak law enforcement and division between rival administrations largely affect the ability for the provision of protection and basic services to IDPs, returnees and host communities. The deteriorating economic situation, inflation rate, devaluation of the Libyan dinar in the black market and shortage in cash liquidity in the banking system have all led to the reduced purchasing power of the Libyan population, especially those affected by the conflict, which further deepens the vulnerabilities and increases exposure to various protection risks. Given the volatile security situation in Libya, safety and security of civilians is severely affected, and shelter, cash and NFI needs are constantly increasing. This displaced population is also suffering from several displacements and currently live with host communities, rented apartments, collective centres and unfinished buildings, spontaneous camps. In the current situation, the most vulnerable are those exposed to risks due to limited shelter options and being forced to reside in conditions that are unsuitable for human habitation. Women and children are particularly at risk. Due to loss of home, collapse of social and family structure, an increase of domestic violence, divorce rate and early marriage has been reported. UNHCR will strengthen partnerships with agencies like Handicap International to develop victim assistance and other services for persons with disability and specific needs. The majority of IDPs in Libya are staying in urban environments with host community families, in rented accommodation or collective shelters. According to the 2019 HNO, the affected populations continue to face critical protection challenges, including risk of death and injury due to indiscriminate use of weapons, restrictions on freedom of movement and conflict-related psychological trauma. Violations of international human rights and humanitarian law, including violations of children and women’s rights are widespread but underreported. Reporting on the GBV continue to be weak, due to the absence of proper structure, supportive judicial system and fear of social stigma surrounding reporting on protection risks which women might face. As a result of the prolonged crisis, the psychological distress and exploitation from criminal networks are reported. Legal documentation is one of the major concerns due to lack of access to civil registration (births, marriages, deaths, etc.), leaving IDPs exposed to various protection risks and hinders their access to basic services. Moreover, IDPs suffer from limited access to livelihood opportunities, which prevents them from becoming self-sufficient and increases their reliance on humanitarian assistance. Protracted displacement remain one of the high concerns. Despite several local reconciliation efforts and agreements which included measures to encourage their return, until now, the majority of protracted IDPs such as IDPs from Tawergha and Benghazi are unable to return to their areas of origin in fear of retaliation, physical safety for them and their families, not to mention lack of basic services in areas of return. The protracted IDP situation may hamper the reconciliation efforts due to prolonged disconnect between those who left and those who stayed behind. There is a need to enhance coordination with local municipalities, Ministry of Social Affairs, Ministry of Local Governance, Ministry of Interior, Ministry of IDPs, the Supreme Committee for IDPs, and IDPs, returnees and host communities, which are all key stakeholders contributing to enhance the protection environment and response to IDPs’ needs. There is also a need to develop safeguards, adopted at national level, to enhance the rights environment for protection of IDPs and IDP returnees.

Activities

UNHCR focuses to deliver strengthened services and protection to persons of concern, including to those identified with specific needs. It also aims to ensure humanitarian action integrates protection and AGD mainstreaming and that persons of concern have equitable access to protection and assistance. UNHCR’s key priorities in the IDP response is to identify and report critical protection risks, violations of human rights and international humanitarian law, and intervene to assist individuals and groups at- risk. Key activities include protection monitoring, field missions and strengthened communications with communities. UNHCR will also focus on responding to such risks and violations through various means including establishing proper and confidential referral mechanisms to address immediate needs. UNHCR will work on enhancing protection space in urban settings, community participation, social cohesion/peaceful co-existence and access to services through establishment of community-based structure, community projects and outreach activities.With regards to refugees and asylum seekers, UNHCR will focus on provision of, SGBV, CP, community activities, registration, cash-based support for protection purposes and PSS etc. Main activities GBV: Case management, referrals for responses including PSS services and medical assistance but in addition awareness/capacity building activities with other stakeholders. Location: mainly in Tripoli. Modality: CDCs and mobile units. Main activities CP: Case management, BID panels, referrals for responses including PSS services and medical assistance, awareness/capacity building activities with other actors. Location: mainly in Tripoli. Modality: CDCs and mobile units).GBVs and CP activities implemented and planned by UNHCR will carried out in coordination with other GBV and CP actors, as well as the protection sector and sub-groups, in particular GBV and CP sub-sectors, and this to ensure comprehensive and coordinated approach. Establishment of 6 Community Day Centres (3 for IDPs in Misrata, Benghazi and Zawyia and 3 for refuges in Tripoli. The CDC will serves as one-stop shop for service providers and will be open for all protection actors to provide protection services to PoCs. Six referral pathways mechanisms will be stablished in each centre to strengthen the referral system between partners. The CDC for IDPs will be a platform where IDPs and host community can engage and benefit from the services provided. The CDCs will be the main platform for disseminating protection information/awareness-raising. UNHCR will start providing legal counselling and legal assistance on several protection issues such as civil documentations and HLPs. UNHCR will also provide technical support and trainings for the state actors to improve the legal protection for PoCs including displacement, refugees and other PoCs. As well as providing trainings and capacity building on protection relates issues to PoCs. The overall Protection situation, whether in urban settings, detention facilities or at disembarkation points will continue to be monitored together with other stakeholders. UNHCR will continue to support and engage with the government of Libya on asylum and protection space while advocating for refugee/asylum seekers rights as well as working on alternatives to detention.

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

Access: provision of services in CDCs that are located in areas where beneficiaries are concentrated and can easy to access. For those who cannot access the CDCs for vulnerability related reasons, the mobile units are reaching out to where they live. Safety: The services that UNHCR and its partners are located in areas that considered safe and in case of deterioration of the security situation in the areas, security notification to community outreach volunteers and for those who were scheduled for appointment are sent. All GBV and CP PSS sessions are conducted at confidential space within the center or during the outreach visits. UNHCR has PSEA SOPs and designated focal points. Complaint boxes will be installed in visible and accessible location s in the CDCs as and generic email address is shared with refugees to report any concerns to UNHCR. Dignity: CDCS are prepared to provide dignified spaces and availability of WASH facilities. Staff are trained to treat beneficiaries with dignity and respect. UNHCR provides transport to medical facilities for those with serious medical needs. UNHCR and its partners will distribute sanitary napkins and dignity kits, for women and girls at reproductive age. WASH facilities and spaces are segregated by gender.
