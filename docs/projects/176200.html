Project Summary

Prevailing social norms, gender roles, national conflict and other hazards like COVID-19, floods, a highly militarized environment, and the frequent cattle raiding and revenge killings among the different clans and tribes exacerbate the risk of GBV in South Sudan. Protection concerns such as generalized violence and insecurity, access to food, water and firewood, expose women to a high risk of GBV. There is, therefore, an urgent need to enhance GBV prevention efforts with additional strategies to mitigate risks, such as fuel strategies and protective accompaniments. Furthermore, lack of recognition of GBV as violation of fundamental human rights continues a culture of impunity and other injustices throughout the country. Therefore, notwithstanding the vitalized peace agreement, it is important to continue to raise awareness, reduce prevalent stigmas in the communities and increase identification and documentation of GBV cases as well as providing survivor’s access to psychosocial support and other services for their recovery and healing. VOP GBV Intervention aims to Ensure the provision of critical protection related assistance and specialized services through an integrated/mainstreamed approach to address the priority needs of targeted people among vulnerable women, men, girls and boys and inclusion of elderly, people with disability and people living with HIV/AIDs in hard-to-reach and priority geographical areas and Prevent, mitigate and respond to GBV risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience. VOP will provide psychosocial support services, women and girls friendly spaces, CBP preparedness, Cash base intervention and protection risk mitigation. Individual/ comprehensive case management and recreational activities. Cases will be identified through the community engagement groups, local authorities, community-based protection networks and will be referred to trained skilled GBV Caseworkers. VOP will continue with empowerment of women through income generating activities that support their livelihood through mainstreaming GBV implemented in Pibor. A selected group will be train on business skills on starting their own business in developing a business plan. In order to confront social norms that drive GBV, VOP will specifically focus on engaging men and boys through EMAP strategy in its programming to become change agents for shifting gender roles and ensuring gender gaps are covered through gender responsiveness. The discussion around gender and social norms will be facilitated in regular workshops, including influential community leaders to reflect on masculinity and the reasons behind their violence towards women, gender, sexuality, and substance abuse. VOP will conduct awareness campaigns addressing specific problems affecting girls such as child and forced marriage and the low inclusion of girls in school. VOP will play an important role in strengthening coordination among Humanitarian actors present in Pibor County. VOP as a GBV lead in GPAA assign by GBV Sub Cluster will strengthening coordination with the GBV Working group, as well as coordinating strengthening of the GBV referral pathways. VOP in Pibor will be in close collaboration with GBV sub cluster in Juba, and joint activities with Partners to prevent duplication. VOP GBV interventions will be aligned with the GBV Sub-cluster Strategy. To effectively Respond to GBV, VOP will collaborate with strategic partners to contribute to the provision of the basic package of multi-sectoral GBV services at community level and build capacity of service providers and communities to deliver quality GBV services in line with best practices and minimum standards for humanitarian settings. VOP will strengthen GBV prevention and risk mitigation across its interventions through mainstreaming GBV in Education, WASH, FSL, legal/justice and Health sectors. VOP will ensure Community engagement and have an exit strategy plan to sustain the implementation of the project.

1 - Needs

In Jonglei (GPAA) like other former states affected by the decades of conflicts in South Sudan, Rape and sexual violence among other GBV types have been perpetrated on women and girls during crisis yet opportunities to access basic life-saving services have been limited. The need for multi-sectoral humanitarian response to address the multiple needs of the population and scaling up of existing services to meet the growing demand of most vulnerable and conflict affected population therefore becomes eminent. Pibor counties of former Jonglei State have experienced multiple displacements and insecurity due to internal conflicts and flooding. Key drivers of conflict in Jonglei, specifically in the above-mentioned counties were identified as food insecurity, flooding, cattle raiding/theft for commercial purposes and rising bride prices. According to HNO, Ongoing sub-national violence escalated in May, with an estimated 80,000 people displaced. While More than 60,000 people were displaced by sub-national violence in Jonglei State in June and the violence led to the disruption of humanitarian services which is one of the reasons why Pibor is on catastrophic need. Lack of progress on governance and peace building, combined with reduced regional economic activity and increased pressure on public finances, is likely to result in continued localized and sub-national violence with armed groups fighting for control over territory and resources. Although cessation of hostilities is holding in most places, fighting between parties of the revitalized peace agreement continues in parts of Jonglei and Pibor in particular. Violence, forced and early marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, abduction, discriminatory practices within the legal system and harmful traditional practices – is a persistent and serious problem in South Sudan and especially Pibor which is ranked in a catastrophic need as per 2021 Gender based violence (GBV) is grossly underreported due to fear of retaliation and social stigma. Prevailing social norms, gender roles, national conflict, a highly militarized environment, and the frequent cattle raiding and revenge killings among the different clans and tribes exacerbate the risk of GBV in South Sudan. Protection concerns such as generalized violence and insecurity, access to food, water and firewood, expose women to a high risk of GBV. South Sudan 2021 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), as well as assessments conducted by VOP, show how protection risks continue to affect the South Sudanese population and especially in Pibor County. People’s physical and mental wellbeing, living standards and coping mechanisms are expected to further deteriorate in 2021. Some 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. This is an 800,000 increase in absolute numbers from the 7.5 million people in need in 2020. According to the intersectoral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs are most concerning in Pibor County in Jonglei which was classified as the one of the county in catastrophic need. VOP will provide a holistic GBV prevention, risk mitigation and response interventions, the project will directly support 51191 (20,000 Women, 15,000 girls, 8096 boys and 8094 boys) in Jongolei State, Pibor County. According to HNO (2020) and VOP rapid assessments of 2020, findings show that 50% of girls in South Sudan get married before the age of 18 years which has been worsened by closed schools due to Covid 19. Historically, Nuer, Dinka and Murle pastoralists all participated in cyclical cattle raiding and child abduction. Cattle are central not only to all three communities’ livelihoods. In addition to reports of women and girls being raped and abducted, children were also abducted, or separated from their families during the fight. The IDPs still express feelings of insecurity.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

Effectiveness is measured in relation to objectives, and so the effectiveness different transfers in meeting the different kinds of transfer will partly depend on the specific objectives of designed outcome of a project. Evaluators need first to assess how far the stated objectives Timeliness (which transfers can be matched the needs of the target groups. Evaluators will look at the parameters or indicators of outcomes considered (e.g. food consumption, malnutrition rates, school enrolment rates) to see how well they capture the desired objective. The choice of transfer may have included explicit justification based on effectiveness. Evidence underlying any assumptions made should be scrutinised to see how realistic they were. Different kinds of transfers will usually result in different results since people will rarely buy exactly the same items that are chosen for them by aid agencies (e.g. they may prefer to buy cheaper building material or different kinds of food). Their actual choices are hard to predict, since different households will make different decisions, based on their circumstances and priorities. An evaluator will assess how sensible any assumptions made were (e.g. about likely expenditures or consumption outcomes) and how well-reasoned the link was between choice of transfer and desired outcomes at the decision-making stage. Timeliness is an important aspect of effectiveness, particularly in emergencies. The time required to set up and deliver an intervention should be considered in decision-making, when urgency is a priority. The effectiveness of a transfer may depend far more upon its size/value, targeting, etc. than on the type of transfer.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The feasibility of cash and vouchers rests on the expected future ability of appropriate price and quality markets to respond to increased demand if cash or vouchers were provided, Access of beneficiaries to markets and not simply on the state of the market before any intervention. Decision making should include consideration of possible measures that could be taken to address any market constraints (e.g. addressing constraints facing traders, such as capital, credit, storage and transport). Seasonal, regional trends can all influence prices. Possible price rises caused by external factors do not rule out the use of cash and vouchers, if they can be managed through the programme design. There is no consensus on when a comprehensive assessment of markets is necessary, when a ‘light’ assessment makes sense or the extent of analysis required in areas with well-functioning markets. If in-kind assistance is purchased locally or regionally, the ability of traders to ensure timely supply at an appropriate price and quality may be critical. The potential impacts on markets of different types of transfers should also be considered.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

VOP staff who come into contact with beneficiaries sign a Prevention of SEA Code of Conduct to ensure that they prevent sexual exploitation and abuse of project beneficiaries. Child protection and safeguarding code of conduct is also signed and trainings are also provided for staff and community structures that work with children. Internal trainings are also conducted to refresh staff on their obligations to prevent SEA by timely reporting cases. VOP staff will be training/ empowering the community on PSEA through community radio, radio talk show and through social distancing community meeting and awareness raising through door to door methodology. The project will establish and disseminate pictorial information on awareness raising on protection mechanisms (prevention, risk mitigation, response and referral pathway) to the community.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

VOP has established multi-sector Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRM) in all its operational sites including in the proposed project location through which the beneficiaries will be informed on their rights to launch complaints or give other forms of feedback in regard to the implementation process and the quality of services they are receiving from the VOP. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the VOP to handle any compliant report and feedback. The reporting procedures will be displayed in a visible place in the project location and complains and feedback boxes will be placed at WGSS and project site offices for those who are literate to use. Views and feedback will be collected from women, girls and community beneficiaries including those living with different disabilities and the minority, authorities from the start of the project to the intervention and monthly thereafter.Feedback and complaints will generally be collected using FGD’s with groups of men, women, boys, and girls; during community sensitization and awareness sessions; and through the use of suggestion boxes. VOP’s community feedback mechanism allows for a range of complaints to be received and managed, including a mechanism for confidentially fast-tracking concerns of a serious nature such as sexual exploitation and abuse. Through this discussion, the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries of the VOP will explain its procedures, decisions and allow women and girls to hold VOP accountable. Awareness raising on existing PSEA Networks or Focal Point(s) will be included to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms.
