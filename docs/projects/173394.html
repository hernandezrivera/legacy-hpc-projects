Project Summary

The COVID-19 health pandemic saw a surge in violence against women and girls and a contraction of Sexual and Reproductive Health (SRH) services including Family Planning. This is corroborated by SG’s own research work during the pandemic including Field reports from 23 districts, a research on Gendered Impact of COVID as well as research by others. The lockdown disrupted GBV response mechanisms and services, including RH services, shelters, police stations and the judicial system. Survivors and those at risk of GBV could only rely on a handful of people to facilitate access to information and services, underscoring the vitality of immediately available local resource persons. On-line assistance/services became the need of the hour but a challenge for service providers and for the communities who did not know about these services and how to access them. Further the technological divide as well as restricted mobility faced by women made access to services almost impossible.

Humjolian will create a pool of informed capable community female champions and male allies linked to local organizations as a first line of support in 7 districts (2 each in Sindh, Punjab and Khyber-Pakhtunkwa, and 1 in Balochistan). The project will work to identify communities most in need based on their economic vulnerability, religion, ethnicity and gender. Furthermore it will work with equal number of men and women in the districts. Engagement with men is essential, as sensitizing men of the same community on GBV creates community buy-in and decreases the threat of violence on the women as men hold power in the families and changing their perceptions is essential for sustained positive change.
The project intends to build capacity of equal number of men and women to engage communities through awareness raising on SRH and GBV issues and the pathways to access services; engage with and assist survivors of GBV and clients seeking out RH services; provide a physical referral directory of SRH and GBV service providers and response both public and private and amplify enhanced access to information through a telephone App already developed but updated to include districts not covered in the app already, Larkana, Khairpur and Bhakkar. The app will also include a guide on how to deal with GBV issues, including Sexual Assualt, reported in the field. Furthermore the project will link the tele-health provider allowing for local champions to link clients in the field with minimal resources with the health providers.
The project will further train Lady Health Workers (LHWs), community-based midwives and facility-based health care providers to identify cases of GBV and implement care protocols. Linkages between community champions and service providers such as LHW, police, health officials including Medico Legal Officers (MLOs) and shelter will be facilitated and strengthened. These person to person connections often are integral in making sure emergency care is given even when emergency situations lead to disruptions in services. Moreover, engaged champions and communities will advocate for a multi-sectoral approach and push for integration of GBV services with health services.
The project will create messaging adapted to public responses, concerns and their evolving behaviour as well as the evolving situation regarding RH and GBV issues and services.

Humjolian will result in women and girls’ access to a strong well-equipped support and referral system around GBV and SRH; enhanced access to information and services through a telephone App; more aware communities regarding Family Planning and Reproductive Health(FPRH) and GBV; better linked key stakeholders; advocacy for more gender-responsive practices in delivering services. Project will contribute to Increase community buy-in through using pre-existing networks and organizations, and leave behind a strong group trained to deal with GBV cases

1- Project Activities

Humjolain will employ a strategy of building community rapport and support through a two phased interaction; the first through Reproductive Health (RH) awareness sessions and the second on Gender Based Violence (GBV). At the outset, Community Based Organisations (CBOs) will be carefully selected from within SG’s existing network of partners to facilitate and monitor activities at the grassroots level. CBOs will assist with identification of community champions, male allies and Lady Health Workers (LHWs) who will receive training on Gender, RH including mensuration, FP and ante-natal and postnatal services. Capacity building of the core group of champions and male allies will be on gender, SRHR issues and client generation; GBV issues, national and provincial laws to address GBV, relevant law enforcement, medical and legal aid providers and protocols for dealing with survivors of GBV. This will be done sequentially with 5 day training on gender and SRHR services and a second 5 day training on GBV. The aim of the trainings will be to help the champions to respond to GBV cases and RH clients in a gender sensitive and effective manner. The Shirkat Gah teams along with champions and CBOs engaged will map services relating to RH and Gender Based Violence (GBV) in their districts and the data collected will be used to create referral pathway directories which will also feed into an existing mobile application (property of SG) of referral services for GBV responses. This exercise will allow for local champions to engage with these services in person. The champions will further carry out Social Action Plans (SAPs) at the local level for awareness on SRH and then in the second round on GBV. SAPs will facilitate linkage building with the community, help in client generation and utilize Tele-health helplines during the sessions to immediately address any health related issues that are reported by the participants. Separate awareness sessions will be held for men and for women; allowing for a safe space for women to easily share their concerns.To supplement the awareness raising efforts, the SG team will be creating pamphlets on different RH and GBV topics which will be distributed in the communities. 2 pamphlets will be made for RH; one on mensuration and health and the other on Family Planning and RH in general. 3 pamphlets will be made; one with information regarding Forced Marriage and Early Age marriage, one on domestic violence and one on Sexual assault. To maximise outreach, short messages will be created regarding GBV and RH and displayed as ticker messages on select local cable television channels for 4 consecutive months. District Networking meetings between Community Champions with health, police and shelter officials will be organized to better understand their operational requirements and strengthen linkages. Six Month Provincial reviews will be conducted collectively for Sindh and Balochistan and for Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP). In these reviews community voices and concerns will be amplified and shared with relevant departments such as the health, women development departments (WDD), and the Commissions on the Status of Women. Further these meetings will assess project impact. 2 one day health camps will be set up in each district where women and families can avail services like family planning, vaccinations and antenatal and postnatal care. Young girls will be able to get information on menstrual health. The camps will be equipped to provide medical attention for ailments like anemia etc. The health camps will be important spaces for linking women from marginalized communities with essential services. District project closure will be held to share celebrated achievements and present advocacy demands for increasing access to and quality of services. Quarterly M&E visits of SG teams in all districts with communities and champions for community feedback and any course correction needed.
