Needs

IDPs and conflict affected population, especially women and children, remain particularly vulnerable to the risks of human trafficking and GBV. However, the problem remains largely invisible and under addressed due to the limited resources to assess situation, identify, report and assist victims;In 2017, IOM Ukraine identified and assisted 1,259 victims of trafficking. This is the highest number of victims of human trafficking (VoTs) assisted by IOM since 2000 and represents an increase of 35% compared to the victims identified in 2013 (pre-crisis year). IOM also notes emerge of new [for Ukraine] vulnerable to trafficking groups of population [IDPs, ex-combatants] and new forms of exploitation [involvement into criminal activities];Until September 2018, IOM has assisted nearly 150 IDPs and other conflict-affected population who fell victims to human trafficking. IOM Ukraine caseload also shows that out of all trafficking survivors assisted in 2017, 90% were trafficked and exploited in the period of the conflict and respective crisis;Ukrainian citizens’ expectations when looking for jobs abroad have decreased, i.e. if before the crisis the promised salary was an average of USD 1,000, in the crisis years victims who turned for assistance informed that they were promised much lower salary, ranging between USD 200 – 500, which was still considered satisfying. Counterparts working in ECA indicate increased prevalence of labour exploitation and sexual abuse, including among children, in the regions bordering contact line. However, these cases are under identified and not referred for assistance, thus widening protection gaps in the overall humanitarian response to the crisis;Aggravated situation calls for increased and proactive prevention measures to inform IDPs and conflict affected population about human trafficking risks, as well as to strengthen identification efforts, streamlining counter-trafficking actions across the humanitarian assistance and thus ensuring protection and assistance for trafficking survivors;IOM’s National Monitoring Survey conducted in 2018, reports that eight (8%) per cent of IDPs household reported encountering since the beginning of the conflict at least one situation involving deceit by the employer or forced labour;Human trafficking is an abuse of human rights that affects men and women, boys and girls. In 2018, among victims of trafficking identified and assisted by IOM 59% were men, 41% - women. However, different types of trafficking have their gender aspects, i.e. the majority of those trafficked for sexual exploitation are female, while labour exploitation victims are equally men and women; the majority of general public believes trafficking targets only women; gender stereotypes of “strong men-breadwinners” push men to accept more risky offers, as well as prevent men from recognizing themselves as victims and seeking assistance.

Activities & outcomes

Output 1: Male and female victims of trafficking and labour abuse identified among IDPs have access to comprehensive reintegration assistance. - Activities for output 1: 1) Identify VoTs directly (proactively) and via external referrals (including through referral mechanisms established with the human rights organizations); 2) Organize trainings for relevant stakeholders at border crossing points and in communities located in the proximity of the contact line as part of the protection mainstreaming and victim identification effort; 3) Screen VoTs and victims of labour abuse displaced from the conflict zone and develop comprehensive individual reintegration plans that include medical, psychological, legal* and other types of assistance based on the victim’s individual needs; 4) Provide comprehensive medical and psychological treatment to victims of trafficking, as needed, in a safe and confidential manner. *IOM cooperates with a pool of legal experts from different regions of the country who are regularly trained on specifics and sensitives of work with victims of trafficking. In cases where victims identified among IDPs require legal assistance specific to IDPs needs that might fall beyond competencies of IOM’s legal experts, the referrals will be made to other protection partners ensuring confidentiality, data protection and informed consent of the victim. Output 2: IDPs and other vulnerable persons are better informed on the risks of human trafficking and other forms of abuse through targeted preventive efforts and general awareness raising campaigns - Activities for output 2: 1) Outreach to IDPs, including from Crimea, conflict affected population and other vulnerable persons through information sessions, and other targeted prevention efforts; 2) Advertise the the Counter Trafficking Hotline to IDPs and make it accessible to vulnerable population; 3) Raise awareness of risks of trafficking in persons in eastern regions immediately bordering the conflict area, and other regions that have highest concentration of IDPs and other vulnerable population; 4) Run the Counter Trafficking Hotline to provide consultations to IDPs and refer them to assistance if needed. Integrating CT operations in the humanitarian response, IOM is fulfilling commitment to protection mainstreaming and accountability to affected populations initiatives. IOM’s Institutional Framework - the Migration Crisis Operational Framework (MCOF) devotes Sector 10 specifically to CT and Assisting Vulnerable Migrants (AVM). Strong institutional framework is operationalized through models and tools developed and proved efficient in 20 years of IOM Ukraine counter-trafficking programming in Ukraine, before and during the conflict. In designing and implementing its counter-trafficking interventions as concerns IDPs and other conflict-affected population, IOM is committed to incorporate recommendation for a collective, system-wide approach on AAP in Ukraine, engaging through IASC Cluster Coordination Mechanism and transferring the accountability principles to its implementing partners nationwide. The project’s approach to gender is underpinned by the recognition that the phenomenon of human trafficking is an abuse of human rights, rather than a criminal activity suffered by women or men, girls or boys. Equal access to all project activities is provided to all beneficiaries independently of their sex. At the same time, a gender sensitive approach is applied in assessing the needs of beneficiaries and the provision of rehabilitation and reintegration services to them. Sex disaggregated data is collected for all activities. Project monitoring and evaluation tools will be designed and focus on the relevance, efficiency, effectiveness, impact and sustainability of project activities and results.

Indicators & targets

Output 1: Male and female victims of trafficking and labour abuse identified among IDPs and conflict affected population have access to comprehensive reintegration assistance: 1) # of victims identified and assisted through IOM Reintegration programme (target: 300 VoT, 50% men); 2) # of victims assisted at the Medical Rehabilitation Centre Run by IOM (target: 100 VoTs, 50% men); 3) % of VoTs successfully reintegrated into society after completion of the reintegration program (target: at least 80%); 4) # of border guards trained to identify VoTs at the crossing points (target: 100, of them 80% men). Output 2: IDPs and other vulnerable persons are better informed on the risks of human trafficking and other forms of abuse through targeted preventive efforts and general awareness raising campaigns: 1) # of IDPs and other vulnerable persons are covered by counter trafficking information sessions (target: at least 10’000 participants, 70% women); 2) # of consultations provided by CT and migrant advice hotline to IDPs and other conflict affected population (target: at least 1’600 consultations); 3) # of persons covered through CT awareness raising campaigns (target: at least 300’000).
