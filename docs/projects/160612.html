Project Summary

South Sudan has experienced a long history of civil war since its secession from Sudan in 2011 and two internal conflicts have erupted in 2013 and 2016. Since the signing of the Revitalized Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in South Sudan (R-ARCSS) on 12 September 2018, the number of human rights violations and abuses committed has largely decreased in most parts of the country however, a notable exception to this trend was observed in Central Equatoria, where civilians have continued to bear the brunt of violence perpetrated mainly by worrying parties in South Sudan. According to UNMISS Human Rights Division (HRD), September 2018 to April 2019; a total of 95 incidents were documented in central Equatoria, including 30 attacks on villages leading to the unlawful killing of 104 civilians and wounding 35 others, as well as the abduction of at least 187 civilians for forced recruitment, forced labour and sexual slavery. At least 99 women and girls and some are as young as 12 years old were targeted, rape or sexual assault during the reporting period. Looting and destruction of civilian homes, private and public properties was also documented and has contributed to the spill-over of the same violence to other states like Western Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria, Northern Bahr-el-Ghazal among others. These acts of conflict related violation & abuses particularly in Central Equatoria has led to the internal displacement of more than 56,000 individuals (IDPs) within South Sudan as well as 19,719 Refugees to the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and Uganda.
Voice for Change (VFC) is a National NGO with presence in Yei, Juba & Magwi Counties with established operational bases. The organization has well trained, self-motivated and experienced staff in GBV programming and general protection and have participated in various inter-agency assessment missions coordinated by OCHA in areas outside Yei town, Morobo, and Lainya County. With robust experience and expertise VFC plans to address some of these GBV critical gaps identified during the outreach assessment missions which inclde;- a) limited case management & PSS services; b) inadequate care for survivors both adult & children; c) limited protection & Justice support; d) livelihood support, etc. This project targets 10,400 beneficiaries from the mentioned counties with the ultimate goal to ;- (1) Ensure provision of quality survivor-centered service for GBV risk mitigation and response with a focus on capacity building for service providers (law enforcement & legal justice actors, teachers, PTA, psychosocial workers/ counselors, and Health care providers on GBV - CMR package; (2) Reduce the suffering of women, girls, boys and men who suffered different forms of violence including Gender-Based Violence through provision of direct services such as; a) Caring for Survivors; b) Case management & Psychosocial support or counseling ; c) livelihood support for income-generating activities through the Women & Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) and referrals to health care & justice system; (3) increase advocacy for protective laws & by-laws to achieve a durable solution and peaceful coexistence amongst the target counties. Specific objective is to; - (1) Increase awareness raising on GBV prevention and response messaging including general protection; (2) improve data collection system through safety audit, monitoring assessment & review of institutional data. The expected results is to create a safer, healthier and peaceful community for women & girls, boys & men to live free of Sexual & Gender-Based Violence and ensure that rights, freedom & dignity of women & girl is upheld.

1 - Needs

Sexual & Gender Based Violence remains one of the critical humanitarian concerns to address particularly the population in the conflict affected counties of Central Equatoria State namely ;-Morobo, Lainya, Kajo-Keji, Juba and Magwi in Eastern Equatoria. These counties since 2016 to date, security situation remains extremely volatile especially along the roads with tensions and occasional clashes between various armed groups, particularly SSPDF, NAS, and SPLA-IO. These interconnected dynamics of violence has worsened the human rights situation in the county and that is characterized by frequent displacements, limited road access for humanitarian actors to deliver relief assistance etc. The conflict therefore has impacted negatively on the individuals and communities’ well-being, as it has destabilized the coping mechanisms, weakened institutional capacity to provide basic services to the vulnerable populations and denied rural communities from accessing basic services in the town such as health care, education among others. The conflict too has aggravated all forms of abuses including Sexual & Gender Based Violence (SGBV) such as abductions, gang rape, sexual assault, physical assault, psychological violence and trafficking. Domestic violence too has increased in the families due to economic crisis which the Community Care project intends to address. During the inter-agency assessment missions coordinated by OCHA for example; the mission to Koyoki & Berigo Bomas - Wuji Payam - Lainya County (date: 05 April 2019); - protection cluster observed a number of young mothers and girls between 15 – 17 of age breastfeeding and others pregnant. This signifies presence of child/ forced marriage since that area is a front line and absence of Post Rape Care Drugs (PEP KITs) puts most women/girls at risk of getting unwanted pregnancy. Also, Lasu assessment mission (date: 2nd, April, 2019); the protection cluster report indicates rape and abduction as the common GBV problem and limited access to life saving services such as medical treatment (PEP KITs) for prevention of STIs infections and unwanted pregnancy. The report also noted limited access to case management & PSS; legal aid support and limited access to sustainable livelihoods services which increases vulnerability of the population in most conflict affected Payams and Bomas especially - Panyume Payam, Morobo payam, Lujulo, Panyume In Morobo County; Mugwo payam, Otogo, Lasu, Tore and Pakula in Yei County, Mukaya and Kupera in Lainya County and all the 5 Payams of Kajo-Keji County (Kangapo I & II, Lire, Liwolo & Nyepo). VFC intervention will use Community Engagement & Action approach (form community discussion groups) as tool for social norms transformation in addressing the root causes & contributing factors to Sexual & Gender Based Violence (UNICEF Community Care Toolkit 2014). Life skill activities in schools (GBV awareness for adolescence girls & boys, debates, dramas and radio talk shows) will also be used to increase GBV prevention & response messaging. Women & Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) activities for Livelihood support and entrepreneurship trainings will also be used to support vulnerable women & girls including survivors of child marriages for self-reliance. Community outreach awareness on GBV prevention & response and Capacity Building of service provider on GBV packages, case management, MHPSS, CMR for health care providers, legal justice packages and peace building. To achieve this milestones, VFC will work in collaboration with other partners on the ground such as SSUHA, Islamic Relief, SSDO, TITI foundation, South Sudan Law society and State Ministry of Gender, Child &social welfare, and STEWARD women in Nimule to implement joint activities such as safety audits, Protection assessment, Multisectoral trainings, advocacy, information sharing & Referral pathway development & dissemination as we used to do in Yei River County for the last 6 years.

2 - Activities

• Comprehensive case management, individual & group psychosocial support or counseling will be implemented through the established Women & Girls Friendly Space (WGFS) which are constructed within the community and in any case the survivor feels insecure at the WGFS for fear of reprisal attack by the perpetrator, the caseworkers can make an appointments to meet privately either in the office or any confidential place the survivor consent to sit for the counseling sessions. Referrals to other protection desks within the county or one stop centre at the hospital will be used for other needs (health, protection, NFI etc). Case conference meetings will also be conducted only with partners involved in the case to lobby support from other partners and VFC used to do in Yei. • Community outreach awareness on GBV prevention & response, dissemination of referral pathway including launching of public events i.e. (16th days of activism), International women's day etc will be some of the interventions in collaboration with other humanitarian partners on the ground. • Women & Girl's Friendly Space (WGFS) activities for sustainable livelihood support will be implemented for self-reliance. Already two WGFS centers are operational in Yei town with support from IsraAID in 2018/2019 and 4 more will be constructed in other project locations. • Capacity building of service providers including law enforcement & legal justice actors, teachers & PTA, Psychosocial workers, and health workers on GBV- CMR packages will be in the plan. VFC from 2015 - 2018 with support from UNICEF has implemented training programmes for psychosocial workers, senior women teachers, Police, court clerks, Judiciary, Chiefs and women leaders using the UNICEF toolkit 2014 and same training modules will be implemented in new project locations. • Life skill activities in schools will be implemented (GBV awareness for adolescent girls & boys, debates, drama and radio talk shows) to increase the coverage of GBV prevention & response messaging. • Community Engagement & Action activities (formation of at least 6 community discussion groups) for women, youth, and men comprising of 25 members each will be formed to discuss social norms topics for 15 weeks, come up with action plan, launch and implement the actions This approach has been used for the past 3 years in Yei for social norms transformation in addressing the root causes of SGBV and it was proved effective. Component of peace building (peace negotiation, & reconciliation) will be included in the discussions groups. To date, VFC has facilitated 21 discussion groups from 2014 in Yei and there is need to expand to new locations including areas outside Yei town. • Coordinate with other partners on the ground and conduct protection assessment and safety audits as part of the GBV risk mitigation measure. • Coordinate and partner with health actors on ground through referrals to provide medical services to victims and survivors of rape • Conduct service mapping on available GBV services to ensure that the GBV referral pathway is established/strengthened, updated and functional before providing training for frontline service providers and do information session on available services • Contribute to the joint development of GBV standard operating procedures (GBV SoPs) in partnership with other actors to guide provision of coherence of quality GBV services. • Advocacy for protective laws or by-laws that can safeguard women's and girl’s rights, freedom and dignity. Methodology; - Recruitment of community volunteers (focal persons or net-workers or outreach officers or community discussion leaders) will be done and training them to support the social workers. Mobile PSS services will be done in hard to reach areas weekly. Hiring of an additional car will be done to easy mobility. Airtime support for government service providers listed on the referral pathway will be considered for effectiveness.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

All beneficiaries have legal rights to access resources, knowledge and humanitarian support for the project. The first step is to recognize that women have equal rights with men and all deserve the respect of dignity. Women/girls' activities will be exclusive for women & girls; no interference by men e.g. group psychosocial support for women/girls or income-generating activities support like bed-sheet knitting. During outreach awareness-raising, female and males views during participation are recognized and no side is allowed to dominate the talking especially men who used to be. Disables are also given the chance to raise up their concerns for objective decision making. Group rules and regulations will be set in any gathering to control the discussions. concern of the survivor to access the services will be sought before a referral is made. All these are the minimum standard in a humanitarian setting and will be recognized.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project wills benefits all groups (host community, IDPs and returnee and persons with disability) without discrimination. For example, the current Village Saving and Loans Association (VSLA) group for Yei has 5 members are disables out of 70 beneficiaries (women/girls) and comprises of IDPs and host communities. The same approach will be used in new locations if VFC proposal is accepted and hopefully returnees will increase in numbers given the peace prospect. Same thing applied for the trainings,

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The women & girls friendly space (WGFS) activities for livelihood support are a long term intervention which is linked to government & community system because it includes construction of facilities. Trainings and protection services are also parts of the government priorities for development. In terms of collaboration and monitoring, the state and county department of Gender Child and Social Welfare will be one of the important stakeholders to be involved in the planning stages to support policy formulation and endorsement. They will also provide protection to the implementing staff, the beneficiaries at the WGFS centre and the community at large. During trainings for example, government authorities are always invited for official opening and closure of the workshop as a sign of recognition that they are part of the project. Also in the referral pathway, protection and legal justice actors especially the police gender desk is part of the entity to reefer GBV survivors for legal services with an informed consent.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Community care project is linked to the national strategy for GBV sub-cluster - 2020 which presents a framework to; - a) Increase access for the most vulnerable group to quality multi-sectoral humanitarian GBV services and reduce incidents of GBV through prevention and risk mitigation activities in South Sudan ;-b) Expanding the available basic package of multi-pectoral GBV services, e.g. Building capacity of service providers and support communities to deliver and access quality GBV services is the best practice for minimum standards in humanitarian settings which VFC has much experience to champion. Through this project in the locations mentioned, these counties if peace prevails will host most of the returnees from Uganda, DRC Congo and the IDPs who wish to return to their ancestral land to settle.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Most areas of Yei, Morobo and Lainya are safe and accessible. However, some areas cannot be accessed for example Tore payam in Yei county due to the broken bridge and the worn off kaya bridge connecting kajo- keji county through panyume. This makes Kajo -keji only accessible through air or via Uganda. Juba and Magwi is safe and accessible. The general problem is the negotiated access through OCHA which used to be an obstacle for humanitarian workers to access hard to reach areas especially IO controlled areas and with the relative peace hopefully, the road blocks will be removed and access will be free for all humanitarian agencies without approval letter from government authorities. At the momement, there is a need to establish a base in Kajo-keji County to solve the access problem. While areas of Lainya and Morobo can be accessed easily through the VFC base in Yei while the organization moves slowly to establish permanent offices in these new locations. Field staff (social workers & supervisors) will then be recruited from within the counties so that protective environment will be guaranteed for them while other technical staff like coordinators or programme manager can be based in Yei and schedule frequent monitoring visit.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Women & girls friendly space will be sustainable after the capacity of women/ girls beneficiaries is fully build in business skills for self reliance. The WGFS facility will be handed to the community after the project ends. In 2018/2019, VFC in Yei constructed 2 WGFS with support from IsraAID and the 2 facilities all belongs to the community and supervised by VFC. Currently; VFC has introduced Village Saving and Loans Association (VSLA) in Juba, Yei and Nimule for livelihood sustainability and 70 women & girls including 5 disables have benefitted from the economic support. The same approach will apply to other project location using the available resources on the ground.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

All groups including the minority will be included during the consultation meetings e.g. disables, elderly and people from other counties and all category of people will be involved during the awareness raising, training, and selection for direct benefit (WGFS - livelihood activities). Advocacy and feedback will be for all to solve problems of risk of exposure to violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance and services. The problem of Interference of external stakeholders in impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries will be solved by screening and verification of of the list of beneficiaries presented by community leaders and this is the roles of the VFC social workers in collaboration with the community focal persons who are also selected from the community. We always conduct home to home visit during the verification exercises so that the right beneficiary is approved to avoid problems of chiefs registering most of his/her family members or closest relatives as beneficiaries. Large leaflets and few small copies of the GBV Referral pathway will be taken along during the community outreach awareness raising so that majority can access the messages for prevention & response to GBV incidences.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Following are the feedback mechanism for the project; Local language will be used during the training presentations and awareness raising so that every person can understands and contributes in the discussion. Equal consideration and participation during all sessions will be a priority so that minority and marginalized group can feel to be part of the group. Pre and post test questions can be given to participants during trainings to accommodate feedback from every member. At the community level or in the WGFS, suggestion box can be used so that people who feel shy to talk can drop their written suggestion in to the box or contact the social worker privately to give his/her inputs. Raising of hands up when talking will be encouraged so that facilitator can pick at random and in case others feel shy, rotational contribution can motivate others to talk. Grouping of the population according to age and sex during monitoring assessment (focus group discussion or safety audit) will be consideration so that every one is comfortable to contribute the discussion.
