Project Summary

The project has 3 main objectives:
1- To reduce the risk posed by explosive hazards and the likelihood of exposure to them through implementing the Risk Education pillar of Mine Action and raising the awareness and knowledge of civilians and humanitarian workers through implementing tailored EORE programming in 1 subdistrict in Ain Al Arab.
This objective will be achieved through targeting children and adults directly with EORE awareness-raising sessions in schools, community centers, camps, and public spaces, and also target them indirectly through community focal points (CFPs) and thus expanding the scope of EORE awareness dissemination within the targeted communities and ensure sustainable impact through the CFPs.
The project will also include CIS of the targeted communities and provide support to victims through providing victim assistance or referring them to other services such as PSS, LHs, health...etc.
2- Enhance equitable access to quality Child Protection services in Hajin, Kisreh, and Al Karama sub-districts.
The project provides CP interventions at 3 levels: a) CP prevention b) risk mitigation, and c) specialized response services.
That means that under the response level, services such as CP case management services, referrals, and case fund will be provided to CP cases of high, medium, and low risk, taking into account the importance of ensuring effective coordination with other humanitarian actors on the ground through having updated service map and strengthening the referral pathway, as well as, coordinating with the NES PWG.
While under prevention and risk mitigation, Bahar will implement awareness raising programs and IEC campaigns targeting adults and children, structured and sustained PSS programs for children 7-17 years old including children with disabilities who will be provided with transportation to ensure effective and safe access to the project’s services, and finally parenting programs for children’s parents and caregivers.
The project will be implemented through 2 approaches: A) CFSs-based within 3 CFSs in Kisreh, Hajin, and Al Karama which will be tailored and rehabilitated to be inclusive for CwDs, and B) through an outreach-based approach to expand the geographical scope of the project and reach children and caregivers living in distant locations and are not able to access the CFS due to economic, social, or physical barriers.
3- The GBV component aims to provide access for MWBGs survivors to life-saving specialized GBV response services provided at 2 WGSS in Ar-Raqqa and Kisreh sub districts.
The project also will be implemented through static (WGSSs)- based and community-based approaches, where at the WGSS, group PSS and AR programs will be implemented using the updated AR toolkit my safety-my well-being curriculum, focusing on women and adolescent girls, given that they are considered among the most vulnerable groups to GBV.
The AR program will be associated with empowerment activities to enhance women and adolescent girls’ psychosocial well-being and empower them economically.
These services will contribute to efforts made to prevent and mitigate the risk of GBV in the targeted communities. However, in order to respond to those who have already experienced or are experiencing GBV, Bahar will provide focused PSS and specialized case management supported by case fund, and referrals to other humanitarian services
For the community-based outreach approach, Bahar will run mobile teams to expand the scope of prevention and response services and reach more vulnerable beneficiaries, most importantly, referring survivors and those at risk of GBV to the CM team at the WGSS safely.
Moreover, transportation for W&G with disabilities will be provided to/from the WGSS, given that the WGSS will be rehabilitated to be inclusive.
Also, training will be provided to the GBV staff on GBV basics and CM, and they, in turn, will provide refresher training to non-GBV staff on PFA and referrals and GBV basics.

3 - Needs

MA: According to the HNO 2022-2023, approx. 1 in 3 communities is potentially contaminated by EO which poses significant physical and phycological risks to civilians, humanitarians, and their families.Ain Al Arab are at 3rd severity scale level and the majority of the communities in these sub-districts are agricultural, where contamination is frequently reported.Therefore, Bahar will provide EORE sessions, training for CFPs, victim assistance, and will ensure that the teams are capacitated.CP: Kisreh & Hajin are at 4th severity scale according to the HNO 2022-2023. Bahar has ongoing CP projects in 2 out of these 3 areas and according to the CM trackers, most of the cases reported are child labor, child marriage, neglect, sexual violence, along with other vulnerabilities such as CwDs. This matches with the HNO 2022-2023 stating that Child labor is occurring in 84% of communities; child marriage for adolescent girls in 71% of communities. Therefore, Bahar will provide CM, structured PSS, parenting programs, and community-based AR.GBV: Ar-Raqqa and Kisreh are at 4/5 PRT severity scale according to the HNO 2022-2023. Bahar has been implemented GBV in Ar-Raqqa and Kisreh last year and the GBV cases reported by the field staff show an increase in early marriage, IPV, denial of resources, rape and sexual violence.Bahar, therefore, will provide GBV prevention and mitigation AR, provide CM to survivors and women and girls at-risk, and will build the capacity of the GBV staff.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Bahar has a Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA policy that usually is attached to each employee’s job contract clarifying all the related procedures. Bahar will ensure the field teams attend PSEA\safeguarding training and refreshing sessions provided by Bahar internally or externally. Bahar’s PSEA focal points responsibility is to ensure training are delivered to the project staff, informative materials (either soft copies in case of online or in-place approach of implementation) are distributed to both staff and beneficiaries, and updating and develop the PSEA policies including the complaints pathway/reporting mechanism regularly. The MEAL team will monitor if any danger is threatening the safety of the beneficiaries and will report findings in order to take the appropriate measures. Bahar team will also inform the community, particularly men and youth, about the objective of the project and its aim in order to mitigate exposing women and girls to any potential harm or violence.Moreover, 1 PSEA focal point will be assigned in each area of implementation; this FP will be responsible for receiving the allegations and providing awareness and distributing age-appropriate printing materials on PSEA and reporting mechanism through the hotlines, emails, and the FPs, and will be also taking part in the investigation process.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk 1: COVID-19 pandemic may impose a lack of BNFs’ access and participation in services due to curfew restrictions, home lockdown, isolation, or people’s fear of physical interaction, as well as the in-place approach might expose the participant to the risk of getting infected. Mitigation Measure 1: Bahar will provide sanitizers and Personal Protection Equipment PPE to BNFs accessing the facilities, also during the outreach activities and will ensure applying other prevention measures including social distancing, minimizing the groups-sessions participants to 5-7 max. BNFs will be informed also about measures taken to encourage them to access the services. Bahar was also a member of the GBV SC COVID-19 Task Force and will implement the assistance according to the guidance provided for GBV COVID-19 guidance and the other Sub sectors’ guidance and instructions as well when feasible. Finally, COVID-19 AR messages will be mainstreamed and disseminated along the course of the project. Risk 2: Breaching Bahar’s CoC, safeguarding, and PSEA policies by the project staff against the BNFs in an intended or unintended way. Mitigation Measure 2: Bahar includes these policies in the employees’ contracts, and they are being delivered with orientation sessions by the HR department or the FP. In addition, Bahar has assigned FPs to conduct training on those policies, receive the allegations and act accordingly, ensure timely response and investigate the allegations to take proper action.
