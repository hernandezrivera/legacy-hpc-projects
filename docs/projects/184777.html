Project Summary

The Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Aamin 2022 targets Mataban, Kismayu, Galkayo, Dhusamareb, Baidoa, Jowhar, Balcad, Quraceel and Benadir. We target 20,269 beneficiaries as we plan to expand our humanitarian services to areas needed most. Aamin Organization will provide management of SAM, MAM and PLWs. Undertake community screening, emergency free ambulance services, Mother led MUAC, training caretakers on the identification of malnutrition and ensure early action in treatments. Micronutrient support for vulnerable groups (Pregnant and nursing women and U5 children) with Vitamin A & MMN, Nutrition, Health, Hygiene (NHHP) preventative, and promotional support including IYCF support for the affected populace among them the IDPs, Host communities and refugees under 2022 the HRP project.
Somalia’s prolonged humanitarian crisis is characterized by ongoing conflicts, climate-related shocks, communicable disease outbreaks and weak social protection mechanisms. Since the beginning of 2020, three additional shocks have contributed to a deterioration of humanitarian conditions: Extensive floods, desert locust infestations and the COVID-19 pandemic. These compounding shocks have exacerbated humanitarian needs among a population already living under the strain of widespread poverty and decades of armed conflict and insecurity. According to the FSNAU report posted in February 2021, it was found that Up to 2.7 million people across Somalia are expected to face food consumption gaps or depletion of livelihood assets indicative of Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through mid-2021 in the absence of humanitarian assistance. Moreover, approximately 840 000 children under the age of five are likely to be acutely malnourished, including nearly 143 000 who are likely to be severely malnourished. According to latest Galmudug Report 27% of children under five are stunted (Short for their age), 90% of children have ever breastfeed, 61% of children start breastfeeding within the first hour of delivery, 39% of children were introduced to complementary feeding at the month of six and eight and 16% of women at the age of 15- 49 are thin and while 20% are overweight. In accordance with the latest food security assessment conducted in food insecure districts in Mudug region by FAO/FSNAU (Food Security and Nutrition Assessment Unit), it was found that only 44,415 food insecure households are inhabiting in only Galkayo District and its Villages in Mudug Region.
Aamin Organization is currently implementing an integrated health and nutrition project in Benadir Region supporting 3 health facilities funded by UNICEF led by ACF. This project has saw over 178,000 receive health and nutrition services covering three districts in Benadir region. Aamin is also implementing mobile service project funded by SHF reserve allocation 2021 in Galkayo district. Aamin Ambulance also provides a twenty-four-hour, unparalleled free ambulance service in Mogadishu, Somalia. In Q3 2021 (July-Sept), Aamin ambulance transported and facilitated 1638 affected people (1208 female, 430 male) in Benadir Region. This was a significant increase compared to last quarter by 284. The increase can be attributed to increased awareness of the availability of the free ambulance service in the ten districts. Aamin ambulance thus seeks to bridge the gap of unreliable health and emergency response system through this free ambulance and first aid system to the people of Mogadishu. We take cognizance of the needs of the vulnerable in the community particularly women and children. Our project design therefore ensures the needs of the vulnerable are entrenched in all project activities.
Aamin will remain true to the centrality of protection in its implementation of projects through protection mainstreaming, accountability to affected populations and monitoring of the protection environment.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

1: Protection of civilians: Aamin Shall ensure the following:• The access routes to the health facilities and services are safe by identifying routes for our ambulances to operate and avoid areas near military installations or threats such as attacks, stray bullets, crossfire or violence. • The project employ staff with skills and experiences working with children’s patients • Consult both displaced and host communities about health needs and assess whether Aamin ambulance interventions could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. Risk 2: Exclusion and marginalization • We shall ensure that beneficiaries know of available health services and assistance where/how to obtain it. • We shall communicate information through various means to ensure messages reaches the broader community • We shall involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to health needs. • Consult women, men, boys, girls, persons with disabilities, chronically ill, older persons, pregnant and lactating women and marginalized persons to collect accurate information about their specific needs and preferences for emergency health services. • Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response Risk 3: Protection mainstreaming:• In cooperation with protection actors, establish reporting mechanisms to report security incidents concerning hospitals to advocate against attacks on hospitals and military use of hospitals.• Ensure the location and access routes to health facilities and services are safe.• We are aware of how power dynamics between our staff and the patient can affect the patient’s response and attitude towards the staff/organization and service provided • Ensure all our staff, implementing partners and volunteers working with the beneficiaries understand, sign and adhere to Aamin Code of Conduct • In cooperation with protection actors, we shall enhance the capacity of community health workers to monitor, report and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures.
