Project Summary

Extraordinary rainfall events related to the climate crisis have increased water-related diseases, especially cholera and acute diarrhea. In addition, exceptional floods coupled with the population dynamics in internally displaced persons (IDP) camps, such as the high density of the population and the increase of returnees to their places of origin, have resulted in challenges for the operation and maintenance of water and sanitation infrastructure, and for the proper management of fecal sludge and solid waste, with a high environmental and public health impact.
All the above is in a context of an increase in the number of people in need of water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) assistance mostly concentrated in the northeastern part of the country. According to the Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (MSNA), between 2021 and 2, Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (BAY) states experienced an increased number from three million to five million people in need of WASH humanitarian assistance across internally displaced persons (+29%), returnees (+113%) and hosting communities (+95%).
In response to the needs identified, in contribution to the Nigeria WASH Sector objectives and in line with its purposes, IOM will scale up its provision of WASH services targeting internally displaced people and returnees in camp, camp-like settings, as well as hosting communities in BAY states also through the distribution of hygiene kits following the national/cluster standards. In addition, IOM will reinforce the WASH sector common pipeline strategy to facilitate access to hygiene kits for regional humanitarian actors.
The main activity will be creating access to standard hygiene kits supplies through IOM's common supply unit. This aims to ensure beneficiaries can access various hygiene kits, including Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) kits, replenishment kits, Cholera kits, and sanitation kits. This will also support rainy season preparedness and response, including cholera preparedness and response as a critical activity.
The project will be implemented in the BAY states targeting estimated 2,600,000 individuals. Further, the project focuses on hotspot areas based on previous flash reports and previous occurrences. The different local government areas (LGAs) identified are Ngala, Dikwa, Mobbar, Damboa, Monguno, Gwoza, Kala Balge, Mafa, Nganzai , Maiduguri, Jere, Konduga and Bama in Borno State; Yola South, Yola North, Mubi North, Mubi South, Lamurde, Gerei in Adamawa State, and Gujba, Gulani, Damaturu, Geidam, Yunusari, Tarmuwa, Fika in Yobe State.
IOM will ensure that the design and implementation of all WASH activities take into consideration the age, gender, as well as disability-specific needs of the project beneficiaries and addresses explicitly women and girls' needs, such as MHM.
Regarding assessments, monitoring, and evaluation of gender, age, and disability-specific needs and the impact of activities, IOM will engage with community structures on the ground in these locations during missions and set up communication channels where possible.
Using the IOM WASH Monitoring and Evaluation Framework and the Gender Inclusion and gender-based violence (GBV) Risk Mitigation Toolkit for WASH Programming, IOM will ensure that “Do No Harm” principles are adhered to throughout the WASH interventions, particularly in WASH activities of latrine and water point locations and construction, using various mechanisms, such as consultations with vulnerable groups.
IOM will analyze the potential risks of context-specific environmental degradation and implement its activities by prioritizing actions that mitigate ecological risks. In that regard, IOM will favor the use of locally made items and local procurements within the BAY states for cost-effectiveness and time efficiency and to support the local economy and build the capacity of local communities. In addition, when procuring items, IOM will favor products with greater durability and high recycled content.

1 - Needs

WASH needs, mainly access to critical WASH Supplies, including MHM kits, replenishment kits, soap, sanitation kits, and cholera kits, have continued to increase in scale and severity across Northeast Nigeria. In addition, new dynamics of population displacement and return and the effects of the climate crisis (cycles of floods and droughts) have increased the population in need and the challenges in managing water resources and WASH services.In the Nigeria HRP 2022, WASH Sector has indicated that the provision of in-kind supplies will still constitute a key response modality of the lifesaving responses in areas affected by emergencies, such as the mass displacement and cholera outbreaks which have been devastating large areas in north-east Nigeria from 2019 to date. Most of these areas are situated remotely without basic infrastructures such as roads and capacity for delivering other forms of assistance such as cash transfers or similar approaches. Lack of basic services and repeated shocks experienced by the households are only a few of the main obstacles preventing people from recovering from years of conflict and violence. IOM Nigeria, through the management of the WASH core pipeline program, will commit to efficiently supporting WASH in-kind response modality by ensuring the reliable, cost-effective, and steady stream of core relief supplies to partners for distribution to the crisis-affected populations across BAY states.The common pipeline response will not be limited to the planned camps – but also the new arrival sites, transit and reception centers, spontaneous camps and camp-like settings, and host communities with limited or no WASH supplies in the BAY States.

2 - Activities

IOM will strengthen its humanitarian activities according to the objectives and guidelines of the Nigerian WASH sector and the global IOM's WASH principles of implementing actions that are immediate and rapid; appropriate, innovative, and compatible with needs; scalable to respond to the changing dynamics of the Nigerian context; and sustainable to respond to environmental issues and the challenges of WASH resource management and environmental risk management.Management & Provision of WASH NFI Kits for the affected population through WASH Sector partners. • IOM ensures this through its common pipeline unit through robust management of procurement, transportation, prepositioning, replenishing, proper storing, and dispatching of WASH essential items. Agencies that deliver frontline services can access the items in as little as 48 – 72 hours and provide the crisis-affected population with lifesaving assistance. Prepositioning • Common pipeline intervention is also an effective solution to tackle the complex logistical context common in many humanitarian emergency responses. In Northeast Nigeria, access to remote locations has been a challenge due to a lack of infrastructure, seasonal changes, and security constraints. In addition, the roads in many parts of Northeast Nigeria are only accessible during the dry season. Prepositioning basic WASH NFI in critical locations will help respond to cholera outbreaks during the rainy season.Accountability & Monitoring • IOM will support WASH Sector and humanitarian partners in scaling up and strengthening the humanitarian response monitoring efforts in Northeast Nigeria. IOM Common Pipeline commits to the Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) framework through its existing monitoring structure. In coordination with the WASH Sector and partners, the IOM pipeline conducts PDM. The PDMs aims to assess the appropriateness, quality, and effectiveness of partners' responses utilizing the core pipeline supplies received from IOM, identify challenges and propose recommendations for future WASH responses. Further, IOM will include the local market assessment in the PDM's methodologies. The findings will be shared with WASH Sector and partners to see if other response modalities, such as cash programming or other sustainable options, could be introduced in this area.Information Sharing • Real-time updates of pipeline stock status to inform WASH Sector partners of available critical WASH supplies in the key IOM warehouses across the BAY States will be provided. Also assists in collecting utilization/distribution reports from partners to support the Sector in ensuring that in-kind assistance has been used/distributed accordingly. An annual satisfaction survey as a periodical assessment of IOM Pipeline's performance based on the feedback of its partners is conducted. This provides valuable insights into whether IOM Pipeline performance/services satisfactorily meet partners' requirements in responding to the needs of the affected population. The resulting findings and recommendations are used to improve IOM Pipeline services and management to serve the humanitarian partners and, ultimately, the beneficiaries.Localization and Capacity Building • The pipeline will support localization efforts and strengthen the capacity of national WASH partners to respond to needs – insofar as the significant cost implications of procuring, transporting, and accessing warehousing for kits have impaired national partners’ ability to respond. In emergency response scenarios, the pipeline will prioritize the initial response to the most urgent needs within 72 hours. Additionally, IOM will develop capacity-building activities for key stakeholders, authorities, and partners, including capacity-building on humanitarian project cycle management focusing on WASH NFI Distributions and Post Distribution Monitoring (PDM) activities. 7.1.a - Please mention the HDP package and its goal, its other components and key actors, and your project’s interaction with them

IOM aims to target populations and authorities who are encouraged to take ownership of WASH programs, promoting long-term resilience and sustainability while supporting the operationalization of the HDPN.The transition and recovery of IOM WASH Nigeria actions begin in humanitarian settings by applying development principles in such settings. The IOM WASH approach aims to gradually turn the dividends of humanitarian action into sustainable crisis recovery, resilience building, and development opportunities. Durable solutions, self-reliance, and community stabilization are key components of transition and recovery, as they can contribute to peacebuilding by addressing social tensions. These factors, therefore, lay the foundations for sustainable recovery and peace and reduce the likelihood of further displacement. IOM WASH interventions are uniquely placed to deliver on these objectives by providing tangible services as an entry point for delivering basic needs while delivering on additional aspects, such as governance, institutional capacity building, job creation, and conflict resolution simultaneously. IOM Nigeria will develop this strategy by supporting WASH beneficiaries, key stakeholders, and local governance to gradually hand over WASH management and finance responsibilities to beneficiaries and regulatory authorities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Protecting the rights of persons displaced and affected by the humanitarian crisis has always been paramount during programming by IOM. The programs designed not only enhance the protection of vulnerable populations but also ensure that protection and humanitarian principles, such as do-no-harm, are mainstreamed into its programming. This continues to allow IOM to deliver assistance in a manner that promotes the protection, safety, and dignity of the affected population and provides equity to women, girls, men, and boys. As part of this project, the IOM WASH program will closely coordinate with the IOM Protection staff and community-based WASH committees during the planning, design & adaptation of the proposed activities. Protection staff will also support the training of WASH staff, most notably those undertaking community-based assessments, on how to respond to protection issues, including instances of GBV. This will ensure that protection concerns are identified early on and integrated into the implementation of the project. In addition, any protection concerns raised through the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism will also be addressed.IOM is an active member of the Protection Sector Working Group, Child Protection Sub-Working Group, and GBV Sub-Working Group and will consider any in-country standards and principles agreed upon by the Sector. In addition, IOM will integrate the global Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) and IOM standards and practices, including those outlined in the IOM Institutional Framework for Addressing GBV.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

In line with the WASH Sector and its strategies, IOM will maintain its commitment to engaging with affected communities at all phases of the program cycle. The pipeline project works directly with organizations that will implement their own projects but will also work within the directions of accountability to affected populations within their respective sectors. IOM's Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) framework ensure that each project implemented is carried out effectively and continually reviewed in line with the community needs and humanitarian frameworks. IOM will utilize feedback from previous assessments, and PDMs conducted reflecting the needs and specific vulnerabilities of the affected populations. This will ensure that the materials and kits provided can adequately support the targeted population. IOM's existing complaints and feedback mechanism will continue to encourage and provide a platform for affected communities to submit complaints through the suggestion boxes or other elements of the community-based complaint and feedback mechanism. After the project has been completed, IOM will conduct post-intervention surveys to ensure further accountability to affected populations and reinforce knowledge management practices, particularly on best practices and lessons learned.
