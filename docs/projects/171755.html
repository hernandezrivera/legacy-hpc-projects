Project Summary

Mercy-USA for Aid and Development intends to set up a project to address the urgent health needs in Banadir, Galgadud, Hiran, Mudug and Togdheer regions of Somalia. Since the collapse of Somalia's central government in 1991, repeated conflict in conjunction with poor food security, limited healthcare and nutrition service availability and has resulted in Somalia having among the worst health-related indicators in the world.
According to FSNAU-FEWSNET-2020-Post-Gu-Technical-Release, 2.1 million people across Somalia are expected to face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher) outcomes between now and December without sustained humanitarian assistance. FEWS NET’S research shows the NMME forecast has a strong capability to predict below-average rainfall during the October to December 2020 rainfall season in East Africa. Combined with the persistent threat of desert locust and COVID 19 pandemic, the impact of below-average rains on crop and livestock production is expected to lead to widespread deterioration of the crisis situation in northern and central pastoral areas and southern agropastoral areas in the absence of sustained assistance. There is an urgent need for a scale-up of the current response to ensure preparedness and increase resilience of the most vulnerable people i.e. already health compromised refugees, internally displaced persons and all for whom everyday sanitation, access to clean water, and the ability to practice social distancing may be virtually impossible.
This project will be a multi-sectoral initiative that is itself fully integrated into a broader humanitarian response crossing all sectors, implemented by Mercy-USA, other NGOs, other humanitarian actors, government actors, and the communities themselves. The project will be integrated to the maximum possible extent with local government entities, including the District Commissioner and head of government department in the district.
Mercy-USA will deliver primary health care services through 39 Maternal and Child Health Centers (MCHs). Mercy-USA will be facilitating the provision of medicines, supplies and equipment to the health facilities through UNICEF, while working together with the Ministry of Health (MOH) in each respective region to ensure standard provision of care and to build local capacity. Mercy-USA will train and support healthcare staff and Community Health Workers (CHWs) at each MCH facility to diagnose and treat common illnesses, provide safe pre-natal and post-natal care, and support vaccination and other health services. Mercy-USA will also promote local health, family planning counseling, safe-motherhood and safe-childhood initiatives through community sensitization.
Mercy-USA will ensure that people with disabilities participate in the implementation of project activities by ensuring that they are represented in all community meetings. The project will consult with PWDs on the location, design, safety, and facilities' appropriateness. During project implementation, segregated focus group discussions will be held with the community to identify gender inequalities, discuss all the genders' needs, and understand the risks faced by PWDs in accessing health services. Activities will target to address these needs during program implementation.
Access to children with disabilities and their caretakers and/or disabled pregnant and lactating women will be ensured and increased through outreach activities and behavioral change communication campaigns.
During routine and regular program monitoring and evaluation, Mercy-USA staff will collect disaggregated data by disability, age and gender to monitor the impact of programs on vulnerable groups. Mercy-USA, during both monthly and quarterly reviews and analysis of data, will seek to monitor the inclusiveness of programming by comparing achievements versus available population data.

Project Activities

• Support the provision of maternal and child health services at 39 MCHs. Each MCH will include nurses, assistant nurses, midwives, vaccinators, community health workers (CHWs). Unskilled staff will include cleaners and security guards. The project will support 39 health facilities in 5 regions in Somalia which will be going to provide primary health care including CEmONC service in 5 facilities. • Support 12 outreaches to take health and nutrition services to the hard to reach areas. The facilities will support far to reach areas through outreach teams who will be visiting each site on a weekly basis.• Support the rehabilitation of 5 new MCHs and 5 existing MCH facilities with inadequate water systems, sanitation facilities and incinerators, to ensure access to adequate WASH services in health facilities. The lack of WASH services puts those seeking care and those providing at significant risk. The risks of site-related or preventable infections among health staff and patients decrease with access to appropriate WASH services and proper waste management systems. Given the importance of water availability and good hygiene, WASH is considered both a precondition and an entry point for good quality of care. Mercy-USA will ensure the appropriate design of the water and sanitation facilities to be accessible to people with disabilities, late-term pregnant women, the elderly, and sick people to support core universal health care aspects of quality, equity, and dignity for all people.• Support the training of healthcare providers through two 5-day MCH refresher trainings. The technical healthcare workers will be trained on IMCI, ETAT and obstetric emergencies to provide quality health services to beneficiaries. This will be followed by regular on-job trainings.• Support the provision of antenatal, reproductive and postnatal treatment, services and referrals. All pregnant mothers will be given antenatal service at the facilities and they will be adived to deliver at the facilities and after delivery they will be given postnatal services.• Support the provision of communicable disease immunization, treatment and referral at MCHs and outreaches. Mercy-USA will support the provision of care for communicable diseases at 39 static Maternal and Child Health Clinics (MCHs). The focus of this program will be to treat and prevent common causes of morbidity and mortality. This program will seek to prioritize the provision of care for children <5 years of age.

Mitigation Measures

Risk that the project does not have sufficient internal mechanisms to combat and prevent child safeguarding and PSEA:• Mercy-USA, guided by internal PSEA and Child Safeguarding Policy and international best practices, will safeguard gender justice and equity and promote gender equality. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities. The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk. • Mercy-USA will carry out mapping exercises on a semi-annual basis to understand marginalized communities in the areas of operation and their needs. • Data collected for program implementation will be segregated in gender and disability, ensuring that the program reaches marginalized communities. • Mercy-USA will ensure that formal confidential feedback and complaints mechanisms are accessible to all community members, especially marginalized communities. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms. The following mitigation measures will be applied to address this risk. Mercy-USA will have three forms of confidential feedback mechanisms to ensure that all community members are reached, including illiterate people, those who cannot physically travel, and finally, those who do not have resources to feedback. The follow mechanisms will be in place:• Suggestion boxes • Hotline numbers• Focal persons at the community level. • Community mobilization will be intensively carried out to ensure the uptake of the formal feedback mechanisms established.• Mercy-USA will continuously monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of the mechanisms established and make improvements continually. Heightened insecurity that might result in interruption of program activities. Mercy-USA will mitigate this risk by implementing program activities through community acceptance and working closely with local communities to ensure safety of operations. Limited accessibility due to poor roads worsened by seasonal rains. Mercy-USA will select suitable four wheeled vehicles for program operations. Logistics department will develop a detailed travel plan that is season specific identifying the ideal periods of supply delivery. High levels of displacement due to failure of rains, floods, higher malnutrition rates, low food stocks and spike in new IDPs. Mercy-USA will mitigate this risk by tracking humanitarian conditions and advocating for additional funds if necessary to support new arrivals.Criminal threats to staff and beneficiaries in operational environment leading to robberies, destruction of property, attacks on staff, or beneficiaries. Mercy-USA will carry out regular and ongoing staff security updates and establish an active and rigorous evacuation plan detailing roles of local and international staff. Communities are forced to leave their place of residence to access humanitarian assistance (humanitarian assistance as pull factor). The following mitigation measures will be applied:• Mercy-USA will continuously map the location of beneficiaries to better target IDPs and pastoralists ensuring that services are provided at the convenience of the target beneficiary.• Mercy-USA will reach beneficiaries who are located far from health centers through the provision of outreach activities. • Mercy-USA will select CHWs from the local community, ensuring no settlement is left out in the program geographical reach.

Please provide further explanation

Mercy-USA will provide health services in facilities that are easily accessible by communities. Services at the facilities will be provided in a confidential environment with care given to support appropriate services for children, the elderly, and the disabled. All midwives hired in the program will be female, and Mercy-USA will endeavor to support a majority of staff, including nurses and Community Health Workers (CHWs) to be female in order to provide appropriate health services in the local context. Health workers will be trained on basic protection issues involving children, rights for the elderly and the disabled at the beginning of program activities in order to identify and support appropriate care responses and potential referral cases. Educational and counseling sessions held at the facilities will use appropriate pictorial cards and IEC materials to help explain messages to the widest possible audience.
