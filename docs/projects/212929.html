Project Summary

As HNO 2024,16.7 million people (50% female) are in need for assistance across Syria, which is increase from 15.3 million in 2023. As Syria enters its 13th year of crisis, individuals remain more vulnerable and exposed to negative copying mechanism due to the ongoing deterioration of the economic situation which makes people unable to meet their basic needs including access to energy. The severe shortages and disruptions in electricity and fuel supplies, further complicating the lives of over 80% of the population living in poverty and hardship conditions. The energy deficit deeply impacts various aspects of daily life i.e, hospitals face critical service disruptions which as HNO2023 30% of functional public hospitals rely on backup generators or solar energy systems to operate. While, lack of electricity in schools, affecting over 2 M children out of school. As such the shortage of fuel, particularly of diesel, affected poultry production during the summer due to the extraordinary heatwaves. It also had a negative impact on most agricultural field operations during the summer, especially irrigation, underground water extraction, food processing and transportation. According to HNO2023 73% of households reported having electricity for less than one third of the day. Among households with access to renewable energy sources, 53 per cent have access to electricity for more than nine hours per day, although it may be reliant on multiple sources such as the main network, generators or batteries to reach this figure. In 2023 AKF Syria has piloted a sustainable community-based clean energy model in Khurais village, Hama Governorate, installing a fully automated 30 Kilo Watt solar photovoltaic (PV) system. This system provides electricity to 24 households and public spaces, delivering one kW of electricity/household daily, sufficient for lighting, refrigeration, and other essential devices. AKF Syria aims to expand this successful model to serve a larger population, offering clean and affordable energy access to more beneficiaries. Through a new project in 2024, AKF aims to implement 10 microgrid solar PV systems each system about 78kW in selected villages to reach 650 households in total, which will be done through community consultations to assess energy needs, and about 2500 individuals will have access to clean energy. Feasibility studies will be conducted to evaluate technical, environmental, and financial aspects. Building local capacity will be prioritized through training programs for community members on system installation and maintenance. Collaboration with local authorities and stakeholders will ensure smooth integration of the solar infrastructure into village systems. Through rigorous monitoring and evaluation, we aim to track progress, measure impact, and continuously enhance the sustainability and effectiveness of the project for long-term benefits to the communities involved.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

AKF is committed to safeguarding including Prevention from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and has the Global Safeguarding Manual (GSM). It has processes and procedures in place to mainstream PSEA into all project cycle and activities stages. i.e., from the design to the closure.The project will develop a specific Risks’ Register to identify SEA risks and develop relevant mitigation measures to reduce the likelihoods and the impact of the risks on children and vulnerable adults. The risks’ register will be reviewed frequently to evaluate the residual risks and modify the procedures accordingly. There are Responding procedures in place to handle and address SEA cases including speak-up channels, reporting mechanisms, case management for SEA cases, investigation procedures, and disciplinary process that adopts the approach of ‘’Zero Tolerance’’. Our project will include robust measures for the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). We will prioritize the establishment of clear guidelines and protocols to safeguard vulnerable community members, particularly women and children, from exploitation and abuse. Comprehensive training sessions will be conducted for project staff, volunteers, and community leaders to raise awareness about PSEA principles and reporting mechanisms. Regular monitoring and supervision will be implemented to ensure adherence to PSEA standards throughout the project lifecycle. We are committed to fostering a safe and supportive environment where all individuals are respected, protected, and empowered to participate fully in project activities.
