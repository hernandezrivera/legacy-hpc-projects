Project Summary

The project was designed based on complementarity, efficiency, and effectiveness through its innovative approach and implementation methodology. 3 main teams will be set to directly supervise and manage the project activities; each team is responsible for a geographical cluster that consists of several sub-districts with high severity needs through 10 centralized existing learning centers to provide effective services solving major community problems in the Syrian Context. It is easily observed that basic education services are widely provided in the targeted area, but high-risks in the sector still exist, thousands of school-aged children are still out of schools, Pre-schools almost do not exist, community minorities such as children with physical disabilities are not receiving sufficient support related to their access to education, Remedial courses are mainly implemented as summer activities targeting random students that are not particularly in need for such activities.
For that, we developed the proposed methodology to mitigate those risks, through the following utilized approach:
1- Each selected center will be one out of at least 14 other learning centers/schools in a certain community.
2- The Center will be operational in the first shift using 2 vacant classrooms for the ECE and in the second shift for the other activities where it is vacant as it is originally supported by other education actors in the first shift, followed by evaluating these children when they advance to grade one to advocate this activity to the donor community.
3- Free Transportation Services will be available to support the children with physical disabilities for the first time in the Syrian Emergency Context through 14 well-designed vehicles.
4- OOSC will be enrolled in the second shift of the school to receive an accelerated learning education program.
5- Remedial Courses will be available in 3 phases, each phase for 4 months duration, and students will be selected with a very effective method through assessment of at least 400 schools’ students to pick the most critically needed children that are at high risk of being dropped out of school.

4 - Needs

Based on recent assessment (2021) 77,361 children 5-17 in NWS were assessed to indicate any type of functional difficulty, 8.8% were found to having at least one type of disability, 929 Children were found to have walking disability (541 F and 478 M). Another recent assessment covering 415 Schools, providing non-formal education activities to children aged 6-17 (Grade 1 to 12) reaching 144,980 Students. We found out that 321 Classrooms are completely vacant and unused by these schools. In addition, 121 Schools are only operating in one shift with a total of 1,214 Only 16 schools out of 415 are giving special consideration for children facing difficulties in learning. HNO 2022: Young learners are not prepared for school, only about 11 per cent of four-year-old and 30 per cent of five-year-old. From eleven years old there is a steep dropout impacting boys more than girls. Lack of access to secondary education in many areas limits the ability of adolescents to continue their education. · · Idleb has a critical confluence of negative factors: low attendance rates, the highest classroom to school aged population ratio, the only governorate with triple and quadruple shifts, nearly half the schools are non-operational and the largest proportion of children traveling +45 minutes to school and the highest rates of attacks on education. With an estimated 18% of school age children not attending any form of learning, out of school children remain one of the most affected groups.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

HRP's PRA: Learning facilities could be attacked. Attacks may include airstrikes, bombs/explosions/shelling, fighting/gunshots, arson/fire, looting/theft; kidnapping/arrest of a teacher or students, direct attack on teacher or student, intimidation of school staff/students, or military use of the school.Risk of explosives, as landmines, exploitations, harassment that students encounter on their ways to and from schools/learning facilities.Students and education personnel may encounter unexploded ordinance, crossfire, exploitations, harassment, arbitrary arrest and detainment, physical or verbal threats, etc. when they travel to and travel from learning facilities. -All children will be registered with full consideration of gender balance between girls and boys.-CwDs will be supported with transportation services.-CwDs related to learning difficulties such as Remembering, focusing, learning, anxiety, and depression will be directly targeted with Remedial Courses.-Children with at least one year drop-out up to zero schooling will be targeted with ALP to be enrolled later in the formal or non-formal education system. -Parents who are suffering from transporting their CwDs will benefit from the project and will be able to use their daily time as other parents. -Women who cannot access the labor market because of parenting children between 3-5 years old will be able to register their children in the ECE program and enroll with paid jobs or higher education.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

HRP's EDU PRA: Aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) policies should be in place, practiced and monitored. Polices should cover standards of behavior for the organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and include accountability measures. Polices should address risks at the organizational level and the program level.The targeted communities and learning facilities should be aware of the organization’s policies and procedures.The organization’s staff and targeted communities should have access to safe and effective reporting systems including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously. Organizations should ensure a survivor centered approach that ensures the safety and confidentiality of the affected person. The survivor should have a say in the processes.A poor handling of allegations of sexual explanation and abuse can put the accuser/survivor, alleged perpetrator and others at risk. If you need support in dealing with PSEA issues, please contact OCHA who has technical PSEA experts.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1: Children enrolled with AEP do not commit to the full programEnsure that the parents are planning for their children to re-join the school through interviews to have their support in case of any potential drop-out.2: OOSC not accepting to be enrolled in AEP non-formal program due to financial needs and moving towards child-labour instead. Coordinate with other actors, for a back-up plan that are working in the same targeted areas directly or through the FSL cluster to assess the families with critical needs for provision of food or cash support.3: Security issues in frontline nearby areas with potential military conflicts near the targeted areas.Keep monitoring the situation on daily basis, suspend activities in high-risk areas in case of any military attacks from any side of the conflicted parties. 4: Un-availability of qualified personnel for the ECE Program.Target teachers specialised in grade 1 and provide them with on-job capacity building courses through our trainers.5: Difficulties in approaching remote schools with data management of students through assessments to target remedial courses’ students. Coordinate with the ED and get support. 6: Risk for children to come to our centres from other schools to receive the Remedial Courses.Prioritize students that are living or studying in the same area, and appoint the teachers to support children in all risk types.7- COVID-19 Risks: Ensure that teachers are vaccinated, masks available, physical distancing.
