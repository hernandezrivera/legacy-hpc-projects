1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

WFP’s assistance to conflict- and displacement-affected communities in northern Mozambique in the context of a deteriorating humanitarian crisis has a two-fold approach: 1) Provision of life-saving food assistance through General Food Distributions (GFD) and Food Assistance for Assets (FFA) using different transfer modalities that include in-kind, vouchers and cash-based modalities,and 2) Facilitating the provision of complementary assistance for other agencies and organizations. WFP targets 750,000 people in the provinces of Cabo Delgado, Nampula and Niassa, including both IDPs and host communities. Activities will be adjusted according to the needs and conditions of the supported target groups: Recently displaced, or on the move and ‘hard-to-reach' beneficiaries: As people seek safety from violence they need immediate life-saving assistance. WFP also includes those who find themselves in hard-to-reach areas into this category. Displaced populations: IDPs in temporary centers and staying with host communities that have gone through the registration and verification process but have not been allocated land to resettle and have no access to livelihood options. Host communities and resettled populations: Resettled populations or those living with host communities who have gained access to livelihoods as well as host communities living in proximity will be assisted to alleviate pressure on shared resources and contribute to social cohesion.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

WFP priority activities are categorized per level of access constraints as outlined below: No access constraints / moderate access constraints (Nampula, Niassa and southern/western/coastal Cabo Delgado) Recently displaced will receive Immediate Response Rations (IRRs) that cover 100% of the daily requirements for 7 days for those recently displaced until they have been verified and registered. Displaced IDPs: WFP will provide unconditional food assistance to registered IDPs through a combination of in-kind food, commodity vouchers, value voucher or cash modalities considering local contexts and market assessment findings. WFP will aim to provide Family Food Rations (FFRs) of 100% of daily kilocalorie needs for those most in need and adjust assistance for those who have found other sources of income. Host communities and resettled IDPs: Resettled populations are expected to increasingly gain access to livelihoods and have reduced vulnerability levels. These beneficiaries will receive rations of 75% of the standard FFR, similarly food assistance will be provided through a combination of in-kind food, commodity vouchers, value voucher or cash modalities considering local contexts and market assessment findings. Food For Assets (FFA) activities which will be used to improve the situation of IDPs in resettlement sites. Where possible, WFP will design FFA to promote livelihoods of the affected population and contribute to social cohesion between IDPs and host communities High access constraints (Central Cabo Delgado)In areas with access constraints, WFP plans to use the Rapid Response Mechanism (RRM) as a flexible system for delivery of life-saving food assistance . The RRM will require scale-up of logistics support by WFP and will be delivered through a combination of air, sea and road logistics. Complementary activities: 1) Essential Needs Assessment: A Minimum Expenditure Basket will be calculated to ensure a shared understanding of basic needs and inform program design.2) Rapid Conflict Analysis: as the basis for conflict-sensitive programming 3) Enhance coordination and overall FSC response through capacity building of partners and information sharing and support to Cash working group.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

WFP is committed to ensuring the centrality of protection throughout the humanitarian assistance. WFP takes calculated measures to ensure a conflict sensitive approach that does not negatively affect humanitarian access in the immediate nor long term, considering the rapidly changing dynamics in the evolving conflict. WFP works with humanitarian actors through local and national level coordination groups such as the protection cluster and community engagement/ AAP working group to share and apply feedback from the affected population. Findings from surveys and other community-based assessments are analyzed and reflected in operational planning, in parallel with reports from the CFM as relevant and possible in the dynamic context. WFP is committed to ensuring zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, ensured through codes of conduct, online and face-to-face training, and several reporting mechanisms (see above). As the lead agency for Linha Verde 1458, WFP works with the PSEA network to ensure awareness of the service and to determine constructive approaches to handle SEA cases in a timely manner internally and with the Government as relevant. All SEA reports are acted on within 24 hours, prioritizing the safety and well-being of the survivor, working in collaboration with WFP’s ethics office and OIGI.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

WFP involves beneficiaries in all stages of the project lifecycle. In the design phase of this project, beneficiaries have been consulted on the choice of modality, considering security, distance from markets and food preferences. WFP has established community committees via a community election process. The beneficiaries engage in consultations via the community committee platform and via focus group discussions ensuring continuous feedback on the assistance provided. Minority and vulnerable groups are targeted for focus group discussions to ensure that they have a voice. Well established complaints and feedback mechanisms exist, consisting of help desks staffed by CP staff and complaints and feedback boxes at distribution sites. Linha Verde 1458, the tollfree inter-agency hotline, managed by WFP is accessible to the affected population, providing clarifications on beneficiary rights, capturing needs and associated gaps in the assistance as well as linking the affected population to vital services in the cases of SEA, SGBV and child protection. The field office benefits from a designated protection and AAP focal point who ensures that communications are up to date and that cases followed up on accordingly in order to close the feedback loop in a timely manner. The following activities will support AAP and are available for all agencies: 1) Beneficiary Data Management (BDM): establishing a platform that can be used to support interventions from different actors and inter-sectorial coordination.2) Beneficiary registration: using SCOPE, WFPs ‘end to end’ beneficiary data management platform. 3) Community Feedback Mechanism: WFP will continue operating an inter-agency complaints and feedback mechanism, 'Linha Verde 1458' to promote protection and accountability to affected populations.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Understanding the significant access barriers for disabled people to access timely information and attend distributions, WFP works with implementation partners and local authorities to ensure clarity on vulnerability criteria, seeking targeted assistance to directly identify and support people with disabilities to access their entitlements in a safe and fair manner. In addition, WFP works through the protection cluster and in collaboration with the disability working group to identify access barriers and identify measures to address these effectively.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

All program activities will be screened for environmental and social risks during design, at a stage when the implementation details (i.e. location, targeted beneficiaries, inputs, transfer modalities, outputs, and timeline) are being defined, but before the design is finalized. From 2020, The WFP Environmental and Social Safeguards are the set of principles, standards, and tools to avoid or manage the risk of causing environmental or social harm to people, communities, or the natural environment. The WFP Environmental and Social Standards will be used serve as a benchmark for the risk screening and risk management. All the asset creation activities planned as part of the FFA will be analyzed with WFP’s Environmental and Social Risks assessments tools. Risk screening not only helps identify environmental and social risks: Each activity will be classified by the level of the risk: low, medium, or high. Any program activity that entails some risks (medium or high) will come with risk management measures that aim to avoid or reduce the risks or mitigate or offset the expected adverse impacts.
