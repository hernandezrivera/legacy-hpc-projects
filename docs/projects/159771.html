Project Summary

Relief International (RI) will increase access to life-saving preventative nutrition services to Pregnant and Lactating Women and Girls (PLWG) and children aged 6 – 59 months of age from marginalised and displaced communities in Banadir, Hiraan and Gedo regions. RI aims to improve infant and young child feeding practices and hygiene in the target communities.
Delayed, and poor performing Gu rains across Somalia have led to widespread crop failure and reduced livestock productivity. The 2019 Gu harvest in Southern Somalia is the lowest output since 1995 . Repeated climatic shocks, including more frequent prolonged dry spells and episodic flood events, undermine food security, compounded by widespread insecurity, displacement, and poor access to basic services including health, water, and sanitation increase household vulnerability to malnutrition.
317,400 people in the three target regions are likely to experience acute malnutrition which comprises 27% of the expected malnutrition burden for Somalia. In September 2019, RI found that 7% of children suffered from severe malnutrition and 17% from moderate malnutrition (n=579) in Belet Hawa. In the proposed areas, FSNAU projects a Critical GAM rate (15-30%). In 12 health centres in the target regions, from January - September 2019, RI screened 29,149 children aged 6 – 59 months admitted or referred 4,688 (16%) for acute malnutrition. Over the same period, over 7000 children under 5 suffered from acute watery diseases (ADD) and 16, 364 from acute respiratory infection (ARI). 2.2 million people across Somalia are likely to experience IPC 3 and 4 through September. FSNAU further forecasts that between October and December 2019, 367,000 people in the target locations will experience IPC 3 and 4 or 17% of the overall projected figures for IPC 3 and 4. Despite a promising start to the Deyr season (October – December), three successive failed harvests in Hiraan and other parts of southern Somalia has undermined household resilience and will require the urgent scale-up of the humanitarian assistance to avert a further deterioration.
Aligned to the nutrition cluster objectives, RI will address the needs of displaced and rural PLWG and children aged 6 – 59 months in need of immediate and sustained nutrition interventions. Women and children comprise the majority of patients seen at the health facilities. Data collected from RI’s health centres reveal that in many cases, women and children in need of health and nutrition services travel long distances. These journeys invariably expose these vulnerable groups to protection risks, as they traverse through unfamiliar and insecure environments. Anchored to static health facilities, RI will deploy mobile health and nutrition teams to serve underserved host and internally displaced communities outside the main urban hubs. These mobile teams will increase access to lifesaving nutrition services to vulnerable people who are unable to routinely access the static health facilities districts in Gedo, Hiraan and Banadir. Mobile teams will screen PLWG and children aged 6 – 59 months, disseminate key nutrition messages, treat uncomplicated malnutrition cases, refer malnourished children as necessary, counsel mothers on IYCF/IYCF-E, initiate Mother to Mother (M2M) support groups, train others on mid-arm circumference (MUAC) measurement, and mobilize target communities around emergency preparedness plans that promote resilience.
RI will select safe and accessible sites for the mobile nutrition units after consultation with community structures, including those representing minorities, women, and youth. The sites will also be accessible to people with disabilities. Men, boys, and leaders will be engaged to support improved feeding practices at household level.
The nutrition service provision at the supported static facilities with the mobile teams will reach 192,200 PLWG and children, predominantly from IDP and underserved rural and remote households.

Project Activities

RI will implement the following activities in alignment to the nutrition cluster strategic objectives:- Train community nutrition workers and health facility staff on providing IYCF counseling and messaging to beneficiaries [target: 40]- Counsel pregnant and lactating women on appropriate IYCF techniques [target: 10,000]- Provide boys and girls aged 6-59 months with MNPs and Vitamin A supplements [target: 10,000]-Provide pregnant and lactating women micronutrients including iron folate [target: 4000]- Screen PLWG for acute malnutrition [target: 27,000];- Screen children aged 6-59 months for acute malnutrition [target 60,000];- Treat children 6-59 months with severe acute malnutrition [target: 3,000];- Treat children 6-59 months with moderate acute malnutrition [target: 9000]- Train a select group of mothers on MUAC measurement [target: 60];- Establish M2M support groups [target: 10]

Mitigation Measures

1b:In order to receive nutrition services, beneficiaries will need to leave their homes. The majority of cases will include PLWG and children. As a result, RI will ensure sites are:- Accessible- Operating in daylight hours- Being mobile units, RI will ensure sites selected will service multiple communities - especially those marginalised or on the fringes of towns/cities/districts/societies.- RI will ensure that beneficiaries are not required to access parts of a city or town they wouldn't otherwise normally for fear of safety/protection- RI will engage and encourage husbands to accompany their wives and children when attending nutrition units. 2b:This project is directly seeking to reach women, girls and boys in need. As such RI is aware of the fact that these groups attribute the majority of malnutrition cases. Therefore, RI will consult past projects, local health staff, community leaders and religious leaders in determining where best and how best to include women, girls and boys in decision processe.- RI is cognisant of the fact that decisions do not usually rest with women and children. Mobile medical units will actively seek to meet the needs and wishes and requirements of these groups with the use of their input into service provision (learning from them what works well).- Use female community health workers to go out into communities and meet and speak with women and their children regarding project activities.-Ensure project sites are accessible and mindful of women and children from informal settlements.-Ensure sites are accessible and in convenient areas for people with disabilities. 3b:RI will reduce the risk of sexual exploitation by:- Engaging men/husbands to accompany their wives and children to attend mobile medical units- Select open, safe and secure sites for nutrition activities- Conduct activities during daylight hours- Ensure women and children from marginalised groups are not targeted whilst attending nutrition services.- Promote security through key community elders and religious leadrs who can influence a sense of community policing and protection for beneficiaries attending nutrition units.
