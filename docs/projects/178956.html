Project Summary

The United Nations (UN) estimates that the number of crisis-affected people in need of humanitarian assistance in Syria is 14.2 million, of which 6 million are in acute need. The above figure of people in need includes 6.7 million protracted internally displaced persons (IDPs); estimated 320,000 spontaneous IDPs return; 75,000 refugees return; 438,000 Palestine refugees and the rest overburdened host communities.
More than 75% of the number of people in need of humanitarian assistance are accessible from within Syria. Overall, 12.4 million of people are estimated to be food insecure (including 1.27 million severely food insecure) and 1.9 million people at risk of food insecurity.
In this context, FAO has designed a project to assist 120,000 vulnerable crisis-affected food insecure farming households (equivalent to 750,000 people) in Syria. About 15% of the targeted householders will be selected from those who receive food support to help them shift from depending on food assistance to producing their own food and enhancing their self-sufficiency. The objective of the proposed action is to protect and enhance the livelihoods of vulnerable farmers and IDPs through the restoration of critical irrigation infrastructures to increase household food production and security. The project outputs include:
• Output 1: Local small-scale irrigation infrastructures in the targeted governorates are assessed and light rehabilitation undertaken.
• Output 2: Capacity of technical staff and relevant stakeholders strengthened.
A training programme on enhancing irrigation management, water use efficiency in small-scale irrigation, agricultural land management will be based on FAO standards and approaches. It is expected that stakeholders and farmers (water user association) will join this training.
This project is expected to contribute to increasing access to irrigation water for crisis-affected farmers and their families in the targeted areas through the light rehabilitation of local small scale irrigation infrastructure. Cultivation and other agricultural practices, including livestock and animal feed production, will consequently benefit from the water supply plans as an emergency response to crisis. The proposed intervention will strengthen household food production capacities and knowledge and address immediate food security and nutrition needs through increasing local availability and supply of food. This may in turn promote flourishing of agricultural based livelihoods in the rural community, which would contribute to social cohesion, peace and stability. The project seeks to utilize intensive labour in order to maximize community participation and inject cash into the local community.
FAO as part of the CWG will discuss at project level with the group to identify the rate, modality, etc. of the cash intervention to be in line with the CWG and to achieve the purpose of the intervention

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria is in its tenth year and the humanitarian situation continues to deteriorate. According to the UN estimates for 2021: 14.2 million Syrians will require humanitarian aid in the coming year, of which 12.4 million are food insecure (including 6.0 million severely food insecure), while a further 1.8 million are at risk of becoming acutely food insecure. Similar to other key sectors, the crisis has fragmented the structure of the agriculture sector, degraded soil and water resources, reduced agricultural production and thus lowered its contribution to national income, seriously affecting stability of rural and farming society and affecting food and nutrition security. The financial cost of the damages and losses to the agriculture sector from 2011 to 2016 was estimated to be US$ 16 billion (FAO Agriculture Damage and Loss Need Assessment 2017). Agricultural infrastructure has suffered extensive damage, theft and neglect since 2011. Persistent dry spells and drought has further worsened agricultural conditions. The result has been the disruption of large sections of the agriculture system with consequent substantial weakening of its components, including farmers who rely on irrigation systems. The Ministry of Water Resources estimates that Syria’s average annual water requirement is about 17.743 billion m3, of which 15.613 billion m3 (88%) is required for agriculture. The average annual deficit (about 1.533 billion m3) increased to reach 5 billion m3 in the recent dry years. It is estimated that 25% of the irrigation infrastructures are damaged especially in the major areas of wheat production. 5 - Implementation of activities

Output 1: Critical Local small-scale irrigation infrastructures in the targeted governorates are assessed and light rehabilitation undertaken. Activities: 1)Conduct field missions with the MoWR, GCWR, and MAAR to the main accessible irrigation projects in the targeted Governorates and assess the irrigation infrastructure damage and needs. 2)Agree with counterparts on a priority list for urgent interventions to rehabilitate critical irrigation infrastructures and introduce efficient irrigation systems.3)Development of detailed beneficiary selection criteria in consultation with key partners and stakeholders, including gender needs analysis; participatory identification of neediest and most vulnerable groups;4)Formalize, finalize agreement with pre-selected implementing partner. 5)Rehabilitate the prioritized irrigation infrastructures.6)Monitor and evaluate the project activities in a timely and effective manner Output 2: Capacity of technical staff and relevant stakeholders strengthened. Activities:1)Prepare training programme and capacity building materials.2)Establish water users association 2)Select beneficiaries upon developed criteria, giving priority to women-headed households and youth.3)Conduct a short training programme on good agricultural practices, water management, maintenance and operation of irrigation schemes .4)Conduct a short, practical field training on project-relevant features, including: Practical support of installation and operation of drip irrigation systems and solar panels and practical support for operating and maintaining of small-scale irrigation systems. While such activities will benefit the entire population, they will focus locally through a gender-based approach.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk: Beneficiaries being targeted as a result of being selected for project/Risk of community tension arising from beneficiary selection. Mitigation measures: Inclusion of different categories of affected population (for example: IDPs and host communities; returnees and refugees; men, women, boys, girls and youth) based their specific needs and clear selection criteria agreed upon by local committees.2) Risk: Inclusion and exclusion errors. Mitigation measures: Establishing a local beneficiary selection committee with equitable representation of vulnerable groups; putting in place clear and precise guidance on beneficiary selection criteria and process; ensuring effective communication for guidance in local language.3) Risk of diversion of inputs/assistance. Mitigation measures: Establish a community-based beneficiary selection and community-based monitoring.The design of the project has taken into account protection elements, in line with the do no harm principles, including GBV and PSEA risk mitigation measures. Measures will be taken to limit the risk of beneficiaries being targeted because of the being selected for the project. Women and youth will be prioritized, inputs and trainings will take account of the different requirements of women and men, boys and girls. Returnees will also be targeted so that re-settlement is supported. Where IDPs are selected, representatives from the host communities will be included as well to limit possible friction between the two groups. The implementation of field activities will be in line with the principles and guidelines on protection developed by the Protection Cluster. 8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1.Risks: Deterioration of the security situation precluding access to areas of implementation. Probability: Likely. Impact: Severe. Mitigation measures: Preventive: continue to gather information from range of sources; advocate with RC/HC and other stakeholders to regain access; develop contingency plans in coordination with service providers and, if necessary, move activities to alternative locations.2.Risks: Major displacements that could put more pressure on livelihood strategies and/or limit the impact of activities. Probability: Possible. Impact: Insignificant. Mitigation measures: Continue advocacy in order to scale up programmes to cover the additional requirements to promote livelihoods opportunities, conduct advocacy with other organizations to include new arrivals in assistance programmes.3.Risks: Changes in the conditions related to cross-border movements and operations. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Moderate. Mitigation measures: Ensure that service providers have capacity to deliver the programme within the changed legal framework, diversify activities complementing international procurement with local procurement and ensure different delivery modalities (scaling up cross lines to balance reduced cross borders).4. Risk: Limitations on partnership agreements. Probability: Unlikely. Impact: Major.
