Project Summary

Overall objective: To enhance the safety and security of communities in Darfur at risk of violence and promote nonviolent responses to conflict
Specific objective 1: To strengthen and mobilise inclusive community protection networks within high-risk communities to enhance the scale and impact of existing and emerging local community self-protection initiatives, processes or infrastructures
Specific objective 2: To provide direct protection to civilians from imminent threats of violence
Specific objective 3: To increase and improve the responsiveness of actors with a mandate or responsibility to protect civilians as well as third party service providers
This intervention will operate on three fronts (local communities, duty bearers and wider advocacy) with the primary focus being on equipping communities with the skills necessary to prevent, mitigate and address local-level conflicts. While the national political and military context continues without clear direction, focusing on local level options for protection and security is the most opportunistic way to move forward.
Direct Activities – community infrastructure
NP’s teams will work with communities under threat of violence, to strengthen their ability to self-identify key threats that could impact them and working collaboratively to find ways to protect themselves. This will be conducted primarily through continued support and upskilling of Women’s Protection Teams (a tried and tested methodology in Zamzam camp), Youth Protection Teams and Early Warning Early Response groups. NP considers Women Peace and Security to be a core part of its work, as gender inequality so often perpetuates the cycles of violence that the organisation works to interrupt. Accordingly, this action engages the four pillars set out in SCR 1325: participation, protection, prevention, and relief and recovery. UCP encourages women to actively participate in peace and security processes, drawing them into the security domain and equipping them with the tools necessary to engage in peace and security processes, and to protect themselves and their communities.
Direct activities – presence
Further, the team will also provide direct protection where appropriate to individuals or groups who are specifically threatened. This will take the form of accompaniment where necessary and patrols in areas highlighted to be of particular risk to the community.
Direct activities – humanitarian access
Continuing the work of 2023, NP will also makes itself available to support humanitarian access for the wider national and international community where there are opportunities to do so. This may involve accompanying other agencies to locations inaccessible to them, supporting coordination or negotiation between organisations and armed actors or communities or providing training on conflict sensitivity and protection mainstreaming for service providers.
Direct activities – key stakeholders PoC understanding and facilitation
NP will continue to work directly with duty bearers, community leaders and armed groups, to bring about improvement in understanding and infrastructure necessary to protect civilians, as well as support communities to make their own relationships with duty bearers. Key stakeholders include the Darfur Mediation Committees, joint forces, local commanders, engagement with the PCC and with local tribal structures.
Advocacy – protection monitoring
Protection monitoring in support of UNHCR’s overall national initiative will form a core part of NP’s programming. This will cover Zamzam particularly and parts of El Fasher.
Advocacy – Analysis
Key to thoughtful advocacy and engagement from key stakeholders, particularly at the political level, is an informed and nuanced understanding of realities on the ground. NP will continue to produce and share sophisticated context and conflict analysis to support advocacy and policy making at the national and international level.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

All project activities are designed to protect civilians and reduce violence, and thus inherently prioritize their safety. NP’s principle of ensuring the “primacy of local actors” ensures that the beneficiaries remain the central participants in all of NP’s programming, and promotes their self-protection capacities. Activities such as creating safe spaces for conducting strategic/protective accompaniments for civilians to access authorities or security actors, are prime examples of this methodology. Such an approach prioritizes the dignity of beneficiaries, treating them as partners in implementation. The proposed programming is designed to enable equal and impartial access to services. Vulnerable subsets of the community, such as women having to travel long-distances, older persons or persons with disabilities who are less able to protect themselves, minority ethnic groups who can easily be overlooked in other types of aid are the starting point of specifically tailored activities. NP also engages with community leadership and advocates for the inclusion of the concerns of these groups, or when possible representatives of these groups to be included in leadership structures. The protection mainstreaming component of the program helps to support vulnerable groups in obtaining meaningful access to assistance and services provided by other actors. NP carries out in-depth context analysis and community consultations about the impact and potential effect of our work, prior to and throughout program implementation. Field staff are trained in protection mainstreaming. Furthermore, while NP will be directly involved in ensuring the centrality of protection at the distribution, the Team will also support humanitarian actors in protection mainstreaming, such as identifying and prioritizing vulnerable individuals, analysing the areas where distribution sites are being set up for protection concerns, participating in negotiations with local authorities about distributions as well as providing technical assistance in the form of training and agreement making for inter-cluster response missions (i.e. helping partners think through the impact of and commitments to hiring casual laborers, introducing protection as a cross-cutting component, identifying the activities and indicators that might put communities at risk, etc.). Trainings will target national implementing staff and volunteers of agencies and local community structures to enhance knowledge and practical capacity in protection mainstreaming. Training curricula includes definitions of protection, international standards, including “do no harm” and principles of humanitarian aid, confidentiality, ensuring the safety and security of beneficiaries in all activities and a planning session for how agencies can improve the way protection is mainstreamed within agencies and local community structures.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will utilize regular community feedback for continuous improvement and adaptation of activities. This process involves recurring, timely, and accessible information sharing, often concurrently with project activities. Such an approach seeks to ensure that all project stakeholders, including local authorities, community leaders, and community members of different genders, ages and backgrounds, are well-informed about the project's goals, their rights, NP’s requisite standards of conduct and how they may participate in project design or adjustments. Emphasizing the critical role of transparent communication, NP clearly outlines mechanisms for stakeholders to offer feedback and lodge complaints, along with detailed procedures on how these inputs are processed and addressed. For example, NP has launched a platform called Speak Up. This reporting platform can be accessed by anyone, online, by phone or email. People can report in any language, safely and confidentially. Reports are sent to an external party who translate/transcribe reports and provide these back to NP. The Speak Up email address is monitored by NP’s ED and Head of People and Culture, and may also be monitored by a third party or a separate staff member. Platform allows for settings and permissions for various roles when dealing with reports, including that of the board, which provides sufficient independence. Recognizing diverse needs within communities, NP formulates specific localized strategies to encourage active participation in defining and adapting project activities. This includes regularly organizing key informant interviews and gender-segregated focus group discussions for men, women, boys, and girls. These discussions are tailored to uncover their preferred channels for providing feedback or reporting issues. These interactions provide a platform for stakeholders to engage in dialogue, make decisions, and directly influence the project's direction, ensuring alignment with the five commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) as stipulated by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). This multifaceted approach to community feedback not only seeks to foster a sense of project ownership among community members but also ensures that the project remains responsive and adaptable to the evolving needs and expectations of the community.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

At the core of NP's mission is a steadfast commitment to accountability towards the people we serve. Embracing the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) framework, NP embeds its five key elements into all programming and operations: Leadership/Governance, Transparency, Feedback and Complaints, Participation and Design, and Monitoring and Evaluation. These principles, in conjunction with adherence to SPHERE standards and Do No Harm approach, are integral values for NP staff working with civilians. NP interprets AAP as a commitment to using power responsibly, being answerable to, and held accountable by, those we aim to assist. This includes a dedicated focus on supporting inter-agency efforts in accountability and feedback processes, particularly in addressing complaints of sexual exploitation and abuse. Understanding the critical role of robust feedback and complaint mechanisms, NP has developed a comprehensive strategy that incorporates these elements as foundational to both safeguarding and enhancing program quality. This strategy includes provisions for whistleblowing, underlining our commitment to accountability and responsiveness in all areas of operation. For example, The Speak Up mechanism provides a confidential channel for community members to lodge complaints, including those related to Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). Operated through an independent third party, this system ensures that complaints and feedback are received, transcribed, and then relayed to NP's headquarters. Responses are crafted and communicated back to the complainants. When necessary, an investigation team is assembled to examine the legitimacy of complaints and determine appropriate actions. Central to this is the thorough training of all staff in safeguarding practices, equipping them to interact appropriately with children and vulnerable adults and adhere to mandatory PSEA policies. NP upholds a zero-tolerance policy towards PSEA. Every staff member is required to understand and sign a code of conduct and complete an online course on preventing sexual exploitation and abuse before assuming their role. The senior management team foster a culture where staff are encouraged and obligated to report any suspicions or concerns. NP’s safeguarding mechanisms include a Safeguarding and Accountability Committee that was formed by the Board of Directors to ensure governance level oversight. This committee has implemented a Safeguarding Children and Vulnerable Adults Policy that reiterates provisions in the NP Code of Conduct and covers the prevention and protection of children and vulnerable adults from all forms of abuse, particularly sexual exploitation and abuse. The policy also requires that all staff members attend a mandatory safeguarding training within the first two weeks of employment by the organisation and attend mandatory safeguarding training annually thereafter.
