Project Summary

The proposed project targets at providing life saving and essential health and nutrition services for conflict and disaster affected people in need in South Darfur, East Darfur and South Kordofan States. The targeted localities are Gereida, Kass and Buram in South Darfur; Adila, Abu Jabrah and Bahr Al Arab in East Darfur; Abu Jubayhah, At Tadamon-SK in South Kordofan. The project will directly support 15 health facilities with deployment of health workers, provision of essential supplies and standardized service delivery. It will also deploy 4 mobile health units to reach out to hard to reach areas with life saving health and nutrition services. A total of 256000 beneficiaries will directly benefit from this project. These beneficiaries include all population groups, IDPs, host communities, women, men, people living with disability and children. The services will be tailored in a way that include curative, promotive and preventive activities. The approach is comprehensive including linkage of services from community outreach to secondary health care ensuring continuum of care at all levels. The project will also contribute to improve the capacity of local communities and MOH towards enhancing prevention and health care seeking practices, mitigating and responding disasters and outbreaks. This humanitarian intervention will be delivered with close support and collaboration with MOH, UNHCR, COR and the local community and other partners. Accountability measures, including feedback mechanisms, will be established at all levels to ensure that beneficiaries are receiving quality services meeting their needs with their active participation. The project will also rely on core humanitarian principles and common humanitarian Standards. The project activities and indicators are aligned with the overarching humanitarian framework and objectives. Robust monitoring and quality assurance mechanism will be laid out to ensure the success of this project.

1 - Needs

Medical Teams International used information collected from the state and federal Ministries of Health, implementing partners, cluster and relevant health data and information. The targeted localities have high number of people in need and unmet gaps with health and nutrition services. In addition, according to HNO 2022, the health and nutrition problems in the localities are so severe that without any humanitarian assistance, the people will continue to face high level of morbidity and mortality. The health systems in conflict-affected states remains weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic life-saving health and nutrition services, and it is unable timely respond to outbreaks or nutrition crises. In disasters affected areas, many health facilities, as high as 30%, are not fully functional based on accepted standards. Health workforce distribution in Sudan is not equitable. 70% of health workers work in the urban areas with 38% in the capital, Khartoum. Moreover, 67% of the staff works in secondary and tertiary care creating a significant gap in primary health care provision. Most facilities lack the skills and supplies to diagnose and treat Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs), mental health problems and traumas. This is also linked with inadequate and untimely management and referral of complicated cases to the next level of health care. One and a half million pregnant women lack access to both Essential obstetric care, Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care services (BEmONC) and Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care services (CEmONC). Only 13.4 per cent of women across the country had their deliveries in health facilities during 2021 with 40 per cent of the home deliveries were not attended by a qualified healthcare person. Measles vaccination coverage declined by end of 2020 to reach 67 per cent with 29 localities across the country reporting coverages of less than 50% mainly in South Darfur and South Kordofan. The total reported suspected cases reached 1,252 cases including 21 deaths. There are also other outbreaks like AWD, malaria and viral hemorrhagic fevers that occurred frequently and affecting displaced communities. Malaria currently accounts for up to 25% of medical consultations. Recently, hepatitis E outbreak has been reported in Darfur and East Sudan. Disease surveillance remains low as only 30% of health facilities are reporting. According to the latest S3M (2018) survey, 13.6% of under-five children are acute malnourished; and 4.4% of these have severe acute malnutrition. 79 localities had very high (15% and above) prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) of which 9 localities in four states (East Darfur, South Darfur, Red Sea and River Nile) had catastrophic levels. Good IYCF Practice, appropriate diet diversity and meal frequency are 23%, 24% and 63.4% among children under two years of age. Malnourished children face heightened mortality risk. Children under five with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) are estimated to be 12 times more likely to die than their well-nourished peers. The coverage of CMAM, including SC, remains low despite huge needs in targeted localities. In all health and nutrition problems, women, children and elderly, people with disabilities remain to be highly vulnerable and disproportionally affected. Chronic underinvestment in health and nutrition together with recurrent crises resulted in inadequate referral means and poor health infrastructure. Recurrent floods, the COVID pandemic, the political uncertainty, climate change/drought conditions, economic crisis with soaring inflation, shortage of supplies at country level all contribute to increasing the risk of health and nutrition related morbidity and mortality among the vulnerable people living in targeted areas which are recurrently affected by conflicts and other disasters. The severity of health and nutrition problems is high and very high in the targeted localities as per the current HNO.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

To mitigate the environmental impact associated with (rehabilitation of clinics, waste from RUTF packaging, drugs etc.), Medical Teams International will support collection all RUTF waste packaging from beneficiaries. Medical waste management will be ensured in all clinics using MOH and acceptable standards. Medical Teams International will follow WHO and national medical waste management guidelines.. All rehabilitation will take into consideration the environmentally friendly materials. Expired drugs will be disposed in collaboration with local MOH officials. Training and awareness sessions with health workers and communities’ environmental awareness will include sessions on the correlation between a safe environment and improved health status.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The safety and security of both beneficiaries and staff are central to all of Medical Teams Projects. All Medical Teams staff will be trained on prevention of sexual exploitation at all levels. The health facilities will be developed and managed in close coordination with local leaders and government agencies and Medical Teams works to maintain a positive presence in the communities in which it works.
