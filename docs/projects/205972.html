Project Summary

Greater Tonj comprising of Tonj East (TE), Tonj North (TN), Tonj South (TS) and Greater Kapoeta: Kapoeta East (KE), Kapoeta North (KN) and Kapoeta South (KS) counties in Warrap and Eastern Equatoria States respectively, have persistently suffered consequences of various forms of humanitarian emergencies that affect health care. There is a total of 1,257,751 people (614,518 in Greater Tonj and 643,233 in Greater Kapoeta) living in these two separate regions. The people are predominantly cattle-keepers and growing crops as means of livelihoods. Flooding is frequent and often results in internal displacement and hindering access to health facilities.
Malaria is the most common health problem, followed by respiratory tract infections, and acute watery diarrhea. The rainy season, extending from June to September every year – has marked increase in consultations due to malaria.
Health Care is based on the delivery of the Basic Package of Health Services through 64 health facilities, which include 3 County Hospitals, 16 PHCCs and 45 PHCUs across the 2 regions. Access to these facilities remain a challenge when going further into the rural areas, with women and children bearing the burden. For example, Marial Lou Hospital (the only hospital in Tonj North County) of Greater Tonj which served minimally as CEmONC centre, stopped since 2022 due to lack of support. As a result, Tonj Civil Hospital the only Hospital in Tonj South has been working as the only referral hospital for that region in Greater Tonj.
With HRP2024 support, we propose to reach 81,420 (59,437 in Greater Tonj and 21,983 in Greater Kapoeta) people in the two regions. In TE (12,482) – 1,336 girls, 1,285 boys, 5,029 women, and 4,832 men; In TN (34,473) – 3,691 girls, 3,548 boys, 13,889 women, and 13,345 men; and TS (12,482) – 1,336 girls, 1,285 boys, 5,029 women, and 4,832 men. KE (13,190) – 1,412 girls, 1,357 boys, 5,314 women, and 5,107 men; KN (3,297) – 352 girls, 340 boys, 1,328 women, 1,277 men; KS (5,496) – 588 girls, 566 boys, 2,214 women and 2,128 men. Total of 8,715 girls, 8,381 boys, 32,803 women and 31,521 men.
The funds that will be required to complement our interventions to reach these targets is $500,000 cash. Additionally, we hope to access non-cash supplies of emergency kits from various other pipelines – WHO for IEHK, UNFPA for MISP supplies and UNICEF for vaccine and other medical kits. We will collaborate with other partners (IOM and NGOS) for additional support on other WASH needs at facility.
Amref has project staff of mixed expertise in health services for clinical, maternal and child health, in addition to support team on supply management, monitoring and evaluation We will collaborate with local authorities to ensure effective feedback mechanism and collaboration with beneficiaries to ensure social accountability and conflict sensitivity planning.

1. Needs

Greater Tonj and Kapoeta comprise of the 6 counties of (Tonj East, Tonj North and Tonj South) and (Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North and Kapoeta South) of Warrap and Eastern Equatoria States respectively, in South Sudan. National Bureau of Statistics (NBS, 2023) estimates that the area of the two regions has a total population of 1,257,751 (614,518 in Greater Tonj - (193,459 in TE, 276,371 in TN, and 144,688 in TS) and in 643,233 in Greater Kapoeta - (303,982 in KE, 191,459 in KN and 147, 792 in KS) with children under 5 years of age comprise 21% (264,126) while women of childbearing age constitute 25% (314,438) of the population. The vast rural population mainly practices cattle-keeping and growing crops as means of livelihoods. Flooding is frequent because the area is generally low land, and this often results in internal displacement and poor crop yields. According to the DHIS2 data for 2022, only 15,039 women attended 4th Antenatal Care (ANC4) visits and 9,914 deliveries were conducted by Skilled birth Attendants in health facilities. The most common causes of morbidity are infectious diseases: with Malaria accounting for 169,603 of community consultations by Boma Healthcare Workers, followed by respiratory tract infections of 100,343 and acute watery diarrhoea 87,881 resulting in a total of 357,827 combined consultations in the year. The rainy season, extending from June to September every year – has marked an increase in consultations due to rampant Malaria. Health care in Greater Tonj and Kapoeta is mainly through a network of 64 health facilities, which include 3 County Hospitals, 16 PHCCs and 45 PHCUs across the six counties. The services include maternal and child health - safe motherhood, treatment of common illnesses and routine immunization, as well as COVID- 19 vaccination. Tonj and Kapoeta Civil Hospitals are the only referral facilities in the regions providing Comprehensive Emergency and Obstetric and Newborn Care (CEmONC) as well as general surgeries. Women prefer to deliver at home under the care of traditional birth attendants due to long distances and inadequate delivery equipment in health facilities. Consequently, there are delays in referrals to the CEoMNC centers, which result in poor maternal and neonatal outcomes. Uptake of vaccine preventable diseases remains critically low, affecting pentavalent and measles coverages. Among children under one, only 51,885 completed three recommended doses of pentavalent vaccine and 50,766 for measles vaccine according to DHIS2 data of 2022. This is largely due to factors that either interrupt service provision or limit access to facilities, including low care seeking behavior in the community and long distances from facilities leading to defaulter rates; recurrent intercommunal violence causing displacement; and flooding causing inaccessibility. High staff turnover compromise sensitive program activities such as routine immunization, antenatal care, skilled deliveries, and consultations targets. Women and children are most affected by the situation, being strongly exposed to armed actors and gender base violence. In line with HRP 2024, our program aims to improve lifesaving interventions to address emergency health needs of under five children, women and returnees. We will design the approach to integrate Primary Health Care services nutrition and other protection, Education and WASH responses; create a viable synergy of referral system and promoting a community-based approach. Basic health trainings on immunization in practice, MISP packages for SRH in emergencies, and other PSEA rules will be critical. Amref will ensure protection and gender are central to design, implementation and monitoring of health activities. Integrated outreach will be in areas where there is no health centers to ensure ease of access to services. The health needs for children (girls, boys) and women will be prioritized.

2. Project activities

In line with the needs identified, Amref proposed to support the implementation of the following activities;• Establish an Outreach Team, who will act as an Emergency Response Team for each County.• Strengthen immunization services in remote communities, among displaced persons and Point of Entry - PoE.• Strengthen skilled birth delivery through deployment of qualified personnel.• Conduct training of health workers on MHPSS, MISP for SRH in Emergency.• Provide curative services at health facilities and outreach sites (as integrated outreaches)• Facilitate acquisition, transportation and last-mile delivery of Interagency Emergency Health kits (IEHK)• Strengthen stakeholder coordination meetings in the Counties of Greater Kapoeta & Tonj in collaboration with the CHDs and partners.• Conduct community meetings with community leaders and groups• Facilitate IDSR reporting and support data verifications• Facilitate Health activities in Inter-Sectoral Needs Assessments - ISNA with other sector teams
