Project Summary

AFH has been supporting Mahmudli's school camp since 2018. 4 phases have been executed in the school supporting more than 729 (350 M – 379 F) students aged from 6 to 18 in terms of education and protection. Students with disabilities have been given special preparations like focus from teachers, Separate bathrooms with ramps and case management in case of CP cases through our case managers and social workers as AFH is part of the protection sub-working group. The school consists of tents that include classrooms as well as administrative offices.
The absence of schools inside the camps has made UNICEF’s Self-Learning Program (SLP) the only replacement for learning among the displaced children. The local community of the IDP camp have appreciated the work conducted by AFH and has advocated for the continuation of the school to grant a chance for their children to obtain their universal right of obtaining a proper Education, as well as the continuation of education among their intermediate children. During the previous phase, the school provided educational support to more than 729 children while there is a need for more than 3000 children in need of education.
The project aims to bring back the students that have dropped out of school earlier due to the lack of available intermediate level, and to follow up with the students that are studying at the primary level to continue their study in the next stage and avoid illiteracy among the society, also to keep the children from facing protections risk.
The implementation will be through dividing children in school into (3) three levels. The level of each student is determined based on the decision of a committee formed specifically for this purpose, regardless of age. The target of the project is 500 (250 M- 250 F) students, of which 15% will be PWD, the type beneficiaries are IDPs living in Mahmudli camp. The project will mainly target children who dropped out of school after finishing the 6th grade and found no school or learning centre to join after finishing the elementary level.
The duration of the project and the study period in the centre is (7) months as the study year for the intermediate level can be covered by this period. Each level is allocated (3.5) months, and the transfer is carried out from one level to another based on a test conducted by the educational committee. The school will give the subjects (mathematics, Arabic, English, and general sciences) according to specific curricula provided by UNICEF and developed before the start of the teaching process by teachers specialized in the mentioned subjects. The educational process in the centre is managed by an education official responsible for checking the curricula established with teachers, supervising teaching and following up the work of the admission committee and transferring from one level to another.
A detailed description of the school:
The school contains (4) four theoretical classrooms equipped with chairs, tables, study chairs, whiteboards, a display screen and a projector to help present the theoretical lessons that are prepared on the principle of PowerPoint presentations and replace the practical and laboratory lessons with showing videos of the experiments during the lesson.
The day is divided into (5) lessons, the duration of each lesson is (45) minutes, and a break between the second, third, fourth and fifth sessions is carried out for a period of (20) minutes.
In addition to a special room for each of the centre management and psychological counsellor, and a break room for teachers. The centre has also an outdoor yard.
The centre is equipped with a source of clean and sterilized water in sufficient quantity and benefits and a main and backup source of electricity. The centre is provided with appropriate heating means during the winter and coolant in the summer. Provided that while in the centre, the rules of spatial distancing and personal protection should be applied.

3 - Needs

The situation in Mahmudli camp is emerging from conflict and there are no education sectors or NGOs covering the age period of 12-18 years old where the camp administration and parents of students requested from AFH through an official letter to support intermediate-level students. IDP children who were living in camps were significantly more likely to be out of school than IDP children living outside of camps, 36% of IDPs in camp aren’t attending school according to 2022 HNO. This is most likely due to the camps having insufficient or no learning spaces for children to attend. 7% of children 5-17 years were reported having a health condition, or disability, this group was composed of more boys than girls (59% boys). They likely need specific interventions to enable them to access school. These interventions would be provided through specially trained teachers and school personnel, physical infrastructure and learning materials and teaching aids. Through establishing an education centre that attracts age groups between 12-18 who have not enrolled in schools with the aim of including them in the coming periods. It provides the opportunity for students to experience positive interactions with each other inside and outside the classroom for children who have not received an education appropriate to their age group, with a focus on children residing in Mahmudli camp and those with special needs and humanitarian cases.Ref: Humanitarian Needs Assessment 2022- Syria Republic

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The main protection risks that might face the implementation of the project are as follows: 1- Child abuse, threat, or mistreatment by teachers or educational personnel. In this regard, AFH will make sure to train all teachers and educational staff on safeguarding and child protection. AFH will make sure that all teachers and project staff will sign the Organization's Code of Conduct. 2- Acts of sexual harassment conducted by boys on young girls in latrines or unsafe places. AFH will ensure to rehabilitate of the sanitation facilities in a way that provides privacy and gender segregation to protect girls from any SG or harassment risk.3- Services not matching the needs of people with disabilities and the difficulty of people with disabilities going to distant schools, as well as the lack of health facilities (bathrooms, wheelchairs...). in this regard, AFH will make sure that all the facilities are PWD-friendly including classrooms and sanitation facilities.

9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The main risks that might affect the execution of the project are the following: 1- Parentresistance to children's education and especially girls as a result of negative coping mechanisms such as child labour for economic reasons or forced and early marriage as a cultural and traditional reason. In this regard, the AFH team will conduct several awareness-raising sessions for caregivers about the importance of education to their children. Furthermore, the team will engage the community leaders and the elderly in advocating for children's access to education.2- Lack of qualified teachers in the area. This risk can be mitigated throughout the capacity building program that would be integrated into the project so that recruited teachers will undergo a package of training on PSEA, SEA and GBV cases. The teachers will also receive several training in active learning, child protection, methods of dealing with children, case management, as well as protection assessment.3- possible theft of schools' assets and equipment. In order to avoid this risk, AFH will hire two security guards to ensure the protection of the school. Furthermore, the team will engage the community leaders and the well-respected individuals in the camp to increase their sense of ownership.4- Health risks such as cholera, and Covid-19 are mitigated through daily health awareness sessions, and focusing on spatial distancing and providing personal protective equipment such as sterilizers.
