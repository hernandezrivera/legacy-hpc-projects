Project Summary

QC will conduct an identification/ assessment of existing water sources through a systematic assessment/ mapping, including the functionality status, major issues (if not functional) and estimated number of populations benefitting from the water source in the targeted areas. Based on the results of the assessment, the project will provide safe water fitted by submersible pumps, for communities that do not have access to water stations. QC will choose to repair/reconstruct water sources over constructing new water facilities.
In order to monitor the effectiveness of this result, reports will be maintained by assistant engineers, tracking the number of IDPs/host communities who have access to safe drinking water, and the percentage of beneficiaries who have access to functional water sources. In addition to this, water quality tests will be conducted and the number of main stations and sub stations which are functional will also be monitored.
Fuel will be required for running the generators, which operate the pumps, powering the movement of water to cities, from the water stations. Engine oil, oil and diesel filters are necessary for the ongoing maintenance of these generators. The electrical panelboards are the electrical circuitry running the water station, which control the operation of the generators.
Water will be treated with chlorine, to ensure the safe consumption by beneficiaries. Additionally, dosing chlorine pumps provide the correct amount of chlorine treatment, just outside of the water well, in order to ensure that water is appropriate for human consumption. Water testing will be completed on a daily, weekly and monthly basis, in order to ensure water quality. Water samples will be taken at random locations throughout the entire water network and tested for Free Residual Chlorine (FRC) and microbiological presence, ensuring that the water is pathogen free.
QC will meet with the local council and local water authority to explain how the repair and rehabilitation of the systems will be conducted, and organize trainings for them in order to continue the water pumping after the project is completed through the collection system, which secures the sustainability of the service.
For those HHs that aren’t able to connect to the city water network, metal tanks and safe and clean water tankers will be distributed to them.
The sewage systems in the most urban areas/district capitals in northern Syria, discharge to a network of sewage ponds that provide secondary treatment for sewage. In assessments carried out by QC’s team, it was found that some of the sewage systems are provided with pumps and power supply for pumping the wastewater, through the piped network to the ponds. Some of these ponds are situated alongside an existing natural water system. As long as households have access to a sufficient and reliable water supply as well as support to the pumping system, wastewater will move through the network consistently. However, there are blockages and leakages in the sewage system, as with any network. Thus, QC will support the target communities with resources to repair blockages and leaks, so the system is in good working order.
The project team will conduct hygiene promotion sessions through a hygiene promotion team to promote diseases prevention mechanisms, focusing on the most vulnerable groups, such as children < 5 years, lactating mothers and pregnant women, the elderly and households with critically ill family members, and then hygiene kits will be distributed to the targeted groups.

4 - Needs

Comprehensive WASH assessments were conducted in June-August 2019 to inform the 2020 HNO, including a country-wide individual household-level survey at sub-district level (25,644 household level interviews from 268 sub-districts across Syria) and a household-level survey focused on IDP sites in north-west and north-east Syria (2,910 household level interviews in 280 IDP sites in 34 sub-districts). Both assessments included drinking water sampling for free residual chlorine (FRC) testing at the end-user level to determine water safety. The assessments revealed that, despite overall slight improvement of WASH conditions in Syria, vast WASH related needs continuous to exist in many parts of Syria, affecting various population groups. Access to sufficient and affordable safe water, adequate sanitation and/or hygiene supplies remain a challenge for newly displaced populations, IDPs living in IDP sites , as well as other most vulnerable households with limited livelihood opportunities and access to basic WASH services. Newly displaced populations require a timely and quality response based on comprehensive preparedness, and smooth transition into regular programming, in particular in NWS where large scale movement of IDPs is continuously observed. IDPs in formal sites and informal settlements are highly dependent on continuous humanitarian assistance. Disruptions of services in IDP sites and their quality will have direct negative impact on people physical and mental well-being as well as protection consequences, specifically for women and girls. After nine years of conflict, the WASH infrastructure and service provision in many parts of Syria require significant investments, repair and operational support. WASH systems have suffered from damages owing to years of functioning at high capacity, limited or no maintenance, destructions, displacement of technical staff and effects of climate change and poor water resources management.

5 - Implementation of activities

QC will directly implement project through qualiﬁed staff as the following activities: - Rehabilitation of major water networks, such as water stations and boreholes, and the construction of new water systems for areas without access to established water facilities in communities, camps, hospitals, health centers and schools.- Provision of Fuel for water stations, Lubricants and spare parts stocks such as filters to the local water authorities- Construction/Rehabilitation of the sewage networks for communities, camps, hospitals, health centers and schools.- Capacity building to local council and water authority for operations and maintenance of water system and cost recovery system.- Hygiene promotion campaigns, with more focus on pregnant and breastfeeding women and girls, including the distribution of Hygiene kits.- Distribution of water tanks to the IDPs in camps and communities to increase the HH water storage capacity for adequate and safe water - Distribution of hygiene kits to the IDPs in camps and communities- Organize single sex focus group discussions for women, girls, boys and men to choose the location and design latrines and bathrooms

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project will carry continuous Protection Risk Analysis as part of the implementation plan. The risks might include inequality in accessing services, work safety, gender-based violence and exposure to explosive hazard. The project will mitigate the risk of inequality in accessing services by selecting of the target beneficiaries based on need assessment and that SPHERE/sector agreed standards are strictly followed and adapted to the context for each activity of the project and the project will coordinate with the stakeholders to ensure equitable access to the project activities. The project will address risks related to work safety by adopting the standard safety measures during the operation and maintenance of the WASH facilities. Workers will use the Personal Protective Equipment to minimize risk of injuries or exposure to diseases. The project will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of vulnerable groups (women, adolescent girls, etc.) to GBV. The project team will ensure women’s voices is heard during design of WASH interventions and identify any GBV risk(s). The locations of the WASH facilities will be central, accessible and in well-lit areas with good visibility of the surrounding area. staff will be trained on GBV concepts and referral pathways to properly support survivors. Women, adolescent girls and people with disabilities will be consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of each project, and put the mechanisms in place to facilitate feedback from users. For the high level of explosive hazard contamination within the proximity of WASH infrastructure to frontlines, or contamination of accumulated garbage, which potentially put humanitarian staff in danger during WASH assessment, rehabilitation works and operation/maintenance of these systems. Where relevant WASH staff will be trained on explosive hazard risk mitigation, and risk education sessions and awareness material for beneficiaries will be integrated into project.
