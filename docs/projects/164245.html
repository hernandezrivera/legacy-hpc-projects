Project Summary

The complexity of needs across Syria continues to grow and civilian populations continue to suffer the consequences of the conflict that caused massive destruction of infrastructure and institutions. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2019, this has led to an estimated 15.5 million people who are in need of WASH-specific assistance; 6.2 million are estimated to be in acute need. The status of the WASH facilities before the conflict was 100% operated water systems throughout the country. Now, only a mere 9% of the population is still served by functional wastewater treatment plants (WWTP). This can be directly linked to the fact that much of Syria’s WASH infrastructure is no longer functional. These challenges have led to increased use of unregulated water markets, which are often of poor quality and increasing the risk of waterborne illness. The 2019 HNO further reiterates that these risks are increased due to the extremely high levels of displacement; thus, affecting peoples’ wellbeing and limiting access to already lacking WASH services. The overcrowding of small living spaces has augmented the abovementioned problems and the inability to access hygiene items and little. In response to the above-mentioned problems, EPDC will provide life-saving WASH support to 26,000 (7525W, 7390M, 5584G & 5501B) displaced and war-affected people inside Syria. The focus will be on the provision of safe water, improved sanitation, and hygiene promotion in Southern areas, Dara’a. Interventions will include various activities including rehabilitation of water sources. This project plans to improve hygiene practices through hygiene kits distribution and hygiene promotion that are culturally sensitive and relevant to the identified needs among the local communities.
Water sources rehabilitation will target 16000 (4629W, 4546 M, 3438G & 3387 B).
Hygiene kits and hygiene promotion will target 10000 (2896 W, 2844 M, 2146 G & 2114 B).

4 - Needs

The main WASH need across Syria remains access to sufficient quantities of safe water. Results of free chlorine (FRC) testing indicate that countrywide only 40% of water received from networks has a sufficient level of FRC at the point of use (within both national drinking water and emergency standards of min. 0.2 mg/l). The worst situation is observed in Dar’a and Idleb where only approximately 13% of the population is served exclusively via piped water. In Dar’a water trucking is the only source of water for 35-45% of the population. Results of FRC testing for water supplied by water tankers indicates that water safety is much lower than water from networks, with only 13% of samples taken across Syria indicating a sufficient level of chlorine at the point of use. Regarding sanitation, at least 70% of sewage is untreated and at least half of the sewage systems are either not functional or only partially functional, leading to significant community-level health risks. Sanitation needs are also considerably higher among IDPs living in informal settlements and camps compared to the rest of the population, with overcrowding, cleanliness and protection concerns linked with substandard conditions of sanitation facilities frequently reported. Among people in need, there are groups with specific WASH needs including children under 2 years (7%), women of reproductive age (33%) and people with disabilities living in camps. IDP and returnee female-headed households have more difficulties to afford the majority of hygiene items in comparison with female-headed households in host communities. For instance, affordability of baby diapers for female-headed households is an issue for 17% of returnees and 8% of IDPs, compared to less than 1% of host communities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Child labor (across all activities): Children below 18 years old conduct works on behalf of the humanitarian organization, its partners or contractors. The likelihood is high. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: Ensure proper registration system for staff/partners/contractors’ laborers; add zero-tolerance disclosure on child labor in the contracts; check and register civil documentation. Provision of NFI packages: Inequality in distribution;Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. The likelihood is high. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: Promote Establishment of the adequate management plan; Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on the package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partner staff. Gender insensitive contents varieties; of kits with inconsistent items. The likelihood is medium. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: Harmonise and standardize gender-friendly hygiene kits among the sector members and indicate quantity and instruction for use; hygiene promotion.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Support to water supply systems: Risk of theft of WASH rehabilitation/construction materials such as generators and construction materials (during delivery/storage/ after installation) by armed groups and used in conflict activities. The likelihood is low. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials and rehabilitated/new facilities. Promote cost recovery systems for longer-term maintenance and ownership to infrastructure to promote community ownership. Risk of theft of chemicals such as chlorine/Sodium Hypochlorite which may be attractive to armed groups. The likelihood is medium. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: Work closely with all partners on the ground; Promote distribution stock between multiple warehouses in the target regions (for large quantities of stock).Potential exposure to the explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/ rehabilitation of water supply infrastructure (water stations, towers, distribution networks). The likelihood is medium. The impact is high. The mitigation measures: Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared; Conduct safety training with staff and contractors; Add explosive hazard awareness material to our programming.Provision of NFI packages: Sale or exchange of NFI kits at a lesser value. The likelihood is high. The impact is medium. The mitigation measures: Consult community throughout the programme cycle to determine the reasoning behind the sale/exchange of assistance and solicit their feedback with regards to solutions.
