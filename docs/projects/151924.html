Project Summary

The proposed intervention is implemented jointly by Norwegian Church Aid (NCA) and its local partners-Ekklesiya Ya’uwa Nigeria (EYN), Federation of Muslim Women Association(FOMWAN) and Jireh Doo Foundation (JDF) in Ten conflict affected Local Government Areas (LGAs) of Borno state, Nigeria.
The proposed intervention focuses on provision of life-saving emergency GBV assistance to the most vulnerable populations (women, men, girls and boys) in conflict affected areas, mainly for the new arrivals and populations residing in and in close proximity to the over-congested camp settings, as well as capacity building and increased efficiency and quality of those services. The proposed intervention feeds into the Humanitarian Response Plan 2019-2021 (HRP) and is coordinated through respective GBV sub sector cluster with lead agencies as well as other partners in the target areas. All partners are particularly focused on protection and GBV mainstreaming in order to tackle the underlying causes of GBV and discrimination. The affected populations (women, men, girls and boys) are in the center of the intervention and participatory methods are used throughout the project cycle management to ensure ownership and sustainability of project results. On top of that, NCA GBV intervention focuses in strengthening of the GBV response through capacity building and development of guidelines and trainings especially in specialized GBV programming that will allow all GBV partners in Borno state to provide better services.

Needs

Accordingly to the GBV sub sector, there are 2,964,939 people in need, among are 1,130,298 IDPs, 980,551 returnees, 527,317 living in hard to reach areas while 326,773 are host populations. The GBV HRP for 2019 targets a total of 1,281,314 individuals in Borno, Adamawa and Yobe states-Northeast Nigeria. Out of these, 1,140,785 are women and girls and 940 of the total target population lives in Borno state. There has been limited funding provided to the current 2018 HRP with up to 48.7% (OCHA October 2018), and Protection/GBV sector has received 14.3%, representing only 20.3% of the requested funding of the 2018 needs. Currently only 24.1% of the GBV targets in the HRP have been reached (Periodic Monitoring Report, ISCG January-June,2018) and between January-August 2018, the Protection sector reached 1,818,510 individuals (67% of the 2.7 million individuals targeted in the 2018 HRP). The GBV sector identified the following key gaps: (i) improvement in the quality of service provision, (ii) capacity building for field staff and (iii) improvement in field oversight. Gaps include: referrals for specialized services (30% coverage), provision of specialized multi-sectorial services to GBV survivors (14% coverage), sensitization on GBV/SEA principles, and prevention and reporting (22% coverage). The InterAction Protection Analysis (September 2018) recommended to UN agencies and INGOs; a) to invest in the prevention of gender-based violence, b) to develop targeted GBV specific, prevention strategies; including the necessary roles and contributions from various actors, c) to put in place competencies and mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation, abuse, and harassment by and of humanitarian personnel. in addition to the above, there has been and there is still increased in number of new arrivals to the local government areas including but not limited to Gwoza, Dikwa, Monguno, Ngala, Mafa, Jere, Konduga, Maiduguri. The aftermath of these arrivals leads to high camp congestion, thus increasing the rate of GBV incidences like rape, sexual harassment and abuse, early pregnancy, child marriage. The limited and scarce resources in these areas leaves the women and girls with no less option than using sex for survival and men including security actors use these periods to exploit women and girls who lack or have limited resources and means of living.The lack of GBV prevention and response activities, limited or no specialized GBV case management, limited to no knowledge of what services exist or how to access them, lack of community mechanisms to protect women and girls, and attacks by AOGs are a key issues to ensure beneficiaries, especially women and girls to access quality and live saving GBV services in these locations and in Northeast Nigeria. Most of the locations have limited GBV actors with specialised services while others have no actor with specialized GBV services including case management, CMR, referral for services, PSS, establishment of women and girls safe spaces and specialised programs for adolescent girls to raise their awareness and provide them with life skills and necessary knowledge on GBV is mostly not focused on by many actors.Most communities are not easily accessible and where they are, humanitarian actors can not fully establish programs due to security threats and where these continue, these locations could not access any services. Thus carrying out GBV mobile response activities is needed for these kind of locations to enable the beneficiaries benefit from some services. Humanitarian actors are present in almost many of the LGAs and the level of awareness raised and gained by communities including establishing mechanisms is still low, thus making it difficult for sustained knowledge and capacity of community leaders and the actors to prevent GBV. Working with communities and stakeholders on GBV prevention and local mechanisms would minimizes the challenge and ensure local leadership/drive.

Activities

- GBV response to new arrivals. NCA and its partners will while continuing with current safe spaces, will establish new one in locations where these are not available or limited to increase access of new arrival to services provided at the safe spaces.- Case management services will be provided for GBV survivors through the use of the inter agency guidelines and tools including the GBVIMS+/primero.- PSS. NCA and it partners especially EYN , will provide daily PSS activities at individual and group level at the safe spaces and other well-being activities will be provided as identified by the women and girls. the introduction of skills acquisition training will be carried out at the spaces to engage women and girls.- CMR. NCA will continue to provide capacity building to health care workers and provision of onsight technical support health facilities in close collaboration with ministry of health and UNFPA.- GBV mobile response. NCA will work close and build the capacity of its partners to enable both NCA and partners carryout weekly mobile services including case management in hard to reach areas or where services are not available/limited.- NCA and it partners will provide context and age appropriate NFIs/material support to vulnerable women and girls and including boys.- Field coordination. NCA will ensure leadership at field level and ensure coordination of all GBV activities at both community, capm and reception centres levels and will coordinate closely with sector. monthly meetings will be held and reporting to cluster.- Referral pathways will be developed in areas where NCA is a lead agency at field level through an effective service ,mapping of all services. safety audit will be done seldom and as the need arises. - NCA and its partners will ensure the capacity building of local leaders and communities and will support in establishing local mechanisms for GBV prevention.- NCA and its partners based on identified need from women and girls, will provide various empowement training and livelihood supports t women and girls who visit the safe spaces and livelihood material supports will be provided for various skills learnt. - Numerous community outreach, sensitisations and dialogue sessions will be carried out and engagement of communities and various stakeholders in GBV prevention. various media will be used to carryout these activities.- capacity building of front line workers on GBV integration, referral mechanisms and basics of GBV. field level workers will be provided with various capacity to enable them appropriately respond to beneficiaries needs and provide standardized packages at field level and ensure implementation. - NCA and its partners will also ensure appropriate feedback mechanism through CRM and will be available at the safe spaces through mobile call/ and also conduct regular focused group discussions. - NCA will implement the GBVIMS+ and provide support in the roll-out and implementation. capacity building initiatives will be done for both NCA and other DGO partners.- NCA will conduct by-monthly safety audits and the monitoring of GBV risk and measures implemented.
