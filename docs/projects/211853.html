Project Summary

Gülümseme ve Zeytin has been managing the Humanitarian Field Officers project in Northwest Syria since 2017, to provide humanitarian actors with highly needed information on the ongoing response in the region and to support OCHA with regular sectoral and inter-sectoral assessments, coordination, facilitated missions, and advocacy. This project aims to facilitate cross-border activities from Turkey into Syria through the HFOs team stationed in various locations of the NSAG-controlled areas. The objective is to gather reliable and up-to-date information from trusted local sources and disseminate it to the planned humanitarian response. In consultation with OCHA Turkey, the team will be overseen by GvZ in Gaziantep. The project comprises the following components, providing broad support for formal coordination, Information gathering and Community engagement and AAP.
1-Information gathering:
This component collects ground-level data to create reports on the humanitarian situation. This data helps with response planning and advocacy. Cluster focal points provide critical information on needs and gaps, while the HFO supports data collection related to humanitarian access constraints.
2-Community engagement and AAP:
The project team plans to engage with the affected communities, humanitarian partners, and other stakeholders by organizing meetings to gather feedback on their needs and determine a suitable response. This community engagement initiative aims to strengthen the humanitarian response. Additionally, the project will focus on enhancing the capacity of humanitarian partners in areas such as AAP, gender, and inclusion
3- Supporting the coordination process:
The HFOs team will focus on establishing and maintaining relationships with local authorities, NGOs, UN agencies, and other stakeholders to facilitate, collaboration, information sharing, and joint decision-making. They acted as key facilitators in coordinating the efforts of various humanitarian actors to ensure a well-coordinated response. Additionally, they promoted effective communication channels to ensure that all relevant parties are engaged and working together towards common goals. They will also participate in incoordination meetings to ensure effective coordination under OCHA Coordination Unit guidance.
GvZ follows ethical standards for research and data collection in crisis zones. Key principles include informed consent, privacy, confidentiality, cultural respect, and prevention of harm. the team prioritizes clear communication, potential risks and benefits, confidentiality, well-being, and data protection regulations.
The project prioritizes the safety of its team members through a comprehensive security plan that includes personal evacuation procedures. (Risks): Risks during the reporting period include limited access, military operations, kidnapping, and risk of injury. (Mitigation): GVZ has taken measures to address these risks, including updating safety procedures, securing information and equipment, and developing an emergency plan.
Risks of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), including harassment against affected persons: The organization has policies for handling sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) complaints. These policies have zero tolerance for sexual exploitation and abuse, emphasize confidentiality and a survivor-centered approach, and provide accessible reporting channels. Comprehensive investigations and support services are provided to survivors. The organization works to promote a safe environment and is constantly working to improve prevention strategies and support services through awareness campaigns about the right of beneficiaries to report any abuse and emphasizing the confidentiality of the reporting mechanism by publishing posters that include information on how to report, in addition to having designated numbers for complaints affiliated with PSEA. It is also a member of the PSEA network.
