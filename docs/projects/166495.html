Project Summary

The intervention is designed to contribute to mitigating the impact and further spread of COVID 19 amongst vulnerable internally displaced persons, and extremely vulnerable hosting communities and returnees within Born and Adamawa State. The proposed intervention targets a total of 46,251 extremely vulnerable individuals. The proposed design is intended to provide families with supplies, services and information required to enable families take timely prevention measures. More specifically, the intervention will deliver tailored hygiene promotion messages on focusing on prevention measures against Covid 19, risk communication and community engagement, provision of hand washing stations, provision of hygiene supplies (soap, water collection containers, plastic kettles, buckets), increased provision of clean water to support proper hand washing practices, training of community management committees , operation and maintenance of existing water and sanitation facilities and provision of access to dignified latrines.

1 - Needs

The Nigerian Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates around 3.6 million people in need of WASH support across Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa. In 2019 however, the response was significant underfunded at only 28.7% funded. Existing low levels of access to WASH services prior to the crisis has exacerbated the situation, while ongoing protracted displacement continues to put additional stress on already existing and limited services/facilities. The Nigerian HNO indicates that North-eastern Nigeria is highly endemic for diarrheal and other diseases due to extreme flooding during rainy season and other seasonal factors, lack of access to safe potable water and sanitation infrastructure, and other vulnerabilities arising from protracted displacement into overcrowded and under-serviced camps and settlements and underlying poverty. In December 2019, NRC conducted a Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment of living conditions and gaps within formal and informal camp settings in Maiduguri and Jere of Borno State. During the MSNA, NRC identified that only 43% of the respondents reported that all HHs have safe water for drinking, while 50% reported that all HHs have water both for washing and bathing. This underscores the lack of water sources and water not fit for drinking. Regarding safe and dignified latrines, only 36% of respondents report that all HHs have access to facilities, while 34% report that many or all the existing latrines are broken/not functional . Further Multi Sector Needs Assessments (MSNAs) were also conducted by REACH in 2019 in both Adamawa and Borno state. The relevant indicators for COVID 19 have been highlighted in the two reports reproduced in April 2020. The results from the assessments in Borno indicates that 54% of respondents wash their hands but with water only, 10% not having washed their hands a day prior to data collection, 38% of HHs reported reducing water consumption for cleaning, bathing and washing, 60% of the households with no access to bar soap and 25% having to wait for more than 30 minutes to collect water from the water sources . The results for Adamawa State indicates that that 67% of respondents wash their hands but with water only, 8% not having washed their hands a day prior to data collection, 48% of HHs reported reducing water consumption for cleaning, bathing and washing, 58% of the households with no access to bar soap and 19% having to wait for more than 30 minutes to collect water from the water sources Though the country mission had not specifically looked at COVID 19 as a potential public health risk at the time of data collection, some findings and recommendations from the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) can be linked to COVID 19 preparedness and response strategy. The Nigerian HNO indicates that North-eastern Nigeria is highly endemic for diarrheal and other diseases due to extreme flooding during rainy season and other seasonal factors, lack of access to safe potable water and sanitation infrastructure, and other vulnerabilities arising from protracted displacement into overcrowded and under-serviced camps and settlements and underlying poverty. This includes the risk of cholera, hepatitis E, meningitis and measles. The impact of COVID 19 can be severe in conditions like lack of adequate water infrastructure, congestion and underlying poverty. In terms of morbidity, Borno State has already reported a total number 71 Confirmed Cases as at 2nd May 2020 while Adamawa reported a total of 4 confirmed cases, cumulatively up to the 2nd May 2020. Testing and contact tracing is currently ongoing, the situation is currently being monitored on a daily basis and reports shared through Nigerian’s Disease Control Center (NCDC).

2 - Activities

Tailored Hygiene promotion awareness creation and community sensitizations against Covid 19 In order to ensure the target beneficiaries have the correct information to prevent a potential outbreak, NRC will engage the target communities to ensure that they understand the risks related to the pandemic and that the take appropriate measures to protect themselves. To improve on the level of acceptance of the messages disseminated, NRC will particularly engage with community leaders, women leaders, youth leaders, religious leaders, as an entry point to the communities.With this proposed project, NRC plans to increase the number of community-based hygiene promoters within the target communities. NRC will engage additional 35 community workers (gender sensitive) who will be trained and deployed to disseminate messages on prevention of COVID-19. Community based workers will be trained through the training module already developed by the Borno State Ministry of Health for non-health workers. For hand washing, reinforcement shall be done to encourage people to wash their hands with soap and water more regularly. To reinforce the message on social distancing and reduce the risk of transmission, the target beneficiaries will be encouraged to observe social distancing during distribution of NFIs, at the water collection points, in the markets and other common social facilities. Hygiene promotion sessions shall be done through door to door by trained community hygiene promoters, while observing social distancing, and group messaging shall be discouraged. Community meetings shall be done in such a manner that the participants shall observe social distance. Hand washing facilities shall be provided at the meeting venues as well as distribution points. Reinforce the enabling environment for the target beneficiaries to improve on hygiene practices To support the target beneficiaries to enhance hygiene practices, accompanying infrastructure and supplies will be put in place. Through the proposed response, NRC aims to increase the available water per person per day to support proper hand washing through water trucking and operation and maintenance of existing boreholes (this may include repair of broken water systems, as well as optimization is some cases). Water treatment, through the use of chlorine, will be done for the existing water supplies and for water distributed through trucking. Where it is not possible to find enough sources to meet the rising water demand, NRC will opt for new borehole construction and equipping, with related reticulations. Distribution of essential hygiene supplies (soap, buckets, plastic kettles, germicide, hand gloves, brooms etc.) shall be done to support more frequent proper hand washing practice. Shared hand washing stations shall be placed in strategic locations in the camps such as the entrance, communal shared latrines, distribution points and meeting points. In order to ensure proper management of the water and hygiene facilities provided, NRC will work through the community management structures such as the Water and Sanitation Committees to manage and ensure continued operation. The hand washing station shall be put under the management of the committees who will ensure they are regularly filled with water and there is soap available for use. For sanitation, proper desludging and operation and maintenance will be done, coupled with cleaning to ensure continuous access to latrines. For this response NRC will not focus on constructions but will maintain the facilities already constructed, this is considered the most appropriate response due to the rapid nature of the situation and the land access issue that regularly arise with new constructions. Should a dire need a rise to construct new latrines, NRC will do so, in close coordination with the affected communities. Latrine cleaning kits shall be provided for every new facility constructed.
