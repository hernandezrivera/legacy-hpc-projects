Project Summary

Provision of technical, financial and administrative support to the affected women, men, girls and boys in South Darfur, East Darfur, and South Kordofan states. Main activities include VSLAs establishment and support; training on business skills development and IGAs; initial capital transfer in the form of in kind assistance to selected women and men; rehabilitation of market infrastructures and committees strengthening; promoting community base management of natural resources and public services such as health facilities, education, and community centers; PSEA and GBV awareness and capacity building; training for women and youth in leadership, management, representation and coordination.

1 - Needs

The food insecurity and related indicators if deterioration have been on the rise as families face new wave economic crisis in Sudan. With an increase of more than half a million people classified as IPC phase 3 and above , the food insecurity situation in Sudan continues to worsen in 2019 due to continued economic decline and natural shocks, resulting in the increased vulnerability of people. In 2020, it is estimated that 6.2 million individuals, representing 14.2 percent of the total population, will need urgent and timely lifesaving food and livelihoods security support. The highest prevalence of food insecurity is in the 6 states that for the first time have been classified as IPC phase 3 and above, including South Kordofan (40 percent prevalence), Red Sea (28 percent), North Darfur (24 percent), Central Darfur (22 percent), South and West Darfur (21 percent). The rapidly deteriorating economic situation, conflict and natural shocks in Sudan had an overall impact on the agricultural season and the food security of all. As a result, at least 17.7 million individuals , representing 42 percent of the population suffer from some level of food insecurity. Under the Food Security and Livelihoods Sector, 6.2 million people are in need of food and livelihoods related humanitarian assistance. CARE and its national partners have conducted a number of ongoing assessments in the targeted operational areas and have confirmed the needs and priorities of the affected women and men, mainly the livelihoods and food security gaps in meeting daily needs of individuals. Proposals are developed based on these needs and HNO documents.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

CARE promotes environmental friendly and risk avert mechanisms in its water and sanitation, health and nutrition response programs and livelihoods diversification interventions do have impact on the health of the public, environment and natural resources. The project provides training to its implementing staff, and community leaders as the project implementation plans and progresses for environmental sensitive actions. Mainly, income generating activities, pasture land rehabilitation, livestock vaccination, and market initiation activities are discussed and agreed with each sector ministry experts.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE uses different strategies in relation to the activities planned to be implemented: as women engage on income generating activities, their domestic work burden, mobility related risks, and access and control over resources are well considered. Engaging men and boys, creating awareness on PSEA and others are among mitigation measures planned. Protection at individual, family and community levels are what will help to address the risks associated to the project activities planned. A number of dialogue and discussion sessions are planned by the project on PSEA, gender discrimination, women in business and leadership.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARE has established a number of channels to receive feedback and complaints from women, men, boys, girls and others with special needs. Phone number, complaint box, complaint day or help desk, face-to-face focus group discussion, and home visit, as appropriate. All community groups will be oriented on the use and procedures on annual basis. Two community accountability focal persons will be selected from each target community (a woman and man) and trained to facilitate the process.
