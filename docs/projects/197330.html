Project Summary

The Syria crisis is one of the most complex humanitarian crises in recent memory. Humanitarian assistance is provided by a large variety of actors on the ground, operating both from within Syria and from neighbouring countries. Coordination hubs have been established in Damascus, Gaziantep, and NES, with WOS in Amman, which ensures common strategies and the best use of resources from the different hubs.
WASH needs of affected populations will continue to be high across 2023 with sector partners expected to provide quality emergency WASH services and maintain WASH facilities and infrastructure. WASH needs will largely be influenced by:
• Water scarcity and drought-like conditions: The evolution of the situation will depend on winter precipitation and the ability to fill in the dams along the Euphrates River, which will impact hydropower generation and functionality of water systems powered by them.
• Energy supply crisis: If intermittency and further degradation of the electricity supply to WASH systems continue, this will trigger a further increase in the number of people in need of WASH assistance.
• New displacements: New displacements may also be triggered by hostilities or economic or water crises.
• Population living in IDP sites: The population in last resort sites will continue because of the ongoing economic and water crises and the closure of collective centers. Due to the funding decrease, a deterioration of the WASH conditions in IDP sites may be expected.
•Returnee movements: No major change is expected due to political stasis.
As Sector/Cluster Lead agency, UNICEF must ensure that sectoral coordination needs at Hub and WOS levels are met. There is also a growing need to create an enabling environment allowing for the Government of Syria and more specifically the Ministry of Water Resources (MOWR) to be more involved with existing coordination structures. The sector will try to encourage the MOWR management team to engage in leading the sector activities and with data collection processes at different levels.
This project will fulfill the core coordination functions at WOS and Hub levels to ensure the following objectives: -Increased predictability of geographic coverage and sectoral responsibility -Demonstrated accountability (for sector-wide performance). -Increased field-level capacities to address gaps. -Quality and uninterrupted WASH services. -Maximized coordination and partnership and buy-in from the major humanitarian actors to ensure a more strategic and coherent response. -Advocacy efforts to fund sector needs. -Reinforced sectoral and intersectoral coordination at all levels.
Following the AWD/cholera outbreak declaration in September 2022, UNICEF is playing a key role in the coordination of the response, including in the Incident Management System (IMS) set up in Amman, Jordan for WOS, and in the Cholera Task Forces/IMS in Damascus and Gaziantep. UNICEF leads the pillars for WASH and RCCE, as well as extensively contributing to the case management pillar and co-leading the Operations, Supply, and Logistics pillars. UNICEF, as CLA, continues to coordinate the WASH response at WOS level and across the affected hubs and governorates, through the sector in Damascus, the Cluster in Gaziantep for the cross-border operations, and by collaborating with the WASH Working Group in NES. The WOS WASH Cluster supports the Hub Coordination Teams to ensure inter-Hub coordination, advocacy, and technical support for cholera. The WASH sector/cluster coordinators in Gaziantep and Damascus within the local IMS/Cholera Task forces, co-lead the coordination of the response with the Health Cluster/sector, RCCE, and leads the WASH Pillar, while developing technical SOP and guidance for the response, capacity building of WASH actors.

3 - Needs

Power outages are common across the country which impacts the functionality of water systems, leading to restricted pumping hours and bypassing of water treatment systems, which reduce water quality. The extremely low water level in the Euphrates affects the production of electricity and water. Some large water systems are split between different areas of control, which challenges equitable water supply access for hundreds of thousands of people. Alouk water station which provides water to over 460,000 people in Al-Hasakeh governorate, has not been operational for several weeks since August 2022, during AWD/cholera outbreak. The effects of climate change, cumulative drought trend, and aquifer overexploitation, also for irrigation purposes, combined with the lack of agreements between riparian countries on water use in transboundary river basins and aquifers Reliability and efficiency of water systems have decreased since 2016, with nearly 3 million fewer people using water networks as the only household water supply modality, in comparison to mid-2020. This means up to 52% of the population is relying on often unsafe alternatives to piped water, to meet their water needs. At least 70% of sewage is discharged untreated and at least half of the sewerage systems are not functional. in NES, no wastewater treatment exists for the IDP sites, resulting in environmental pollution and increased disease prevalence.

4 - Implementation of activities

Activities will be implemented through the WOS WASH coordination group supported by WOS sector Coordination team. the sector will continue to promote and lead inter-hub needs assessments and common analysis, contribute towards intersectoral analysis, as well as strong monitoring of the response. Coordinate with specialized agencies to monitor the market of water and essential hygiene items. The sector will also provide capacity building in key technical areas, including risk-informed management, gender, and protection mainstreaming. The sector will ensure effective coordination between the hubs and at WOS, as per the IASC standards, including: •Ensuring effective Information Management at a WOS level •Promoting the exchange of best practices and technical standards •Representing and advocating on behalf of, the sector in the relevant fora, at the hub and WOS level and at intersectoral level •Effective coordination ongoing in three hubs, and at WoS level, and with relevant sectors (Nutrition, Health, Education, SNFI, FSA, ERLand Protection) •Organization of joint WASH assessments between the hubs •Activity monitoring (4W) at WOS level updated monthly •Sector specific needs identified •Common WOS strategy developed •WASH participation in all HPC initiatives, both at Hub and WOS level •Implementation of capacity building strategy based on UNICEF MENA RO (TOT) •WASH bottleneck analysis •Represent the WASH Cluster in Cholera taskforce platforms, meetings, and technical support.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

The WASH Sector will ensure that the 'do no harm' principle is systematically applied. Potential protection risks related to the implementation of WASH-related activities, along with a description of the relevant mitigation measures and resources required for monitoring those risks have been identified.[1] WASH partners will take adequate measures to minimize exposure of the most vulnerable groups, including women, adolescent girls, children, people with disabilities, and female-headed households, to greater protection risks, particularly GBV. WASH staff will be trained on key GBV concepts and referral pathways. Women, adolescent girls, and people with disabilities will be consulted during project cycle steps to facilitate feedback. High levels of explosive hazard contamination close to water and sanitation infrastructure, or contamination of accumulated garbage/rubbles, which potentially put humanitarian staff in danger during WASH assessments, rehabilitation works, and operation/maintenance will be considered. Where relevant, WASH staff will be trained on explosive hazard risk mitigation, while risk education sessions and awareness-raising materials for beneficiaries will be integrated as much as possible into WASH programming.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNICEF as co-lead agency for the inter-agency network (ICN) on PSEA, will continue to support and work closely with other agencies aiming at enhancing the portfolio of protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. The main goal of the ICN is to enhance the networking for the benefit of people at risk and ensure access to complaint mechanisms and specialized services for victims. Therefore, continuous capacity building for humanitarian workers on zero tolerance for SEA and awareness raising for the community on rights to humanitarian aid free of any exchange in a dignified way is integrated with project activities. Consequently, as per-condition for contracting implementing partners, PSEA assurance and capacity-building plans are guaranteed.Close coordination with the Inter-Agency Country Network (ICN) on PSEA will be continuously guaranteed.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Delay in obtaining the required approvals by IPs to implement projects: UNICEF will work closely with all potential partners to have the agreement documents ready and shared with relevant authorities in advance in order to get the required approvals on time. Suspension of sector partners: UNICEF will provide the required technical support and capacity building to partners on adherence to humanitarian principles and financial risk management. Identified risks may be internal and external.
