Project Summary

According to SHF 2021,WASH Cluster objectives, 2021HRP strategic objectives, and AKDN’s Global and in-country frameworks, the response addresses humanitarian needs with the objective of increasing the resilience of the affected and at-risk communities by improving access to quality water services, emergency WASH services and safe and protective sanitation and hygienic behavior and practices vulnerable rural and peri-urban areas of Salamieh(80% living on less than $2/day), namely, Bari Al Sharqe,Tal Jdeed, Fraytan, ALSaan, Aqareb, Jaddouah, Tal Altot, Zeel Al Ejal, and Sabourah. The Majority of the population are dependent only on groundwater sourced from private water wells. The influx of IDPs and returnees over the last few years; around 28K people(OCHA population data report August 2020) placed further pressure on the limited water resources. During 2020, Aga Khan Agency for Habitat(AKAH) started its humanitarian response focusing on acute shortage of drinking water and delivering clean water to 41communities in Salamieh through water tracking from the RO plants However, provision of clean drinking water is still highly needed in these communities, considering the shortage of water supply capacity through the networks. AKAH continues to support access to clean drinking water through water tracking in 30 settlements including households, health facilities and schools during 2021-22. However, along with these immediate humanitarian response, a need for more sustained and long term solutions are required to support the communities in the longer term, This can be addressed by starting restoration of the local water sources through provision of solarized pumps and ongoing maintenance by engaging the communities at local levels. AKAH with the communities and other stakeholders developed an integrated water management plan for Salamieh, focusing on short term and long-term interventions.This plan is aligned to the HRP2022 Strategic objective 3 “Increase the resilience of affected communities by improving access to livelihood opportunities and basic services, especially among the most vulnerable households and communities.” Interventions will contribute to the objective 3.3. “Access to basic services such as electricity, water, education or health is improved”. The project will address the needs of 31,100, 9 highly vulnerable villages from the three sub-districts in Eastern Bari, As-Saan, Sabboura, and Salamiyeh sub-district.There are13 boreholes with a capacity range of 12-20 m3/h working daily for 3 hours. But due to lack of electricity and fuel needed to utilize alternative energy resources, these boreholes are not fully functional to address the water issue in a sustainable way and improve access of households to required water, AKAH proposes the following: 1.Improving the existing water source and supply system by installing hybrid PV systems to produce electricity and run the existing boreholes for at least 10-13 h/day. By installing the systems, the daily pumping capacity will improve the pumping efficiency by 70%. 2.Install drinking water pipeline connecting Alshomariah-Salamiyah pipeline with East Barri village. Awareness raising of local communities on local water governance, utility, management including renewable energy and hygienic practices to avoid water bone diseases. 3.Establish community-based water management committees to ensure sustainable management and governance at local levels. The project will establish a community-based monitoring and operations support mechanism that aims to enhance the participation of communities in managing local resources, addressing the low efficiency of water services; designing solutions and raising awareness on water saving, efficiency and continuity. In coordination with WASH Cluster, interventions have been designed to be gender, age and protection-sensitive, and reach either broader or distinct segments of the population depending on the need and nature of the activity

4 - Needs

In 2021,need assessment done by AKAH in Salamieh, all communities depending on water trucking services to access adequate quantity of water which could be expensive and unsafe. In rural areas, people are being supplied water through the network15-20 days.The local water resources are insufficient to meet daily demand, where severe fuel shortage and electricity disruptions, resulted in inconsistent water services as a lasting effect of infrastructure damaged by conflict, energy resources falling out of government control, and sanctions. Essential services, natural resources and infrastructure are affected by both climate change and conflict pressures. Local water costs around 6,000SYP/1m3; 50-60,000SYP every month.The authorities covers only40% of regular maintenance costs and20% of emergency maintenance. There is a high risk of contamination of aquifers from wastewater treatment plants in the Salamieh watershed. Indoor heating fuel is limited to100liters/household in winter. Daily gap between supply and demand is estimated to be40-46%, as city residents receiving water once every six days while countryside residents once every20 days.The daily water need is 22,000-24,000m3, while the pumped supply, delivered only once every 6days, meeting half of daily requirement. Also,in Rural Salamieh daily requirement is around 2,000–2,500m3 while the pumped quantity from internal and external resources is less than1000m3/day with supply frequency varying between villages from 3to20days.

5 - Implementation of activities

AKAH will implement the following activities in collaboration with local communities: A.Provision and Installation of the Hybrid Solar Systems Considering the current situation of fuel shortage in the country. Solar energy have become popular in Salamiyeh as an alternative power resource for domestic, industrial, business and agriculture purposes. AKAH will provide and install Hybrid PV systems consisting of Solar panels, inverters, steel frames and metal protection fense for each location for a period of 6 months. AKAH will implement works either with contracting private sector or with local trained technicians. B.Establish community based water management committees to ensure the following:•Train committees on water management at local levels •Ensure community-based monitoring and operations support mechanism and enhance ownership and accountability practices. C.Arrange awareness session at community levels on water saving, energy efficiency and health and hygiene practices. D.Implement and install drinking water pipeline. E.Monitoring and Evaluation process: conducting Regular technical assessment of the systems performance and support maintenance works; Conduct regular monitoring and follow up processes and FGDs with the local communities for project impact assessment. F.Document the community engagement exercise, showing the results of enhancing the community participation and ownership in improving the efficiency of managing the local resources at the community level.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

In Salamiyeh district, the risk of ERWs is considered high. The involved WASH staff and the local-communities committees will be trained on the mitigation measures along with the implementation of AKAH-supported MRE program in the area for raising the awareness among the beneficiaries. AKAH will ensure that the ’do no harm’ principle is systematically applied, and the local communities are continuously contacted and engaged in assessing the impact of the project and addressing any protection issues.The local committees might be exposed to a risk of being not fully accepted by the communities and criticized, in similar cases, AKAH in cooperation with AKF, is providing capacity building to the committees on addressing the community’s behavior and the mitigation measures required to reduce these cases.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk 1: There is a risk that the project activity may lead to safeguarding issues for women involved in the project.Risk 2: The project activities time and locations might be unsafe for the vulnerable groups especially for women and children.In line with AKF and AKAH safeguarding policy, manual and standards, the project PSEA strategy will ensure the following mitigation measures are in place:• Train all project staff on safeguarding policies.• Ensure all involved partners adhere to AKF's Safeguarding Policy and Code of Conduct• Develop gender-responsive referral pathways and ensure project beneficiaries and vulnerable groups are aware of safeguarding policies and can access reporting mechanisms.• All data collection will consciously abide by robust ethical standards, guided by AKF’s Codes of Conduct and Safeguarding Policies.• Conduct an assessment of safety aspects in spaces used for activities implementation as well as the surrounding environment. The project will adopt measures and mechanisms to protect beneficiaries and hold superiors and subordinates to account alike.AKF and AKAH follow specific process and procedures to integrate safeguarding in the Project Cycle which are:• Determine expected safeguarding risk using (Risk Register tool)• Determine safeguarding measures to reduce the risks through (mitigation plan and indicators).• Evaluating safeguarding at the project Cycle before, during and after implementing the project, using Project Cycle Checklist.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A. Instability of the market prices and the sharp changes within short time, affect the implementation of the activities. The donor will be constantly consulted and informed on the situation and potential solutions. B. Lack of specialised workers to do the installation in the targeted sites. AKAH currently – in cooperation with AKF- is implementing R2R project targeting agriculture areas with solar system to improve the irrigation systems, the works are managed by technical local teams trained. Same modality can be utilized for this project. C. Local communities are not fully cooperative with the committees. AKAH will provide a capacity building to the committees’ members on behaviour change related communication at local level and will relay on the existing social structures to raise the communities’ awareness on the ownership and the participation aspects. D. The installed systems exposed to damage due to human or natural reasons. AKAH is considered in the design of the systems, to provide protection metal fence.
