Project Summary

This project constitutes protection of persons arrested and detained within Cameroon in relation to the Anglophone crisis, as well as reduction of the vulnerability of IDPs (from South west/North west region) to human trafficking and exploitation. It aims to:
-Provide legal assistance to 400 persons arrested and detained in relation to the crisis .
-Determine conditions that prevail in 3 prisons, Buea, Bamenda and Kumba for detainees,
-Provide urgent medical assistance to 100 detainees in critical condition.
-Reduce the vulnerability of IDPs to trafficking and exploitation.
I'ts target outcomes include:
1-Four hundred persons arrested and detained in relation to the crisis access legal assistance.
2- Protection standards and needs in Buea, Kumba and Bamenda prisons are fully ascertained.
3- One hundred detainees with life-threatening conditions receive medical attention.
4-Vulnerability of IDPs, especially young girls/boys, to trafficking and exploitation is significantly reduced.

Besoins / Needs

CHRDA went through a systematic process to determine the protection needs of persons arrested and detained because of the crisis and persons displaced as a result of the crisis. And During the process we analyzed their present conditions and compared it with wanted or desired conditions; and identified gaps which are the object of this project proposal.Lawyers from the legal unit of CHRDA visited prisons in Buea, Bamenda and Kumba for a period of six months and during that time they talked to more than 1,000 detainees. CHRDA lawyers carried formal and informal interviews with them to find out circumstances surrounding their arrest, conditions of detention and their situation as regards legal representation.Semi-structured interviews were used but in the most part, the discussions were informal, one-on-one and also in small groups of 3-4 detainees at a time. CHRDA interviewed prison wardens, prison administrators and social workers.From the above interactions, CHRDA determined that 400 detainees arrested in relation to the crisis were in need of legal counsel and 100 are in need of emergency medical assistance. There is need to file habeas corpus at the high courts of Buea and Bamenda to challenge the legality of their arrest; and, on behalf of detainees held in military detention, file memorandums of appearance at the military tribunals of Buea and Bamenda to notify the court that CHRDA lawyers will be representing the said detainees.One hundred detainees are suffering serious health conditions directly linked to their arrest and detention. Some suffered gunshot wounds during their arrest, some have undergone various forms of torture and even had acid poured on them. They illnesses range from acute high blood pressure, asthma, diabetes to loss of eye sight.From prison and detainee profiling, CHRDA determined that conditions in Buea, Bamenda and Kumba prisons do not meet standards set out by International human rights law as what should apply to detention and imprisonment, including the rights of individuals held in detention or imprisonment, the conditions under which they are held, and standards for the work of the prison service and prison officers. Conditions fall far short of what is recommended under the Revised United Nations Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners (Nelson Mandela Rules).During humanitarian relief distribution carried out by CHRDA in the South West and North West regions, we came across stories of girls being exploited sexually and boys recruited into fighting groups. Many of these young people fall between 12-18 and some are unaccompanied children. A contributing factor to their vulnerability is that they lack livelihood skills for survival. CHRDA proposes to have these youths trained in various vocations.
