Project Summary

The deteriorating socio-economic situation in Gaza combined with recurrent rounds of hostilities and the impact of COVID-19 pandemic are putting further pressure to the health system in the Gaza Strip.
UNRWA continues to provide essential primary health care services to 1.4 million Palestine refugees in Gaza. Expanding the capacity of the UNRWA health services to absorb increasing needs is necessary to support the already over-stretched public health services, ensuring access of Gaza’s vulnerable population to health services. UNRWA’s ability to respond to emergency health needs in Gaza is greatly constrained by the tight funding situation as well as the increasing needs, and ensuring the availability of this intervention is crucial.
In light of these conditions, this proposed intervention aims to support the provision of critical healthcare needs for Palestine refugees in the Gaza Strip, while alleviating the pressure on public health system, through: (1) ensuring access to secondary and tertiary health services for poor refugees in response to MoH’s long-waiting list, (2) providing critical medicines especially for NCD patients, (3) hiring additional staff to cope with increasing needs, (4) enhancing telemedicine services, (5) strengthening the infection prevention control, (6) enhancing health response capabilities in emergency, (7) conducting home visits for vulnerable patients, (8) providing necessary assistive tools to children with special needs, and (9) providing mental health and psychosocial support especially to women, vulnerable groups, people affected by the hostilities and staff.

1 - Needs

The process of gradual deterioration under the blockade has left essential services in Gaza barely able to function, directly impacting people’s basic right to health access. The aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic and recurrent hostilities added further strain to the already overstretched health system in Gaza. According to the Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment conducted in July 2022, of the 89.6 per cent of Gaza households reporting a healthcare need requiring care, 99.8 per cent reported a barrier to care. Of these, 76.8 per cent reported the high cost of services as a major barrier. Furthermore, the study showed that the majority of households, at 59.2 per cent, reported that they would seek primary healthcare for non-emergency issues at UNRWA health centers, compared to 35.2 per cent for Ministry of Health facilities, because of availability of medicine (70.1 per cent) and because they cannot afford other options (36.7 percent). UNRWA provides free access to primary healthcare for Palestine refugees, and the 2022 MSNA results proved that the importance of the continuation of UNRWA health services in adequate level. The Health Cluster identified the limited resources in Gaza including chronic shortages of drugs and consumables, equipment, and human resources as one of the key drivers of health needs in 2023. High burden of non-communicable / chronic diseases is another driver of health needs in Gaza (HNO 2023). The MSNA data shows that on average 53 per cent of surveyed households in Gaza have at least one member with chronic disease. For the vulnerable patients especially the NCD bed-ridden cases and people with disabilities, home visits are necessary to ensure that the most vulnerable people have access to health services, considering that 8.2 per cent of MSNA surveyed households reported that distance and transportation constraints as the barrier to accessing healthcare. The Health Cluster identified that the increased incidences of military conflict in the Gaza Strip and confrontations in the West Bank resulted in increased demand for trauma and emergency services. A study on “the Emergency Preparedness of Level-Four Primary Health Care Centres in the Gaza Strip, the Occupied Palestinian Territory: An Assessment of Primary Care Providers’ Competencies and Facilities Preparedness” conducted by MDM France in June 2022 shows that 89 per cent of participants is in need of Primary Trauma Care training, as well as other trainings. The 2022 MSNA also highlighted that the households with people with disabilities require even higher needs of healthcare. Among the 21.1 per cent of households with one or more members with disabilities, 96.1 per cent of households reported a member of their household having a healthcare need in the 3 months prior to data collection, compared to 89.6 per cent for all surveyed cases. 21.1 per cent of households were assessed to include at least a member of their households having a disability, and 9.3 per cent of households were assessed to have at least one child (age 5 – 17) in the households having a disability.Furthermore, the recurrent escalations and ongoing COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the continuous need for mental health and psychosocial services, including GBV survivors, adolescent with problematic behavior, and patients and other affected with COVID-19, among others. According to MSNA, 40.0 per cent of surveyed households have at least one member experiencing signs of psychological distress of trauma in the year prior to the data collection. Specific population groups, including women, children, and persons with disability are considered particularly at risk for experiencing protection threats.In Gaza’s deteriorating socioeconomic situation, the health demand continues to increase. Alleviating the pressure on the public health sector and strengthening the health services already provided by UNRWA to approximately two thirds of the population across the Gaza Strip are immediate needs in order to cope with the on-going crisis.

2 - Activities

UNRWA provides primary healthcare services to 1.4 million Palestine refugees across its 22 health centres in the Gaza Strip. In order to respond to existing and emerging needs, the Agency aims to provide further support to poor refugees facing the burden of high cost of healthcare to ensure their access to secondary and tertiary health services, while alleviating the burden on the public health system. UNRWA will refer vulnerable refugees who are not able to afford private hospital treatment to a network of partnered hospitals partially covering the hospitalization costs of medical treatment or reimbursing the patients for the medical treatment costs incurred. Hospitalization treatments covered include procedures considered lifesaving, urgent and those with long waiting lists at the MoH hospitals that have an impact on the quality of life of the patients. UNRWA Health Programme will work jointly with the MoH and WHO to determine the procedures that are identified as priority needs and will be referred to UNRWA contracted hospitals. This support is in addition to UNRWA’s regular operation of referring patients to its contracted hospitals for cases identified within UNRWA health services.In addition, UNRWA will procure critical medicines to support refugees’ access to essential health services, mainly for patients with non-communicable diseases (NCDs) and chronic diseases including different kind of insulins which are not available at UNRWA health centers as they are not included in UNRWA’s essential medicine list that are funded by UNRWA’s core-fund for primary healthcare.UNRWA will also expand its response capacity within its primary health care services by hiring additional staff in order to cope with the increasing demand in health care. The Agency will ensure that additional staff capacity is available to respond to increasing needs as UNRWA services tend to be the only option Palestine refugees have.The telemedicine service will continue to be available to respond to increasing demand with the protracted crisis and the deteriorated public health services in Gaza. Providing telemedicine service will help providing the first triage to demands that could create burden on health centres with physical attendance. It also is used as vital means for providing primary health care at times of escalations and limited movement, which was the case during the August 2022 escalation.In 2023, UNRWA will continue to integrate the measures introduced since 2020 to mitigate the risk of any endemics or pandemics, as a part of the Agency's Infection Prevention Control protocols, including medical waste management. This is to mitigate the risk of spreading any types of infectious diseases.Furthermore, given the volatility of the security situation in Gaza and the health needs arising from emergency situations, UNRWA will strengthen its emergency capacity by equipping each health centre with life-saving emergency equipment and 25 percent of UNRWA health staff, mainly medical officers and nurses, will be trained on Trauma Life Support. In order to ensure that the most vulnerable Palestine refugee patients will be provided with adequate health care support, UNRWA plans to conduct home visits to patients who cannot reach Agency's health centres, mainly NCD bed-ridden patients and people with disabilities. The home visit team will provide health and psychosocial support (PSS) to the patients as well as counselling and health education to their care giver.As part of the school health activities, UNRWA continues medical screenings in schools for all refugee children to identify those with special needs, including those with disabilities. UNRWA will support children with special needs, providing necessary assistive devices that vulnerable families are not able to afford otherwise.Finally, UNRWA’s mental health and psychosocial support at health centres as well as tele-counselling will continue to respond to increasing demand targeting vulnerable Palestine refugees identified as in need of support (women of reproductive age and children, elderlies, people with disabilities, GBV cases) and staff. Recognizing the relationship between health and other protection risks, additional support, such as legal counselling, will also be provided as required.UNRWA is mandated to provide primary healthcare services to Palestine refugees. Interventions included in HRP are outside of UNRWA’s core primary healthcare services but supporting poor refugees who have been living under the prolonged blockade and would not have needed life-saving healthcare services otherwise.

9.1a - Specify the mechanisms for feedback and/or complaints

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Feedback is sent to the management for information, evaluation and corrective action as required. The beneficiaries’ feedback contributes to continuously improve UNRWA’s health system at different levels.

9.1b - If 'Other', please specify :

Every UNRWA health centre have a Women committee, Youth committee and Health Center Friendship committee that are composed by community members. The committees provide guidance and feedback on needs and the services provided by UNRWA. In normal circumstances, satisfaction surveys and exit interviews are conducted on a regular basis by UNRWA health officers to ensure optimal delivery of health services to Palestine refugees. If in-person collection of feedback can’t be performed, UNRWA counts on telemedicine services and hotlines, through which the beneficiaries can convey any feedback or concern. UNRWA also has a dedicated portal through which beneficiaries can raise any general query including feedback on the quality of services delivered. All staff are required to take mandatory PSEA training. UNRWA is represented in the National PSEA committee and the PSEA taskforce, while all UNRWA departments have representatives within the Agency composing well-established complaint mechanism to follow up.

9.1d - Explain how you have ensured that mechanism are accessible to all population groups?

UNRWA has a multitude of options to receive feedback and ensure accountability to the affected population, including in-person reception in all UNRWA installations, a targeted online and telephone complaints portal (nominal and anonymous), a 24/7 WhatsApp number available through text or phone for SEA complaints; a centralized (Amman) confidential online/telephone/email complaints mechanism to respond to specific confidential complaints. Direct (in person) and indirect (press, social media, etc) outreach is further undertaken to disseminate the information on the above mechanisms.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

UNRWA has a dedicated Protection and Neutrality Office to guarantee the provision of its services in an impartial and non-discriminatory way. UNRWA regularly assess the level of compliance with Protection standards of the services provided to refugees through a Protection Audit. Staff in health centres, who are regularly targeted by capacity building activities including on protection mainstreaming and gender, are able to refer specific protection cases to MHPSS counsellors for follow up. In addition, anew mechanism is developed to support critical and urgent cases of GBV and protection cases. In normal circumstances, the crowd control system is in place and a specific calendar is set up to avoid and limit potential incidents and/or disturbance. A complaint response mechanism is accessible in health centres and mechanism to provide timely feedback is available through specific response committees.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

UNRWA is targeting all Palestinian refugees in need for healthcare through its 22 health centres across the Gaza Strip. Location of health centres takes into consideration safety concerns as well as catchment area, distance and potential risks to reach the area for different groups of population. In case of lockdown and movement restriction, people in need of primary health care are still able to access UNRWA’s health services through telemedicine. The health centres take into consideration specific vulnerability concerns and adopted specific SOPs to provide meaningful access and prioritize healthcare to vulnerable groups (abandoned women, pregnant women, persons with disabilities, elderly, etc.). A follow up system is also in place whenever assistance is not received in order to identify and follow specific concerns and potential protection cases. Further, health centres are clearly marked and visible, respecting UNRWA neutrality policy. All UNRWA health centres have an elevator to ease the access for people with movement disabilities, and some health centers are piloted to have posters and guidance with sign language as well as tactile paving floor tile for people with visual impairment.
