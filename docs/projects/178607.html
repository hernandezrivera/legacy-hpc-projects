Project Summary

Contribute to improve the food security status and reduce the negative coping mechanisms of food insecure people in Northwest Syria through the distribution of:
1) Monthly Cash for Food for vulnerable displaced persons and host community members in need;
2) Monthly food baskets for vulnerable displaced persons and host community members in need;
3) One-off short- term food assistance through cash for newly displaced households and/or crisis-affected vulnerable people as part of a rapid response mechanism;
4) Ready-To-Eat-Rations (RTERs) assistance for newly displaced households and/or crisis-affected vulnerable people as part of a rapid response mechanism.

4 - Needs

Ongoing insecurity and the impact of displacement, with worsening socio-economic conditions, have prompted an almost 20% increase in the number of people in need compared to early 2020, that includes a 57 per cent increase in the number of food insecure people to 12.4 million. Of these, 1.27 million people are considered severely food insecure. Short-term emergency needs are increasingly compounded by longer-term needs across host and IDP communities. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2021, today, 13.4 million people in Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance - a 21% increase compared to 2020. Nearly half the population of NWS now reside in emergency shelter. IDPs, host community members, and returnees face mounting challenges due to prolonged displacement, poor living conditions, restricted economic opportunities, and humanitarian actors’ limited access on the ground. The 2021 HNO highlights that across Syria in the past year the number of IDPs has increased from 6.1 to 6.7 million –third of the overall population. Furthermore, all 1.7 million people living in camps are deemed to be 100% food insecure, and an additional 1.8 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity. Moreover, the latest HNAP assessment conducted in 2020 identified priority needs in NWS as food (77%), shelter (63%), and basic services (59%). Syria faces new socioeconomic difficulties that are exacerbating the food security and livelihood situation of families. The most recent Rapid Needs Assessment that was conducted by IOM highlights that food needs are one of the top priorities reaching 100% in ten camps, seven of them have requested cash for food while the three others requested as in-kind food.

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM FS activities will be as follows:• Selection of implementing partner.• Selection of locations based on the updated needs and gaps in food severity ranking and the FSL Cluster’s gaps analysis.• Registration of beneficiaries by IPs. Verification of 20% of beneficiaries by IOM M&E through commissioned TPM. • Initial assessment of FCS and CSI scores.• Food baskets: Selection of food baskets vendor. Production of food baskets, laboratory testing of food samples at source to ensure safety and quality and storage of food baskets in vendor warehouse if required. Transportation of food baskets into Syria, Storage of food baskets in IP warehouse selected in coordination with IOM.• RTERS: Selection of RTERs. Production of RTERs, laboratory testing of RTERs samples at source to ensure safety and quality and storage of RTERs in vendor warehouse if required. Transportation of RTERs into Syria and storage of RTERs in IP warehouse for emergency distribution in coordination with IOM.• Conduct sensitization sessions with site managers, IDP committees and IDPs.• Distribution of food assistance under the observation of IOM’s TPM.• CASH: IOM and IP to ensure/reassess replicability of cash distribution month on month in targeted locations.• Conduct distribution monitoring and exit interviews by IOM TPM.• Conduct satisfaction surveys at the end of the project to measure the change in FCS and RCSI.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. IOM response activities unintentionally lead to intra-community tensions arising from the beneﬁciary selection or limited resources and response capacities. 2. The principle of “Do no harm” is not guaranteed due to unclear objectives based on the community needs, validated through a transparent community consultation and participation process. 3. Risk of violence in communities (protests) or general security incidents.4. Pressure on the implementing partners from the local authorities on who should or should not receive the assistance. Mitigation measures: 1. Perform conﬂict analysis before planning the response, ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the community or communities concerned and keep them informed of the project implementation.2. Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from a representative community board.3. Ensure registrations/distribution processes are well organized and explained clearly to community leaders.4. Raise awareness with implementing partners about the importance of principled humanitarian assistance, and reporting any incidents of interference.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:-Security situation in Aleppo and Idleb allows the implementation of IOM response activities.-Activities to be implemented in coordination with local authorities.-Proper beneficiary selection procedures are followed.-Commodities are stored in appropriate warehouses in Syria.-Cash, FBs and RTERs distribution within C19 will follow health protocols as well as hygiene procedures to limit the risk of virus transmitting. Risks:-Security situation in Aleppo/Idleb doesn’t allow for viable IOM response activities to be undertaken.-Local councils influence IOM response activities.-Influence of local council on beneficiary selection.-Proper and safe storage conditions are exposed to weather conditions.-Cash, FBs and RTERs distribution is carried out inadequately and not following C19 prevention measures. Risk Mitigation measures:-Establish a security and accessibility plan for targeted areas and a process to consult communities on how to proceed with proposed response in a deteriorating security environment and develop a ‘duty of care’ strategy for local partners.-IPs ensure full coordination with local councils. Local councils are represented in Community Engagement Teams and have a participatory role throughout the process.-Wider and transparent call for beneficiary registration by IPs. Conduct 20% beneficiary verification by IOM TPM. Beneficiary selection based on vulnerability scoring.-Complete the distribution of commodities within 10 days from off-loading at warehouse, conduct warehouse verification pre-off-loading and during the distribution of commodities.-Close coordination with field team to ensure all preventive measures are taken.
