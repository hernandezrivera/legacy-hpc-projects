Project Summary

Relief International (RI) will increase awareness on integrated messages related to health, COVID-19, Cholera, routine immunization, sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and mental health through outreach activities while conducting critical distributions to enhance hygiene practices and ensure health and safety of women in the reproductive age. CHWs will raise awareness on health-related topics, detect any suspected cases of communicable diseases (Measles, hepatitis A, Cholera, acute flaccid paralysis, and food poisoning), and report them to the Epidemiology Surveillance Unit (ESU) at the MOPH using the community surveillance system's national hotline. CHWs will also refer vaccine defaulters to the PHCCs for vaccination, Pregnant women for ANC services, detect, and refer those in need of mental health services.
RI will continue to respond to emergencies and outbreaks including Cholera and COVID-19 by mobilizing rapid response teams (RRTs) in high-risk areas in North and Akkar, training frontliners on infection prevention and control (IPC) measures, and supporting health facilities with capacity building and procurement of medical supplies.
Aligned with the health sector’s strategic objectives, RI will strengthen the supported primary health care centers’ (PHCCs) capacity to deliver quality essential services through conducting social mobilization and outreach to vulnerable and affected communities including COVID-19, health promotion, SRH, mental health, and deploying home care teams to provide care to patients with limited mobility during outbreaks. Moreover, RI will support these PHCCs with essential acute medications and supplies to meet increasing demands and cover the shortages that these PHCCs might experience.
Due to the continually worsening economic situation in Lebanon, households (HHs) across the country have been pushed into severe economic difficulty. 2021 and 2022 MSNA findings have shown that the socio-economic collapse has had and will likely continue to have a tremendous negative impact on the Lebanese, Palestinian Refugees in Lebanon (PRL) , and migrant HHs within Lebanon. 2022 Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment identified needs for Lebanese and PRL HHs across all sectors, the most reported essential needs that HHs had trouble meeting were food needs (as reported by 57% of the Lebanese HHs and 63% of PRL HHs), health needs (56% of Lebanese HHs and 52% of PRL HHs) and electricity needs (28% for Lebanese HHs and 39% of PRL HHs). Moreover, the most reported essential needs that migrant HHs had trouble meeting were food needs (43%), communication needs (28%), and health needs (24%) - often due to financial issues.
RI will address the health needs of 60,239 Lebanese, PRL, and migrant communities in Beirut, Mount Lebanon, Bekaa, Baalback-Hermel, South, North and Akkar. With existing extensive outreach and experience, RI will bolster healthcare efforts to the most vulnerable Lebanese, PRL, and migrant families. This will be done through essential preventative measures for maternal healthcare, focused community outreach and awareness raising, accompanied with the distribution of essential hygiene and feminine kits. Individuals in need of health care services including mental health and access to medications will be referred by RI’s community health team either internally to RI’s mental health team or externally to supported PHCCs where they can get their essential treatment.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

Protecting participants from harm will be the center of RI’s interventions. RI will offer services to eligible groups regardless of their nationality and gender following clear selection criteria and outreach components to prevent exclusion of any vulnerable group from obtaining assistance. The feedback, complaints, and response mechanism (FCRM) will ensure and reinforce protection during the proposed activities as it will be emphasized to the targeted communities for reporting any complaints, including safeguarding concerns. Moreover, staff will be hired taking into consideration gender balance and will be trained on gender-based violence (GBV) safeguarding, and accountability to affected population (AAP). Such training will not be limited to only RI staff and will also be provided to PHCCs considered under this proposed intervention.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

RI adheres to the Interagency Standing Committee’s (IASC) five commitments to AAP focusing on 1) Participation and Inclusion through Community Health Workers (CHWs) selected from the targeted communities, outreach activities, surveys while focusing on regular 2) Information Sharing and Transparency in disseminating relevant and timely information on its activities at PHCC and community-levels along with available services at RI and other agencies-level through its referrals mechanism. The 3) FCRM is set with clear pathways relevant to complaints and feedback received through accountability channels, collected/secured by accountability team and shared on a need-to-know basis in relevance to feedback and complaint sensitivity levels. Findings will serve in 4) Program Adaptation through data collected under FCRM and MEAL activities reflecting participants' feedback on services provided and shared with the program team during regular meetings and lessons learned to address needs
