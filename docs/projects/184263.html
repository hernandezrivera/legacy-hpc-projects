Project Summary

Action Against Hunger provide Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) support to pastoralist, agro-pastoralist, fisher forks, peri-urban/urban and internally displaced (IDP) and non-IDP populations affected by multiple shocks ( persistent periods of armed conflict, political instability as well as environmental and economic shocks) in Somalia. The 2022 FSL project target 60,572 individuals (13,568 men, 15,991 women, 11,872 boys and 12,357 girls) living in Bakool (Xudur, Elbarde, Wajid, Rabdhure), Lower Shebelle ( Afgooye), Nugaal (Eyl)regions. The project will prioritize the 42,400 vulnerable individuals in IPC (3/4 or worse) to improve access to acute food needs through UCT, Vouchers (fresh food) and CFW to mitigate shocks and stress in line with FSL cluster objective 1. Further 18,172 individuals’ (IPC 3) will be supported with seasonal livelihood inputs in the target districts through emergency agriculture, livestock, and other livelihoods support to protect livelihoods and related food sources in line with FSL cluster objective 2.

1. - Projects activities

ACF will prioritize implementation of activities, which provide mechanisms to discourage negative coping strategies, protecting productive assets and offset exposure for immediate recovery from shock in line with FSC strategic objectives (1 &2) and Action Against Hunger country strategy 2021 -2025. These interventions are:a) Improve access to food for 42,400 of people facing severe acute food insecurity including IDPs and non-IDPs in 6 districts (Afgooye, Xudur, Wajid, Elbarde, Rabdhure, and Eyl) through• Unconditional Cash Transfer (UCT) target most 5,000 vulnerable households (30,000 individuals) in host and IDP populations who do not have the capacity to participate in CFW programs; the project will provide UCT to meet immediate households’ food and other basic needs. The UCT will prioritize HHs considered at risk (without productive assets, income or active Labour resources, malnutrition caseloads) ; these include female-headed HHs already engaged in negative coping strategies, HHs with malnourished U5 children and PLW and HHs at risk of COVID-19 pandemic ( Elderly and chronically ill member). This will be implemented during rapid and slow onset crisis that spark massive displacement since vulnerable HHs lacks vital safety net. The monthly transfer value depend on recommendation by Somalia CWG based on regional MEB. Action Against Hunger will support vulnerable households for at least two transfer cycles for each target households during 2022• Fresh Food Voucher (FFV): ACF will provide 400 vulnerable households (2,400 individuals) with fresh food vouchers. The action target malnourished children U5 with severe acute malnutrition and PLW with moderate acute malnutrition. The priority will be given to those from extremely vulnerable households’ and are at risk of dying, influenced by severe drought and rapid onset disasters (cholera/AWD’s, flash floods, displacement) in IDP caseloads in IPC 4 zone. The voucher consist of pre-determined list of nutrition rich fresh food through restricted vouchers provision by local vendor. Each target household will receive food voucher value of 75% percent of regional food transfer value recommendation by Somalia CWG. Markets assessment will be conducted at onset to determine availability and access of the food and fresh food items commonly used as a major source of food purchased from the market. It is envisioned that the ration will complement therapeutic supplements and households own food access strategies.• Cash for Work (CfW) provides immediate life-saving cash relief to meet emergency basic needs while setting the foundations for medium term recovery by rebuilding both livelihoods and infrastructure such as rehabilitation of productive farmland, rangeland and water infrastructure critical to community survival ahead of the rainy seasons. The action target 1,667 vulnerable households (10,000 individuals) for a 2 cycles. The daily Labor rate depend on recommendation by Somalia CWG. CfW intervention will provide short-term employment (income) during the lean season (dry) when HH purchasing powers are limited. ACF will employ the Inclusive Community Based Targeting (ICBT) methodology validated by HH vulnerability indicators in targeting. The biometric systems will be applied to capture beneficiary data and minimize the risk of duplication. The disbursement of cash will be done through mobile transactions to individual households. The CFW productive infrastructures recommended for rehabilitation will be selected from community action and adaptation plans. b) Provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs to 3,029 most vulnerable households (18,172 individuals) to protect livelihoods and related food sources of pastoralists, agro pastoralist, fisher forks, peri-urban and rural IDPs across Somalia through emergency agriculture, livestock, and other livelihoods support.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash modality- Given the range of needs that Somali households in crisis exhibit, MPCA is an appropriate, tried, and tested tool to support household access to critical basic needs and strengthen food security and resilience. Cash transfers allows recipient Choice to decide on how cash are spent enabling people to choose what they most need depending on specific households' needs and preference. Cash transfers has multiplier effects on boosting local economic in local markets and trade if the money is spent locally, and facilitate investment in agricultural production and other areas of livelihoods including VSLA. Where markets are functional and can support increases in demand as a result of transfers, MPCA has been proven to strengthen and stabilize household consumption, to improve food consumption and dietary diversity, and to reduce reliance on more severe and less reversible coping strategies. Ultimately, Action Against Hunger expects the delivery of MPCA to stave off the worst effects of the crisis on the most vulnerable. MPCA will be delivered through electronic means (mobile money) which is cost efficient, transparent, faster and maintain dignity of the beneficiaries compared to commodity-based alternatives and this is expected to enable households to adhere to COVID 19 protocols such as distancing measures and avoid venturing outside, thus limiting inter-household transmissionCommodity vouchers: Voucher support will include fresh food or livelihoods input vouchers in areas where the markets are functional and able to adapt. This modality has proven to be faster, and more cost-effective than in-kind assistance for fresh food and inputs ( seeds, tools, vet inputs, emergency fodder etc). Action Against Hunger has successfully implemented this modality in Somalia and contributed to improvements in market functionality and sustained growth. The vouchers will be utilized to ensure that the proposed emergency project contributes to strengthening of the local market which is largely managed by the private sector.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Action Against Hunger will conduct organizational own monthly market assessments and further participate in WFP weekly market survey and also participate in Somali REACH market assessment . The information collected over time will confirm the availability and affordability of basic needs in the local markets in project target locations. During the assessment, the vendors' capacity to restock the goods, and accessibility of transporters to supply goods will be considered. As such, Action Against Hunger has a long-standing presence in the areas, hence better understanding of the local market infrastructure and services and how the targeted population interact with market even in times of crises. The market monitoring information will be used to inform the proposed project preparedness planning to mitigate shocks. The market functionality will be critical factor in providing immediate life-saving assistance to the affected populations.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1 - Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance.Action Against Hunger will include minority clans/ethnic groups in consultations at the design, implementation and monitoring of the projects to ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable populations including minorities. Action Against Hunger will identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community through inclusive community based targeting approach, malnutrition caseloads and validated by households vulnerability criteria in all –inclusive fair beneficiary targeting process. Action Against Hunger in consultation with Local authorities and target communities will ensure the all –inclusive representation of all target communities, including minorities in community based governance structures to represent interest from varied groups. Action against Hunger will ensure the Complaint response and feedback systems are functional and accessible to all program participants for timely monitoring and response of any issues including exclusion. 4.3 - Exposure of displaced boys and girls to violence, caused by living in highly insecure settlements.(low/medium/high)There are high risk of girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) in accessing humanitarian servoces,forceful recruitment of boys by AS, and forceful displacement in highly insecure environment. Action Against Hunger have strong global/in-country policies in place to prevent Fraud, Conflict of Interest, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), along with a strong Whistleblowing policy to receive serious complaints. Action Against Hunger already maintains an active Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project areas, including contact numbers. Each complaint or request for assistance is responded to in a timely manner, and have established confidential reporting and management systems for serious complaints to ensure accountability. Moreover, Action Against Hunger will ensure access to basic services and opportunities for the households, which host displaced boys and girls in delivery of humanitarian services and ensure access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards). Action Against Hunger cash based assistance will target households which host malnourished boys and Girls – graduating from nutrition and health screening centres and will ensure that any beneficiary who reports or suspected cases of GBV and protection is referred to health staff who will then link the survivor to appropriate service provider through established linkages.
