Project Summary

HRO will work to improve the access to and the quality of MHPSS services through the provision of a comprehensive package that links community based services to health facility based activities by expanding the number of entry points to nine; Al Bab, Qbasin,Shmarkh, Al Dana, Harim, Saalqin Kafar Takharim, Tell Abiad, and Kherbt Al Joz (Mental health clinic, G0704034!Hope Revival MHPSS center) countryside. It is designed as a multi-layered approach that incorporates support to static facilities, the establishment of robust referral systems and community outreach activities. Mental health services will be available to host populations and IDPs who are experiencing extreme adversity and distress from living in a war zone for the past eight years. HRO’s psycho-social workers, and trained doctor will address issues such as anxiety, acute stress, grief, despair, anger issues, attention deficiencies and problems with interpersonal relations, among other symptoms that result from humanitarian emergencies. Meanwhile the community health workers will conduct awareness raising sessions within the communities to inform them about the newly available services, and to increase knowledge and understanding about mental health and well-being, which HRO believes will in turn reduce stigma.
As a part of the project, the technical staff will focus on delivery of awareness sessions, strengthening family support on MHPSS, assessment and case study (evaluation), Formulating care plan in coordination among the psycho-social worker, mhGAP doctor, counselling through PM+ and interpersonal therapy, psychological first aid, psycho-education (Group & Individual), referral to other services providers (Specialized and non-specialized), based on case management module, identification of vulnerabilities of child protection and GBV, support in mobile clinic over most of the above-mentioned services in the surrounding communities, providing specialized MHPSS services for beneficiaries who suffer from psycho-social consequences of child protection and GBV cases, providing non-pharmacological forms of psychotherapy (under supervision); Cognitive Behavioral Therapy, and other means of psychotherapy. It should be mentioned that all individual and structured group services are supervised according to WHO recommendations. The technical clinic staff will be consisting of 11 mhGAP doctors (8 in MU, and 3 in fixed facilities) and 22 PSWs along with field supervisor for mhGAP doctors and field supervisor for PSWs.

4 - Needs

In Northwest Syria, the ongoing conflict has been labeled as a protracted emergency that continues to destroy the mental health well-being of the Syrians, especially to the most vulnerable group of Syrians. According to the results Of MHPSS Needs Assessment (NA) in Northwest Syria conducted by WHO across NW Syria (Idleb, Afrin, and Euphrashield) from August 3rd-22nd, 2019 Among the IDPs interviewed, many arrived over the last six months (39% of adult respondents and 42 % of youth respondents), although some have been displaced since 2011 onward.Many of the respondents expressed sadness, fear, anxiety, loneliness, stress and worry over the current displacement and ongoing violence. All respondents reported the displacement affecting them and their families negatively. A group of male youth described feeling humiliated and oppressed.39% of adult IDPs reported being aware of MHPSS services and a few named the local hospital, their school, a PSS center and the internet as places where they are aware of services, although not all had sought the service, they merely heard about them through neighbors and friends. Moreover, most of the respondents who answered yes, referred to their family and friends as supporting them. The remaining 66% responded that they were not aware of such services or were unsure if they existed.86% of the adult respondents reported a significant need for MHPSS services in the area.When adults from the host community were asked if they were aware of any MHPSS services in the area, 49% said yes and named women centers, mobile clinics and health facilities, kindergartens, neighbors and relatives, Facebook and the local council as places where they are aware of services. They also mentioned awareness campaigns on MHPSS that were conducted in the community. The remaining 51% responded answered that they were not aware of such services or were unsure if they existed. 95% of the adult respondents reported a significant need for MHPSS services in the area

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk: Unavailability of transportation means for people with disabilities to attend the session, especially the awareness-raising sessions and PSS sessions.Mitigation: Providing means to facilitate their attendance such as wheelchairs.2-Risk: The inability of the team to deal with people with disabilities and vulnerable groups such as the elderly.Mitigation: Training the team on the method of receiving all ages and specifying programs to these categories. As for the people with disabilities, they enjoy the priority to attend all of these sessions.3-Risk: The team is not aware of risks related to PSEA.Mitigation: Training the team on PSEA.4-Risk: The team and beneficiaries are not aware of the importance of accountability and having high-quality services.Mitigation: Establishing a complaint mechanism, familiarizing beneficiaries with the method of reporting complaints, familiarizing the team with the importance of this mechanism in order to develop a sense of accountability. 5-Risk: When conducting referral for an MHPSS case, the case may be exposed to a higher risk from the new service provider which it has been referred to him/her such as the inability of the service provider to deal with the case, or even abusing the deal with him/her.Mitigation: a Higher level of coordination with the service provider prior to referring MH cases, along with raising the awareness of the provider on how to deal with cases. Also, providing transportation means and accompanying the referred case to the location of the service provider.6- Risk: Difficult access to the centers to which MHPSS cases are referredMitigation: Provide transportation for MH cases and their families to reach the centers referred to it or MHPSS case managers will go with them to the referral centers. 7- Risk: mental health stigmaMitigation: Conducting awareness sessions for the cases Family and community on mental health issues and psycho-social support.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption 1: security situation in targeted area is stable. Risk 1: deteriorating security situation affects the implementation. Mitigation: establishing a response plan. Designing the activities in a flexible way where we can change the way of conducting according to severity and we can focus on more suitable activities like doing PFA . Stopping the activities for a short period. moving the center to another place if deemed necessary after negotiation with OCHA. Assumption 2: Local council and authorities are continuing to allow us to work. Risk: local authorities decide to prevent us from implementing the project or part of it. Mitigation: coordinating and negotiating with local authorities. Assumption 3: The attitudes and needs of the population stay the same as we found in the needs assessments. Risk 3: changing in the attitudes or priorities of the population that will affect their willingness to benefit from project activities. Mitigation: working on awareness-raising throughout the project period and getting feedback from beneficiaries about services and adapt accordingly. Assumption 4: There are experienced people in targeted communities. Risk: lack of experienced people in targeted communities, IDP may be recruited based on their previous experience. Mitigation: equality in opportunities among host community and IDPs will be ensured throughout selecting people who are residing in targeted area regardless of being IDP or from host community. Job vacancies advertisements will be shared with local councils. Assumption 5: materials will be purchased from local vendors in the target area, Risk 5: Risk of triggering sensitivity with the local vendors during the procurement and purchasing materials from outside the targeted areas. Mitigation: All materials that are available in the targeted area should be purchased locally in accordance with HRO procurement policies.
