Project Summary

The goal of the this project is to contribute to the achievement of SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) by 2030 by ensuring crisis-affected populations, including , internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees, and host populations, have safe access to adequate and nutritious food during and after crises in targeted communities in the NW/SW region of Cameroon.
The project will support crisis-affected in priority areas with emergency agriculture assistance and income-generating activities, aiming to improve livelihoods, strengthen self-reliance, and facilitate early recovery. This will be done through building capacity of targeted families and supporting them with startup kits and farm inputs. Support will focus on small ruminant, local poultry, table birds, piggery, fishery and agriculture.
This assistance will lead to improved food security, strengthened livelihoods, enhanced nutrition-related practices, and reduced acute malnutrition among vulnerable families in the NW/SW region in a sustainable way.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

Globally, food insecurity levels continue rising to unprecedented levels. According to the latest Global Report on Food Crises, the number of people facing acute food insecurity increased to 258 million people across 58 countries/territories in 2022 (FSIN et al., 2023). According to the Global Report on Food Crises report, August 2023 which provided a partial update on acute food insecurity numbers, an estimate of 279million people are suffering from high level of food insecurity Cameroon, a nation at the crossroads of three complex humanitarian crises, finds itself grappling with the repercussions of the Lake Chad basin conflict, the North-West and South-West crisis, and the Central African Republic (CAR) refugee crisis. These crises have cast a daunting shadow over nine out of the country's ten regions, affecting millions and creating a multifaceted humanitarian challenge. More than 3.6 million people, constituting over 12% of the 28 million population, are ensnared in the clutches of acute food insecurity. Among them, at least 2 million individuals face the harsh reality of internal displacement, forced returns, or refuge . The relentless exposure to crises, exacerbated by insufficient humanitarian assistance, has precipitated distressing coping mechanisms, including child labor, child marriage, and survival sex. The North-West and South-West Regions witness the plight of more than 715,000 people facing human rights violations and abuses, with acute needs spanning protection, food, water, health, and education. Alarming statistics project that this year, one in six people living in Cameroon, totaling 4.7 million, will require humanitarian assistance and protection. The specter of high insecurity in affected regions is expected to persist, limiting access to food and essential needs. Prolonged crises, climate change impacts, and conflicts over natural resources are anticipated to escalate tensions, compelling communities and displaced populations to contend for limited resources.
