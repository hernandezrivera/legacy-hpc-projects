Project Summary

To achieve the goal of improving water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) conditions for Rohingya refugees in camps 10 and 13, World Vision aim to implement comprehensive strategies aligned with the WASH Sector's Joint Response Plan (JRP) which involves enhancing WASH facilities and interventions while addressing key issues through community engagement to increase access to sustainable WASH services.
World Vision will prioritize community involvement, focusing on gender-responsive approaches to ensure the participation of women and girls in decision-making regarding essential WASH services. This will involve the improvement of facilities, hygiene practices, waste and fecal sludge management and provision of critical supplies such as soap and menstrual hygiene management materials. With a focus on accountability, protection, and considering age, gender, and diversity, the project aims to implement activities following the principles of 'do no harm.' This includes emergency support for various situations like disease outbreaks (AWD, scabies, COVID-19), natural disasters (fire, flood, monsoon, cyclone), and environmental considerations.
The initiative builds upon World Vision's previous experience in implementing essential WASH projects in different camps since 2018, now aiming to ensure quality water supply, improved sanitation facilities & hygiene practices and improved waste & fecal sludge management for approximately 4,501 households, covering around 22,409 individuals in 3 blocks of camps 10 and 13 in respect with Sphere WASH Standards and WASH Sector guidelines. Operations and maintenance of essential WASH facilities and services will be a core focus, ensuring functionality and accessibility to sanitation facilities for all, in collaboration with camp WASH focal points and Camp-in-Charge officials (CiCs). Also, coordination among various partners and engagement with camp authorities, local government, and existing consortium partners will be crucial in achieving these objectives. This integrated approach aims to not only address immediate WASH needs but also to promote sustainable solutions, community resilience, and comprehensive support aligned with humanitarian goals and standards.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

It is imperative that WASH actors continue to step up their efforts to guarantee that all women, men, girls, and boys, as well as people of all ages and disabilities, have respectful, adequate, equitable, and quality access to WASH services in light of the significant number of refugees. In consideration with the following gaps World Vision will implement the sustainable solution of safe drinking water, improved sanitation facilities and appropriate hygiene education in cooperation with community structures.Water: According to J-MSNA October, 2023 report, 14% of the Rohingya population in FDMN camps reported not having enough water for their family needs. It also stated that, 70% of HH using tap-stand as Primary Source for Drinking Water per Camps, moreover only Average 20 liters Domestic Water Collected Per Person per Day in Camps. Day by day, As the groundwater table descends, numerous tube-wells face disfunction, particularly in the dry season. Hand pumps continue to serve as a primary source for drinking water, bathing, and domestic use. Taking these circumstances into account, this project will prioritize ensuring high-quality water at the household level, recognizing it as a crucial stage in the safe water chain.Sanitation: In 2024, the WASH Sector’s strategic focus will be on operation and maintenance of existing WASH services through cost efficient solutions. Still 62% of Female Family Member Feel Safe using the Communal Latrine at Night As per J-MSNA October, 2023 report. Solid waste management is another mentionable challenge as only 71% of the households are Segregating Waste at household level. As per WASH Sector Service Monitoring Report, October, 2023, 15% households with visible waste in the vicinity of their accommodation. Moreover, As per WASH Sector’s strategic 2024, 1/3 of the waste is need to process and new landfill site will need to be built in 2024. Proper waste and fecal sludge management is another concerning issue in this area.Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: As per J-MSNA October, 2023 report, 10% of the community have not access to soap for hand washing. According to this report, 62% of women and girl accessing menstrual hygiene materials, Nevertheless, the situation at the practical level is far from ideal. Many females continue to face obstacles in accessing MHM facilities due to stigma, taboos, and conservative practices. There are instances where we have observed that Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials related to MHM lack contextualization. Moreover, there is a rising trend in communicable diseases such as Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and Dengue cases at the camp level.Taking into account these crucial factors and the existing gaps that demand attention, World Vision is committed to sustaining its ongoing WASH program in camps 10 and 13. This initiative will receive technical and financial support from World Vision Taiwan. World Vision Bangladesh will actively contribute to the operations and maintenance systems required to broaden and secure the sustainability of WASH infrastructure. This commitment involves fostering community participation and engagement, utilizing a multi-level approach. This approach aims to identify and resolve issues at the individual, household, community, and environmental levels in a complementary manner, building upon existing projects for greater impact.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

Enhancing public health, illness prevention, and disease protection through improved water, sanitation, and hygiene support for FDMN population is the ultimate goal of the WASH project. The project's design and execution will incorporate integrated techniques and an adoptive strategy.World Vision has been continuing coordination with health, protection, education, SMSD, Nutrition and food security sector for effective and inclusive WASH intervention though close collaboration with WASH Sector. World Vision will continue the same camp level coordination mechanism like coordination with health sector regarding epidemiological disease control, coordination with shelter and SMS for effective collaboration and construction site selection, WASH in school will be ensure through coordination with education sector. CiC office’s will be engaged through need assessment, approval, site selection for new WASH facilities, beneficiaries’ selection, quality monitoring, community feedback and project completion. Since 2018, World Vision has been working in different camps targeted more than 70,000 population and building upon that experience, we are planning to continue WASH Project at Camp 10 and 13 in upcoming year as well with keeping in consideration of continuity and stability. World Vision's main feature of this project is to ensure sustainable WASH intervention through strengthen community capacity and community engagements as well as ownership building. Frequent consultation meeting with different community groups will take place to ensure community accountability. Strong liaison with government stakeholders, WASH Focal, SMS and other WASH actors will be ensured for addressing service gap and to avoid service overlapping. We make sure that reports are submitted on time, that we abide by all laws and regulations, and that we carry out any new directives from the government in order to strengthen our relationships with the government and other partners.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

In this project, our commitment to the community's safety is unwavering, guided by the four core protection principles. As we constructed number of WASH facilities, our foremost concern is ensuring the safety and dignity of every community member through operation maintenance of those facilities. Upholding impartiality and equity, we guarantee equal access to WASH services without any form of discrimination. Special attention will be given to the needs of AGD groups, ensuring no individual is excluded from these essential services. Our project management strategy involves regular engagement with diverse community groups and implementing targeted training programs to enhance their capacity.

3.3 - Age & Disability

Through this initiative, it will be made sure that every child is safe from harm and has equitable access to WASH services. When design this project, consideration has been given to all age groups. Since children make up a higher percentage of the community, it will be ensured that they participate effectively in every stage of the project cycle. The expectations and perception of children will be reflected through WASH facility design. By constructing walls and fences around exposed water sources, such as tanks or wells, this initiative will guarantee that WASH facilities are designed with children's safety in mind. The project also takes the elderly population very seriously. This project will ensure aged friendly WASH facilities, a railing-equipped access road to the WASH facilities, and WASH facilities that are closer to the elderly.This comprehensive WASH initiative has been crafted with the aim of inclusivity for various demographics, including women, girls, men, boys, older individuals, and those with disabilities. By following the guideline of Washington questionnaire, project will identify the person with disability and provide friendly & need based infrastructure. The primary goal of this project is to guarantee fair and non-discriminatory access to WASH services for all genders, age groups, and individuals with disabilities. The planning and design phase involves extensive community consultations, ensuring the engagement of a diverse range of people. Implementation and monitoring will also prioritize the active involvement of this diverse group. The project specifically focuses on creating WASH facilities that are accessible and accommodating for individuals with disabilities, and certain facilities will be upgraded to better meet the diverse needs of the community.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

World Vision will fully accountable to the affected people through this project and beside ensuring their need of quality and equitable access to WASH facility, World vision also aim to make them aware of their rights and entitlements under the project. This project will ensure the participation of the affected people in every phase which will be effectively reflected in this project. All of their opinions will be counted and responded accordingly by establishing Complaint and Feedback Response Mechanism (CFRM) system which will collect their needs, perspectives, and priorities in a regular basis with prompt response. Adequate mechanisms, which are accessible to aged people and person with disabilities, will be put in place to allow beneficiaries to raise their complaints in an accessible, safe and dignified manner. For better learning and effective intervention community consultation meeting, focus group discussion, user group meetings etc. will be conducted as an open discussion platforms. Moreover, community-led monitoring mechanism will be reinforced for effective monitoring.

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

World Vision has a dedicated PSEA focal who will provide comprehensive training on PSEA and GVB to the staff and community based volunteers, reinforcing our dedication to protecting individuals from sexual exploitation and gender-based violence while fostering an environment of inclusivity and equality. If any cases are received through CFRM system, organization will take action according to the organizational PSEA policy. Accessible information on PSEA will be shared on every major WASH infrastructure through posters. World Vision will follow ‘Zero Tolerance’ policy in this regards.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, this project integrates DRR/DRM by implementing a preparedness course of action. This includes maintaining and repairing WASH facilities, stabilizing surrounding slopes, and safeguarding the WASH site. Disaster-resilient features will be incorporated into the WASH facility, encompassing activities like water point disinfection and securing the WASH structure. World Vision holds contingency supplies (water jars, aqua tabs, desludging machines, bamboo, bleaching powder, etc.) to offer immediate post-disaster support. Environmental impact will be minimal, with a focus on safety systems for toilet pits, ultimately leading to a positive impact. Additionally, during the construction of WASH facilities, consideration is given to averting potential risks, such as average flood levels.
