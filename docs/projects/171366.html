Project Summary

According to post Gu’ FSNAU report released in September 2020, over three million people are in need of humanitarian assistance. Shocks related to floods, outbreak of COVID-19 pandemic and infestation of Desert locust in parts of the country worsened the situation for vulnerable communities who have not fully recovered from the past shocks. Women and children are the most affected population in Somalia. According to the national Education Cluster, over 1.4 million school age children are in need of humanitarian support. Evidence shows, that adults and children with disabilities face three to four times’ greater protection risks than persons without disabilities. In the current COVID-19 pandemic circumstances, public restrictions, self-isolation of households and disruption of community dynamics, services and social support may lead to aggravated protection risks for persons with disabilities and their caregivers. Marginalization and discrimination of Children is prevalent and access to education for children marginalized groups, children with disabilities and girls is being hampered. In emergency settings, materials and facilities are scarce, and often teachers are not equipped with the skill to handle classroom management and conduct their lessons in a child friendly manner. Among regions affected include Lower Shabelle, Galgadud and Banadir.
The project aims to intervene the situation through provision of life saving and protective quality education to out of school children from IDP, returnees and vulnerable host communities living in Banadir, Galgadud and Lower Shabelle regions. The project targets to enroll 3000 (1320 girls and 1680 boys) out of school children into 20 operational public schools in the target districts of Marka, Banadir, Afgoye, Qoryoley, Barawe and Dhusamareb districts. Among the target children will be 450 children living with disabilities. Disadvantaged Children including girls, children with disabilities, children from poor families and minority groups will have increased access to appropriate, participatory and affordable primary education in schools managed by the communities in the three regions .The project plans to conduct “Go to school” campaign aimed at enrolling these vulnerable children, whose parents cannot afford to pay school fees, in existing operational public schools. These children will not be required to pay school fees as the project will provide emergency teacher incentives to 120 (80 male and 40 female) teachers who will be teaching learners in 20 selected schools. The project plans to support the target children in scholastic materials, WASH related services and provision of teacher incentives. For delivery of quality education to the target children teachers and school managements’ capacity will be built. Communities’ awareness on children’s rights and importance of sending children to school will be built through establishing community dialogue to raise awareness on mitigating risks and all kinds of child abuse practices to enhance children’s access and retention in education. The project will also focus on the enrolment of boys and girls living with disabilities. Communities’ and school managements’ awareness on importance of sending children living with disabilities to schools. Children have a right to basic education. Their lives can be greatly improved if they receive good quality education and, conversely, their lives can be adversely affected by a lack of education, or by poor education and negative experiences in school. Children have a lot to offer and have exhibited a willingness to contribute to the improvement of school environment. They also have the ability to influence and raise awareness of communities, and can play a key role in advocacy for enrolment and attendance, as they are better at identifying marginalized children who are out of school, such as those with disabilities or those from minority groups. Women will make 50% of CECs to be trained.

Project Activities

The following are the main activities project plans to execute: 1. The project will conduct “go to school” campaign to enroll 3000 (1680 boys and 1320 girls) out of school children in Lower Shabelle, Galgudud and Banadir regions.2. The project will distribute scholastic materials to 3000 (1680 boys and 1320 girls) out of school children enrolled in public schools, including 450 boys and girls with disabilities in 20 schools in Lower Shabelle, Banadir and Galgudud regions. Materials to be distributed include: (exercise books, pens, pencils, erasers, sharpeners and solar lights). Children targeted are from IDP, returnees and vulnerable host community households. 3. The project will provide emergency teacher incentives to 120 teachers (40 female and 80 male) for ten months. Since parents cannot afford to pay fees the project will recruit 120 teachers to operate in the 20 selected schools where the target vulnerable children were enrolled. 4. The project will build the capacity of teachers by training 120 (80 male and 40 female) on life-skills training such as PSS, mine risk reduction, and multi-age child centered teaching methodology, school management and communication with children. The training will also include INEE inclusive education, protection and Socio-Emotional learning and awareness on Teachers Code of conduct.5. School managements’ awareness on children’s rights will be raised through training. The project targets to train 100 (50 male and 50 female) CEC members from the 20 school communities targeted will be trained on children’s rights and how to make schools environment free from violence with zero tolerance to child abuse of any kind.6. The project will raise children’s awareness on child rights and facilitate the establishment of child clubs in schools. 20 child clubs will be created with the aim of helping allure out of school children enroll with schools. 7. The project will provide clean drinking water to the 20 target schools in the target regions. The schools will then be safe place for children in terms of health8. 1000 adolescent girls will be provided with reusable sanitary materials once in every term. This will reduce girl’s absenteeism during menses which often lead to girls’ dropping out of school. 9. Provision of safe, dignified and gender friendly wash facilities including adequate gender segregated latrines (targets 40 latrines in 20 target schools) 10. The project will appoint Child Protection focal points at school (1 male and 1 female) with additional PSS First Aid training and knowledge of referral pathways to specific child protection. 40 (20 female and 20 male) focal points appointed. 11. The project will facilitate structured gender sensitive recreational and PSS activities at the schools for both boys and girls- forty recreational facilities (two for each school) will be installed. 12. The project will establish community dialogues to raise awareness on mitigating risks and all kinds of child abuse practices to enhance children’s access and retention in education. Twenty locations targeted 13. Provision of COVID-19 related PPEs (face masks) for the 3000 (1320 girls and 1680 boys) school children14. Mapping of marginalization dynamics will be done in the targeted community (targets 20 locations)

Mitigation Measures

MitigationThe project will have strategies to mitigate the possible protection concerns the project and beneficiaries might encounter which can negatively impact the project out come. Below are possible risks and their proposed mitigation: a) Risks that beneficiaries are exposed to violence 1. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at learning centres, phone lines 2. Consider the gender balance of education staff - ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for female teachers and students. 3. Ensure that all staff and committee representatives receive protection mainstreaming training. 4. Work closely with authorities and protection service providers to help refer children with protection issues.b) Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual abuse 1. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions c) Marginalized groups not accessing consultation meetings 1. The project will involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to education needs. Different criteria may affect the power dynamics. 2. Consultation will be made with women, men, boys, girls, adolescents, children with disabilities, teachers and parents to understand their needs and preferences for school location, design and education services. 3. Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response 4. Talk to girls and boys separately about the barriers they face and hold discussions with groups of children that could face added difficulties. 5. Establish participatory student associations and parent-teacher committees and engage them in the design of education programs. Such associations can boost the confidence and self-protection capacities of those involved whilst benefiting others. 6. Ensure student, teacher, parent and school management committees are democratic and inclusive and members represent all groups within the community (e.g. gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities). 7. Reinforce the capacity of parents and teachers to act as a protective layer for children against violations. d) Beneficiaries not accessing complaint/feedback mechanism1. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. feedback box at learning centres, phone lines 2. Consider the gender balance of education staff - ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for female teachers and students. 3. Ensure that all staff and committee representatives receive protection mainstreaming training. 4. In cooperation with protection actors, enhance the capacity of teachers to monitor, report and refer protection cases (such as abuse and exploitation) in accordance with standard operating procedures. e) Explosive risks in the project area for school children1: Avoidance of placing education facilities near military installations and conflict front-lines 2. Considering risks associated with land mines, explosive remnants of war, busy roads, exposed wires and dangerous materials on the ground. 3. Keeping in mind the distance children have to travel and their mode of travel. 4. Ensuring the timing of classes is safe for children; classes will be set for the day time with sufficient time to reach home before nightfall. 5. Situating play areas in clearly visible and safe locations.
