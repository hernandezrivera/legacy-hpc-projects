Project Summary

The IRC proposes to deliver a range of livelihoods and recovery activities, including Cash for Work, vocational training, apprenticeships and SME development, to improve the access of 2,409 conflict-affected households to more sustainable income and support the recovery of conflict-affected communities. CfW projects will be selected to offer the greatest benefit to the target community, support market recovery and offer short-term income-generating opportunities for different beneficiary groups, including women, people with disabilities and the elderly. Vocational training, apprenticeship and SME development activities will be identified and supported on the basis of market and labor market assessments to ensure that they are responsive to market demand and increase beneficiaries' access to sustainable income-generating opportunities. IRC will target at least 50% women's participation and 10% participation of people with disabilities.

4 - Needs

An estimated 64 to 84 per cent of the Syrian population live below the poverty line. Widespread disruption of income-generating activities, destruction of infrastructure, reduced public sector jobs, repeated shocks, and multiple displacements have contributed to high unemployment and reliance on harmful coping strategies to meet basic needs. Over 50% of Syrians are unemployed (75% among youth), and 83% live below the poverty line. Some 69 percent of Syrians are believed to be living on less than USD 1.90 a day, more than double since 2011. Women’s participation in the labor force is estimated at 16%. Coupled with inflated prices and fluctuating exchange rates, Syrian household purchasing power is severely weakened, limiting the capacity to meet basic needs and leading the majority of households to rely on negative coping strategies. This situation is exacerbated by gender inequality and poverty, where many families have lost their savings and productive assets, remain still in debt and, for the most vulnerable, fall into a cycle of harmful coping mechanisms due to limited access to both opportunities and resources. There is a lack of economic and livelihood opportunities for women, particularly those who are divorced or widowed, where engagement of women in these activities is perceived as culturally inappropriate or neglectful of domestic responsibilities. As such, women remain with reduced decision making and power within the household and community, and forced into the private sphere- thus continuing the cycle of harmful gender norms. Other vulnerable groups, including the elderly, youth and people with disabilities, also face constraints on economic participation.

5 - Implementation of activities

Prior to all Early Recovery and Livelihoods interventions, the IRC will conduct a market assessment and consultations with target communities (through KIIs and age- and gender-disaggregated FGDs) to understand market demand, the needs and priorities of different groups, as well as barriers to economic participation, gender roles and other dynamics that will inform project implementation. Cash for Work (CfW) projects will be identified and selected through a project management committee established by and with the participation of IRC. Projects offering the greatest benefit to the community and contributing to market recovery will be prioritized, such as rehabilitation of communal infrastructure that was damaged or abandoned during the conflict and can be sustainably maintained after the IRC’s intervention ends. CfW projects are varied to meet the needs of the community, and to ensure the participation of different beneficiary groups, such as women and older adults. Livelihoods: Economic sectors and vocations to be supported will be identified based on the market assessment and in consultation with community members to ensure that the assistance offered will improve access to income. The IRC will register potential beneficiaries using a scoring system, the results of which will determine the most appropriate livelihoods activity. IRC will offer vocational training (58 sessions, including 8 life skills sessions), apprenticeships with business owners in the local markets (48 sessions, including 8 life skills sessions) and SME development (9 financial management sessions, followed by an invitation to submit a business proposal in a competitive process, with selected proposals receiving small grants. IRC conducts follow-up monitoring to assess changes in access to income and savings patterns as a result of the assistance provided.IRC will target at least 50% women and 10% PWDs to participate in the activities.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Diversion: IRC shares FARM materials and explains to beneficiaries that they are not expected to provide anything to anyone to benefit from the assistance. IRC closely monitors risk using the FARM and PDM, and any evidence of malfeasance is reported to IRC’s Ethics and Compliance Unit to be investigated and addressed in a prompt, comprehensive and safe manner. The IRC’s reporting system provides employees with multiple channels to report concerns, while consolidating all issues in the internal EthicsPoint hotline and case management system. The IRC’s procurement controls also serve to mitigate risk of diversion: individual supplier due diligence is a required step in a competitive supplier bid and contracting process, and includes anti-terrorism compliance (ATC) checks and checking for debarment. The IRC screens all entities using the LexisNexis Bridger database, which screens the names and entities against over 40 watch lists. Loss of access: - IRC ensures regular review and updating of contingency plans for its operations inside Syria to mitigate against changes in context. This has recently been updated for NES based on the current context and including multiple potential scenarios regarding changes in territorial control, continuation or interruption of cross-border access and access to locations of implementation. Humanitarian access staff conduct negotiations with local actors, work closely with program leads to identify and manage risks and engage with local councils to avoid any interference or diversion of aid. IRC has zero tolerance for interference and\or diversion of aid to any parties to the conflict. IRC has a Security Management Plan (SMP) and Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) to guide IRC operations and staff to provide lifesaving assistance using the safest possible methods.
