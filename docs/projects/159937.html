Project Summary

This proposal is specifically designed to address the 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan’s strategic objectives of providing support to highly vulnerable populations in areas of displacement and return, providing support to highly vulnerable people inadequately covered under the social protection floor, and providing specialized assistance and protection to survivors of violence.
The project aims at providing protection monitoring response to drought and conflict affected IDPs and vulnerable host communities in Mogadishu, Luuq and Baidoa. Through two mobile protection teams, Human Appeal will provide regular coverage of Banadir, Kismayo, Baidoa and Luuq which hosts the largest IDP population in Somalia.
Human Appeal will engage protection team who will carry out household and community-level assessments, develop and provide timely and reliable information and key protection messaging out of the existing information from different actors, monitor returns and the camp consolidation/eviction processes, and provide information on services in areas of return to IDPs intended to return through joined “Know before you go” campaigns with the government.
The project will specifically focus on promoting child protection through tracing and reunification of the unaccompanied children to enable them have access to protection and social interaction. Also, the project will focus on supporting GBV survivors through establishing survivor-centered multi-sectoral assistance, e.g. legal, medical, psycho-social, safety etc. for the returnees, IDPs and host communities in target area and case management. Furthermore, HA will establish child friendly spaces for the children in the IDP settlements to improvement their integration and protect them from possible risks. Mitigation Measures

A recent needs assessment exercise conducted by HA in the target location shows that children and older persons are considered most in need of assistance. The chronically ill, persons with disability and child-headed households are identified as being the most vulnerable social groups at risk of health concerns. Children are, however; perceived to be one of the most vulnerable groups across all sectors, with children under the age of 5 and children aged 5-12 years having been identified as the first and third most vulnerable groups. In the 3 proposed locations; Key Informants also ranked infants/toddlers under 5 to be the age-group most at risk of health concerns followed by older persons (60+ years) and children between the ages of 5 and 12 years. The assessed locations are under-served in all aspects. HA applies Protection Mainstreaming principles to all programming and ensures the safety of all direct and indirect beneficiaries of the projects. Human Appeal will ensure the following four key Protection Mainstreaming principles are met through the project:- Safety and dignity in implementation- Meaningful access to services- Participation and empowerment of affected communities to the services provided - Accountability. HA will ensure that safety and dignity of beneficiaries is ensured by maintaining confidentiality from first contact, through assessments, referrals, provision of assistance and distributions. Safety and dignity will also be established by ensuring that all sites are free of hazards, are in safe locations, and are clearly humanitarian spaces. HA will ensure meaningful access to services is met by establishing distribution locations that are accessible to persons with disabilities, in close proximity to where beneficiaries reside. Key informants in Banadir region (Kahda & Daynile) reported (as per the HA rapid assessment) having difficulty in accessing justice, including access to legal documentation, HLP cases, and criminal cases, issues that would be monitored and assisted through HA’s proposed mobile protection team in the these regions. Main protection-related needs are: 1. Sexual exploitation 2. Discrimination in access to assistance 3. Rape/sexual abuse 4. Diversion of distributions 5. Tension between IDPs and host communities and 6. Poor access to lifesaving and basic humanitarian assistance.

Furthermore, Human Appeal's safety and security approach to its operations in the proposed areas has been defined through specific Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). Human Appeal has classified areas according to their security status, mapping the whole operation areas as a) partially secured (e.g. center of Mogadishu), b) moderate risk (e.g. newly retaken areas from Al-Shabab or even the IDP camps) or c) high risk zones (e.g. locations adjacent to areas under Al-Shabab control). The definition of these areas has been based on field risk assessments performed by Human Appeal security teams combined with a close relation with other actors, including UN-OCHA RSO, INSO, NISA (Somali National Investigation and Security Agency), local authorities and the host community with whom Human Appeal has established a network of informants able to update the organization in a timely manner on the security context. For locations where the risk of security incidents is high, Human Appeal establishes movements in convoy with multiple communication mechanisms, tracking capabilities based on GPS and as redundant communications, as well as hourly communication checks in order to allow an on-time monitoring of the teams. 1.
