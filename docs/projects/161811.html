Project Summary

The northeast remains highly endemic for diseases and the situation is further exacerbated by unsafe water, inadequate hygiene and sanitary services, with women and children in particular increasingly susceptible to disease outbreaks, including cholera (HRP 2019 -2021) . The plan is for humanitarian partners to continue to deliver life-saving humanitarian assistance and protection prioritizing the most acute needs identified in the HNO.
A joint needs assessment conducted by the Nigeria Joint Response (NJR), a consortium of 5 INGOs, revealed that 40.80% of individuals interviewed across 6 locations (4 camps, 2 host communities) shows that 40.8% buy water for use; 53.0% wait for more than 15 minutes to collect water. Access to latrines is 35.30% while 65% said they do not have soap for domestic use.
ZOA and other INGOs continue to work in Monguno, however the gaps remain large. A rapid assessment conducted by ZOA in October 2019 shows that 27 blocks of dysfunctional latrines in Waterboard need rehabilitation.. Gaps in hygiene promotion, hygiene kits and sanitation facilities continue to be exacerbated by the influx of IDPs . Recently, suspension of other INGOs providing hygiene promotion and water trucking have recently resumed but service stoppage also created a gap in service delivery. Monguno is classified as extreme”in the severity mapping for the HNO process.
. ZOA proposes 2.5 solar boreholes to meet this water gap of 14,652 individuals. ZOA also proposes 3.2 solar boreholes as an exit strategy to 18,000 new arrivals who are currently benefiting from water trucking. In total, 6 boreholes will meet both needs (14,652 +18,000 = 32,625 individuals).
ZOA proposes to construct 30 blocks of latrines to meet this gap. ZOA will also coordinate with the sector to carry out hygiene promotion where there are gaps and to continue hygiene promotion among 19,100 individuals in its areas of operation within Monguno. Hygiene promotion activities will include prevention messages around water borne diseases including cholera.

1 - Needs

A rapid multi-sectoral needs assessment of new arrivals conducted by REACH in February 2019 shows that 62% of those interviewed self-reported that they did not have enough water to cover their daily needs (cooking, bathing, drinking). A total of 74% reported that collecting water lasted more than 30 minutes, indicating overcrowding of the existing water points and insufficient capacity to cover the water needs. Also, 82% of new arrivals surveyed by REACH said that women or children were the main collectors of water used in the HHs. 69% said they had access to latrines, but 25% reported practicing open defecation in the bush, and 6% said they practiced open defecation in designated areas. 69% of new arrivals did not have access to soap and the lack of affordability was the reason for not having access to soap as cited by 71% of those interviewed.

2 - Activities

Activity 1: Drill 6 motorized boreholes to meet the gaps of 220 m3 or 14,625 individuals (3 boreholes) and to provide exit strategy to the water needs of 18,000 new arrivals (need 3 boreholes) currently benefiting from temporary water trucking. 6 boreholes serve (14,625 + 18,000 =) 32,625 individuals or 5,438 families. Average HH size = 6 Activity 2: Recruit and train 46 hygiene promoters to conduct intense and targeted hygiene promotion Activity 3: Recruit and train 20 WASHCOM members to manage the water points Activity 4: Provide training for 15 persons to operate the boreholes properly, do maintenance, and carry out small repairs Activity 5: Construct 30 blocks (each block has 5 stances) of latrines Activity 6: Procure and install 30 pieces of handwashing stations Activity 7: Include mainstream protection messages in hygiene promotion activities
