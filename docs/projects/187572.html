Project Summary

The proposed project will provide critical humanitarian services through an evidence-based, integrated strategy that will adapt service delivery within the complex Syrian context, combining direct implementation and partnerships with Shafak to address humanitarian needs and utilizing robust monitoring. IRC and Shafak will meet the immediate needs of the most at-risk populations through the provision of distinct and integrated protection services in the targeted locations through 1) protection monitoring and inter-agency coordination that promotes cluster-level advocacy and informs humanitarian actors’ 2) protection mainstreaming, and 3) Provision of critical information that enables displaced populations to obtain referrals for services, 4) legal services and individual consultations, 5) IPA with linkages to Cash for Protection services, 6) General Protection Case Management, 7) Psychosocial support activities for men.
IRC proposes to reduce barriers to accessing essential services in NWS and for highly affected populations and to focus on quality, impact, and provision of holistic support through increased collaboration across sectors and programs in Syria. IRC’s direct rapid response services in Idleb will be available through mobile teams with dedicated staff for specific client groups and specialization in supporting women, children, and men for integrated service provision and effective intersectoral referral pathways and provision of critical and timely information.

4 - Needs

Civil documentation, Housing Land and Property (HLP) issues, and freedom of movement remain major countrywide protection issues. Lack/loss of civil documentation was reported in 76% of assessed communities (compared to 61% in 2020) and spread across all governorates. Restrictions on freedom of movement were reported in 51% of assessed communities (compared to 65% in 2020) and 61% of assessed communities reported HLP concerns (compared to 50% in 2020).Lack/loss of civil documentation impacts all population groups with specific vulnerabilities for women and children. It limits the ability of widowed or divorced women to inherit property, get custody of children, legally remarry, or register children born through subsequent relationships. Registration provides official recognition of a child's existence, identity, and nationality. Unregistered children may face difficulties in accessing basic services and rights, including health and education. It compounds a series of existing protection risks and vulnerabilities in terms of freedom of movement and HLP issues including security of tenure and increased risk of statelessness, and limits even the ability to access humanitarian assistance. 5 - Implementation of activities

IRC and Shafak will continue protection monitoring in NWS and IRC will continue to co-lead the protection cluster through 2022. Routine monitoring will be conducted using a combination of methodologies. Once collected, data will be analyzed and developed into information management products, to be shared through coordination platforms and with other relevant humanitarian stakeholders. During Emergencies, an integrated rapid protection assessment will be conducted, and a specific protection team will be deployed to respond. IRC and Shafak will triage risks, prioritize those that threaten survival, and support people at risk until service access barriers and underlying threats to safety are resolved. Protection teams will also conduct frontline outreach, incorporating the dissemination of information to affected communities about their rights, available services, and assistance. Legal services. Awareness sessions and assistance, where appropriate, will be conducted by IRC and Shafak to provide reliable and responsive information on CSD. Cash for protection assistance will be implemented by protection teams who are able to gauge various needs of individuals and households that may require cash components to solve. IRC and Shafak will continue supporting PSS activities targeting men, elderly persons, and persons with disabilities and their caregivers, General Protection CM will be provided as well.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The IRC’s approach to integrated protection programming in Syria seeks to provide themost vulnerable clients with access to comprehensive, tailored services that adhere to theprinciple of Do No Harm. To do so, the IRC depends on regular context analysisthrough stakeholder analyses, updated security assessments, monthly protectionmonitoring, regular communication with camp and local authorities, informationsharing across partners and humanitarian networks; and meaningful participation ofaffected populations throughout the project cycle. Service interruption of any kind canhave negative impacts on clients, but the interruption of protection services can beparticularly harmful and destabilizing. The IRC’s mixed mobile and static approach andtimely, safe referrals (including internal IRC services) mitigate the risk of an abrupt end toservice provision. Protection monitoring allows the IRC to track whether clients are unableto access protection services, as well as their degree of satisfaction or any concernsassociated with the services received. With data gathered on a monthly basis, insightsfrom respondents can be translated into course corrections and program adaptations, andinform IRC’s understanding of unintended impacts of humanitarian service provision morebroadly. 10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

IRC and IRC Staff recognize the unequal power dynamic and the resulting potentialfor exploitation inherent in our work with local beneficiaries. IRC and IRC Stafffurther recognize that exploitation undermines the credibility of our work andseverely damages victims of these exploitive acts and their families andcommunities. Accordingly, IRC maintains and upholds the present policy whichexplicitly prohibits all forms of beneficiary exploitation and abuse by IRC staff,including but not limited to sexual exploitation and abuse as a recurrent andespecially abhorrent form of benefciary exploitation and abuse. IRC expects itsStaff to uphold the highest ethical standard of integrity, accountability, andtransparency in the delivery of goods and services while executing theresponsibilities of their position. Accordingly, the following requirements apply toIRC Workers: IRC Staff are prohibited from engaging in sexual relationships withbeneficiaries. IRC Staff are also prohibited from organizing, facilitating, orparticipating in sexual relationships or activities with benefciaries. IRC employeesare prohibited from engaging in any form of intimidating, humiliating, degrading,or aggressive behavior towards beneficiaries. Sexual activity with children (de􀂦nedaccording to the Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC) as all persons underthe age of eighteen) is strictly prohibited regardless of the age of majority or age ofconsent locally.
