Project Summary

In line with the Protection Cluster priority areas, WATAN will provide critically needed Protection services to an estimated 6,000 vulnerable people in Albab -Afrin- Azaz districts -Aleppo Northwest Syria
WATAN will implement integrated protection services to prevent, mitigate, and respond to harm exploitation, and abuse for the most vulnerable boys, girls, men and women in northern Syria.
The proposed intervention will include working with communities to shift social norms that subordinate women and girls and condone violence as a means to control them; engaging men and boys in addressing GBV; responding to the immediate and long-term needs of GBV survivors.
WATAN intends to provide support to the most vulnerable groups in particular children, boys, girls and female-headed households, women and girls which continues The aim is to establish community centers in the targeted areas to create as a safe environment where women, children, girls, and boys can interact and socialize with women coming from similar backgrounds, thereby allowing the center serves the psychosocial needs of the women, children and helping the women and girls, including the GBV survivors, in their coping mechanism, the WGSS as part of community center will emerge as a key strategy for supporting the protection and empowerment of crisis-affected women and girls A safe space is a place where women and girls feel physically and emotionally safe, free of violence (or fear of violence) or abuse; free to express themselves without fear of judgment or harm.
These community centers will serve as a foundation from which to engage the community in providing direct activities The aim of proposed activities is to enhance the prevention and mitigation of protection concerns by the provision of quality and integrated protection services including prevention activities such as awareness-raising, Provision of PFA and PSS activities including case management response activities targeting people at risk of GBV concerns.
The project is designed to be twelve months, this includes one month of staff technical training to build their capacity on identifying protection risks, needs and response and mitigate the GBV, CP risks, as well as establishing referral pathway.

4 - Needs

As underlined in the HNO 2020 report, it is reported that communities are facing at least one out of seven protection risks and some face even more protection issues in their communities. Majority of the people who are seeking humanitarian assistance are the most vulnerable such as women-headed households and the ones escaping from the heavy shells in countryside of Idlep. There is lack of capacity regarding the proper shelter for the people in need which people who previously were displaced are now facing difficulties sharing their shelters since it is a very well-known fact that when the number of people increases in the shelter, the vulnerability increases accordingly even further especially for women and children.In order to provide a sustainable assistance, the humanitarian aid activities need to continue by providing protection assistance and mitigation of the risk factors for especially the most vulnerable people. With the increased violent environment, GBV risk factors increased further for women and girls compared to the past which is already traditionally and culturally exist in the society.It is a natural process that during conflicts the moral values are collapsed accordingly lawlessness appears which paves the way for violence especially targeting women and girls. As reported in the Voices of Syria (2019), customs and traditions are making a huge barrier to disclose GBV.Child marriage rate augments even further after the conflict the reason why parents assert a claim that their daughters are better protected under marriage due to the harsh physical and economic conditions in addition to the state that many people share the same shelters and girls face sexual exploitation and abuse due to lack of privacy and collapsed moral norms. Child labor also continues to be another risk factor for children, such as recruitment to the armed forces and bagging. In the other terms, children are dropped out of school and lost their future.

5 - Implementation of activities

GBV, CP team will be consist of facilitators whom conducting PSS, PFA and awareness raising sessions, case workers whom will be carrying out the individual cases, team will be provided by the GBV, CP related trainings, covering subjects such as GBV guiding principles, referral mechanisms, and GBV, CP case management training. GBV SC tool kits manual will be used in the community centers, 30 non GBV actors will be trained as well on GBV core-concept.the project will provide individual support, including specialized GBV, CP case management to women, girls, children at risk by using GBV SC referral pathway, coordinating with GBV SC focal point in the area to ensure timely referrals. Identification of vulnerable women and girls, children in need of individual support by the case workers or GBV cases in need of GBV and CP case management will be done through the activities run in the community centers. emergency services will be provided especially for the new commers by outreach team. the CP services will include recreational PSS, CM for vulnerable children, and parenting skills. The activities and awareness sessions are will be identified and altered, based on the changing concerns and needs of the women in the field. The awareness sessions will empower women by providing them information on various subjects on SC GBV tool kit. Moreover, prevention interventions activities will also include awareness-raising at the community level, on existing protection risks, particularly those affecting children and adolescents.community mobilization to promote access to services, in order to let accessible for PWDs along with provision of transportation service to PWDs if needed.M&E plan: the developed GBV SC M&E guidance will be used for monitoring the ongoing activities including specific tools will be used by M&E team such as survivor satisfaction surveys, base line- endline survey will be conducted in design to engage women in the planned proposed activities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

GBV Risks: for GBV actors their security because of their role. Privacy concerns on confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access GBV response services.difficulties in accessing to GBV services because of stigmatization for individuals.For humanitarian workers, this reflects the need to provide specialized services focused on ARS on GBV topics, mitigating protection risks,WATAN has identified several mitigation strategies to reduce the impact of these risks on program implementation, including: consistent community outreach and engagement, ensuring that curricula/materials at the centers are reviewed or approved by the protection cluster and/or local stakeholders to ensure they are appropriate and acceptable, and continuous monitoring of the security situation and use of remote management/monitoring techniques as needed if the security situation deteriorates.Children and female-headed households will be also prioritized to raise the awareness and ensure that complaint mechanisms are well understood.for CP Risks: Risk harmful traditional practices and the conflict of community would reflect against child.protection principles. Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys through the provision of specialised child protectionservices if not adequately trained/ services are of low quality. Risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys and their families if staff are not appropriately trainedor if client data is not managed appropriately. Measures: Engage with diverse members of community on safety and security in the community. Ensure consultation andmobilization of key persons in the community. Provide information as key message on child protection issues and its consequences. Regarding PSEA; the CRM such as complaint boxes in the center and encouraging complaint through the posters and brochures in the center will be initiated.staff will be trained on PSEA, PSEA policy will be signed.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The borders of Turkey and Syria will be open to the crossings for humanitarian agencies in regard to goods and staff. WATAN has an alternative plan just in case the border is closed, such as targeting the less accessible means of delivery. During implementation, the humanitarian principles such as impartiality and neutrality will be strictly followed up. Still if the areas of work are restricted, WATAN will make sure to look for alternative areas and will try hard to solve the possible issue. WATAN will collaborate with the city councils, women’s communities, community leaders, CBOs especially working protection sector and the other humanitarian stakeholders in order to prevent any kind of risk factors. The ongoing violent actions do not make a barrier for humanitarian assistance since humanitarian law is in place by both armed and unarmed parties which humanitarian actors can have access in the locations where people are in need. However, WATAN will ensure a continuous risk analysis in regards to the staff and the target people in need. Once the situation is unbearable and cannot implement the program but rather harm, WATAN will take the responsibility to terminate the planned program and close the community center with an exit strategy by informing the community and the other stakeholders and if possible negotiating with another stakeholder to hand over based on the possibilities and elaboration of the risk factors. the exit strategy plan is coordinating with other actors in the field and/or trying to secure fund to proceed in the implementation of the proposed activities. WATAN will seek other donors’ funds to continue, however, if no fund is secured, then we will hand over the center and the activities to the other implementers and in full coordination with protection cluster under ensuring protection of beneficiaries’ information and Identifying the location of staff and maintain contact with them in case of sudden displacement.
