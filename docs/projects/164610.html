Project Summary

SAMS through this project aims to continue providing support to the female and male survivors of sexual and gender-based violence, as well as their extended networks (families, communities, relief workers, care providers, and civil society) in Northwest Syria including the provision of safe spaces for women and girls in health facilities in NW Syria, provision of specialized GBV services to survivors, including case management and referrals for health and MHPSS services, enable the empowerment of GBV women survivors and their families through group discussions, various activities and the distribution of IEC materials, and introduce service providers to proper GBV screening processes and techniques.
SAMS will work on supporting VA services in Termanin and Alnafs Almotmaenna facilities, SAMS will support also outreach team in number of supported health facilities, each outreach team will consist of 2 PSS workers and 2 Physiotherapist, in addition to one team leader which will be leading the outreach teams; SAMS will refer the cases to the specialized services (MHPSS, Physiotherapy, and any other services as needed); SAMS will procure assistive products and consumables to support the direct and indirect victims; Means of transportation services will be provided to GBV survivors and direct and indirect victims. SAMS will build the capacity of service providers.
Through this project, SAMS will work on achieving the following objectives:
Objective 1: Improve the access to specialized quality GBV services for both female and male in the supported locations (HFs and safe space)
Objective 2: Enhance strategies to empower women and girls and raise awareness about GBV in North-West Syria.
Objective 3: Increase the number of direct and indirect victims of explosive hazard accidents receiving needs-appropriate assistance in northwest Syria
Objective 3: Build the capacity of the Service Providers
Continue Implementation Plan section: Mine Action: Provide Physiotherapy consultations and treatment sessions to EH victims. Provide Assistive products and consumables to victims. Provide individual mental health consultation. Provide group sessions and self-care sessions to victims and their families. Provide community-based VA services through outreach team in areas near facilities. 6. Refer the victims to specialized services as needed. SAMS will monitor the project’s activities in terms of achievement and percentage of progress against the targets and will conduct quarterly monitoring visits to the supported faculties. SAMS developed a new Monitoring tool for the GBV activities (which respect the privacy and sensitivity of the cases) it will be used under the supervision of the GBV specialist, SAMS will monitor the quality of the project also, through KAP surveys and semi-annual FGDs. For the VA services, will use Functional Independence Measure, and WHO DAS.

4 - Needs

As per HNO 2020, total PiN for GBV services in Syria 13.6 M (50%F, 50% M). GBV, especially child/forced marriage, sexual harassment and SV (including rape), denial of resources, emotional/psychological violence and physical abuse continue to pervade lives of women and girls, resulting in limited spaces to feel safe. Women, girls, boys, and men confirmed that GBV is everywhere. Physical, emotional, psychological and sexual violence, exploitation and harassment, domestic violence, violence against women and girls and early/forced marriage are types of GBV identified over years. Specifically, humanitarian situation in NWS has experienced continuous deterioration during 2019, as displacement patterns over 2019 saw people impacted by hostilities moving toward densely populated areas of northern Idleb governorate, with Dana SD receiving largest number of individuals. Women and girls that experienced several displacements are extremely vulnerable to additional shocks and at greater risk of harm and exploitation during flight.11.5 M (51% F, 49% M), in 2,562 communities reported explosive hazard contamination in the last 2 years, HNO 2020. 1 in 2 people in Syria live at risk of explosive hazards. These communities are spread throughout the country and correlate to areas that have seen a high intensity of hostilities over last year. Explosive hazard contamination can have severe socio-economic implications on families and communities, depriving them of livelihoods, and increasing vulnerabilities. It also prevents safe delivery of humanitarian assistance, affects population movement and limits recovery efforts. Explosive hazard accidents can result in serious injury and death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families. Number of casualties from explosive accidents has steadily increased burden on the health sector, particularly provision of specialized services including trauma, physical rehabilitation, and psychosocial support.

5 - Implementation of activities

Activities will aim to maintain and expand on current safe spaces in the targeted HFs and outreach services including the recruitment of case managers, CHWs, and PSS workers. SAMS through its activities will provide GBV services (both case management and referrals) to women and girls survivors within the HF safe spaces. In addition, SAMS will provide GBV empowerment to survivors and their families with awareness-raising sessions and distributing IEC materials while utilizing the GBV SC Awareness Raising Tool in which the tool 7 messages will be disseminated to targeted groups of different genders and age groups starting from the root cause of gender-based violence. Alongside the tool, pre and post-tests for targeted groups will be utilized in order to measure improvement along the process. Moreover, and in order to strengthen integration and collaboration of health service providers with the ongoing activities, they will be involved in the different training and awareness sessions provided under this activity including CMR, PSEA, First Responders, etc. On the other hand, referrals will be enhanced through focusing on the First Responders training specifically under the Disclosure and Referral topic while also keeping an updated network of service maps. The prog will provide 76 humanitarian actors (non-GBV) with GBV basics and risk mitigation training. SAMS will provide outreach activities through CHWs including GBV messages and tackling stigma and will advocate on social media campaigns to raise awareness and promote the rights of SGBV survivors. Emergency protection services will be provided including PFA and referrals at zero points. SAMS will develop guidelines on topics related to GBV and produce it to service providers in coordination with GBV Sub-cluster. Also, SAMS will develop guidelines on the integration of male survivors in SAMS GBV activities.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Privacy concerns along with confidentiality and safety might prevent women & girls to access services. Possibility: High Impact: High Mitigation: Ensure that confidentiality principles are in line with global standards, ensure data protection protocols and private physical space are in place. Risk: Part of the populations in the targeted communities may not welcome some intervention. Possibility: High Impact: High Mitigation: Awareness raising about our project in the community. Engaging men and neutral community leaders and informing them about the services. Risk: Women and girls are prevented from attending certain training or sessions, by their husbands, or brothers or fathers. Possibility: High Impact: High Mitigation: Coordinate and communicate with the community leaders to identify our work, in addition to conduct outreach visits. Risk: Beneﬁciaries subject to abuse and abuse by center staff due to poor eﬃciency and lack of knowledge in providing services. Possibility: Low Impact: High Mitigation: The availability of a clear complaint mechanism within the service centres and the deﬁnition of it during the deﬁnition of the beneﬁciaries of the services of the Center and explain the mechanism of its ﬂag "complaints box follows the complaints section directly to the private line and mail moderator complaints section and brochures and the mechanism of regular complaints, in addition to the availability of periodic training for service providers and ensure supervision Effective technical service. Risk: Persons with disabilities, older persons and areas away from centres. Possibility: High Impact: High Mitigation: Outreach teams will be allocated to reach beneﬁciaries who cannot access the centres to participate in activities for people with severe disabilities and elderly people with mobility diﬃculties in remote areas. Beneﬁciaries access will be facilitated by providing transportation as needed to ensure their effective participation.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Attack by GoS and its allies/others. Mitigation measures: Selecting fortified locations; Security assessment (site & building); Activation of contingency plans for each centre in cases of near bombing or close clashes or intrusion or siege or fire; Evacuation SOP; Sourcing alternative locations, in case of need for relocation. Risk: Hostage-taking/Kidnap for ransom. Mitigation measure: Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan. Risk: Control by unaccepted groups/entities or GoS. Mitigation measures: Anti-Terrorism Check (ATC), Pause / Cessation; Information security SOP; Strengthening the relations with the local communities; Relocating the activities locations; Inform the partner civil authorities (Health Directorates and Civil Local Authorities), of the suspension and the reasons, in order to play their role in negotiation and advocacy, and apply the agreements of neutralizing humanitarian facilities. Risk: Theft Mitigation Measures: Safety and security upgrades for SAMS facilities, including CCTV cameras and guards; Photo verification of received shipments, signed waybills, and notification if any missing items noted in HF count within 72 hours; Review of stock/inventory records during monitoring visits; Hostile Environment and Safety Management Training; Access and movement SOPs; Local networks to monitor security situation and trends; Communication plan and local acceptance; Quarterly physical inventory of warehouses; review of monthly consumption reports.Risk: Aid Diversion risks (Cash transfer). Mitigation Measure: SAMS utilizes official channels, authorized by the Government of Turkey, to transfer money from Turkey to Syria. The process is: SAMS request all facilities managers to open a bank account in the PTT. Upon receiving confirmation of the opening the bank accounts SAMS transfer the amounts due to the designated PTT bank account
