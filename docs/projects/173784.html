Project Summary

Access to sanitation is a major concern in camps in Borno state, where new arrivals and dilapidated sanitation infrastructures are observed. This situation encourages open defecation, leading to both sanitation and protection issues, particularly for women and children. The latrines’ access is still below SPHERE standards in most locations. When combined with an unsanitary environment, the low access to WASH facilities and the limited knowledge of basic hygiene practices to prevent waterborne diseases become a major risk in vector control management, particularly in an area regularly impacted by disease outbreaks.
Overall, the various assessments conducted by SI teams in Maiduguri, Monguno, Dikwa and Ngala LGAs underline the poor water and sanitation coverage for both host communities and IDPs. 70% of the community do not have access to disinfected water points and are using groundwater, which exposes them to waterborne diseases. In addition, although 81% of existing water sources are functional in the three assessed LGAs, they are overcrowded due to the continual arrival of IDPs. Thus, the access to water is regularly below emergency Sphere standard and the number of persons per water facility exceeds 500, leading the WaSH sector to count 2880 person/borehole.
In Maiduguri in December 2018, 41% of people interviewed, both IDPs and host communities, reported 30 minutes to one hour as their minimum waiting time at public borehole. The KAP survey conducted in Dikwa in December 2018, highlighted that 48% of IDPs wait for more than an hour to get water. In the most recent KAP survey in Ngala’s 2 IDP camps, 43% of IDPs wait between 30 minutes to 1 hour. The findings of SI’s assessment conducted in early 2020 in Monguno, Dikwa and Ngala, revealed that wash remains precariously high: 37% on average (respectively 30%, 38% and 43%) reported 30min to one hour as their minimum waiting time at public borehole (SI assessment, February-March 2020). The access of sanitation remains equally concerning, especially in camps where, in Dikwa and Monguno, only 19% of respondents have access to sanitation services in each location while in Ngala only 4% of the community within camps have access to pit latrines; mainly due to the caseload available per sanitation facilities. One latrine is available for over 138 persons in Ngala, 98 in Monguno and 63 in Dikwa, compared to SPHERE standard of one toilet for 50 persons for communal toilets.
This situation encourages open defecation, leading to both sanitation and protection issues, particularly for women and children. According to DTM round 30, in Borno, 100 % of latrines (up from 97% in the last round of assessment) were not hygienic in camp and camp-like situations.
SI is thus proposing to intervene in Ngala, Dikwa, Monguno, MMC and Jere LGAs under this project to achieve its specific objective, which is “to improve to sustainable Safe Water, Sanitary facilities n and Hygiene Services to the affected population in Monguno, Dikwa, Ngala, MMC and Jere through:
- Rehabilitation, operation and maintenance of water points in the most vulnerable camps as well as host communities in the 5 LGA’s;
- Rehabilitation of exisiting boreholes/ water points in Ngala and Dikwa LGA;
- Construction and rehabilitation of emergency blocks of latrines and bathing facilities in vulnerable camps and host communities in all the locations;
- Hygiene promotion activities and campaigns with the support of IEC materials;
- Menstrual Health Management activities;
- Distribution of Hygiene and dignity kits

1 - Needs

A major part of Borno State remains inaccessible to humanitarian actors, with limited visibility on the demographics and the needs at stake. Even though the emergency response has been provided to accessible four targeted LGAs (Maiduguri, Monguno, Dikwa, Ngala), the scarcity of direct implementers in these remote areas leaves significant multi-sectorial gaps in WaSH, shelter and FSL. As Wash lead agencies in Borno State, SI addressing some of these gaps through different interventions, ranging from emergency response to early recovery where possible. The formal IDP camps are already overcrowded, the huge number of these IDPs are settling down outside these camps within host communities in informal settlements. IDPs and host communities are thus compelled to share the already stretched resources leading to the insufficient quantity and/or unsafe water, inappropriate housing conditions and inadequate access to shelter, sanitation facilities and health care services. Poor living conditions, overcrowded camps and congestion create an unhealthy environment conducive to outbreaks (cholera/AWD, COVID), leading to tensions/conflicts. Under this project, the SI plan provides life-saving support to these beneficiaries by providing safe drinking water, emergency sanitation facilities and hygiene awareness along with COVID preventive measures while specifically targeting the IDP population within the host communities. This funding is addressing the conflict mitigation strategy and strengthening life-saving services to cope with foreseeable arrivals.

2 - Activities

WASH: SI is currently implementing emergency WASH activities in camps and host communities in the 4 targeted areas of intervention (Maiduguri, Monguno, Dikwa and Ngala) under other grants. Under the proposed project. Under this project, SI will implement the WASH activities but focus on the hard to reach areas, namely Monguno, Dikwa and Ngala. In addition, SI will continue to maintain a WASH response capacity during outbreaks. Some of SI’s objectives under this project are:1) Rehabilitate/Construction of nonfunctional/new WaSH infrastructure and maintenance of those built under previous projects, including boreholes, hand pumps and latrines, as it was identified as the most cost-effective approach to reach a maximum of beneficiaries and ensure the sustainability of WASH services;2) Provision of menstrual hygiene kits under menstrual health management (MHM) approach; 3) Provision of essential hygiene promotion activities to prevent water borne diseases and improve health outcomes for IDP population (shift from messaging to more participatory approaches in terms of a hygiene promotion strategy, following an Approach focused on Behavior Change Determinants (ABCD) – which will be replaced by a COVID-adapted approach during the COVID-19 epidemic.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

SI prioritizes the needs of various vulnerable groups in their programming. Protection of children, women, and people with special needs is a cross-cutting theme across all programs to increase the efficiency of humanitarian assistance. Ignoring the specific needs of vulnerable population groups can have disastrous implications in terms of protection and survival for the victims of humanitarian crisis. Under this project, SI will ensure that participatory methods and human-centered approaches are used to access and incorporate specific needs in all aspects of the project cycle with a specific focus on gender and vulnerable population during community discussions and assessments.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

SI will collect the need of the community since the assessment phase through community consultation and participatory approaches. This would be done by using the mechanism to accurately collect the different community groups' specific needs, considering gender and age specificity, and other specific vulnerabilities. SI will establish through community participation, community-based complaint management committees that members will comprise of elder statement and women of integrity whom are known and well respected by the community members. These people are usually the point of call as traditional means of settling the conflict in the community. SI has already established feedback and complaint response mechanism (FCRM) and feedback, so that community can provide feedback about SI intervention through different channels, i.e., CRM boxes, Hotline numbers, feedback collected by MEAL team on a regular basis. All Solidarites staff have been trained on protection issues by Protection actors and can identify and refer all cases of GBV, child abuse/etc. if needed. SI employees have been trained about PSEA risks. SI is a member of PSEA network in Nigeria. The needs analysis and assessments conducted by SI capture the differences between men and women, and both groups are consulted. Although all the beneficiaries will benefit from the proposed intervention equally, women will be involved in identifying the locations for Water and Sanitation services. Gender and age disaggregated data on programme coverage and impact will be collected, analysed and routinely reported on and programme activities monitored for improvement in self-reliance as well as beneficiary satisfaction for both women and men, girls and boys. SI will use at least 1% of this budget towards protection mainstreaming activities.
