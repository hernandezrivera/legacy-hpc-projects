Project Summary

In line with the HRP objective; To reduce morbidity and mortality Vulnerable crises-affected people through equitable, safe, and dignified access to life-saving assistance to meet their needs and FSL Cluster specific objectives of: 1) Providing food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 4 and 5); 2) Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock; and 3) Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes, this project seeks to address the immediate lifesaving food needs and livelihoods challenges faced by the IDPs, returnees, and host community members in Koch, Guit and Leer counties of Unity state. It will enable vulnerable households affected by the humanitarian crisis to withstand the effects of the food security and livelihood shocks as well as put them back on the road to recovery. Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), returnees, and host community members will be targeted to complement UNIDOR’s relief and recovery activities in Koch, Guit and Leer counties of Unity state. UNIDOR will undertake multiple activities using vulnerability and eligibility criteria factoring gender, disability, poverty, nutrition status, and other social inclusion factors. Activities will include distribution of agricultural and fishing inputs, multi-purpose cash, establishing VSLA groups, and income-generation activities. UNIDOR will also be supporting communities to transition off humanitarian assistance through sustainable and long-term systemic interventions. UNIDOR has identified that flood displaced and other conflict-affected households in Koch, Guit and Leer are facing multiple shocks that has severely limited livelihoods, which has come on top of economic erosion, failed harvests that are stretching the lean season flooding, communal violence, and displacement.
UNIDOR will support 7,000 HHs (42,000 people) with above mentioned interventions across the 3 counties.
UNIDOR has presence and ongoing interventions in Guit, Koch and Leer, in FSL, Nutrition, Health, Wash and Education.

1. Needs

The scale and severity of acute food insecurity in South Sudan remain high due to the protracted negative impacts of conflict and insecurity, poor macro-economic conditions, and high food and NFI prices, combined with the seasonal declines in food stocks and low income-earning opportunities as the dry season progresses in much of the unimodal areas of unity state. As a result, many HHs are projected to continue facing large to extreme food consumption gaps, indicative of Emergency (IPC Phase 4) or worse, between March and May 2024. According to the IPC AMN (July – Sept 2023), the acute food insecurity in South Sudan remains worrying with the latest data showing that 5.83 million people (46.3% of the population) are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity classified as IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). An estimated 35,000 people are in IPC Phase 5 in Jonglei State and Unity State, while 14,000 South Sudanese returnees who fled the ongoing conflict in Sudan are also classified in IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). A further 1.64 million people are in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency). From Sept to Nov 2023, an estimated 653,000 people (58% of the state’s population) are facing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity in Unity State of which 15,000 people are estimated to be in Catastrophe (IPC Phase 5) acute food insecurity, 182,000 people are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and 456,000 people are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. During this period, only Rubkona County is classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, with Leer, Koch and Guit being among the rest of the 9 counties that are classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). During the harvest/post-harvest period of Dec 2023 - March 2024, the food security situation in Unity State will begin to deteriorate with an estimated 691,000 people (61% of the state’s population) likely to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 194,000 people are likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity, and 497,000 people are likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. During period, Leer, Koch, and Guit will be classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. Food security will deteriorate because stocks are likely to be depleted for majority of the households during this period; livestock pests and diseases are likely to continue, hence reducing availability of and access to livestock products; Limited trade flows resulting from high cost of transportation, poor infrastructure, and the conflict in Sudan are likely to result in unusually high staple food prices, thus limiting food access for majority of households. The famine early warning systems network (FEWS NET) reported that an emergency would be declared in several counties during the harvest amid returnee influx; the assessment indicated that during the peak of the lean season in July-August, over 60% of the population experienced significant consumption shortfalls, indicative of a crisis (IPC 3) or an emergency (IPC 4). These factors are compounding the already-existing challenge of obtaining an adequate food supply during the lean season. Although market supplies are likely to increase because of drier conditions that permit road transportation, prices are expected to be higher thus limiting most households’ access to food from the market. Later in the year during the lean season projection period of April - July 2024, the food security situation will seasonally deteriorate in Unity State an estimated 744,00 people (66% of the state’s population) likely to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 202,000 people will likely be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and 542,000 people will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity. During this period Leer, Koch and Guit will be among 6 counties will be classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity.

2. Project activities

CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3)- Distribution of Unconditional cash transfer to the most vulnerable households - Distribution of inputs (fishing kits & quick maturing vegetable seeds)CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock support in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19 (compulsory use of new SOP & guidelines);- Provision of 3,000 Emergency food & livelihood support: fishing kits and vegetable kits to help them meet their food needs from own production and improve livelihood condition - Conducting extension trainings for most vulnerable farmers on modern agricultural techniques- Livelihood support to returnee households through provision of multipurpose cash - Establish kitchen gardens near nutrition sites- Conducting awareness for most vulnerable populations both female and male on environment protection, gender involvement and equality.- CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks in a way that prevents the spread & transmission of COVID 19- Training and supporting agricultural extension workers to provide sessions and technical support to agricultural beneficiaries - Establish and support VSLA groups for vulnerable households to improve savings- Establishment of Farmers Field Schools (FFSs).- Training the beneficiaries in postharvest handling techniques - Establishment and training of project management and AAP committees made up of 60% women and 40% men. - Sharing COVID-19 prevention measures messages
