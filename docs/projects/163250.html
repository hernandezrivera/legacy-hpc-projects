Project Summary

The consequences of the various humanitarian crises in Cameroon are aggravating factors for the deterioration of the already fragile nutritional status of affected populations. Indeed, the prevalence of stunting in children under 5 is above the WHO serious threshold of 30% in all regions affected by humanitarian crises and rates of acute malnutrition do not show significant changes for several years and remain on the poor thresholds. Children under the age of 5 with acute malnutrition are more likely to die than their healthy peers.
Pregnant and lactating women are also particularly affected because they have limited access to adequate quality and quantity of food in a context of limited access to basic social services. Added to this are the close links between the nutritional status of mother and child during the first 1,000 days of life (from conception to the age of two). In a context of massive displacement of people (internally displaced persons or refugees), women and young children are particularly exposed to poor living conditions and the accumulation of trauma that can affect practices feeding infants and young children, in particular the ability of mothers to ensure optimal breastfeeding, further weakening the nutritional status of the already most vulnerable populations
The level of acute malnutrition in the North West and South West regions are not well known as access constraints has hampered the realization of a formal nutrition survey. Nutrition indicators has been integrated into emergency food security assessment (EFSA) conducted in October 2019 and revealed a proxy Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates of 5.1% and 7.4% for the NW and SW regions respectively. Furthermore and prior to the crisis, the 2 regions were facing high levels of stunting and poor Infant and young Children feeding indicators with stunting prevalence of 36% and 28% in NW and SW respectively (MICS 2014), exclusive breast-feeding rates of 42.5% and 58.3% in NW and SW region respectively. Furthermore, the proportion of children who meet their minimum acceptable diet remains extremely low at less than 30% per cent.
The nutrition situation is likely to deteriorate in the coming months since acute malnutrition in children aged 6-59 months is a direct outcome indicator of recent changes in the nutritional status which could be attributed to changes in food security, health and WASH situations among others considering that the displacements started in October/November 2017.
WFP’s programme focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with focus on the first 100days of life. The programme targets all children aged 6-23 months and pregnant and lactating women and girls in food insecure household. The programme activities include:
1.Screening for malnutrition and referral of severe cases for treatment, Social and behavioral change communication in favor of appropriate infant and young child feeding practices and other Family practices, distribution of specialized nutritious food to children aged 6-23months and Pregnant and lactating women and girls(PLWGs) and promotion of the use of the blanket feeding platform for provision of complementary services including immunization, micro-nutrient supplementation, deworming, malaria and other childhood illnesses prevention, family planning etc
WFP in 2020 intends to target about 40,000 beneficiaries across all 6 regions ( North west,24150 and South west15,400 with the package of malnutrition prevention activities
