Project Summary

The intervention will mainly support vulnerable communities affected by the Israeli occupation (Area C) to access standard quality shelters and NFIs. The project will also reinforce the capacities of households and institutions in withstanding crisis caused by different aspects including general context in the West Bank, manmade, and natural crisis in the most affected locations of the West Bank (including H2, Area C as well as Area A and B). Through this project, Action Against Hunger (AAH) will work on three main components in response to the most pressing needs and gaps identified.

Firstly, the project will benefit vulnerable herding and Bedouin families in Area C living in inadequate shelters due to the challenging environment – including restrictions on movement, settler violence and lack of building permits. The proposed shelter interventions include the rehabilitation and insulation of substandard shelters and winterization for vulnerable communities. Families with a member that is living with a disability will be prioritized with accessibility arrangements made within and outside the shelter.
Through these interventions access and accessibility to the shelter will be increased while conditions within the shelter (e.g., insulation) and capacities of the vulnerable families to face adverse weather conditions will be improved thus positively contributing to women, men, boys and girl privacy, safety, and dignity. When implementing this intervention, AAH will adopt it’s newly developed Shelter Model approach. In which, the targeted households will receive support to have a complete residential unit though multi-sectoral interventions. i.e. Not limited to the shelter unit, but also to WASH facilities, Shelter access and fencing, and home garden. This approach aims to Ensuring people’s basic needs are addressed through comprehensive responsive, protective, preventive measures as well as will improve the socio-economic conditions of the people, increase their capacity, and participation through cash-based interventions. AAH has implemented a pilot project based on this approach under the current HF allocation.
Secondly, AAH will support economically vulnerable families in Areas A, B, C and H2 to improve their capacities in developing preventive and responsive measures while having access to limited financial means by distributing essential Nonfood-items (NFI) and hygiene materials at household level. These interventions will positively contribute to protecting the most vulnerable family members, including elderly and people with chronic diseases, while limiting the pressure on bread winners and the household’s budget.
Lastly, the project aims at supporting Palestinian community-based organizations (CBOs) and Palestinian local institutions in reinforcing their emergency capacities by providing care centers to have the necessary equipment (NFI/hygiene) to fulfil their mission. The project is estimated to benefit a total of 6,350 individuals, 15 care centers and 20 CBOs and Local Government Units (LGUs). To ensure achieving this component, AAH will adopt the recently developed localization approach. It aims to ensure adopting AAH localization strategy of AAH and it objectives; Local actors rooted in knowledge and experience, National and Local actors cogenerate/co-construct with Action Against Hunger a mutual institutional capacity, leadership and influence aimed at addressing the needs, and support a people-centered approach and the participation of affected communities in addressing humanitarian needs.

It matches the Shelter framework, in line with the 2023 HNO, and directly support the Shelter Cluster objectives which are inline also with the HRP 2023 general objective, namely SO2 and SO3.

1 - Needs

Based on previous extensive experience and assessments efforts, AAH has identified Area C as well as other locations most affected by settler violence as the two main priority areas for 2023 while being areas very much exposed to protection threats. The below specific needs were mainly identified during the Community Protection Approach (CPA) process, the results of which were released in early 2022: 1. Protection threats: Area C inhabitants cope with regular violations of human rights, destruction and violence from settlers and the Israeli Civil Administration which affects women, men, boys and girls in different manners. The 2023 HNO identified the protection needs of more than 72,000 Palestinian families the majority of which are living in Area C. These needs are related to accommodation (threat of demolition, poor living and sanitation standards, health associated risks and lack of privacy). In nine months with a total of 214 residential structures demolished or confiscated, rendering more than 700 people, half of whom children, homeless and in need of urgent shelter assistance. In Area C alone, and during 2022 there was an increase in the demolition orders issued against Palestinian structures adding to the total number of pending demolition orders. The discussion around the plan of Annexation and the uncertainty about what it would translate into has put an additional pressure on women, men, boys and girls in 2022 and is expected to continue throughout 2023 add significant psychological and physical pressure on the inhabitants of the Area C at risk of transfer. 2. Bad shelter conditions: Water infiltration, insufficient ventilation and lack of insulation present a constant health hazard for Area C’s inhabitants, particularly for women, children and the elderly, as they spend the most time in the shelters. Considering the basic and often temporary nature many shelters in the area, people with disabilities have no arrangements made to suit their needs and are even more marginalized. In the baseline conducted for the ongoing shelter projects implemented during 2022 (HF & Consortium projects), AAH found that almost all the shelter assessed were temporary in nature and more than 50% had no floor (as opposed to concrete floor). These facts justifies the need for immediate response to improve the living conditions of people in these communities. 3. Lack of privacy: Shelters often found in the Area C community fail to meet privacy and safety minimum standards for women, men, boys, and girls. In the baseline of the ongoing shelter projects implemented during 2022, AAH found that the average number of rooms is 1.7 or less. There is a clear need to construct/rehabilitate salters with proper and sufficient space as well as internal partitions required to provide some kind of privacy mainly for women. 4. Lack of availability of shelters and access to shelters: the population suffers from a significant shortage of basic shelter units. According to the MSNA, the updated assessment for these communities showed that there are more than 40,000 people are in urgent need to rehabilitate their existing shelters. This situation also applies to residents in East Jerusalem, who suffer from a large housing deficit due to the significant restrictions imposed on new construction, forcing people to find alternatives in other locations. 5. General hygiene practices and awareness needs: AAH has identified remaining gaps with their institutional partners including: 1. Lack of access to hygiene items: Being economically vulnerable families, most of the farmers and herding families living in Area C have reduced the incentive of households to invest in the hygiene materials necessary to maintain preventive and responsive measures as they compete with food and basic needs expenditures. The post monitoring exercise led by AAH with beneficiaries of hygiene kit distribution in 2021, more than 70% of the assessed beneficiaries stated that they could not afford these items from the market. The provided response matched with their family needs in terms of hygiene; 51% of the families stated that they agreed and 49% strongly agreed with such statement. 2. Lack of means of Palestinian institutions and service providers: budget restrictions & lack of available stocks has brought many service providers in the West Bank to turn to NGOs and donors for support. Some of the care centres still lack the necessary equipment and NFI to be able to stay operational as long as needed in 2023. All the needs outlined above will be corroborated through planned field visits by AAH teams. These visits should also help to provide accurate assessments in terms of the number of beneficiaries and type of interventions based on the agreed selection criteria. Specifically, the technical assessment implemented by AAH team at the targeted communities stated that the majority of the shelters at the targeted communities are in urgent need of rehabilitation works and prioritized 80 residential shelters including 10 PWDs. These shelters lack the minimum shelter standards with their residents being subjected to harsh weather conditions. I addition, most of the HH at the targeted communities are at risk of exposure or affected by natural or manmade hazards (e.g., winter storms) and in urgent need to have 50 winterization kits and NFIs.

2 - Activities

The proposed activities aim to protect vulnerable families through an appropriate response to their immediate needs, thus increasing their resilience to manmade and natural shocks and integrating risk reduction measures during the implementation. These activities will also support these families in withstanding the consequences of these shocks to be better equipped to implement the preventive and responsive measures to withstand them. Said activities will respond to the critical identified needs of women, men, girls and boys, to improve their living conditions by upgrading their shelters to the minimum standards. Safe access to better sanitation facilities will improve women’s health and provide increased privacy through internal partitions. The project will capitalize on AAH’s extensive technical experience in implementing shelter activities in the targeted areas and will also benefit from other experience among other partners and cluster guidelines. Activity 1: Rehabilitation and/or replacement of 80 substandard shelters including 10 shelters for People with disability (PWD) all located in Area C. The work will be done taken in consideration technical, cultural and protection considerations, diverse needs of women, men, boys, girls, and the elderly. As well as 10 shelters for people with disabilities. This activity will be implemented based on the latest “Shelter Model Approach” mentioned in the project summary section above. Considering this approach, and in order to cover all shelter need as a complete residential unit, AAH will provide essential shelter NFIs, hygiene and disinfection materials to 450 individuals with focus on families and individuals living in substandard and overcrowded shelters. In order to Do No Harm, where the security situation requires it, direct beneficiary implementation with focus on cash component will be adopted as a method of implementation for the rehabilitation to avoid exposure and to mitigate protection risks. Technical solutions will be adapted accordingly. Activity 2: Provision of 50 winterization kits and NFIs, including plastic cover sheets – Area C, for Bedouin and herder dwellings and most vulnerable households at risk of exposure or affected by natural or manmade hazards (e.g., winter storms), to mitigate winter-related hazards. Appropriate kitchen kits will also be distributed where needed. Activity 3: Provision of adequate and timely standard or customized NFIs – mainly Area A & B to 15 care centers, considering the diverse needs of vulnerable, elderly, and individuals subject to natural or manmade hazards. Activity 4: Capacity building of 20 CBOs & LGUs by training and tools for emergency response, self-recovery, or protection measures to be able to support internally displaced people (IDPs) and affected people during and post emergencies. Under this activity AAH will be considering the localization approach mentioned earlier in order to ensure that the localization concept is adopted within these interventions.

3 - Indicators

A baseline and endline survey will be conducted by the MEAL team at AAH for all the above-mentioned interventions. This survey will facilitate the supervision, monitoring and evaluation of the activities, identify possibilities of improvement and quantify the advantages brought to the communities by the project. AAH will also systematically conduct a gender and age analysis and produce sex and age disaggregated data throughout the follow up of its indicators. Satisfaction and dignity of people with disability and different gender group will be monitored at the end of the project. Additionally, project activities will be monitored through (1) working field staff; (2) community representatives; (3) beneficiary lists and donation certificates; (4) donor and cluster monitoring visits; (5) project progress and impact reports. Accountability and transparency will be ensured through a beneficiary feedback mechanism that will be carefully monitored. The protection committees and village councils will support AAH to identify and liaise with beneficiaries, provide ongoing field monitoring of the rehabilitated shelters, and advise on the protection concerns as well as the ongoing security situation to determine the optimal timing of field operations. The expected objective verifiable indicators from the project are: # of shelter units rehabilitated or reconstructed – target 80 # of shelter units made accessible for people with disability – target 10 # of winterization kits distributed – target: 50 # of people supported with winterization assistance – target: 275 individuals including 74 Boys; 70 Girls; 66 Men; 65 Women and 3 PWD # of vulnerable HHs supported with proper NFIs, hygiene, disinfection materials and awareness sessions for and other chronic disease – target: 80 (450 individuals) # of care centers supported – target 15 # of people in care centers supported – target: 680 # of CBOs ad LGUs received training and tools for emergency response – target: 20. To further ensure protection is mainstreamed throughout the project implementation, the protection mainstreaming indicator (PMI) “% of communities/project beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” is added and will be measured through an end line survey of the beneficiaries.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Throughout the whole project cycle AAH ensures protection mainstreaming and considers safety and dignity, meaningful access, accountability as well as participation and empowerment of paramount importance. To ensure safety and do no harm specifically in projects targeting Area C like this one, AAH prioritises a context specific risk analyses and seeks legal support from peer agencies to limit the risk of destruction and confiscation of assets by the Israeli authorities and to avoid exposing further communities. Overall AAH seeks to complement its own actions with for instance legal support to inform risk mitigation measures and consequently to increase complementary humanitarian action. Safety is considered in the rehabilitation of the shelter itself (e.g. safe material, safe arrangements for children) as well as in the choice of the activities themselves. In terms of dignity, when feasible, the household members will themselves participate in the rehabilitation of their shelter, therefore ensuring their ownership of the work and contribution to the improvement of their own space. This modality of implementation will further avoid exposure of the project and participants to security risks and mitigate protection risks. Increased privacy will be a result of shelter improvements (through filling gaps and building internal partitions), particularly important for the needs of women and girls. The project will also contribute to increased protection of the families at all times. By not only improving shelter conditions do the families will get improved comfort but the project will also provide necessary hygiene and disinfection kits in order to keep the shelters clean and safe for individuals who quarantine inside. AAH staff will prevent doing harm by respecting international guidelines in terms of contact with beneficiaries and communities and will wear adequate protective equipment during distributions and meetings. AAH has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff, who have the obligation to adhere to the organisation code of conduct based on international standards. AAH also has in place gender focal points at base and mission levels, as well as safe and confidential ways to report abuse. AAH’s gender and protection manager in country assists project teams to ensure gender and protection considerations are included throughout the whole project cycle. The indicator “% of communities/ project beneficiaries reporting that humanitarian assistance is delivered in a safe and dignified manner” will be measured through an end line survey of the beneficiaries.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As one of AAH’s core values, AAH thrives to prevent discrimination and to ensure equal and impartial access though transparency and accountability to affected populations. AAH with its partners aims for all community members to be involved and consulted as much as possible in project design and implementation, as well as evaluation, including through household and community consultations and focus group discussions, accessible to all. In order for their participation to be meaningful, AAH focused on ‘qualitative inclusion’ meaning safe spaces for beneficiaries to participate. In addition, AAH started in its assessments to consider ‘barriers’ of particularly vulnerable groups and ‘enabling factors’ for people with specific needs. A monthly meeting will be conducted at community level to update beneficiaries and community leaders relating to project development and challenges. With regard to access in this specific project, AAH will work on achieving equal and impartial access through prioritizing people and groups on the basis of needs, using the same standards for prioritisation and for construction; final list of beneficiaries will be based on needs and technical assessment as well as consultation of communities and key stakeholders. Thus, AAH aims to consult with everyone, ensuring access to vulnerable people and people with specific needs to ensure that all their needs are met. The project will also provide both women and men with the same benefits for their input and their work in construction, preventing discrimination to female headed households.
