Project Summary

Throughout 2023, Violet Organization is planning to implement comprehensive SNFI interventions by applying a variety of modalities such as in-kind, cash, and vouchers to address the emergency needs of the different groups with the most suitable modality.
The proposed project will effectively contribute to addressing the life-saving and life-sustaining response through the provision of shelter and seasonal non-food items as well as NFIs assistance to the most vulnerable and IDPs which will help to avoid negative coping strategies. the planned activities for 2023 are the following activities
1. Provision of essential and Core NFI items for 2000 HHs through the provision of non food items by modality
2. Distribution of seasonal and winterization assistance to 12000 households through different modalities including cash, Voucher, and in-kind assistance.
3. Distribution of shelter kits to 1000 households
4. Rehabilitation of 500 partially damaged and unfinished houses
5. repair of 500 tents and replacement of 800 tents.
To ensure no overlap will occur, Violet coordinated with its partners who are funding Violet's projects and only submitted the activities that are not reported by other partners. Moreover, Violet will ensure close coordination in both fields and Turkey levels and the through the SNFI cluster to ensure a coordinated and effective humanitarian response. However, Violet coordinated with its donors / partners including CARE and SCI to design the HRP and took into account their submission. So Violet doesn't have any any overlap with the other intermediate donors.
Violet requires 4,191,000 to implement the above activities. Unfortunately, no fund is secured yet for 2023. However, form the previous experience and Historical data, Violet has the capacity to implement up to 6000,000 USD

3 - Needs

Northwest Syria is classified as an area with a severity of needs according to OCHA reports, in 2023, 4.1 million people will require humanitarian assistance; out of the total population in Northwest Syria ( 4.8 million people) including 1.8 million people living in camps with needs that keep increasing across all sectors. Across Syria, the 2022-2023 winter season is anticipated to be one of the harshest considering the catastrophic fuel and electricity shortages, with approximately 6 million out of the 14.6 million people in need requiring winter assistance. This represents a 33 per cent ncrease compared to 2021. At least 2.2 million of the 2022 winter caseloads are in the Syria Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) response areas. Temperatures routinely fall below freezing in the elevated, mountainous parts of the country whilst the plains are prone to flooding. Vulnerable communities, particularly the displaced residing in camps and in temporary settlements face greater risks to their health, and safety. The risks of fires in tented areas due to the improper use of heating and cooking stoves are also heightened during the winter.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Risk of discrimination if the service is provided to a specific group and others are excludedLikelihood: Medium; Impact: High Before implementation, Violet conducts assessments and selects vulnerable communities based on severity; selection criteria (eligibility/vulnerability) transparently communicated through the AAP team. AAP team has access to service mapping to communicate informed with communities about alternative services.2) PWD or at-risk groups might be excluded from assistance or distribution. Likelihood: Low; Impact: High Information sharing and distribution points are suitable for PWDs, conduct AAP sessions to inform about requesting special assistance (transportation/delivery) for PWD/elderly/pregnant BNFs, consultation with stakeholders to identify excluded beneficiaries, training staff on inclusion.3) Risk of GBV to and from or in overcrowded distributions Likelihood: Medium; Impact: High Selection of suitable distribution (time, location, accessibility), referral of special cases, avoid long queues and allow clear space for waiting, operate with multiple distribution sites.4) At-risk group do not receive needed services Likelihood: Medium; Impact: Medium Coordination with relevant sectors to avoid duplications, collaborate with local protection team to ensure equitable access to services, consult BNFs in sex-segregated FGDs, systematically use Washington Group Questions as tool to include disability in selection, consider intersectionality in targeting


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Violet targets PSEA risk through a) training on PSEA for staff, BNFs, vendors; b) integrating PSEA in AAP with safe and accessible complaints mechanism; c) appointment of PSEA focal points in each location to handle reports of misconduct, d) support to survivors through case management, e) regular information of affected community on their entitlements.Potential risks:1) Request of favors, e.g. SEA, for selecting BNFs to the services Mitigation: clear information on the provided services in terms of quality and quantity and that nothing is expected in return in any case, emphasise on complaints mechanisms, provision of multiple channels to raise complaints, referral to inter-agency hotlines as alternative, ensure appropriate staffing during any distribution or service provision. 2) SEA misconduct from staff to selected beneficiaries or staff Mitigation: ensure regular staff training on PSEA and how to engage with beneficiaries, communication of PSEA policy and complaints mechanisms to BNFs, ensure diverse and gender balanced project and MEAL teams, clear visibility of staff, avoid overcrowded distribution sites and allocate clear waiting areas, identify at-risk households and provide special services e.g. transportation or delivery, participation of protection team in the distribution, if needed. Violet will conduct sex-segregated FGDs and KIIs to ensure participation of women and girls during all phases of the project, especially in design of the activities and monitorin


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- Logistical operations are affected by the security situation like transportation, storing the assistanceMitigation: The safety & Security team will study all possible scenarios for the storing & transportation & the distribution will be HHs to HHs not in one place2- inability to respond to the IDPs within the first phase of their displacementMitigation: VO has an IDPs tracking initiative by setting communication channels with all LCs in the whole community which usually hosts IDPs, then once an emergency occurs, the LC will inform VO focal points to respond immediately3. HC HHs might ask to include their families in the projectMitigation: Selection criteria will be clear with no chance for personal judgment taking into consideration the participation of the local communities as references & the IDs information. MoUs will be signed with all local councils to clarify the project's scope of work which will facilitate the processes in their communities & apply the selection criteria transparently4: COVID-19 transmission as a result of the actionMitigation: adjusting the activities & applying the preventive measures including sterilizing the Distribution sites, wearing the masks during the distribution, screening the BNFs temperature before getting into the distribution site & supplying the distribution point with adequate hygiene solutions, minimizing the number of BNF at the distribution site & setting different distribution sites at once to avoid the overcrowding
