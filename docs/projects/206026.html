Project Summary

Leer and Mayendit counties populations are the most affected by many years of conflict, flood and other natural disasters with majority of the population being food insecure, in IPC 4 in Mayendit and IPC 3 in Leer. The number of people bordering IPC 5 catastrophe and Emergency IPC 4 are continuing to increase in the two counties. The Torrential rains across South Sudan have repeatedly devastated several parts of the country in the year 2020 and in 2021, rainfall continue to cause floods. OCHA has placed the current affected population at 10,000, from a total projected total Mayendit county population of 66,015 and a total of 26,145 in Leer County. According to Inter-Agency Flood Assessment Report, Mayendit and Leer County, Unity State, 18th – 21st August 2021, 7,971 people have voluntarily moved out of Mayendit South due to the flood. An estimated population of 15,000 people affected by floods are yet to be evacuated from Mayendit South. As the IDPs integrate in the community, there is pressure on resources in Mayendit North. An estimated total of 24,000 people affected by floods in Mayendit South are expected to move to Mayendit North. According to the IRNA reports, some of the displaced populations moved to Leer County. This is despite the fact that in Leer County a total of 6,944 households and 41,664 individuals have been affected by floods. An estimated 23% of 66,015 people in Mayendit North is already or will be affected by the current flood as the water level keeps rising. Malaria, Diarrhea and injuries were highly recorded in the health facilities.
The floods have affected WASH facilities in Mayendit County and a total of 13 hand pumps were reported submerged and are not accessible, while 20 hand pumps were reported flooded but accessible by the communities. Besides the submerged boreholes, coalition for humanity has identified more than 50 boreholes that are broken down and need rehabilitation in Leer County while those flooded but functional require raising of the platform to minimize contamination. Due to floods, 43 household and 18 institutional latrines were reported to have collapsed. People have to wait till late to ease themselves and this put women and girls in danger of rape and sexual abuse. WASH facilities in schools are also affected, 18 schools (learning spaces) were reported flooded, and children were relocated to highlands.
Open defecation has become unsafe especially for women living with disability, who have increasingly faced sexual abuse and violence at night. On 12th July one rape case was reported in Pilieny Payam. Cases of rape, sexual harassment and abuse are common during dry season because perpetrators have been reported to take advantage of the women and girls who wake up very early to take their water buckets at the water points so as to avoid longer hours of queueing. Conflicts at water points among women and girls are very common. Intimate partner violence is very common when women delay at water points. It arises as a result of men suspicion that their women could have been with other men at the water points hence their delay to come back home. Women and girls remain vulnerable to SGBV cases. Cases of women fighting at water points over the limited access to water at current IDP settlement in Mayendit North have been reported. Communities’ social institutions like women and child friendly spaces got flooded hence access to justice system whether legal or customary courts to handle GBV related complains are rendered unfunctional. Women report to NGO staff cases of assault, but some don’t due to fear and shame that comes along the ordeal. Personal hygiene is very poor and women of the childbearing age and adolescent girls find it hard to cope during their monthly menstrual cycles leading to increase of women & girls’ health/hygiene risks. There is need to rehabilitate water points close to where women live to reduce congestion at one water points.

1. Needs

There is need for rehabilitation and disinfection of boreholes damaged by floods, rehabilitation and construction of latrine blocks in schools and other institutions housing the IDPs. There is also need for provision of safe place for women and girls who are displaced, as well as supporting them with menstrual hygiene management kits. According to GBV risk analysis conducted by coalition for Humanity, women have indicated that there is lack of lighting, and are unable to use the few available WASH facilities. Therefore, there is need to provide a source of lighting for the latrine blocks to be constructed in the institutions that have been hosting IDPs. Based on increased needs, it’s important that the project expands coverage to the 8 affected payams in Leer County as well as 4 payams in Mayendit north in order to respond to the WASH gap created due to floods. Due to movement, congestion, it is recommended that awareness raising on hygienic use of latrines, water treatment be enhanced. There is need for protection monitoring, awareness raising on Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) as well as establishment of SGBV referral pathways to help the displaced vulnerable women and girls to access services. Coalition for Humanity is currently implementing a WASH project in Leer and Mayenit County. The leer project is in partnership with Concern Worldwide, funded by GAC. The current project covers only 4 Payams, out of 16, while 9 payams are now affected by floods. The Mayendit project was funded by South Sudan Humanitarian fund and rehabilitated only 12 boreholes. The current project does not have budget for further rehabilitation of boreholes, and had limited budget for latrine blocks. Coalition for humanity has projects running in Leer County and Mayendit counties but with limited resources that cannot respond to the increased needs. Coalition for Humanity is seeking funding to scale up its response.Based on experience from previous projects, beyond repairing broken boreholes, there is need to raise the platforms, this is because the repaired boreholes without raising the platforms can not be used during flooding and allow water contamination. Coalition for Humanity has also learned that female latrine placed side by side to male latrines is a barrier to usage, therefore moving forward, 1 latrine block can not have a male and female door, all doors on one block will be dedicated to one gender and different gender blocks will not be placed close to each other. Coalition for Humanity has also learned the shortage of locally available materials in Leer and Mayendit counties, this has made it impossible to implement pure CLTs without subsidy. The soils structure in Leer county is sandy prone to collapsing, with a high-water table, that allows ground water sipping into latrines making it full. Therefore, for subsequent projets, CH will pilot an improved latrine lined with concrete rings made with water proof cement covered at the bottom to avoid ground water sipping. Coalition for Humanity has recognized the importance of local leaders and opinion leaders in influencing change, the local leaders will be engaged to champion adoption of improved sanitation in their locations.

2. Project activities

Lack of Sanitation facilities leads to open defecation, which contaminates water, and food, causing diseases like diarrhoea, typhoid, dysentery, and polio. The provision of WASH facilities will reduce open defecation, reduce contamination of food and water and improve access to clean safe water. Most of the time, women and girls fear reporting cases of Gender Bases Violence (GBV) and Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). This project will monitor GBV and protection risks, raise awareness against GBV and SEA. This action will improve the knowledge and awareness of women and girls, they will be able to report the cases of GBV and SEA in the community. The project will design referral pathways and connect women and girls to reliable service providers. In order to stop GBV and SEA, the leaders must establish laws and punish perpetuators, the punishment for such crimes will establish a social norm against such crimes against women and girls. By Building the capacity of leaders through trainings, they will improve their knowledge, awareness about GBV, PSEA, they will come up with community-based redress mechanisms for affected women and girls, they will be able to preside over GBV cases in a fair manner hence reducing the suffering of vulnerable women and girls, by reducing cases of GBV and PSEA. Coalition for Humanity will focus on the following specific activities.Activity 1211.1: Construction and Repair of 23 Boreholes and improving their resilience to flooding.Activity 1211.2: Capacity Building of Water resource management committeeActivity 1212.1: Training and capacity building of hygiene promoters and staff Activity 1212.2: Conduct Hygiene Promotion SessionActivity 1212.3: Provision of Menstrual Hygiene Kits and sensitizationActivity 1213.1: Construction and maintenance of 15 sex segregated communal latrinesActivity 1213.2: Promote construction of 1,000 household latrines through Social Behaviour change.Activity 1214.1: Increase protection monitoring, GBV risk analysis and advocacy Activity 1214.2: Improve capacity of 120 community leaders in protection risk identification, analysis and community-based mechanisms of preventing sexual exploitation, and abuseActivity 1214.3: Improving capacity of 20 men in prevention of GBVActivity 1214.4: Awareness raising on GBV protection and PSEA
