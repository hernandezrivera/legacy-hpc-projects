Project Summary

The main goals to provide a live-saving intervention that will prevent the death of children due to malnutrition and contribute to reduction and prevention of malnutrition in communities affected by the Anglophone crisis.
Specific Objective:
• To improve access and utilization of nutrition and basic health services in primary health care facilities in accessible communities
• To improve the knowledge and practice of members of communities on good nutrition and key household practices
• Improve coordination and integration of nutrition specific intervention with other multi-sector nutrition sensitive interventions at the community level.
Position mapping of all relevant stakeholders to identify community focal point and ensure continual feedback from the community, which should improve program outcome. Community mobilization and sensitization: H4BF will utilize the existing structure of community volunteers (CVs) provided by the existing humanitarian actors in the various communities to access and reach out to these vulnerable populations.
In situations where H4BF confirms the existing structures of CVs is faulty, H4BF will work closely with the community leaders to select members of the community based on established criteria who are living in the community to be engaged in direct implementation of the program.
H4BF will ensure that CVs continuously engage and sensitize members of the community on key household practices including hygiene promotion and good nutrition practice. Women in the community including grandmother, mothers of SAM/MAM children, children with disabilities and women of childbearing age will be encouraged to form joint action-oriented Infant and Young Child Feeding IYCF support groups. The activities of this support groups will be facility by community leads supervised and supported by IYCF-trained health workers. The community leads and where necessary referred to nearest health facility for skilled attention and counselling by health workers will properly counsel mothers with difficulties in IYCF practices. Active Case Finding: Selected members of the community referred to as community leads, will be trained by H4BF with support from UNICEF on active case finding using MUAC tapes. Trained community leads will be subjected to a standardization test to ensure that those with precision and accuracy in MUAC data collection are utilized. Group of community leads will be supervised by H4BF staff trained on CMAM and IYCF with experience in nutrition programming and understand the dynamic context of NE intervention. Children. H4BF shall collaborate with UNFPA sub cluster to train GBV case manager and social workers to manage survivors of GBV and provide psychosocial support to affected women and children.
Children identified to be SAM, will be referred to the nearest outpatient therapeutic program OTP within the communities for treatment. Community leads shall monitor the progress of SAM treatment of children they referred, to ensure that default rate is mitigated. Home visits to households of SAM children to ensure adherence to treatment protocol at household level to achieve cure rate above SPHERE standard. Mothers of children identified with moderately acute malnutrition will be counsel or referred to HF IYCF corners where such services exist for counselling. Such mothers will be referred to join an action oriented support group to improve her knowledge and practice of good nutrition.
The Above intervention will be carryout within 8 districts areas in North West, 4 in Mezam (Bamendakwe, Mankon, Nkwen, Bali and Santa) and in 3 Donga Mantung (Nkambe, Nwa, Misaje and Ako)

1 - Besoins / Needs

Prior to the crisis, the regions presented vulnerabilities to malnutrition with high rates of stunting of 36% and 28% in NW and SW respectively. Furthermore, poor indicators on Infant and young child feeding practices were reported with 42.5% and 58.3% of exclusive breast-feeding rates for NW and SW region respectively. Complementary feeding also appears compromised, with only 30 per cent of children 6-23 months consuming the minimum acceptable diet and over 40% (44.3% NW and 41.9% SW) of children aged 6-59months were reported to have anaemiaLots of progress have been made in addressing malnutrition related issues caused by NWSW crisis insurgency as reported by recent surveys. Elderly, persons with disabilities, orphaned, separated or unaccompanied children are also at a higher risk of malnutrition within Mezam and Donga Mantung as they have limited access to services and humanitarian assistance . In NWSW regions, more than 15% of the women of reproductive age are at risk of malnutrition or malnourished, it rises at more than 20% for the pregnant women; Women are face constraints in accessing humanitarian services, including food, because of insecurity, cultural discrimination and limited mobility leading to increased physiological requirements. However, as the lean season gets to its peak, household foods stores are completely depleted, and purchasing power significantly stretched with consistent increase in food prices and lingering recessive national economic condition, women and girls as well as children with disabilities are more at risk of becoming malnourished. the prevalence of stunting in children under 5 is above the WHO the serious threshold of 30% in all regions affected by humanitarian crises, and rates of acute malnutrition do not show significant changes for several years and remain on the poor thresholds. Children under the age of 5 with acute malnutrition are more likely to die than their healthy peers. Pregnant and lactating women are also particularly affected because they have limited access to adequate quality and quantity of food in a context of limited access to basic social services. Added to this are the close links between the nutritional status of mother and child during the first 1000days.There is high need for a nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive intervention in other to preserve and prevent the nutritional status of these vulnerable groups from deteriorating; otherwise all efforts made through the humanitarian response will be lost to obvious aggravating factors. Displaced Women and girls within Mezam (Bamendakwe, Mankon, Nkwen, Bali and Santa) and Donga Mantung (Nkambe, Nwa, Misaje and Ako) are forced to reduce their food intake as a coping strategy in favor of other household members contributing to under-nutrition among women and girls. Some displaced women and girls in Bamenda are forced to exchange their bodies for money to enable them get food and basic commodities; this is contributing to increase in GBV and human rights violation. regions, COVID-19 restrictions further reduced trade flows between goods from cities to villages and hence affecting staple food prices. Families lose members, are forced to migrate to safer regions and are separated from their relatives and supportive networks; adults are traumatized or too busy trying to cope with the crisis to provide support to spouses or offspring.
