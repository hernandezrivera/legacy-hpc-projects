Project Summary

In 2019, NRC will aim to assist government offices and local authorities in the rehabilitation of Sewage Treatment Plants and in the improvement of Solid Waste Disposal solutions in all NRC’s operational areas. NRC will also operate, maintain, manage and advocate for existing water and sanitation services and promote an enabling environment for good hygiene practices in protracted displacement settings where dignified, voluntary return or resettlement is not possible.

Needs

As Iraq moves into a stage of early recovery, it is expected that the process of internally displaced persons (IDPs) returning to their areas of origin will continue. Though 2018 was marked by significant returns, the number of returnees across the country remained below those projected within the 2018 HRP. During the first seven months of the year, the International Organisation for Migration (IOM) recorded 736,428 individuals as returnees; however, nearly two million individuals remain recorded as internally displaced. 64% of the IDP population reside in five governorates; Anbar, Baghdad, Dohuk, Kirkuk and Ninewa, with Ninewa as the recorded destination for 74% of returnees during the same period. The protracted conflict in Iraq has resulted in massive damage to municipal water supply systems, distribution networks and treatment facilities – in some cases, these facilities have been completely destroyed. Although most of the damaged water supply infrastructures in retaken areas such as Ninewa, Kirkuk and Anbar have been fully or partially rehabilitated over the past year, as existing capacity and resources have been focused on these activities, many districts in Iraq suffer from insufficient access to potable water and inadequate sanitation services. A continued influx of returnees to these areas will serve to put these services under increased stress, which has a negative impact on the country’s public health as a whole. Moving forward, humanitarian actors have been particularly encouraged to consider the needs of communities in the area of solid waste and faecal sludge management. In the response so far, these issues have been necessarily deprioritised in the face of the maintenance of WASH infrastructure – however, a lack of solid waste and faecal sludge management currently constitute a danger to public health and the environment. In particular, vector control and pollution of waterways and rivers is a serious concern.In camps, the Iraq WASH Cluster has emphasised the continued need for rapid rehabilitation of infrastructure, as approximately 229,000 IDPs in 33 camps are receiving below the minimum standards of water per person per day. In addition, sanitation and latrine coverage in 22 IDP camps in Iraq are below minimum standards, with poorly designed or substandard sanitation facilities in some IDP camps putting almost half a million people in camps and surrounding areas at risk of water-borne diseases as a result.

Activities

In 2019, NRC will gradually move away from the rehabilitation of Water Supply Infrastructure and put more focus on Solid Waste and Faecal Sludge Management, especially as the capacity of government to do this type of work grows. NRC will also continue to provide advice and support to our Camp Management and Protection teams in relation to the ‘care & maintenance’ of WASH services within existing camps and to advocate on behalf of the most vulnerable IDPs and returnees forced to remain in those camps. NRC will retain some limited capacity to respond, as and when appropriate to small scale emergencies involving groups of people displaced, in transition and upon return to places of origin. In NRC’s other areas of operation including Anbar governorate, NRC’s WASH Engineers will consult with engineers from the Directorates of Water to identify Sewage Treatment Plants and Sewage Lifting Stations for rehabilitation. Those that can be easily and repaired at a relatively low cost, with a high cost to impact ratio, will be targeted first. Contract documents with Bills of Quantities, Designs and Specifications and will be drawn up and put out for tender to qualified and experienced contractors. After signing of contracts and mobilization of successful contractors NRC will supervise and manage the works at hand reporting directly to the relevant Directorate of Water. Modalities for the management and clearing of Solid Waste will be discussed and devised with the municipal authorities and managed by NRC.NRC Camp Management teams will liaise with their WASH colleagues to ensure WASH facilities in existing camps in Anbar and Ninewa are maintained and improved where funds allow, as services are expected to continue to decline throughout the year. NRC’s Camp Management team will continue to monitor needs and gaps, and share key concerns with the WASH Cluster for response, responding directly in Anbar and Ninevah where possible. Where populations are found to be on the move, NRC will retain an emergency capacity to respond to their needs in the WASH sector in the most appropriate manner.

Protection Risk Analysis

Those already displaced or facing imminent displacement due to drought and scarcity of water – The situation in the south of the country and the emergence of a new IDP caseload has recently been highlighted by the Iraqi government, the media and donors. This group of vulnerable people takes centre stage, not least because of possible implications for regional security. The effective breakdown of government, mass movement of people and rising levels of food insecurity and lack of basic WASH services could provide a fertile breeding ground for ISIS sponsored terrorism and a repeat of the last five years of conflict. Protracted Displacements - those living in protracted displacement (both in and out of camps), are unable to return due to insecurity, destruction of homes, disruption to basic services, movement restrictions, or fear of social tension or harassment. In some cases, protracted displacement could become permanent as people decide to settle in areas away from their former homes, which will be driven by the changing demographic geography seen in disputed areas. Returnees are divided into two sub groups - firstly, those returning to urban areas where water supply systems are gradually coming back on line and government offices are quickly building capacity and getting ready to receive larger support packages. Secondly, smaller and more distinct groups of people moving back to war damaged villages and peri-urban areas. These latter group are receiving less attention, and protection risks can be associated with this demographic as their needs are more likely to be overlooked.

Mitigation Measures

In order to mitigate protection risks to vulnerable beneficiaries, latrines will be carefully placed and located in well-lit areas, and disabled access to WASH facilities will be carefully studied and implemented where necessary. Disabled individuals will be identified before construction work commences, and changes will be made to designs in order to ease access to facilities including ramps, holding bars and lowering of handwashing facilities. Consultation with individuals identified to be vulnerable will be integral to project design. Awareness raising methodologies will take into account the age, gender and diversity of the civilian population, ensuring adequate access and learning possibilities for populations who are generally excluded from decision-making processes. NRC will also engage in a rollout of safe market-based interventions. NRC will analyze vendor locations based on protection concerns, access constraints and safety per beneficiaries’ age, vulnerability and gender. This will act as mitigation measure to avoid the possible of exploitation and abuse of the most vulnerable members of the displaced and host communities.

How is your project accountable to affected population

NRC believes that the most important aspect of humanitarian interventions is that the assistance is actually accountable to the people it aims to support. This accountability – both in terms of quality of assistance and ensuring that it addresses real needs – is critical for humanitarian actors to maintain their relevance to affected communities. NRC understands that Quality and Accountability (Q&A) of our intervention can only be realised through vigilant and conscious efforts at community and organizational level. Throughout the implementation stages of the response phase, the application of Sphere Standards, the Accountability to Affected Population (AAP) Framework, and the NRC Code of Conduct are emphasized. During the assessment and design stage, NRC ensures active participation from the affected community through consultations, meetings, interviews and focus group discussions and integrates findings into the design. NRC has several accountability mechanisms in place to ensure that the affected communities are well informed and aware of modes for approaching NRC with feedback at all stages of project cycle. These enable and empower communities in holding the organization accountable towards the humanitarian principles and code of conduct. Throughout all programming, NRC shares planned project activities with communities through community committees that are established when the organisation initiates a project. These community structures facilitate transparent communication directly with the organisation, and enables NRC to respond directly to feedback and improve programming accordingly. NRC also establishes and runs Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRM) as per well-defined Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). NRC shares information about the CFRM through banners, flyers and community-based awareness raising. The affected population are able to provide complaints and feedback through dedicated hotline numbers, staff field visits, complaints and suggestion boxes and via email. NRC uses this mechanism to resolve any issues arising and adjust programming plans to better suit the local context. In the SoPs of CFRM, clear and well-defined guidelines are available for sensitive and serious issues including issues related to protection. Trained and dedicated staff managed these issues effectively and timely. Dedicated accountability staff reports directly to senior management. The organization applies participatory approach within its Monitoring & Evaluation (M&E) process, based on the principles of Accountability i.e. confidentially, accessible, safe, transparent, response, feedback and records. NRC’s feedback and information mechanisms ensure that communication with communities is carried out in the language, formats and media that are easily understood by the communities, respectful and culturally appropriate of different members of community especially vulnerable and marginalized. Our staff pays particular attention to the gender, age, and diversity issues of collecting, responding to, and analysing feedback.
