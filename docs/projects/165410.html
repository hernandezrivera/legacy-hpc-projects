Project Summary

Oxfam will support a variety of micro and small enterprises based on market demand, giving priority to food and other basic products and services. This will be achieved through skills training, provision of start-up kits, linking trainees to local labor markets, and encouraging SNGOs to establish systems linking labor force and employers. Skills training will include specific trades as well as business development training for current and prospective businesses to ensure responsiveness to market demands, business planning, marketing and meeting the triple bottom line of profitability, social impact and environmental impact. Life skills will be mainstreamed into all trainings. Special measures will be taken for illiterate persons offering them literacy skills and additional support. The training will also enhance the competence of micro and small businesses to apply for small grants and loans. Among the beneficiaries, the ESFVL will target youth age 16 and above whose education was disrupted due to war. It will do so according to Syria law on vocational training and labor law which generally complies with ILO international standards.
Oxfam will utilize these activities combined with bespoke research activities to explore how women are able to access livelihoods assistance and enter the labour market.
Oxfam will ensure that each stakeholder involved in the project can safely participate in its activities. Safe programming (or protection mainstreaming) is implemented as part of the project cycle. Oxfam’s staff, volunteers and implementing partners are trained in safe programming and risk assessments. This includes the identification of the risks, possible targets, and risk prevention, mitigation and management measures: this is then implemented and reviewed regularly. Particular attention will be paid to addressing risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse and aid diversion, and a conflict sensitivity approach will be adopted.

4 - Needs

Across Syria the unemployment rate is over 50%, rising to 75% among 15-24 year olds and estimated to be even higher among women, with over half of the pre-crisis jobs lost (HNO 2018). According to the Syrian Economic Science Society, the crisis has seen a significant skills gap develop across the governorate of Aleppo with a 38% labour market gap between qualifications and skills and available employment. The latest REACH data highlights that most families across Aleppo are earning less than 50,000 SYP a month, with the monthly food basket costing half that much. The widespread destruction and contamination of agriculture related infrastructure and value chains; depletion of productive assets and savings, increasing debt; and limited economic opportunities have all contributed to socio-economic hardship and the disruption of livelihoods. Before the crisis, the agricultural sector contributed to 18% of the GDP and provided livelihood opportunities and employment to approximately 37% of the population6. However, seven years of continued violence and destruction have devastated the country's agricultural capacity at national and household levels. The total area of land under cultivation in Syria has shrunk by 40%,7 further escalating food insecurity and the abuse by various parties to the conflict that have made fortunes by dominating the food trade in many regions of the country, particularly in those with high intensity conflict. Many farmers have been forced to choose lower-quality alternatives or create their own solutions, like digging wells or using solar energy and biogas. Vulnerable groups, such as IDPs and women and children have been moving towards low-paying and low-skill livelihood activities like agricultural seasonal work. Shifts in traditional gender roles have intensified the burden of work and protections risks for women and girls while also opening up opportunities for their empowerment and engagement in newly emerging labour markets.

The project's Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) system will be in line with Oxfam's Common Approach to MEL and Social Accountability (CAMSA) which will be adapted to the project context according to the Alternative Operation Procedures (AoP) for Syria. Despite the limitations, Oxfam in Syria will where possible, conduct regular monitoring according to a planned schedule (MEAL framework, plan and Indicator Performance Tracking Table [IPTTs]) developed while achievements towards indicators are reviewed monthly and updated in Oxfam’s internal monitoring and reporting System. This System also tracks scheduling, issues arising, and lessons learned with the support of the project team.
