Project Summary

This project will be implemented in Kapoeta East, North, South, Maridi, Ibba & Mvolo Counties to provides essential integrated quality health care services to improve access and scale - up response to the most vulnerable populations targeting 6,667 ( 2,822 females & 2,711 Male, 578 girls and 556 boys) in need for humanitarian Emergency health care services through provision of Medical consultation and treatment for common diseases, maternal child health care such Immunization, ANC, delivery & Post-natal care, Preventive intervention such as Health Education on integrated health (WASH, Nutrition GBV etc.); - psychosocial counseling and the project will also support active surveillance for epidemic prone diseases such as malaria, cholera, measles and meningitis throughout the 12 months of implementation periods with tailored with focus on IDPs, Returnees and host communities.
The response plan will prioritize epidemiological profile of disease outbreaks, cholera hotspot areas, measles outbreaks, malaria seasonality with response modalities community surveillance. IDSR, EWARS, attacks on health care and disability tracking tools inform the determination of the feasibility of the response through guidance from health cluster to save lives by providing timely and integrated multi sectoral assistance to increase service access for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders to reduced acute needs among the most vulnerable women, men, girls and boys.
Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment & Needs (OPEN) had vast experience implementing 18 months emergencies health services in Greater Kapoeta Counties :- SSHF A1 2018 in Kapoeta East, SSHF A2 2018/2019 in Kapoeta South and SSHF A1 2019 in Kapoeta North with support from Health cluster and SSHF rounds allocated benefited 18,687 (6,504 male, 6,483 female, 2,858 boys and 2,842 girls) of which 1,716 children under treated from common diseases such as Malaria, Pneumonia, diarrhoea, RTI etc., 3,676 under 5 completed PENTA 3 in Kapoeta East. In Kapoeta South, 26,313 (5,503 boys & 5,961 girls under 5 years and below 15 years, 7,693 Women both child-bearing and PWLs and 7,156 men). And Kapoeta North 15,833 (5,3,088 boys & 3,444 girls under 5 years and below 15 years, 5,179 Women both child bearing and PWLs and 4,122 men) vulnerable populations 862 (439 M & 423 F) Pneumonia, 1,477 Malaria cases diagnosed and treated among under 5 years children. 519 (265 M & 254 F) EPI services, 192 ANCs services at first visit and 611 ANCs in 1st - 4th visits, 19,740 (9,673 M & 10,067 F) received Health education on integrated services (WASH, Nutrition & protection), 519 Treated AWD, ABD (13) under 5 years and AWD for over 5 years children and these results complement efforts toward to lifesaving given few functional health facilities in the Counties in 2019 and with this HRP 2020 proposed activities intends to bridge the huge health needs and contributes to realization of Health cluster strategic Objectives 1, 2 & 3:-
1. Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities.
2. Improve access and scale - up response to integrated quality essential health care services to vulnerable populations
3. Increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders.

1 - Needs

The Greater Kapoeta areas are pastoralist communities and the trends of movement is seasonality drives with reported cattle rustling, Food Insecure, flood displacement while Maridi, Ibba & Mvolo Counties boarders Democratic Republic of Congo posting threat of Ebola given cross border economic activities, Returnees movement trends and fluid insecurities this posts huge challenges to already limited access to existing health care systems in the counties. In 52% of assessed settlements according REACH Report August 2019, there were reportedly either no health care services accessible, or they were more than one hour walk away, suggesting that the existing health infrastructure may not adequately serve vulnerable populations incapable of travelling long distances when unwell. 27% Population of Greater Kapoeta reporting no access to health services, 74% reported that facilities were either too far or not there at all, rising to almost 100%. Further 19% reported a lack of drugs and staff, implying that for some communities’ buildings exist but no health care services are provided by Health development partners. Consistent with previous quarters, malaria remained the main reported cause of death (48%) and much higher proportions of (between 80%-86%) reported malaria as the main cause of deaths in all the Counties. These factors combined with low access to health facilities are likely to result to poor health outcome. whilst Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene access to improved sources of water remained relatively high across the counties where possibilities of reported safety concerns limiting access to their preferred water point. Incidents of cattle raiding, driven by lack of access to water resources, may have reduced the perception of safe access to preferred water points for these communities, in particular because women and children are commonly the ones to collect water. The risk of waterborne disease and possible epidemic outbreaks remained relatively high. https://reliefweb.int/sites/reliefweb.int/files/resources/reach_ssd_ees_ces_situation_overview_q2_2019.pdf. Additional to Need contextual analysis, IRNA, IDSR, EWARS and EPI Reports indicated that, incidence rate for common diseases standing at 16 % in Kapoeta South with CFR for common diseases 65% in Kapoeta North while Measles coverage gaps for children from 6 months – 15 years 79 %, PENTA 3 coverage gaps of 70% in Kapoeta East, Measles coverage of 77% in Mvolo county with 8% incidence of Common diseases. Therefore, an estimated population of 6,667 (2,822 females & 2,711 Male, 578 girls and 556 boys) in Kapoeta East, North, South, Maridi, Ibba & Mvolo Counties are in need for humanitarian emergency healthcare services in 2020. Due to high disease burden resulting from common diseases, epidemic outbreak, pandemic and neglected diseases the population targeted for humanitarian healthcare services is 76,595 including IDPs + Returnees and an unknown number of returnees anticipated in early 2020 as a result of the recent peace agreement and stability. The prevailing health systems fragilities are exacerbated by lack of water quality control, infection prevention and control measures, healthcare waste management and expansive malnutrition necessitating a need for holistic integration of health responses to ensure the necessary health services to mitigate disease spread and enhance appropriates access and utilization of health care services to all most vulnerable population. The number of people targeted for healthcare services based on multiple factors including, displacements, malnutrition, high disease burden from vaccine preventable and communicable diseases. Seasonal outbreaks of common diseases including cholera and measles with continued endemicity of malaria is posing a challenge to a health system which is already fragile.

2 - Activities

This intervention will focus on provision of Medical consultation and treatment for common diseases, maternal child health such as immunization, ANC, delivery & Post-natal care. Preventive intervention; Health Education on integrated health (WASH, Nutrition GBV etc.); - psychosocial counseling, distribution long lasting insecticide treated nets and the project will support active surveillance for epidemic prone diseases such as malaria, cholera, measles, hemorrhagic fever and meningitis, referrals for Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CeMONC), minor trauma and surgery, scale up TB/HIV/AIDS/Disability care, Scale up integrated response to SAM with medical complications and Procurement 5% of emergency health pipeline to bridge in gaps for medical supplies and other essential equipment' through provision of mobile clinical packages in 8 hard to reach areas, support 8 Primary Health Care units (PHCUs) and 5 Primary Health care centers (PHCCs) in Kapoeta East, North, South, Maridi, Ibba & Mvolo Counties.
