Project Summary

WASH needs affect living standards of vulnerable communities in Greater Upper Nile. Low WASH standards directly impact efforts to reduce malnutrition amongst U5s and PLWs. Alliance for Community Development (ACD) works in Greater Upper Nile providing much needed WASH, Nutrition, Education, FSL and Protection support to vulnerable households and communities affected by displacement, conflict, climatic shocks amongst other issues. Through this project, ACD intends to reach a total of 11,111 people (55% female) in the county of Baliet with emergency WASH support. Both temporary and (semi-)permanent solutions will be considered to ensure that most vulnerable communities are reached with timely and equitable basic WASH services. The project will deliver rapid assistance to affected communities affected by a sudden or rapid onset of disasters mainly with emergency services. This will involve distribution of WASH minimum packages to promote access to safe water, access to basic and dignified gender sensitive sanitation and hygiene promotion. Repairs and maintenance of water facilities including contamination protection through flood-proofing and disinfection and refresher trainings of WASH community structures will be done to ensure continuity and proper management of water sources. Vulnerable HHs will be provided with water containers and minimum package for water treatment products to enable safe and hygienic water access. Communities will be trained on hygiene and sanitation around water sources, maintenance of water containers and hygiene behaviour and practices to reduce disease, prevent malnutrition and improve general well-being. Hygiene promotion will focus on safe excreta management, handwashing, and safe water chain among other WASH domains.

1. Needs

Baliet County in Upper Nile is faced with some of the most challenged humanitarian scenarios as a result conflict, displacement, harsh weather cycles and low livelihood opportunities. The South Sudan WASH Cluster Severity Mappings and People in Need figures indicate that less than 38% of populations in Baliet have access to clean water while 11% of the population has access to adequate sanitation. The latest IPC projects for 2024 indicates levels of crisis and emergency food insecurity and malnutrition. Fresh arrivals of returnees from Sudan have exerted pressure on the existing WASH facilities where they have settled. Some of the villages have no protected water sources, while the existing water sources are dysfunctional due to lack of maintenance. Pastoralist displacement is linked to poor WASH services, creating scenarios of high disease burdens. As a result of a high level of SAM cases admitted at nutrition centres, provision of WASH services at nutrition centres is essential in reducing the effects of limited services that end up exacerbating the already dire situation by being centres of infection. Similarly, lack of sanitation and hygiene awareness has created rampant open defecation thus the need for safe sanitation practices, hygiene behaviour change and construction of flood resistant latrines. Adolescent girls face risks of being attacked in location which have no safe sanitation facilities while women are exposed to high GBV risks while walking long distances in search of water from unprotected sources such as seasonal rivers and surface ponds. Female learners face the risks of harassment at schools lacking safe sanitation facilities and while women including girls are exposed to rape and other forms of gender-based violence (GBV) risks while walking long distances to fetch drinking water from protected sources. ACD will work to support in coordination with civil authorities in the target areas, rehabilitate existing water sources to improve access to safe drinking water. This is through working with community leaders, women and youth groups to create awareness towards attitude and behaviour change aiming at adaptation of good hygiene and sanitation practices.

2. Project activities

ACD aims at reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition, ensuring adequate clean and safe water is available through the rrehabilitation of water points at nutrition centres, WQTs, setting up of waste bins, handwashing facilities, distribution of MHM Kits and WASH standard kits for caregivers. ACD will further ensure that women, men, girls and boys have safe access to quality basic, inclusive, safe WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties. ACD will Integrate WASH in nutrition response through famine prevention minimum package. - Assessment of WASH facilities; boreholes, latrines at OTP/SC facilities. - Rehabilitate/repair of 15 boreholes and handpumps at OTP/SC facilities. - Conduct full range water quality analysis. - Construct gender sensitive, flood resistant latrines in 10 Nutrition OTP/SC facilities - Refresh/training of water management committees (15 committees) - Train village pump mechanics on the O&M - Hygiene promotion sessions on human and animal open defecation near water points. - Train 10 CNVs in WASH-Nutrition interventions for caregivers at Nutrition OTP/SC - Visibility IEC/BCC materials with Hygiene-Nutrition messages. - Conduct WASH KAP survey - Joint monitoring of project activities with state authorities and cluster partners ACD will provide WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties including WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation. This will ensure that the projects - Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees. - Rehabilitation/repair of 5 boreholes and handpumps. - Support O&M of existing boreholes at IDPs, returnees and host communities. - Construction and rehabilitation/repair of gender sensitive institutional VIP latrines in schools. - Conduct mass clean-up campaigns and repair/rehabilitation of solid waste collection points. - Improvement of drainage systems; drains, soak away pits. - Mark the 4 Global WASH calendar events through sensitization campaigns on world water day, world menstruation day, global handwashing day. - Conducting GBV risk analysis sessions for Project activities and events. - Conduct trainings for community water management committees. - Distribute 1,500 MHM dignity hygiene kits among adolescent girls.
