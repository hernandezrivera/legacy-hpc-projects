Project Summary

Sudan Evangelical Mission (SEM) aims at provision of support to conflict-affected and most vulnerable girls, boys, youths, women, and men in Mundri East, Mundri West and Mvolo Counties of Western Equatoria State and Jur River County of Western Bahr El Ghazal State, in South Sudan where we have an operational presence and target to reach 15,520 beneficiaries (2600 boys, 2600 girls, 4960 men, and 5360 women).
We propose this project that will contribute to achieving South Sudan’s HRP objectives of general protection cluster (PO 1) by ensuring the provision of critical protection-related assistance and specialized services through an integrated approach to address the priority needs of targeted people and (PO3) where we will enable durable solutions for IDPs and other population.
The activities of this project are aimed at:
Provision of the timely, comprehensive case management process to 1000 beneficiaries (200 boys, 200 girls, 400 women, and 200 men) that will address their needs from the point they were identified until when their cases shall be closed.
We will provide group and individualized psychosocial support (PSS) and recreational services that will target 400 boys, 400 girls, 400 men, and 800 women according to their individual needs. We shall also have recreational activities targeting adolescent girls, boys, and women to connect socially with each other and create a platform where peer-to-peer support will be reinvigorated. Basic livelihood skills like beadwork, basketry, breadmaking, and embroidery will avail an opportunity where participants will generate income to care for their families and build their resilience to economic hardships.
Community awareness-raising via mediums like community public rallies, sports, cultural dances peer to peer sessions, we shall raise awareness to the community on protection issues where we shall target will target 10400 people (1200 boys, 1200 girls, 4000 men, and 4000 women). We shall target various groups via different modes, for example, young people would benefit more through peer-to-peer educational programs. The sessions will help the community to widen the social responsibility of protection, where the communities will understand they all have a role to play in laying a stable ground for their protection.
By training 160 women and 160men in conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation, and peacebuilding, the community will understand their roles in the process where broken relationships are healed and mended. The community will be supported to map out a list of structures, institutions, and processes that they shall build or rebuild for ensuring sustainable peace in the community. A peaceful environment in the community will translate to reduced disruptions in education for children, health services, agricultural activities, health and thus enable the community members to enjoy improved health, education, and living standards.

1 - Needs

According to a REACH situational overview in Western Equatoria that was conducted from April- September 2020, humanitarian needs in Western Equatoria State (WES) increased due to a deteriorating security situation, displacement, unusually heavy rainfall and flooding, and COVID-19 related movement restrictions. However, due to insecurity, bad road conditions, and flooding, the food security situation has raised concerns in Greater Mundri where this project will be implemented. These continuous cycles of conflict and aftermath of disasters undermine people’s dignity and long-term psychological wellbeing, as well as having a detrimental effect on the social fabric of displaced communities. Therefore, SEM will aim at addressing the needs of the people in Mundri East and Mundri West, Mvolo and Jur River Counties by drawing attention to their fundamental needs and building local capacities to prevent conflicts, mitigate the impacts of conflicts and promote a greater degree of social transformation. This project has an activity that will build the capacity of men and women in the community to tackle conflict and promote peace. The training will boost confidence in conflict resolution and create a sense of community ownership of the peacebuilding process leading to sustainability.For men, assessed settlements continued to report that fighting between neighbors was the main protection concern (41% in March and 26% in September). The impact of long-term effects for men and boys who have experienced SGBV and alcohol use, particularly for a community already traumatized by conflict and violence becomes a concern that cannot be overlooked, and will therefore require to be addressed by psychosocial support to increase their resilience and create an integrated development approach to promote their psychosocial wellbeing like the ones SEM is proposing in this project. The potential that youths hold for change and promoting positive actions in reducing alcohol use and in peacebuilding will also be utilized. The peacebuilding component of this project aims at addressing the root causes and drivers of such conflicts while involving socially marginalized groups such as people with disabilities, widows, and IDPs to make an active contribution to the process of reconciliation.An increase in insecurity in Mvolo, and Mundri East counties during Q2 and Q3 negatively impacted the protection situation across the state. In Mvolo County, 27% of assessed settlements in May reported civilian casualties (compared to 6% in March) and 18% looting, likely partially caused by raiding incidents. In Mundri East and West and Maridi County reportedly were abduction of men (17%, 15%, and 15% respectively),48 as well as forced recruitment of men and boys,49 likely due to the heightened presence of armed groups in the area. By implementing this project, SEM seeks to empower the targeted vulnerable communities to mitigate threats they face from the ongoing effects of armed conflict by enabling them to have a better understanding of the diversity in their communities, threats that they face, and resources available within reach that they are able to utilize to promote their own protection. They will also be empowered to identify and adopt peacebuilding strategies that will promote harmonious coexistence in the community. Addressing the psychological needs of such communities is a priority. Through our targeted beneficiaries' participation in PSS, they will gain an increase in control over their lives, as well as the life of the community as well as appreciating survivors’ efforts to deal with and come to terms with stressful experiences.

3 - Project indicators

We shall refer to the following indicators in the course of implementing this project:(1) Number of women, men, boys, and girls with protection concerns supported through case management and referral services: we shall increase the number of targeted beneficiaries who have received a timely, comprehensive case management process that is tailored to address their unique needs.(2) Number of women, men, girls, and boys provided with individual, group, specialized PSS, and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services, ): we shall seek to increase the number of survivors who receive individualized and group psychosocial support, recreational services, and basic livelihood skills.(3) Number of women, men, girls, and boys reached through awareness-raising activities: we shall increase the number of boys, girls, men, and women who receive key messages that will focus on awareness of gender-based violence, prevention/ harm reduction, and service points that are available to them in the community.(4) Number of women and men trained in conflict resolution, mitigation, mediation, and peacebuilding: we shall increase the number of men and women who have developed skills, knowledge, and attitude change in conflict resolution, mediation, community peace dialogues, and reconciliation activities.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

We will disseminate key messages on PSEA through local FM radio stations, through printed materials like posters, and verbal means via languages that are familiar to our targeted population. Presenting the information in a language that beneficiaries understand enables them to be empowered and become active participants in our planned response.During the implementation of activities like trainings and awareness-raising, we shall pass information through announcements during every activity on our responsibility as SEM in upholding and promoting the highest standards of ethical and professional conduct.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

We shall use various mediums to ensure that we collect feedback from most vulnerable members.Among them, will be through focus group discussions with specific groups like persons with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls on a regular basis. This will be done in locations where they will be accessing community services like in child, women, girls and child friendly spaces, churches, schools and other locations. Such a method will be very friendly to the less literate and to persons who are blind, as they will be able to freely participate in the discussions and get feedback on the project. For such FGD`s we shall give a provision of reasonable accommodation, where persons with disabilities will attend the company of their assistants to facilitate translation to persons who are deaf, or to assist persons with physical disabilities to move from home to FGD venue. We shall also ensure that we hold the FGD sessions in venues that are accessible for easy access to persons with disabilities. All these adaptations will eliminate barriers that hinder communication.We will make our feedback mechanism to be friendly to the less literate through availing mediums like our telephone hotline where they can call if they are not able to write to our suggestion boxes. Persons who are blind will also be able to make telephone calls to our hotline and get feedback to their concerns. They shall also be able to give feedback/ complaints verbally to our staff in the field. Our caseworkers are already trained to work with persons with disabilities, and thus they will use their South Sudan sign language skills to communicate to the deaf, as well as allowing them to use interpreters who are familiar with their communication methods.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

During community meetings, awareness meetings, trainings, and field activities, we will set aside time at the end of every activity session where beneficiaries can voice their concerns and complaints either publicly or privately with our field staff who are present.We will share written information on the project scope and also pass the same basic information about the SEM and this proposed project and the necessity to understand their rights and entitlements, within the scope of the project that we are implementing. It will be vital to get a sense across to the communities that they have the right to complain and that all complaints will be dealt with fairly, efficiently, and as simply as possible.As we collect the feedback, we shall ask persons with disabilities who have participated in the activities to give their feedback regarding our interventions. We shall use verbal means to reach out to blind persons with disabilities while using sign language communication with persons who are deaf. This creates an avenue for feedback collection among persons with disabilities. Participants with speech difficulties will write their feedback in a language that is familiar to them.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Beneficiary complaints and feedback will help SEM to review actions and undertake an investigation on issues that need to be put right, amended, or changed to improve the situation and resolve the issue. Such feedback will allow SEM to rectify minor and unintended mistakes; to manage risk before it gets out of hand. Effective response enhances the integrity of this project as it demonstrates our willingness in listening to our beneficiaries. We shall also use the feedback as information for project monitoring as it provides this project with valuable management information on how we are implementing our project.Beneficiary complaints and feedback will enable us to adapt this project to the changing context that was triggered by the feedback received from our beneficiaries.We shall also involve beneficiaries in the process to update, revise or redesign project plans on the basis of the findings from consultations, and feedback that they gave to enable an inclusive process that takes into consideration the views of all the beneficiaries including persons with disabilities, women, men and less literate persons.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Lack of access to/awareness of referral services for children and survivors of protection incidents (GBV, child protection) will be mitigated through maintaining a current list of local resources, intersectoral services that are available in Mundri East, Mvolo, and Jur River Counties and make it a standard part of the information available to survivors of protection incidents and their family/ caregivers. We shall further disseminate information through leaflets, posters, and information cards to the community to reach as many people as possible. When passing this information, we shall work with family members to reach persons with disabilities with messages on service availability. We shall do so by working together to translate the messages to persons with hearing impairment and persons with intellectual disabilities. Family members will work with our case workers to read messages that are in print form to persons who are blind to ensure that they are not left out of services.To access services among people with disabilities, we shall adequately consult them while ensuring reasonable accommodation to overcome barriers that may arise during project designing, and implementation stages. This will include using interpreters for persons who are deaf, personal assistants for those with physical and intellectual disabilities.Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be established via suggestion boxes that will be strategically placed in public spaces and display of phone numbers for complaint and feedback focal points as well as dissemination of the information to all project stakeholders to provide an avenue where they will freely share complaints and feedback on this project. We shall adapt the feedback mechanisms to facilitate persons with disabilities, and less literate persons to give feedback.Unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries- By making use of our code of conduct, all our staff shall be expected to abide by the clauses that refer to the safety of data and responsible use of collected data. This will prevent the distribution, publishing, or sharing of any collected data without the consent of the beneficiaries and SEM to safeguard the confidentiality of our beneficiaries.
