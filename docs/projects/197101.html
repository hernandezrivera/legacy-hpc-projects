Project Summary

The food security situation in Syria has drastically deteriorated since last year. In 2023, an estimated 15 million people will need some form of food and agriculture assistance, representing 68 per cent of the total population. At least 12.1 million people are estimated to be food insecure, including all 2 million people living in camps who are deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure, and 2.9 million people are estimated to be at risk of food insecurity, an alarming increase of people at risk who could shift to become food insecure during 2023 if the crisis continues at same worsening levels. (HNO, 2023)
The proposed project is aiming to contribute to achieving the SO2 and SO1 by achieve two goals:
1) Supporting the widows’ camps in addition to the most vulnerable populations (IDPs in Camps) in the following camps in Idleb-Maaratmisrin: Al Dahroj, Maryam, Ebd Alrahman(Kafrehmul), Sham, AlOkhwa, Aljub, Alhareth, Aldahouk, Alsaro, Alsadaqa, Alqasha, Almaqbara and Zaytun Office in adition to the follwing camps in Afrin and Albab: Qebat Alsheih, Mazrat Shwiha, Alwadi Alakhdar, Bershaya, Matenla, Kurtek 2, Alsatur, Alhezb, Aljasem, Albalata, Mhamadia, Mhamadia 1 and Deir Ballut.
HIHFAD will keep high level of coordination with FSL and CCCM clusters to ensure avoiding any potential overlaps in adition to keep the consideration for the coordination on the field level.
2) Enhancing wheat value chain in two governorates (Idleb and Aleppo), and consequently enhancing food security in the target areas through inter-linked components.
The project was built on achievements of the previous interventions of HIHFAD (irrigated farms and, a farmer association, agricultural inputs for the vulnerable households and free bread distributions) in the target areas.
10900 HHs (Men 13868, Women 14402, Boys 12268 and Girls 12802) in total will directly benefit from this project.
The project consists of five main components:
1) Supporting 400 vegetable farmers with supplementary irrigation costs enough for 2 Dunums (Using of diesel vouchers modality), in addition to do a light rehabilitation to 20 km of the water canals (10 KM in Idlib and 10 KM in Afrin) which serving 800 hectares by cash for work scheme for the irrigation works.
2) Supporting the wheat value chain through purchasing wheat seeds from 1750 MT from up to 1750 farmers (1250 from Idleb and 500 from Aleppo).
3) Supporting 4000 farmers (2800 in Idleb and 1200 in Aleppo) with agriculture inputs (wheat seeds purchased from the local farmers and supplementary irrigation).
4) Supporting 250 barley farmers with agricultural inputs such as seeds, fertilizer and harvesting cost in
5) Processing 750 MT of purchased wheat into 585 MT of wheat flour and distribute it as a fresh bread bundle BB over 4500 HHs, this component will target the widow’s camps at the targeted subdistricts in addition to the IDPs in camps for 6 months of distribution 26 days per month via contracting local bakeries.
Climate smart agricultural training will be given to 650 BNs regarding the appropriate and recommended dosage and amount of pesticide and fertilizer the proper irrigation techniques to save water and decrease the negative impact on the environment.
The selection criteria for the bread distribution will be:
1. IDPs living in camps.
2. not receiving bread from other actors.
3. families with 4 and less members will receive 1 BB, and 5 and more members will receive 2 BBs.
as for the The distribution points will consider the males and females, in addition to that HIHFAD will carry out PSEA trainuing to all project staff.
in the begining of the project, HIHFAD team will conduct FGDs with all communties components including PwDs, elderlies, womens and girls. where during the implementation phase HIHFAD team will distribute the assisstance by door to door to Poeple with disableties and eldaries.

3 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 11 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction, explosive ordnance contamination and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world . In addition to protection threats, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. The overall estimated Sector PIN for 2022 – of food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity – is estimated at 13.9 million, with 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The acute food insecure figure includes 2 million people living in camps and deemed to be 100 per cent food insecure. FSA sector foresees an increase in the number of food-insecure.The similar trends in food insecurity were confirmed in HIHFAD specific needs assessment, where stated that the crisis brought devastating impact on the infra structure including irrigation systems, accessing to the core agricultural inputs, and the economic effects on farmers income. Many people were obliged to discontinue a gricultural production, including crop and livestock production, because they did not have the means to afford through the crisis, despite the deterioration in farming activities across the country, agriculture still represents an important source of income and food in all assessed areas.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Persons with disabilities are at higher risks of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers. Women and girls with disabilities are two time sat higher risk of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers, to compare with their peers without disabilities all of this makes the persons with disabilities in higher risks of SEA . Females with disabilities remain most invisible groups in the Syrian society, they may not leave their houses, and not participating social events in the communities hugely isolated. Thus, the project will ensure outreaching house-bounded females by ensuring appropriate gender balance within the team, so ensure our access to this group. HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per area. HIHFAD has a pre-established feedback system and safeguarding reporting channel that supports the ability of the targeted populations to submit theircomplaints, comments, and suggestions in a beneficiary-friendly and easy-to-access way. The system provides diverse options for the convenience of the beneficiaries to deliver their feedback including WhatsApp hotlines, feedback boxes presented at all HIHFAD supported facilities in addition to the direct feedback.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Regarding the COVID-19 Pandemic Risk assessment for the proposed interventions, the probability of this Risky is moderate:Mitigation Measures:-HIHFAD already assigned a special medical task force to follow up all of its activities and provide SOPs.-all of the distribution points will follow the agreed distribution SOP under COVID-19.-all of the training session will take in consideration of the COVID-19 mitigation measures, or it will be online.-HIHFAD will print out a big awareness signs and banners.regarding the risk of agricultural crops:R1: drought season and decrease of the yield.Mitigation: the project suggest to purchase one MT from one farmers which is considered as a quarter of the hectare production.R2: the fluctuation of the exchange rate between USD and Turkish Lira.Mitigation: HIHFAD is planning to purchase the crop from farmers by US Doller.R3: cash and vouchers fraud risk, P: Moderate. HIHFAD will print watermark vouchers and distribute them to the BNs, in addition to conduct training to the vendors and BNs about the Voucher distribution modality.R4: Fuel Prices, P: Moderate.Mitigation: - HIHFAD will sign a contract with fixed prices with Mills, bakeries and trucks from the begining of the project based on genaral natunal tender.-If Fuel prices increased significantly, in this case HIHFAD will try to import the fuel from Turkey.-Coordinate with the FSL Cluster and done to adapt the quantities according to the new prices.
