Project Summary

Through this project, Child Houses pioneers a holistic and sustainable initiative to address the urgent protection needs of unaccompanied, separated, and abandoned children in North-West Syria, targeting a total of 4,120 beneficiaries. This approach will center around the continuation of the first emergency interim care shelter, providing essential nutrition, health, and medical care for a targeted 100 beneficiaries. Within this safe haven, our highly trained caregiving staff, emphasizing child safeguarding, will ensure the emotional and psychological well-being of each child through daily psychosocial support activities.
Recognizing that a family setting is in the best interest of the child, the project strives to create long-term care plans that adhere to Child Protection Minimum Standards. Family reunification will be prioritized, and when not feasible, alternative arrangements with extended family members will be sought. In cases where this is not possible, such as with intentionally abandoned newborns, the project introduces an innovative family-based alternative care solution through foster placement. Despite cultural complexities, this approach has been widely accepted in the local community.
The project commitment will extend to active engagement with families and communities, considering them integral components of the solution. Through comprehensive case management and a robust referral system, the project will address underlying causes of protection concerns affecting a targeted 420 vulnerable children, with a focus on 100 unaccompanied, separated and abandoned children and 50 children deprived of their liberty. Thorough assessments by social workers will guide the development of holistic plans that focus on mitigating risks and safeguarding the child's best interests. In certain cases, Emergency Case Funds will be provided for essential assistance, addressing immediate protection needs of around 200 children (and their families). Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) will be used to support the targeted 50 children deprived of their liberty.
Child Houses will go beyond the immediate needs, extending specialized services to unaccompanied and separated children within closed institutions. Leveraging its unique access, Child Houses will conduct Psychosocial Support (PSS) sessions and provide personalized protection kits to address psychological distress for around 600 children in institutions. Capacity-building programs targeting 50 staff and caregivers, focusing on fundamental child protection principles, will contribute to improved care environments. Additionally, the project will support the establishment of Child-Friendly Spaces within institutions, promoting play and effective solutions.
At the core of this sustainable approach will be community engagement. Child Houses will conduct awareness sessions within the local community, shedding light on the risks faced by unaccompanied and separated children and emphasizing prevention strategies. These sessions, targeting 3,000 community members (1,000 children + 2,000 adults), will empower community members to actively participate in addressing challenges and utilize our services effectively.
Child Houses has been recognized and embraced by the local community and key partners and stands as a trailblazer in the field of child protection in North-West Syria. Its dedicated focus on the most vulnerable children, coupled with innovative solutions and a child-centered approach, position us as a prominent and respected actor in the region.
