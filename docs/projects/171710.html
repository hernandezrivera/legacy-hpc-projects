Project Summary

IDPs living under the most vulnerable conditions are typically those in informal and largely unplanned settlements. They are often from minority and marginalized groups that do not have alternative residence options. IDPs living in protracted situations in informal IDP settlements are in need of a durable solution as most of them want to settle in the location they are displaced to and do not want to return to the place of origin due to insecurity and lack of employment opportunities. Provision of durable shelters with long term security of tenure is a challenge mainly due to lack of funding and the unavailability of suitable land with long term security of tenure. Mercy Corps proposes to provide life-saving and sustainable assistance to 15,000 beneficiaries (5,042 women, 3,358 men, Boys 13,235 and Girls 3,365) displacement -affected communities in Somalia through provision of emergency shelter kits (ESK) and non-food items (NFI) either through cash or in-kind distribution to . The intervention modality will be determined through a market study of the targeted location and site planning/decongestant measure to determine availability of the shelter and NFI items and the cost thereof. Displacement-affected communities comprise of IDPs and host communities. Targeting will ensure that a right-based approach of people in need is ensured through a strict beneficiary selection criterion that picks the most vulnerable and marginalized members of the society. For newly displaced IDPs, Mercy Corps shall provide 2,000 ESKs and 2,000 NFIs; while for IDPs in protracted situation, the intervention may include repair of their shelter or provision of 500 transitional shelter (TS). This also includes negotiating for extension of land lease/tenure to allow for a longer stay until full re-integration is sought.
To ensure that MC is accountable to beneficiaries, government and the donors, a Post-Distribution Monitoring (PDM) will be undertaken for ESK/NFI related activities while post construction monitoring (PCM) will be undertaken for TS. Mercy Corps shall also use her beneficiary complaints and feedback mechanism to collect views from targeted people on areas of concern or on how to improve the project activities during implementation and after.

Project Activities

Mercy Corps and other shelter cluster partners have undertaken a needs assessment and presented a Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and isolated the People in Need (PIN) in Somalia. On that basis, Mercy Corps will address emergency shelter needs and non-food items (ESK/NFI) for newly displaced people in Baidoa, Baladweyne, Garowe and Kismayo Districts. Mercy Corps will also target protracted IDPs and returnees through provision of TS in Baidoa, Baladweyne, Garowe & Kismayo Districts. Mercy Corps will target 2000 households ESK and 2,000 NFI newly displaced IDP households; and 500 households in protracted IDP or returnee or host community households with TS). The standard emergency NFI package will include plastic sheeting, blankets, jerry cans, sleeping mats,kitchen sets and kitchen sets. Where possible, Mercy Corps will give the NFIs through cash transfers or vouchers so that beneficiaries can decide based on their needs. Mercy Corps will also assist with ESK. The ESK includes plastic sheets, supporting poles and a rope for tying down the structure. This provides the most basic shelter which can offer protection from extreme weather. The ESK helps the displaced household to put up by a shelter structure as per the cluster standards within the first few days of displacement. For IDPs in protracted situation, Mercy Corps seeks to improve their living conditions by assisting them with transitional shelter and NFIs. Mercy corps will also undertake site planning and decongestant during the implementation of the program. Mercy Corps follows strict selection criterion that is based on AGD as well as vulnerability to ensure that assistance goes to the ones who deserve it most. Female-headed households, families with many children and with people aged (65+ year-olds), persons living with disabilities (PLDs) or families with such persons, will be given priority. Mercy Corps will involve government especially local authorities in joint monitoring visits as one way of building their capacity in preparedness and response to emergency and therefore ensure that they assume their role as duty bearers. To ensure that IDPs and returnees enjoy their rights, Mercy Corps will also advocate for housing, land and property (HLP) rights with duty bearers especially government. At the end of the intervention, Mercy Corps will undertake post distribution monitoring (PDM) for ESK/NFI and post construction monitoring (PCM) for TS to generate learning from the project and therefore help inform future shelter/NFI programmes.
