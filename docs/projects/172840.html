Project Summary

In line with the UNICEF Strategic Plan 2018-2021 and the Health Cluster Strategy For The 2021 Humanitarian Program, the project is aimed at achieving equitable health outcomes for the poorest, most-in-need and hardest-to-reach children. This will be achieved through addressing humanitarian health needs while building the resilience of families and communities against future health emergencies and linking with development interventions. UNICEF will respond to emerging health emergencies in accordance to the Core Commitments for Children in Humanitarian Action. The key modes of interventions in this project are service provision, national capacity building, community mobilization and advocacy.

1 - Needs

About 30% of the population of Sudan (12% urban, 39% rural) live more than 5 kilometres from a primary health facility, with a wide disparity of coverage by State. Only 24% of health facilities provide a full package of primary health care services. An estimated 2 million children under-five are still affected each year by diarrhoea. The proportion of children receiving appropriate treatment for diarrhoea increased from 15% in 2010 to 59.3% in 2014 while an estimated 90% of children were given ORS or another recommended homemade fluid47. Wealthier households were more likely to seek treatment. Despite efforts to reduce malaria in Sudan, its prevalence remained stagnant at 5.9% in 2016, with 4.1% in urban areas and 6.1% in rural areas . Children under 15 years of age are the most affected as well as those in the poorest quintile (9.9%) as compared to richest quintiles (1.3%). Conflict-affected states have malaria prevalence far above the national average (up to 21.8% in one of the Darfur states). The prevention of malaria using insecticide treated nets remains low. Only 37% of people sleep under LLINs despite 66% of houses having LLINs. An estimated 1.2 million children remain under the threat of acute respiratory infections every year. Vaccination programs have been unable to reach all children in Sudan. The proportion of children 12-23 months who are fully immunized decreased from 49% in 2010 to 43% in 2014. Sudan experienced a measles outbreak in all states between 2011 and 2015, which affected over 12,000 children. Population movements, conflict, inaccessibility of some areas, and inadequate funding coupled with ineffective vaccines management all contributed to the increase in unvaccinated children. There has been one promising upward trend however. Children vaccinated with pentavalent increased from 58% in 2010 to 64% in 2014. Sudan has a high rate of maternal mortality, related to a lack of access to skilled birth attendants and antenatal care. Maternal mortality was estimated at 311 per 100,000 live births in 2015 (the nationally accepted estimate is 216 per 100,000 live births from 2010 ). Less than half of women in rural areas regularly visit antenatal care services. In conflict affected states, only a low proportion of women attend four antenatal care clinics. The estimated HIV prevalence rate in Sudan is 0.2 per cent among adults aged 15-49, with an estimated 4,400 new infections annually. Prevention of mother-to-child transmission of HIV is hampered by women’s limited antenatal care attendance and the high level of stigma associated with the virus. In 2016, only 83,605 pregnant women were tested for HIV, of which 88 were diagnosed HIV-positive. Only 56 of these women were enrolled in anti-retroviral therapy (ART) centres for care and treatment (MOH). The trend in continuum of care between 2006 and 2014 indicates gaps between interventions. “Silo programming” continues to lead to huge lost opportunities for integration of lifesaving child and maternal health programmes, in a context of limited funding for the health sector.Since the first case of COVID was detected in Sudan in March 2020, Sudan has recorded 15,649 cases of COVID with an attack rate of 35.3%. In addition to the direct impacts of COVID-19 on the health of children and their families, interventions aimed at reducing the transmission of COVID, such as restrictions to movement and closure of some service delivery points have resulted in interruptions of the routine health services and supply chains. In 2020 thousands of children missed out on vaccinations due to the impact of COVID-19. Subsequently, vaccine-derived poliovirus type 2 (cVDPV2) re-emerged among Sudanese children with a total of 44 paralyzed children in 13 states up to November 2020. Reductions in Maternal and Neonatal health, IMCI and nutrition service provision capacity were also reported.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The following are the key activities to be implemented through this project:1. Procurement and distribute the essential heath supplies including IPC supplies, PPEs, vaccinces and the associated supplies and equipment, ORS, Zinc, IMCI kits, MW kits, LLITNs, IEHKs, AWD kits and other critical supplies;2. Build the capacity of the front-line health care providers (women and men) at facility and community levels to deliver quality life-saving preventive, promotive and curative health services. this includes capacity building on EPI, case management of childhood illnesses, PMTCT, maternal and newborn care service provision, case management of communicable diseases, COVID-19 prevention and managment and on IPC;3. Support referral of severe/complicated cases to the secondary level of care.4. Provide operational cost for health facilities, mobile clinics, outreach and mobile immunization teams to ensure that services points are open/functional;5. Support preparedness and timely response to health emergencies among the vulnerable populations including COVID-19 prepardeness and response;6. implementation of community-based and outreach/mobile health services to the vulnerable populations including the IMCI community component, ICCM, and outreach vaccination;6. Printing and distribution of health related protocols, guidelines, training modules, recording forms andregistery books;8. Strengthen monitoring and evaluation9. Documentation of best practices.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Climate change is one of the causes of the increased vulnerability of the population of Sudan to natural disasters including floods, droughts and their consequences such as eruption of conflicts related to competition for resources causing displacements, increase in vector-borne diseases outbreak and others. This project aims at supporting to strengthen the capacity of the health system in Sudan to make it more resilient to shocks as well as to be able to detect early signs of shocks before they happen to minimize their risks. Those localities with vulnerable communities related to the impact of climate change are targeted through this project. The project’s support at the community level will particularly contribute to community’s resilience to and ownership of their own health as part of the mitigation measures.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Beneficiary's safety and protection is one of UNICEF's key priorities in its all programming. It has zero tolerance for violence, including sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment. For the last couple of years, UNICEF has been training and sensitizing (both online and class room trainings) its staffs and implementing partners to raise awareness on Prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). UNICEF has put an internal mechanism for detecting, mitigating and reporting any such violence not only those may be happening within its staffs bit also with its beneficiaries. Because of its focus on this, UNICEF’s highest level (representative) is held accountable at organizational level to put in place this mechanism. In addition, UNICEF has started screening its implementing partners for their sensitivity to the matter including assessing the mechanisms they have put in place to detect, mitigate, manage and report any violence, be it sexual or otherwise, against beneficiaries. The following principles underpin and inform UNICEF’s strategy: -The children, women, men and communities that UNICEF serves have the right to be treated with dignity and respect and to receive assistance without threat of exploitation and abuse; -UNICEF has zero tolerance for SEA and SH. As such, it has a responsibility to create and maintain an environment that prevents sexual exploitation and abuse and a responsibility to provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance and support to survivors; -All UNICEF personnel have the right to work in an environment free from sexual harassment; -All UNICEF personnel are expected to act with tolerance, sensitivity and respect for diversity. They have the obligation to ensure that they do not engage in, condone or tolerate behavior that would constitute sexual harassment. The following are its strategic focus on preventing SEA and SH - An Organizational Culture of zero tolerance: Create and nurture an organizational culture based on Accountability where there is a zero tolerance for SEA and SH, where rights are recognized, promoted and protected and where violations are actively prevented. - Reporting mechanisms that are safe and trusted: Empower and support individuals, communities, UNICEF personnel and partners to feel safe to report violations and to feel safe that reports will be handled in a manner that respects due process and other human rights. - Swift and Credible Investigation and Sanctions: Ensure a fair process for swift and credible investigations and sanctions for violations by UNICEF personnel, and actively promote swift and fair investigations and sanctions by (as appropriate) Government partners, implementing partners, commercial vendors, and other UN System organizations. - Survivor-Centered Response: Provide survivor-centered assistance and support that is timely, predictable, sustainable and adequately resourced. - Engaging Partners in the fight against SEA and SH.
