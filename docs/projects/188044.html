Project Summary

Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency characterized by over 10 years of ongoing hostilities and their long-term consequences including widespread destruction, explosive ordnance contamination and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world . In addition to protection threats, the accelerating economic deterioration and impacts of climate change have become additional key drivers of needs, compounding vulnerabilities even further. In 2022, 14.6 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in 2021. (HNO, 2022)
The proposed project will contribute to achieve the SO1:To Scale-up and sustain minimum food consumption needs through lifesaving and life-sustaining food assistance via swiftly addressing food insecurity of 22,000 households (132,000 individuals), where10,000 households from the IDPs in Idleb and Afrin countryside will be supported via eight rounds of food distribution, After informing details of food distribution 3-7 days prior to the event, 10,000 households will receive food baskets for 8 months through small and multiple distribution points at each camp or village, all of the contents of the food basket will be selected based on the FSL recommendations and beneficiaries feedbacks on similar previous interventions, in addition to distribute a fresh bread on daily basis to 12,000 HHs (72,000 individuals) whom living in the camps in North of Syria for continues 6 months, where each family will receive 1 K.G of bread for 6 days a week for 6 months, the bread will be from wheat flour baked in local bakeries selected after a public tender taking in consideration that the quality of the bread should be inline with the global standards and inline with Syrian bread, the bread will be delivered to each camp on the morning, many distribution points in each camp will be to avoid the crowded lines.
Ensuring the beneficiaries feedbacks and recommendations to inform the project implementation, a leaflet on feedback mechanism will be circulated during distribution in addition to PSEA leaflets.
Through setting a selection criteria based on vulnerability analysis, the most vulnerable groups among the IDP will be supported and they can reduce their likeliness of engaging negative coping mechanism, and their life-risks related to food shortage to be mitigated which will be verified through post distribution monitoring activities.
the project is considered as a planned.

4 - Needs

Syria remains one of the largest humanitarian responses in the world, with assistance delivered to 6.8 million people per month in 2021. (HNO, 2022)The similar trends in food insecurity were confirmed in HIHFAD specific needs assessment, where stated that the crisis brought devastating impact on the infrastructure including irrigation systems, accessing to the core agricultural inputs, and the economic effects on farmers income. Many people were obliged to discontinue agricultural production, including crop and livestock production, because they did not have the means to afford through the crisis, despite the deterioration in farming activities across the country, agriculture still represents an important source of income and food in all assessed areas, the figures showed the following:1- All the camp managements reported the food as a must urgent need for the households within their camps 2- 96% of the targeted camps were benefited from a bread distribution projects but those projects were not enough to cover all the need for bread in 80% of those camps.3- 87% of the households’ heads reported that they were receiving a bread assistance, the majority of them were benefited from HIHFAD bread distribution project.4- 97% of the households’ heads said that they will rely on debt to have the daily needed bread.5- The result of calculating the coping strategy index, for the bread sample households, was 21.1 which indicates to food insecure situation.

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will be implemented directly by HIHFAD staff and will provide 10,000 HHs from the IDPs with food baskets for 3 months, HIHFAD will conduct the distribution through many small distribution points at each village. also HIHFAD team will distribute wheat bread to 12000 HHs from the IDPs whom living in the camps on daily basis for 6 days a week along of 6 months of distribution. the project implementation will be through 5 main activities: 1. HIHFAD field team will start to collect the updated new IDPs beneficiaries list. 2. HIHFAD logistic team will announce for 3 public tenders for procurement of Food baskets and the wheat flour, and the 3rd one will be to bake the bread inside Syria. 3. while the head office in Turkey is working on the procurement of the food baskets and wheat flour the HIHFAD fi eld team will conduct home visits for verification of the Beneficiaries. 4. HIHFAD field team will conduct kick-off workshops with all of the local councils and local authorities and camps managements in all of the targeted villages to sign a MOUs. 5. HIHFAD distribution team will conduct the distribution of the food baskets for the selected beneficiaries for three rounds, and will conduct the daily bread distribution in the selected camps. 6. HIHFAD field MEAL team will conduct the PDM after each round of food baskets distribution and on monthly basis forthe bread's one to measure the beneficiaries satisfaction regarding the quality of the distributed

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Persons with disabilities are at higher risks of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers. Women and girls with disabilities are two times at higher risk of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers, to compare with their peers without disabilities all of this makes the persons with disabilities in higher risks of SEA . Females with disabilities remain most invisible groups in the Syrian society, they may not leave their houses, and not participating social events in the communities hugely isolated. Thus, the project will ensure outreaching house-bounded females by ensuring appropriate gender balance within the team, so ensure our access to this group. HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per area. HIHFAD has a pre-established feedback system and safeguarding reporting channel that supports the ability of the targeted populations to submit their complaints, comments, and suggestions in a beneficiary-friendly and easy-to-access way. The system provides diverse options for the convenience of the beneficiaries to deliver their feedback including WhatsApp hotlines, feedback boxes presented at all HIHFAD supported facilities in addition to the direct feedback.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access to specific Sub-districts remains open. The Food baskets arrived to our warehouses on time. No drastic changes on the Bab-Alhawa border regulations. Risks: Risk 1: escalation the airstrikes in targeted areas. Mitigation 1: Conducting the distribution via many micro distribution points meaning don't have any high number of collected people in the same place in addition to cancel the day of distribution when mentioning any fighter jet hovering in the sky. Risk 2: Turkish Government decided to close the Bab-Alhawa border for urgent issue. Mitigation 2: 2.1. put this issue on the contract with the supplier and put a condition that after signing the contract if Bab-Alhawa closed for any issue, the supplier will provide HIHFAD with the same quantities of agreed items in Side Syria with the same specification from local resources. 2.2. Immediate contracting with multi suppliers inside Syria to provide the same food items with the same quantities. Risk3: Delay from the supplier on delivering the Food Baskets to HIHFAD warehouse. Mitigation 3: Put a high penalty for the delay in delivering on the supplier in the signed contract, so the supplier will be more focus on his commitment with the delivery date.
