Project Summary

As part of the COVID19 response, TWB will provide multilingual communication support to all CCCM sector partners for more effective communication with communities, with a particular focus on multilingual feedback mechanism and audio messaging in local languages.

1 - Needs

The information needs of the IDP population in North East Nigeria Camp during the COVID19 era are clear: populations require clear, prioritised information in languages that enable to make the best decisions for themselves, their families and their communities. Without clear, credible, trusted and actionable information, every other aspect of the humanitarian will be seriously compromised.

2 - Activities

TWB will support the CCCM sector and agencies (and SEMA/NEMA camp managers and community volunteers) with community engagement expertise, regardless of if, when and whether humanitarian actors have access to camp site areas. At this time feedback and complaints mechanisms must be strengthened to ensure that everyone in the community is able to reach out and be responded to around areas of concern in the camp sites. With TWBs ability to translate languages used across most camps in the north east, we will provide the capacity for CCCM actors to be able to respond to issues, as well as learn and adjust their COVID19 responses through the analysis of data to enable timely adaptation measures. TWB will create specific COVID19 glossaries- that are tested within the north east communities – to enable community health workers, volunteers, site management staff to engage with keywords and information around health aspects of COVID19. Research and field testing of the glossaries will be undertaken with in face to face focus groups (if and when possible) or through remote research methods. This glossary will be in addition to TWBs already large and targeted CCCM glossaries.TWB will undertake rumour checking work within the community (which can also be done remotely). The language and cultural research and the analysis will be available on a dashboard for the CCCM sector to also feed into and use. Information about rumours will be updated and shared weekly within the CCCM sector. TWB has already seen an increase in requests for translation taking place. We will increase our translation volume capacity (currently 9 languages). This includes offering a plain language service to agencies so that their material has maximum impact with their targeted communities, advocating for all CE materials to be available in a wide variety of languages, and also creating graphics, pictorials and storyboards. Translations of important COVID19 will also be made available in audio formats for playing in camps on loud speakers or PA systems, used by agencies on narrow band radio programming, through their own programming on local radio, or through sharing via mobile phones. TWB undertakes all of its work (research, outreach, translations, feedback, advocacy) through a strong protection lens: ensuring that marginal and vulnerable groups are considered, included and catered for in all aspects of our work.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Through TWB’s audio mechanisms, we are able to create the same information for all nine language groups that we serve. We place a particular emphasis on making information, communication and messaging available – and in a language that is well-understood – to those who are less mobile, visually impaired, illiterate, very young or elderly. During research periods, we reach out to vulnerable members of the community for specific feedback and information to ensure their concerns are recorded, analysed and acted upon at site management level.The use of a TWB COVID19-specific glossary by all community members and site management staff and volunteers will also create valuable avenues and opportunities for feedback for, and from the most vulnerable members of the community.
