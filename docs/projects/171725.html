Project Summary

Diakonie Katastrophenhilfe (DKH), and national partners (IPs) in Somalia plan to continue prioritizing the provision of accessible, improved, and lifesaving WASH services to the emergency affected people targeting 28,456 most vulnerable people, IDPs, and people at risk of AWD/cholera, while at the same time enhancing COVID-19 infection prevention and control (IPC). The interventions will strengthen the gender and diversity focus by considering the needs, priorities, and capacities of girls, boys, women, and men and people living with disabilities. The targeted interventions include preparedness, response, and recovery.
DKH will utilize different strategies to increase access to basic water, sanitation, and hygiene services, including increased access to child-friendly and gender-sensitive WASH facilities with menstrual hygiene management (MHM) facilities to primary school girls. These include rehabilitation and improvement of available water sources; provision of water to the vulnerable populations during an emergency through water trucking, rehabilitation, or construction of sanitation targeting communities without any access and promotion of safe hygiene practices, including COVID-19 IPC. DKH makes use of Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA), where feasible, guided by evidenced market information using MISMA and EMMA tools to determine the market-based CVA values. Retailers are identified and pooled among retail-suppliers that accept payment through vouchers and will be contracted based on the financial capacity for the redemption of restricted vouchers to ensure an unlimited supply of water before getting reimbursed. These are aimed at improving the practice of hygiene behaviours and the use of improved water and sanitation services, which are particularly important to minimize COVID-19 effects. Beneficiaries for vouchers are identified at the household (HH) level based on vulnerability criteria for unconditional multipurpose cash through IPC, and cash-for-work/assets. The beneficiaries will receive the vouchers personally through mobile money. A PDM will be done for compliance per distribution. DKH will provide unconditional and restricted water vouchers to households that are affected by acute water shortage during droughts, thereby supporting existing water logistic and economic structures of the private sector, while at the same time enhancing the purchasing power of the end-users of water supply for their survival. After rehabilitation of the boreholes, DKH will establish and train WASH committees (WMCs) on operations and maintenance of the water points for sustainability. Hygiene awareness campaigns shall be integrated into all water and sanitation activities. Further, MHM kits will be distributed to school girls. In congested settlements, camp cleaning campaigns shall be done through vouchers for work, and latrines shall be desludged by making use of small-scale enterprises that use equipment that can access the latrines. Disposal of liquid waste shall be only at allowed sites. Solid waste in IDP settlements shall be collected and disposed or recycled at designated places. DKH’s IP, SYPD will intervene in case of floods from Shabelle River through the distribution of treated water from the river to displaced people in Beletweyne and Jowhar where two EmWatKits are prepositioned, and emergency latrines to the affected. Construction and rehabilitation of surface water sources, such as water pans, sand dams, shallow wells, berkads, in addition to the creation of multiple community WMC activities to assist communities to prepare and mitigate climate-induced shocks. Sensitization on the treatment of water from unsafe sources for human consumption at HH will be done. DKH will conduct random water quality testing using DKH's water testing kit. In IDP settlements, the project addresses the protection issue of women and girls through the installation of solar lights on the way to toilets and inside the toilets.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will use cash for work for them to the beneficiaries in emergency situation to access safe water for at least 2 months. The strategy is to target vulnerable households to participate in this activity so that they will be able to cushion themselves during emergency in their locality. Use of cash enhances decision-making and dignity of the beneficiary as they are able to make an independent choice on the quantity of water that they can buy, with the flexibility provided by cash. While cash enhances the beneficiaries' income level, it also contributes to strengthening local market functionality with the injection of the cash into the local markets.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project has considered market functionality through conducting assessments, including market and seasonality analysis. The cash will be provided based on the minimum expenditure basket, ensuring that while the beneficiaries' purchasing power is increased they will be able to get access to livelihoods from the existing markets. The results of the seasonality analysis will inform the appropriate time of the year to implement the interventions, to ensure that the markets function as normal and beneficiaries are able to access goods and services from the markets. Besides, the amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market. The cash amounts distributed with be guided and based on the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) per region of interventions, as defined by the Somalia Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU). During the implementation of the project, the team will liaise with other stakeholders to monitor the price behaviour and supplies for commodities to inform the effects the cash intervention is having on the local economy. Decisions will be made appropriately to ensure the cash is used for the right purpose, with no or little effect on the market functionality.

Please provide further explanation

While the project focuses mainly on the emergency aspects of the response, providing immediate support to displaced communities including host communities, internally displaced persons (IDPs) affected by drought, floods, and insecurity, the design is embedded to include interventions that have long-term effects hence contributing to the humanitarian-development nexus in Somalia. The project upholds consultation and use of the existing community structures, which in most cases are the host community leadership and this will promote peaceful co-existence between the IDPs and the host community. The Project also focuses on the establishment and strengthening of the community structures such as the Water management committees, and they are trained to ensure effective operation and management of the rehabilitated and constructed WASH facilities in the localities. The programme is further developed through participatory approaches to avoid bias or discrimination between IDPs and hosts, in addition to ensuring ownership of the results. Through working with national implementing partners the project contributes to the strengthening of the capacities of the local capacity in resource mobilization and management. The project will be implemented in consultation and within specific guidelines developed to ensure longer-lasting effects in the areas of implementation.
