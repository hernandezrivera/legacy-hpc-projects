Project Summary

The multisectoral emergency assistance to returnees and host communities in Eastern Chad aims at providing protection to the most vulnerable returnees and host communities population through the provision of life-saving assistance and essential services. The response strategy is designed based on NRC expertise in delivering large-scale emergency assistance and on the added value of a complementary, integrated and protection-driven approach centred on relevant NRC’s emergency flagship core competencies of Education, camp management, shelter & NFI, food security & livelihoods and protection services.
The local communities in eastern Chad live in extreme poverty. The load posed over the years by the displaced populations has depleted natural ressources and excacerbated the conditions. The new wave of refugees is further deteriorating the situation. Prices of food are soaring in towns near the Sudan border with markets disrupted by conflict and rising demand. 1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

The local communities in eastern Chad live in extreme poverty. The load posed over the years by the displaced populations has depleted natural resources and exacerbated the conditions. The new wave of refugees is further deteriorating the situation. Prices of food are soaring in towns near the Sudan border with markets disrupted by conflict and rising demand. As a direct consequence, the number of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) among children is 60% higher than previously predicted. As refugees, returnees, and host communities have nearly the same vulnerabilities. Therefore, to avoid escalating tensions, NRC will provide life-saving assistance to the most vulnerable, including returnees, and extremely vulnerable members of the host community. Focus will be on women, children, and youth, with special attention to age, gender, disability, and ethnicity related vulnerabilities. Community participation is integrated in the NRC programme in Chad from the onset of the response and will be an instrumental approach to programme development, implementation modality, and feedback and complain mechanisms, ensuring effective and barrier-free access to assistance. NRC primary geographical target will be the retournees camps and host communities along the new camps and the informal displacement sites established along the border with Sudan. In case of new emergencies throughout Chad, NRC will consider responding based on capacity and relevance. NRC response in Chad is designed and will be further developed around three main pillars, within the overarching objective to provide protection and reduce immediate suffering to the refugee populations, and through an evidence-based approach. The three pillars will include: 1) life-saving assistance, providing cash-based food assistance, emergency livelihood and safe and protective environments through emergency shelter and EiE programme; 2) access to basic services, enabling the rights of vulnerable people to access essential assistance through civil documentation, HLP rights support, site management support, and community-based protection (CBP); 3) youth programming, promoting, through a range of integrated and complementary actions, the development of foundational skills, employability and self-reliance, particularly relevant in the severely underfunded humanitarian crises in Chad. In Eastern Chad, NRC will implement the three pillars through the following integrated core competencies programming: Shelter & Settlement – most of the returnees families still live in makeshift shelters, while the shortage of NFIs is reported to be among the highest priorities for both returnees and host communities. In 2024, the need to transit to longer-term shelter solutions will bring the sectoral gap to severe levels. NRC response will include the provision to new returnees arrivals of dignified shelters to ensure immediate protection from the elements, privacy and safety, and cash-based core relief items assistance to both returnees and host communities, once the local market will have adequate functionality. Beyond the first-line emergency response, NRC will strive to deliver medium-term solutions, where technical capacity will be critical, in integration with complementary Education, Livelihood and ICLA (HLP) programming. Education - Chad has one of the lowest education rates globally and eastern Chad is particularly neglected. In the department of Assoungha, local education services are already overstretched and are only accessible in urban centres. With almost no education response delivered to date and extremely limited actors with capacity to do so, it’s fundamental for NRC to implement EiE activities. This will provide immediate protection and ensure foundational development through an inclusive, quality non-formal education to children and adolescents of both refugee and host communities. The Better Learning Programme will be introduced from the early stage as a psychosocial support to heavily traumatised children. During 2024, the education response will be expanded to formal education and youth programming, in close integration with LFS and Protection from Violence activities. Information Counselling and Legal Assistance (ICLA) – With very limited response on this so far by other partners,Additionally, tensions over natural resources (water, firewood) are reported by both host and refugees communities. Similarly, it was reported that returnees could not access land to plant seedreceived from humanitarian actors. Access to land remains an essential requirement to enjoy humanitarian aid, including in Eastern Chad. NRC will aim to become a key actor by supporting three main areas including: 1) access to essential services, through information sharing and counselling; 2) Legal Identity (LID), through support in securing civil documents; and 3) HLP rights/Collaborative Dispute Resolution (CDR), through mapping and community sensitisation. ICLA services will be key in 2024 to enable durable solutions across the multisectoral response. Livelihood and Food Security – In 2024, NRC will aim at providing access to sufficient, safe, nutritious food to the most vulnerable, based on local preferences. If markets develop adequately, the programme will prioritise CVA modality. A livelihood response, mainly focusing on promoting agricultural production and supporting self-employment through vocational trainings, will also be integrated to actively involve the refugee populations in sustainable practices, promoting inclusive systems, collaboration and exchange of expertise with local actors. A women and youth-centred approach will be prioritised, aiming at reducing negative coping mechanisms and protection risks. Protection from Violence (PfV) – Protection concerns among the refugees, returnees and host communities population are high and very poorly addressed. NRC will strive to establish a PfV which will include protection monitoring, CBP, individual protection assistance and a Camp/Site Management support programme, enhancing integration between host communities and refugees, complementarity among actors and community-based assistance. WASH – WASH needs are among the most critical and challenging to meet as the refugee population grows and natural resources are limited. To date, water supply remains among the most problematic life-saving services to be addressed. Considering the substantial number of WASH actors active in Eastern and across Chad, NRC will consider complementing its programme with elements of the broader WASH sectors, which could include integrating WASH in School into the Education response, assisting to the most vulnerable with dignity kits and core hygiene items through C&M modalities, and facilitating the development of water sources through HLP support from the ICLA team to WASH actors. The programme will be integrated through key cross-cutting themes like C&M and environmental protection. This will include multipurpose cash assistance, cash-for-work, sectoral Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA), where market assessments will confirm their feasibility, and activities aimed at limiting NRC’s operations footprint and natural resources’ exploitation. Incorporating these thematics will in turn promote safer and inclusive programming (SIP), including reducing GBV risks related to firewood collection and improving social cohesion (e.g. by boosting the local economy, limiting the exploitation of natural resources, promoting host community and local actors' engagement). Through this integrated approach NRC aims to reach 20,700 host communities/returnees - 9,137 men and 11,563 women
