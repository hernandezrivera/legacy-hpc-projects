Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect food production capacities and income
sources in locations that are most at risk due to the chronic electricity deficit in the Gaza
Strip. The rationale of this project is to target areas most impacted by the increasing
electricity deficit in order to protect current levels of food and agricultural production,
livelihoods and food security by providing vulnerable agricultural producers in the Gaza
Strip with a consistent, clean and renewable source of energy to guarantee sufficient
electricity for essential food production. The intervention pays particular attention to the
impact of the electricity deficit on female-headed households and the workload of
women and youth. This project is aligned with the HRP strategic objective SO3: The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued. The project will contribute to achieve the FSS SO1 “the resilience of vulnerable households is protected, and their livelihoods, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced”,
as it invests in solar energy sources to ensure that targeted farmers have a consistent electricity supply when required in order to cope with frequent energy cuts as part of the protracted crisis.
As an emergency intervention, this project aims to minimize damages and losses to domestic crop production in the current season and to enable normal levels of cultivation
in subsequent seasons. In doing so, this project will protect agriculture-based livelihoods,
food security and domestic food production that are at risk given the chronic electricity
deficit and will enable normal levels of cultivation if electricity supply to the Gaza Strip is
further reduced.
The intervention proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable farmers
whose livelihoods are endangered. The selection of farmers is solely based on needs and
the most food-insecure, and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory
factors. FAO will also ensure the implementation of Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and
Abuse (PSEA) principles through implementing the built-in process for reporting such
incidents.
At least 440 farming households (2,420 individuals) will benefit directly from the project in the governorates of Khan Yunis, Rafah, Middle area, Gaza and North Gaza where the impact of the electricity crisis is most acute. At least 300 additional farming households (1,650 individuals approx.), will benefit indirectly from the project via access to irrigation water for their plant production farms at a significantly reduced cost due to its solar-powered extraction. The 440 farming households are: 220 farmers and 220 breeders.

1 - Needs

Since 2007, the Gaza Strip has been plagued with many chronic drawbacks due to the long-standing deficits in the power supply. During 2020 and 2021, the Gaza Strip had 13 hours per day of power supply and 11 hours with blackouts. The Gaza Strip has a chronic supply deficit, with average of 404 megawatts in 2017 and 229 in 2021 according to OCHA OPt . This deficit for more than decade has undermined the already fragile living and economic conditions. The ongoing power shortage has severely affected the availability of essential services, and has inflicted severe consequences on the environment, including the increase of carbon dioxide emissions and noise. At the heart of the affected sectors is the agricultural sector due to its demand for power supply for irrigation wells, ponds, cold storage, food production and poultry hatcheries, among other things. Many of the value chain facilities acquire energy for 24 hours a day, seven days a week, like poultry hatcheries, fish hatcheries, cold storage and refrigerators for meat. As the power crisis continued, many farmers could no longer afford the higher cost of irrigation with irregular irrigation which causes damage to their produce (OCHA OPt). The food industries incurred unjustifiable costs, with operating costs rising 25 percent above the norm. Some of the small producers left their businesses and fell into the poverty trap fueled by food and nutrition insecurity. In addition, the service providers who have suffered from unstable connections with the supply chain. The consumers buy produce and processed food products with some damage and at a higher cost than normal. Gaza’s farmers have been impacted considerably by the ongoing chronic electricity deficit, particularly in the southeast of the Gaza Strip where a significant proportion of local agricultural produce is sourced. With electricity supply down, Gaza’s farmers are faced with the choice of either substantially reducing the scale of agricultural production or investing in costly diesel generators with which to pump the required amounts of irrigation water. This chronic situation has also severely affected the more vulnerable groups like women, children and older people. With women being the main caretakers of the house, they now have the burden to perform all the house duties and supporting their children education within the few hours of electricity provided. This inflicts more pressure on women and particularly female-headed households who perform work duties inside the house and outside of it. Similarly, older people face more challenges with the limited power supply as movement becomes more challenging within the hours when the power is cut. Prioritization of vulnerability factors including HHs’ characteristics, dependency ratio, income, assets, and other vulnerabilities will be further detailed in the preparation phase. When possible, female headed HHs will be prioritized in the selection. Beneficiaries with vulnerabilities (like people with disabilities and older people) will also be prioritized.Farmers in the Gaza Strip are increasingly less able to cope with sudden shocks, such as the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic, the May 2021 escalations, trade restrictions and persistent electricity shortages in Gaza, natural and environmental hazards, such as winter rainstorms, the effects of climate change, and inadequate water and sanitation services (OCHA, 2020b). The most serious escalation of hostilities between Israel and Palestine in Gaza since the 2014 conflict took place in May 2021. Between 10 and 21 May, Israeli forces carried out over 1 500 strikes from air, land and sea across Gaza. A total of 58 education facilities, nine hospitals and 19 primary healthcare centers sustained damage across the Strip. Multiple water and sanitation facilities and infrastructure were also damaged as reported by the MoA in Gaza. Daily power supply has been reduced to only five to six hours, down from 15 during the escalation month. All this has severely undermined the living conditions and access to basic services by the entire population. The energy crisis, coupled with already low levels of food security, adds on to an already fragile context. As reported in the latest Socio-economic and Food Security Survey, the Gaza Strip accommodates most of the food insecure households in Palestine carrying as much as 87 percent of the severely food-insecure and a mere 16 percent of food-secure households. These findings suggest that the low levels of food security have been driven by a combination of adverse factors further aggravated by energy shortages: more households have become poorer, less resilient and more food-deprived. This poses a particular challenge for women, youth and female-headed households who perform tasks and roles that take care of the house members and children and need electricity to complete them for example cooking, cleaning and other essential roles. In 2021, an average of 115 megawatts of energy was partially supplied by Israel, and approximately 72 megawatts of domestic energy was generated locally by the Gaza Power Plant (GPP) with ongoing donor-supported provision of fuel aid. According to Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) estimates, the cost of one cubic metre of irrigation water has increased by up to 40 percent since Gaza’s sole power plant ceased operations in April 2017, before resuming at very limited capacity, with fears that the cost of irrigation water will increase further should the supply of electricity decrease further. Moreover, according to the MoA, up to 60 percent of agricultural land across the Gaza Strip remains at risk of being set aside due to the lack of electricity with which to pump water for irrigation. At the Inception phase of the project implementation, community mobilization workshops will be held to raise the awareness of the targeted communities including the various population groups.

2 - Activities

The proposed intervention will systematically consider Accountability to Affected Population in general and PSEA standards in particular. As part of the project activities, farming communities will be informed about the project objectives, intended support, targeted group and selection criteria through information sheets that will be made available to all community members including elderly, women and children. The information sheets will include information regarding FAO grievance mechanism and channels of communications including for submitting PSEA related complaints. The intervention will deliver the following outcome, outputs and activities: Outcome: At least 440 at risk, food producing households have their food production capacities, income sources and food security protected with a further 300 food producing households benefiting indirectly Output 1: Agricultural producers supported with solar energy systems to ensure sufficient irrigation of crops and protection of farmer’s livelihoods. Based on the horsepower of the water pump, different beneficiaries will have different PV systems designed. Activity 1.1: 20 x irrigation wells equipped with solar powered pumping systems (10 kw per well) in: Rafah (x5) Khan Yunis (x6) Deir al Balah (x3) North Gaza (x3) Gaza city (x3) Activity 1.2: Installation of solar energy units for 200 water harvesting ponds (3 kw per pond) in: Deir al Balah and the middle area (30); Khan Yunis: Al Sattar (20); Abasan al Kabira and Aljadida (20); Bani Suhela (15) and Qizan Al Najar (35); Rafah: Shoka (20); Muraj (25); Mawasi, Rafah and Khan Younis: (15), and Mosabih (20). Note: For Activity 1.3, agreements will be signed by the groups of farmers who own the wells to provide irrigation water to neighboring farmers at a reduced price to be determined and monitored in cooperation with the MoA. 1.4, Awareness campaign and training for women and men on irrigation management, solar energy maintenance in coordination with the MoA Output 2: Poultry and Dairy Farms supported with solar energy units to maintain normal levels of egg and milk production, and to mitigate climate change damage. Activity 2.1: 19 x Poultry Farms across the Gaza Strip supported with solar energy systems with which to maintain essential lighting for egg production, operating water sprinkler network during heat waves and air extraction fans in summer (0.66 kw per farm) in: North Gaza (40), Gaza (40), Deir al Balah (30), Khan Younis (50) and Rafah (30) Activity 2.2: 30 x dairy farms across the Gaza Strip support with solar energy systems for operation of portable milking machines (2.66 kw per farm) in: North Gaza (8), Gaza (7), Deir al Balah (5), Khan Yunis (5) and Rafah (5). At the inception phase of the project implementation, community mobilization workshops will be held to raise the awareness of the targeted communities including the various population groups around PSEA-related issues and available communication networks. At the conclusion of the activities, a post distribution monitoring survey will be implemented to seek communities’ feedback on the awareness and the responsiveness of the FAO grievance mechanism, including for PSEA-related complaints.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention proposed by FAO aims to support marginalized and vulnerable farmers whose livelihoods are endangered The selection of farmers is solely based on needs and the most food-insecure and does not consider any other difference or discriminatory factors. The interventions proposed under this project do not put beneficiaries at risk, however, FAO aims to ensure that all efforts will be made to prevent and minimize as much as possible unintended negative effects of the interventions. As part of the project inception phase, FAO will develop a risk log identifying all potential risks, their probability and ways of mitigation. The assessment and analysis of risks will be followed by a coordinated application of resources to monitor, minimize and control the probability and/or impact of risks. External risk factors will continuously be considered in order to ensure an adequate understanding of the context of FAO operations during the implementation of the project. FAO will also ensure the implementation of PSEA principles and will have a built-in process for reporting such incidents. The proposed intervention is meant to protect endangered livelihoods of vulnerable female and male farmers and herders in the Gaza Strip through securing the availability of time critical indispensable inputs and services, hence the core of the intervention aims at protecting beneficiaries rather than doing any harm. Priority whenever possible, will be given to vulnerable marginalized groups including women, people with disabilities, and older people. FAO also abides by the principle of Do No Harm; and respects a low profile during implementation of activities to avoid any harm coming to beneficiaries. Through FAO’s participation in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the development of the political situation in the WBGS will regularly be monitored and reviewed in line with project objectives. FAO contributes to policy-level initiatives to protect Palestinians from further reduction in access to land, sea and livelihood resources (e.g. water and agricultural inputs) owing to the political situation, through its participation in the UNCT Commendation Group and HCT Advocacy Working Group. All advocacy efforts will be conducted under the banner of the UN in the WBGS to ensure that FAO’s mandate is not compromised.
