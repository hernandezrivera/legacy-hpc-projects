Project Summary

According to latest humanitarian need overview released in January 2021, reported that nearly 8.3 million people in South Sudan are estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance in 2021. These include 8,000,000 South Sudanese women, men, girls and boys and 310,000 refugees and asylum seekers. This is an 800,000 increase in absolute numbers from the 7.5 million people in need in 2020. According to the intersect oral severity of needs analysis, humanitarian needs are most concerning in Pibor County in Jonglei which was classified as the only county in catastrophic need. A total of 72 counties face extreme needs while five are in severe need.
Lack of access to water has led to tension and conflict between communities over competition of already scarce resources. Forty-one per cent of South Sudanese households access their water from an unimproved water source. The highest proportion of households relying on surface water are in Upper Nile, followed by Jonglei and Central Equatoria.
The report revealed that every fifth person is unable to collect enough water for drinking, and every third reports insufficient access to water for hand washing. The availability and quality of water have shaped how households prepare food, which influences the nutrition status of families. Only 35 per cent of households have access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns73 and 35 per cent of households take more than 30 minutes round trip to collect water as reported by key informants.74 An assessment conducted before the 2020 flooding found that overall, some 40 per cent of IDPs and half of returnees live in settlements where the main water source is over 20 minutes away on foot, while about a third of IDPs and returnees live in settlements reporting water unfit for human consumption. Additionally, 20 per cent of IDPs and 28 per cent of returnees live in settlements where people feel unsafe when they go to collect water,
Poor access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health, nutrition and sexual based violence against women and girls conditions across South Sudan and contributes to one of the highest child mortality rates in the world. Some 70 per cent of households report members affected by a water or vector-borne disease.

The WASH needs are severely huge based on the South Sudan WASH Cluster people in need report which revealed that 50% of the households across the 10 states have only access to hand pump functioning at least 3 liters of water per person per day which is below SPHERE minimum standard that states 15 liter of water per person a day. Greater Upper Nile region and it counties are the most severely in term of access to safe water supplies have reported 78% Upper Nile, Jonglei 50% and Unity 39% which totaling to 55.6% households have access to boreholes /taps stands and at least 3 liters of water per person per day below SPHERE Standard that put minimum of 15 liters per person per day.The recent IPC report released in January 2021 coupled with South Sudan WASH Cluster population in need report has indicated that 1.9 million people reported is severely have no access to sufficient quantity of water supply for drinking ,inadequate access to sanitation and hyhgiene,presense of water borne diseases and malnutrition among children. It is further noted that poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. Also access to sanitation remained low, with only 77% of households reported no access to family shared latrines or communal eg Upper Nile 13%, Jonglei 10% and Unity 11% .Mothers and care takers of children admitted with SAM/MAM or malnourished children lack adequate basic hygiene knowledge and access to safe water supply as in case of Pigi/Canal reporting 0.00% of the households have no access to improved sources as result people are depending on unimproved water sources –river water that are highly polluted with human wastes. Almost if not all the counties mentioned in this response plan have the lowest access to hand washing and these are Canal/pigi, Fangak, Akobo, Nasir, Ulang,Leer,Maiwut,Uror and Nyirol with only 5% of the households have access to hand washing .In term of access to WASH nonfood items, Canal/Pigi,Fangak and Nasir reported limited or lower access to WASNFI and have been affected by floods It is important to note that water related protection and gender based violence in the counties targeted by this response have the highest WASH/GBV issues reported and these counties among others; Ulang 56%,Nasir 45.3%, and Akobo just to cite a few Mothers and care takers of malnourished children lack basic hygiene knowledge and access to safe water at domicile jeopardizing the benefits gained in nutrition programs. The number of reported cases of acute watery diarrhea and acute bloody diarrhea have spiked dramatically in 2020. By the end of October, New health-related WASH needs arose in 2020 as a result of COVID-19 and will persisted into 2021 as a vaccination campaign is not expected to reach a sufficient number of people soon, implying WASH Cluster HRP 2021 Guidance Page a greater need for functional hand washing facilities in areas of high density such as the urban areas and IDP settlements. Women and girls who are traditionally responsible for water collection facing an increased risk of harassment, assault and sexual violence when collecting water far from their homes. Presence of armed youths and inter-communal conflict is a driver of population movement and conflict over water source and property in some areas and requires careful management to ensure a conflict-sensitive approach that strengthens social cohesion.
