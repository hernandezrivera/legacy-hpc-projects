Needs

High malnutrition rates remains a challenge among the Rohingya forcibly displaced Myanmar nationals. Results from the recent emergency nutrition assessment round 2 April – May 2018 indicate a GAM rate of 12% which is classified as serious according to World Health Organization. Although the statistics shows decline in prevalence of malnutrition when compared to round 1 nutrition assessment (GAM 19.3%), more efforts is required to sustain the current curative services while scaling up the preventive interventions. Infant and young child feeding practices are sub optimal with half (50%) of the children 0-6months not exclusively breastfeed. This implies infants are introduced to water and fluids at an early age which predisposes them to diarrhoea diseases given the poor sanitation and hygiene practices. Dietary diversity is low and anaemia prevalence among children 6-59 months is above the WHO threshold for high public health significance (>40%). On average, refugee’s households consume only 3.6 food groups per day hence not reaching the minimum acceptable standard levels which contributes to chronic malnutrition. Similar dietary patterns apply to the local host community at 3.7 food groups per day.While food consumption gaps affect the entire household, children <5 and pregnant and lactating mothers are extremely vulnerable. Pregnancy and lactation considerably augment nutritional needs in terms of dietary quantity and quality, yet in times of food stress, women are the most likely to go without food in the households. According to the REVA August 2018 report, women often reduce their food consumption in favour of adult males and, even more so, of children with men and boys often prioritized to eat first in the family . Early marriage is common among the Rohingya as well as the host community which means that first pregnancy and lactation coincides with adolescence when nutritional requirements for considerable growth required for this stage competes with those of pregnancy and lactation hence contributing to the poor nutritional status of pregnant and lactating mothers. The dramatic increase in population has strained the already limited resources, infrastructure, public services and the local economy mostly in Unions in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas. There is increased pressures including rising food prices and competition for jobs which necessitates extending support to the host community to enable them cope with the effects of the refugee influx.Currently, Concern is implementing nutrition activities in 9 OTP and 3 TSFP/BSFP centres and has made a clear institutional commitment to continue providing quality nutrition programs that support children’s nutritional status. UNICEF has committed to continue funding the 8 OTP centres with provision of SAM treatment while WFP will support MAM treatment in 5 TSFP/BSFP centres. This will ensure continued treatment for an estimated 3,017 SAM and 8,636 (8,380 U5 and 256 PLW) with MAM in 2019. The agency continues to invest in highly skilled staff to ensure delivery of quality nutritional interventions. However, Infant and Young Child Feeding practices are still suboptimal as reflected by the SMART survey which calls for more investment in preventive approaches including hygiene and sanitation and child care practices. Infrastructure is poor in some nutrition sites and requires improvement to ensure they are cyclone and monsoon resilient. Besides, the government has proposed to move some populations from the flood and/or landslide prone areas to new sites which requires construction of infrastructure for service delivery.

Activities or outputs

The proposed activities are aligned with sector priorities and will contribute to the achievement of the overall nutrition sector strategic objectives one and two of the 2019 JRP. The activities are designed to complement ongoing nutrition programs in the target refugee camps and host community. Although women are the primary child caregivers, men are the main decision makers in the household, therefore both women and men will be involved and empowered to take action to improve nutritional situation of children under 5 and pregnant and lactating mothers. Concern will continue providing care and treatment services for children 6-59months and PLW with acute malnutrition in 8 OTP and 5 TSFP/BSFP centres. Micronutrient supplementation will be integrated within the OTP/TSFP services, outreaches and campaigns to reach more children under 5 years and PLW. Facility based infant and young child feeding promotion activities will be an integral component of the CMAM. MIYCF activities will be supported in 9 camps including the Oman site. Appropriate behaviour and practices that are socially and culturally accepted among the beneficiaries will be promoted through Behaviour Change Communication approaches. Nutrition education will emphasise the linkage between IYCF, hygiene and sanitation, dietary diversity and child care with nutritional status of children. Mothers will be linked to the various Mother to Mother Support Group (MtMSG) near their homes for peer to peer support. In the host communities, Concern will support MIYCF in 5 Unions to address gaps that exists in the preventive interventions at the community level. Concern will apply the learning from current mother to mother support groups to scale up the approach in the host communities. Mother to mother support groups will be established and used to disseminate health and nutrition messages, peer support and experience sharing on IYCF practices. Programme implementation will promote the protection, safety, dignity, inclusion and integrity of girls, boys, women and men. Gender balance will be considered for volunteer and staff composition.Output 1: Children under 5 and PLW in target camps receive timely and appropriate treatment for malnutrition.• Screen, refer and treat severe and moderate acutely malnourished children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women• Maintenance of 8 OTP/IYCF centres to ensure they are cyclone and monsoon resilient • Train staff and volunteers (male and female) on CMAM and IYCF in line with National and Nutrition Sector Guidelines• Supportive supervision and on-job coaching to ensure staff and volunteers are equipped with knowledge skills and competencies to provide high quality services.Output 2: Improved knowledge and practices on MIYCF amongst caregivers/PLW in target communities• Support capacity building efforts for staff and volunteers on IYCF• Support nutrition action week, world breastfeeding week and other campaign events• Establish and train Mother to Mother Support Groups for promotion of appropriate infant and young child feeding (IYCF) and child care practices • Nutrition education and counselling on IYCF and child care practices at the OTP/TSFP/BSFP sites and community level• Support kitchen gardening and participatory cooking demonstrations • Community outreach and awareness sessions targeting mothers, fathers and other key influencers to promote positive IYCF practices as well as optimal maternal nutrition. Output 3: Improved access of children under U5 and PLW in target communities to preventives services. • Train staff and volunteers (male and female) on growth monitoring and promotion • Support linkage and referral of children < 5 years, adolescent and PLW for micronutrient supplementation, ANC and PNC • Support feedback and complaint mechanisms, enabling the communities to have a voice in planning and monitoring of the quality and effectiveness of nutrition services.
