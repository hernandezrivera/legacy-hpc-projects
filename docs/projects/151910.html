Needs

Libya is contaminated with mines, cluster munitions and other explosive remnants of war (ERW) from historical conflict, the uprising in 2011 and intermittent armed violence in the years that followed. In all areas subjected to recent fighting, a comprehensive mine action response is required to increase the safety of local and returning populations through education on the risks posed by explosive hazards as well as the survey, clearance and destruction of identified unexploded ordnance. There is no national data available on the levels of contamination in Libya from mines or ERW, however priority areas include Benghazi, Sirte, Tripoli, Derna and Tawergha for the removal of the most significant threats - explosive hazards affecting the daily security of civilians, in particular children, risking life and limb. Contamination also impacts on the reconstruction of damaged infrastructure and the access of humanitarian personnel to areas of recent conflict. The project aims to benefit displaced and returning Libyan communities as a priority, seeking to increase their physical safety when on the move and when returning home to conflict-affected areas. However, all civilians living within the targeted areas will also benefit from increased access to safe land and the removal of barriers to access to services through the removal of explosive contamination. This could include Libyans and non-Libyans residing in the most highly affected areas. Risk education will be delivered in different community settings with a priority of reaching children and adolescents in schools, during which HI will ensure that all vulnerable children and young people within the community and in different educational settings are reached with the life-saving awareness messaging. Through a community liaison approach of first assessing the needs with the community themselves and then keeping them informed of the progress during implementation, activities will be adapted to the different needs of men, women, boys and girls and also to the role that different genders play in reducing the risks of explosive hazards within the community. All mine action projects conducted in Libya will also work in support of the authorities in enhancing data collection to enable targeted deployment of teams in priority areas. HI will ensure coordination with other actors throughout project implementation. This includes attendance at the monthly meeting chaired by LibMAC’s Director and facilitated by UNMAS to discuss activities, challenges and priorities in the sector. HI is also part of the Mine Action sub-working group coordinated by UNMAS, where priorities and plans responses are also discussed. HI will ensure there is no duplication of efforts or resources during project implementation through ad-hoc coordination meetings, regular contact through emails and phone calls between HI and UNMAS.Mine Action actors, including HI and UNMAS are working in the same areas because these are the priority areas identified by LibMAC but it is not a duplication of efforts since the needs are vast and resources/capacities limited, therefore the work complements each other based on the LibMAC’s guidance and tasking.

Activities

- Deployment of Community Liaison teams;- Identification of agents of change to disseminate key awareness messages (community leaders/teachers etc.);- Set up of a referral mechanism and facilitate telephone hotline for community reporting of suspected hazardous areas to HI in collaboration with authorities;- Delivery of direct Risk Education sessions by HI teams;- Development and distribution of Risk Education information, education and communication (IEC) materials through Risk Education sessions;- ERW Threat Assessments conducted in areas with suspected contamination;- Training and deployment of Multi-task team clearance teams;- Non-Technical and Technical Survey and Clearance (removal and destruction of ERW) conducted in high priority, confirmed hazardous areas;- Reporting to the Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA).

Access, safety and dignity of beneficiaries

The overall objective of the project is to increase the safety of beneficiaries, which will be monitored through pre/post questionnaires during risk education sessions, as well as by the number of items of explosive hazards removed from land and destroyed, and the number of m2 of land cleared and declared safe. HI will ensure consultation with children and adults at the beginning of the project and systematically throughout implementation. Consultation will be done at the outset through a KAP survey to examine individuals' perceptions of the risks related to explosive hazards, their existing level of knowledge in order to tailor materials and safety messaging and to inform priority areas/groups for risk education. Through Community Liaison teams, who work alongside clearance teams, HI ensures that beneficiaries are consulted before, during and after clearance activities to take into account their needs, priorities and satisfaction with the services delivered. By the beginning of 2019, all HI's staff, partners and suppliers will have been sensitized to the organisation's new Code of Conduct, of which the PSEA policy is a central component. A dedicated human resource within the program has been identified to act as the PSEA focal point who will (following training) be assigned specific PSEA responsibilities, in close link with the Head of Mission.HI will actively seek feedback from beneficiaries at all stage of the project cycle. Different avenues will be established depending on the type and nature of feedback, with the mechanisms established to do so including a telephone number published on communication materials to contact HI for feedback/complaints, social media accounts through with beneficiaries and the local population can contact the organisation, as well as through monitoring visits conducted by the Head of Mission, Project Coordinator and Project Manager, allowing dedicated time to direct feedback from the beneficiary or benefiting community.
