Project Summary

The proposed project is to keep running the acute mental health unit in Sarmada/Idleb for seven more months with the two mental health mobile units. The main goal of the project is to promote specialized mental health service to address more severe mental health disorders.
Upon the need of the affected people to specialized MH services which are not sufficiently available in the area, UOSSM contributed to the response by establishing an acute MH inpatient and outpatient unit where services are provided for the most urgent presentations of severe mental health disorders, like schizophrenia (acute psychotic episodes), bipolar disorder (manic episode), severe depression, suicidal risk, severe substance misuse disorder and severe behavioral disorder related to mental health problems which cannot be managed and addressed in the community, in addition to providing a multidisciplinary mental health service in the community (outpatient psychiatric units, psychiatric supervision, follow up and case management, individual, couple and group psychotherapy and tele-mental health supervision), and receiving referrals of more severe and complicated psychiatric beneficiaries from other agencies and health providers.
Also, to cover as many areas as possible, the acute MH unit is linked with two MH mobile clinics, where MH interventions are provided by doctors trained on MHGAP program for beneficiaries of children and adults with signs and symptoms of mild and moderate mental health disorders, in addition to psychosocial support activities for children, which are: self-recovery techniques, coping skills with different situation skills, emotional expression techniques, PFA and PFA for children. Also, to facilitate of awareness campaigns on mental health and protection concerns, such as (detection of signs and symptoms of mental disorders, early marriage, GBV prevention. etc.). Not only the detection and referral of the serious and severe beneficiaries to the acute MH unit in Sarmada, but also the referral of beneficiaries or families to community resources to help in recovery from mental or physical illnesses, and to facilitate access to services provided by other humanitarian actors.
Objectives of the Project:
•To respond to the most urgent acute and serious mental health problems of the affected populations in the area
•To facilitate an access of the affected populations to long-term specialized MH services
•To enhance and promote the affected communities’ healthy coping mechanisms for both locals and IDPs
•To facilitate access for the affected populations to the available services in the area through an active referral pathway.

4 - Needs

Eight years into the crisis,additional support is urgently needed to scale up services to address increasing mental health needs,with estimated 27,635 people in need for mental health services through health centers,61,788 people in need for outpatient psychiatric care,and almost 12,000 requiring inpatient psychiatric care.WHO estimates that one in thirty people of the people living in Syria suffer from severe mental health conditions(such as severe depression,psychosis,or disabling anxiety),and at least one in five suffers from mild to moderate mental health conditions(such as depression or anxiety disorders).The overall Percentage of mental health(MH) and substance use disorders (SUD) out of total population is: 11.64% (Our World in Data).The total population of areas out of governance control are approximately 4.5 Million (HNO 2018).The estimated number of MH and SUD patients is11.64%*4.5 Million=523,800.On Average 2%-5% out of total MH patients,except substance use disorder,need a hospitalization, keeping them in a health unit for one to three weeks on average till recovery(Dr.Satto,MHPSS Coordinator,UOSSM-North region).On average 30%-35% out of total substance use disorder patients are addicted and need a hospitalization. Each MH-SUD Center includes two units MH and SUD,which can serve 720 patients in a hospitalization section and 4,400 in OP Clinics in a year;the total number of beneficiaries is 5,120.By analyzing the previous values and indicators,the overall needed MH-SUD Centers can be obtained as following: o Needed MH-SUD Centers=Total Number of patients/MH-SUD Center beneficiaries Needed MH-SUD Centers=523,800/5,210=102.On average, only 5to7percentage of these patients accept to visit such kind of services due many reasons; the main reason is due reputation.(Dr. Satto,MHPSS Coordinator,UOSSM-North region).We can summary the result of needed MH-SUD Units is 102, and when we count the previous indicator.the Actual number of needed MH-SUD units are 7.

5 - Implementation of activities

The inpatient unit:specialized psychiatric interventions,including full assessment,diagnosis with prescription of the appropriate psychotropic medications,will be provided for beneficiaries from both communities IDPs and host,women and men i.e.adults above 18 years old with the most urgent presentations of severe mental health disorders by a professional psychiatrist,which cannot be managed and addressed in the community,in addition to provision of group PSS sessions by a team of PSWs for beneficiaries.The outpatient unit: focused non-specialized interventions based on MHGAP programs,including the needed assessment and diagnosis with prescription of the appropriate psychotropic medications,will be provided,by a team of MHGAP doctors, for beneficiaries from both communities IDPs and host;women,men,children(above 5 years old)and adolescents presenting signs and symptoms of mild or moderate MH disorder,in addition to referral of acute and serious cases of beneficiaries to the acute inpatient unit.Also, provision of evidence-based individual consultations and group awareness raising sessions,by the team of the PSWs,for beneficiaries,families and caregivers on MH concerns using evidence-based sustained PSS programs.Two MH Mobile Clinics:MH interventions are provided by doctors trained on MHGAP program for beneficiaries of children and adults with signs and symptoms of mild and moderate mental health disorders including referral of acute or serious cases of beneficiaries to the psychiatrist in the acute MH unit in Sarmada.Also, to secure a referral network internally or externally,a service mapping of the available services and humanitarian actors in the areas of implementation will be created and developed including referral of beneficiaries or families to community resources to help in recovery from mental or physical illnesses, and to facilitate access to services provided by other humanitarian actors.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Risk-based violence,gender,and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding and shared toilets as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery:possibility: high, effect: highMitigating Measures:To mitigate and prevent these issues as possible, the wards of the beneficiaries in the acute unit will be separated, part for men and a part for women.In addition,for baths and toilets will be also isolated and separated for men and women and also for the staff.The MH unit will be also provided by a complaint box reached by beneficiaries.Above all, all the staff members will sign a code of conduct.For further control and supervision of the violence,gender and exploitation issues, the role of M&E will be activated during the project cycle. Also, there will be a client satisfaction survey conducted each three months by the PSWs for beneficiaries.2-Neglecting the most vulnerable(children, women, the elderly and the disabled):possibility:moderate,effect:highMitigating Measures:Taking into consideration the context and the environment roles,traditions and customs,the MH unit staff will be mixed of the two genders,males and females.Also,the working hours in the outpatient unit will be during daylight which enable women reach the services available.In addition,the building will be equipped by facilities for disabled beneficiaries such as ramp,wheelchair and modified toilets.Above all,the outreach PSWs will facilitate PSS activities targeting children and adolescences in schools and community centers in the area on a weekly or biweekly basis.3-Discrimination in service deliverypossibility: moderate,effect:highMitigating Measures:All the services provided will be based on humanitarian perspectives,UNHCR Humanitarian Principles,for both host communities and IDPs,and to achieve this goal many things will be taken into accounts;the location and place of the MH unit and mobile units will be safe and accessible for beneficiaries.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Since UOSSM is already registered in Turkey, UOSSM has a governmental validation from the Turkish Ministry of Health in Hatay Province for the implementation of its activities inside Syria especially in the areas controlled by the Turkish Government, this will facilitate access to work in Sarmada area and it is also maintained in an acceptable security conditions since the area is stabilized, managed and controlled by the Turkish Government. In addition, based on the services and interventions UOSSM provide inside Syria since 2012, local authorities, service providers and humanitarian stakeholders are familiar with those services leading to effective collaboration and coordination. Above all, Since UOSSM is already acting inside Syria in many locations and areas such as Idleb and Afrin, UOSSM has developed security management measures to limit its staff exposure to risk and also for proper respond regarding any unexpected risks as the following:• A Security Manager who has an extensive network of contacts in the areas of interventions allowing to collect information and to issue rapid alerts. • The Security Team also supervise the security monitoring of intervention's areas and regularly conduct evaluations upon the need. • Regular training for staff acting inside Syria by the Security Team to build their capacity and raise their knowledge to anticipate, mitigate and react to potential security threats• Moreover, exit strategy developed by the Technical & Operational Team for each project.
