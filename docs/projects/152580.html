Project Summary

Multiply displaced communities face significant health challenges in parts of Jonglei, Upper Nile and Eastern Equatoria, which prior to the conflict had limited or no access to health care services. The project seeks to provide access to essential lifesaving health care services to vulnerable populations focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality in the counties of Ayod,Uror (Jonglei); Longechuk, Ulang (Upper Nile State) and Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East (Eastern Equatoria State)
CMD aims at fostering improved access, prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone diseases, improve resilience among vulnerable populations and deliver an integrated response cutting across health, WASH, Nutrition, food security and protection. The project will implement and expand essential primary health care emergency clinical packages targeting the most vulnerable, carry out preventive health measures through immunisation targeting children, PLWs and <5s. Working alongside health cluster leads; CMD will implement outbreak investigation and response in the targeted locations while mainstreaming accountability to affected populations.
An expanded program will include HIV/AIDS/TB care and reproductive health (RH) services including SGBV. CMD will implement integrated response for severe acute malnutrition with medical complications especially in locations with high cases of malnutrition as well as implement integrated response for epidemic prone communicable diseases including diarrheal, vector, Viral Hemorrhagic diseases with WASH/Nut/FSL. Emergency health protection for people with disabilities will be provided across all project locations.
In the locations of Kapoeta South, East and Ulang, CMD will carry out referrals for Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (CeMONC), minor trauma and surgery, and work with development health partners to scale up integrated response to SAM with medical complications.
The project will directly benefit 15,000 people (51% female, 48% IDPs, 45% vulnerable host communities, 7% returnees and other groups in the locations of Ayod, Uror (Jonglei); Longechuk, Ulang (Upper Nile State) and Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East (Eastern Equatoria State)

Needs

Chronic health care challenges continue to emerge in Greater Upper Nile and Equatorias, due to the brunt effects of displacement, insecurity, food security, poor WASH and protection issues, which have equally undermined efforts of communities to cope with emerging needs, resulting in increased morbidity and mortality. In Greater Upper Nile, the counties of Ayod, Uror, Longechuk and Ulang jointly have populations close to 458,783 persons more than half of these women and children. (NBSS, 2018); with 128,691 of these internally displaced. Kapoeta East and South jointly host 239,804 persons, 10% of these IDPs. Health cluster projections place the number of persons in need at 88,800 in Ayod, 118,689 in Uror, 32,494 in Longechuk, 54,098 in Ulang, 18,341 in Kapoeta South and 44,205 in Kapoeta East with more than half of these women and children. Despite gains done through humanitarian support, non-functionality of health facilities, low levels of health supplies and shortage of staffing have all been a contributory factor to a slag in achieving desired results and projections. Some of the locations targeted lie under the Sobat and Eastern flood plains and are further prone to flooding, resulting in upsurges in communicable diseases. A cholera outbreak in Ayod continued throughout the previous dry season necessitating a comprehensive preventive health and WASH response. IPC projections for the locations targeted indicate high levels of food insecurity and malnutrition, alongside WASH cluster needs analyses that indicate low levels of sanitation and hygiene practices and lack of adequate clean and safe water. Mobility of populations due to several drivers such as conflict, food insecurity and livelihood opportunities will further necessitate adoption of mobile clinics as a methodology to counter possibility of disease outbreaks amongst multiply displaced populations, mining and pastoral communities. Significant disruption of markets, high levels of inflation have further lessened abilities of communities to access essential drugs, thereby relying on pipeline supplies provided by partners. EPI coverage remains low in the county, as a result of lack of cold chain facilities, access constraints and low or inadequate staffing. Protection issues are widely reported in the locations targeted, with limited capacity of the community structures to clinically deal with cases of SGBV. The project will directly benefit 15,000 people (51% female, 48% IDPs, 45% vulnerable host communities, 7% returnees and other groups in the locations of Ayod, Uror (Jonglei); Longechuk, Ulang (Upper Nile State) and Kapoeta South, Kapoeta East (Eastern Equatoria State)

Activities

As per the health cluster guidelines and packages CMD will carry out the following activities in locations of Ayod, Uror, Longechuk, Ulang, Kapoeta South and Kapoeta East through screening/detection, provision of essential health care package, management of cmmon illness/treatment, referral, promotive and preventive health services, surveillance/EWARN/reporting and health promotion/risk communication. These include:• ICCM• CHP• PHCU• PHCC/UPGRADED PHCC• MOBILE TEAM• County Hospital.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

It's entailed in the organisational values to provide beneficiaries with information, as well as a donor requirement and demand from beneficiaries. Several methodologies have been adopted which include publication of progressive reports that are availed in hard and soft copies to stakeholders, focus group constitution as a means to pass on information, involvement of beneficiary representatives in project review exercises, project literacy campaigns, use of visibility items such as banners, posters, T - Shirts, used to create awareness of the project. CMD also runs a social media platform through which project achievements are detailed. A suggestion box is placed at major project locations, through which formal feedback is obtained from stakeholders and responses provided.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are designed to reach beneficiaries in the language they best understand. Design of messages involves a representative of the different gender groups including women, boys, girls, men and the elderly. Special needs persons are also included, with support from key actors such as Handicap International and HelpAge. Messages are mindful of beliefs, cultures, attitudes and practices of communities, and are aimed at delivering results based on project plans and goals.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

The organisation has a PSEA policy in place, alongside a child safeguarding policy to prevent harm to children. These include PSEA procedures applies at staff recruitment, induction/training, performance management, whistle- blowing, discipline and grievance, programming guidelines, partnership arrangements and complaints/reporting. Specific responsibilities have been assigned for PSEA, and the organisation ensures staff, volunteers and associates understand and work to PSEA requirements. CMD engages communities to ensure that communities are fully aware of the expected behavior of the staff, how to complain if commitments are not met and ensuring appropriate awareness raising tools are available. This also applies to partners, suppliers and contractors, by assessment of capacity to implement PSEA measures and inclusion of clauses on PSEA in contract agreements. The project seeks to further identify potential risks of sexual exploitation and abuse, and has planned activities on PSEA awareness and sensitivity. CMD has developed a comprehensive complaints mechanism that aims to instill an organisational culture in which all complaints are taken seriously and acted upon.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CMD carried out several consultations across different strata, aiming at obtaining maximum response from all gender groups including women, girls, boys, men, elderly and persons with special needs. These included focus group discussions, house to house surveys, interviews with key informants, interviews with key beneficiaries at institutions, communities as well as village structures.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

As a commitment to IASC standards on accountability to affected populations, CMD has adopted and enforced clear feedback and complaints pathways, aimed at obtaining information on key programmatic aspects from the beneficiaries. These include provision of suggestion boxes at key project sites, that are periodically checked and feedback/complaints addressed, provision of feedback telephone/email addresses and focal persons with whom the community will be free with to provide information about the project. Review meetings are organized at all level with beneficiaries represented, aimed at getting feedback on project related matters.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

A monitoring and evaluations framework is designed at the inception of the project, with participation of the community, different gender group representatives and people with special needs. These are orientated on the tools to be used for the exercise and then form part of the team that carries out the exercise with focus on particular areas of common interest.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project has identified specific areas of risk which include safety and dignity, humanitarian access to crisis affected populations, populations access to aid, beneficiary privacy, individuals with special needs/risks, social relations, fraud and diversion with protection implications and market impacts with protection implications. The project will include affected communities as participants in all phases of the programme cycle to ensure that affected populations identify their own protection risks, benefits and self protection mechanisms. CMD will consider whether intervention will create or exacerbate protection risks or benefits for individuals, households and communities, engage individuals with different and specific needs and protection risks, establish two way feedback mechanisms and focal points to ensure regular communication. CMD has done a gender, age and diversity analysis.
