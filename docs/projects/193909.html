Project Summary

The action, based on the WC vulnerability map, the WC comprehensive assessment in schools that involved CESVI, consultations carried out with key stakeholders (WC, PWA, MoE, MoLG, municipalities), targets the HRP2023 WASH specific objective 2 “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians” and focuses on:
-WASH and Protection support in HHs
The project targets 200 vulnerable HHs (1,040 individuals) in Hebron Governorate.
Thanks to the consolidated experience from recent projects, CESVI is proposing to scale up activities for vulnerable HHs and enlarge the access to water, by ensuring safe water and rehabilitating sanitation facilities. More, as an innovative component, the distribution and installation of filters, prior approval by the PWA, will make available bacteriological contamination-free water. Improving access to safe water for drinking and hygienic use prevents further deterioration of HHs’ living and health conditions, enhances protection against inadequate WASH facilities and exposure to nutrition risks, limits negative coping mechanisms (e.g. water purchase from unreliable vendors, using open spaces as toilets). CESVI has developed strong expertise in WASH (previous/ongoing projects in Hebron and Gaza for adequate access to water quantity and quality). The project design has been coordinated with relevant stakeholders (WC, PWA) and simultaneously addresses urgent needs of vulnerable populations while endowing them with proper WASH systems.
-Support for Waste service providers
The project supports service providers in different communities located in Hebron Governorate, supplying 359 bins to improve the capacity and the environmental situation of existing collection points.
-WASH support in public institutions (schools)
CESVI has direct knowledge about WASH-related risks and needs affecting these institutions and thanks to previous and ongoing projects in Hebron, gained relevant experience in improving WASH facilities in schools. During 2022 Cesvi developed assessment tools to implement rehabilitation and maintenance, such as ToC, stakeholders and problems mapping, and a technical tool to report needs.
The project proposes rehabilitation and maintenance interventions of WASH facilities in 3 schools in Hebron Governorate. The activity will be accompanied by a Behaviour change campaign on proper hand washing practises, in line with Cesvi expertise. This component is deemed extremely important as revealed in the frame of the Covid19 pandemic due to lack of improved WASH facilities and related worsened health risks.
Cesvi’s specific expertise combined with close relationships with relevant authorities (MoE and MoH), results in assessments pre-identifying necessary and specific interventions in each facility.
-Construction of a waste water treatment plant and sewer network
The project aims to design and construct an innovative wastewater treatment plant in one of the most vulnerable areas in Hebron: Ad Deirat community, located in Khallet al Mayya municipality. The project will provide a sewer network for 153 families that will be connected to an innovative water treatment plant sized to the needs of the community.
The activity is conducted on a research-based approach according to the expertize of Cesvi and PPU. The activity is conducted with a pilot approach, that will be tested and analyzed in order to adopt its replicability in other vulnerable communities in the future.
The project is supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA) with specific focus on the most vulnerable groups, including women, girls, boys, the disabled and the elderly. Gender principles are mainstreamed throughout the entire action. Finally, Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) is core to the proposed Action.

1 - Needs

It is estimated that over 1.4 million Palestinians across the oPt are suffering from insufficient access to WASH services and will be in need of related humanitarian assistance in 2023. The restrictive planning regime enforced by the Israeli authorities in Area C of the West Bank and East Jerusalem is still preventing Palestinian communities from connecting to the water and sewage networks, as well as the deployment of rain-harvesting cisterns and mobile latrines (HNO 2023). The WASH Cluster Vulnerability Map 2022 shows that nearly 210,000 Palestinians in the West Bank live in areas either not connected to piped water services or very poorly supplied. For own water supply HHs mainly rely on rainwater harvesting (runoff) and/or trucked water. A recent assessment carried out by Cesvi in July-September 2022 covering 406 vulnerable families in Khallet Al Mayya, Khallet Eddar, Beit Einoon and Al Baqa’a areas (South Hebron hills) shows that drinking water quality is strongly contaminated due to the use of underground water cisterns for the storage of rainwater together with truck-purchased water and occasional supply of water from the network (86% of HHs are at high risk of water contamination). Data show 23% of the population in these communities suffered from diarrhea during the 3 previous days before the assessment due to the combination of poor hygiene practices, inadequate hand-washing and sanitation facilities, and water quality. These conditions harm women’ and girls’ dignity as they have to adopt risky coping mechanisms. Protection principles were considered to evaluate risks related to WASH facilities access and use. 31% of HHs don’t meet basic WASH infrastructure needs including access to safe toilets and wash facilities, and lack of proper condition of kitchens. Moreover, in the targeted locations almost 4% of the assessed population have disabilities and those are the most affected by this unsafe sanitation. Adequate access to safe WASH facilities and practices are prerequisite for healthy and safe schools. Adolescent girls in particular may be discouraged from attending school. The overall status of WASH facilities in schools does not meet international standards, with no sufficient ratio of toilets and 44% of schools not reaching even minimal standards in the West Bank. An additional deficiency that hampers infrastructure quality is related to the lack of maintenance. A technical assessment conducted by Cesvi in September 2022 in 10 schools in the West Bank found that there are difficulties in managing water and sanitation infrastructures in schools, leading to a decreased level of service. In addition, students' hygienic behavior related to hand washing is poor, leading to high health risks due to disease transmission. Solid waste management in the West Bank is challenged by several issues that hamper its actual implementation capacity during the different phases of collection, treatment, transport and final disposal. A chronic lack of sufficient resources and means, due to limited budget available – even more exacerbated by insufficient revenue collection with no recovery of operational costs, impedes institutions in charge (municipalities and JSCs) from delivering the service according to the needs, and as a result waste is not properly and wholly collected and gets accumulated by collection containers (where available), or is improperly disposed of in open areas. Abandoned waste is then usually burnt by citizens as the only way they have to get rid of it; indeed, this improper behaviour comes from a lack of individual and social environmental awareness and care, not having paid at institutional level dedicated attention and priority to the improvement or protection of the environmental situation. On the other hand, the population and the amount of waste generated are increasing and urgent solutions are necessary while waiting for more comprehensive approaches and interventions aiming at reducing waste generation as a whole through the application of ‘R principles’, as already stated in national policies and programs. This situation is especially affecting small scattered communities located far from municipalities’ centers and main towns, where the waste collection is irregular. Most of HHs in the selected target communities for waste management activities have broken waste disposal bins, being 85% of them older than 8 years. This causes problems of dirtyness and sewage leakage in homes and in the surrounding environment and impacts on the performance of the collection service. The wastewater sector in Palestine faces many challenges ranging from lack of sewer networks to insufficient number of treatment facilities and high pollution loads in effluent streams. The total amount of wastewater generated in Palestine in 2015 was approximately 115 MCM (ARIJ and PCBS) and is expected to increase to 140 MCM by 2025. Approx. only 33% of waste water is collected through sewage networks.The situation in Hebron Governorate is the worst one for wastewater collection and treatment of wastewater: <1% out of the total amount of generated wastewater is treated in Hebron Governorate and the rest is discharged into cesspits or open wadies; only part of Hebron city is connected to the sewage network, while most of other localities in the Governorate, especially rural communities and remote areas in area C are not connected at all to any sewage network.The discharge of untreated wastewater in the open environment and wadis poses major environmental and public health threats. Underground leaching of various contaminants into groundwater, i.e. the main source of drinking water in Palestine, will increase health risks of waterborne diseases, in addition to leakage of wastewater and contaminates from porous cesspits that are the most common wastewater collection systems used in Palestine, into groundwater and rain water collection wells.

2 - Activities

Activity 1 – Provision of HH water filters. Based on PWA indications and approval, CESVI provides 200 targeted HHs with water filters to minimize the risk of cross-contamination before consumption. HHs are selected based on an assessment conducted by CESVI in June-September 2022 on possible sources of water contamination at HH level in South Hebron Hills communities. The design and selection of the components are based on CESVI previous experience in the West Bank and Gaza and are developed in close coordination with the water quality working group. Technical performance and user acceptance evaluation methodology were assessed since 2018 with five projects funded by HIF, UNICEF and AICS. The specific type of filter proposed for this activity is also selected based on a study conducted by CESVI in Gaza "Finding the appropriate household water filter for emergencies". During 2022, in the frame of 2 projects funded by AICS where water filters are distributed, a monitoring campaign for the evaluation of their performance is conducted with the collaboration of the PWA, and will serve to provide evidence-based and data-driven results to coordinate with the PWA the proposed activity. The project team test the water quality at the supplying point, after the treatment and at the point of use (at the cup) in order to ensure the targeted HH’s adequate access to safe water.Activity 2- Rehabilitation and upgrade of sanitation facilities at households level. Cesvi supports 75 HHs in South Hebron hills through conditional cash assistance to rehabilitate and upgrade Wash facilities (kitchens, toilets, connection of drinking water tanks to a sink equipped with water-saving supply, and connection of domestic water tank to the water network inside the house). The selection of the HHs is based on an assessment conducted by CESVI in June-September 2022 on HH conditions covering 406 HHs. Work implementation is followed up and monitored through visits conducted by CESVI to each HH to provide technical support, review progress made by beneficiaries and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel cash instalments. Monitoring visits are also used to identify any technical issues beneficiaries could face and solve them on the spot through complementary training and guidance.Activity 3 – Support to Municipalities for SWM. In coordination with the municipalities of Hebron governorate, CESVI addresses 5 localities’ urgent needs for the improvement of the waste collection service, by way of supply of community containers to enhance capacity and environmental conditions of waste collection public points. CESVI provides 359 waste bins with a capacity of 200 liters. The activity includes the procurement process and quality control to ensure meeting the service provider’s standards, and thus ensuring the population of the target municipalities and the access to safe waste disposal system.Activity 4 – WASH in schools. Findings from the response framework for WASH in Schools Vulnerability Mapping conducted in 2021 by the WC, with the active support of CESVI, highlighting a pressing need to enhance WASH facilities in West Bank, are still confirmed. Based on technical assessment of selected schools, in collaboration with MoE and MoH and with a specific focus on needs of people with disabilities, as well as age- and gender-sensitive concerns, Bills of Quantities (BOQs) are developed for 3 institutions in Hebron Governorate, outlining rehabilitation and maintenance WASH needs and related action plans. Contractors are selected through a transparent tender process. CESVI conducts regular follow-up and monitoring visits to ensure respect of the plan and of WASH and SPHERE standards, as well as to provide corrective actions where needed. Together with the Rehabilitation of wash facilities, CESVI and its partner PPU conduct a behaviour change campaign in the 3 schools to promote correct and enhanced hygiene practices of handwashing.Activity 5 – Implementation of an innovative technology for waste water treatment plant and sewer network. The main objective of the project is to design and construct a wastewater treatment plant (WWTP) in Ad Deirat. The activity includes also establishing a sewer network for wastewater collection from 153 houses and 800 beneficiaries and their connection to the WWTP. The design of the proposed plant is based on considering as fresh water consumption 50 l/c/d; being the quantity of wastewater generated approx. 85% of the total freshwater consumption, the daily community wastewater production sums 34 m3/d. This value is multiplied by a safety factor = 1.15 (to factor future increase of wastewater production due to population growth), for a total amount of approx. ~ 40 m3/day used to design the WWTP.The innovative wastewater treatment unit mainly consists of a primary physical-chemical step, followed by a secondary biological treatment step based on aerobic decomposition processes.The following methodological framework will be applied:(i) Selecting, designing, constructing, and operating wastewater treatment plant and sewer network.(ii) Experimental parametric study for optimizing wastewater treatment process.(iii) Field surveys, wastewater records, sampling, and testing wastewater biological and physical-chemical characteristics including: TDS, TSS, COD, BOD and concentrations of selected ions and chemical compounds. The design of the system is provided by a PPU research engineer as well as the conduction of the the testing of the infratructure, while a contractor managed and supervised by Cesvi implements the construction. Laboratory analysis are conducted at the PPU laboratory.The development of the executive design, the specific treatment technology, the exact location of the infrastructure will be agreed and coordinated during the implementation phase by the technical committee established by the PWA, Cesvi, PPU, with the involvement of the Municipality and the support of the WC.

3 - Indicators

Indicators are selected based on clusters' indicators. In addition to those, CESVI and its Partner will measure the following additional indicators:# of households sanitation facilities rehabilitated (Target: 75; Means of verification: BoQs, technical report, handover certificate)# of school WASH facilities constructed/ rehabilitated (Target: 3; Means of verification: technical report, handover certificate)# of household water treatment units provided/ installed (Target: 200; Means of verification: technical report, handover certificate)# of solid waste containers/bins provided (Target: 359; Means of Verification: handover certificate)# of water water treatment unit installed (Target: 1; Means of Verification: technical report; handover certificate; Access to performance data).# Lenght (M.L) of sewer network constructed (Target 1500; Means of Verification: handover certificate; Sewer mapping) Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. The CESVI Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) officer will be in charge of measuring project outcomes and progress indicators through a variety of approaches and methodology. MEAL activities will enable the gathering and reporting of detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneficiaries and to measure the project outcomes of different target groups. Eventually, satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), both managed by the MEAL officer will enable partners to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanisn will be designed both culturally and gender-sensitive including PSEA complaints. Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) complying with HAP and CHS standards and SoPs. The mechanism will feature several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits, to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries, and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. Following the completion of the project, the committees will be responsible for monitoring, identifying and reporting any technical or protection issues in regard to rehabilitation of land and assets to CESVI as well as the relevant local service and maintenance providers, to ensure the sustainability of the project’s outputs and outcomes. The financial aspects will be regularly monitored as per Cesvi procedures, which include the development of an initial purchase plan, monthly spending monitors and monthly updates of the expenses incurred and planned. Cesvi will thus manage and prepare financial progress analysis and document checks. Tools: Purchasing and procurement plan; analytical and accounting summary, forecast and planning of expenses (BFU - Budget Forecast Update).Ensuring equal opportunity and considering different age-, gender-, and disabilities-related needs are core to the proposed project, together with the identification of specific needs of the most vulnerable groups (women, children, the elderly, PwDs). Appropriate disaggregated data collection and adequate data analysis ensure activities are implemented reflecting assessed needs. While prioritising needs to be addressed, it must be noted that women and girls for istance are disproportionally impacted by water quantity and quality limitations affecting substandard sanitation facilities (at HH level as well as in schools), for their own health including menstrual health and hygiene. All these aspects are taken in consideration during the implementation of the project. The participatory approach proposed by Cesvi includes questionnaires, FGDs, assessments and awareness campaign geared to include all family members. FGDs, for example, always ensure the participation of women, and, if necessary, through the organization of 2 separate male and female groups. Cesvi is oriented to ensure at least one female component in its staff, especially where communication is needed on women’s, adolescent girls’, children with disabilities’ perception on hygiene, sanitation, and access to water.

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through theproject Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFM), implemented in all CESVI projects, in line with Core Humanitarian Standard. It is harmonised with PSEA complaints and whistle-blower procedure. The system is overseen by MEAL officer. All beneficiaries of the action will be explained their right to provide feedback and complaints in confidentiality and without interfering with the potential assistance and will provide with information material summarizing the complaint process and the different avenues available for submitting a complaint. It is worth mentioning that CFM relies on existing and solid terms of reference, SoPs, field paper forms, compliant tracking databases and clear independent solving mechanisms, as well as mandatory timely feedback to the complainants. This CFM system will enable partners to identify and solve any issues reported by beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. The mechanisms feature several channels, including a dedicate hotline and frequent visits by staff to ensure that feedback is received from beneciaries, and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. The final communication channels will be identified with the support of the community, considering all the age and gender groups. Engagement and acceptance of community members is a key component of the design, implementation and monitoring of projects. Beneficiaries' satisfaction is continuously monitored through regular visits and interviews to beneficiaries regarding ongoing activities. The MEAL officer will report any issues brought to its attention.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

The intervention mainstreams protection following indications provided by the Protection Cluster and relevant guidelines and tools (e.g. Protection Mainstreaming toolkit, Gender & WASH Toolkit). Both activities and implementation modalities are designed in order to prioritize safety and dignity, and avoid causing harm. Possible risks - security, safety, physical, and psychosocial - were considered, including unintended negative effects as much as possible. Accountability is ensured by a dedicated tool - project complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) - specifically tailored on the project as per Cesvi procedure. It will also support - along with other specific activities (e.g. workshops, regular monitoring with a questionnaire) - participation of beneficiaries and their empowerment. The action improves the resilience of the communities, due to improved access to WASH services, and a better coping capacity. In particular, water treatment units are not at risk of confiscation, thus can improve the quality of water consumed even in the case of forcible transfer. They do not need any electricity, consumable nor chemical, thus they will work with no additional cost for the household. Cesvi commits to: - Prioritize safety & dignity and avoid causing harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention, which can increase people’s vulnerability to both physical and psychosocial risks. For instance, Cesvi will ensure that the location of EH facilities and routes to them are away from actual or potential threats such as violence; especially the risk or threat of gender-based violence (GBV); use discussion groups and observation to detect/discuss this and ask the community to come up with solutions; HWTS design will preserve the safety and dignity of its users, facilities design must preserve the safety and dignity of its users - Accountability: Setup appropriate mechanisms through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns and complaints. Cases of violations should be referred promptly and in accordance with standard operating procedures established in the area. Eventually, Cesvi staff will be trained on protection procedures for referral of eviction and demolition orders. Based on the safeguarding policy Cesvi commits to doing all the needful for preventing programmes having any negative effect on children. Cesvi takes all the complaints and concerns about violence against children seriously and it initiates a rigorous investigation of complaints indicating a possible violation of this Policy as well as of Cesvi Code of Conduct and of Cesvi Ethical Code. The safeguarding policy complements, without replacing: Cesvi Ethical Code, Cesvi Staff Code of Conduct, Cesvi PSEA Policy, Cesvi Policy on the Use of Images of Children and Young People and Human Resources Policy. RCSD agreed to follow the CESVI procedure for having an aligned approach to the communities.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The proposal is based on the need assessment and context analysis carried out by Cesvi and other stakeholders. The action proposed met the recognize that different groups and individuals have different needs and provide an appropriate answer in proportion to need. Needs were identified household per household before the submission of the proposal but will be confirmed during the implementation, in order to crosscheck again conditions of beneficiaries and confirm the selection based on vulnerabilities. Water treatment units, rehabilitation of sanitation facilities and awareness will be adapted and introduced in order to guarantee meaningful access for all the beneficiaries, removing any barrier. The above-mentioned project complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) will support the project staff to adapt the response to the emerging needs or to correct the ongoing actions. Furthermore, it will be provided information, in accessible formats and languages, about entitlements and the complaint-feedback mechanism in place. Cesvi commits to: - Meaningful Access: Arrange for people’s access to assistance and services – in proportion to need and without any barriers (e.g. discrimination). Cesvi will pay special attention to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and services, for instance, the installation of ramps and railings to EH facilities -Participation and empowerment: Support the development of self-protection capacities and assist people to claim their rights, including – not exclusively – the rights to shelter, food, water and sanitation, health, and education..In line with Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) guidelines on the accountability to affected populations, partners are engaging beneficiaries in the intervention design for construction or rehabilitation of WASH facilities, both in households (families) and in public institutions (staff managing schools). Prior to the procurement of assistance, each household and public institution will be informed on the planned assistance in order to determine if needs have significantly changed since the original needs assessment. If outstanding needs are identified through this process, the material assistance will be adapted to meet those needs, as long as it fits within the budgeted amount for the location. The involvement of Community representatives will ensure communities’ interests are duly taken into consideration throughout the implementation process. This way, the repair to be undertaken by the household is decided in collaboration within the household members (women and male) and is in line with their priorities and needs. They will also ensure that all protection and privacy concerns faced by the household due to the substandard WASH facilities (toilets, wash facilities and kitchens) are addressed in priority.A similar approach will be adopted while implementing activities in public institutions (schools), according to the needs represented by the authorities in charge (MoE) and assessed during the development of the proposal, that will be assessed again at the time of the project start.
