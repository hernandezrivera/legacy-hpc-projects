Project Summary

After identifying the initial need by OCHA-WHO in the affected Syrian areas in addition to HeRams reports, Youth Charity Association based on these reports carried out its own assessment by studying and analyzing the situation through visits by the survey team of the association to Al-Zabdani area of Rural Damascus governorate and meeting with key people to reach an accurate study and determine the need optimally, and an important reason that helped in the ease of access to execute survey is that some of the team members are from the population of the targeted areas, and which helped to gain more confidence.
Al-Zabdani is a city and popular hill station in southwestern Syria in the Rif Dimashq Governorate, close to the border with Lebanon. It is located in the center of a green valley surrounded by high mountains at an elevation of around 1,100 m, and one of the affected areas by the Syrian crisis, with a damage exceeds 70% of the area including medical centers and related health sector facilities. The area is now considered under government control.
Al-Zabdani is an area that has a population of over 30,000 out of this: 45 % women, 25 % men, 17 % boys and 13 % girls. Most residents are currently very poor and internally displaced after ten years of war and encircle, resulted in most of them need medical care and services. High percentage of the population are children and families who rely on women as their main breadwinners. The whole targeted area has no hospitals nor medical centers with the exception of one hospital that has been converted into an isolation center for people suspected of being infected with Coronavirus 19 which means lack of medical services, and the medical situation there is very poor with no existence of the clinics for people and very poor medical services.
Due to the high population of Al-Zabdani including surroundings and the inability of many of them to leave the area (due to security/financial reasons i.e. medical compensations). The project aims at establishing one Mobile Medical Clinic in Al-Zabdani area in order to provide free medical services will provide it to 30,000 of the aforementioned. (Primary Health Services, Secondary Health Services and Trauma Services) with referral system, in addition, providing free medication, and find job opportunities to jobless people of the area (mainly women). The number of beneficiaries was determined according to a specific logical study, where approximately /50/ patients are received per day in one clinic. Those patients in return receive medical consultations by specialized doctors in addition to medical analysis, surgeries, and trauma services.
The location of the mobile medical clinic will be located in Al-Zabdani – Al Silan Square.
And referral system will be executed in coordination with local hospitals in Damascus for the available medical services and the ability of beneficiaries to travel, and referrals to hospitals in Damascus for the medical services that are not available in Al Zabadani hospital. And medical services include free medication and the source of medicines is the Pharmacists Syndicate. Trauma services will be provided in the emergency clinic in mobile medical clinics in the following cases: emergency wounds, fractures, and abscesses.
YC maintains provision of the aforementioned health care services to all who seek medical care without ethnic, religious, gender, and/or political discrimination, as all medical services are being.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risk of failure to provide full free service, and this will be addressed through permanent monitoring and daily contact with beneficiaries and by implementing active strict quality procedure and penalties and if impossible to deal with this case in Daraa without the budget we will transfer the case for executive management team of the YC association In referral system there is the risk of the non-availability of a specified medical service and doctors in the local area, and this will be addressed by transferring referrals of beneficiaries to Damascus and provide beneficiaries with medical attendance cards to facilitate mobility. The risk of continuous power outages and this will be addressed by providing generator to mobile clinic. The difficulty of reaching (security) from the nearby countryside, and here it is addressed by choosing secure strategic locations of the mobile medical clinic which are easily accessible by beneficiaries in addition to the fact that most villages in the area have returned to government control. The risk of lacking commitment of some beneficiaries of the continuing treatment process and will be addressed by daily follow-up medical system and conducting daily questionnaires to beneficiaries and closing each case separately. The issue of non-occurrence of gender-based violence (women & children), violence against children, and child labor will be pursued, The raising in exchange rates due to the economic situation in the country.
