1 - Needs

On August 4, 2020, a warehouse at the Beirut Port containing 2,750 tons of improperly stored ammonium nitrate exploded. After an initial explosion, a subsequent blast caused widespread damage, with reports of destruction more than 20 kilometres from the port area. The explosion impacted several neighbourhoods in Greater Beirut and adjacent quarters in the Mount Lebanon governorate.Oxfam has been triangulating informataion from various sources, including parnter asessmsents and is also taking part in the joint livelihoods assessments. We have identified the following key needs: Livelihoods: Lebanon was already facing an economic crisis prior to the explosion, with 45% of its population categorized as poor9. The explosion worsened this already dire situation. The destruction affects micro, small, and medium businesses (MSMEs) and shops across all sectors, especially those in the services sector. A survey by LRC was conducted to assess the recovery capacity of people and business owners in the affected areas. The results indicate a limited capacity for recovery, and difficulty to exercise any form of resilience as 78% of the responders stated that they have no saving, and 37% stated that they have no income10. The closure of the businesses, as well as damage of the port is expected to significantly increase the number of unemployed people, resulting in worsening the economic crisis. The damage of the ports, in addition, is expected to severely impact the trade (import and export) sector, as well as all actors in the supply chain leading to unavailability of material in the market. All of this is expected to induce a major shock to the market and labour force. The results of these initial assessments put forward the need to provide different types of livelihood interventions, including provision of temporary cash assistance to individuals who lost their income as a result of the explosion, distribution of cash assistance to affected businesses (especially micro and small in vulnerable areas) to support their early recovery, and job matching and creation of jobs. The livelihood actors and other actors (e.g. ILO) are currently implementing joint business impact assessments, and labour assessments which will help design a coordinated response, utilizing tailored approaches, to respond to the needs. Food Security: As previously mentioned, hyperinflation has led to an increase in the cost of food prior to the explosion. The assessments did not identify any increase in prices post-explosion. This, however, remains a risk due to the loss of 85% of Lebanon’s cereal reserves due to the explosion, and the unstable economic and political situation that Lebanon is undergoing. The possibility of a further increase in prices is also linked to the dysfunctionality of Beirut port, and the shifting of the sea traffic to Tripoli port (with an already-limited capacity and is 85 Km North of Beirut). Fear over food security was already a main worry among many Lebanese prior to the explosion11, where families where already reducing their food portions and meat consumption prior to the explosion12. The impact of the explosion on the labour force and the business environment has increased the fear for food insecurity, as it further reduced the purchasing power of individuals. Similar to the recommendations for the Livelihood needs, cash programming is suggested as one response to mitigate the fear for food insecurity. There is a big issue of currency devalution and increasing prices, even since the explosion. Cash needs to be sufficient to meet basic needs and must therefore be based on the latest market asseessments.

2 - Activities

Oxfam will seek to address the complex needs of the affected populations through integrated projects implemented through local actors. Oxfam will work with five local partners, who are delivering projects across multiple sectors. Activites under Food and Cash[SO1] Enhanced Food Security through provision of food rations (1860 individuals) KAFA 500 women and girls impacted by Gender Based violence assisted with food parcels ARM – 200 migrant workers and their families assisted with food support (760 individuals)Helem – 100 individuals, members of the LGBTQ community will receive meals for four monthsCLDH - 500 women and girls will receive food parcels [SO2] Enhanced Food Security through cash based programming (3645 individuals) CLDH 400 families (1900 individuals) will receive emergency cash assistance KAFA - 74 single women head of household will benefit from financial assistance for shelter rehabilitation, 17 women/girls affected and/or having lost their income will receive temporary cash assistance, 38 women & girls will receive a one-time cash assistance to cover emergency and medical needs. B&Z – 400 vulnerable families (1520 individuals) assisted with temporary cash assistance LPHU 100 Individuals who are elderly and people with disabilities assisted with emergency cash assistance All projects will use targeting criteria that are in line with the sector guidelines. All cash amounts will be aligned with sector guidelines. Specific attention will be paid to ensure that women are able to access the services and where alterations need to be made in order for them to do this, they will be made. Oxfam and partners ensure gender balance within their staff to ensure that cultural sensitivities are considered. Data protection is key, particulary as some of the groups targeted are especially vulnerable, such as LGBT communities, GBV survivors and refugees. Oxfam adheres to the GDPR data protection guidelines. All activities are monitored and a feedback mechanism is rolled out that is harmonised across all Oxfam partners and complaints are addressed by an independant team. Oxfam takes concrete steps to mitigate the risks of sexual exploitation and abuse, including an independant whistleblowing hotline. Parnters will be trained on OXfams approach to safeguarding and beneficairies will be informed of their rights around recieving aid, with particular attention paid to the fact that some people may have limited education. Oxfam strives to exceed its Grand Bargain committments and works for better, more equal partnerships in all its work. This project has been designed by our local parnters, based on their own identifications of the needs.
