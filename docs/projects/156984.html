Project Summary

The health intervention will focus on the injured pathway, as part of its victim assistance approach, i.e. will support the health partners and structures and manage its health teams for the provision of various services.
The NGO, its implementing partners and the health facilities supported will comprehensively address the urgent needs of persons with functional limitations through a combination of physical rehabilitation (including provision of assistive devices and education sessions for caregivers), P&O and PSS services within the global rehabilitation framework, and MHPSS. These services will aim to foster the beneficiary's recovery of functional independence and acceptance of his new condition to regain a certain level of confidence, autonomy and wellbeing. Interventions will be delivered through stand-alone, complementary and integrated services. Further to supporting beneficiaries with functional limitations, the Action will target caregivers for a holistic service delivery to alleviate and help them cope with stress, fatigue and other psychosocial impacts and vulnerabilities related to their new caregiving responsibility. In NES, the Organization will consolidate and strengthen its support to health facilities including referral hospitals so as to set up sustainable rehab and prosthetics services while reaching out to remote and underserved communities as part of its first-line emergency response in the three governorates. In GoS-controlled areas, the INGO will focus at developing rehab and MHPSS services in areas where services are not present or functioning, through the support and capacity-building to local partners.
The MHPSS component, offered to assessed-persons suffering from psychosocial disabilities, will be built on the innovative pilot activity conducted during the 2018/2019 Action. In parallel, the Organization will develop capacity development plans for its health partners and the health facilities supported to build their independence through technical and organizational long-term support. the Organization will also seek to foster operational coordination between humanitarian actors at hub and WoS levels so as to improve the quality of interventions.
i) physical and functional rehabilitation (including physical and functional rehabilitation sessions, provision of assistive devices, education sessions for caregivers),
ii) P&O services (sessions and provision of P&O items),
iii) PSS sessions to people with functional limitations and their caregivers
iv) MHPSS services (psychological first aid, psychoeducation, individual session therapy or MHPSS group sessions).

Needs

Before the crisis, Syria's health system was already inadequate to address injury and disability related needs, and the protracted conflict has further eroded and overburdened the health system: large gaps remain in the provision of post-operative rehabilitation care required by the war injured. Rehabilitation is essential as, according to the HNO 2019, an average of 45% of persons injured during the Syria crisis are expected to sustain a permanent impairment. Based on the Organization's past interventions, 47% of beneficiaries had fractures, 15% had been amputated, 10% had peripheral nerve damage, and 5% had a Spinal Cord Injury (SCI) leading to paralysis. At least 15% of the population requires mental health and psychosocial services (MHPSS) and the crisis was labelled as the cause of 73% of the severe Mental Health cases identified, (HNO 2019). Indeed an IMC 2017 report identified severe emotional disorders (54% of the MH issues reported) as the most common MH issue, including depression, anxiety, with as main emotional stressors security and protection concerns, access and availability of basic services and lack of medical staff due to the disruption or destruction of key infrastructures. The Syrian Arab Association of Psychiatrists stated that 92% of severe MH cases cannot reach technical support or follow up, despite the fact that not addressing the MHPSS impact of violence exacerbates profound and persistent existential concerns of safety, trust, identity, social cohesion and community resilience. Likewise, health was among the most commonly reported top priority needs across the Deir Ezzor governorate (REACH, Oct 2018). In the West Line, key informants in 83% of communities reported that health was a top-three priority need, followed by 74% in North Line, and 64% in the East Line. The Physical Rehabilitation Disability Working Group conducted a survey in 2018 to address the information gap in accessible areas of Aleppo, Idleb and Ar-Raqqa governorates. The prevalence of Pwd was reported at 33% which is double of the world average (WHO 2011 world report on disability). In Ar-Raqqa governorate, prevalence rate of having one or more disabled member in the household was reported at 27%. Respondents with disabilities were asked if the Syrian conflict had exacerbated their difficulties, and in each governorate, over half of respondents said yes, reflecting the increased vulnerability of persons with disabilities in conflict settings. Those who said `yes' (60% in Aleppo, 80% in Idleb, and 67% in Ar-Raqqa) most commonly reported the following reasons: lack of access to medical/health care, lack of assistive devices or resources, and difficulties in addressing their needs, due to the crisis. Disruption of the individual's social network was a less commonly reported reason. In 2018, the Organization preliminary data places are up to 19,897 beneficiaries, with support to 38 health structures by providing emergency multi-disciplinary rehabilitation services. 71% of the reported beneficiaries are male,29% are female. Further, 84% are adults. 63% suffered from injury, 40% of which were conflict-related injuries. The need for rehabilitation services is increasing, yet there are few actors with the technical capacity to support such work, little reliable data to understand the scope of the rehabilitation needs, and non-specialized organizations are failing to adopt an inclusive approach to their work. This requires a specific working group to focus first on Quality of services, define minimum technical standards for rehabilitation management of people with injuries/disabilities, including provision of tools and training to support rehabilitation actors with complex patients in Syria and no technical support.

Protection Risk Analysis

In most health structures in Syria, and particular field hospitals, the majority of the staff are male. the Organization will strive to include men and women in this activity as much as possible in a balanced way, as well as in its recruitments. the INGO will also encourage the health structures with a strong gender imbalance among their staff to correct / redress this imbalance, making their recruitment practices are more inclusive. Health structures (field hospitals in particular) are regularly targeted with air strikes and shelling. In addition, wounded persons (in particular adult males) face a higher risk of arrest or abduction due to their (real or presumed) affiliation with armed groups. the INGO has put in place different measures to avoid exposing its beneficiaries and its staff to further harm: 1) in areas with high intensity of conflict and frequent targeting of health structures, rehabilitation services are temporarily suspended and/or are delivered with mobile teams and home visits, instead of in exposed health structures; 2) all personal data of beneficiaries are treated in strict confidential way; 3) specialized services (such as prosthetics and orthotics) that require several visits over different days are offered in areas with lower intensity of conflict and lower risk of targeting by armed groups.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Preconditions. Access to the targeted areas is maintained in acceptable security conditions during the project duration; The terms and commitments of partnership are respected by the parties; The various stakeholders coordinate themselves; Acceptance by local population is granted to the INGO staff and partners; Relations with authorities allow the INGO to operate in the country. Assumptions and risks. 1. Deterioration of the security situation 2. Risk of investigation and arrest for stakeholders involved in aid provision 3. Denegation of approval and support of the local authorities (Syrian, Turkish, Iraqi, Jordanian and Lebanese) 4. Shift of the geopolitical situation, related to shift in power or control/new agreements 5. Logistic and fund transfer issues jeopardize the implementation of activities 6. Suspension of cross-border access for shipping of supplies and movement of health care workers for training 7. Difficulties faced in the recruitment of high profile positions and of gender balanced team. Mitigation measures. 1. The access to intervention areas is not maintained in acceptable security conditions. the Organization has developed an access strategy and will refer to it if needed 2. In case of accident, the INGO activates its duty of care Policy 3. the Organization's teams are well-perceived on the ground by both the local communities and local authorities. The INGO, has active registration/MoU with the governments of Iraq, Jordan and Lebanon as well as the Kurdish administration. The organization is currently seeking registration with the Government of Syria and the Government of Turkey 4. Shift of territory control that might lead to a loss of access. The Organization will refer to its access strategy 5. Fund transfer issues will be mitigated by assessing several transfer partners as well as by enhancing coordination with INGO working in this area 6. Most trainings are done remotely from Amman, in a fully equipped training room in order to provide quality remote trainings. Local procurement and a contingency stock are being explored to enable continuation of activities should border closes 7. The organization has a strong technical unit in Amman with the mandate to build local capacities through remote training and coaching. The INGO puts continuous effort in the recruitment of female workers in the field and ensures all teams are mixed. In light of the recent evolution of the context in NES, the INGO has hired in early January 2019 a contingency planner who will support the team in the field and in coordination to work on the definition of the most likely scenarios in order to anticipate the potential impacts on the projects and prepare the implementation of the contingency measures.
