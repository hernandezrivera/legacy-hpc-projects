Project Summary

In March 2019, Cyclone Idai tore through eastern Zimbabwe, destroying infrastructure, homes, schools, and clinics, displacing families and creating internal stress in communities. This afflicted an already vulnerable population due to food insecurity, poverty caused by an unstable macro economy, and drought. Eight months later, storms and strong winds marking the beginning of the rainy season caused further destruction, distress, and trauma in these already suffering communities. There is a huge remaining need for safe shelter for those internally displaced and those living in unsafe shelters while enduring another rainy season.
CRS has the history and experience of providing safe shelters around the world during and after emergencies. Immediately after Cyclone Idai hit Zimbabwe, CRS implemented a transitional shelter and latrine construction project to help those most affected in one of the poorest districts hit by the cyclone. However, there is still a great need there and in adjacent districts. CRS proposes a year-long transitional shelter project targeting the most vulnerable of the cyclone and flood-affected population in eastern Zimbabwe to aid and empower those families to regain control and normalcy in their lives. CRS has already established positive stakeholder relationships from national down to village level in previous projects and worked heavily with the Provincial and District Civil Protection Unit and Rural District Council to identify the most affected areas, and ward councilors and village heads to identify the most vulnerable out of those affected. CRS always seeks to engage, empower, and strengthen leadership and accountability within government, specifically local, to be better prepared for future disasters. For this project, CRS would continue those relationships to establish a baseline database of affected individuals, which would consist of those whose homes were completely destroyed or damaged to a point where the family is living in fear of the house collapsing with no resources to rebuild. The transitional house design would be based on Sphere Standards and local requirements of households to ensure the design is both safe and desirable, and government, participants, and other partners in shelter would be consulted during the design process. Of these affected households, the vulnerable groups targeted by the project would be female or child-headed households, households with multiple children under five, elderly, chronically ill, and physically or mentally challenged persons. Participants, especially female participants, and local leadership would be consulted in project design and implementation. CRS would work closely with other partners in the area in order to establish positive collaboration in targeting, project management, and implementation.
