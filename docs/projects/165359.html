Project Summary

In 2020, Mercy Corps aims to strengthen our protection interventions to support of the overall protection cluster objectives towards the provision of quality specialized and integrated protection services with a focus on community-based approaches. In 2019, Mercy Corps supported community based child protection and Psychosocial Support programming in North Syria. In 2020, Mercy Corps will scale up the commitment to protection programming, to projects that support youth and livelihoods, social cohesion partners in NES and integrating Mercy Corps projects to support vulnerable persons. Mercy Corps has a three-pillar approach to our protection response:
1) Continuing child protection programming in Syria through direct programing and with partners in locations inaccessible to Mercy Corps, with a focus on youth/adolescents and livelihoods where possible across northern Syria.
2) Providing cash assistance to vulnerable individuals and households, in coordination with protection actors in NES. Mercy Corps cash assistance is aimed at addressing protection risks and outcomes. Cash will be provided to vulnerable households especially those at risk of child labor and early/forced marriage to be able to meet basic needs. Similarly, throughout our experience in North East Syria, due to inadequate humanitarian service providers, individual with protection needs often have to pay for available services from private providers. Where services are inadequate, Mercy Corps will maintain a Case Management fund to support emergency referrals such as payment of transportation and payment of service fees where applicable on a case-by-case basis.
3) Embedding protection mainstreaming across all our activities, including strengthening data collection and referral systems. Mercy Corps will ensure training of staff and partners on protection mainstreaming principles and approaches, and ensure safe referrals among sectors. Adequate protection mainstreaming will not only contribute to improvement in the quality of programming but will as well ensure our program activities are safe and do not cause further harm and empower communities. Our community accountability feedback and response mechanism will be fully rolled out in all project communities in order to ensure proper accountability to the communities in which we work.
The protection response supported by Mercy Corps is in line with the protection needs overview (including child protection) and protection severity ranking, with partners operating in locations ranked severity 5/6 in the HNO severity scale and in coordination with the child protection AoR.

4 - Needs

The protracted crisis in Syria has caused severe disruption in the formal and informal protection services provided to children causing many of them to become increasingly more vulnerable. It is estimated that 5.7 million children are in need of humanitarian assistance and protection needs across Syria. Children and adolescents have bear the burden of the crisis with grave violations perpetrated against children and adolescents by all sides of the conflict. Child protection concerns that have been identified are related to documentation, child labour, early marriage and psychosocial stress. The number of children with disabilities has also increased with limited access to services they require. Grave child rights violations remain a critical concern with countless children killed and injured through persistent use of explosive weapons in civilian areas, recruitment and use of children by all parties to the conflict, torture, detention, abduction, sexual violence, attacks on schools and hospitals and denial of humanitarian access What Mercy Corps staff and partners have witnessed is the deepening of negative coping mechanisms and the need to orient their programming to individuals and households that have traditionally not beneﬁted as much from the response such as adolescents, youth and persons living with disabilities. In NES, repeated assessments conducted by different humanitarian actors finding continues to indicate that children drop out of schools to work, children ﬁnd themselves being married early, and struggle to access health assistance and legal remedy. The escalation of violence in the last quarter of 2019 exacerbated existing protection risks and concerns faced by women, girls, men and boys. Mercy Corps’ November 2019 RNA, conducted in Al-Hasakeh, Quamishli, Al-Malikeyyeh, Ras Al Ain, and Ar-Raqqa sub-districts reveals that of the displaced individuals, 18% are under the age of five, 4% are new-borns, 6% are women who are pregnant or breastfeeding,.

5 - Implementation of activities

Mercy Corps will take a multi-sector approach to protection, and participate in referral pathway and case management systems. Specialized Child Protection services will be targeted at children and adolescents including engagement of caregivers to support the protection of children and adolescents. Child Protection activities that will be implemented by Mercy Corps include case management and referrals to other specialized services especially Mental Health and Psychosocial support. Mercy Corps activities to enhance the protective environment are implemented using community based approach. Community based protection systems and structures will be strengthened through outreach such as social mobilization and community awareness, community risk mapping and risk mitigation and training for local structures such as child protection committees, youth groups, women groups, teachers as well as health care workers to be able to support children and adolescents especially using local resources. Support safe spaces of adolescent and youth In targeted communities of the , Mercy Corps, together with implementing partners, will form local Safe Spaces to support adolescents, youth, and women. Adolescents and youth are better protected, less vulnerable and have a decreased use of negative coping mechanisms thanks to increased engagement within safe and cohesive social networks, access to local economic opportunities, and active participation in decision making. Mercy Corps will work with local partners to implement child protection activities such as awareness raising, PSS and referrals in locations in accessible to Mercy Corps in order to increase our reach. Local partners will benefit from capacity building initiatives including training and mentoring programs while at the same time linking them with the overall coordination mechanism through the cluster. Mercy Corps will conduct protection monitoring for programming and advocacy purpose and train actors on protection principle

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps (MC) recognized the risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality similarly and risk of stigma for boys and girls receiving specialized services if staff are not adequately trained. MC will ensure to build the capacity of team members that will be directly engaged in the provision of specialized services. An initial case management training is will be provided to managers and training on coaching and supervision for supervisors. MC will adopt supervision tools from the global child protection case management task force ensures all trainings provided are in line with CP minimum standards. MC will directly implement case Management. MC recognized that there is a high risk of community tension arising from perceived inequality, discrimination, favoritism including over selection of locations for community services like safe space and risk for staff and partners. This has high impact on programs and beneficiaries. Therefore, MC will liaise with other actors to harmonize vulnerability selection criteria. MC does not use civil documentation as a requirement for service delivery rather vulnerability selection criteria form the basis for assistance. MC has adopted guidelines and checklist for setting up safe spaces that are in line with CP standardsMC activities include conducting assessment and protection monitoring as such, there is a high risk of receiving distorted information from communities due to their perception of humanitarian services and expectations of the respondents. To mitigate this risk, MC will ensure to recruit and provide adequate training to assessment teams and will use a range of assessment methodology that will allow for triangulation of information from assessmentMC will adopt the protection sector risk analysis for the project implementation.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Local authorities restrict activities of partners due to lack of support for protection interventions. Where programming is sensitive to authorities but a priority for local communities, Mercy Corps will endeavor to support partners in their intervention methodology and engagement with local actors to increase opportunities for operating. Persons without documents refused assistance: As part of a protection-mainstreamed intervention, Mercy Corps does not require beneﬁciaries of protection or non-protection projects to possess national ID documents. In line with protection cluster guidelines, information can be triangulated using local veriﬁcation processes. Increased insecurity and lack of access to communities could contribute to decline in activities.Mercy Corps PaQ team will lead the project monitoring and evaluation effort in close collaboration with the Project Manager, Officers, partners and Protection and Gender technical Advisor:Collection and analysis of SADD: Mercy Corps has an established system and approach to Monitoring and Evaluation. For all activities, Mercy Corps will collect analyze and use sex, age and diversity data including information about persons with disabilities. This data analysis will be used for program decision. For both specialized services and community based PSS, Mercy Corps will conduct periodic monitoring using safe different tools including safe space registration and attendance register as well as safe space monitoring tool measuring quality standards through the completion of a check list. Baseline/Endline data will be collected on the project outcome and indicators to assess the success of the project. Deployment of CPIMS: Mercy Corps will deploy the Child Protection Information Management System (CPIMS) as a key action for monitoring of and evaluation of specialized services especially case management. Mercy Corps will collect case studies/success stories from the beneficiary population to use in quarterly reviews.
