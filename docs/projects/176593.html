1 - Needs

Humanitarian needs in Rubkona and Panyijar counties are mainly driven by the impacts of years of conflict and exacerbated by the impact of climate change. Spikes in violence as a result of cattle rustling and revenge attacks have resulted in displacements and instilled fear among communities. COVID-19 has had a devastating and multi-faceted socioeconomic impact on people, including severe economic contractions, spikes in prices of basic commodities, loss of livelihoods (particularly in Rubkona county and urban areas of Panyijar), increased protection risks, and disrupted access to basic services. Unity state has continued to reel from the impacts of flooding which began in June 2020 and displaced over 7,146 households. Overall displacement due to flooding in Unity state by December was estimated at over 53,000 people. Physical access to the hardest-hit payams remained challenging, leaving market areas, livestock grazing areas, and crop gardens submerged and rerouting fish habitats, thus deepening people’s vulnerabilities and increasing displacements. This in turn served to increase competition for scarce resources, with many individuals resorting to negative coping mechanisms, including gathering wild fruits, eating roots and wild leaves, conducting forced child and early marriages. The conditions also served to further exacerbate domestic violence in homes.From October to November 2020, Unity state was in (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity, of which 500,000 people were in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity, and 204,000 people were in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity. The key drivers of food insecurity included COVID-19 related loss of employment and income, devaluation of South Sudanese Pound, flooding that destroyed crops and affected livestock, pockets of insecurity, high food prices, and markets that are not functioning optimally. This however is expected to improve in Rubkona (IPC3), except for Panyijar which will remain in IPC 4 . The Humanitarian needs overview South Sudan, estimates that a total of 93,700 persons from Panyijar and another 200,000 from Rubkona will be in need of assistance as per the IPC classifications due to shocks faced in 2020.

2 - Project activities

• Conduct monthly market monitoring on price fluctuations, value chains and finance in relation to immediate, short term and long-term impacts of COVID-19 shock. As part of Mercy Corps’ ongoing effort to understand the impact of COVID-19, the team will monitor monthly market fluctuations. Given the widespread closure of local markets and the potential severity the pandemic may have upon livelihoods and food security, market monitoring will yield critical information to ensure the programme is adapting to the needs of the target communities and not exacerbating challenges. • Carry out Covid-19 messages provided through mass media, posters and traditional media as culturally appropriate, simple and easy to understand, consistent while still providing the relevant information for informed decision making by those most affected. This will be done in collaboration with the RCC. Mercy Corps will also provide facilities and equipment needed to protect participants engaged in program activities with protective equipment to mitigate spread of covid-19. •Provide training to 600 Lead Farmers as part of strengthening community agricultural extension service provision on climate-sensitive agricultural practices and improved production techniques using Mercy Corps approach on small holder farmer resilience design approach that will be cascade down to 4,000 other smallholders’ households farmer by the lead farmers in both Panyijar and Rubkona counties. •Mobilize, identify, select youth and women for IGA training through the local leaders and the existing program management committees and conducted vocational and IGA skills trainings at the established youth centers in both Panyijar and Rubkona. Provide the 300 women and 300 youth graduate with business startup kits and up grants. •Provision of cash for work grants to 800 most vulnerable households with labor ability (of which at least 50% are female headed) in Unity State to respond to flooding and facilitate creation and rehabilitation of community assets to mitigate against. • Distribute high value and assorted vegetable seeds (OPVs) such as Kalez, okra, Jews melon, spinach, pumpkin, amaranths, Eggplant, cabbage, onions, cowpeas and tomatoes) to 4,000 most vulnerable targeted households and additional 1,200 households with staple crop inputs including; both yellow and longe 5 maize seeds and provide with tools (hoes or maloda and a watering can) for both vegetable and staple crop producers. • Provide training on fish production, handling, processing and marketing to 1,200 fisher folk households in both Panyijar and Rubkona. Further provide participants with fishing kits including materials for canoe making and fish preservation materials as source of income diversification. • Conducting of Post Distribution Monitoring to assess the impact of the inputs and cash transfers conducted in the program implementation to provide lesson learnt.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Underpinned by Mercy Corps’ internal Safeguarding policies and Community Accountability and Reporting Mechanism (CARM), this project will fall under the oversight and supports which cover all programming and community-based activities. Specifically, the zero tolerance policy states: treating beneficiaries with dignity and compassionately is a matter of priority, no act or behavior exploitation and abuse shall be condoned. A key component of this project will be to highlight referral services and pathways, including the capacity development of formal, semi-formal, and non-formal teaching and learning institutions and spaces, as well as developing the capacity within the individual roles of teachers and other school personnel. This project will work in collaboration, consultation, and coordination with other partners in the area to consolidate workable and effective referral pathways for the survivors to access services without hindrance, as they are needed.MC reinforced its global Integrity Hotline and response to complaints. MC has a dedicated, independent Ethics Team staffed by trained, professional investigators to monitor the hotline and oversee investigations. An in-house team works to identify options for stronger processes and procedures for vetting new team members such as background-checking mechanisms, safeguarding-screening questions etc. Another team works to find legal ways to ensure anyone terminated for sexual misconduct at MC cannot find work in the NGO sector again. On the ground, MC will ensure that its field staff will be specifically trained on PSEA. Messages on PSEA will be posted in offices and public spaces to ensure that communities receive the information. Information on PSEA will also be shared and discussed during meetings with beneficiaries. The messages shall be in local language as much as possible.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will work with community and other partners on ground and use various media to ensure that community members are aware of Mercy Corps’ project activities and those feedback channels available. Sensitization meetings will be done with different groups to ensure information reaches all communities in the project target areas. Community engagement as a component of Monitoring, Evaluating, and Learning (MEL) includes measures such as interviews, focus group discussions, and post-distribution monitoring surveys to track efficacy of activities and interventions. Furthermore, participatory meetings are continuously featured during implementation of project activities and will also be use as monitoring and evaluation mechanisms.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Accountability to the affected populations is and will be ensured in various ways:1. The affected populations have been involved during the project design phase in terms of identifying needs and activities to conduct. During the implementation, they belong to the main actors to organize and further design activities. 2. MC uses feedback and complaints mechanisms to adapt its response accordingly to ensure that the needs of the most vulnerable are met and their risks are minimized. That is, MC uses the Community Accountability Response Mechanism (CARM), utilized across MC programming globally, to report achievements and problems, involve the community in problem solving and ensure feedback loops reap the appropriate response through a meaningful and ethical accountability structure and also includes a community complaints mechanism. CARM works through trained focal point staff and is embedded into routine program monitoring.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Involvement of all stakeholders, especially those who are most vulnerable, will enable the definite achievement of goals in a timely manner. Community engagement meetings will draw upon participants from all groups of beneficiaries, regardless of age, ability, gender, and position in the community and women groups. Ensuring that a diverse range of perspectives, lived experiences, and voices are represented in these conversations, prioritizing all types of community members will continue be the prerequisite of accomplishing Mercy Corps’ project goals and aims. Messages will be tailored to suit age- and gender-specific groups. Information, education and communication materials will be in local languages (e.g. Nuer) for Rubkona and Panyijiar counties.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

Panyijiar and Rubkona counties have enjoyed relative calm, yet some risks still remain, particularly during specific times and in specific locations. Protection cluster partners and advisory groups will be consulted in the lead up to registrations and distributions to ensure that recipients of material goods and non-food items—children (both boys and girls), parents and caregivers, and people living with disabilities—can receive education-related goods and services and support with safety and dignity. Mitigation measures and risk assessments will continue to be a component of distribution and registration activities conducted by Mercy Corps and partners. Prioritizing participant perspectives, including members of marginalized communities and minority groups, this project will have an inclusive implementation of all activities, comprised of all stakeholders’ participation: regardless of sex, race, religion, language (mother tongue), and ability by a way of conducting community engagement meetings for collective decision making, school meetings, and other wide ranging consultation mechanisms. Embodied in this project is the issue of equity and equality as stipulated in the SDG4. Women, girls, and youth have been and will continue to be highly involved in consultations and decision-making processes. Multiple, simultaneous community-based approaches will be leveraged to break down socio- and cultural-normative practices which exclude or otherwise limit the participation of women and girls. These approaches will seek to address multiple community perspectives, especially those of women and girls, and include those of power-holders, to ensure safe and sustainable progress supported by the community.
