Project Summary

The project has identiﬁed signiﬁcant need for both IDPs and host families who have lost productive agriculture due to ongoing conﬂict and displacement, this led to reduced purchasing power and income generation, which resulted ﬁnally in hindering food security While the need for ongoing emergency food aid to vulnerable populations in Syria is clear, complimentary food security and livelihoods activities that enhance early recovery are also needed to assist these families in building resiliency in the face of ongoing conflict.
The project aims to strengthen vulnerable women and youth’ livelihoods, provide them with hope for the future, reduce their negative coping mechanisms to ensure they are better able to play active role in rebuilding their communities.
The project component Improves local infrastructure relevant for agriculture and, more broadly, the livelihoods sector, the Activities include the provision of short-term labour opportunities. Temporary labour opportunities will involve the completion of essential works, both those prioritized by the local council and benefitting the wider community in the target locations, particularly in relation to agricultural infrastructure. roads Which lead to farms from the villages, especially the mud is an obstacle for farmers to access only their farms. 4 - Needs

Due to the latest escalation in northwestern of Syria, especially in Hama and Idlib governorates. Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) which takes into account health, education and basic needs related indicators showed that the national poverty rate in Syria is at 38.9 percent. In light of the severe impact of the crisis, unemployment has risen to 55 percent and purchasing power has declined to 1/10 of precrisis level (if compared to the income in 2010). A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 percent of an unskilled laborer’s monthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary thus demonstrating the existence of “working poor” in Syria.The inability to have full access to the main markets in this area because of the different obstacles such as road failures in 68% of covered communities. In 24% of the covered villages, residents rely on credit in order to purchase or borrow items they lack. The population depends in 49% of the covered villages on gas as a source of cooking fuel, while the most coping strategies used in this.In project areas district, there is an absence of full access to main markets and only partial access exists. This absence is related to obstacles such as road closures and clashes took place last year. This report exhibits that 68% of the villages in project areas endure the above-mentioned difficulties. However, difficulties in accessing markets as a result of restrictions on the movement of women and people with special needs showed up in 32% of the covered villages in project district.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

RF during implementing the activities of the project may face these risks according to the project's activities. Protection risk 1: Community tension arising from the beneﬁciary selection. Likelihood: high / Impact: High. Mitigation: RF will priorities the most vulnerable female-headed or males headed with PSNs HHs, considering the children situations and diﬃculties within these HHs (priorities to HHs including children with disabilities or chronic diseases), to receive services during this project. RF has partnered with local leaders (to make them aware of the whole project’s activities and interventions) in designing the project to minimize harm and support local capacities to build peace. Protection risk 2: Risk of harassment of women and girls by the humanitarian workers. Likelihood: medium, Impact: high. Mitigation: establishing a conﬁdential complaint mechanism and enhancing accountability mechanisms besides training the staff to deal with beneﬁciaries in addition to providing workers with training on humanitarian standards and signing code of conduct.
