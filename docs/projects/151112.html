Basic Info

Project Name

Assisting conflict affected boy, girls and their families including parents and elderly, males and females, to increase their resilience and reduce protection risks in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts (both GCA and NGCA) with a special focus on the contact line

Start Date

01/01/2019

End Date

31/12/2019

Project Summary

The objective of the project is to increase resilience and reduce vulnerability of the conflict-affected boys, girls and their families, parents and elderly, males and females, through life-saving direct support/assistance, MHPSS activities, informal education and recreational activities by strengthening protective environment and improving access to child protection services.

Needs

The number of people affected has not changed significantly since the beginning of the crisis in Ukraine. As of today, an estimated 4.4 million people are affected, and 3.4 million people need humanitarian assistance. The most vulnerable groups in need of humanitarian assistance are the elderly, single-headed households with low income, people with disabilities and children.The conflict in eastern Ukraine continues to undermine the psychological well-being of children. The negative consequences of the conflict continue to impact the children, educators and the whole education system, especially in the areas along the “contact line”. For over four years, prolonged fear, conflict-related stress and trauma follow the children and teachers day and night. The situation is particularly severe for the 242,000 children and teachers, who live within the 20-km of the “contact line”, as shelling and extreme levels of mine-contamination pose constant risk to their safety and well-being. At the same time, weakened family and community support mechanisms as a result of conflict and displacement put children at heightened risk of neglect and domestic violence.The conflict also creates long-term financial burdens on a state that struggles to pay for critical social policies and increases dissatisfaction among displacement-affected communities, as well as for those living in areas beyond Government control. The ‘contact line’ has become a de facto border. In areas along the ‘contact line’, the protection concerns and humanitarian needs are more acute.Child protection remains a major concern in the conflict area. Children are particularly vulnerable since their protective environment is weakened. Family separation and extreme stress on their caregivers as well as breakdown of the social fabric in general are of concern, especially for those children with disabilities or living in institutions. Men and boys are at risk of pressure to join armed groups and there have been reports of recruitment efforts in educational institutions as well as reports of military use of education facilities.The conflict continues to threaten the well-being of children throughout the region. Some of the most vulnerable of boys and girls are those living in proximity to the contact line. UNICEF estimates that 200,000 children live within 15 kilometers of each side of the contact line of which about 2 per cent are regularly forced to take refuge in improvised bomb shelters. Children are exposed to the risk of explosive remnants of war and landmines, and girls are particularly at risk of different forms of gender-based violence. Over 55,000 children who live along the “contact line”, need access to child-friendly spaces, psychosocial support and mine risk education (According to UNICEF data)Psychosocial issues have been reported amongst all age groups, including boys and girls, the elderly, and working age men and women. Physiological signs of distress, such as difficulty sleeping, and emotional difficulties, including grief, fear and crying for no reason, were also reported in a recent Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) assessment.On-going fighting and economic vulnerability exposes children to new hazards, exacerbates existing problems and erodes pre-conflict protection systems. Major issues regarding child protection along the contact line include children living in consistently shelled areas, families forced to spend considerable time in makeshift bomb shelters, children making dangerous crossings of the contact line to get to school, mines and explosive remnants of war, psychological distress, military presence in schools, gender-based violence, sexual exploitation and neglect (The Children of the Contact Line in East Ukraine: An Assessment of the Situation of Children and Their Families Living in Government-Controlled Areas along the Contact Line in the East Ukraine Conflict Zone by UNICEF, 2017).

Activities & outcomes

Output 1. Delivering immediate, life-saving humanitarian assistance along the contact line of GCA and NGCA Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. Boys and girls of different age groups, are provided with psychosocial services through structured activities at community centers and child spaces established in education institutions, libraries and culture institutions.1) Strengthen 3 existing community centers with 12 mobile units along the contact line. Identify locations and establish 4 new community centers. Identify locations along the contact line and establish 20 child spaces and 10 mobile libraries units.2) Strengthen 2 existing Case Management unit of oblast level and identify locations and establish 5 new CMUs of regional level to provide individual support for the most vulnerable categories of children.3) Continue with a comprehensive program of psychosocial support including SCI signature programs HEART, Child to child, Life Skills and Resiliency and MRE for children aged 6-18 y.o. Conduct child protection and gender sensitivity public awareness campaign.4) Reinforcing the existing referral and case management systems at the local level through capacity building of duty bearers and protection actors with strong linkages to social services in GCA and NGCA.5) Provide individual support for children and youth, boys and girls, who find themselves in a difficult life situation with the case management approach6) Establish an emergency fund to provide support for the most vulnerable children and meet their individual needs.7) With Child-to Child approach increase child participation at different levels of local communities to identify and address specific CP risks in the conflict-affected areas, to increase their resilience and sustainability (child-led initiatives are supported with cash/in-kind support).Output 2. Adults, parents and caregivers, elderly males and females, of conflict affected vulnerable boys and girls, are better equipped to care for, protect children in distress, need of psychosocial support.1) Continue with positive parenting program and positive discipline sessions to increase their knowledge, form practical skills how to support children experienced crisis situations and traumatic events.2) Continue with SCI signature programs like Life Skills and Resilience and Journey of Hope programs for parents and caregivers. The activities are planned to meet the urgent need of psycho-social support for youth and their families. 3) Hold community meetings and events to mobilize local communities and resources, make families more united and supportive; inform about existing local mechanisms, available services or referral pathways.Output 3. Local professionals, males and females, staff of centers of social services, school/after school institutions, public libraries, culture institutions, volunteers, local authorities representatives, local NGOs are trained on how to provide better support to children including identifying signs of distress and providing support personally or through referrals, case management approach, strengthening referral system and coordination at all levels, regular updating of service mapping.1) Hold 10 learning events on Humanitarian principles, CPMS and CPiE, MHPSS recommendations, PFA, Case Management approach, Child Participation, etc.2) Conduct Life skills and Resiliency for children and youth trainings; HEART (art therapy) and Child to Child direct trainings with further mentorship and supervision sessions.3) Ensure the mentioned programs are further implemented through community centers and child spaces to meet PSS needs of children and their parents, caregivers.4) Formalize referral system at local levels and ensure proper referral mechanisms in place.Output 4. Identify key implementing partners from local organizations and increase their and other local NGOs capacities through tailor made and general capacity building program
