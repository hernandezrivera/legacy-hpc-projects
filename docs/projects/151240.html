Project Summary

UNICEF proposes admissions into severe acute malnutrition therapeutic treatment as an entry point to target beneficiaries for emergency safety net cash assistance. This design aims at channeling assistance to the most vulnerable families using severe acute malnutrition (SAM) as a proxy for extreme vulnerability; and thus to prevent children from relapsing into acute malnutrition by addressing the multifaceted causes of malnutrition (including extreme poverty, lack of access to basic services, disease outbreaks, poor hygiene) and aiming to enhance impact of nutrition therapeutic interventions. Cash assistance could also help reduce related negative coping mechanisms such as sharing nutrition therapeutic commodities at household level. The program will also aim to build on current cash plus interventions including positive messaging to change behaviours and social norms that may be contributing to vulnerability, extend community engagement, accountability and feedback systems, integration of WASH services through hygiene kit distributions at discharge from SAM treatment centres, strong referral mechanisms to health program and deployment of a robust research and monitoring element to expand context speci􀂡c evidence base on cash as a modality to support nutritional outcomes. Geographical targeting will start with the areas where UNICEF is already providing cash assistance in Bay and Bakool and aims to be extended to rural areas in those districts, which may encourage return in safe areas and initial contributions to rebuilding livelihoods and resilience to shocks and crises. Additionally, UNICEF aims at piloting this approach in Gedo and Banadir where a resilience programme in partnership with WFP, and funded by the German cooperation, is being implemented. The selected household upon diagnosis of SAM, the mother or the regular care giver will be enrolled to the UNICEF supported therapeutic feeding centres. Upon admissions mother or the regular care giver will received monthly cash assistance. The beneficiaries will be register in SCOPE allowing them to track beneficiary across the different type of assistance received. This would also enhance coordination efforts with other partner to avoid duplication of assistance being received (medical treatment vs cash transfers).

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Despite recent improvements in food security across the country, this has not directly translated to improvements in nutrition status in the country. The prevalence of acute malnutrition among IDPs and vulnerable host communities across the country remains critically high. The results from the Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) post-Gu assessments estimated nutritional situation in August 2018 revealed Critical Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) rates above 15 percent in multiple IDP locations including Baidoa, Doolow and Banadir. Hence, UNICEF sees a critical opportunity to design nutrition focused cash interventions in Somalia, leveraging a growing global evidence base on the positive impact cash assistance can have in contributing to address root causes of acute malnutrition and enhance outcomes of therapeutic treatment programs. UNICEF will leverage its partnership with WFP and use SCOPE to deliver cash assistance to mothers/caretakers of children diagnosed with SAM, primarily in areas hosting IDPs. Beneficiaries increasingly request UNICEF to deliver assistance via mobile money instead of cash-in hand through local banks. These requests are backed by assessed transportation cost and security risk beneficiaries incur for every trip they must make to distribution points as well as operational challenges linked to POS recognition of their fingerprints. UNICEF is committed to act upon beneficiaries’ preferences and ensure their feedback is heard. While mobile money will start to be deployed for all those with an existing account the current cash in hand delivery system will remain available until the entire beneficiary caseload will transition to mobile money. These delivery and targeting mechanisms aim to comprehensively address possible exclusion based on displacement status and ensure that the most vulnerable are being reached with critical assistance.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

SAM is a medical condition requiring medical treatment. As such it will be treated by nutrition therapeutic feeding whenever diagnosed. Additionally, UNICEF will ensure community engagement and sensitization around optimal maternal and child feeding and care practices, plus messages that change social norms related to other root causes of malnutrition (health, food security, WASH, for example) through the delivery of an integrated package of behaviour change communication and counselling matched with cash transfer assistance. The selection of targeted households will be categorical upon diagnosis of SAM. Mothers/caretakers will be enrolled by implementing partners across the network of UNICEF supported therapeutic feeding centres capable of diagnosing and treating SAM. Upon admission for SAM treatment, mothers/caretakers will start receiving a monthly cash transfer. Implementing partners at the therapeutic feeding centres will register the beneficiary in SCOPE allowing them to track the beneficiary across the different type of assistance being received (medical treatment vs cash transfers). The focus on targeting children U5 and registering mother, primarily, to received assistance aims to ensure minimal exclusion based on gender and age.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

UNICEF nutrition program works with over 50 NGOs, with the majority of them being local organizations with extensive understanding of local context in hard-to-reach areas. In the event of escalating armed conflict, violence and displacement, the project will scale up to reach the affected target groups, while maintaining critical mitigation measures to ensure beneficiary and implementing staff safety and security.
