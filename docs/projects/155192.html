Objectives

Needs

1. Camp 13 has a population of 40976(UNHCR September 2018 Fact sheet).Gender and age dissageration is as follows;Adult female,25%( 10343) and adult male 20% (8300). Boys below 17years are 18% (7015) and girls 18% (7182) The rest,19% (7636) are children below five years. In camp 19,there are 20251 individuals;31% (5046) are adult female, 24% (3947) are adult male.Below 17years;females are 22% (3569) while males are 22% (3613), The rest ,1% (4076) are children below 5 years. Based on this gender and age dynamic,there is need for tailored approaches with GBV preventon and response interventions that are responsive to specific needs of different age and gender groups,have wider reach and take into account the vulnerabilities of women and girls. 2. In camp 13, Case management coverage is 25% provided through one WSS in Block B6 operated by Mukti and none in camp 19 (Acccording to service map). This means in camp 13, many women and girls from far to reach blocks C,D and E are unlikely to access the needed support .In camp 19,women and girls have to travel long distances to access services from camps 20 or 13. This may discourage reporting GBV related incidences to access care and support and further limit women and girls participation in empowerment and other psychosocial support activities.World Vision's interventions will cover these blocks not yet reached plus camp 19 3. Information provision and dissemination that are tailored to the specific age and gender groups described above needs improvements and so do referral systems, including systems to connect survivors and other vulnerable women and girls to appropriate services in the camp 4. Inadequate understanding by other World Vision sector personnel and community volunteers on how to identify GBV risks,appropriate mitigation measures and how to conduct safe and ethical referrals (World Vision WASH,Shelter/NFI,Nutrition,FSL and CP) 5. Pre-existing gender inequalities coupled with limited access to livelihood opportunities have increased women and girls exposure to the various forms of GBV in the camp 6.There is inadequate engagement of men and boys in both primary and secondary GBV prevention interventions

Activities or outputs

1. Provide survivor-centred case management services through one Women Safe Space(WSS) and 6 existing World Visions Women and Young Child Spaces(WAYC) for intergrated GBV and nutrition in camp 13(blocks C,D,E) and 19 2. Support women and girls led structured empowerment programming at the women safe spaces 3. Facilitate psychosocial support activities with women and girls in identified safe spaces in the camp 4. Conduct structured community dialogues with men and boys using World Vision's Social Convention Theory addressing primary causes of GBV. 5. Conduct community awareness sessions through neighbourhood forums targeting households to address secondary causes of GBV and disseminate information on where and how to safely report GBV incidences to access care and support 6. Conduct regular multisectoral safety audits and develop reccomendations to inform advocacy for improved safety of women and girls 7. Carry out two advocacy campaigns to promote women's rights through the international advocacy events (International Women's Day and 16 days of Activism) 8. Capacity building for 300 staff and community volunteers of other World Vision sectors(WASH,Site development,Nutrition&CP) to implement GBV risk mitigation actions,mainstream GBV key messages and provide safe and ethical referrals.

Key project/performance indicators

1. 9420 women,7793 men,6308 girls and 6378 boys benefitted from awareness activities that include info on general GBV concept, the locations for services and the benefits of service for survivors (by identity,age and sex) - 298992. 300 staff of service providers and other humanitarian actors trained on GBV (GBV minimum standards, GBV mainstreaming, case management, GBVIMS, CMR, GBV IASC Guidelines, etc) - 3003. 9000 women and 6000 girls benefitting from structured PSS services that meet minimum standards (by identity, age, sex) - 150004. 350 women and 50 adolescent mothers benefiting from structured skills and empowerment programming (by age) - 4005. % of survivors supported with needed services (including PSS, health, legal, empowerment programs, etc)
