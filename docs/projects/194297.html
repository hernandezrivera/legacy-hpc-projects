Project Summary

Given the high level of food insecurity, Concern will continue to provide FSL support to most vulnerable households in Aweil North, Aweil West and Aweil South Counties in Northern Bahr el Ghazal (NBeG) state while the nutrition sensitive agriculture support is integrated across all nutrition centres in Juba IDP camp in Central Equatoria and Guit County in Unity State. Concern will provide access to food, agriculture inputs and improve agriculture production to prevent famine and support vegetable production to improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations. Concern will engage with the County Agriculture Department (CAD), extension workers and lead farmers for training on agriculture extension services and Climate Smart Agriculture practices, informal seeds systems, pest and post-harvest management. Concern will also support vegetable production across all targeted location to increase HHs dietary diversity and contributing to improved complementary feeding practices among children 6-23 months of age. Considering asset depletion, crop losses due to flooding, inflation and limited access to livelihood, hunger is expected to deepen due mainly to depleted food stocks and high food prices therefore Concern in partnership with WFP in NBeG continue to provide emergency food assistance through General Food Distribution, School Feeding Program and Cash/Food for Asset program.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan has continued to deteriorate with the number of people in need is growing due to multitude of crises, including the socio-economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, continual climate shocks, dwindling economic conditions, and most recently with extreme flooding and spiralling costs of food and fuel due to Ukraine crisis have created a renewed famine risk. The primary challenge driving needs is food insecurity or lack of food, which weakens people’s health and nutritional status and exposes them to greater protection risks. The latest Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) and Acute Malnutrition (AMN) analysis, released on November 3, 2022, shows about two-thirds of the South Sudanese population (7.8 million people) are likely to face acute food insecurity during the April-July 2023 lean season while 1.4 million children will be malnourished. In target locations, the IPC analysis (Apr-Jul ‘23) indicate an emergency level (IPC 4) of food insecurity in Aweil North, Aweil West, Aweil South and Guit counties while Juba IDP camp is classified in crisis level (IPC 3). Similarly, as per IPC (AMN) projection for March-June 2023; Aweil South, Aweil North, Aweil West and Guit Counties are classified in IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical). The subsistence agriculture remains the main source of food and income for the vast majority of the population, however; despite strong agriculture potential the cereal production remains low due to weak seed system, poor access to seeds, storage and improved agriculture practices. The protracted conflict and recurrent floods, which destroyed food stocks, livestock, and crops ahead of the main harvest season, have aggravated the food security situation increasing the risk of vulnerable communities slipping into IPC 5. The prices of most important food communities for poor rural household such as Maize and sorghum remained at exceptionally high levels. This is likely to further deteriorate due to the disruptions in imports, food production and the related surge in food prices induced by the current conflict in Ukraine as South Sudan relies on commercial imports of basic commodities including cereals and pulses from neighbouring countries. Concern FSL interventions will aim to meet immediate food needs of vulnerable food insecure households and build local coping mechanisms to food insecurity and adverse climatic conditions while at the same time support communities build communally owned assets/livelihood to earn income that will support them in supplementing diet and meet other basic household need.

2 - Project activities

CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3): In partnership with WFP, Concern will improve food security through increased food access and improve livelihoods through temporary employment and building of community assets. During the lean period, Concern will ensure food access through conditional cash transfers and food distributions to vulnerable populations. Temporary employment and asset creation will be provided through Cash for Assets (CFA) and Food for Asset (FFA) where vulnerable households will receive cash/food for construction on community-prioritised assets. The activities will promote community resilience through supporting community infrastructure, climate smart agriculture production and food production at local level. Similarly, Concern in partnership with WFP in NBeG continue to provide emergency food assistance through General Food Distribution (including more hybrid rations in kind plus cash component) and School Feeding Program. CO2: Enhance and sustain emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop livelihood inputs, fishing and livestock: The agriculture interventions aims to address immediate emergency needs, enhance recovery, and contribute to the goal of preventing widespread food insecurity among vulnerable populations. The smallholder farmers will be supported to improve their agricultural production through technical guidance and access to seeds and farm implements. Alongside agronomic trainings, the farmers will be supported with animal traction and training on Drought Animal Technology (DAT) use either ox-or donkey ploughs to improve tillage practices to enable and improve food production and crop yields to ensure enough seeds are produced and stored for subsequent planting seasons. To improve food and nutrition security of targeted HHs, Concern will support vegetable production through home gardening and vegetable production groups in all targeted counties. Mainly the nutrition beneficiaries, mother and father groups will be supported with vegetable seeds and tools to improve their access to nutritious food and increase their dietary diversity. In addition, Concern will target vulnerable fishing households in NBeG/Unity with fishing nets, hooks and fishing line. CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb & adapt to shocks & contextual changes; The beneficiaries will receive integrated assistance supported by multiple donor including flood resilience programme implemented by Concern in Aweil North/West. Concern will procure locally available seed through seed fairs and promote seed security & multiplication strengthening/ Support. The interventions will also support Training & capacity building; both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (farmer/agro-pastoral field schools, demonstration plots), income generation and support village saving and loan association (VSLA). Concern will continue to collaborate with County Agriculture Department (CAD) through supporting them and engaging Community-Based Agriculture Extension Workers (CBAEWs) from CAD to deliver services and agronomic trainings to improved community resilience and receive sustainable support. Concern will identify lead farmers in the community to support other farmers in their locality. Concern will train beneficiaries on the farmer group approach, establishment of demonstration plots, farmer-to-farmer learning, and on mass agricultural campaigns. Concern will conduct refresher trainings to CAD and CBAEWs. The training is aimed at reinforcing skills and knowledge so that government extension staff and extension workers can reach out to farmers with properly structured and quality agriculture extension messaging over a sustained period of time.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Concern understand different vulnerabilities within target communities that may exclude target beneficiaries in their access to feedback and complaint mechanism. Therefore, Concern will consult with the target community, the local chief and state authorities to understand the overall target population profile, access to communication channel and nature of vulnerabilities. While establishing the Feedback and Complaint mechanism, Concern will consider such vulnerabilities and access constrained therefore the target population will be consulted on their preferred mode of complaint/feedback channel. Different mode of feedback such as complaint boxes, phone numbers, email, verbal feedback/complaint during Concern monitoring visits, in-person to Concern office and Help desk at distribution points/beneficiaries registration points will be used to avoid exclusion of access as for as possible considering the operational challenges in target locations. Concern will use both pictorial, written and verbal channels in local languages to communicate the feedback mechanism to target beneficiaries. The individuals living with disabilities, illiterate, women and girls can also share their complaint/feedback through their local representative and relatives. Concern will maintain a help and feedback desk at distribution points. In addition, during monitoring and support visits FGDs will be conducted separately with women and men to seek feedback and provide opportunity to discuss any concern/issues or complaint.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Concern commitment to APP and Core Humanitarian Standard ensure participatory and inclusive approach. Prior to the initiating project activities and delivery of assistance, Concern will conduct an inception meeting with the target beneficiaries and stakeholders to inform them about the project scope, target criteria and their entitlements. Similarly, during the inception meetings and during regular consultations with the community, Concern will inform the beneficiaries about the feedback mechanism, their right to assistance and complaints. Depending on type of assistance, context and target population profile, Concern ensure that the banners with information on complaint channel and feedback mechanism are displayed broadly in the target areas and at the distribution points.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

Concern has a Code of Conduct and its associated policies including Program Participant Protection Policy (P4), Child Safeguarding, and Anti-Trafficking in-person Policy. Concern staff and any one engaged by Concern including consultants, contractors, partners and visitor are required to understand and comply with Concern safeguarding policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct and Associated Policy is consistent with the six core principles of IASC Task Force on PSEA in Humanitarian Crises. During project inception and regular consultations with the beneficiaries and relevant stake holders, Concern informs them about their right to assistance, entitlements, right to appropriate behavior including Concern’s commitment to zero tolerance on Sexual exploitation and abuse by humanitarian workers. The beneficiaries will be informed about the Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) to raise their voice in case of any issue pertaining to PSEA. The monitoring mechanism will ensure that the beneficiaries are aware about PSEA and how to complaint in case of any exploitation and abuse. In PoC sites Concern refers complaint to the camp manager and protection concerns to the Protection partner. In all locations, Concern has a Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) where beneficiaries can register complaints about gender related or protection concerns which are referred to Protection partners. All recruitments of staff and trainings ensure that there is equal access to women and men to ensure balanced representation. Monitoring will collect data on any information on safety and protection concerns and to improve future programme designs.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Concern engage with the target community and relevant state authorities during the needs assessment, project design and monitoring to seek their feedback and adapt project assistance/delivery accordingly. The broader consultations with the community during the assessment will inform about the project design, priority activities and modality of assistance. The project/villages committees including most vulnerable such as women, elderly, disable will be formed at inception stage to engage in project delivery and monitoring. The project/villages committees from target beneficiaries share feedback to adapt the assistance accordingly. The regular monitoring and quarterly reviews will collate the feedback, lessons and inform about necessary adaptation to project assistance. Similarly, the Complaint and Feedback Mechanism and regular consultation with beneficiaries during monitoring will provide opportunity to beneficiaries to share their feedback which will be considered in adapting the program accordingly. Concern conduct project end-line survey to review the progress on project outcome and seek feedback from future improvements. The lesson will be document and shared with cluster and other relevant stakeholders.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

During assessments, Concern will coordinate with Protection partners and identify safety, security and protection risks and findings will be used to adapt the programme. The interventions will prioritize gender and protection mainstreaming, and ensure inclusion of older people and PWDs based on our experience and learnings from previous programs. Concern will use the Gender-Age Marker toolkit as well as protection guidelines during the assessments, ongoing monitoring, and evaluations of projects. The project targeting criteria and entitlements will be shared with the community and Concern will ensure that the program priorities are always identified by the affected community, beneficiary selection criteria are always inclusive, and the community is always involved in project design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation. Prior to distributions, Concern will consult with communities to identify distribution sites to ensure they are in safe locations such as churches, schools and health facilities and close to beneficiaries to minimise travel times. Concern will conduct sensitisations on risk reduction measures before distributions such as travelling to sites in mixed groups and during daylight hours. Dates of distributions and fairs will be on a “need to know” basis to minimise information circulating. Help desks will be used to identify and refer protection cases to Protection partners and to adapt the programme to minimize further risks. Concern safeguarding policies require all staff and any one engaged in program to comply with code of conduct and reporting of breaches. The beneficiaries will be sensitised on CRM and their right to share feedback and complaint. Concern will ensure that the staff were trained on GBV, aware about referral pathways and sensitise the beneficiaries on reporting and use of GBV pathways.
