Project Summary

The proposed project will target 1750 households representing 10500 beneficiaries (2625 women, 3150 girls, 2835 boys, and 1890 men). The overall objective of the project is to improve access to food and protection of livelihood assets through 1) distribution of farm input to 550 farmers in Dolow and Belet Xaawo benefiting 3300 beneficiaries (594 men, 825 women 990 girls and 891 boys) including people with disabilities), to start their farming activities at the target locations, 2) conduct training to 200 farmers ( 110 women and 90 men) benefiting 1200 beneficiaries(300 women, 216 men, 360 girls and 324 boys) on best farming technologies, methodologies and animal husbandry and 3) Rehabilitate 24 Kms flood affected feeder roads between Belet Xaawa and Dolow Districts with Cash for Work( CFW) through voucher for 1000 skilled and unskilled workers ( 600 for Belet Xaawa and 400 Dolow), benefiting approximately 600 beneficiaries women men and children. Each skilled worker will receive USD $7 per day and each unskilled worker will receive $5 per day for three months, they will get the payments from the nearest money vendor to avoid risks of looting.
Agricultural input support shall includ seeds (25 kg of maize/sorghum, 10kg of beans and 5kg of sesame) and sets of farming tools, each set shall contain (1 rake, 1 hoe, 1 stainless pick and mattock, 1 machete and 1 shovel) to 550 farmers.
CoDHNet will maintain regular communication, and coordination mechanisms with all the project stakeholders including the community, local authority, regional food security cluster, cluster partners, and will submit monthly reports through 4W Matrix to FSC and OCHA will receive monthly financial and narrative reports for accountability purposes.

Project Activities

CoDHNet will organize inception meetings with the district local authorities and Community to inform about the project and gain endorsement and collaboration with them. CoDHNet will carry out the following activities 1) Conduct training to 200 farmers ( 110 women and 90 men) benefiting 1200 beneficiaries(300 women, 216 men, 360 girls and 324 boys) on best-farming technologies, methodologies and animal husbandry.2) Distribute farm input to 550 farmers in Dolow and Belet Xaawo benefiting 3300 beneficiaries ( 594 men, 825 women 990 girls and 891 boys) including people with disabilities), to prepare their farms and start farming activities. 3) Rehabilitate 24 Kms flood-affected feeder roads in Belet Xaawa and Dolow Districts with Cash for Work( CFW) through a voucher for 1000 skilled and unskilled workers ( 600 for Belet Xaawa and 400 Dolow),benefiting approximately 600 beneficiaries women men and children.CoDHNet will establish village seed distribution committees with the inclusion of women selected from respective villages to identify and register the beneficiaries. The selection criteria for the beneficiaries include women-headed households, access to farmland, most vulnerable/poor and IDPs. CoDHNet will also identify and select local agro-dealers at the target locations through a selection criteria, competitive and transparent process.

Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement Internal displacement is the main risk which has resulted in social exclusion, marginalisation and discrimination; the outcomes of the profiling of the IDP situation depict a grim picture of inequality between IDPs and other population segments living in similarly impoverished conditions. Lack of information and unawareness of rights renders IDPs and refugees vulnerable to exploitation and abuse and excludes them from participating in any decision-making processes even when affecting their own future. IDPs for example show little awareness of their rights against being forcibly evicted, their right to freedom of movement and derived from that their right to choose where to find a durable solution. This exclusion adds to their social marginalization and exclusion. the proposed actions Cash-based interventions will initially enable displacement- affected populations accessing livelihoods and protective services and as well o Rights awareness of IDPs will be enhanced enabling them to better participate in decision-making processes affecting their future, and the social cohesion among IDPs and the host communities is strengthened. project will enhance gender equality by ensuring equal participation of all gender including, women, men, boys and girls of all ages. Most of GBV cases and survivors in Somalia are women and girls, thus keeping this in mind, the project will ensure that all sectors of the community including minorities will get the services equally.

Please provide further explanation

The project locations can be easily accessed by the local and international organizations and no incidents were reported recently. CoDHNet has been operating in both project locations, Thus the relationship between CoDHNet and the community in the district and enjoys full access to both district. CoDHNet will undertake stakeholder engagement targeting beneficiaries in the target villages, their leadership, local authority and Ministry of health officials to provide an overview of the intended project including the objectives, key activities, indicator, and the target beneficiaries. Participatory monitoring and evaluation will be employed about frequency, keeping track/ assessing the effectiveness of the interventions to work within and where necessary adjusting the work plan to enhance efficiency and realization of project objectives. CoDHNet will use four-tier level monitoring approaches (i) reporting against agree work plan (ii) staff field visit, meetings, supervision and technical support, (iii) Baseline assessment and post-project assessment will be conducted to measure the impact of the intervention (iv) Community Feedback and Response Mechanism (CFRM) to check on satisfaction, curb aid diversion and re-evaluation and possible re-adjustments of the intervention. The PM develops a detailed monitoring plan and participatory monitoring tools to ensure all the stakeholders including the beneficiaries participate in M&E of the activities. Telephone numbers of beneficiaries, community elder and authorities will be collected to allow independent and remote monitoring to OCHA, FSC and CoDHNet top management. Monitoring reports of project activities accompanied by photographic evidence with GPS tags will be sent to the CoDHNet program director and OCHA for evidence. CoDHNet will develop elaborate beneficiary complaint and feedback tools, project beneficiaries and stakeholders will be given one day workshop on how to use the complaint/feedback system to enhance transparency and accountability. Monthly and quarterly progress reports will be shared. Also, CoDHNet will hire an independent post distribution assessment consultant and will organize joint monitoring missions with OCHA/FSC partners after the project is completed.
