Needs

Somalia is one of the countries with the highest prevalence of malnutrition in the world at 17.1% GAM amongst children under 5 years with 4.2% being severely malnourished. Mogadishu carries the heaviest burden of malnutrition in the country with 63% of the cases of children under 5 years being malnourished. In Wadajir, Bondere, Afgoye, Deynile and Lower Shabelle, approximately 19,253 children under 5 years are reported to be severely acutely malnourished (SAM). Afgoye town is the entry point for IDPs seeking aid within the Afgoye Corridor and Mogadishu. From new IDPs, Concern Worldwide assessments have estimated that less than 20% of children have access to routine immunization services and greater than 50% suffer from acute malnutrition thereby being the worst hit by disease outbreaks. It is reported that only 12% of mothers have had a tetanus vaccine and only 10% attended antenatal clinics. From Concern’s experience as implementing agency, which is also in line with the WHO Somalia Humanitarian response plan 2017, access to basic and lifesaving health care services still remains a challenge that needs to be prioritized. Health service coverage is considerably low and Acute Watery Diarrhoea/cholera, measles, malaria, and other communicable diseases outbreaks are constantly reported in many areas including in IDP settlements worst notably in the Mogadishu-Afgoye corridor where most of the IDPs settle. The GAM rate among Banadir IDPs is at 20.4%, (greater than the overall country GAM of 17.4%). The overall number of acutely malnourished is likely to remain substantially high, with over 300,000 children under the age of five acutely malnourished, including more than 50,000 children likely to be severely malnourished . Flooding associated with the Gu rains has led to an outbreak of disease and there has already been an upsurge in cases of AWD/Cholera and Malaria across Somalia and especially within IDP camps.

Activities

The project plans to continue provision of lifesaving integrated health services to affected communities using the approaches of supporting static non-functioning health facilities and hard to reach communities at eleven sites in Banadhir and Lower Shabele regionsConcern is proposing to support and increase access to maternal and child health services including immunizations, ANC, PNC and outpatient treatment for diseases like malaria, cholera, AWD and all antigens listed for Somalia in the EPI health services package from birth polio and BCG to Measles will be offered. CWW and Partner staff Health workers will be trained on provision of EPI vaccines as well as cold chain management. OPD consultation services will be offered to all members of the community in addition to PLWs and children under 18. This will target common illnesses and endemic conditions like malaria, ARIs, pneumonia, diarrhea, intestinal worms, skin conditions etc. The basic service provision will support communities through the prevention and treatment of malnutrition (SAM and MAM) also focusing on community health workers including IYCF messaging at both community and facility level for better detection of malnutrition and other illnesses and thus shock responsive early referral to relevant services at community and facility levels. The project will support the work of Community Heath workers in sensitizing and mobilizing communities in accessing health and nutrition services

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

Concern has worked in Somalia for years and is highly knowledgeable to the current environment. The project will support communities in areas not served by government structures. The project will adopt a system strengthening approach through the capacity development of health workers and the provision of training for Community Health Workers. The provision of essential health and nutrition services is pivotal in the creation of resilient communities. The project is community lead and this will facilitate a handover once the government has the necessary capacity to provide these essential services. Concern promotes active participation of beneficiaries and their representatives in all stages of the project life cycle, including activities as well as decision-making on interventions. In this project, prior to commencement of any activity, Concern will carry out mobilization campaigns in which necessary intervention information will be fully shared with the target communities, their views will be solicited and their active participation will be sought for the achievement of objectives. Through community engagement and mobilization, all prospective beneficiaries will be informed of their entitlements from the project.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

There is escalation of conflict in South-Central Somalia as a result of the Somali National Armed Forces (SNAF)/AMISOM and Somali Government offensive against Al-Shabaab. The biggest risk for the proposed intervention is that if this conflict gets prolonged Concern will see massive civilian displacement with resultant increasing demand for health and nutrition services. This project will provide essential services to all in need and will respond accordingly to any increasing in demand resulting from displacement

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Concern Somalia recognizes that the current humanitarian crisis in the country has different implications for Somali women, girls, boys and men. This project is specifically gender and age designed based on the provision of essential health and nutrition service to under fives and pregnant and lactating women. Therefore this project has being designed in a way that takes issues around gender into account. Furthermore the project will use female workers as key to mobilizing women and their to uptake the essential services provided

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

There is escalation of conflict in South-Central Somalia as a result of the Somali National Armed Forces (SNAF)/AMISOM and Somali Government offensive against Al-Shabaab. The biggest risk for the proposed intervention is that if this conflict gets prolonged Concern will see massive civilian displacement with resultant increased demand for health and nutrition services. The project will have an emergency response and preparedness plan. This will ensure continued delivery of services for the targeted population.
