Project Summary

After nine years of war in South Sudan, the socio-economic hardship continues to have a profound impact leading to increased food insecurity for 8.9 million people. Existing barriers exclude persons with disabilities, women, and vulnerable groups from social and economic activities at the community level, further perpetuating the cycle of poverty and disability. The project aims to strengthen socio-economic resilience and promote inclusion in disaster and conflict-affected communities in Juba, Yei, Bentiu, Pibor and Aweil South, with a focus on the most vulnerable groups. Through the
• capacity building of local partners,
• establishment of an inclusive and gender-sensitive network,
• enhanced psycho-social wellbeing and independence of persons with disabilities,
• Inclusive cash and voucher assistance
• individualized development plans
• inclusive income-generating activities for Persons with Disabilities will be enabled to improve their economic situation and resilience through the empowerment at individual level and active integration of community structures.

1 - Needs

South Sudan is a fragile state, wracked by conflict, political instability, and recurrent natural shocks such as drought and flooding. These factors have led to the widespread internal displacement of populations, hunger, and malnutrition, posing a major challenge to all levels of government and partners. For the last 3 years, these issues have been compounded by severe flooding and desert locust invasions, and by the outbreak of COVID-19, which has placed additional stress on health systems and livelihoods. In 2022, the humanitarian community in South Sudan estimates that more than two-thirds of South Sudan's population, 8.9 million people, are in need of humanitarian assistance, an increase of 600,000 since 2021. Out of these People in Need, 15% are estimated to be persons with disabilities (WHO global estimate). (See link below)(https://fscluster.org/sites/default/files/documents/ssd_hno_2022_26feb2022.pdf)As per the 2022 humanitarian needs overview and key findings, an estimated 8.3 million people, including refugees, are expected to experience severe food insecurity by the depth of the lean season (May-July 2022) as shocks appear to be intensifying. There are 13 counties with extreme levels of food insecurity in 2022, compared to 6 in 2021. In addition, an estimated 2 million people, including 1.3 million children under the age of 5, and 676,000 pregnant and lactating women, are expected to be acutely malnourished in 2022. In South Sudan, persons with disabilities especially women and their family members are facing significant obstacles in timely and meaningful access to food security and to securing an income to meet daily expenditure and reduce dependency on humanitarian aid, family and kinship structures for basic needs. The majority of people with disabilities of working age are unemployed and begging is often the main mean of survival, owing to limited or no access to education and skill training, coupled with misconceptions of disabling factors and structural inequalities they’re prevented from being equally competitive with their peers without disability on livelihood activities. Statistics or comprehensive information on the number of people with disabilities in South Sudan is lacking. However, recent surveys and estimates suggest that it is likely to be even higher than the global estimate of 15% due to the protracted crisis situation and the increased lack of basic health systems leading to higher prevalence of disabling conditions. In the recent FSNMS+ (2021) qualitative survey, it indicated that persons with disabilities were identified as most vulnerable to food insecurity and continue to experience access barriers and exclusion from humanitarian assistance including food and livelihood opportunities.To respond to these challenges, Humanity & Inclusion (HI) in South Sudan has adopted a holistic and comprehensive approach for mainstreaming disability inclusion to humanitarian support, enshrined in its Country Strategic Plan, that seeks to respond to critical needs of the Persons with Disabilities while supporting national systems and working with partners to priorities needs of the Persons with disabilities to achieve sustainable and inclusive livelihoods.

2 - Project activities

The proposed project will enable the beneficiaries through a comprehensive approach combining individualized support and empowerment of vulnerable groups as well as building networks and working with existing structures, to improve the economic situation and increase resilience to shocks. CO1: Provide food assistance to prevent famine and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies for vulnerable populations (in IPC 5, 4 & some 3)• Provide disability inclusive cash assistance for persons with disabilities. • Establish disability Inclusive cash for work projects for creation and rehabilitation of community assets and infrastructure.• Provide tailored support and access to food for persons with disabilities, women, and other most vulnerable groups through referrals linkages and technical guidance on disability Inclusive food assistance for food assistance partners within the FSL cluster.• Strengthening capacities of local partner’s including organizations of persons with disabilities’ capacities to provide inclusive food assistance through tailored development plans including capacity assessments, trainings, adaptations and monitoring of barriers to accessing food for persons with disabilities, women and other vulnerable groups. CO2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support (in IPC 3, 4 & 5)• Support establishment of disability Inclusive permagardens/Kitchen gardens.• Facilitate access to agricultural inputs by conducting inclusive and targeted seeds and adapted tools distribution to households with people with disabilities. • Provide onsite training for households of people with disabilities on Good Agricultural Practices (GAP) and principles and be supported by Peer Extension Agents to ensure the success of the project. (All targeted households will receive both staple crops and vegetable seeds)• Provide Individualized inclusive livelihood support to persons with disabilities to ensure their meaningful participation in livelihood and food security activities through individualized action plans.• Provide technical support to cluster livelihood actors on mainstreaming disability Inclusion in their agricultural activities. CO3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks (implemented across all 78 counties)• Provide capital/inputs for business start-ups or business strengthening and business skills training to persons with disabilities. • Establish an inclusive and gender-sensitive Disability Inclusion Ambassadors’ network, ensuring protection and promoting their active involvement in identifying and mitigating barriers to community and economic inclusion (Mapping of persons with disabilities and mapping of relevant actors who are supporting inclusive community activities within target communities, recruitment and training of the “Disability Inclusion Ambassadors”, awareness-raising activities in the target communities, mapping of barriers to access, service mapping, links formed with local authorities, local and community-based organizations and local service providers). • Establish peer support groups for persons with disabilities and/or their caregivers, to enhance the psychosocial wellbeing and independence of persons with disabilities and to contribute to improved referral and community support for persons with disabilities in accessing food (identification of persons with disabilities, mapping of services, establishment of peer support groups and networks, orientation/referral to services).

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Using cash or vouchers recognizes that beneficiaries are best placed to decide what their households need, and gives them the flexibility to choose according to their own diverse needs and priorities.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

HI will use the proposed inclusive cash and voucher assistance to address basic needs* for food and income and/or to protect, establish or re-establish livelihoods or productive capacity* for persons with disabilities. Because of the significant social-economic impact of COVID-19 coupled with food security stresses, there are changes in the price and supply of essential goods with a significant fall in the demand for the goods of local market actors or services which led to distortions in markets that undermined the future viability of local livelihoods, jobs and businesses. HI will conduct market assessments prior to the implementation of the project. The assessment data for a market analysis for the proposed cash programme will include information on, but is not limited to (protection risk, stakeholders’ operational capacity, financial services mapping, Disability/gender consideration, etc.). HI has developed a rapid guideline for Multipurpose cash and food assistance – which includes tools for those assessments (modality decision tool and market analysis).

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Any complaint related to HI programming and areas of operation that are non-sensitive is categorized as an operational complaint. This includes but is not limited to complaints such as • dissatisfaction with the provided goods and services • quality and quantity of the distributed items • failure to provide timely assistance or request for additional assistance • issues related to eligibility, registration, or distribution • lack of general project information • non-serious, yet impolite or irresponsible behavior of HI staff or partner staff Operational or a programme relevant complaints are critical for the FCMS focal point as they need to track them until they are resolved and closed. For operational complaints, the procedure is as follows: 1. The operational complaint is forwarded as an email to the relevant project/program manager, copying the area manager or Senior Project Liaison Officer (PLO) and where appropriate technical specialist of the respective sector (with MEAL Manager in cc) on the same day of receiving the complaint 2. Complainant receives a written receipt (see External Acknowledgement Form) in their preferred modality: paper copy, email, or SMS text message within max. 7 calendar days 3. Relevant Program staff reviews the complaint, brings forward recommendations for action, and shares these with Complaints Focal Point and/or MEAL Manager. Some complaints may require Country Director oversight or consultation. 4. Complaint Desk provides feedback to the complainant in his/her preferred modality: paper copy, email, or SMS text message, and informs about the appeals procedure within max. 21 calendar days The complainant has the right to appeal the feedback provided by HI within a period of maximum 30 calendar days. Appeals shall be dealt with on a case-by-case basis unless case-type precedence has been established.Complaints may require immediate action, for example, an issue that poses a security threat to beneficiaries, HI staff, HI resources/assets, or HI reputation should immediately be acted upon. In such a scenario, the complaint focal point needs to contact the relevant person, either by phone or by visiting them (If they are in the same once) such as area manager, safety advisor or his/her replacement and country director, deputy country directors, etc. These managers should decide the future course of action by assessing the severity and the nature of the complaint. Following this, the complaint focal point should write an email containing all the details about the complaint provided by the complainant to ensure the formal submission along with adding this to FCMS centralized datasheet. All non-programmatic HI-related complaints should be forwarded to the relevant staff head of the department by copying their immediate supervisor. The relevance of the complaint will be determined by the nature of the complaint as explained above. Operational complaints will be processed and delegated internally by the complaints and response focal person by forwarding the complaints and response log to the Area Manager. If the complaint entails a senior member of staff or is about the Area Manager/PLO, the complaint will be sent directly to the CD in Juba. If the complaint is about the CD, the complaints will be sent to the HQ via email: alert@hi.org However for most other programmatic complaints, the Area Manager/PLO will review the complaints listed and refer them by email to the relevant department and person For urgent action, the feedback will directly be communicated for immediate reaction. It will be included in the project action plan as soon as possible to ensure the problem will be solved timely. Regarding project/program-related feedback - the feedback will be taken into account through the usual coordination mechanism, including coordination meetings (including operations and support services), to ensure the project adapts to the feedback.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

HI has a robust feedback and complaints mechanism for beneficiaries that combines both unsolicited communication channels such as suggestion boxes, text, and phone lines as well as an anonymous reporting email service, with solicited forms such as monthly focus group discussions, interviews, and post-intervention monitoring surveys– designed to close the loop. To ensure the most vulnerable community members can access feedback and complaints mechanisms, HI will include considerations such as the use of local languages, reasonable accommodation for persons with disabilities (i.e. accessible venues, sign-language interpretation, sighted guides, accessible information materials), as well as gender specific mobilization and messaging to minimize any barriers these key groups encounter. Moreover, HI is proposing many entry points for feedback/complaints to reach all: 1. An in-person visit to FCMS Desk 2. HI community & group meetings 3. Complaint/Feedback Box 4. Toll-free telephone Hotline and SMS 5. HI email address 6. Beneficiary satisfaction surveys.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

HI benefits from a professional whistleblowing mechanism, upgraded in July 2020, with a technical solution delivered by a specialized professional service provider (NAVEX Global). HI has reinforced its system for reporting suspicions and incidents related to fraud, corruption, non-compliance with humanitarian neutrality, sexual abuse and exploitation, harassment, and child protection, through the dedicated web platform. This simplifies the reporting of suspicions of unethical acts, in complete safety and confidentiality. It optimizes the analysis and qualification of reports, the monitoring of investigations and the management of the incidents by the authorized officers, and the follow-up by the management bodies. This centralized mechanism complements the proximity systems and initiatives open on the field to beneficiaries and partners. Thus, HI has all the components of the alert and incident management chain: reports, evaluation, and investigation (including a specific handbook) and case management. HI’s Feedback & Complaints Management System (FCMS) in South Sudan aims to ensure accountability and improve the relevance, effectiveness, and suitability of HIs interventions. Through both solicited and non-solicited feedback, its purpose is to provide a safe, accessible, and effective channel for beneficiaries and project stakeholders to obtain information about the organization and its programs, offer feedback and complaints, and be provided response or redress. Further, it protects beneficiaries from any kind of abuse or potential corrupt behavior of HI or partner staff. To foster stronger relationships with the community, to allow community feedback to shape and improve programming and to ensure that HI South Sudan provides relevant, accessible and appropriate assistance. Further, HI aims to integrate and implement with adherence to the Core Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS). This document specifically focuses on Commitments 4 and 5 of the CHS, namely, that communities and people affected by crisis know their rights and entitlements, have access to information and participate in decisions that affect them; and have access to safe and responsive mechanisms to handle complaints. HI Complaint Mechanism Objective • To enable HI beneficiaries, staff and other stakeholders to provide HI management with information about the quality of the organization’s humanitarian and development assistance and serious misconducts of HI and/or HI partners’ staff;• Provides a framework to assist those responsible for ensuring standards and quality. • Makes explicit what users of the service can expect. • Provides a basis for accountability if practice falls below the expected standards. • To allow for HI to respond and communicate with beneficiaries • To ensure transparency and accountability of its operations • To receive valuable feedback, which may lead to alterations in project implementation • To create a platform from which to refer beneficiaries to other governmental or non-governmental agencies for additional services outside the scope and mandate of HI • To protect HI beneficiaries from any kind of exploitation and abuse (including physical and sexual) and corruptive behaviors of HI and/ or HI partners’ staff.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Given that certain target locations particularly away from urban centers are heavily contaminated with cluster munitions, ERW and UXOs it is critical that due diligence and coordination with mine-action partners is completed before conducting outreach activities in rural areas. The presence of explosive hazards in communities would heavily impact the delivery of activities but also factor into risk awareness and livelihood approaches. In all locations, disputes over housing and land are expected, particularly given the nature of land rights and legislation in South Sudan. The nature of conflict and displacement has also complicated claims to land and led to the expropriation of displaced populations' property. To mitigate this effectively, HI will monitor HLP issues in communities. Strong coordination with HLP partners, as well as the government is required to mediate issues effectively and mediate disputes. In target locations, there are high numbers of female IDPs living in insecure and unsafe settlements. HI will coordinate closely with GBV partners, camp management (if available), UNHCR and health providers to ensure post-incident support to survivors, as well as to develop solutions to address risks with protection partners. Given that the project is intending to support vulnerable beneficiaries it is essential that needs-based selection criteria are adhered to. HI will ensure to have accessible complaint and feedback mechanisms for all beneficiaries groups. To mitigate external interference, HI will utilize standardized vulnerability and targeting criteria, and consult all local stakeholders on this process to avoid any bias in the process. Furthermore, HI will work closely with a community-based organization and representatives of marginalized groups to ensure we reach the intended beneficiaries.
