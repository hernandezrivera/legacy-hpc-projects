Project Summary

The overall goal of the intervention is to provide emergency food assistance and livelihood support to protect lives, alleviate suffering and to improve people’s living standards. This will be achieved through building resilience by increasing household (HH) positive coping strategies and providing them with tools to recover from crisis and shocks.
The proposed FSL activities have been designed to improve the food security of people living in IPC 3 and higher locations by increasing access and availability of food, HH purchasing power by distributing agricultural inputs, provision of cash based interventions (CBI), and encouraging resilience through the support of income generating activities (IGA) and skills trainings.
DRC aims to reduce the negative impact on the environment, and to foster local-level, sustainable solutions that build resilience, food security and livelihood development through a circular bio-economy approach. DRC will advocate for and train people on the use of climate-smart agricultural techniques (CSA). All projects have been linked with information campaigns and trainings, and key topics across all areas will include environmental, protection and gender mainstreaming.
DRC vision is to provide a dignified life for all people affected by displacement. As such, DRC Food Security and Livelihood (FSL) activities are sectorial inter-linked with Protection [(including sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV)] and Armed Violence Reduction (AVR) response.

1 - Needs

Despite the advent of the September 2018 revitalized peace agreement, South Sudan continues to experience unprecedented humanitarian need. Almost seven million individuals need urgent humanitarian assistance, approximately 1.5 million are internally displaced, and human rights abuses continue to be perpetuated by all parties to the conflict with near impunity. While it is acknowledged that, the current conditions are not in place for the dignified return of refugees and IDPs in South Sudan, areas such as Unity State have seen a rapid increase in population movement with refugees arriving from Sudan, placing the already scarce resources of the host community under increased pressure. A continued cycle of protracted conflict, displacement and underdevelopment have restricted livelihood opportunities and undermined households resilience to shocks and stressors. An estimated 6.35 million people are experiencing Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food security outcomes. While seasonal food security will be observed as households begin to harvest their crops, the risk of Famine (IPC Phase 5) will continue to persist in areas already classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or higher. Conflict and the subsequent displacement are the main drivers of food insecurity. The conflict has severely disrupted local trade reduced household purchasing power, restricted humanitarian access, and eroded traditional safety nets. The assets of displaced households have been lost or destroyed during the conflict, forcing households to turn to negative coping mechanisms. Conflict and displacement affect men, women, boys, and girls differently, and aggravates the preexisting gender gap and inequalities present in South Sudan. It is estimated that more than 60% of households experiencing IPC 3 + across five states (Jonglei, Unity, Warrap, Upper Nile and Lakes), are female headed. The compounded vulnerabilities linked to conflict and food insecurity have led to women and girls to use survival sex as a coping mechanism. Additionally, women experience food insecurity at a higher rate than men as due to engendered cultural and social roles they as are the household food providers, and women are more likely to pass on their rations to their children during periods of household food scarcity. According to FEWSNET, the number of beneficiaries reached with humanitarian food assistance remains lower than planned and below numbers reached in previous years. Climatic shocks and stressors, such as flooding and drought, exacerbate the ongoing food security crisis. As a result, access to areas with food insecurity remains essential and the need for FSL humanitarian assistance across the state remains critical. Overall, the security situation remains volatile and depending on the outcome of the November 12th deadline to form a Unity Government, a rapid deterioration in the security and humanitarian situation may occur. Even peace and stability return to South Sudan, the decades of conflict and the sustained economic decline mean that it the transition to a stable food secure nation will be a lengthy process.

2 - Activities

As a protection mandated organization, all activities will be implemented in close collaboration with the DRC protection teams and protection principles. Considering this, DRC proposes to implement the following activities: SO1: Physical and mental wellbeing: reducing morbidity, mortality and suffering,SO2: Living standards: access to critical integrated services (including kitchen gardens)CO2: Complementary livelihood support (cropping/veg/fish livestock)Support access and availability of food:1. Distribution of agricultural inputs, such as seeds and tools, fishing kits, boats, and livelihood kits2. Establishment of Community Sustainability Centres (CSCs). The CSC will function as joint community livelihood training centres and business hubs that will provide permanent demonstration bases for income generating activities (IGA) and FSL activities. On-site FSL activities will include the creation of demonstration plots for kitchen gardening (perma-gardens), agroecological farms where climate-relevant techniques, such as agroforestry and relevant earthworks can be tested and showcased to the local population. 3. Cash based interventions (CBI): DRC will support the needs of vulnerable households and the demand of locally established producing cooperatives through the provision of voucher assistance for basic needs such as charcoal, soap, cooking oil. SO3: Recovery and resilience: recover from crisis and resilience to acute shocks and chronic stress.CO3: Ability to recover and absorb shocks Support economic recovery and resilience1. Provision of conditional food assistance through Food For Assets (FFA): FFA households will receive food/cash assistance for 15 days a month. Monthly food rations will be distributed after completion of planned works, and DRC will work closely with community and farm leaders to monitor and validate the works completed before the food rations are distributed. 2. Trainings on basic business skills: DRC will form women’s livelihood cooperatives and provide income generating activity (IGA) workshops with the aim of skills diversiﬁcation for improved livelihood outcomes. Cooperative members will be trained through a 6-month programme on tailoring, and be complemented with numeracy and literacy skills trainings. 3. CBI: DRC will implement CBIs, for example the distribution of voucher and cash start-up grants to enable the establishment of community businesses (agri-business and other IGAs): such as soap making, seed pressing, green charcoal production, one top shops and butchery to facilitate income diversiﬁcation and household economic resiliency. 4. Opportunities that increase relevant community assets and knowledge will be supported through Cash for Work (CfW), examples of activities will include: nursery management, seed collection, community asset construction and/or repair. 5. Impart trainings on relevant skills in order to provide the local population with new adaptive capacities.6. Support natural resource management (NRM) activities to mitigate the negative impacts of displacement and inadequate NRM. Activities include treemarking, distribution of energy efficient stoves (EES), woodlot management committees, tree planting, tree nursery management.7. Awareness raising campaigns on FSL and environmental themes including cross cutting topics such as nutrition, SGBV/FSL, WASH and others.

8.6 Risk of dispute on land ownership/land tenure in targeted project areasThe conflict has seen a rise in the incidents of housing, land, and property (HLP) disputes with the land of displaced households often resettled in their absence. The situation is particularly challenging for female headed households, who have land rights under the constitution and national framework, but often experience discrimination in fulfilling these rights at the local level due restrictive social and cultural norms at the household level. Considering these challenges, this project will be closely implemented with DRCs Protection team who are currently implementing HLP projects. In addition, DRC will carry out community sensitisation and ensure land verification (in so far as possible) to ensure that households have the right to carry out agricultural activities on that land. 8.1. Members of marginalised community’s/minority groups are not included in consultation and/or excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. DRC will carry out sensitisation among its own team members to raise their awareness about the need to engage members from all segments in the consultations/project implementation. DRC will also carry out home to home visits to ensure the inclusion of community members who may be unable to leave their household due to physical or cultural/social restrictions. 8.10. Exposure of women and girls to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse in relation to access to humanitarian assistance. As was observed in Unity State, late last year, women and girls face an increased risk of sexual violence when accessing humanitarian assistance. Considering this, DRC will ensure protection mainstreaming across its programming and seek to reduce/mitigate protection risks that may be incurred during the course of the project. Examples, of steps that will be taken by DRC include engagement of beneficiary members around different the selection of distribution location.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

DRC has established Project Monitoring Committees and Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (F-CRM): DRC adopts a community-based approach, where beneficiaries and local authorities are included in the prioritising, planning and implementation of activities as an important measure of rehabilitation and capacity building. DRC is a certified member of the Core humanitarian Standards (CHS) and as such fulfils the CHS benchmarks on accountability through information, involvement, participation and adequate handling of complaints. DRC has deployed beneficiary feedback and complaints mechanism and operates complaints desks to enable community members to flag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. DRC also has a feedback hotline Number (9686) that beneficiaries can call to register complaints, feedback or requests. DRC will also carry out continuous community engagement and feedback, ensuring safety and dignity of beneficiaries are prioritized throughout the intervention. Through its activities, DRC will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols. Complaints and feedback submitted to the F-CRM will be reviewed to identify any issues in implementation of the AVR and livelihood project activities and any additional information on the perception of the project in the communities.
