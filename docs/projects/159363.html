Project Summary

Overall objective: To provide emergency assistance and ensure non-discriminatory access to quality essential services for the most vulnerable people affected by the humanitarian crisis in Luhansk region of Ukraine (along the contact line and on NGCA). SOS CV UA works in the Luhansk region, focusing on child protection, with complementary provision of non-food items to the vulnerable families and wide range of PSS for children and adults. The main aim is to mitigate the impact of conflict on the vulnerable families with children living along the contact line and in Luhansk, NGCA and ensure that children are protected in family environment.

1 - Needs

1. Protection needs. Access to life-saving information and services, improvement in referralsand multi-sectorial coordination remain a key protection need. Needs in child protection remain burning. Nearly a sixth of school and kindergarten interviewees to UNICEF mentioned without direct prompting that many parents have paid much less attention to their children as a result of the stress of living in conflict and poverty. With low resilience among confict-affected people, parents/caregivers are overstretched and are increasingly adopting negative coping mechanisms such as alcohol abuse, as our state partners arereporting, which is leading to an increase in domestic violence and child neglect. Amongthe most burning needs are the needs in psycho-social support (PSS), especially along thecontact line, with such age and gender differences: children and elderly people reportedlyhave more problems with night sleep and deterioration of psysiological functions, womenare more often express psychosomatic reactions; more problems with aggressiveness inboys and men. Women are more willing to get help. Another problematic category inneed of PSS is teenagers, both girls and boys, including increased number of suicideattempts. 2. Needs in NFIs. The conflict significantly affected provision of families withbasic children's goods in districts along the contact line and on NGCA after outflow ofsuppliers and delivery limitations due to safety risks. The general impoverishment of theaffected population, limited selection of goods and high prices on children goods haveforced some parents to stop purchasing clothing and footwear, diapers, special hygieneproducts for children. 3. Physical health needs increased due to the lack of qualitysecondary medical service and as a result of stress/PTSD, sever anxieties, psychosomaticdisorders, decreased immunity, and cannot be treated well due to lack of finance.According to HNO, access to health for one third of households in the conflict area ishampered, in an environment where every second family reports having one or morefamily members suffering from a chronic illness. The biggest family health expendituresare usually purchasing of medicines, and the prices are continuously rising. From thesocial point of view, needs assessment has shown that one of the most vulnerablecategories still are families with children under 3 living along the contact line. In case ofpost-delivery and breast-feeding connected complications there are no qualified medicalspecialists (gynecologists, mammologists, etc.), as well as psychologists or therapists tohelp in case of post-delivery depression. The need in PSS is bigger for parents with a babyunder 1 and a pregnant women. These difficulties can result in deterioration of children’sand mothers’ health, even with a risk for life, severe post-delivery depression of mothers,early rejection from breast feeding (majority of mothers - according to observations oflocal doctors), hypothermia, malnutrition and underweight of children, their distressedpsychological state, delays in child development. If a vulnerable family fails to satisfy thedescribed needs, child protection risks increase: neglect and domestic violence againstchildren leads to families breaking down, with parents sending their children tograndparents or other relatives/non-relatives (according to REACH assessment 19% ofhouseholds reported caring for unrelated minors) or leaving them to be cared for by careinstitutions. Unfortunately, state services organizations cannot provide sufficient responsedue to a lack of financial and human resources. More children receive the status ofsuffered from the war, but no state services are available to help them. Local socialworkers, teachers, medical staff are lacking the knowledge and skills to work with childrenand families experienced war trauma.

2 - Activities & outcomes

For the next year, SOS CV UA plans to provide comprehensive psycho-social support andemergency relief on the basis of community centers in Stanitsa Luhanska and Luhansk atleast to 500 children (250 girls and 250 boys), 343 adults (women and men), and at least 350households. These children and families will receive comprehensible PSS, protectioninformation (including on prevention of GBV), NFIs according to their individual needs.Work of mobile team along the contact line will enable provision of psycho-social support andprotection information to at least to 1400 children (700 girls and 700 boys), and 1225caregivers (1100 women and 125 men).The Accountability to Affected Population in the project will be ensured by: 1)Functioning of beneficiaries’ committees gathering according to the need but at least 3times through the project: in the beginning to share our plans with the community andensure better adaptation of project activities to specific local needs, including gender andage-based; during mid-term review to apply corrective measures; in the projectfinalization phase to give possibility to beneficiaries to evaluate the project results, assessquality of services received and define how much satisfaction of their needs increaseddue to the project (this information will be used also for informing the next planningcycle) 2) Signing the acquaintance and strict following by all the project staff such keydocuments as the SOS Code of Conduct and SOS Care Promise, where accountabilityobligation is included 3) feedback and complaints system serving to adjust and changeoperational plans during the programme cycle so affected populations can comment onthe quality, appropriateness and/or adequacy of programme interventions, and reportviolations of the law, SOS policies and Code of conduct 4) proper informing thebeneficiaries whenever changes in the project are made.
