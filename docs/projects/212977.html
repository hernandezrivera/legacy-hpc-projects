Project Summary

The association owns an under-construction building in Um-Elamad village. During 2024 the association aims to establishing a rehabilitation and support center (and its mobile team) for People with disabilities. That includes the following activities and target groups (center-based and mobile):
• Mental health and psychosocial support (Individual and group)
• Linguistic and educational support including speech therapy
• Technical and social support and training for life skills
• Support for those with hearing impairment
• Support for those with physical impairment including physical therapy
• Support for victims of war (injured) by providing medical & PSS services (INMAA has registered 70 cases of mine victims). In general, there are about a 1,000 war injuries in the region, 700 of them have become permanent disability.
• Provision of case management for the mentioned above target groups
• Support to informal learning opportunity and provision of recreational activities
• Support for families of children with disabilities including mental support and teaching them how to behave with and treat those in their care (parenting programs)
• Implement awareness raising sessions, community events and activity days on child protection issues.
• Assistive devices for children with disabilities (wheelchairs, crutches, hearing devices, Text-To-Speech assistive tools, prosthetics, Braille boxs, Optacon devices).
Target beneficiaries will be people with disabilities (regardless of gender and age), however a large focus will be placed on ages 3-15.
the project aims to provide protection services to all residents of the villages of the Al Makhrim region (+30 villages), reaching the remote areas located in the Syrian desert to the east.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

The targeted areas are considered to be in severity need of protection and medical services. There are no hospital, health centers or protection center in a circumference of more than 40 km in all directions. On the other hand, the population exceeds 60 thousand people (60% women and children). The region also hosts dense concentrations of internally displaced persons (IDPs), who constitute 33% of the population.The association currently has a total of 250 registered people with disabilities, all of whom are also registered as PWDs within the Ministry of Social Affairs and Labor. Their disabilities include mental disabilities (72%), hearing disabilities and visual impairments (12%), loss of limb and/or lack of mobility from war or birth defects (16%).The services will be offered to the PWDs themselves, as well as their families/caretakers, in order to allow them to improve their capacities and skills when caring for their loved ones.Most activities will be in class forms and can be done either individually or in groups, depending on the activity and the unique cases of beneficiaries involved. While the mobile team will attract new cases and provide services at home for difficult cases who are unable to move. Also, Monitoring and evaluation will be done periodically.PWDs and females with disabilities in particular face higher risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, thus the center will offer awareness sessions, as well as trainings to build livelihood capacities. Additionally, codes of conducts will be put in place in order to ensure a safe environment amongst workers, as well as ensuring that if workers are not predominantly female, there is at least an equal ratio of females to male workers.The center will deal with potential protection risks such as lack of permission from families in regards to equal access of services due to gender, this will be mitigated through the mobile teams delivering services at PWD's homes when it is of utmost urgency, as well as reaching out to community leaders and building a trust-based relationship that extends throughout the local community. In addition, humanitarian workers lacking capacities to work with the unique cases that might visit the center, which will be mitigated by hiring highly qualified personnel and ensuring they receive routine trainings from specialists, as well as having regular monitoring and evaluation of the services to ensure the beneficiaries are being well-treated.
