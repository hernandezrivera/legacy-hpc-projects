Project Summary

INTERSOS continues to provide Nutrition Services focusing on Children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) living in Jowhar and Balad districts of Middle Shabelle, Somalia. The OTP and TSFP activities are enhanced thanks UNICEF and WFP partnership for the supply of RUTF, CSB & drugs. INTERSOS supports 4 fixed OTP sites and 2 Mobile sites in Balcad, Jowhar, Hawadley and SC in Jowhar Regional Referral Hospital. The Moderate acute malnutrition and Maternal Child Health and Nutrition (MCHN) managed through TSFP and MCHN in Jowhar. INTERSOS also carries out IYCF activities targeting predominantly mothers of malnourished children, which mainly focuses on counselling for behaviour change communication & counselling (for improved awareness) on breastfeeding and young child feeding practices and livelihood (for improved food/nutrition security) at household level. Community screening targets boys and girls equally and depending on their nutritional status they are referred to the OTP or TSFP based on the admission criteria.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in Somalia has continued to deteriorate due to the ongoing impacts of drought and conflict. Although famine was averted, the risk of famine persists. A Nutrition assessment conducted in July 2018 shown that, nearly 954,007 children are expected to be acutely malnourished, including 173,594 who are expected to be severely malnourished during the next one-year period (Sep 2018-Sep 2019). The surveys indicate Critical levels of GAM (15-29.9%) in 12 of which Bay and Middle Shabelle was among the 12 areas, out of 33 populations surveyed (7 IDP and 5 Rural) while Critical levels of SAM prevalence (≥4-5.6) were only seen among IDPs in Mogadishu and Guban pastoral. Similarly, the Crude Death Rate (CDR) rates are Critical (1 to 20 %) in many parts of the country but highest among population groups surveyed in northeast and central parts of the country. Overall level of acute malnutrition is Serious (median GAM of 14 per cent), this is an improvement from Critical in 2017 Gu (median GAM of 17.4 per cent). The same Survey shown Baidoa IDPs to have a GAM rate of 17.7% SAM-4.6%, Shabelle region riverine and agro pastoral population had a GAM of 14.5% and 12.4% and SAM of 2.9% and 2.2% respectively. In Bakool and Banadir had a GAM of 16.7% and 10.1% and a SAM of 4.1% and 0.6% respectively. The underlying causes of malnutrition can be attributed to high morbidity (disease incidence e.g. AWD/cholera, measles), low humanitarian support, poor child feeding and caring practices. Similarly, most partners on the ground reported that food insecurity, limited health service availability, like poor EPI coverage, and increased morbidity, poor health seeking behaviour, and difficulty of accessing clean water supply are driving factors for the current situation. If the conflict, instability, and other food insecurity drivers do not improve, malnutrition rates among most vulnerable population like pregnant and lactating women and children are likely to deteriorate.

Activities

• Provision of IMAM (Integrated Management of Acute Malnutrition) with a combination of inpatient (SC) and out-patient (OTP) therapeutic feeding for the treatment of severe acute malnutrition in children aged 0-59 month in the Middle Shabelle, Bay and Bakool Region; the nutrition service will be done through a network of mobile clinics, static OTPs and SC in Middle Shabelle. The network of OTPs is structured on 11 static sites, 11 mobile. (6 fixed OTPs in Middle Shabelle (1 in Jowhar, 2 in Balcad,1 in Hawadley, 2 in Mahaday) 3 Fixed OTP in Bay region (1 in Baidoa and 1 in Dinsoor, 1 Qansandeere), 2 Fixed OTP in Bakool (Waajid). 1 SC will be based in Jowhar Referral and Regional Hospital. • Integrate EPI services (immunization services delivery) in OTP services as part of the static and mobile nutrition program through health funding. • Provide and integrate Nutrition, health and hygiene education (NHHP) education in all static and mobile OTP sites. • Ensure that children have access to vitamin A supplementations and deworming. For PLW, as recommended by WHO, supplementation with iron+folic acid and using single-dose of albendazole (400 mg) • Promote breastfeeding practices among mothers providing IYCF counselling services. • Link SAM case-management to MAM treatment through WFP funding (INTERSOS will seek extension of existing WFP project). • Ensure a community screening of malnutrition, provide by CHWs and mother-led MUAC for both children 6-59 months and PLW. • Capacity building for the staff with refresher training on management and prevention of acute malnutrition and training on IYCF. Community health worker(CHWs) will also be given refresher training on detection and referral of acute malnutrition, IYCF and NHHP promotion. Finally, 300 mothers would be trained on Mother lead MUAC Screening and provided with MUAC tapes for early detection and referral of malnourished children.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

INTERSOS views the proposed intervention as an entry door for longer term humanitarian implementation in such a critical areas that needs urgent and constant humanitarian assistance. Somalia is an unstable volatile political and humanitarian context, and the ability of humanitarian organizations to foresee the transition of funding and responsibility to local mechanisms is limited. Across the regions, the gap in the basic humanitarian’s services remains large due to prolonged conflict and a very weak central government overseeing what was intended to be a highly centralized human care system. Due to this, it is clear that the need for external humanitarian support to these areas will remain high. Recently, the Somali Federal Government established the Ministerial Emergency Response Committee, describing the unfolding situation, “as a precursor to the situation in 2011 in terms of intensity.” INTERSOS Somalia has been responding proactively to the unfolding crisis through its traditional emergency programming, concerted efforts to raise awareness with traditional government donors, SOs and multi-lateral donors, and targeted early actions in its livelihood activities communities to protect resilience gains and avert emergency. For this project, INTERSOS will transfer responsibilities to the established local authorities/community members. In the long term, INTERSOS is optimistic that as the government grows stronger; its institutions will be able to absorb increasing responsibility for the delivery of humanitarian services. To further enhance the sustainability and the ownership of the project it will include activities and support to ensure sustainable protection outcomes beyond the period of protection interventions (such as referrals, awareness sessions and enrollment in activities to support income generation based on the activities prioritized by the community). This will be achieved through coordination with other agencies and partners, engagement of affected population and local authorities as well as development an exit strategy together with the beneficiaries which will rely on structures already existing at community level.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Some of the major protection risks facing the targeted population especially PLWs includes incidents of Gender-based violence (GBV), especially in IDP sites. Abuses against civilians, including widespread sexual and gender-based violence (SGBV), child recruitment, child separation and limitations on the freedom of movement, social exclusion of marginalized communities, forced evictions and secondary displacement remain a pervasive feature of the conflict in targeted locations.INTERSOS will ensure meaningful representation of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities) and play a key role in identifying issues related to exclusion and discrimination and be proactive in ensuring the voice of marginalized groups is represented

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

In order to cater for the specific needs of person of different age groups, INTERSOS will collect and use data disaggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming.INTERSOS will identify groups who face difficulties accessing services (e.g. women, girls, persons with disabilities, older persons, persons from minority groups, persons belonging to marginalized groups) and work with community leaders, religious leaders and service providers to support equal and safe access to services without discrimination. INTERSOS will identify the power dynamics in the area by focusing on who has access to service, who manages the facilities and who is vulnerable and marginalised.INTERSOS will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by Identifying and prioritising the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized and use this information to inform monitoring activities and identify any barriers to access services or discrimination against particular groups. Take this into consideration when determining locations for interventions. Furthermore, INTERSOS field staffs will undertake trainings on protection mainstreaming with the support of INTERSOS Protection coordinator.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

• The project will Involve and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to protection needs specifically by consulting women, men, youth, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalised groups to understand their needs, coping strategies and preferences for protection services.• Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect the targeted population, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response.• Promote and support self-protection capacities and avoid persons resorting to negative coping strategies to mitigate risks. Reinforce the capacity of the community and family to act as a protective layer for children against violations.• INTERSOS will set up accessible, confidential and locally understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improving programming, understanding community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct eg. Feedback box in centres. A strong feedback mechanism will enhance the capacity of INTERSOS Staff to prevent challenges and address areas of the project weakness thus improving risks management in relation of the delivery of services.
