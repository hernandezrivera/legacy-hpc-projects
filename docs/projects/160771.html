Project Summary

Danish Refugee Council/Danish Demining Group (DDG) will deploy four highly mobile Multi Task Teams (MTTs), together with four embedded Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) teams to respond to urgent threats to physical safety, dignity, freedom of movement and access to humanitarian assistance within communities. The objectives of the project are to:
1. Provide immediate lifesaving assistance to address the urgent protection needs of the most vulnerable (including women, children, elderly, disabled and survivors of sexual violence in conﬂict) in hard-to-reach areas.
2. Enhance prevention and preparedness programming to mitigate risks and strengthen resilience
3. Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other affected populations
Tasks which will support the humanitarian response in the country will be prioritised through the following key activities:
• Non-technical survey (NTS) and technical survey (TS) – to target and prioritise clearance according to humanitarian need, survey will be conducted using a mixture of key informant interviews and household questionnaires held with the local population and authorities. NTS will determine the likely locations and density of ERW contamination in target locations, whilst assessing the impact of these hazards on vulnerable populations in any given location. TS (using detectors) will be used upon discovery of an area with a high concentration of explosive hazards.
• MMC / BAC - the MTTs will be able to act as a MMC / BAC capacity, performing static clearance activities across larger, clearly deﬁned areas (identiﬁed through the above data collection activities and technical survey as appropriate).
• Explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) – once NTS has determined the nature of the threat, technical capacities within the teams will be deployed to remove and destroy identiﬁed hazards.
• Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE) – all teams will contain a capacity to deliver risk messaging raising awareness about how to recognise dangerous items, as well as associated safe practices that should be followed, and high risk behaviour that should be avoided in relation to mines and explosive remnants of war.
The activities outlined in this document will primarily target 72,000 IDPs, returnees and host community members living across Central, Western and Eastern Equatoria States, Upper Nile and Western Bahr el Ghazal, including in hard-to-reach locations where mine/ERW contamination remains substantial. DDG will target these populations with EORE, improving their knowledge of the explosive hazards they can encounter, and at the same time collect evidence for clearance tasks. EORE will be delivered based on analysis of speciﬁc needs of women, men, boys and girls. A total of 13,860 women, 11,340 men, 25,740 girls and 21,060 boys will be targeted by this intervention. Furthermore, DDG will, through Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), will identify the major service delivery points which will be a primary target for clearance responses.
This project has broadly been designed to directly beneﬁt the 2020 multisector response in South Sudan. The intervention will be delivered as a protection mainstreaming effort, designed to target survey, clearance and RE towards areas to which population movements are expected, as well as ensuring beneﬁciaries are able to safely access water points, schools, health facilities and other socio-economic assets being supported / rehabilitated by agencies on the ground. In doing so, DDG will ensure that beneﬁciaries of other partners are not at undue risk when accessing services.

1 - Needs

While the revitalized September 2018 peace agreement presents an opportunity for peace and stability, South Sudan continues to experience unprecedented humanitarian need. An estimated six million individuals are in need of protection assistance and decades of conflict have left South Sudan heavily contaminated by landmines, munitions, and unexploded ordinance (UXO). According to IMSMA September 2019, there are 467 hazard areas identified across the country, including 186 minefields and 115 UXO, or approximately 24,162,362 square metres of contamination, all of which pose a threat to the safety and free movement of civilians. Landmines and unexploded remnants of war (ERW) pose a threat to stability and development. The presence of landmines and ERW prevent households from accessing their agricultural land and accessing essential services, such as health and education. They hamper the freedom of movement, forcibly shifts livelihood patterns, and obstructs the delivery of humanitarian aid. In addition, landmines and ERW pose physical protection risks to the affected population. From January to September 2019, 34 people were killed or injured as a result of ERW incidents across South Sudan, with children at particular risk. According to IMSMA 2019, the majority or 45% of victims in 2019 were young boys. However, landmines and ERW do not discriminate, and people of all ages and gender have been gravely affected by explosive hazards in 2019. Considering this, DDG promotes mainstreams gender and age diversity across its mine risk education programming to ensure a comprehensive approach. The DDG approach seeks to strengthen existing coping mechanisms across all sections of ERW affected communities, including potential victims or their relatives. While the overall security environment has improved in South Sudan, the situation remains volatile and localised ethnic conflict and cattle raiding continues to be observed. To ensure that it can reach areas most in need, DDG will implement a roving response capacity which will rapidly deploy to areas where ERW poses a grave protection risk to communities. In close collaboration with the Protection team under the Danish Refugee Council (DRC), DDG will target displaced and conflict affected populations through mapping and prioritisation systems that are designed to target the most vulnerable. Furthermore, the existing DRC-DDG infrastructure in Aburoc, Bentiu, Mayom, Juba, Maban, Malakal, and Wau will enable DDG to rapidly deploy to priority locations identified by the Protection Cluster. DDG will aim to ensure gender equality is sufficiently assessed and monitored. DDG is an equal opportunity employer and strives for a balanced representation of women and men in the workplace. The existing DDG female staff will facilitate access to female beneficiaries in conservative settings. Gender components will be mainstreamed into all project assessments and evaluations, and data will be gender disaggregated to allow for effective measurement. DDG will support further mainstreaming of gender within its programme throughout the lifetime of the project. DDG ensures that the EORE and NTS is carried out by teams with both male and female staff, and that all staff conducting NTS and EORE are trained in strategies (eg. timing/location of sessions, gender balanced teams) ensuring that less represented groups can also participate in DDG activities and inform programming. For example, DDG has previous experience that afternoon sessions are appropriate for inclusion of women, when lunch food has been cooked and dinner is not being prepared yet.

2 - Activities

The overall goal of this project is to prevent and mitigate against the physical risks posed by ERW, by enhancing community preparedness and resilience, through a roving mine action response. This proposed intervention directly aligns with the SO2, in that clearing land of explosive hazards will reduce physical protection risks and support people to travel to access essential services. Considering this, DDG proposes to undertake the following activities. 1. Provide immediate lifesaving assistance to address priority protection needs for the most vulnerable populations in hard-to-reach areas 1.1. Carry out desk research and engage with other stakeholders to identify areas affected by conﬂict and that are likely to have been heavily affected by ERW which risks constraining humanitarian access. 1.2. Conduct non-technical surveys (NTS) in severity levels 3&4 areas through MTT to identify and map immediate physical protection risks and humanitarian access constraints posed by ERW. 1.3. Survey infrastructure and transit route through NTS to facilitate the timely, effective and safe delivery of humanitarian assistance by other stakeholders. 1.4: Conduct meetings with humanitarian agencies to prioritise locations in terms of humanitarian access / service delivery, including warehouses, compounds, distribution sites and/or schools. 1.5: Conduct refresher training for DDG staff on NTS, technical survey / TS (using detectors) and clearance methodologies. NTS training includes strategies designed to ensure participation and active engagement from women and children, recognising the obstacles presented by societal norms, and gender / age-speciﬁc daily routines and movement patterns 1.6: Conduct EOD / BAC / MMC to remove and destroy dangerous items. 2. Prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks through enhanced preparedness and resilience 2.1: Deploy 4 integrated Explosive Ordnance Risk education (EORE) teams to deliver sessions to at-risk women, men, boys and girls within displaced and host communities to promote greater awareness and safer practices in relation to ERW related risks. 2.2: Conduct refresher training for DDG staff on EORE delivery, including key messaging, participatory learning methods and engagement strategies aimed at securing participation across different age and gender groups. DDG will monitor gender and age speciﬁc attitudes towards ERW, with the ability to develop tailored risk reduction strategies where required. 2.3: Conduct NTS with women, men, boys and girls, identifying target groups likely to be affected by ERW contamination. 3. Enable durable solutions for IDPs and other affected populations 3.1: Desk research and liaison with partner organisations, identifying areas likely to experience returns / settlement that are also heavily affected by ERW 3.2: Deploy MTTs to identiﬁed returns / settlement locations, to conduct NTS, identifying and mapping physical security threats posed by HAs, and their impact on safe settlement / returns of displaced populations. 3.4: Conduct refresher training for DDG staff on NTS, TS and clearance methodologies. NTS training includes strategies designed to ensure participation and active engagement from women and children, recognising the obstacles presented by societal norms, and gender / age- speciﬁc daily routines and movement patterns 3.5: Conduct EOD / BAC / MMC to remove and destroy dangerous items. 3.6: Submit to the Protection Cluster / NMAA / UNMAS reconnaissance / needs assessment reports indicating the need for a mine action response in potential target areas.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

It is highly probably that explosive hazards will be present in target locations, as the purpose of this project is to address this contamination. DDG teams are trained and accredited to conduct activities in contaminated areas, and to manage the associated risks accordingly. While there is a risk that DDG teams will be exposed to violence in project locations, DDG's in-house safety capacity enables teams to rapidly deploy to new areas without taking undue risks with respect to staff safety. DDG's parent organization DRC currently employs 2 NGO Safety Advisors across the Greater Upper Nile region, located in Bentiu and Malakal, both managed by a Country Safety Advisor based in Juba. These Safety Advisors are permanently based in these locations and are responsible for gathering and disseminating detailed, regular security information. The Juba-based Country Safety Advisor therefore can dedicate time to rapidly responding to short notice requests for information regarding harder to reach or "grey zone" areas in which public information is sparse, drawing on contacts from other humanitarian agencies, national authorities, as well as other safety and security agencies. To date, this has allowed DDG teams to be deployed on the basis of thorough analysis within a very short notice period. DDG is thus in a unique position to respond in an informed and rapid manner into otherwise inaccessible areas.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Accountability to the affected population is one of DDGs organisational principles. As part of DDGs adherence to this principle, it actively shares project information with beneficiaries, this ensures that communities do not develop unrealistic expectations about the programme. In addition, DDG also shares information with the target community about protection risk, human rights, and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). DDG has an established community feedback and complaint mechanism in place across all it’s programming. For example, DDG/DRC currently have a feedback hotline number that beneficiaries can call to register complaints or feedback. DDG also operates complaint and feedback desks at its project locations to enable beneficiaries and wider community members to report issues in person. Wherever possible, DDG conducts an evaluation of project activities in order to ascertain and measure impact. DDG endeavors to achieve rigorous monitoring, evaluation and learning standards in its humanitarian response. DDG’s monitoring, evaluation and learning (MEL) approach is built on a number of key principles including 1) the use of flexible, mixed methodologies, 2) the integration of MEL into ‘everyday’ work, 3) linking evidence and learning with decision-making, 4) independence and neutrality of MEL staff, 5) involvement of key stakeholders and diverse voices, and 6) transparency with processes, progress and results.
