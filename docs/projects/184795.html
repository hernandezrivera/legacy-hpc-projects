Project Summary

In 2022 UNHCR will continue to focus its intervention on providing protection and other multi-sectoral assistance and working towards the attainment of durable solutions for refugees, asylum-seekers and returnees working together with international and national actors in support of the Government efforts. In addition to 30,800 refugees and asylum seekers and 39,820 refugee returnees from different countries of asylum -mainly Kenya and Yemen- are expected to be assisted with return to Somalia in the course of 2022, UNHCR and partners will also focus their efforts on working closely with the Government of Somalia for identification and protection of persons at risk of statelessness (those with undetermined nationality), as well as eradication of statelessness.

1. - Projects activities

The Refugee Response Plan is fully in line with the HRP objectives and is included in the 2022 HRP, in particular targeting 30,800 refugees and 39,820 refugee returnees. In 2022, UNHCR and partners will implement multi-sectoral assistance, to strengthen among others the protection environment, and will also focus on and solutions-oriented interventions in close collaboration with relevant government counterparts at the federal, regional, and member state levels. Specifically, UNHCR will continue working with the Government towards the implementation of the Global Compact on Refugees (GCR) and Comprehensive Refugee Response Framework (CRRF) to ensure that refugees, returnees, and asylum seekers are included in national development initiatives, including social protection and safety net programmes. The implementation of the GCR in Somalia is deeply rooted in regional processes led by the Intergovernmental Authority on Development (IGAD) and guided by the 2017 Nairobi Declaration on Durable Solutions (the Nairobi Declaration). The Nairobi Declaration, and its accompanying Plan of Action, proposes a comprehensive regional approach to delivering durable solutions for Somali refugees and to creating an enabling environment for their return and reintegration.UNHCR will continue advocacy and capacity building efforts providing technical support to the Government of Somalia in building its national asylum system in line with key international legal instruments that establish the legal framework for protection and solutions for refugees, namely the 1951 Refugee Convention and1967 Protocol, as well as the 1969 OAU Convention Governing the Specific Aspects of Refugee Problems in Africa. This will include support with adoption and implementation of the Refugee Law. Further, UNHCR will continue to provide technical support to the FGS through the enactment and effective implementation of the Federal Government of Somalia IDP Act. Additionally, UNHCR and partners will provide technical support to the FGS to meet the pledges made during the High-Level Segment on Statelessness at ExCom in 2019 namely, (i) to accede to the 1954 Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons and to the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness; (ii) to conduct and publish a qualitative study to better understand the situation of stateless groups and individuals and groups at potential risk of statelessness with a view to preventing and reducing statelessness.The National Durable Solutions Strategy, launched by the FGS in March 2021, contributes to the implementation of GCR and the FGS’s pledges made at the Global Refugee Forum. This crucial strategy contributes to HRP’s Strategic Objective 3 where persons of concern will be able to enjoy their rights and have access to strengthened national frameworks in line with relevant international standards.Further, multi-sector assistance and essential services, such as access to food and nutrition, shelter, health, education, water - through provision of multi-purpose cash grants as well as livelihoods, registration and issuance of requisite identity documentation will be provided to refugees and asylum seekers with particular attention to strengthening the protection environment and the capacity of vulnerable individuals towards resilience building and self-reliance. This intervention will contribute to HRP’s Strategic Objective 2 where persons of concern will have improved living conditions, services and a broader range of livelihoods opportunities. Further, refugee returnees residing in countries of asylum will be provided with information on the country of origin (Somalia), as well as benefit from reintegration support. Refugees and asylum seekers in need will be supported through resettlement and complementary pathways opportunities. This will contribute to HRP’s Strategic Objective 1 and 2. When implementing the programme, UNHCR and partners will put protection at the center of the response.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

UNHCR uses cash-based interventions (CBI) to provide basic needs and mitigate protection risks. CBI promotes greater dignity, flexibility, and choice for persons of concern to identify their priorities and address their own needs. Cash-based interventions contribute to increased protection outcomes, facilitate inclusion and solutions, and improve efficiency and effectiveness in programme delivery. Experience highlights that CBI can increase protection outcomes, allows to build and support livelihoods, contributes to the local economy and fosters positive relations with host communities. The flexibility that comes with the use of cash makes it a more dignified way of delivering assistance, an assertion that has been echoed explicitly by persons of concern (returnee refugees, refugees/asylum seekers and IDPs during focus group discussions and post distribution monitoring exercises. UNHCR has institutionalized CBIs as a corporate tool for delivering assistance. The flexibility that CBIs offer makes them a more dignified form of assistance, giving refugees and other beneficiaries the ability to immediately prioritize and choose what they need. UNHCR Somalia is ranked among the top ten CBI Operations globally, and the Operation continues to draw from the global institution’s strong expertise – including at HQ, Bureau (regional) and Country-level – on effective CBI programming. In doing so, the Operation leverages all the corporate tools developed over the years in order to promote efficiency of assistance, and empower beneficiaries to have a voice concerning their lives and assert greater control of their futures.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

UNHCR Somalia actively participates in the Cash Working Group (CWG) and the joint multi-sectoral needs assessments as well as market assessments during the response analysis and planning and programming phases to inform the modalities and delivery options of assistance. As a pre-requisite for CBIs, market functionality and access have been major determinants for CBI interventions. Based on the identified needs across sectors, a minimum expenditure basket has been used to inform transfer values for beneficiaries, which in turn have been informed by market price monitoring. In programming for CBIs, contingency measures have been put in place in striking a balance on cash versus in-kind modalities due to insecurity which can create market disruption and/or inaccessibility. Since 2014, UNHCR has a Framework Agreement with Standard Chartered Bank (SCB) to implement CBIs, executed through AMAL Bank Somalia. Protection interventions will be implemented through this agreement and UNHCR’s CBI Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). As a member the CWG, UNHCR will continue to participate in joint assessments (needs and market) to inform CBIs and will strive for common platforms (joint Financial Service Providers procurement, joint post distribution monitoring, joint targeting, etc.) across agencies for delivering assistance with improved efficiencies.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

UNHCR will ensure implementation of programs in line with the organization’s policy on Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD Policy of 2018). The AGD Policy focuses on six areas of engagement that comprise the framework for achieving accountability to persons of concern within the AGD approach and ensures that all groups in the community are consulted on the proposed interventions with the goal of maximizing coverage to beneficiaries and minimizing potential risks for persons with specific needs. Further, UNHCR will employ vulnerability-targeting criteria for identification and selection of beneficiaries.UNHCR will also ensure that prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) is mainstreamed across the whole project cycle and that partners and community leadership structures are also aware of their obligation in this regard. Communities will be sensitized on complaint mechanisms to ensure high standards of accountability are maintained and to meet UNHCR’s zero tolerance policy on SEA. Additionally, UNHCR will foster a zero-tolerance policy towards fraud across all interventions and projects and vis a vis all stakeholders. Existing feedback and complaints mechanisms will be maintained and strengthened where applicable, and communities will be made fully aware of these mechanisms. Any confidential and personalized data will be restricted to authorized protection and community services staff and will be shared only on a “need to know” basis and adhering to UNHCR data protection policies. Any use of data by UNHCR and its implementing partners will be regulated by the general principle of confidentiality and related provisions in project agreements with partners. UNHCR will pursue projects that promote peaceful co-existence to minimize risk of clashes with the host communities.UNHCR will conduct regular protection monitoring and continuous advocacy with relevant stakeholders to mitigate and address all risks identified.
