Project Summary

Through the support of village farming schools and the creation of kitchen gardens for women, the project aims at establishing sustainable agricultural production and improving the livelihood situation of the most vulnerable rural populations. This shall be achieved with the distribution of seeds and tools and the teaching of improved cultivation techniques, sustainable agricultural practices and through cooking demonstrations about how to prepare and process nutritious foods. Around 500 women will receive support through kitchen gardens and 500 farmers will be assisted through agricultural field schools. In total, around 1,000 households will benefit from the project.

Needs

The crisis has pushed the country into an economic collapse with disrupted markets and continuously rising food prices. Most households lack access to sufficient food, given that local production has gone back drastically and extremely high prices are preventing households from purchasing even minimal amounts of basic staple foods. Farm production is 26% less than last year according to the latest OCHA report and food deficits continue due to a decrease in local food production and poor food stocks from previous harvests. In the counties of Ikwoto, Kideopo and Geria, in Torit (former Eastern Equatoria) State, the rains so far in 2018 were inadequate and have led to poor agricultural production and in some cases to complete crop failure. Poverty and the dependence on traditional and archaic agricultural production practices (limiting farm size and household crop production to small surplus, subsistence, or sub-subsistence levels) are the main inter-linked drivers of food insecurity. It has been generally recognized that investing in a climate-resilient agriculture is an important step towards building resilient livelihoods.

Activities

To promote resilience and self-reliance and eliminate poverty, activities are required that will enable rural households to achieve food and nutrition security through the reliable and sustainable production of an adequate volume and diversity of agricultural produce. This shall be achieved through the following measures:- Creation and strenthenging of farming groups (village based agropastoralist field schols and women's kitchen garden groups)- Creation of women's kitchen gardens with practical training in land preparation, soil types, weeding, mulching, pruning and harvesting - Creation of village gardens as place to teach the relationship between food production and an efficient management of water, as well as a demonstrative place for watershed and land management practices and the conservation of water and other natural resources- Disribution of seeds and tools and production of seedlings in village nursery beds- Teaching of processing and preparing foods and cooking demonstrations for optimal nutritional value and income- Practical training to the farmers groups and women groups in fruit tree grafting and tree husbandry- Provision of training in integrated pest management (pest/disease control through traditional methods with ash and/or neem leaves) .- Introduction of good practices for post-harvest grain handling and secure storage.- Introduction of measures to create additional income, f.ex. by providing equipment for apiculture or for manual oil seed pressing.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

With each community, a MoU will be signed. The farming groups and women kitchen garden groups will select a representative who will serve as link between the beneficiary groups and the project team. Regular feedback sessions will be conducted with the group representatives and other members of the communities to collect feedback from the beneficiaries.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The representative of each farming group or women kitchen group will be asked to participate in the monitoring visits carried out by the project staff. This way, the representatives will be able to understand the issues of concern and the challenges that arise during the implementation of the activities. The observations will then be discussed either individually with the beneficiaries or in group discussions, together with the representative of the farmers or mothers. A project evaluation at the end of the project will be conducted by an internal or external consultant. The results of the evaluation will be shared with the community. The problems will be discussed and a list of lessons learnt will be established, together with the community.
