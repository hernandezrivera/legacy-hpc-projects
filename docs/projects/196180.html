Project Summary

SECD, during this proposal, aims at increasing resilience and affected people’s access to
basic Education based on the needs assessment conducted by SECD during a full
academic year (9 months) from September to June. Also, the project focuses on the areas with high severity (3,4) and priority schools, through the following HRP objectives: Increased safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth; SECD, through this proposal, is going to increase enrolment rate to formal and non-formal education, back to learning activities, rehabilitation and maintenance of existing education facilities ,
provision of teaching and learning materials, gender sensitive and disabled friendly WASH interventions, provision of school supplies. SECD is planning to provide access to education for students with disabilities and vulnerable students through providing transportation services and improve school environment and provide assistance within the schools in order to encourage them to continue their education and integrate
them with their peers. Enhanced quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth; through training activities for educational personnel such as COVID-19, CSG, INEE Minimum Standards and PSEA and also provision of incentives for them. conducting vocational trainings to 300 students
and support 17 schools divided into 8 schools at Idleb governorate, Jisr Ash-shughur district, sub-districts of (Badama, Janudiyeh, and Darkosh) and 9 schools at Aleppo governorate, Jebel Saman district, sub-district of Atareb and Daret Azze. Due to COVID-19, SECD will be committed to do periodic
sterilization process for classrooms and WASH facilities, awareness sessions for students, training for teachers and educational staff, and provision sterilization materials for all schools.
The targeted number of beneficiaries is disaggregated as 10,200 students (4990 boys and
5210 girls) 290 (0.02 %) of them are CWDs, and 150 teachers and educational personnel (60 males, 90 females) will receive education assistance.
SECD, in case the fund is lower than the required, will prioritize the rehabilitation,
construction and that under COVID-19 activities in Badama, Atareb and Daret Azze where
the severity is 5 with focusing on the most vulnerable groups such as PWDs and poor
students.
SECD is going to support 10,200 students (4990 boys and 5210 girls) through rehabilitate 235 classrooms. The rehabilitation will include repairing 17 schools in the 5 districts of Daret Azze, Atareb, Janudiyeh, Badama, and Darkosh. The rehabilitation works will consider the needs of girls and CWDs in regard to WASH facilities; constructing additional latrines blocks to include 1 latrine in each school for CWDs including ramps. Installation of solar power for 17 schools. Furniture schools. Conducting BTL and COVID-19 awareness sessions 10,200 students. Provision of learning materials (Schoolbags, stationery kit, teacher's kit) for 10,200 students (4990 boys and 5210 girls) with regard to the difference in color and design between males and females and 150 teachers (60 males, 90 females).
Enhanced quality of education through providing in-class trainings to the educational
personnel about COVID-19, child safeguarding, CoC, INEE, and PSEA for 150 teachers
(Females 60%, males 40%), also providing vocational trainings to (300) students (120 males,180
females) aged from 18-24 in the non-formal schools. Provision incentives for 150 teachers and educational personnel. Involve parents in the educational process through
conducting monthly meetings for PTA members
The M & E team will be responsible for ensuring the implementation of strict procedures of SECD in all the implementation processes. to Ensure that the activities and learning materials are compatible with BNFs requirement by implementing satisfaction survey after each activity.

3 - Needs

Based on the last Humanitarian Needs Overview (2023) quality education remains a critical need in Syria, 6.9M are in need of emergency education services, (97%) children, (15%) PWD. (73%) in severe situations,12 % of children do not attend school, and about 2M (6-17) are out of school. Learners face challenges in attending school due to transportation, potential harassment, violence and GBV risks, the use of schools for non-educational purposes and other safety concerns, attacks on schools and military use.Also, Covid-19 outbreak and some diseases forced all schools to move between physical, remote and blended learning modalities.SECD conducted needs assessmen in 5 sub-districts in Idleb and Aleppo, so based on it, the proposal is going to address the following needs: back to learning activities , rehabilitation of existing education facilities as well as establishment of new temporary education centers, provision of teaching and learning materials, gender sensitive and disabled friendly WASH interventions, provision of school supplies.According to the need assessment, many schools in the targeted areas are in a dire need for the rehabilitation since it was damaged due to the ongoing crisis and the absence of the regular maintenance

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

SECD has an absolute commitment to the safety and protection of all children, and preventing harm to beneficiaries. We recognize risks faced by beneficiaries and children, and the difficulties they face in reporting harm theyexperience by the organization will not tolerate our staff, partners or associates to engage in or support any type of sexual exploitation and abuse or child abuse. The principle of ‘best interests’ will guide our actions in response to concerns and breaches of this policy. SECD understands that, in order for the PSEA/Child Safeguarding policy to be well understood and effectively implemented, it is essential that there are high levels of awareness regarding the policy and that staff and others are clear, confident and competent in putting the policy into practice. Disseminate and promote the policy widely. Copies of the policy will be made available and accessible to all stakeholders, both in hard copy and electronically. The policy will be distributed to partners and any other relevant parties beyond SECD to ensure that the policy commitments, principles and practices are publicized and that everyone is clear about SECD position in relation to PSEA and safeguarding children SECD is committed to improve the quality and effectiveness of the assistance we provide and the project will ensure that project staff, IPs and all project activities are accountable to the affected populations, work in line with humanitarian principles and follow the principles


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: The targeted areas are stable, and no large-scale military operations areanticipated, procurement and shipment roads/lines are stable, and relations with theEducation Directorates and local authorities are accessible. Risk 1: COVID-19 pandemicoutbreak in the project targeted areas. Mitigating measures: SECD had already measuresfrom the beginning of the outbreak of the pandemic in the North-west of Syria throughsocial distance, sterilization of the centers and schools. Furthermore, in this proposal willbe distributed PPE kits. Risk 2: Out the blue new wave of displacement, Likelihood:medium Impact: High mitigating measures: The security situation in the project locationswill be followed, and integration with other stakeholders to provide emergency services.Risk 3: Sudden targeting to the project areas: Likelihood: High Impact High mitigatingmeasures: Activation of the e-learning through supporting the teachers and students withinternet packages. Risk 4: Stealing materials such as schools’ assets Likelihood: Low,Impact: high, mitigating measures: MoUs will be signed with the local councils or the localauthorities to protect the materials. Risk 5: Sudden closure of the cross border with TurkeyLikelihood: medium, Impact: high, mitigating measures: Some materials are available inthe local market yet there are missing materials, coordination with the Turkish governmentto allow the humanitarian assistance to be delivered.
