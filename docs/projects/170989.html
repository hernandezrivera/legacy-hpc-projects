Project Summary

The project aims at contributing to improving food access of food insecure vulnerable communities living in Galgudud, Lower Shabelle and Banadir regions. The target population include IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities living in urban areas. The project also targets vulnerable rural communities in food crisis. The target populations have been facing shocks resulting from natural and man-made disasters that affected their livelihood. Before the populations totally recovered from the drought of 2017 and the subsequent floods that followed in the riverine areas (areas perceived to be the Somali food basket), out break of COVID-19 and infestation of desert locust, coupled with yet another flood, worsened the situation for the already vulnerable communities. To intervene the situation the project is designed to reach 16668 beneficiaries in the three regions of Galgudud, Banadir and Lower Shabelle. The project aims at reaching 800 households (4800 beneficiaries) with malnourished children living in the three target regions. Target will be IDPs, returnees and poor host communities. The target households will be receiving monthly unconditional cash transfer of US$55 per household per month for three months.
The project also plans to provide farm inputs to 800 farming households (4800 beneficiaries) with small holdings living in Lower Shabelle Region. Farmers will be given maize, beans and sesame seeds. They will also be provided with hoes for weeding their farms.The project will give priority to those farmers who were affected by the recent floods of River Shabelle. The project is also designed to have 6 secondary canals in Lower Shabelle region rehabilitated in order to improve food production. 648 food insecure households (3888 beneficiaries) will rehabilitate their community secondary canals to improve irrigation. Participants will be provided with conditional monthly cash of US$55 via mobile transfer for the three month exercise. This intervention will target Lower Shabelle riverine livelihood.
The project will also provide skill training to 500 unskilled youth (250 male and 250 female) from 500 food insecure IDP HHs, returnees and vulnerable host communities (3000 beneficiaries) living in Banadir and Barawe. New Ways has training centres both in Banadir and Barawe. The trainees will be provided with conditional cash of US$55 per month for six month period via voucher.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project involves CTP where 1200 households with malnourished children will receive cash transfer of US$50 per month for three months via mobile cash. The project opt for this because of the following reasons:1. The target beneficiaries are vulnerable IDPs, returnees and poor host communities who might encounter security threat if given assistance in kind2. Those beneficiaries who live far away from distribution centres might not be able to access distribution points3. With cash money in their phones they can buy variety of food staff unlike in kind programming where they can only get limited food items.4. distribution of food (in kind) at distribution points may pose health risks amid COVID-19 pandemic

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The project design considered market functionality by adhering to the cash working group's recommendation of quarter three of 2020. The project used MEB recommended the respective regions by the cash working group as of August 2020

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk that beneficiaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services: The project will mitigate this risk by making sure that registration and distribution will be done venues close to beneficiaries (less than 2 km distance). Registration and distribution exercises will be done during day time.2. Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance: Food/voucher distribution venues will be selected areas far from armed groups/military camps. Project staff will be trained on humanitarian code of conduct and consequences of sexual exploitation. Beneficiaries awareness on sexual exploitation will be raised and complaint mechanism will be provided3. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.): The project will closely work with organizations providing protection services in the project area and refer people with protection issues to them.4. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities: To mitigate this risk, the project will develop criteria that will consider people living with disabilities, members from marginalized groups, IDPs, women, boys and girls to be included in the community project committees supporting agency in the project implementation. Women and people with disabilities will also be considered in the staffing.

Please provide further explanation

The project will closely work with the Government in order to build a resilient and responsive system. New Ways Organization will focus both on the everyday routine challenges that project staff face on a regular basis as well as focus on sudden shocks such as disease outbreaks. The organization will work to build the leadership capacity of district and regional state representatives and in this regard will target Government key managers, field staff, community members as well as Community resource Persons so as to empower them to solve problems at their level and build relationships and social networks. The agency will focus on strengthening existing social networks to provide access to multiple resources available in the community and forms of value such as community awareness and reputation that can build and restore resilience to the existing systems. New Ways will use the model of everyday resilience building in the systems of the Government through a combination of absorptive, adaptive and transformative strategies that authorities, staff and community members can use to adopt the system in responding to strains and shocks. This will help in producing a strengthened and more resourceful resilient system infrastructure. The organization will coordinate with the Food Security Cluster and the Cash Working Group.
