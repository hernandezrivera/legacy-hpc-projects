Project Summary

The ongoing crisis in Syria necessitates addressing the education needs of millions of children and teachers, including a significant proportion of CLwD. WVI and partners aim to provide focused and protective formal and non-formal education services across Syria, building on existing programming from an early-recovery lens. With over 2.45 million OoSC, the project aims to increase access, reintegrate OoSC, and enhance the quality of education in vulnerable areas while addressing factors such as economic hardship and inadequate infrastructure.
In NES, Al-Kisrah and Hajin sub-districts will be strategically introduced to address critical gaps in educational access and retention, particularly among vulnerable populations. WVI aims to target 300 HHs, providing education to approximately 500 children from the Al-Hawl camp and 600 children in Kisra who have previously dropped out of school. In Hajin, the project will extend its reach to approximately 1,200 individuals, including refugees, displaced persons, and vulnerable communities. Leveraging existing initiatives in livelihood development, targeting more than 1500 families in agriculture and livestock activities, will make significant strides in increasing household incomes, reducing instances of underage marriage and school dropout rates.
Qualified teacher shortages, damaged schools, and security threats pose significant challenges. Through targeted interventions, including teacher training and psychological care, rehabilitation, and security measures, WVI aims to overcome these barriers and create safe and inclusive learning environments for all children while instilling a zero-tolerance policy for SEA, child safeguarding, GBV, and causing harm.
Community engagement remains crucial, ensuring education services meet standards through local outreach and lifelong learning opportunities, especially for CLwD. Awareness campaigns will reach more than 20,000 caregivers, emphasizing education, health, and CP. Adhering to INEE standards and safe programming principles, the project aims for improved learning outcomes, including BTL campaigns and teacher training.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

Amidst the Syrian crisis, addressing the education needs of millions is vital. With over 7.1 million individuals requiring support, including a significant portion of children, World Vision International (WVI) and partners aim to provide formal and non-formal education services across Syria, focusing on vulnerable areas and populations. The prevalence of over 2.45 million out-of-school children underscores the urgency of the project. Guided by assessments and strategies, we'll increase access, reintegrate out-of-school children, and improve education quality, particularly in critical areas like NES, Al-Kisrah, and Hajin sub-districts. In Al-Hawl camp transition to Kisrah, WVI targets 1100 beneficiaries, providing education to children from the camp and those who dropped out of school. In Hajin, we aim to reach 1200 individuals, addressing factors like poverty and lack of access to education. Leveraging livelihood development initiatives, we'll target 1500 families, reducing instances of underage marriage and school dropout rates. Our holistic approach integrates education, community resilience, and economic stability, creating sustainable pathways for empowerment. Challenges like teacher shortages and security threats are tackled through targeted interventions, including training, rehabilitation, and security measures, ensuring safe learning environments and zero tolerance for harm. Rehabilitating damaged schools and providing diverse educational services aim to address the unique challenges faced by displaced populations, ensuring access to quality education. Community engagement and awareness campaigns further enhance learning outcomes and ensure adherence to safety standards. Establishing Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs) and School Management Committees (SMCs) fosters ownership and ongoing consultation, while incentives recognize the crucial role of staff in education stability. School-based psychosocial support sessions and disaster risk reduction efforts strengthen children's resilience and safety. Contingency plans ensure learning continuity during crises. At the community level, supporting disaster risk reduction efforts and strengthening partnerships with education authorities enhance project impact through ongoing consultation and experience exchange workshops.
