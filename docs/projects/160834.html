Project Summary

GREDO will provide life-saving food security assistance and livelihood support to Internally Displaced Persons, Vulnerable Host Community and Returnees in Budi, Kapoeata North and Pibor Counties in Eastern Equatoria and Jonglei State Respectively. Assistance provided will be in form of crop and vegetable kits, facilitating vegetable and crop farmers to establish farming groups and demonstration gardens, training farmers and farmers groups on improved, sustainable and environment friendly farming practices, post planting and post harvesting surveys, monitoring and referrals of diseases and pest outbreak and participation in Government and FSL cluster assessments, monitoring and evaluation of FSL Activities in target counties. Assistance will also be provided to Fishing communities in form of inputs (fishing kits) and training on improved fishing practices. Fisher Forks will be assisted to form groups through which training will be conducted. The project will adopt in-kind modality and will last for 12 months integrating the FSL intervention to existing GREDO’s projects supported by IOM and UNICEF in Budi and Chukudum (Gender Based Violence, Nutrition and Child Protection) and UNICEF, Plan International supported projects in Pibor County (Child Protection and Livelihood). GREDO will focus on targeting female headed households, unaccompanied elderly, severely sick persons, people with no links to the community, separated children, caregivers of unaccompanied and separated children, women at risk (pregnant and lactating mothers), persons living with disability, Individuals suffering from HIV and AIDS, Tuberculosis, child headed households, people living with disability and returnees whose properties and food stocks were damaged and looted during the conflict. These category of individuals are considered most affected by conflict or natural disasters such as flooding. The response teams will comprise of experienced Food Security and Livelihood officers, Nutrition Personnel and Protection personnel. The team will implement the interventions jointly with other partners’ especially local partners on ground, more specifically ES/NFI, WASH, FSL and Protection actors and in close coordination with the respective clusters. GREDO will conduct in-depth FSL need assessment with the involvement of protection staff and with the participation of the community among the target populations, with consultative community meetings, key informant interviews, focus group discussions with men, women, boys and girls, household level and individual interviews, and observations by the assessment teams at household level and at markets. Post-distribution monitoring (PDM) exercise will be conducted 1 month after the distribution (where possible) by the M&E team to measure the impact of the projects and to determine if the assistance reached the most vulnerable groups. During and after the intervention, a complaint mechanism will be in place. GREDO will use the PDM report for better programming since we will take into account the lessons learnt, complaints raised and recommendations by the beneficiaries. Security and logistical constraints have made it difficult in the recent past for monitoring teams to go back and conduct PDM therefore rapid monitoring will be conducted during the distribution of FSL inputs few days after the distribution. This will help to measure the quality of the program and know the beneficiaries’ satisfaction. In twelve months, the project will directly reach 45560 individuals (29600 Females/15960 Males) with crop and vegetable kits. Out of the 45560 reached, 20,000 individuals will be reached through distribution of fishing kits and training. Four (4) farmers groups and two (2) fisher fork Groups with each group composed of 30 members (15 Females) will be formed and trained as Trainer of Trainers who will be supported to provide training and mentorship extension services to 11100 farmers and 5100 fisher forks in target counties.

1 - Needs

An estimated 296,790 persons (Budi: 69650; Pibor: 128,010 and Kapoeta North: 99130) displaced people, returnees and vulnerable host communities living in displaced settings or integrated into host communities lack access to Food Security assistance or are cut-off from social services in Budi, Kapoeta North and Pibor Counties (FSL Cluster Data, 2019). Eastern Equatorial and Jonglei State have experienced a significant segment of the effects of both July 2016 fighting and current flooding. Eastern Equatorial hosts a substantial number of internally displaced people (Integrated into host community or residing in informal camps). In the State, a high number of returnees are coming back to their original land in Budi, Ikotos and Kapoeta North Juba following a more stable security situation in the county. Families, who were hosted in refugee camps in Uganda, Kenya and the DRC, are returning to their abandoned homes and farms, however, most of their shelters and food stocks are burnt down and their household items were looted after they escaped. In addition, the agricultural land and farming areas were affected by the conflict. According to latest Integrated Phase Classification analysis report, Budi, Pibor and Kapoeta South Counties were classified as emergency (IPC4) food insecure or worse. In the same month, Integrated Phase Classification for Acute Malnutrition placed Budi and Kapoeta North Counties in IPC4 while Pibor County was classified as IPC3 (Crisis). Worst of all, the IPC Analysis Report projects that the three counties (Budi, Pibor and Kapoeta North will remain in emergency phase (IPC4) between September and December 2019 and January to April 2020. This means that population residing in the four counties will be in emergency food insecurity or worse for the next six months. Some of the factors driving food insecurity in the counties include localized conflict, disruption of transport corridors due to insecurity, climatic shocks, pests and diseases, slow recovery from prolonged asset depletion, lack of planting and farming inputs, lack of knowledge and skills on sustainable, improved farming and fishing methods, inflation and sub optimal market functionality. The high level of Malnutrition in the counties is attributed to high morbidity, poor access to health, nutrition and wash services. Based on this numbers, the proposed FSL intervention is lifesaving and will seek to increased food production and enhance access to livelihood opportunities for Food Insecure families in the targeted counties. The interventions will be based on rapid FSL needs assessments preceding any particular response. This will allow FSL teams to respond to the specific needs of the given displaced community, being fully accountable for the identification of the beneficiaries and of their needs according to the FSL cluster strategy. During all interventions, GREDO FSL and Monitoring and Evaluation teams on the ground will collect information from the beneficiaries regarding their needs to be used in the ongoing response, as well as for future reference. Other data sources to be referred during the intervention shall include REACH surveys, IOM DTM, IPC data, IRNAs, FEWSNET, C.F.S.M.A and SMART SURVEYS that already show significant Food Security needs within the affected communities in the four target Counties. GREDO has been in Pibor for the last 5 years implementing protection and livelihood projects courtesy of UNICEF, Finish Church Aid and Plan International. Also, GREDO has worked in Kapoeta North and Budi Counties for the last 4 years implementing Nutrition and Protection Projects supported by IOM and UNICEF. The Organization has offices, staffs, assets and community networks it will rely on to support the proposed project.

2 - Activities

Output 1.1: Increased access to emergency livelihood inputs by food insecure individuals to increase and maintain food production; ACTIVITY 1.1: Mobilization and sensitization of community stakeholders: The exercise will be conducted in close consultation with community members and leaders, representatives from the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry, Beneficiaries representatives and local authorities. This is to inform affected communities about proposed FSL activities, their rights and entitlements, project objectives, duration, expected results, the role of the community, criteria applied to select beneficiaries to avoid misunderstanding, role of GREDO staffs in the intervention and also to seek community support, views and increased participation in project activities. ACTIVITY 1.2: Selection, registration and verification of beneficiaries: The exercise will involve community leaders, chiefs and IDPs representatives, enumerators, GREDO’s staffs, local authorities, women leaders, FSL cluster, and County Departments of Agriculture and Fisheries. Criteria used to register and select the beneficiaries include prioritization of Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees and vulnerable host community households. Also, registration and selection will include vulnerabilities such as Female Headed Households, persons with disabilities, Pregnant and Lactating Women, and the elderly. Persons Living with Disability will not only constitute the beneficiaries but will also participate in consultation meetings in order to obtain specific needs and challenges faced by PLW. Also, PLWs will be among the enumerators and community leaders conducting selection process. ACTIVITY 1.3: Transportation of vegetable, crop and fishing kits. GREDO will collect and transport vegetable, crop and fishing kits from UNFAO warehouse (core pipeline) in Juba to distribution points in Budi, Pibor and Kapoeta North. ACTIVITY 1:4: Distribution of vegetable and crop seeds and fishing kits: GREDO will distribute crop, vegetable and fishing kits to select vulnerable individuals applying UNFAO selection Criteria. Distribution points already will be selected by community leaders, GREDO FSL and Protection staffs and IDPs, Host Community and Returnees representatives taking into consideration security situation, proximity to population areas and distance beneficiaries are likely to safely travel/walk to collect the items. ACTIVITY 1:5: Formation and training of Crop and Vegetable Groups (Trainer of Trainers-TOTs): Four (4) farmers groups each comprising 25 members will be formed and trained in each target county. Training will focus on how to establish gardens and seed beds , land management, planting methods, seed bed management, mulching, soil and water conservation, integrated pest management, harvesting and harvest preservation. ACTIVITY 1.6: Formation and training of Fisher Fork Groups (TOTs): Two (2) fisher fork Groups comprising of 30 (15 Females) members will be established in each target county and trained on net making and mending, fishing techniques including how to protect small fish in rivers and ponds, hygiene and sanitation measures during handling, transportation and storing fish, catch loss reduction, fish preservation (smoking, salting and drying) and packaging. ACTIVITY 1.7: Conduct Post Distribution Monitoring: Two PDMs (one for vegetable and crop kits and the other for fishing kits) will be conducted in each target county through random sampling. PDMs will target 18% of beneficiaries reached in each county. ACTIVITY 1.8: Conduct Joint field visits and stakeholders forum: GREDO will conduct joint field visits together with local authorities, officials from MAF, Community leaders, beneficiary’s representatives to monitor project activities and gather views and perceptions of beneficiaries on the FSL project. Stakeholder’s forum will also be conducted in each target location to review the progress made on the FSL project.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes. The proposed FSL response is a form of protection and is meant to help affectedpopulations to recover and to reorganize their lives. Provision of Crop, Vegetableand Fishing items will ensure protection, dignity, and reduced pressure on familiesto engage in negative coping mechanisms. The Intervention will help at risk womenand girls and food insecure families to cope with risks by meeting their immediatefood needs. Apart from distributing the in-kind items, beneficiaries will be equippedwith skills and knowledge on how to grow their own crops, how to practicesustainable fishing, how to manage harvests and reduce food loss and how todiversify their diets for improved nutrition. Skills and Knowledge acquired duringthe training will be utilized by farmers and fisher groups long after the project hascome to an end. Also, farmers will be trained on seed multiplication to ensure thatthey have their own seeds they can plant during planting seasons rather than torely on aid.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes.The Project is designed with the objective of providing essential Crop, Vegetableand Fishing Kits to Internally Displaced Persons, Returnees and Vulnerable Hostcommunities affected by conflict and natural disaster (flooding). FSL Support andassistance for affected population is aligned to social protection of vulnerable andaffected populations and is within the Framework of ensuring safe and dignifiedreturn of displaced persons to their home of origin. Targeted beneficiaries lost theirassets (household items, shelter and food stocks) to looting, burning ordestruction. Failure to access FSL and inputs is likely to lead to loss of life due tolack of food. Further, food insecurity may compromise the safety, especially forwomen and girls who are forced to borrow or fall to sexual exploitation in exchangefor food. The intervention will enable food insecure families increase their foodproduction and be extension to meet their immediate food needs. By doing so, theproject will ensure dignity, safety, integrity and protection of the beneficiaries.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes. Budi, Kapoeta North and Pibor Counties is by Road or air from Juba. No major insecurityincidences have been reported along the roads leading to the counties. FSLSupplies will be obtained from Juba, Bor and Torit to project locations. The FSLintervention does not anticipate security issues. GREDO will conduct four safetyaudits (one in each county) to obtain information on risks that project staffs andbeneficiaries may encounter during implementation process. 6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes. The project is an emergency intervention aiming at meeting the immediate FSLneeds of conflict affected populations as well as those affected by naturaldisasters (flooding). The response will be conducted in a fragile setting wheretarget communities are enjoying relative peace after the signing of RevitalizedPeace Agreement between warring parties in South Sudan in the Month ofSeptember 2018. Due to conflict, governance structures collapsed in targetlocations while community systems and structures were severely weakened. Assuch, the intervention will only seek to link beneficiaries to existing emergencyservices being provided by other partners in project location such as Wash,Nutrition, Food Security, Health, Protection (Child Protection and Gender BasedViolence) and Education. The aim being to support affected communities torecover from effects of war. Existing community structures such as traditionalleaders, women associations, youth associations and local authorities will betrained to enhance their capacity in disaster risk mitigation and reduction.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

GREDO will strive to gain acceptance from local authorities and stakeholders; follow strict logistics and ﬁnancial procedures; streamline security risk assessments at all stages of program design; gather up-to-date security alerts from various formal and informal sources and adjust our actions. Community awareness of the principles and arrangement of the program, with Community Feedback and Complaint Mechanism. (CFCM) are in place. GREDO staff are equipped with the capacities to negotiate access with the authorities. Access negotiation is based on a strong commitment on the respect of humanitarian principles. Speciﬁc trainings of the key people involved in access negotiation are conducted. In addition to the risks detailed above, GREDO will mitigate the speciﬁc risks such as risks linked to the important variations of the population size in the areas of intervention. Mitigation measures will include advocating with other partners to complement GREDO interventions when necessary. Another risk to take into account is the exclusion of women in project activities. GREDO will ensure increased participation of women in project activities from the start to the end. Community members will be engaged in project planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

This can ensure the continuity of the project outcome. Accountability will be taken into consideration during the proposed action to ensure that the points of view of the affected population and returnees are considered. They will be involved all along the project, from the assessments, to the monitoring of activities, including beneﬁciaries’ selection and composition of kits and implementation of activities. GREDO staffs will be well trained to ensure the needs of the children are assessed. Before the intervention, GREDO will organize a minimum of 3 focus group discussions per location with girls, boys, women, men, elderlies and people with disabilities for the community consultations to ensure community inputs. This feedback will be used to adapt the activities, improve the mobilization and participation of the population, monitor and improve the quality of the work. This approach guarantees safety, dignity and protection of affected people. For each location, GREDO will also create an implementation committee and a complaint committee with members of the community. They will be responsible for the link between affected households and GREDO. They will be able to spread messages, explain the selection criteria, report needs from the community, etc. GREDO will set up a speciﬁc complaint/feedback mechanism adapted to the local context to collect the complaints and feedbacks from the beneﬁciaries and take appropriate action. Considering the context of RENK and Maiwut Counties, GREDO will organize regular feedback desk within each location. In this way, GREDO will be present to listen and register the complaints, to collect information and to answer the questions. GREDO already large experience of accountability to the communities and complaints responses mechanisms, the AAP for this project will be also aligned with the standardized community-led complaints and feedback.
