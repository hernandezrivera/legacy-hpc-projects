Project Summary

Now in its eleven-year, the conflict inside Syria shows all the signs of a complex
protracted humanitarian crisis. Not only have the humanitarian needs increased in spite of the considerable efforts of the international community, but drivers of instability and conflict are growing due to the political situation. While opportunities for people to seek refuge in neighboring countries are reducing, IDPs, who are frequently subjected to multiple displacements, are mostly concentrated in a few (urban) areas, affecting the
socio-economic stability of the host communities and increasing pressure on resources (such as water or land) and services. This has the potential to contribute to localized crises or conflicts, further increasing tensions, and/or leading IDPs and host communities to resort to negative coping strategies.
To support the current urgent needs of stabilization in communities that have maintained minimum security stability, and show the potential of progress toward civil recovery; to resolve some of the lack the opportunity and improve the economy for people in Idleb and Aleppo, Mercy-USA seeks through this project to support vulnerable IDPs population and host community members to contribute towards increased resilience of vulnerable households through sustainable and restorative socio-economic interventions, targeting 300 beneficiaries (150 males and 150 female, 50% HC & 50% IDPs), as below:

3 - Needs

Based on the HNO 2023, general deterioration in access to livelihoods, ability to meet basic needs, availability and affordability of infrastructure and services, threatening the resilience and social cohesion of households. Inflationary trends on key goods and services, widespread low productivity of inputs, low wages, and lack of employment, among other factors, continue to impact households’ priority needs and exacerbate the income-expenditure gap across the country.Households in north-west Syria, 47 per cent of households’ report livelihoods as a need. Data on labor force participation at the household level shows that there is a marked difference in the different areas of controlConsidering that approximately 90% of households in Syria consistently experience a gap between their income earned and expenditure required to meet basic needs, severe economic vulnerability poses increasingly higher risks of reliance on negative coping mechanisms, which include begging. Due to widespread erosion of previous livelihoods and massive displacements, many Syrians are in a situation where their skills are no longer relevant. Many who were skilled to work in livelihoods now find themselves in new areas seeking new employment; other displaced persons come from farther afield and do not always have skills that suit the local economy. IDPs out of sites (9 %) are the population groups with the highest rates of complete inability to meet basic needs with women, PWDs most affected.
