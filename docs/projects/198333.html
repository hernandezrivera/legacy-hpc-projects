1 - Needs

Needs Overview: Northeast Nigeria is home to protection crises in the world (one of the biggest), due to violence perpetrated by Boko Haram (BH) and the Islamic State’s West Africa Province (ISWAP) that has displaced over 2.2 million people, destroying communities means of livelihoods (which is farming) and accessing basic services and humanitarian assistance to some extend impossible. The protracted crisis in Borno, Yobe and Adamawa (BAY states) in Northeast Nigeria results in complex and destructive consequences for women, girls, boys and men who are abducted, recruitment, transported, transferred and expose to all form of coercion. The conflict is dynamic, random and widespread. Female headed and child-headed, separated and unaccompanied children, and adolescent boys and girls, and people living with disability are particularly at risk of all forms Violence. GBV incidents occurring are in the context of sexual slavery and sexual violence as evidenced in the abduction of school girls in Borno and yobe state Northeast Nigeria by the Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs). Women and Girls who were forced and abducted from their homes by NSAGs, many have been raped, forced into marriage and labour, abused physically, sexually and/or emotionally, physiologically, psychologically and worse of all exposed to sexually transmitted diseases, and often impregnated by their captives. Violence against women and girls are widespread and grossly underreported. The conflict affected population are faced with widespread displacement itself, family separation, difficulties and sometimes denial of access to services, limited opportunities to livelihood, breakdown of family, traditional structures and community support mechanisms and stigmatization. Exposure to GBV risks also includes exposure to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). Boys and men are at risk of forced recruitments by NSAGs and at risk of arbitrary detention and exposure to different forms of violence, yet the life-saving services are not readily available in some areas. Increased access to empowerment and livelihood will mitigate the occurrence of survivor. Thus, need for provision of continuous psychosocial support, engagement of women and girls in livelihood (income generating) activities, will encourage women participation in decision making, and ensure we mitigate all risks that might expose disable persons, women and girls "including boys" to GBV.

2 - Activities

Raise awareness at community level on child protection with specific focus on relevant child protection issues including violation against children, GBV and protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) within a given community. Community-based livelihood reintegration support for young boys and girls, and disabled vulnerable children within the community. Improved access to quality lifesaving and well-coordinated GBV response services for survivors and individuals at risk Key activities/referred to service provide; Provide confidential and dignified functional community-based complaint mechanisms for SEA reporting and survivor assistance. Develop/adopt sectorial and modify GBV preventive information and awareness campaigns participatory. Children facing protection risks and children with disabilities, will access case management services. Consistent follow-up and service provision for children at-risk of, or affected by, protection concerns, including case management for GBV and children with disabilities. IDP have increased access to quality emergency MHPSS and protection services. Trained Community-Based Social Workers (CBSWs) (with gender balance) to conduct needs, risk and vulnerability assessment, provision of psychosocial support (PSS), to survivors of abductions, GBV and SEA. Trained CBSWs provide awareness sessions to IDPs, survivors of abductions, GBV and SEA on conflict and displacement-related stressors. Trained CBSWs to teach coping and resilience skills to community members through a Community-Based, gender sensitive, Right-Based Approach, Peer Support group model, with a particular focus on the needs of IDP. CBSWs conduct outreach at camps and host communities to sensitize IDPs and host communities on Complaint and feedback services. Girls impacted by NSAGs have improved access to income generating activities and supplies. Train staffs on gender norms/culture and customs of the the community.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

PPGW will conduct risk assessment to inform development of community feedback mechanisms and develop SOPs for community feedback mechanisms in areas where the proposed project operates. we will also conduct training sessions on protection against sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) for feedback mechanism focal points in a community government structure. After these steps have been completed and structures established, we will implement participatory approach to make sure vulnerable community feedback is taken into consideration during implementation and activities are adapted every two months based on the received feedback and constantly monitored.
