Project Summary

YEDA will target 24,383 individuals in Pibor County (21,751) and Pochalla County (2,632) (GPAA) with cash voucher interventions and in-kind interventions, tailored to the most urgent needs of the flood-affected and conflict-affected population. Particular attention will be posed to prevent negative environmental effects of assistance provided.
The choice of modalities is informed by YEDA’s previous experience, and the need to scale-up CVA as an avenue to recovery and resilience. YEDA has a full operational office and static presence in Pibor town, which allows assistance-delivering to the targeted Counties via road, as well as item prepositioning.
Beneficiary targeting will be guided by the SNFI Cluster criteria of multiple vulnerabilities; PLWs, children <5, the elderly, PWN/PWDs (15%), high number of dependents, combined with multiple displacement, lack of ties with the local community/purchasing power, GBV and health risks associated with SNFI needs.
The in-kind response, implemented in locations with no functional markets, will improve access to essential S/NFIs to 5,526 individuals (27% standard NFI kits, 23% light NFI kits, 50% NFI replacement) and to appropriate and safe shelter to 6,745 individuals (12% standard ES kits, 11% light ES kits, 72% shelter upgrades, 5% shelter reconstruction and rehabilitation). For in-kind response, pipeline items will be requested upon approval of SNFI analysis report by the SNFI Cluster coordinators, in areas without access to local market/shelter material. The transportation from Juba to the field warehouse will be covered by the SNFI Cluster core pipeline, while YEDA will cover the last mile transportation costs.
The CVA, implemented only in Pibor County, will include the establishment of a multi-purpose cash fund for procurement of shelter material (poles/grass/tools) and NFIs from the local market, allowing 2,943 vulnerable people to repair their homes, and ensuring appropriate shelter (60% of CVA for essential ES, 9% reconstruction and rehabilitation), protection and dignity, and NFI replacement (31%). Since in remote areas framing material is not accessible due to protection concerns, the project will include a small CFW component targeting 180 individuals for training in shelter construction by the YEDA local shelter expert, with secondary benefits for host communities (financial empowerment and conflict risk reduction). Wherever possible, shelter materials will be procured locally, using the same system. HLP and CCCM partners on mobile response will be engaged to minimize conflicts over land and shelters, as well as using an integrated response for camp-like settings in order to benefit groups of IDPs, following the HLP due diligence guidelines. Financial service providers will be mapped out in the project sites, and distribution facilitated by YEDA staff. Female heads of household and PWDs will be prioritized, to ensure financial empowerment of intended people and GBV risk reduction.
In line with the Collective Outcome 8.3. “Climate change-related effects including drought and flood displacement are 30 per cent lower in 2025 than in 2021”, YEDA will reach 8,888 beneficiaries with flood kits and 278 community members through awareness sessions on disaster risk reduction. Furthermore, 1 Disaster Management Committees and Climate Change Adaptation Plans per County will be developed.
Coordination with the SNFI cluster coordinators and partners every two weeks, and with other humanitarian partners utilizing humanitarian hubs in Pibor/Pochalla to take part to joint responses, will minimize duplication, and promote integrated interventions, gender and protection mainstreaming, GBV referrals, risk mitigation, and incorporation of health and GBV/PSEA prevention messages. As SFP for GPAA, YEDA will lead humanitarian interventions through the cluster methodology, lead humanitarian coordination and refer need/gaps to ICCG/ICWG. YEDA will also train 2 partners on cluster response methodology

1. Needs

South Sudan is among the top 10 world most vulnerable countries to the impacts of climate change, including droughts and flooding, which have indirect negative effects on peace, security and humanitarian aid delivery (International Humanitarian Summit, 2022).According to the South Sudan HNO report 2023, over one million people are estimated to be in need of emergency shelter and NFI assistance in 2024, including 674,699 IDPs, 363,087 returnees and 352,500 host community members. The need analysis at Country level revealed that three quarters of the South Sudanese people are in need of humanitarian assistance, of which 24% women, 53% children, 15% PWDs.Mobility continues to serve as a critical coping mechanism and source of resilience for population grappling with multiple shocks related to conflict, natural disaster (floods) and diseases (South Sudan HNO, 2023). In GPAA in particular, persistent attacks on civilians by armed cattle keepers, cattle raids and revenge attacks resulted in the displacement of tens of thousands of people, several killings, increased incidents of sexual violence, abductions and looting/destruction of property and resources. Continuous displacement has further depleted natural resources such as timber and grass are causing competition for the limited ones available, also leading to conflict and localized violence, hence further displacement. The markets lack essential items or have become non-functional, making it difficult for the community to access the required items for a safe and dignified life (South Sudan humanitarian snapshot, July 2023). In GPAA, persistent flooding, endemic insecurity (involving the Dinka, Nuer, and Murle communities) and unstable markets have created a state of emergency. This contributed to the atypical corralling of communities into smaller spaces, further disrupting livelihoods, and putting greater stress on available resources (REACH situation overview, 2023). Shelter remains one of the priority needs, as majority of IDPs and returnees are sleeping under the trees, with increased negative effects on their health, protection and psychosocial wellbeing. Those include lack of privacy and protection risks, particularly for women and girls who normally collect the increasingly scarce construction materials form the bushes, and increased morbidity rates, mainly due to malaria and exposure to extreme weather events, and easy transmission of airborne diseases as a result of congestion (South Sudan HNO, 2023). Looting, destruction and displacement also create a lack of life-saving NFIs, especially flood kits and basic items that can protect the most vulnerable people from vector-borne diseases and extreme weather.Affected people in GPAA, in particular Pochalla and Pibor Counties, urgently need access to emergency shelter and NFI materials to prevent further deterioration of their living conditions and of resilience against recurring hazards. For those living outside IDP camps, there are few natural resources available, depleted by multiple displacement and natural/man-made disasters, and often inaccessible to most vulnerable categories.At-risk groups include Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW), children under 5, the elderly and Persons with Special Needs/With Disabilities (PSN/PWDs), due to their higher vulnerability to environmental hazards related to lack of access to essential SNFIs, inability to collect shelter material, and higher vulnerabilities to protection and GBV risks, especially for women and girls. This justifies YEDA’s targeting criteria of most vulnerable households, considering such groups who cannot afford to access shelter materials and cannot afford to pay community members to collect it (labour rates are average $8/day for unskilled labour and $10 for skilled). Finally, findings from various FGDs revealed that the extent of damage on S/NFI differs, which will guide the cash voucher value.

2. Project activities

YEDA will use cash and in-kind modalities in order to target the most vulnerable 24,383 individuals (24% women, 53% children, 15% PWDs) with ES/NFI assistance. For in-kind assistance, activities will include;(1) S/NFI analysis, including protection risk analysis (at least 2 S/NFI partners, one GBV partner, and one health partners) and conduction of community consultations (total duration 2 weeks) and sensitization on disaster risk reduction. The analysis will also include analysis of the existing land rights systems in place and risk of conflict over HLP issues, through discussion with local authorities, host community leaders, HLP and S/NFI partners. (2) Community consultations and establishment of CFRM (3) Formation and training of Village Representation Committees (VRCs, including women/youth/PWD representatives, IDP and host community leaders, and sub-chiefs; 50% female). Training will be on their role and responsibilities during activity implementation, including beneficiary selection, distribution, CFRM, and mediation in cases of disputes, especially over land and property, using the appropriate channels.(4) Beneficiary registration and verification, including CFRM. (5) Request and transportation of loose items/kits from S/NFI cluster core pipeline (6) Local procurement of framing material (wooden poles, bamboo bundles) and roofing material (grass), where deemed feasible(7) Secondary transportation and distribution of pipeline items and shelter material** (8) M&E and reporting: Rapid monitoring at the time of distribution, to record feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries concerning the distribution modalities and type/quality of items received, and PDM within 4 weeks after distribution.***(9) Documentation and reporting to the cluster.For the cash modality, activities will include; (1) Community consultation including protection risk and context analysis, establishment of a CFRM and needs/market analysis, including Covid-19 preventive measures* and sensitization on disaster risk reduction.(2) Beneficiary identification (3) Market/Price monitoring – to update the prices due to currency fluctuation (4) Mapping of service providers for Cash-Based Intervention (cash voucher modality) (5) Community sensitization on cash modalities (vouchers, CFW) supported by community selected volunteers on source of funding, intended purpose, transfer value, CFMs(6) Provision of conditional (Cash For Work-CFW) and unconditional cash voucher assistance through the selected service provider and then YEDA staff**(7) Training in shelter construction(8) Shelter construction and reinforcement/rehabilitation, combined with CWF modality,(9) M&E; Rapid monitoring after cash distribution, M&E (after 1st instalment) by the M&E staff through household visits and FGDs with beneficiaries and later after a month (final instalment) a post distribution monitoring conducted to measure impact and effectiveness***(10) Documentation and reporting to the cluster (YEDA M&E data flow chart), exit activities (case studies, photos, documentaries). NOTES:* At all stages, the VRC members, volunteers and YEDA staff will all be trained/sensitized about epidemics preventive measures (Covid, EVD, cholera, etc.), also with the support of IEC materials in local languages. Gathering of people will be limited to the essential and any activity that involves that will be conducted following the WHO prevention guidelines for Covid-19, including hand-washing stations**Each distribution will incorporate beneficiary sensitization on disaster risk reduction as well as GBV and PSEA sensitization, in collaboration with GBV/protection and health partners in the project locations.***M&E: YEDA may use third-party monitoring/evaluation, by involving a cluster partner, as well as an external consultant to measure effectiveness, efficiency and impact of the intervention, ensuring transparency and quality reporting
