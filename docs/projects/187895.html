Project Summary

This project aims to reach 430,682 target beneficiaries (excluding overlap), while building on the experience, expertise and relationships from its previous project cycles. The goal is to reach the most vulnerable with protection mainstreaming and sustainability at the core of its programme. Medair will focus on rehabilitating and/or re-equipping primary healthcare facilities in areas with high needs, prioritizing hard to reach and newly accessible areas, as well as rehabilitating a maternity ward.

Firstly, Medair will engage in infrastructure rehabilitation and/or re-equipment, then a 12-month programme of capacity building for staff will follow. The community health programme (CHP) will target key health, hygiene and nutrition behaviours through messaging at household level in the clinic catchment population. For the PHCCs rehabilitated and/or re-equipped by Medair in 2021, the supervision, training and CHP will continue into 2022. Medair plans to rehabilitate and/or re-equip up to 6 PHCCs during 2022, 6 PHCCs in 2023, and 4 maternity centers in 2022-2023. Additionally, 3 hospitals will be provided with equipment for COVID-19, including training, PPE for visitors, and equipment to support ICU beds. Healthcare for people with disabilities and initial assistance to GBV will be cohesively integrated. Nutrition screening and treatment will also be integrated – submitted separately as per HRP guidance.

Medair Health officers will conduct field visits and training and existing public sector health staff will provide the services as a sustainability measure. To ensure sustained access to healthcare, Medair will exit the locations at the end of the support period, 12 months after rehabilitation by handover to regional health authorities. 615 health workers and 430 CHWs will be trained in 2022. The training package for both is extensive, and delivered over a 12 month period by Medair staff, qualified MOH staff, and external trainers. Medair provides transport/accommodation costs for trainers if needed. Health worker training comprises 28 days per governorate, covering key gaps (RH (ANC, PNC, FP),mhGAP, PFA, Disability inclusion, NCDs, and IMCI) and COVID-19. CHWs receive 8 days of initial training then 6 refresher days, delivered by clinic. Supervisors also attend M+E days. Topics include ANC/PNC, nutrition, MHPSS awareness, PFA, hygiene, communicable diseases, NCDs, GBV awareness, and disability awareness. CHWs will also identify up to 800 PLWMIs in order to support them with Assistive Devices. Consultation target is calculated based on catchment population, a clinic utilization rate of 1 and number of months.

4 - Needs

After a decade of conflicts, a dwindling economic situation, and now, the COVID-19 pandemic, the Syrian health system remains fragile. The conflict has left near half of the hospitals and primary health centres either not or minimally functional (WHO Emergency Appeal, Syrian Arab Republic, March 2021). 13 million people need healthcare assistance (GHO, 2021). iNGOs have been able to provide assistance with Syrian’s CHW programme’s restart (WHO, Evaluation of the healthy village programme in Syrian Arab Republic, 2007). To date, GOS-Syria has experienced 4 recorded waves of the COVID-19 pandemic with 50,748 confirmed cases with 2,950 deaths (WHO, COVID19 Syria Dashboard, accessed on 18 Jan 2022). 70% of the health workforce has fled the country and the remainder are only able to provide sub-standard healthcare (Devi, Health in Syria: a decade of conflict). Moreover, almost 97% of public health centres struggle in securing a constant presence of a general practitioner (WHO, “HeRAMS 2020 PHC annual report). Since 2020, the medicine shortage in the Syrian market was significant, and basic NCD/CD treatment was absent (“Syrian Government Doubles Medicines’ Prices”; “With Healthcare out of Reach, Syrians Turn to the Black Market for Oxygen”). Inpatient capacity across Syria is severely low (World Bank Dashboard). Syria has only a reported 0.4 HF/0.5M providing BEOC, and 1.2 HF/0.5M deliver CEmOC (WHO, HeRAMS 2020 Hospital Annual Report).

5 - Implementation of activities

Medair will rehabilitate and re-equip 9 PHCCs, 3 Maternity wards, and 3 COVID-19 treatment centres to ensure delivery of Syria BPHS. Priority will be given to clinics in high need areas. Rehabilitation of clinics includes appropriate WASH, privacy, and accessibility for PLWMI. Outpatient consultations are provided with a focus on vulnerable groups including CU5, PLW, elderly, people with MHPSS needs, and PLWMI. Initial management of emergencies and referrals to secondary care are provided, as well as NCD screening, diagnosis and management. EPI services will be strengthened. EWARS notifiable cases will be identified and reported. Accessible and sustainable MHPSS will be provided through PFA and mhGAP provision in PHCCs. ANC/PNC incl. safe delivery planning, family planning, STI treatment, and initial management of GBV cases will be provided. Medair will implement a curriculum for health care staff. The focus will be on meeting key gaps in the current health system: RH, GBV, IMCI, NCDs, PFA, mhGAP HIG, disability inclusion and nutrition. Where security and access allows, a CHP will accompany each supported PHCC. Trained CHWs will share targeted health messages at HH level to improve key health and hygiene behaviours, and identify PLWMI for assistive device distribution. Where possible, joint monthly supervision visits will be done with the DoH. Standardised tools are used to assess service availability/quality. Clinic data and beneficiary feedback will be collected.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

RISK: Exploitation by Medair staff or contractuals. MITIGATION: Medair’s code of conduct, policies forbid PSEA; ensure contractors have or sign codes of conduct; job descriptions being reviewed to include PSEA responsibilities; PSEA responsibilities included in performance reviews; disclosed in summary of code of conduct to affected population; multi-media communication with communities to prevent PSEA and make accountability and feedback mechanisms known; principled response to any disclosed PSEA, abuse, or violence. MONITORING: project documentation; feedback and reporting logs and actions taken. RISK: Exploitation by healthcare providers. MITIGATION: encourage/facilitate use of code of conduct, policies, and continuous training related to forbidding, preventing, and responding appropriately to SEA; inclusive communication with affected population to prevent PSEA and make accountability and feedback mechanisms known; support principled response to any disclosed PSEA, abuse, or violence. 11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Delays in planned procurement and/or importationMitigation: A procurement plan will be completed at start of project cycle to ensure sufficient lead-time for normal operations. Procurement is planned to maximise use of reliable in-country suppliers, transfer of responsibility for import issues to suppliers as they are more familiar and able to manage the process. Risk: Fluctuations in exchange rates, differences between black market and Central Bank of Syria rates and inflation impact suppliers, in turn affecting procurement and budget management. Fluctuating exchange rates also impacting amount of money held in the bank account, causing Medair to lose cash valueMitigation: Monitor exchange rates and inflation, monitor % of funds that need to be in SYP, use rapid procurement processes. Monthly budget and inflation monitoring as well as use of forecasting spending. Appropriate clauses in supplier contracts, for mutual balance, but with clarity that Medair follows Central Bank of Syria exchange rates only and on terms of potential compensations for black market rate inflation.Risk: Planned activities are not approved by relevant Ministries or SARC or approved with but with significant delays or adjusted proceduresMitigation: Access remains an ongoing challenge. Awareness-raising on the project scope and timing to allow enough time for approvals. Maintain engagement with each Line Ministry to facilitate the timeliness of approvals or choices for alternate locations.
