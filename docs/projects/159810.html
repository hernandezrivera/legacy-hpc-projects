Project Summary

While Somaliland has made impressive gains in strengthening its health services, substantial challenges remain for the unrecognized nation. Climate shocks continue to erode the gains made in the various sectors. Persons displaced by the floods, drought and conflict are living in temporary IDP camps across the country with limited infrastructure such as shelter, potable water and healthcare services as well as limited supply of food leading to widespread malnutrition. Floods, drought and conflict are the main underlying factors responsible for the increasing health needs across the country and continue to create suitable conditions for the spread of communicable diseases such as cholera, measles whooping cough and malaria. (WHE Sit report 2018) For instance in 2018 cyclone salgar tore through the coastal towns destroying people’s homes, livelihood and facilities including health.
The proposed project will contribute to the reduction of avoidable mortality and morbidity through the provision of lifesaving primary and reproductive health services to vulnerable internally displaced women, girls, men and boys and drought/conflict affected host communities in the target districts.
COOPI will provide an integrated package of basic life-saving services to highly vulnerable IDP or host-community populations in Burco and Oldwenye. COOPI will ensure that target populations in each identified catchment areas safely access:
-Comprehensive primary healthcare, including health behavior change messaging and community outreach through community health workers (CHWs) designed to reduce the spread of communicable diseases;
-Clinical services will be complemented by an extensive community health component and health interventions will be integrated with nutrition, WASH, and protection services.
