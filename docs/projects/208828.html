Project Summary

The project will address the need for dignity kits for 500 households and GVB services to families affected by the conflict who arrived at Nyala south and North . SAO will further provide protection services to protect 35,000 persons among the people at Nyala south and North in South Darfur State.
SAO will distribute dignity kits to the most affected households at Nyala south and North in South Darfur State, post distribution monitoring for Dignity kits distributed within 3 weeks after distribution. The protection services will include Conduct psychosocial support (PSS) that include psychotherapy (counseling), referral for further treatment if any for the 10 GBV victims during the project timeline, Procurement and distribution 500 dignity kits, Establishment of 2 temporal women and girls Safe Spaces (WGSS ) one at Nyala south and another at Nyala North. Training Sessions on Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) by providing two prominent medical doctors specialized in forensic Medicine. And Support to (CMR) confidential corners at Nyala south and North
Outcome:
Timely to save lives of the conflict affected 35,000 displaced persons in Nyala south and North, South Darfur State through providing dignity kits and protection services to restore their dignity.
Output:
500 households to receive dignity kits, and (500 will receive female hygiene kits) 35,000 persons to receive protection services including female headed HH, persons with special needs, adolescence girls at Nyala south and North, South Darfur State

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Protection is a major component of this project. SAO will ensure GBV is well mainstreamed in each sector and some community level activities related to protection is done. In addition, each sector will ensure that all activities implemented improve the wellbeing of women and children and contribute towards improving women's access to and control over assets created by the project or resources provided by each sector. In Sudan, women and girls are overburdened in domestic labor including preparation of food, fetching water, collecting fire wood, agricultural labor during planting and harvesting, marketing domestic supplies, looking after livestock, preparing flour mills and serving as the sole responsible for child care in the household. Also, women have almost no right to make decisions on the utilization and transfer of the major assets. This project has analyzed all the constraints that affect women and girls and developed strategies that ensure their active participation and benefits. The temporal women and girls Safe Spaces (WGSS) will be put in safe and accessible areas to make sure most women and girls access to the (WGSS) from nearby areas within a distance of 1km, which minimizes loss of time and energy and protects against violence. Also, (WGSS) will be closed around 4 pm to minimize the SGBV risks

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

Besides the strategic choice of service locations, strategic choice of staff can also strengthen protection of beneficiaries. SAO will also be sure to promote peace sensitivity by ensuring that all community groups including IDPs, host community members, returnees, pastoralists, and varying tribal and ethnic groups have equal access to project services by selecting project sites in areas accessible to the different groups and hiring diverse staff. This minimizes the risk of conflict due to competition over social services. The possibility of discrimination by locally hired staff against beneficiaries who do not hold the same tribal or political affiliations mitigated by ensuring that program teams that consist of combinations of locally hired national staff representing all ethnic groups, national staff hired from outside the programmatic area and international staff. SAO have been implementing lifesaving and resilience building actions in Protection sectors in at South Darfur , leveraging resources from donors and addressing a coverage area of around 200,00 persons. Synergies are identified at three levels. There is synergy with other projects in the proposed sectors as well as the different projects currently being implemented by agency members. Overall, the proposed actions will fill significant gaps in services existing in the target communities. Maximum precautions are taken to avoid duplication of efforts. Protection activities will be implemented within the same villages as protection activities funded elsewhere to enhance the impact of all actions. There are other INGOs and NNGO is operating in the same localities, but have agreed to work in different villages or catchment areas to avoid duplication and enhance overall humanitarian service coverage. SAO has been actively involved in the sector coordination led by the respective UN agencies. They will continue its coordination with the line ministries and the clusters to ensure that NGOs are not duplicating resources, effort and geographic areas and to avoid competition over resources. The members participate in the inter-agency joint assessments, monitoring, and evaluation of projects and programs in at Nyala .
