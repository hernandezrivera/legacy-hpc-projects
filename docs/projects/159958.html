Project Summary

Maintain and strengthen the system of data collection in Somalia through IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix (DTM) to support the coordination of humanitarian activities. The DTM provides reliable and up to date information on the number of displaced populations, their location, profile, displacement trends, reasons for displacement, and needs disaggregated by age and sex to better assist and inform the governmental and humanitarian response. The DTM has the potential to be expanded and/or modified to take into account changes in the national context and need to inform durable solutions.
Needs Assessment: Somalia continues to be affected by a humanitarian crisis triggered by natural and man-made factors. While the drought crisis and floods have subsided, the effects of these shocks are still felt across affected areas and residents remain internally displaced. Latest displacement estimates place the number of IDPs in Somalia at 2.6 million people. This continued displacement puts further pressure on areas and populations recovering from previous crises. Protracted conflict-driven displacement, aggravated by natural disasters, limited returns to areas of origin, limited self-sufficiency, and limited access to durable solutions contribute to the continued increase in the number and vulnerability of IDPs. Given access constrains in Somalia, overall IDP figures and needs are calculated through the triangulation of multiple data sources (including IOM’s Displacement Tracking Matrix). Each data source uses different methodologies and indicators which makes the creation of a commonly agreed overview of the situation cumbersome and likely unprecise. In addition, Somalia remains characterized by the lack of systematic displacement tracking tools and limited information on IDPs residing in host communities, potentially hampering the effective delivery of humanitarian assistance to a significant part of the IDP population. As such, IOM intends to assist in the implementation of CCCM Cluster tools and to complement them by filling gaps in displacement-related information through its DTM and ad hoc surveys informing durable solutions. In addition, while multiple actors work in Information Management, an under-served need relates to the reinforcement of Government operational capacities in data collection and Information Management. As such, IOM intends to work closely with relevant Government authorities to reinforce their capacities, including through the implementation of joint surveys, and the provision of technical assistance, as well as the support in designing Information Management tools and products.

Project Activities

1) Displacement Tracking: In order to support an informed and well-coordinated humanitarian response, this action will be twofold: a. Regular collection of data on displacement in Somalia through systematic implementation of the DTM (Baseline assessments). This will include the tracking and monitoring of displacement, production of sex and age disaggregated data, service delivery, and key needs and gaps. IOM will further maintain the implementation of surveys on returns (return intentions) and work with partners to strengthen first alert mechanisms for rapid referral. Finally, IOM will work with the CCCM Cluster and offer its expertise and network to contribute towards the CCCM Cluster Detailed Site Assessment (DSA) tool; b. Regular sharing of information with national authorities and humanitarian partners about the numbers, location, profile, displacement trends, reasons and periods of displacement and needs of IDPs. This will include DTM reports, dashboards, raw data, and maps. Fine tuning of needs analysis will be possible at the request of partners as priorities dictate. 2) Operational support to the Government of Somalia: The project will further provide operational and technical support the Government of Somalia, including at federal, State and district level (MoHADM, NERAD, MRRR, HADMA, JRIA, District Commissioners, Statistical Directorates, etc.). This support will mainly be in the form of joint data collection, data management, and analysis in order to improve the delivery of services and living conditions of IDPs, as well as to better inform and support the achievement of durable solutions. Detailed support activities will be defined according to emergency needs at a given time as well as gaps in data collection, management and analysis capacities of specific actors in specific locations.

Mitigation Measures

Lense 2A: - IOM will ensure that assessments are inclusive of the diverse experiences of IDPs, including those of marginalized communities/minority groups.- This project aims to support service providers by identifying IDPs’ locations, profiles and needs so that they can adapt their activities to the specific needs of the IDPs, including marginalized communities/minority groups.- In order to prevent exclusion, IOM will work, when possible, with Key Informants (KIs) representing all the groups within the community respecting age, gender and diversity. These KIs may include representatives of Women’s Committee, Youth Committee, Elder Committee, Education Committee, as well as gatekeepers, site managers, Women's Community representatives, Community leaders, and other knowledgeable individuals.Lense 2B:- IOM will work towards diversifying its workforce, especially that of enumerators collecting information, in order to ensure its capacity to consult different sectors of the population. This is especially relevant to reach women and girls.- By consulting women, men, girls and boys and disaggregating information by age and gender, IOM will support the tailoring of service provision towards the different needs of IDPs.- IOM requires that assessments be conducted with at least three (3) KIs for a location, each of them representing different groups of the community. - The DTM includes surveys conducted through household or individual interviews, for which enumerators are asked to interview a similar number of female and male head of households.Lense 3A:- Non-State armed groups’ perception of foreign actors in Somalia may result in a risk for Key Informants if they are perceived as providing sensitive information to humanitarian actors. To mitigate this potential risk, IOM will train enumerators on the “do no harm” principle and ensure risks are understood in order to avoid putting at risk KIs working with IOM. - Enumerators recruited for data collection will be from targeted communities, which ensures their comprehension of the local context and risks, as well as their acceptance in the community.- IOM will further use limited visibility material in areas where this could result in a risk for individuals involved in the project.- Enumerators will be required to request for the KIs’ consent prior to collecting any information, ensuring that KIs understand the exercise and willingly participate; while this does not directly reduce the risk, it provides KIs with an opportunity to refuse participation to the project in case they perceive it as a potential risk.
