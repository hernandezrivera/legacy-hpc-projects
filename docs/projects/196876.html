Project Summary

The project will improve access to nutrition services in areas remote areas for children under five and pregnant and lactating women. The project will be implemented in Tal Tamer sub district in Al-Hasakeh governorate and in Hajin and Susat sub district in Abu-Kamal district in Der Ez Zor governorate.
The catchment area in those subdistricts according to OCHA is as follows:
Hajin: 105,177 individuals.
Susat: 32,946 individuals.
Tal Tamer: 49,487 individuals.
2 nutrition mobile teams will provide services in Tal Tamer, while 2 mobile teams and 2 static teams in primary health care centers will provide the nutrition services in Abu Kamal district (Hajin and Susat subdistrict), each team will consist of 4 CHWs and 1 team leader and one nutritionist and 1 data clerk. The total number of teams are 6 teams, the total number of staff is 42.
Each team will identify the list of communities, formal and informal settlements, to be visited, the plan will be reviewed every 3 months.
All the teams will be trained on CMAM and IYCF programs, before starting the activities, and all will be oriented through training on PSEA and safeguarding issues.
The teams will mainly:
1- Provide screening for children under five through MUAC and z-score screening for malnutrition.
2- Refer the malnourished cases to the nearest facility.
3- Screen PLWs through MUAC screening and refer malnourished cases.
4- Train families about using the family MUAC approach.
4- Provide blanket multivitamins to pregnant women.
5- Provide one-to-one counseling sessions for PLWs and caregivers about initiating of breastfeeding, exclusive breastfeeding till up to 6 months, and adequate and appropriate complementary foods 24-59 months.
6- Provide group counseling about the same topics.
7- Provide messages through IEC materials to raise awareness about IYCF and CMAM activities.
8- Follow-up with referred malnourished cases when possible.
The total project target is:
25,127 children under 5, through nutrition activities, and out of the total, there are 5,432 children with disabilities, out of the total there are 12,815 female children and 12,312 male children.
15,009 PLWs, through nutrition activities, and out of the total, there are 3,659 children with disabilities.All the targeted beneficiaries are females.

3 - Needs

More than a decade of a protracted crisis in Syria, women and children were the most affected group due to multiple complexed reasons, the crises led to 5.5 million children and pregnant and lactating women (PLW) in need of life-saving preventive and curative nutrition interventions, compared with 4.9 million in 2021. Almost one in three pregnant women are anemic while almost 90,000 children under the age of five years in Syria are acutely malnourished (HNO, 2021). The results from the only SMART Survey conducted in North-East of Syria give an alert on multiple needs in different geographical areas. The prevalence of combined global acute malnutrition (GAM) reaches 12% in Deir-ez-Zor governorate and 9.4% in Al-Hasakeh governorate, and based on the World Health Organization (WHO) 2018 standards, the combined GAM prevalence was classified as acceptable in Aleppo and Ar-Raqqa governates, whereas in Al-Hasakeh and Deir-ez-Zor the classification is issued as alert. The results further showed alarming levels of stunting among children, the highest in Ar-Raqqa governorate at 27.6%. Furthermore, the findings of malnutrition rates among pregnant and lactating women, especially in Deir-ez-Zoor governorate, reaching up to 20.5%. while the results show the rate of mothers feeding their children as few as 13,2%.

4 - Implementation of activities

The very poor malnutrition rates and poor nutrition practices will be addressed through 4 mobile units in Tal-Tamer subdistrict in Alhasakeh governorate, and Hajin subdistrict in DEZ, and 2 PHC centers which will include a nutrition team in Hajin subdistrict, the teams will provide screening and referral of malnutrition cases in both children under five and pregnant and lactating women, in addition, multivitamins will be provided for PLWs. Education on nutrition practices will also be enhanced, with attention to infant and young child feeding education including community mobilization, initiation of breastfeeding and exclusive breastfeeding till the age of 6 months. IEC and awareness materials will be provided in addition to one-to-one sessions on IYCF practices. The team will be trained before the implementation of the activities, and the activities will be monitored through IPV MEAL department, through field visits, satisfaction surveys, and monitoring the data on a weekly basis, while the program team will analyze the data for further improvement.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1) Staff or patients could be attacked en route to/from the mobile unit locations, the Mitigation: a security assessment will be done prior to planning to any location, any location with increased risk will be excluded. 2) . The risk of aid workers or associated people use their power or influence to sexually exploit or abuse the people they should be serving. Mitigation: -Effective community-based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. - Awareness-raising on PSEA amongst personnel, with oriental training. -Provide information to communities and people affected by the crisis about the NGO, the principles it adheres to, and how it expects its staff to behave. 3) GBV risk: outreach services are often the first and the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, survivors must have safe access to facilities. Mitigation: - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services. -Train the staff on PSS support, in a manner that guarantees the safety to the survivor. - Presence of female staff in outreach teams and fixed facilities increase access of women to services


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project included the SEA risk that could be taken place either between working staff or between working staff with BNFs or even within the BNFs level and between themselves while implementing an activty. IPV has set many mitigation strategies to address PSEA, which include: the PSEA mandatory training and related COC-PSEA and CSG "Child Safe Guarding", which the staff must understand and follow, with the clear clarification that the IPV have zero tolerance with any SEA accidents. IPV has PSEA focal points in the working area and there will be focal points in the project location for any reporting needed or support in PSEA mainstreaming. IPV will do awareness-raising for PSEA which includes community level, working staff level and authority level which could include: brochures and banners, videos, awareness raining messages and hotlines. IPV will ensure that all the working staff are fully understanding the internal reporting and feedback mechanism taken in place in case of SEA occurrence cases. all the project's service providers or contractures who have direct contact with project beneficiaries like children or women must be trained and signed COC. IPV will ensure that during any conducted sessions there will be a female member to ensure beneficiaries safety, especially from children and women.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The inability to find qualified staff with good previous experience , the mitigation is by training the available staff on the selected needed topics. The difficulties to have nutrition supplies in NES due to resolution by UND, the mitigation is to use Fish Khabour border. Activities are totally in DEZ eastern line which considered not a stable area, and one of the less securities in NES, in the case of any individual violence happened "staff being threaten with weapons" as example, it will be reported instantly to a higher level, at the level of the head of field office in DEZ which have a very good relationship with local authorities and the level of HQ, in addition to follow the SOPs inside the facility which will be focusing on enclosing the case, till a higher official solute the problems. If there was a fear of major violence, staff safety should be a priority, and an evacuation process will be done according to the security policy .All centers' staff should attend preparedness training for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents. A continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), and NES forum Flash Updates. IPV will conduct lunching project campaign and information sharing with the targeted communities in the project area to ensure their full knowledge of the proposed activities. This will be held in safe, accessible, not far away from the center to be safe centre to be safe accessed to BNFs
