Project Summary

The intervention aims to enhance the resilience of crisis-affected populations in northern Syria and respond to their needs for the upcoming year, 2023.
This response mainly falls under specific sector objectives 1 and 2 of ERL sector objectives, focusing on improving equitable access to basic services and enhancing access to livelihoods and job opportunities.
The project targets 102,600 beneficiaries who are in need of ERL assistance, this includes people who are directly benefiting from the assistance and those who benefit from accessing rehabilitated infrastructures and enhanced services.
The types of assistance proposed within this intervention are:
- Rehabilitation of infrastructures including roads and markets, and enhance basic services (enhance waste removal/management systems.),
- Provision of vocational and business skills training,
- Support MSMEs to scale up and create jobs,
- Create longer-term job opportunities which are linked with market demand. Also, create short-term jobs opportunities linked to infrastructure rehabilitation. In this activity, we will ensure consultation with the targeted communities and other stakeholders for more informed design, mapping of the services, and coordinating with local and international services providers in the area before implementation. Consideration of enhancing social cohesion will be ensured while designing and implementing this activity.
- Build the capacity of youth and enhance their opportunities to access employment through soft skills training and apprenticeships.
All activities are to take place in different locations in northwest Syria in the northern and western rural of Aleppo and Idleb governorate.
Gender Analysis will be conducted before the implementation of the different ways in which men and women do, or do not, benefit from particular livelihood interventions. This includes the following:
- Gender roles, i.e. ideas around what men should do and what women should be
- What do these gender roles mean in terms of productive and reproductive work, i.e. who has responsibility for care of children, or who must do certain work in agricultural production?
- How do gender roles affect who has authority to make decisions surrounding access and control over resources?
- What are the distinct needs of women and men, given their current roles, or, if those roles were to undergo a fundamental transformation within society as a whole?
PWDs will be consulted during the design and implementation of the project through:
- Focus discussion groups.
- Satisfaction surveys.
- Post distribution/activity monitoring.
- Feedback mechanism.

3 - Needs

The final draft of Syria HNO 2022 estimates that 15M in 2023 are in need of some form of assistance in the ERL sector, about 50% of the people in need are children (under 17 years old) and elderly (above 60 years old). The many relevant reports have highlighted the following: - Economic activity declined by more than half compared to 2011 - the result of fragmentation, trade autarky, and the depreciation of the Syrian Pound. - Deterioration of the HH’s ability to meet basic needs is observed across all population groups. - 58.9% reported they had a job in the last 3 months in NWS. - households across Syria are increasingly dependent on humanitarian aid and external networks. - The top need for 71% of HHs remain food baskets/bread, immediately followed by livelihoods (56%). - The pressure on the availability and affordability of key essential services has been additionally under strain. - 50% of respondents in NWS noted that, despite the availability in the markets, they cannot afford essential items, pointing to the instability and constant fluctuation of prices. - Between 2011 and 2019, the rate of individuals living in extreme poverty increased by 39 percent and there was a 65 percent decrease in purchasing power. - Rising debt has been observed across Syria and across all population groups since 2021, with price increases and loss of income as the two top reasons reported. 4 - Implementation of activities

- Road rehabilitation for 15 KM. This activity will be carried out through contractors and with cash for work modalities. - Removal of 30 tons of solid waste removal and strengthening of a sustainable waste management system for one community. This activity will be carried out through contractors and with cash for work modality. Also, Support three communities with equipment, tools, and materials to enhance waste management service delivery.- Support 800 MSMEs to be established/expanded. This activity includes mapping and assessment of MSMEs, identifying the needs, providing training, and coaching, and providing financial support. - Provide 1000 beneficiaries with vocational and/or business training. This includes labour market assessments, career orientation, and vocational training and /or business skills training. - Create 500 longer term job opportunities which are linked with market demand.- Create 600 short-term job opportunities with 6 months duration in average and these job opportunities are linked with enhancing basic services delivery and rehabilitation of basic infrastructures. - Rehabilitation of 5 markets. This activity is to be conducted through contractors and with cash for work modality. - Capacity building for 600 unemployed youth through the provision of soft skills training and market-based apprenticeships opportunities. - Market functionality will be assessed in the targeted areas.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Children are left at home with an unqualified caregivers during the daily training hours for the mothers.Mitigation:- In each training center there will be a child friendly spaces with a trained and qualified caregiver.Risk: women participating in the vocational training are exposed to GBV. Mitigation: - Ensure safe transportation of women to/ from training centers.- Organize separate session for women, and make sure of contracting with female trainers. - Ensure that multiple feedback and complaint mechanisms are in place and easy to access by all beneficiaries.Risk: Cash for Work beneficiaries working on rehabilitation of roads are exposed to risks of land mines explosive remnants. Mitigation: - Provision of Risk Education sessions to CFW beneficiaries, mainly Mine Risk Education when undertaking activities deemed to be risky.- Coordinate with partners concerned with Mine Action about the safety of the specific area in which the rehabilitation activities take place. Risk: Escalation of the security situation. Mitigation:- Prioritize vocational training in relatively stable areas. - Project implementation through partners, whose staff are embedded in the community and maybe able to retain access for a longer period, particularly for CFW activities. - Ensure there is a process established that allows consulting the communities on how to proceed with the proposed response in a deteriorating security environment.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The project has the following strategy to prevent/mitigate any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse:- Ensure that all newly recruited local staff and members of the affiliate workforce are provided with the knowledge and skills to handle SEA issues.- Make sure that all staff sign the Code of Conduct declaration. - Ensure that PSEA focal points are appointed in each targeted area.- Strengthen community engagement and awareness-raising and make sure that people of concern are aware of their rights and entitlements.- Coordinate with other actors on the ground and design feedback mechanisms for receiving complaints from communities and partners.- Include PSEA indicators in the MEAL tools to be monitored on a regular basis.- Ensure that all staff are enrolled with PSEA training.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Issues of HLP in terms of roads. Assumption: The rehabilitated roads are not new ones. Mitigation: All the roads will be repaired and rehabilitated, not constructed. Risk: Cash and/or assets assistance may be misused, stolen, or diverted. Assumption: All cash and asset assistance provided to the beneficiaries are used to cover their basic needs and for income-generating activities. Mitigation: - Conduct reference checks for BNFs in coordination with local authorities. - Limiting knowledge of cash movements and smaller/more frequent transfers.- Varying distribution days and locations.- Screening all vendors.-Agreements with BNFs.- Post-distribution monitoring and follow-up.- Ensure proper complaint and feedback mechanisms. Risk: Unintentional tension with community members as a result of beneficiaries’ selection.Mitigation:- Ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation, and that this is openly documented and made transparent.- Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, to the local authorities and communities concerned.- Publicize the selection criteria.- Make sure that feedback mechanisms are in place. Risk: CFW may cause disruption to local labour markets.Mitigation:- The wage rates will not be above the average level at the targeted community.- We will avoid implementing CFW at times when they could compete with normal labour opportunities, e.g., harvest time.- We will observe the market before.
