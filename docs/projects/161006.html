Project Summary

As stimulating in the Health Cluster framework/objectives and situation analysis, the project will focus on the three areas, to Reduce excess morbidity and mortality of epidemic prone diseases and health insecurities, improve access and scale-up responsiveness to essential healthcare needs of the vulnerable populations and increase access to services for survivors of SGBV, disabled and mental health disorders by focusing on the major causes of morbidity and mortality through the establishment of health facilities in Yirol East, Yirol West, Aweirial and Pibor (Jebel Boma). The project will focus on major causes of morbidity and mortality through delivery and support to 7 health facilities in Yirol East, West and Aweirial and 5 in Pibor (Jebel Boma) (2 static PHCUs and 3 mobile clinics). The aim of this project is to Prevent, detect and respond to epidemic prone disease outbreaks and promote WASH in health facilities in conflict and flood affected vulnerable populations of four counties throughout the life span of the project. SSUDDHAS project will target a total number of 25,000 women, men, boys and girls, including people affected by flood in Pibor (Jebel Boma) and IDPS from Rumbek North currently residing at Yirol East, who are at risk of disease burden, malnutrition, starvation as result of late and destroyed food and WASH related sanitation and hygiene deficiency. SSUDDHAS will ensure that quality health services through static and mobile operation is provided and delivered to women, children and men, including elderly and people with disabilities, training of health and medical staff in order to provide quality health services, supplies of adequate and timely distribution of medical supplies to all health sites. The project provided Vitamins A supplementation to children less than five years, pregnant and lactating women .ANC and PNC services will be provided and many community mobilization, sensitization and engagement will be carried out during project implementation to create awareness on diseases prevention. The integrated mobile clinics will ensure immunization coverage will increase and referrals of the critical cases to the next level of health care will be greatly improved and reduce mortality rates. There will be consultation with CHD, SMOH, community leaders and other relevant stakeholders to ensure smooth running of the project and capacity building to the government staffs to ensure the sustainability of this project. IDSR will be timely submitted to the MOH and health cluster to ensure timely planning of health intervention in order to reduce diseases morbidity and mortality. The project will train health workers on infection control to ensure that they have the knowledge on how to prevent and control the spread of preventable diseases in the counties. The project will timely submit weekly, monthly and quarterly report to the relevant authority in the counties or national level. SSUDDHAS aims is to strengthen the existing surveillance system to pick the first case and timely raise the alert.
Response modalities will be static, fixed and mobile response in an integrated approach.

1 - Needs

Due to the worsening situation in Eastern Lakes and Boma regions,despite the current underway peace negotiation between the government and the opposition forces, many people are facing humanitarian crisis in the regions and it is expected that,should this peace not hold any meaningful compromise,the country will likely slide back to all out war, and this will affect number of people, particularly women and children in a country where health services are limited,health and medical personnel are not available or taken up by other medical duties where they earn their bread by the of the day.the current climatic condition related to late plantation due to insufficient rain fall in Yirols and the current devastation flood that has displaced and affected thousands of people from their homes in Jonglei State,will have negative impact on the lives of the women and children, posing great risk of disease burden,hygiene and sanitation related condition,cholera, malaria and dysentery and other complications alike. The IPC classified Yirol East,Aweirial and Yirol West in the category 4 (emergency) and Pibor in category 3 (crisis) acute food insecurity as made possible by rain delay,the economic turmoil,displacement and returns of IDPS back to their villages and the long term conflict in the country.In Greater Yirol, regardless of the presence of CUAMM,the main HPF partner,and other health partners,including SSUDDHAS,health condition still very dire.Women and children travel 4 hours to access health services in risky and insecurity environment where women and young girls are exposed to risk of sexual violence,rape and abduction of children or attack by wild animals.This exacerbated by lack of qualified medical personnel,weak institutional policy,inadequate supplies of medical equipment and drugs,paralyzed infrastructure,poor road network and security within the region.The rainy season,when it effectively sets in,is likely to result in increased incidences of human diseases such as malaria and acute watery diarrhea that will affect productivity and deplete savings in the form of increased medical expenses–this will further contribute to household vulnerability and reduce resources available for purchasing food,(IPC South Sudan report).In Jebel Boma County where the world bank is supporting CARE through only 3 health facilities that do not provide enough health and medical coverage, have been one of the locations affected by the on going flood in Jonglei State, lack of medical facilities, high rate of malnutrition, lack of clear drinking water as there is no WASH partner on the ground.Families are engaged in less desirable feeding practices including restricting their meals to an average of once a day comprising only vegetables, wild fruits including mangoes and shea fruits which are already out in May, adults prioritizing children, skipping meals and going to bed hungry.Women and children under 15 years are affected including lactating women and elderly. The ongoing humanitarian catastrophe has put high pressures on the host communities and stretching the resources of the local health system.In addition to these crisis,around 75 health facilities were shutdown because of reduced incentive by the Ministry of Health, making it difficulties to reach women and children with health services.SSUDDHAS is one of the strong emerging women-led NGO working in Yirol supporting health,protection and livelihood activities. SSUDDHAS was supporting 7 community-supported health facilities, but due to resources limitation, SSUDDHAS is now supporting only one health facility in Tinagau payam.This facility is located 7 hours from Yirol hospital and 5 hours from Nyang payam where other health facilities provide limited services supported by CUAMM with support from HPF.With this funding SSUDDHAS will continue to support Tinagau & 2 static facilities and 2 mobiles in each of the counties (Yirol East, West, Aweirial and Jebel Boma) with quality health services.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Through the environmental and social management plan for SSUDDHAS, the accountability to affected population using Primary health Care approach Commitments on Accountability to Affected Populations, which means that SSUDHAS will meaningfully engage affected people at every key stage of their work (assessment, design, implementation and monitoring).

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project specifically targets the most vulnerable members of the community who have been displaced; including IDPs, returnees, those affected by natural disasters like floods. The aim is to enable them recover from the damages caused to them by the prolonged conflict. The target of beneficiaries will focus on the most vulnerable groups among the IDPs, returnees and host communities.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project implementation involves community participatory approach in which all stakeholders including CHD, SMOH, community leaders, community networks, youth groups and women groups, state authorities like RRC and other relevant authorities/chiefs are involved from project inception, implementation, monitoring and reporting.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project is aligned to national objective of BHPNS whose objectives are to contribute to save lives of the vulnerable populations affected by the crisis against diseases and malnutrition and destitution more specifically in contributing to the resumption of most appropriate equality health care program, supplying enough drugs supplies, provide the SGBV services, identification of people with disabilities, and provide report such as IDSR, EWARS and monthly report

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The general security situation is gradually being stable as the inflow of returnees is being witnessed across the counties. The conflicts often occur when one ethnic group enters territories of other groups, competing over scarce resources such as land and water necessary for cattle grazing. The project is tailored in a manner that encourages the participation of CHD, SMOH and have ownership for health care services in the four counties.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Sustainability is hoped to be retained through continued learning and sharing with grass root stakeholders who will remain in their locations after the project life span ends. At the end of the project. SSUDDHAS will organize close out meeting that will share best practices and lessons learnt during project implementation. Together with the community SSUDDHAS will draw out exit plan that will be shared with the county authority, community leaders and other partners. The idea is to draw the attention of other actors who might take up and sustain the project. Throughout the live of the project SSUDDHAS will maximally engage the community at all levels such that they will be able to facilitate and sustain the Primary Health Care activities in the community when the project closes out. The formation of community mobilization group and their work become very crucial and as the project phases out they will remain in the community and act as community monitors, continue with their activities especially among the Payams and Boma’s who have existing institutions and jurisdictions.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

1. Land conflicts: The static and mobile clinics need land and yet the project has no provisions for provision of land. Therefore through the processes to get land for the health facilities, conflicts may come up. This risk will be addressed through signing of MoUs with SMOH involving land owners, local authorities and SSUDDHAS. 2. Ethnic tensions and violence: South Sudan has many ethnic groups with history of ethnic violence and therefore any provocations can cause tensions and violence especially in cases of exclusion of minority groups. This risk will be addressed through reliance on the legally existing administrative boundaries where chiefs and local government will be involved in identifying beneficiaries based on administrative boundaries. 3. Sexual abuse; This may happen during interactions of the beneficiaries, the project and communities where people of the opposite sex may physically, mentally or psychologically act in a manner that sexually embarrasses the other. This risk will be addressed through sensitization, grievance redress mechanism and norms
