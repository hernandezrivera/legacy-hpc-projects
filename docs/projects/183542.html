Project Summary

The project aims at securing sufficient and safe drinking water quantities for 1,655 households (7,628 individuals) of the targeted communities and families living in Area “B” and “C” of the West Bank for household purposes, including 3,748 women and girls in two locations namely in Jammala and Rujeib in Ramallah and Nablus Governorates respectively, in addition it aims at improving WASH conditions in 10 public schools in Nablus Governorate serving 3,087 students (1,753 males and 1,334 females). The project also aims to improve WASH governance in cooperation with the Water Sector Regulatory Council (WSRC) in collecting specific WASH data from 270 WASH service providers in the West Bank. This data will provide updates for preidentified vulnerability indicators such as water consumption, water price, water quality, wastewater management…etc. Such indicators will allow the humanitarian and development actors to develop their WASH programs to respond to critical WASH vulnerabilities. In total, this project directly serves more than 10,715 inhabitants (5,633 males and 5,082 females) 188 persons out of them are with disabilities, on other hand, more than 2,980,808 persons will be indirectly benefited as a result of conducting early warning indicators assessment in the West Bank.
The initiative responds to needs highlighted in the WASH 2021 HNO and it is also fully in line with the oPt. Response plan, contributing to the WASH Cluster Objective 2: Ensure equitable, affordable, and sustainable safely-managed access to sufficient WASH services for the most vulnerable Palestinians communities as well as contributing to WASH Cluster Objective 3: Strengthen WASH preparedness and response capacity to the most vulnerable Palestinians to reduce threats and cope with emergencies and shocks. Access to safe WASH service is a main concern in the West Bank, where according to WSRC more than 500,000 Palestinians in the West Bank are receiving less than 50 l/c/d through the piped water service and according to PCBS more than 60% of the households in the West Bank are not connected to sewer services.
Proposed interventions and priorities were coordinated with WASH cluster coordination team, area focal points, technical focal points and governmental counterpart focal points, the initiatives have been endorsed by PWA, WSRC and MoE, it is in line with the PWA Strategic Plan (2017-22) and in compliance with the Water Master Plan for Area C. The action is in line with the Sustainable Development Goals, specifically contributing with “Goal 6: Ensure access to water and sanitation for all”.
Further, the initiative allows to reduce and more effectively address the core vulnerabilities of communities and educational institutions in need, improving WASH services for unconnected and underserved communities in area “C” and “B” of the West Bank, precisely in Jammala in Ramallah Governorate, Rujeib and 10 public schools in Nablus Governorate as well as improving WASH sector governance in West Bank communities.

1 - Needs

WASH HNO 2021 indicates that more than 1.6 million Palestinians are suffering limited access to WASH services in the West Bank and Gaza and according to WSRC more than 500,000 Palestinians in the West Bank are receiving less than 50 l/c/d through the piped water service and according to PCBS more than 60% of the households in the West Bank are not connected to sewer services, with the spread of the COVID-19 since March 2020, both communities and service providers have been put in a difficult position considering their limited means to obtain necessary water and hygiene material to practice and enforce preventive measures. Recently, in August 2020 PHG has participated in the WASH vulnerability mapping assessment of 850 communities in the West Bank which was conducted by WASH Cluster in cooperation with Cluster partners, PWA, OCHA, and PCBS also PHG and ACPP have conducted a thorough needs assessment in 2020 in West Ramallah communities. The assessment covered 30 vulnerable communities and schools. It was based on; field visits, interviews, focus groups and questionnaires with potential beneficiaries, local authorities and community-based organizations including women CBOs. The assessment was completed in coordination with the PWA and WASH cluster. Following to continuous coordination with PWA, WSRC and MoE regarding the needs and priorities of WASH services improvement in marginalized communities and schools, and reference to WASH vulnerability mapping assessment 2021, it was found that there is a need and priority to rehabilitate and expand the water network in Jammala and Rujeib in Ramallah and Nablus governorates respectively, and by reviewing the history of WASH-related problems in Jammala village, after visits and coordination with the local authorities, it was found that pollution and health incidents have been accrued in 2019 were caused by the leakage of sewage water from cesspits to the public water network, which is more than 35 years old, and the water losses from water network reaches to 40%. PWA has been already started partially bidding to rehabilitate parts of the water network in 2021, and this project aims to complete the rehabilitation works of the remaining parts of the water network in Jamalla including water transmission pipelines, internal distribution network and house connections, the same for Rujeib as endorsed by PWA to rehabilitate parts of the water network especially in areas not connected to sewage network in the community to reduce potential pollution from cesspits and to improve WASH services conditions.At schools’ level, and in coordination with the Ministry of Education regarding WASH needs and priorities in addition to WASH assessment in schools conducted by WASH cluster indicates that 10 schools in Nablus Governorate area are in need of rehabilitation and installation of new sanitary units to improve WaSH conditions by rehabilitating existing sanitary units and building new units. Moreover, the available WASH information system focuses on service providers level without detailed updates about access to WASH services on community level, the lack of early warning indicators that could facilitate the humanitarian response and the lack of emergency preparedness system, also local authorities suffering limited capacities in regarding the data collection and analysis of the WASH sector which is affecting the humanitarian and development WASH actors’ capacities in planning the required WASH interventions.In these communities, water insufficiency can affect negatively impact HHs especially on women who prioritize the use of water for domestic uses rather than personal hygiene, generating negative protection impacts increasing and contagion of COVID. Increased access to WASH facilities, such as water networks and institutions WASH facilities, stands as the humanitarian response to improve communities living conditions. Lack of WASH facilities affects also the hygiene and dignity of women and girls during their menstruation, critically jeopardizing their sense of privacy and personal safety

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

PHG aim with all of its projects is to ‘Do-No-Harm’ and to maximize positive impacts. In conjunction with this policy, PHG has ensured these practices by focusing on female heads of household, empowering the most vulnerable of families. By doing community outreach, PHG is able to help normalize female participation in public spaces. By providing home visits involving female evaluators, women can feel more safe in their own homes. PHG will secure with community representatives and local authorities safety and protection methods and measures, especially at a time of the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic at the community and school level by adoption of restricted health protocols while providing service, working in the field and during meetings including social distancing and prevention tools to reduce The spread of COVID-19. PHG and community representatives will conduct a thorough risk analysis for proposed activities in each targeted location especially in area “C” to identify potential risks and propose mitigation measures, including: - Community legal status: to assess if there are military orders or other legal concerns, and to ensure that the proposed interventions will not trigger the community legal case in a harmful way. - Community location related risks: to assess the community surrounding risks, like the existence of settlements, military base, firing zones and bypass roads, and to ensure that the proposed interventions will be applicable and operational in such context, not exposing beneficiaries to further risks. - Relations and sensitivity inside and outside the community: to assess the different cultural and social relations inside the community and in its surrounding, to ensure that the proposed interventions will not increase the social sensitivity or tension between the community, households, and with the surrounding communities. - Type of activity: to assess the community capacities and needs to follow up on activities such as the communities’ ability to operate and maintain the water networks which can contribute to the sustainability of the delivered assistance. In addition to doing no harm, the participatory approach contributes to the safety and dignity of beneficiaries. In order to further inform its assessments and implementation, PHG continuously works on understanding gender roles and household dynamics. By doing so, PHG believes that the organization mitigates the risks of doing harm. PHG has in place mechanisms to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse by all staff. All PHG staff have the obligation to adhere to the organization code of conduct. PHG will ensure that its activities, especially connecting communities with the pipe water service, will not cause overcrowding of the limited water sources and will not cause competition between the communities regarding water supplies, through the coordination with PWA and water service providers. Moreover, and in cooperation with community representatives, PHG will ensure that the project fully covers the water needs of communities and households, avoiding more tensions between the community families and other surrounding communities. Safety of the students of targeted schools will be improved, through increased adequate access to water and sanitation facilities.The proposed project ensured community participation -promoting the equal participation of the different groups in the community– since the identification of the needs and the most suitable and equal solutions, resulting in increased empowerment at the individual and community level. Particular attention was given to input/feedback provided by women, whose participation is strongly encouraged in any decision-making process. Whenever possible the implementation will be led by community members through a participatory approach, nevertheless, the project will also instruct subcontractors, suppliers, and service providers to implement all needed measures to meet “Do No Harm” requirements.
