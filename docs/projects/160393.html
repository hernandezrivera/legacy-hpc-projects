Project Summary

This project will be targeting 6,000 households approximately 36,000 men, women, girls and boys in Guit and Pigi/Canal Counties. The project will reach 20,000 individuals with safe water, 3,600 individuals with an improved, secured and gender appropriate sanitation facilities, and 36,000 individuals including those reached with safe water and sanitation facilities with hygiene promotion messages. The purpose of this project is to contribute to the WASH Cluster objective (CO3): Provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, returnees and host communities by addressing their vital needs, public health and protection issues through an Accountability to Affected Population (AAP), community approach to target the acute vulnerabilities based on inter/cross - sectoral evaluation and analysis. These specifically will be achieved by:
- Comprehensive assessment and rehabilitation of water points in locations with dysfunctional water points and high needs and severity levels;
- Geophysical survey and drilling of new water points in locations with newly arrived IDPs or returnees or high severity level yet insufficient or no safe water sources;
- Repair or installation of solarized SWAT system for locations where only surface water is the best option for consumption and are with high severity levels;
- Assessment and construction of blocks of emergency latrines in IDP settlement areas or for new returnees;
- Repair or construction of institutional latrines especially in schools, health facilities or nutrition centers;
- Hygiene promotion – awareness raising to create good sanitation and hygiene practices through promotion of CLTS where appropriate and PHAST methodologies through trained community hygiene promoters and facilitators;
- Provision of WASH NFIs, hygiene kits and menstrual hygiene management kits to vulnerable persons among the targeted groups.
Cordaid owns a mechanical drilling rig which is stationed at Bentiu-Unity state as well as a manual(village) drill machine and a complete unit of SWAT system which are stationed at Malakal- Upper Nile state. Cordaid has field offices in 10 locations which include: Bentiu, Malakal, Wau, Bor, Torit, Yambio, Kapoeta, Chukudum, Nimule, Koch and head office in Juba, coordination efforts with national clusters and sub-national state clusters will be enhanced in this project, including complementarity and integration of activities with other implementing partners/actors and Cordaid own Health, Resilience and Strategic partnership Programs. The well equipped field offices will enable us to respond in a timely manner during emergencies (e.g. new IDP and returnee movements), and to support more durable solutions in the recovery process.
Cordaid has vast WASH experience and knowledge with well trained technical staffs in borehole drilling as well as SWAT system installation and in 2019 drilled 6 boreholes, installed 3 SWAT systems, constructed 10 blocks – 5 stance emergency latrines along with hygiene promotion activities complemented by FSL (Cash for work, conditional cash grant, distribution of seeds and tools, vegetable school gardens, etc.) in Guit and Pigi/Canal Counties. Whereas in previous years, 2 SWAT systems solar powered were installed in Wau Shiluk and Dethwok respectively thus, Cordaid in this project will use its experience and knowledge of the areas to ensure locations with severity levels 3 & 4 are reached with required services in a timely manner. Sphere standards and AAP will be used throughout the project cycle to meet the immediate needs of the affected population.
This response will also contribute towards decrease in proportion of population facing IPC 3&4, reduction in excess morbidity and mortality rates of epidemic prone diseases (Diarrhea, ARI, Measles etc.) and Mitigation of WASH-related gender - based violence as well as enhancing recovery and resilience in the activities.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has faced mass population displacement and near economic collapse after years of conflict since 2013. As a result, livelihoods and trade are destroyed. According to OCHA 2019 humanitarian overview, more than half of the population require emergency food assistance, nearly 2 million people including young children, pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers are at the risk of acute malnourishment. Six million people need help in accessing safe water and hygiene. South Sudan in the first quarter of 2019 saw significant rise in health issues and there is a further concern of Ebola due to the weakened health care system.Guit and Pigi/Canal are counties of Unity and Jonglei state have an estimated population of 33,004 people and 99,068 people, respectively, according to 2008 census, South Sudan National Bureau of Statistics (SSNBS). According to the recently released IPC report of September 2019-April 2020, Guit and Pigi/Canal are classified in IPC4 and have the likelihood to remain in the same level or may drop to IPC3 in the first quarter of 2020. The report identified access to WASH services as one of the underlying factors in its recommendation for locations in IPC 3 & 4. The WASH Cluster in its severity mapping classified, Guit and Pigi/Canal to lie at severity level 4 for prevalence of GAM for children between 6-59 months, incidences of WASH related diseases (AWD, Eye infection, Respiratory and are with likelihood risk of cholera outbreak), and access to improved safe water source. REACH fact sheet for greater upper Nile for June/July 2019 indicates that in Canal/Pigi county, only 13% of HH reported having safe access to an improved source of drinking water as their main source, 3% HH reported having access to a latrine (Private, shared, or communal/institutional). An assessment conducted mid this year by Cordaid revealed similar conditions in Pigi/Canal- mostly IDPs and returnees in dire conditions. Whereas in Guit county; 32% of HH reported having safe access to an improved source of drinking water as their main source and only 9% HH reported having access to a latrine (Private, shared, or communal/institutional).Guit and Pigi/Canal have been hosting IDPs since the onset of the conflict in South Sudan. The fluid movement of people has been an ongoing challenge in the counties with their relative stability. With the signed peace deal and the hope that the transitional government holds, more people are expected to return from neighboring countries like Sudan, Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia and even from within South Sudan. The arrival of new returnees in addition to the existing IDPs and returnees will exacerbate and worsen the existing conditions even further by putting more pressure on already stretched services.Cordaid will therefore respond in a timely and equitable manner to avert this situation through provision of dignified and critical cross-sectoral WASH services to meet the basic needs of the populations in Guit and Pigi/Canal who lie in severity level 3 & 4. Cordaid is currently carrying out needs assessment in the locations of its project intervention, results which will be fed into the implementation of the proposed activities in 2020. CORDAID is well placed with a mechanical drill rig, manual village drill rig and a complete unit of SWAT system – all of which enable timely response to immediate needs of the affected population.

2 - Activities

The proposed interventions will be timely and appropriate – the affected population will be enabled to use and manage the cross-sectoral WASH services in a sustainable manner. The following activities and outputs will contribute to WASH Cluster objective (CO3): To provide timely/sustainable, equitable access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene for IDPs, host communities and returnees through;1.1. Coordination with WASH cluster at national and state level, and with other actors on the ground to ensure no duplication and promote synergy and/or integration of services on the ground.1.2. Comprehensive borehole assessment and rehabilitation of 15 dysfunctional water points1.3. Geophysical survey and drilling of 8 new water points fitted with hand pumps and solarization of 2 water yards.1.4. Installation of 2 solar powered SWAT system for locations with only surface water source and high severity levels1.5. Training and equipping of 30 community hand pump mechanics and water system operators – at least 20% are female – with skills and tools on basic hand pump and water yard maintenance and operation.1.6. Training and equipping of 25 water management committees – at least 60% female – with skills and tools for managing community water points.1.7. Construction of 60 stances of shared household emergency latrines for new IDPs and returnees settlements 1.8. Training and equipping of 50 community hygiene facilitators/promoters – 35 females and 15 males – with skills and tools for raising awareness for good sanitation and hygiene practices in the community.1.9. Formation and training of 4 children health clubs targeting 1,200 children in 4 schools – 50% are girls and children will act as change agents in the community.1.10. Conduct 3 rounds of community and household hygiene awareness campaigns/events per week and house to house visits 5-6 times a week by community hygiene facilitators and promoters – 36,000 individuals targeted, 60% females in attendance.1.11. Provision of WASH NFIs – 2 buckets for water collection and storage, soap for laundry and personal hygiene, etc. acquired from the WASH cluster core pipeline to new IDPs and/or new returnees1.12. Sensitization and distribution of menstrual hygiene management kits to vulnerable women and girls of childbearing age acquired from the WASH cluster core pipeline – 2,500 females targeted (1,000 women and 1,500 young girls of reproductive age preferably school going girls)1.13. Promotion of community led total sanitation – CLTS, Child hygiene and sanitation transformation( CHAST) , and Participatory hygiene and sanitation transformation(PHAST) methodologies in two villages, one in Guit county and the other in Pigi/Canal county.Cordaid has pre-positioned one 500T PAT drilling machine at Bentiu, one manual village drill rig machine and a unit of complete SWAT system in Malakal which will enable quick and immediate response. Cordaid has installed one SWAT system in Diel and transformed 2 SWAT systems to Solar powered in Panam and Canal respectively, and previously installed a solar powered SWAT system at Wau Shiluk and Dethwok. 6 boreholes have been drilled in Koch and 10 blocks of 5 stances each emergency latrines have been constructed in Asabnil, Khorfulus and Canal. All these completed activities are fully operational.The proposed activities will also contribute towards decrease in proportion of population facing IPC 3 and 4, reduction in prevalence and incidence rates of epidemic WASH related prone diseases, and mitigation of WASH-related gender - based violence. Recovery and resilience will also be enhanced through the above activities.

Impartiality and non-discrimination are core values Cordaid applies and respects in its humanitarian operations. Cordaid will be intentional in its approach and will uphold the principle of impartiality and non-discrimination in meeting the needs of the affected population. Therefore, this project will be inclusive and all sections of vulnerabilities including displacement - affected communities will have access and benefit from the project.

Yes the project is linked to community systems. In this project, Cordaid will work closely with line government departments and ministries - and through community based structures like water management committees, community hand pump mechanics, community hygiene facilitators, government health facilities and schools by strengthening and enhancing their capacities for self reliance.

Yes, this project is aligned with national objectives. The project supports the objectives of the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission and that of the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs for IDPs, returnees and refugees. The project is also aligned to the rural water strategy of Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation - MWRI at the national government. This project further through its intervention, meets the South Sudan WASH Cluster strategy for 2020 and WASH cluster strategic operational framework

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is sufficiently safe/stable. Cordaid has employed and will employ in this project the principle of "Do-no-harm" by ensuring all affected populations - IDPs, Returnees and host communities, access and benefit from the services provided.Cordaid will engage the local authority specifically the RRC representative to seek information on accessibility, security and beneficiary selection to ensure services are provided timely and inclusive. Cordaid will in this project closely work with other security actors like UNDSS to get first hand information on security of project locations.

Yes, the project in it's design is solely aligned to sustainability/handover strategy. This project will ensure community based structures are functioning and are well equipped. Structures like Water management committees, Community hand pump mechanics, community hygiene facilitators/promoters will be provided with skills, knowledge and tools through well tailored trainings to enhance their capacities to continue functioning even beyond the project lifeline.
