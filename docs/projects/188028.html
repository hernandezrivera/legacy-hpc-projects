Project Summary

This program is going to increasing the resilience and livelihood opportunities of most vulnerable women headed HHs and PWDs. To address the many gaps in livelihoods opportunities and income generation that have arisen in the target locations due to the ongoing crisis in Syria, Ihsan RD is proposing to support 800 HHs, of them 400 HHs (2400 individuals) in 2022, and 400 HHs (2400 individuals) in 2023 with small business grants through cash voucher worth to 800 $. The targeted BNFs will be 350 women head HHs, 350 youth and 100 PWDs). All the supported businesses will be of the non-agribusinesses such as haircut, sewing, embroidery, clothes marketing, minimarket …etc and any other business that not related to agribusiness). The grants will be distributed through two instalments, each instalment will be 400 USD with intervals of one month between the two instalments as follows:
1- First stage: BNFs will receive cash voucher worth to 400 USD (this fund will cover the basic needs of a small business project such as rehabilitation, purchasing of some equipment needed to start the initial of establishment of stage small business projects.
2- Second stage (after one month of first one): BNFs will receive 400 USD (this amount will cover the additional needed materials to complete their own businesses). The second batch of grant will be distributed for the target HHs after making sure that the beneficiary showed a clear obligation to start his/her project and spent the first batch on the established project
In addition to small business grants, each BNFs will be supported with entrepreneurship and innovative training programs to improve their Knowle on planning, designing, managing and marketing for their own businesses.
It worthy to mention that IRD will conduct in depth labor/ need assessment for non-Agri small businesses during implementation to identify the gaps in these professions, then the BNFs involved in this project will be selected based on the following selection criteria
Minimum eligibility criteria: The applicant should not be a beneficiary of a similar grant from another organization, no access to regular income.
Prioritization criteria: Households with pregnant or lactating women, families with a higher number of children, Household has one or more disabled family member.
Note: 50% of the activities for 2022 will be implemented as secured fund, while 50 % are planned activities (unsecured fund). The activities for the year 2023 are planned activities (unsecured fund).
To ensure harmonization with no duplication of efforts in the proposed location; IRD will coordinate and cooperate with ERL cluster and all actors in the target location to obtain full details about their planned interventions in the target area. IRD will also conduct cross reference beneficiary lists through coordination with the acting organizations in the same locations to ensure that households who are directly benefiting from similar projects by other organizations are not included on beneficiaries’ lists for IRD intervention.

4 - Needs

Almost ten years of crisis in Syria have continued to erode the socio-economic foundations of the Syrian people. Moreover, the global COVID-19 pandemic has led to additional stress on social and basic services while also depriving Syrian people of their jobs and livelihoods. The combined effects of these concurrent crises contributed to a significant contraction of the overall economic activity, affecting people’s ability to sustain adequate living standards, seek decent livelihood opportunities, access social services and essential infrastructure, and further undermine social cohesion (HNO,2021). As a result of the ongoing crisis several segments within the population are in particular need of income generating and livelihood support due to increased vulnerability, particularly female-headed households and heads of households living with a disability who reported the lowest average income across all population groups (The first draft of HNO report 2022). Overall, IDP households headed by females are nine times more likely to report having had no income in the last month compared to male-headed households. Income deficit among female-headed households is evidenced by the inability to meet basic needs of their family members (86 per cent), with an overwhelming majority (80 per cent) also indicating a deterioration in their ability to do so compared to last year due to a lack of income and/or access to income generating opportunities.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

1-Child labour: Mitigating measures: Ihsan RD will be sure that the staff working in this project are trained and fully aware of child safeguarding and child protection. Risk (2): Lack/difficulty of accessibility for PWDs. Mitigating measures: Availability of wheelchair for PWD at the reception point, trained staff to deal with PWDs, house to house distributions. Risk (3): Sometimes, crowding during distribution of support, may not provide the services at the desire quality. Mitigating measures: Set up separate waiting and entry lines at registrations, Establishment of multiple distribution sites to avoid creating crowds. Risk(4): Field staff attitude harm beneficiaries’ dignity and rights during assistance distribution. Mitigating measures: Ensuring that all project staff had signed and adhered to code of conduct and gained a full knowledge on the highest ethical standards and respect for humanitarian principles. Risk (5) GBV risks: Mitigating measures: Raise the awareness of community leaders and filed staff on GBV risks, choosing appropriate time for distribution of support (during daylight hours), ensuring regular incidence reporting for GBV focal point, conducting awareness sessions for the women about the issues surrounding with GBV to minimize and avoid such damage as possible, ensuring that the filed staff include female members to deal and coordinate with women for any expected GBV risks


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Based on Ihsan RD PSEA policy and before starting of project interventions, by starting of the project Ihsan RD will manage complaints mechanism and ensure accountability to beneficiaries after advanced coordination with Ihsan RD protection canters and PSEA focal points in the targeted location Ihsan RD will conduct SEA risk assessment to identify all potential SEA risks such as :1- Risk of SEA act by non-PSEA trained staff while provide the support to BNFs,2- BNFs do not report SEA cases for Ihsan RD staff, which results in the Ihsan RD being unable to investigate these cases and provide the needed assistance to them. Mitigation: After in-depth assessment for these risks, Ihsan RD through collaboration with PSEA focal point will designed the mitigation plan all these risks as follows Mitigation measures:1- Ihsan RD will be ensure that all its staff had2- signed code of conduct to prevent acts of SEA by its staff and all recruited staff should attend PSEA training before any contact with BNFs.3- Beneficiary awareness-raising (Beneficiaries must be aware on what SEA is, what their rights, what they can do if SEA occur4- Establish safe and accessible community reporting channels to report SEA complaints tool will be also available (e.g., complaints box / telephone hotline to deal with these cases)5- Choosing appropriate time for distributions of support (during daylight hours to allow women to return home safely, ensuring regular security and incidence reporting,


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1- CVA risks: Beneficiaries are pressured into paying ‘taxes or fees in relation to CVA’ out of their aid transfers. Mitigation measures: Raise beneficiaries’ awareness that the assistance is free of charge, and they are not required to pay ‘fees’ or ‘taxes’ out of it, ensure continuous receiving of feedback of the BN, include questions on taxation or aid diversion, etc. in PDM data collection, Anti-Terrorist Sanctions Checks will be conducted for each contracted Financial Service Provider (FSP), contracting with FSPs who have a record of previous working with other aid agencies and being approved by donors suspend activities, if necessary,2- Changing the security situation. Mitigation: Monitoring and analysing the security situation, temporary suspension of field work 3- Delay in funds. Mitigation: IRD will coordinate and respond to the donor requirements rapidly to ensure that the agreements are signed on time4- Corvid -19. Mitigation: Ensuring that all protective measures had been carefully considered and applied to avoid COVID-19 infections 5- Dominant authorities interfered in the exclusion or inclusion of beneficiaries regardless of the selection criteria. Mitigation: Early ensuring that the local authorities clearly informed that IRD team will be fully independent in BNF selection according to the criteria developed. 6- Market volatility. Mitigation: reporting to donor, conduct an in-depth market assessment to be shared with the donor to take the best action.
