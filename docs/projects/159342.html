Project Summary

The Humanitarian Response Plan identified WASH red spot districts which are at high risk to disease outbreak, water scarcity, high latrine safety index owing to high mobility associated with disaster. WV Somalia will target two regions and having worked in Sool, Baidoa, Burtinle and Garowe districts would like to build on its footprints with regards to WASH service delivery. Sool will be targeted from both the Somaliland region (Ainabo district) and Puntland (Baarmmado). WASH programming will be undertaken at community, school and health levels. The program aims to achieve two results/outcomes, that is, firstly increased access to sustainable and safe water supply for poor, vulnerable communities, children in school and in health facilities and secondly target population has access to reliable and sustainable environmental sanitation. WV has a commitment to supporting capacity-building efforts for local people and government line ministries. World Vision currently works with the Ministry of Energy and Water Resources and the Ministry of Health in the implementation of its WASH activities in South West State, Puntland and Somaliland. This deliberate effort at partnering provides a platform for capacity building for the Ministry personnel, and at the same time strengthens collaboration and ensures World Vision strategy is in sync with the mandate and strategy of the local government. WV is mindful of local custom and culture. It is also committed to participatory and sustainable development and to providing assistance impartially. WV acknowledge their accountabilities to those whom they assist and those who provide support to their work and also committed to ensuring that it does not expose intended beneficiaries, including children, to any form of discrimination, abuse or exploitation. To ensure sustainable delivery, WV seeks to encourage participation in decision making processes and skills training so as to ensure that the beneficiaries will act as an active component in the implementation of the program. The WASH program has been deliberate in ensuring its facilities are inclusive and can be used by people with physical disabilities. One of the key areas has been ensuring that latrines are accessible to wheelchair-bound persons by incorporating ramps as standard at doorways and having wide fully opening doors. For public sanitation facilities, ramps and rails have also been installed as standard for the sanitation facilities. Sector integration will also be fostered. The WASH program will work closely with the Education and Protection and Health and Nutrition sectors. The WASH programme in Health Facilities will focus on ensuring all consultations and admissions, including children under the age of 5 have access to clean water and sanitation within the target locations. At each of the health facilities, World Vision will provide an elaborate water and sanitation management programme, which will include incinerators, autoclaves, clean water, and institutional latrines for proper infection prevention and control practices. The schools will be connected to the main water supply and receive occasional water trucking at times when the main water supply is erratic. Children will also be provided with WASH NFIs, including MHM kits that will be given to adolescent girls at the schools to help reduce on the number of girls who drop out of school during their menstrual cycle. Ideally, the 3-star approach will be adopted and guide operations in schools. DRR will include developing capacity of local government to understand, own and trigger demand for WASH through advocacy to take policy decisions to develop a political will that enable a WASH resilience building

Project Activities

Health, nutrition and resilience outcome are closely related to positive WASH outcome. However, lacking access to sufficient and clean water, inadequate sanitation facilities and commodities and poor hygiene practise have put 2.7 million in need of WASH services (PIN) (WASH cluster, 2019). The anticipated rains are expected to further enhance pasture and water availability; however, except for the improved rainfall can bring more flood risk to the community, it also increase the risk of people rely on contaminated, open source of water. Continued displacement further exacerbated the humanitarian crisis in Somalia. In IDP settlement, open defecation is common due to the absence of proper sanitation facilities. Host communities will also be overburdened by increased incomer. The preliminary finding released for the Joint Multi cluster needs assessment conducted by REACH October 2019 reported the reduction in household consumption due to lack of water at the source was the most commonly cited reason for inadequate water access for both IDP and host community. Overall, access to safe water is 49% in Somalia and the reach quite lower in IDPs. While 52% walk less than 15 minutes to access water, it has been noted because most households access water from water trucking. For those who have access to water, 36% access amounts not enough to cover basic survival amount of 15 litres per person per day. Worrying to note is the fact that 24% of the population do not have access to improved latrines, thus contribution to rampant AWD which has two epidemiological peaks each year. Where water is available it is also worrying to note that 7% of the girls travel longer distances, more than a kilometre to access water. Protection issues are of paramount importance as the WASH Cluster Emergency Response Preparedness (ERP) plan show that 0.4 million women, 1.7 million children and 0.4 people living with disability. Mitigation Measures

Preventing risks of exclusion based on the displacement The selection and prioritization of support will be need-based, regardless of communities' status and identity. Project areas, for instance, will be prioritized base on the humanitarian needs overview. User ratios and quality of WASH infrastructure will be considered. The people displaced by conflict and drought in IDP camps will be supported with the support state security forces. WV Somalia will work with intercluster agencies to ensure that further WASH infrastructure development is undertaken where the displaced have been permitted to settle.Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age The project will ensure water source and hygiene facilities are accessible and appropriate for all in the communities. Gender-specific protection needs will be mainstreamed by providing latrines with locks both inside and outside. Labelling of toilets to ensure separation of latrine use between males and female. Hygiene kits will also be distributed to women and girls to address their menstruation need. Despite rampant gender disparity in Somalia, the project will ensure hygiene promotion is conducted by both men and women. Decision making in local structures like water committees, WV will strike a 50% representation by the woman.Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence Competing over access to essential commodities and services are often one of the significant causes of community conflict. To reduce conflict, the project will closely engage social classes within the target areas. Community engagement will be fostered throughout the project cycle. Examples of engagement will include involvement in site selection, and making sure the accountability system is in place to allow them to voice their concerns and complaint. WV, throughout the project cycle, will ensure the safety of beneficiaries and staff during the program. This will ensure the timely provision of services to prevent late travels by the staff and beneficiaries, which might expose them to security risks. Throughout the project duration, world vision will monitor the security situation and also collaborate with the local administration and the community to ensure that the beneficiaries and the staff are well aware. WV has the security team that closely monitors the security situation within the target areas, hence able to advise and prevent staff and also beneficiaries from being affected.

In 2019, World Vision drilled and equipped two boreholes in Baidoa district. With the master plan for Baidoa town being finalised, WV will work with partners to see how water from the boreholes will be linked to further secondary and tertiary connections. WV successfully conducted a hydrological survey where two drilling sites were identified have been identified. One borehole is already drilled and is in the process of being connected to the beneficiaries. According to the hydrological survey, the borehole was drilled to a depth of 380m, with a borehole diameter of 10 inches and a casing diameter of 8 inches for both steel (top 10m of borehole) and PVC (rest of the borehole) and this will ensure sufficiency of good quality water connected to people. There are two bores in Burtinle; lulub and xxxxx which are supplying water. However, the water is very salty such that it is only used for watering livestock living the community by to resort to contaminated shallow ponds and trucked water from far away distances. Solar desalination plants for the target vilages will be installed. WV Somalia is working on ensuring that inline chlorination mechanism are mounted on mini water systems to ensure water is treated at the point of use. The dams will be lined with 2mm plastic lining to reduce the loss of harvested water to infiltration. During the upgrading of boreholes to solar power; solar panels that generate the desired KW power, pump, inverter, riser pipes, Dry Run Switch - Well probe sensor, Level Switch, control boxes and other miscellaneous components will be delivered and installed. Finally on extensive site training was provided to the borehole operators.Sanitation provision will mainly be the construction of upgradable family shared VIP latrines. An upgradable latrine consists of a lined latrine pit designed to last a minimum of 5 years and much longer if desludging facilities are available. The superstructure is made of cheaper materials, but with emphasis on providing privacy, and satisfying the GBV checklist. The latrine design will be as per latrine design standards set out by the Somalia WASH Cluster. The design feature a superstructure with an offset pit will allow the pit to be easily desludged. The pan is joined to the pit by an angled PVC pipe (no S-bend), 160mm in diameter, at a slope of 2:1. The pit will be covered with a reinforced concrete slab which can be removed for desludging purposes. The slab has a good seal, to prevent the pit from becoming a mosquito breeding site. A vent pipe will be installed for each pit, with wire mesh over the top to prevent flies and mosquitos entering pit. Provision of water facilitated the education programme two-fold, first ensuring children have water for rehydration, sanitation and hygiene and secondly water for ongoing school feeding. At each school, one male and one female teacher will be trained as a hygiene master at the respective schools. The hygiene masters will further train school clubs with support from the WASH team, Ministry of Health and Ministry of Education staff. A range of exercises and educational games were used to teach pupils about the links between personal hygiene and health. The hand-dug wells will be deepened to a minimum depth of 10 m to ensure yearly supply, and then well rings were installed to protect the sides of the wells from collapse. Grouting will be 3m depth, and 0.5 high headwalls added.
