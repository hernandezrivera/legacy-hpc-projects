Project Summary

Medair aims to address the emergency humanitarian needs of the refugees through a multi-sectoral approach which will include Health, Nutrition, Water, Sanitation & Hygiene (WASH) and Emergency Shelter & Non-Food Items. The project targets the refugees in Gedaref, Kassala, White Nile, and the population targeted include the refugees arriving from Ethiopia due to the Northern Ethiopian crisis started since one year ago and other refugees in the country. The goal of the project is to contribute to the reduction of excess mortality and mobidity among the refugees in Sudan, and to restore dignity of them through improving their living conditions including hygiene and sanitation.
Since March 2021, Medair has been providing to the refugee population in the two refugee camps in Gedaef multi-sectoral assistance in the areas of Shelter/NFI, site improvements, and WASH. In Tunaydbah, Medair has enrolled over 360 lead mothers and 2,000 female participants in its community-based Care Groups for Behaviour Change Communication (BCC) programming covering topics of hygiene, nutrition and health. Medair has completed almost 30 km of micro drainage throughout Tunaydbah in full coordination with UNHCR for technical design and linkage to their macro drainage schemes for flood prevention, and constructed 108 permanent latrines and bathing shelters. Medair has been selected as a UNHCR Shelter partner for a transitional shelter project in Tunaydbah. In Um Rakuba, Medair runs a Rapid Response Team for cholera prevention (and response in the event of cases/outbreak), with full support of UNHCR WASH technical guidance.

1 - Needs

Sudan hosts an estimated 1,108,153 refugees and asylum-seekers from the neigbouring countries such as Central African Republic, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Somalia, South Sudan. The main refugee-hosting states include White Nile State, Kassala and Gedaref. White Nile state hosts the country’s highest number of South Sudanese refugees at 220,000. Majority of the refugees and host communities in White Nile experience high overall vulnerability (UNHCR BaNVA Aug 21). Refugees in Gedaref, Sennar, North Darfur, and South Darfur experienced higher food vulnerability compared to their host communities. In August 2021, Medair undertook an assessment looking at the general multi sectoral needs at the locality level in Khartoum, White Nile and Kassala. Medair’s focus group discussion (FGD) results reported weak provision of healthcare, especially in reproductive health with shortage of medical commodities and drugs (stock out lasted more than 3 months). Additionally, there is a lack of qualified staff in the facilities. Women have reported that the facility has no laboratory. Health awareness within the community is also a problem. The 2018 S3M II survey further revealed that White Nile falls behind in some key practices and access to optimal WASH and health & nutrition. While, other health and nutrition data highlights include full EPI vaccination coverage standing at 31%. State-wide GAM is at 7.97% with MAM at 6.34%. At the camps, however, GAM has surpassed the emergency threshold of 15% (UNHCR,19 Aug 21). Pertaining to infant and child feeding, exclusive breastfeeding is practiced among 56% of mothers while IYCF coverage is reported to be at 15%. Apart from Health and Nutrition, WASH and Shelter/NFI gaps remain in camps within White Nile, particularly as the settlements have been established for some time. Flooding regularly affects large areas of White Nile state, resulting in damage, displacements, and increased disease transmission risks. Within the targeted camps, infrastructure requires maintenance and rehabilitation and the affected population continues to need inputs of consumable materials. In addition, ongoing hygiene promotion is needed to prioritise hygiene behaviours, especially related to disease prevention. On the shelter side, new arrivals and shelter damages due to the weather require the distribution of shelter materials and NFI support. Since the northern Ethiopian crisis started in Nov 2020, a total of 49,490 refugees from Ethiopia arrived East Sudan (Gedaref, Kassala) and 7,591 arrived in Blue Nile State (as of Sept 2021). The majority of these refugees are hosted in the two camps in Gedaref - Tunaydbah and Um Rakuba camps. The refugees have walked long distances to reach Sudan. Many indicate they were without food and water during ight. They have witnessed killings, rape, and other grave abuses. Living in the crowded environment the refugees in the camps are additionally exposed to different protection risks related to lack of privacy and safe hygiene. There are huge needs in the adequate provision of primary health and nutrition services. In addition, refugees are at risk of water-related illnesses such as Hepatitis E virus, acute watery diarrhea and cholera, particularly due to lack of adequate coverage of WASH facilities in the camps that has been highlighted by WASH partners and UNHCR. Participants in a Medair conducted FGD with women in Tunaydbah camp in June 2021 revealed their concerns over the limited privacy, particularly for women, to be able to have dignity in managing their personal needs. These concerns were also echoed in the feedback received from our Rapid Response Team in Um Rakuba. The crowded living condition of the refugees needs to be improved as most of them are living with emergency shelters such as tents and these are not supposed to be durable.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Medair will support primary healthcare facilities to deliver integrated packages of health interventions including curative medical consultations, routine EPI, IMNCI consultations, child growth monitoring, management of acute malnutrition, ANC/PNC follow-up, family planning and provision of care to SGBV survivors and referrals to other specialised health care services as appropriate. Medair will participate in all the pillars of the COVID-19 preparedness and response. Medair’s health and nutrition specialists will recruit and build capacity of qualified health staff and continually train its community healthcare/nutrition workers and volunteers in the delivery of integrated packages of primary health care services to refugees and their surrounding host community in the context of the ongoing pandemic, this includes strict compliance to COVID-19 IPC measures. Medair will build MHPSS capacity of health staff and volunteers by providing them tools to support people with psychosocial needs and refer those who need more care for further assessment. Medair will set up an integrated OTP and TSFP unit within the health post to provide screenings, treatment, referrals, and follow-up of acute malnutrition cases to ensure timely recovery to the targeted beneficiaries; this service delivery will strictly adhere to the Sudan COVID-19 guidelines. MUAC screenings will take place in the health facility, however, alternate methods will be explored, where mothers screen for MAM or SAM criteria and refer their children to facilities for therapies such as RUTF, RUSF and CSB. Additionally, micronutrients, deworming, and antibiotics will be provided as per OTP and TSFP guidelines, with referrals to the nearest stabilisation centre for severe SAM cases. These services will primarily target PLWs, mothers and children aged 6-59 months who are particularly vulnerable to the health and nutrition impacts of displacement and disease. At risk children aged less than 6 months will be referred to the stabilisation centre through the nutrition partners. Dissemination of messaging on optimal infant feeding and other critical health information is planned. Medair’s CNVs will be trained to spread facility- and community-based messaging to the targeted groups. Medair will include Behaviour Change Communication (BCC) as a component in the integrated programming through establishing Care Groups/Cascade Groups to share key nutrition, health, MHPSS, hygiene and shelter related messages and provide multi-sectoral support for the beneficiaries. The groups are designed to provide peer support to women of young children for the specific challenges the refugees face, and include referral mechanisms and alerts when additional services are needed. All messages and lessons are provided in the local languages of the refugees and the topics are adjusted as needed. Mediar will construct and maintain the critically needed bathing shelters and latrines in the camps as there are existing camps of these facilities. Medair will distribute WASH NFIs including soaps, jerry cans, menstural hygiene kits to the targeted population. Medair has started a transitional shelter project in Tunaydbah camp as a partner of UNHCR since October 2021. Medair will continue the shelter project to provide durable shelter solution to the refugees in 2022. Medair will distribute in the White Nile region 1,000 Emergency Shelter kits,1,000 NFI kits to households, 200 households with upgrade or maintenance shelter materials, and implement site improvement projects. Medair will distribute and implement in the Gedaref/Kassala region: 500 Emergency Shelter kits, 500 NFI kits to households, 1,000 households with upgrade or maintenance shelter materials, provision of 1,000 Tukuls or mud-brick houses and implement site improvement projects.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The environment is considered through the project planning and implementationprocess. Shelter and NFI has two primary impacts on the environment, these includelittering (of items and packaging for distributed) and deforestation (as a result ofshelter construction). These impacts are mitigated through intentional assessment,cleanup and planning. In this case a careful cleanup of distribution sites will be doneto ensure no litter remains following the distribution and that any packaging is reusedor disposed of responsibly. Deforestation will be prevented and/or mitigated throughthe selection of shelter type, in this case tents have been selected by UNHCR to preventthe depletion of natural resources in the area. Medair would implement shelterprograming in line with recommendations of the cluster and UNHCR site leadership.Through the immediate WASH response planned, targeted population will beencouraged to ensure sustainable use of water to avoid wastages at the household,water collection points and reusing waste water for other purposes namely kitchengardening and cleaning of sanitation facilities. Additionally, through sanitation andhygiene promotion, waste management at household level will be prioritized to ensurethat little waste generated is properly managed.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Specific considerations will be made at all stages of the project (assessment, planning,registration, verification, distribution and post distribution monitoring) to ensure fair,safe and equal access for all. This will ensure inclusion of men, women, child headedhouseholds, PLWs, PLWDs and the elderly within the consultation of needs, planningand follow up. Considerations will be made, in coordination with community membersand relevant organizations to ensure fair, safe and equal access to registration anddistribution activities. This will include risk assessments to establish mitigatingmeasures such as timing, location, layout, function and support given at thedistribution and or service locations. Follow up after the programing, to assessstrength and effectiveness of these measures will be included in the PDM process.Medair will ensure that protection mainstreaming is included throughout all phases ofthe project. Medair will engage and coordinate with the Camp Management partnerand Protection partners in Health, Nutrition, WASH, Shelter and NFI components.Medair will ensure that the needs of vulnerable groups are prioritized and addressedduring the interventions where key beneficiaries will be consulted and encouraged toactively participate.
