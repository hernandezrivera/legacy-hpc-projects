Start Date

Project Summary

The project aims to contribute to the revitalization of conflict-affected communities through integrated psychosocial support for IDPs, returnees and other community members and social cohesion activities aimed at increasing integration and further reconciliation, with particular attention to the needs of most vulnerable groups– women, youth, IDPs, national minorities, ethnic groups, elderly and persons with disabilities.

1 - Needs

1) Based on the raw data as of August 2019 in Multi-Sectoral Needs Assessment within 20km of Contact Line conducted by REACH, around 27% of the respondents living within 20 km from the LoC, and 13% within 0-5 km, mentioned PSS as one of their needs. Around 75% of the respondents were female, whose age was above 50 in more than half of the cases. Approximately half of the respondents, who declared the need of PSS, mentioned shelling, landmines/ERW contamination as their major security concern. Based on the above assessment, lack of PSS was also reported by some of the schools in locations within 0-20km from the LoC. 2) According to the Ukraine Humanitarian Situation Report Midyear 2019 by UNICEF, recurrent ceasefire violations continued to threaten the well-being of the more than 500,000 boys and girls living within 20 kms of each side of the contact line, many of whom continue to experience the risk of exposure to gunfire and shelling and related psycho-social stress, as well as ongoing disruptions to basic services such as health, education but also electricity and water. The same report’s Summary of Programme Results indicates more efforts are required to reach out to people in need of PSS.3) While Health Cluster Ukraine Bulletin January-February 2019 states that PSS remains one of the most requested forms of assistance from conflict-affected people, pointing out insufficient mental health specialists and health care workers available along the 20 km of the LoC and the urgent need to train health care workers and mental health specialists in managing acute distress in populations affected by the conflict.4) MHPSS Working Group meetings in June 2019 in Severodonetsk and Kramatorsk highlighted the need of long-term, planned individual psychological support for children, as no tendency towards decrease in the number children affected by psychological violence was observed, with around 2000 children receiving the status of affected by psychological violence every month in Luhansk region alone. They also stressed the insufficiency of human resources to tackle the issue. At the same time, the meeting records also indicate that parents of the affected children also seek support of a psychologist. Besides, such categories as people who were deprived of their liberty due to the military conflict and relatives of people missing due to the conflict also require continued PSS.5) Due to the protracted conflict, stabilization and social resilience measures are increasingly necessary for the conflict-affected communities in the Donetsk and Luhansk regions. The high number of IDPs in Luhansk and Donetsk regions and continued insecurity place a significant strain on various social services and public infrastructure and increases competition over already limited employment opportunities, service delivery, putting additional pressure to social protection systems, in some cases leading to increased tensions between IDPs and members of the host community. According to the latest data received within IOM's National Monitoring Survey, June 2019, half of surveyed IDPs (50%) reported to be integrated into their local communities. Thus, mitigating measures to prevent the exacerbation of tensions between different groups of the local population and IDP communities through the implementation of activities designed to enhance social cohesion and trust-building are highly in need in Donetsk and Luhansk regions (GCA).

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome: Conflict affected community members have increased access to PSS services available in their community to mitigate negative effects produced by the conflict Output 1: Psychosocial support and social cohesion resources and needs of IDPs, returnees and vulnerable groups are assessedActivities for output 1:1) Identify local civil based organizations (CBOs) and/or community initiative groups (CIGs) through a competitive process, where applicants are requested to submit analysis of local PSS and social cohesion needs, along with proposals for related initiatives to be implemented;2) Identify existing psychosocial support resource persons (e.g. community leaders, local social service workers, psychologists etc.) through a mapping exercise in targeted locations;3) Coordinate planned and existing intervention in the area with the Protection Cluster and Mental Health and Psychosocial Sub Cluster partners, as well as within the United Nations Country Team, Humanitarian Country Team and United Nations Eastern Team. Output 2: Psychosocial support (including on GBV) and community level referral system is established and operational in targeted communities Activities for output 2:1) Based on results of needs assessment, ensure availability PSS appropriate modalities (mobile clinic, renovation of existing facilities etc.) centres in the communities where they are not available in coordination with municipal authorities. Newly created/renovated/equipped PSS facilities will be set in the premises of the social infrastructure objects and will be a space for the provision of individual and group counselling sessions by the trained PSS specialists;2) Referral mechanism establishment at community level to ensure proper identification, screening and further assistance provision;3) Conduct peer to peer psychosocial support training for women and men to be counsellors to identify potential domestic violence cases and to refer them to PSS specialists;4) Conduct advanced PSS training, incl. on GBV and refresher trainings for identified local psychosocial specialists and implement focused support activities and referrals in cooperation with international experts. To improve skills and abilities of the PSS specialists to support the conflict-affected population to address the psychological impact of the ongoing conflict the following topics will be covered during the training: 1) tools for screening and detection of stress and post traumatic symptoms and GBV victims/survivors; 2) coping strategies, techniques and actions to build resilience; 3) best practices of PSS interventions for the IDPs and conflict-affected populations; 4) case management and supervisory support. Additionally supervisors’ and mentoring support will be suggested to the local psychologists to ensure proper case management;5) Identify and refer vulnerable cases to the existing services. Output 3: Conflict affected communities are aware about the importance of psychosocial support (PSS) after traumatic experiences, inc. GBV through implementation of community development initiatives Activities for output 3:1) Open a call among the targeted communities to apply with community-led development initiatives to share the information about PSS related issues;2) Select community level development initiatives through the competitive selection process;3) Monitor the implementation of community led-initiatives. Mechanisms to ensure accountability to the affected population (AAP), by establishing complaints mechanisms, toll-free hotlines and engaging affected communities in needs assessment, design of activities and feedback on implementation are widely used by IOM on its daily activities. IOM currently operates Transparency Hotline and E&S Programme Informational Hotline. IOM strictly follows guidelines highlighted in the IOM Data Protection Policy. Training on Data Protection Policy were provided to the people that are working on hotlines.
