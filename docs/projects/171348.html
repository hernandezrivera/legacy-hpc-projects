Project Summary

Somalia remains in a state of chronic catastrophe characterized by a complex political environment, extreme poverty, food insecurity, conflict and instability. This has led to poor access to basic health services, recurrent disease outbreaks, food insecurity and high malnutrition rates, a lack of safe water supplies, and significant rates of population displacement. Furthermore, the triple threat of the COVID-19, floods and desert locusts has aggravated Somalia’s already complex and protracted humanitarian crisis. The crisis is largely driven by climatic shocks and decades of armed conflict. According to the draft Shelter Cluster Humanitarian Needs Overview 2020, the estimated number of people in need in Somalia remains as high as last year: 3.1 million people (IDPs, poor host communities, refugees and returnees). The population in need lacks ESKs and NFIs and lives in inadequate conditions in existing shelters, economic hardship and a lack of security of tenure. Most IDPs live in makeshift shelters (Buuls) which offer limited privacy and protection from harsh weather conditions. The shelter and NFI needs of IDPs are higher than that of host communities mainly due to the displacement status, lack of security of tenure and lack of employment opportunities. Moreover, there are estimated to be 2,300 IDP sites mostly built on private land without long term security of tenure, thus increasing the risk of eviction. This project aims at providing 1000 standard Non-Food Item Kits to 1000 HH (6000 beneficiaries) and 1000 Emergency Shelter Kits to an additional 1000 HH (6000 beneficiaries) of newly internally displaced persons and 500 transitional shelters to an additional 500 HH (3000 beneficiaries). The project will therefore serve a total of 2500 HH (15000 beneficiaries). The proposed project will be implemented in three regions of Bay (Baidoa, Berdale, Diinsor), Bakol (Xudur and Wajid) and Middle Shabelle (Jowhar and Balcad) regions where INTERSOS has consolidated presence and prior experience. Majority of the envisaged targeted HHs are living in 44 spontaneous IDP settlements in Baidoa without current support from humanitarian agencies. INTERSOS had already successfully implemented transitional shelters in Baidoa through cash based intervention in 2017 and 2018 while supporting returnees through NFI distribution since 2014. The criteria used for selecting the IDPS, refugees and returnees are; female/elderly/child headed HHs, female/elderly/child headed HHs who have not received any support source of livelihood or income, families with eviction threats issued, beneficiaries of the VOLREP program who arrived during the year 2016-2017 and members of the marginalized groups and the disabled. The humanitarian situation in IDP settlements has been exacerbated by the influx of new IDPs who either joined spontaneous settlements or established new sites. The NFI kits will be given in kind while the ESKs will be implemented through cash based intervention where beneficiaries will be issued with vouchers to procure items of their own preference. Due to the sensitivity of cash based programming for shelter, the proposed project will be designed to adhere to the guidelines and essential criteria for cash and voucher assistance as proposed by the Inter Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG). The project intends to contribute to reduction of protection risks among IDPs in relation to eviction and GBV through facilitation of acquisition of land tenure documents for 500 transitional shelters which will translate to 500 HH (3000 beneficiaries). In quest to promote beneficiary accountability, INTERSOS will establish clear communication channels through which beneficiaries will be able to raise their concern and will also set up a beneficiary feedback mechanism using tools translated in the local language. Post Distribution Monitoring will also be conducted using well structured tools to determine the extend of beneficiary satisfaction.

Project Activities

Activity 1.1: Conduct community engagement and mobilization of 15000 beneficiaries (6035 male and 8965 female) to ensure their active participation in project planning, implementation and monitoring. A committee whose members are representatives of all sections of the community (persons with disabilities, women, minority groups and youth) will be reconstituted in coordination with relevant government authorities in respective locations. For each location, the committee will comprise of 12 people (4 IDPs, 4 returnees and 4 people with disabilities) with strict adherence to gender balance.Activity 1.2: Carry out rapid market assessment to establish capacities in the local market (both in terms of materials and expertise) and prices for NFI kits and ESK, taking into consideration existing guidelines/ criteria for cash based programming.Activity 1.3: Distribution of 1000 standard NFIs in kind to 1000 HH/6000 beneficiaries (2561 male and 3439 female) vulnerable and displaced beneficiaries in Bay, Bakool and Middle Shabelle. Activity 1.4: Unconditional cash assistance to provide 1000 Emergency Shelter Kits for 1000 HH/6000 newly displaced beneficiaries (2788 male and 3212 female). Activity 1.5: Conduct community mobilization workshop on the use, implementation modalities, monitoring of NFI, ESK and transitional shelters distribution. The 15000 beneficiaries (6035 male and 8965 female) will be taken through different orientation sessions for their respective project components and also sensitized in the established community feedback and complaint mechanisms. Activity 2.1: Distribution of cash grant for establishing transitional shelters for 500HH/3000 beneficiaries (1200 male and 1800 female) for displacement affected communities with secure land tenure agreement. The design, technical composition and specifications for implementation of transitional shelters will be given during the orientation training for the selected beneficiaries. The beneficiaries will be provided with the design of the houses and type of materials to be purchased. They will be monitored and supported with technical guidance by the shelter specialist. Activity 3.1: Submit 3W matrix to keep relevant clusters informed about planned and implemented activities on monthly and quarterly basis for both in kind and cash based assistance.Activity 3.2: Put in place community based complain mechanism (suggestion box andhotline numbers) to get feedback to support in addressing needs of the beneficiaries. Activity 3.3: Conduct comprehensive post distribution and post cash assistance monitoring during activities’ implementation and within 3 months after activities’ implementation in each of the targeted Districts.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

INTERSOS proposes cash transfer programming for beneficiaries who will be allocated temporary shelters as their needs are multifaceted and this will be an opportunity for them to buy items of their own preference.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

With extensive experience of operation in the proposed locations, INTERSOS has an in-depth understanding of the existing market trends and will be able to enter into official agreements with local suppliers who meet specific criteria in order to the beneficiaries to procure items of their preference from them. The project will strive to establish the availability of materials as well as favorability of the prices through well structured market research. Information collected will be analyzed and a decision made with strict consideration of availability, quality and affordability of the materials

Mitigation Measures

1. Marginalization of some communities due to clan orientations: The project staff will work closely with the clan elders, IDP committees’ umbrellas, youth groups, and people with disabilities groups and representatives of all minority clans to ensure that minority clans are represented in targeting, taking into consideration clan diversity and ensuring meaningful representation in all committees. Data of beneficiaries reached will be segregated on the basis of gender, socio-economic background and individual abilities to inform inclusive programming. 2. Interference of external stakeholders: INTERSOS' project staff are well oriented on humanitarian principles to deliver need based protection and humanitarian assistance. The humanitarian action will be guided by needs assessment and coordinated with all the relevant stakeholders to ensure transparency and accountability. The targeting of beneficiaries will be fair and selection of beneficiaries will be done objectively based on agreed vulnerability matrix. 3. Reference of external stakeholders: INTERSOS' project staff are well oriented on humanitarian principles of (humanity, impartiality, neutrality and independence) to deliver need based protection and humanitarian assistance. The humanitarian action will be guided by needs assessment and coordinated with all the relevant stakeholders to ensure transparency and accountability. The external authorities will be fully engaged to prevent risks arising from miscommunication and unmet expectations. 4. Risk arising from displacement crisis, for example, SGBV in IDPs settlements andevictions. INTERSOS project staff have undergone mandatory PSEA training course and have signed code of conduct which will be followed to the later. The project beneficiaries will be sensitized about possible SGBV incidents and clear reporting mechanisms in case of any suspicious behavior or act. INTERSOS will set up accessible and locally understood feedback mechanism. They will be shared withhotline numbers managed by protection focal point and a swift action will be taken to address the reported incident. INTERSOS is protection centered organization and emergency front liner organization with many years’ of experience at project locations.5. Carry out safety audits in coordination with the CCCM. The safety audits in IDPs settlements will ascertain protection risks that can be used to inform and advocate for actions to address those risks. Focus group discussions will be conducted with women and girls o further validate key protection issues that need immediate attention from stakeholders. Their priority protection concerns will be shared with the protection cluster during ICCG meetings. 6. Armed conflict and violence: INTERSOS project staff will ensure that different sections of the communities are actively involved in decision making regarding identification and selection of beneficiaries in order to promote a sense of ownership. INTERSOS security policy doesn’t allow unauthorized weapons and armed actors at distribution points in order to minimize unprecedented casualties. The community meeting centers and distribution points will not be closer to armybases in order to prevent collateral incidents on civilians’ and project staff. The community members will be sensitized on unexploded ordinance and any other suspicious objects using leaflets secured from UNMASS. 􀀀 Dispute over land ownership: INTERSOS project staff will verify documents pertaining to land ownership with the local authority and in coordination with NRC HLP sector prior to distribution oftransitional shelters.

Please provide further explanation

The transitional shelter will be planned in an area with easy access to sanitation services. Both transitional and ESK shelters will have lockable doors and lightings. The materials for construction will be inspected by technical engineer to ensure quality implementation. Barwaqo site is a new relocation area with an urban design and boreholes that will provide reliable source of water. Theprovision of transitional shelters will foster reintegration and reduce protection risks associated with access to basic services. The project will be implemented with due regard for dignity, safety and accountability by the affected communities. The project activities will be implemented in broad daylight in order to minimize protection risks. The gate keepers’ influence will be minimizedin order to ensure transparent selection of beneficiaries. The communities will be provided with feedback mechanisms and sensitized on clear reporting mechanisms. The project will strengthen the existing community structures through advocacy and capacity injections. The projects aimed at improving the living standards of the displacement affected communities through provisionof transitional shelters. 30% of the project requirement is designed to provide transitional shelters for IDPs affected by protracted crisis and have long term land tenure agreement. The project foresees establishing the shelters on a public land provided by the local authority. Providing transitional shelter to a beneficiary with secured land tenure agreement helps in fostering reintegration status in respective areas and preventing secondary displacement. The project will be integrated with other ongoing INTERSOS projects in area of intervention to promote access to basic services for the targeted communities such as food, education and health service in order toachieve results at scale. The project interventions are aligned with the national policy for refugee returnees and IDPs of the federal government of Somalia and with reference to principle 6 which reiterates right to an adequate standard of living through provision of basic shelter and housing. The existing community structures, for example IDPs umbrellas, camp leaders, women groups, youth,people with disabilities and relevant local authorities will be actively involved in order to promote community ownership and sustainability. This will be achieved through their active participation in project cycle and strengthening of feedback mechanisms with a view to inform durable solution programming. The communities will be engaged in advocacy meetings to shun away with donormindset tendencies (behavior change) in order to articulate impact of the project beyond its lifespan. The proposed intervention is need based and intended to address challenges of displacement affected communities and maximize impact. The proposed project is aligned with addressing bothnational and state level priorities of addressing displacement crisis and fostering achievement of durable solution. The proposed project therefore foresees establishing collaboration and synergies with all relevant government line ministries in the process of delivering this aid.
