Project Summary

The project aims to contribute saving lives and enhancing local communities resilience by effectively preventing and treating acute malnutrition through integrated/multisector interventions, with specific attention to the most vulnerable groups
The project will ensure increased access to
-quality life-saving management of acute malnutrition for the most vulnerable and at-risk under-five children, pregnant and lactating women in both host and displaced population
-under nutrition prevention programs targeting mothers, infant, children
-other services expected to maximize the impact of and integrated to nutrition ones, as health care for malnutrition complications and adequate wash facilities. Nutrition services will also become the entry point for SGBV victims and other particularly vulnerable people to have access to adequate and comprehensive care.
Finally, the project will promote nutrition situation monitoring and analysis and utilization of resulting data and information for early warning and timely coordinated response, with the affected communities developing skills and knowledge to cope with recurrent crises.
The project will target Awerial, Cuibet, Rumbek North, Yirol West, Wulu Counties and Rumbek and Lui Hospitals (in Rumbek Centre and Mundri East Counties). According to the last IPC (September2018-March2019), Wulu/RumbekCentre/MundriEast are considered crisis situation, while the others are classified as emergency area (with the risk, for Awerial, of deteriorating up to the famine status if the humanitarian support will not continue). The SMART Survey conducted at the beginning of 2018 by CUAMM in YW reported a GAM of 13%, while that conducted by IMC in Rumbek North in September 2018 a GAM of 10.1%. Other Counties have not been assessed yet, but they are likely to present similar concerning values, as suggested by the FSNMS Round 21 data on food security and consumption, showing that the majority of these areas situation is below National average. For instance, in Cuibet, 18.2% of the population is severely food insecure (against 10% nationwide) and 60.6% has a poor food consumption (while it is 55% in South Sudan at large). Considering this last aspect, only Rumbek North is close to national average, with all the other Counties being considerably worst (with Rumbek Centre and Awerial showing the most concerning situation, having 80.8% and 91.4% of their inhabitants reporting poor food consumption)

Needs

In Former Lakes State, people nutrition status is concerning and worsening, at the point that the area passed from “stressed” to “crise” level and that in September 2018 it was estimated 73.20% of local population to be in crises/emergency/catastrophe situation. This % is expected to decrease by March 2019, but remaining above 50% (IPC Sept2018-March2019). In 2017, the % of food insecure population has slightly reduced but it is still noticeably high (75%). Further, it has increased the % of those reporting poor food consumption (69%, against the national average of 55%), with 14% of households forced to put in place emergency coping strategies when managing the food stock. 84.4% of LKS household are reporting from moderate to severe hunger (against the national average of 54.2%). Particularly alarming WCBA situation, 16.8% of them wasting, confirming an increasing trend. Only 1.5% of children benefit of minimum dietary diversity. The acute malnutrition rate is above the 15% emergency threshold. (FSNMR Round21). The Nutrition Cluster has estimated in 8% the average SAM burden in the area. Particularly concerning the situation in those Counties experiencing IDPs influx, as Yirol East (where, in June 2018, the IRNA team reported a GAM rate close to 50%) and Yirol West. The SMART Survey CUAMM conducted in YW in April 2017 showed stable situation compared to 2016, suggesting CUAMM intervention was being quite effective in containing further deterioration. However, services need to be strengthen to address the increasing demand of nutrition services and to be able not only to identify and treat malnourished children and PLW but also to prevent the malnutrition scale up. The combination of different programs, guaranteeing continuity of care from complicated SAM to MAM is considered crucial to limit drops in adherence and lost of follow up and to maximize the outcome of the nutrition intervention. With this in mind, since 2017, CUAMM is including TSFP and OTP in the package of services offered at PHC facilities and Hospitals, with these latest serving also as SC. However, health and nutrition integration is not always successful, resulting in missed opportunities for SAM/MAM identification and in poor referral and attendance of malnutrition complications. Because of lack of qualified dedicated staff, CNVs are asked to help in the HFs, limiting their impact in terms of communities based prevention, referral, follow up. As donors support to health facilities is likely to drop, it is crucial resources to be allocated to avoid nutrition component to be compromised. In Mundri East County (Former Western Equatoria State), various partners have started intervening on Nutrition in the peripheral area, being penalized by the ongoing insecurity, which hampers services functionality and people access to them. Further, they are not in conditions to offer a properly operating Stabilization Centre. Lui Hospital, managed and supported by CUAMM, is the only facility that never stopped working since the beginning of the crises in mid 2015. It is serving as Stabilization Centre for the entire Greater Mundri Region and it represents a positive example of full integration between health and nutrition package.

Activities

The intervention will strengthen Nutrition Services integration into LKS Health System under CHD leadership and with communities involvement, to ensure responsiveness and sensitivity to the needs of the most vulnerable. The intervention will be implemented in 5 Counties and in Rumbek Hospital in Lakes, for a total of 47 PHC facilities offering both TSFP and OTP and 3 Hospitals working as SC as well. In WES, the intervention will focus only at Lui Hospital for the maintenance of the Stabilization Centre. TSFP/OPT/SC services will be integrated at each kind of HF, ensuring MAM/SAM management as per national guidelines (and expanded criteria for treatment). Nutrition service delivery will be sustained in terms of staff (recruitment/training/supervision), supplies and equipment (acquired in kind or procured as per needs on ground), infrastructures upgrade. MUAC and Growth monitoring at EPI/ANC/OPD will make these services safe and accessible entry points for U5/PLW enrolment into Nutrition Programs. Attention will be put on prevention component, with individual and group counselling performed at at HFs by health/nutrition staff and in the villages by CNVs and outreaches team, with reference to IYCF principles. Community involvement will be guarantee through VHCs representatives participation at QRM and HFs supervision to ensure services to be adequately provided and accessible without discrimination .The sustainable and effective integration of Nutrition interventions in County Health plan will be further promoted and strengthen through improved nutrition data collection, analysis and reporting for informed and evidence based decision making . The health-nutrition integration will be then made effective through training and supervision of Health staff appointed to nutrition services on how to identify and treat and eventually refer the most common diseases in nutrition programs patients, with specific focus on SAM cases. The early child development principles CUAMM is going to pilot in selected health facilities will be expanded to the nutrition component as well, focusing on child stimulation. Multisector coordination and cooperation will not be limited to the integration of Health and Nutrition components, but it will be expanded to Education, Wash, Protection; . Stakeholders related to all these sectors will be invited to attend QRM and other coordination mechanism, maximizing the final outcome in terms of prevention, detection, treatment of each actor intervention. HFs offering nutrition programs will have improved WASH facilities; latrines will be upgraded and clean water will be permanent availabile, with hand wash points in all the targeted HFs providing health and nutrition services. , nNutrition messages will be included in school health education sessions., Nutrition services will constitute an entry point for SGBV victims to specific care through prompt referral, with Health staff appointed to nutrition services trained and supervised on how to identify, properly handle and eventually refer nutrition programs patients victim of GBV.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project is designed taking into account the main barriers affecting access to services for certain groups (as women, children, displaced). These barriers have been identified in the past through some qualitative studies conducted in certain counties. Although each County has its own peculiarities, the general findings are considered to be common to various context. Ensuring frontline services are provided free of charge and at the closest possible level to the communities is expected to minimize economical and geographical obstacles, usually affecting the most vulnerable when willing to access the needed assistance. This is realized through the integration of nutrition services to mobile and static health ones and the implementation of County wide referral system, activated not only by the HFs but also by the patient for both health and nutrition related problems. Services integration reduce the missing opportunities and maximize the impact of a single access to service. Continuous community representatives’ involvement in services planning, implementation and monitoring contributes to improve both services responsiveness/acceptability and people awareness on their importance, reducing fear, suspect and cultural resistance towards institutional nutrition care. Women inclusion in the process guarantee its gender sensitiveness and create a window of opportunity for women and men to discuss on nutrition services provision and to jointly realize the reciprocal benefit of promoting women and children access to proper assistance

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project does not directly address inequalities or protection threats, however it will be implemented paying particular attention in ensuring the provided services will be accessible to everyone, without discrimination, especially to the most vulnerable and usually neglected as women, children, displaced… Bringing services closer to the people by adopting mobile team strategy or sustaining facilities in the remote areas will be one of the way to minimize the barriers certain categories of people are facing when looking for assistance. Special cases as patients victims of SGBV or patients with disabilities will be carefully handled, to ensure their dignity and privacy will be respected and to facilitate their access to adequate and comprehensive care, provided with the required expertise and competencies
