Project Summary

Through a multisectoral approach and the static team based in the field area, DRC will improve access on Shelter and Non-Food Items Services to the most affected vulnerable women-headed households, the elderly, persons with specific needs boys and girls across South Sudan. The project targets to mitigate exposure to weather elements and disease-causing vectors through the provision of cash and voucher assistance for Shelter and NFIs, provision of in-kind Non-Food Items assistance using the DRC static team based in Malakal, and emergency SNFI support and emergency shelters to crisis-affected populations in hard-to-reach locations across South Sudan. Through the multisectoral teams, the project will manage to safeguard the right to an adequate standard of living to promote safety and dignity through addressing specific needs of women, girls, boys, and men. According to the shelter-NFI cluster strategy, DRC will continue supporting coordination and participating in joint missions to avoid duplication to improve resource sharing and timely response. DRC will seek the support of the SNFI cluster Core pipeline with SNFIs items to support the affected populations. DRC will adhere to the SNFI cluster guideline and priorities, focusing on responses guided by assessments and further post-distribution monitoring to allow feedback and learning for project and response improvement.

1 - Needs

South Sudan continues to face deteriorating humanitarian conditions. According to the 2022 HNO, climate shocks have heightened humanitarian needs, resulting in extraordinary flooding. The other contributing factors have been operation interference, access constraints, endemic violence, conflict, and direct and indirect effects of COVID 19. There has been an increase of 600,000 people in need of humanitarian assistance compared to 2021. The upper Nile and Jonglei state have been the worst hit by flooding and further requests for support directly from Malakal. They contribute to 85% of the country's flood-affected population, including the Unity state. HNO 2022 indicates an increase in the number of people in need of shelter/NFIs by 570,000 due to displacement by flooding and returns from the neighboring countries. The displaced people, returnees, and host communities require the top three shelter/NFI-related needs: access to emergency shelter, NFIs and housing, and land and property (HLP) rights to ensure tenure security. The urgent need to access emergency shelters and NFIs will alleviate the deterioration of the affected population's living conditions and resilience from recurring hazards. The consecutive flooding for the last three years has resulted in multiple displacements and destroyed the shelter and NFIs, which has resulted in the competition of the scarce construction resources, thus increasing the conflict within the communities. The effects of flooding have cut off access to markets challenging the supply of shelter materials, thus leading to functioning markets lacking even essential shelter materials and NFIs. The shelter is the foundation and basis for engagement in livelihoods and economic activities, and it further provides people with protection, identity, peace, stability, and security. Inadequate or lack of shelter can affect people's well-being, dignity, and health, generating increased protection risks, particularly for women and children. Shelter and NFIs should be provided promptly to the vulnerable, the pregnant mothers, children, the elderly persons with specific needs to protect their living conditions, thus mitigating exposure to weather elements and disease-causing vectors.

2 - Project activities

Activity 1: Provision of cash and voucher assistance for shelter and NFIs: DRC SNFIs sector using a multi sectoral approach and, through a community-based approach, targets to implement shelter activities using cash for shelter modality for the most vulnerable host and returnees Women headed household, the elderly, girls and boys under the caregivers, the person with specific needs. The implementation will be conducted jointly between the protection and shelter sector, where the protection concern and shelter vulnerability assessment will be conducted using the protection criterion. The shelter will be based on the approved design. The cash intervention rates will be determined by market analysis. The protection risk analysis will determine whether cash is a safe /appropriate modality for the beneficiaries. The project targets 1200 beneficiaries (200HH) with cash for shelter. Activity 2: Provision of essential in-kind Shelter and Non Food Items replacements; DRC targets to replace damaged NFI items to the most vulnerable IDP and returnee individuals/households in frontline areas based on the cluster analysis report identifying the needs and the protection and do no harm considerations. Response interventions include assessing household items within the Malakal POC and across the country through mobile response modality, replacing the NFIs based on the needs assessment findings, the cluster standard, and recommendations. DRC will preposition supplies for emergency response under DRC mobile approach operating from Juba and will use pipeline supplies from the cluster for its static interventions. The project targets 24,000 beneficiaries (2,166HH) with NFIs replacement and 19,000 beneficiaries (3,166 HH) under mobile response intervention across the country. Activity 3: Provision of in-kind flood response kit: The Upper Nile state has been hard hit by flooding over the past few years, with continued challenges in a timely response. Using the static team based at Malakal, DRC will target the accessible location through Malakal to support the emerging flood emergencies with the Westbank of the Upper Nile state. The response target is to reduce the lead time and resources while mobilizing the Mobile response through Juba to also cover other flood affected locations. The project targets 35,000 beneficiaries (5,833 HH) through flood response support. Activity 4: Rehabilitation of communal and individual emergency shelter: Using a multi-sectoral approach and community engagement DRC will continue supporting the IDPs within the Malakal POC to rehabilitate their damaged emergency shelter. The rehabilitation will be conducted by engaging the IDPs trained artisan from the previous years and with direct supervision from the shelter technical team. DRC will identify and verify beneficiaries referred through the protection, GBV, or CCCM referral path and the complaint and feedback mechanisms (CRM) to receive shelter assistance. The SNFI cluster will provide the materials for rehabilitating the emergency shelters from the pipeline upon request. The project targets 3,900 beneficiaries (650HH) through rehabilitation of emergency shelter.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Through the multisectoral approach, DRC will mitigate any protection concerns that might arise by ensuring all aspects of implementation are in place. The protection team will ensure the complaint mechanism is put in place, regularly monitored and the required feedback is provided timely and using the approved channels. For instance, for a mobile response situation, a complain DRC will set a helpdesk to allow the beneficiaries to raise their complaints in their preferred wayDRC maintains working relationships with local authorities through regular meetings and coordination and sharing information on the nature of our planned implementation. Where the situation becomes complex DRC will engage OCHA to mediate between the parties involved.Using a community-based and multisectoral approach, DRC will use the support of the local leaders to ensure that there is crowd control that supports the most vulnerable and prioritizes service delivery. Further, DRC will disseminate information on the community's registration procedures during the assessment to raise awareness of the process and information on the community's target members. All the gatherings with the community will be conducted observing the laid down measure to mitigate the spread of Covid-19 and disseminating key messages to the community.Using the support of the multisectoral approach, the protection team will identify the most vulnerable groups within the community. The focus will be on the marginalized people such as ethnic minorities, pregnant and nursing women, elderly, unaccompanied and separated children, female-headed households, widows, persons with disabilities. DRC will ensure prioritization of the services to the group.1) The project will prioritize services to specific groups within the population, with a focus on marginalized people such as ethnic minorities, pregnant and nursing women, unaccompanied and separated children, female-headed households, widows, persons with disabilities, including those with physical, intellectual, and psychosocial disabilities- this will therefore result in consultations with such populations 2) DRC will engage with OPDs and community-based protection networks to ensure access to and visibility of these populations 3) DRC will provide IEC materials in multiple formats to ensure accessibility for all DRC will ensure to have diverse field teams in terms of age, gender, and other diversities such as ethnicity.
