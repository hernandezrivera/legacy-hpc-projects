Project Summary

Zimbabwe faces a growing level of chronic malnutrition which is exacerbated by food insecurity and deepening poverty. The humanitarian situation in Zimbabwe continues to drop with various shocks affecting the population. These include droughts, diarrhoeal diseases, flush floods (Cyclone Idai) and food insecurity. Deteriorating economic situations in Zimbabwe has further compounded vulnerabilities of the food and nutrition insecure population as negative impacts continue to affect vulnerable women and children below the age of five. The Zimbabwe Vulnerability Assessment Committee (ZIMVAC) has stated that almost 5.5 million people are in need of humanitarian assistance through April 2020. The levels of severe acute malnutrition (SAM) have increased from 2018 to 2019. This challenge is certainly beyond the capacity of a single sector or agency to address, hence requires multi-sectoral interventions. In addition, malnutrition is one of the most serious health problems affecting infants, children, and women of reproductive age in Zimbabwe. Despite progress to date, millions of children and women in Zimbabwe continue to suffer from one or more forms of undernutrition, including low birth weight, stunting, anemia, underweight, wasting, Vitamin A deficiency, and iodine deficiency disorders. Hence the need for a project that will scale up an emergency response to acute malnutrition and will bring recovery and community resilience building. Community Capacity Building Initiative of Africa (CCBICA) will work on this project focusing mainly on mobilization and awareness campaigns to the community targeting mothers and caregivers for the need for child malnutrition screening at ward level. It will also help in the distribution of micro-nutrients to children between 6-59 months, children below the age of five, and pregnant and lactating mothers (PLW). It will hold coordination and leadership meetings with the local government, District Food and Nutrition Committee (DFNC) and the Civil Protection Unit (CPU). This project will also be able to check on the well-being and living standards of people. It will also address the People in Need (PIN), collect data and focus on humanitarian responses. It will also focus on the Living Standards minimum acceptable diet (MAD), Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM).
