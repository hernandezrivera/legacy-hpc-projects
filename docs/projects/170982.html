Project Summary

The overarching objective is to provide time critical and quick impact support to the
vulnerable farming and herding families affected by man-made and natural shocks
including COVID-19 pandemic. Special focus will be on women and youth. The project aims at ensuring the resilience and protection of the targeted communities and reviving their livelihoods that is affected by the spread of the COVID-19 pandemic.
This project falls under the Food Security Sector Strategic Objective 1: “Livelihoods of
vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive
capacity restored/enhanced” of the HRP 2021 as it improves the coping abilities and
reduces shocks and environmental hazards of farmers and herders , with special focus on women and youth in those communities.
The project falls under Strategic Objective 3 of the Humanitarian Response Strategy "The capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with and overcome protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root
causes of threats and shocks are pursued."

1 - Needs

Female and male farmers and herders in the West Bank and Gaza Strip particularly in the Area C of the West Bank and Access Restricted Areas (ARA) in Gaza Strip suffer from limited access to public services, lack of access to affordable agricultural water, weak adaptive capacity and asset bases. Poor agricultural families, particularly those that are headed by females are particularly affected. Female-headed households are most at risk of suffering from lack of economic capacity to obtain the necessary inputs and basic requirements for meaningful farm and animal health management compared to their male-headed counterparts. This discrepancy is further evidenced by the fact that 30 percent of all female-headed households face moderate and severe food insecurity among compared to 25.8 percent of male-headed households (2018 FIES, outlined in SEFSec 2018, PCBS, WFP, FAO, UNRWA). Palestinian youth, within the ages of 18-29 make up one-third of the total population (PCBS, 2018) though face among the highest share of unemployment rates of 41% in the West Bank and 63% in the Gaza Strip (PCBS 2019). Additionally, women’s limited share of assets makes them increasingly vulnerable during difficult economic times. Overall, the work of rural women in the agricultural sector is under recognized and women farmers are considered “helpers” with many being absent in formal cooperatives regardless of the level of responsibility they have for the household’s agricultural work. Overcoming this gap requires the development of a new mechanism of women affirmative actions while raising their awareness of the skills and the roles they are playing in agriculture, thus protecting their rights and anchoring their role. Despite the large number and wide diversity of projects targeting agriculture, women do not effectively participate due to the socio-cultural obstacles limiting their participation. Water shortage is a serious issue that poses numerous problems for female and male farmers in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. In rural communities of both the West Bank(Slafeet: Dier Balout and Azzawyeh); Jericho (Jiftlik and fasayel), Qalqilia (Izbit Salaman, Hableh, and Azzoun Atmeh) and Gaza Strip area (Biet Hanoun, Jabalia, Dier AlBalah, and Rafah), women and youth are known to suffer more from irrigation water shortage as they tend to take the main responsibility for ensuring the availability and transport of irrigation water especially for small scale farming activities. In certain areas, the distance between the nearest water source and the farm can be few kilometers, causing women and youth extra work burden and time to perform these tasks. The availability of reliable reservoirs, networks and storage cisterns will have significant positive impact in reducing this extra burden on women and youth. Moreover, the COVID-19 pandemic has affected both herders and farmers immensely. Herders depend primarily on selling dairy products (mainly between March and July) and lamb meat (from May to August during feast time). However, due to COVID-19, livestock markets have been frequently closed and movement restrictions have affected the ability of herders to market their products. As a result, most herders have had to shift their dairy products to produce products with longer shelf lives (although less profitable) like stone yogurt (Jamed) and ghee. Some herders have had to sell their lambs and part from his herd at lower prices as demand for lamb meat declined. Due to a lack of liquidity in the market induced by the COVID-19 restrictions, fodder suppliers started to demand cash from herders, forcing herders to accept low prices for their products in order to obtain cash to ensure fodder availability. Similarly, for farmers, throughout 2020, the volume of agri-food trade remained low compared to pre- COVID-19 levels, affected by limited market access, export bans and low purchasing power of consumers, which reflects lower incomes. In Gaza, no exports were observed due to a ban on exports which has tightened after the first quarter of 2020. While low resilience farmers in the ARA typically lack the financial resources required to rehabilitate agricultural land, the COVID-19 pandemic has further exacerbated the situation (FAO biweekly quick survey on COVID-19 impacts on the Palestinian food system). Hence, this intervention aims at protecting the endangered livelihood of those farmers and herders in both the West Bank and Gaza Strip.

2 - Activities

Output 1: Coping capacities enhanced and resilience against environmental shocks improved for vulnerable families (with focus on women and youth) in the targeted herders’ communities in the WBGS .1.1 Distribution of drought tolerant seeds, in coordination with MoA. 1.2 Rehabilitation and/or construction of water cisterns, which would particularly lessen the burden of women in fetching water, from locations far from their house, and on youth.1.3 Rehabilitation of animal sheds which allows easier cleaning of animal barns.1.4 Small-scale poultry farms supported with water sprinkler network and small water pump for mitigation of climate change in summer.1.5 Distribution of small-scale farm management inputs (disinfection preventive material, salt blocks, wool and hooves secateurs). This helps to prevent animal disease and support animals’ wellbeing, accordingly the toolkit will support this purpose of animal disease prevention which takes off women the burden of caring for sick animals.1.6 Distribution of dairy processing equipment (milk jars, milk shakers, and cheese tanks) This intervention will save time and efforts on women as they are the main contributors for producing of dairy products. 1.7 Enhancing and enabling the market accessibility for women and youth through up scaling the capacity of the livestock market in Al Dahrieh and promoting their participation in local exhibitions and providing them with spaces to sell their products. FAO has previously implemented two phases in Al Dahrieh market under two different projects and this will complete the third stage of the market as recommended by the MoA. Output 2: Restored production capacities through immediate rehabilitation/repair of damaged water systems and improved management of water resources. Activity 2.1 restoring pumping efficiency of 3 underground wells through rehabilitation and the provision of 3 solar energy units for 150 farming households cultivating at least 450 dunums in Gaza Strip (Biet Hanoun, Jabalia, and Diwer Al-Balah). Activity 2.2. Rationalizing water use in irrigation through the rehabilitation of 5 damaged water storing towers for 100 farming households cultivating at least 300 dunums in Gaza Strip (East Rafah). Activity 2.3 Improving water accessibility and availability through the rehabilitation of 100 rainwater-harvesting cisterns for the benefit of at least 300 farming households cultivating at least 300 dunums in West Bank (Salfeet: Dier Balout and Azzawyeh)via Conditional Cash Assistance approach (each cistern volume not less than 70 m³). Activity 2.4 Improving water use optimization in irrigation through the rehabilitation of 10 irrigation water ponds in Jordan Valley (Jericho: Jiftlik and Fasayel) of West Bank via Conditional Cash Assistance approach, with total storage capacity up to 30 000 m³ for the benefit of at least 250 farming households cultivating at least 750 dunums. The Conditional Cash Assistance approach was noticed by FAO, through implementing several projects of similar nature, to be effective in addressing not only the needs of households and improve accessibility and availability of water used in irrigation, but also to inject cash (about 10% out of the total project cost) within targeted communities though contracting local labors and suppliers of the same targeted communities in order to perform the works. Consequently, this will capitalize benefits of the rehabilitation work through enabling and creating job opportunities in the targeted localities from one hand, and maximize the ownership of the rehabilitated assets. . Activity 2.5 Reduce water losses and restore water distribution efficiency through repairing/ replacing 90 Km of damaged conveyance pipes at farms level for the benefit of at least 360 farming households cultivating at least 1080 dunums in Qalqilia (Izbit Salaman, Hableh, and Azzoun Atmeh). Activity 2.6 Technical support to water users through the creation of 40 water users committees for at least 40 rehabilitated water systems (8 in Gaza Strip and 32 in West Bank). Output 3: ARA farmers supported through immediate rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs. Activity 3.1 - 500 farming households (HHs) supported with organic and basic chemical fertilizers for 2 dunums for each. Activity 3.2 - 200 dunums of small-scale farmer’s greenhouses rehabilitated with plastic sheeting, insect proof agricultural nets and required wood paneling for 1 dunum per HH. Activity 3.3 - 60 x plastic water harvesting ponds rehabilitated with plastic sheeting, shadow net, safety fence and water pump. Activity 3.4 - 35 concrete water harvesting ponds supported with layer of concrete, shadow net, safety fence and water pumps. Activity 3.5 training and coaching for beneficiaries of greenhouses and water harvesting ponds on irrigation water management conducted by the MoA. Throughout the selection process, FAO will target the most vulnerable and most at risk households due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Women headed households, women’s cooperatives and households whose female members are actively engaged in agriculture related activities in support of household income will be prioritized during the community targeting and beneficiary selection processes.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

A Grievance Redressal Procedure based on FAO guidelines and considering local best practices has been established and is currently functional. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

The interventions proposed under this project do not put beneficiaries in risk, however, FAO aims to ensure that all efforts will be made to prevent and minimize as much as possible unintended negative effects of the interventions. As part of the project inception phase, FAO will develop a risk log identifying all potential risks, their probability and ways of mitigation. The assessment and analysis of risks will be followed by a coordinated application of resources to monitor, minimize and control the probability and/or impact of risks. External risk factors will continuously be considered in order to ensure an adequate understanding of the context of FAO operations during the implementation of the project. FAO will also ensure the implementation of PSEA principles and will have a built-in process for reporting such incidents. The proposed intervention is meant to protect endangered livelihoods of vulnerable female and male farmers and herders in the West Bank and Gaza Strip through securing the availability of time critical indispensable inputs and services, hence the core of the intervention aims at protecting beneficiaries rather than doing any harm. FAO also abides by the principle of Do No Harm; and respects a low profile during implementation of activities to avoid any harm coming tobeneficiaries.Through FAO’s participation in the UN Country Team (UNCT) and Humanitarian Country Team (HCT), the development of the political situation in the WBGS will regularly be monitored and reviewed in line with project objectives. FAO contributes to policy-level initiatives to protect Palestinians from further reduction in access to land, sea and livelihood resources (e.g. water and agricultural inputs) owing to the political situation, through its participation in the UNCT Commendation Group and HCT Advocacy Working Group. All advocacy efforts will be conducted under the banner of the UN in the WBGS to ensure that FAO’s mandate is not compromised.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

FAO’s approach provides a framework for addressing and integrating issues such as gender equality, protection, the inclusion of the aged and people living with disability, and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse by FAO and partner staff. Throughout the process of beneficiary selection, disability and age will be a factor taken into consideration. Beneficiaries with this vulnerability will be prioritized. The project also project seeks to engage with targeted stakeholders and mainstream FAO’s commitments in each phase of the project cycle. FAO maintains its commitments through regular stakeholder engagement and participation, two-way communication, transparent disclosure and a grievance mechanism.
