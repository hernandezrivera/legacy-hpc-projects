Project Summary

Oxfam aims at addressing systemic risks (agro-ecological, local governance, gender inequalities, social tensions) by enhancing socio-economic rights and capacities of 3000 conflict affected households in Qalamoun- Rural Damascus of Syria; all family members (M&F inclusive) lost the ability to enjoy equal access to livelihood opportunities then fulfill their basic rights. Women and adolescent’s girls may face particularly protection risks as a harmful coping mechanism such as child marriage, physical and sexual harassments, etc. EFSVL team is addressing this vulnerability through immediate and sustainable solutions for start-up of micro-businesses, efficient linkages with suppliers and markets, entitlements and enhancing community resilience, including local capacities for social cohesion.
The results will be achieved through a range of outputs including setting-up 60 greenhouses, 1000 kitchen garden toolkits; immediate support to 1000 conflict affected vulnerable livestock breeders; strengthened productive capacity of 35 dairy, bakery and other food processors; technical and managerial skill building of 35 participants; training of 7 local veterinarian, drilling 3 new wells, powering 3 existing wells with solar system and rehabilitation of 3 boreholes will provide income generation opportunities to conflict impacted vulnerable women, men and their families ; bring improvements in market services, soil and water conservation; provision of agricultural inputs; technical support for improved agricultural production; collaboration, networking and alliance building with a range of stakeholders and enhancing communities’ capacity to respond to shock and stresses as a result of conflict.
During the project duration, agricultural and food production will focus on two agricultural seasons of critical crops in the targeted locations. These commodities will be selected based on local seasonal calendar, farmers’ skills and their access to inputs, consultation with communities and potential for supplementing food needs and will directly support the targeted beneficiaries and indirectly their supported families (elderly, boys and girls all inclusive).Furthermore, the project design is paying attention to very specific needs of targeted women and men; distributed kits will be designed to accommodate the differences between women and men. Time and distribution locations will be gender sensitive. Special attention will be paid for impact of intervention on power dynamics in targeted communities to avoid stressing the negative social norms in roles and responsivities of women and men.
This action is based on Oxfam’s approach on developing resilient livelihoods, which integrates humanitarian relief, socioeconomic development, and empowered advocacy through improving social protection/safety nets and strengthening market system. This approach provides a coherent and gender sensitive framework to achieve the action objective, through its processes that concurrently address short-term targeted agricultural outcomes and spillover effects from strengthening of market system.
Through this action, target households will increase capacity to cope with shocks and avoid the harmful survival mechanism such as early, forced and child marriages, child laboring, etc. It increase the levels of agriculture production and income. Through Oxfam’s approach, this will strengthen community organizations, and enhance their ability to access services.

Needs

Al Qutayfah district of Rural Damascus (Sub districts of Al Qalamoun, Jirud, Raheiba) has experienced more than four years of deteriorating socio-economic conditions coupled with severe seasonal drought that have resulted in vulnerable rural populations facing a multiplicity of challenges including food insecurity (especially in the non-state controlled areas), compromised livelihood, poor living conditions and high level of poverty. However, these challenges have triggered community members; especially in the non-state controlled areas to join armed groups including extremist groups even though the communities in these areas were known to have prescribed pre-conflict to moderate Islam, and have voted for left-wing political parties. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (2019), the total population estimated at 144,212, with 10,788 categorized as internally displaced, distributed across Al Qutayfah 4 Sub-districts. Women estimated at 58.5% and 3.5% disabilities. 2016 HNO indicates that the percentage of people of working age who are unemployed was 25%, 45% and more than 60% in Qutayfah, Jirud and Raheiba respectively. Most of the IDPs have been in these areas for more than one year (protracted displacement), and the main reason of displacement is the direct threat of violence and clash in the areas of origin. Most of the IDPs in the area have moved from Eastern Ghouta, which has sizeable territory under opposition control. The area has witnessed a ceasefire agreement on April 2018, based on this agreement opposition fighters who did not desire to hand over their weapons were moved to Idleb, the whole area became under Government control.The unabated violence and dire humanitarian situation continues to have a devastating impact on women and men, boys and girls, They face multiple protection risks and violations of their rights on a daily basis, including early forced and child marriages, child laboring, limited access to basic services, lack of access to education, profound psychosocial distress, physical and economic violence. Chronic unemployment and limited entrepreneurship and participation opportunities have resulted in negatively impacting the transition of young people / adults in Al Qutayfah to adulthood and limits their potential to be full-fledged socially and economically productive members of their communities. These problems have been specially negatively impacting female headed households and persons with disability located in the area they are less capacitated to access scarce resources and disrupted markets. Gender dynamics has been changing as many men have been forced to flee from the conflict for several reasons, which affected and reduced men presence in many families. Thus, women had to assume both the role of caretaker and breadwinner, making them significant participants in the economy, society and family life. However, due to differential gender roles and discriminatory legal provisions, households with a female main income provider have less access to services, resources and justice. Women are often found to be the head of the household carrying the financial burden; one in every three households in Syria are now women-headed. In brief, provision of positive opportunities for learning and positive social, civic and economic engagement as well as stressing food security intervention are an essential protection strategy for directly and indirectly targeted vulnerable people.

Implementation of activities

The methods of implementation and rationale for such methodology;Resilience building approach, support and protection to vulnerable and conflict sensitivity approach- The Action will be developed through a bottom-up participatory approach involving small scale farmers, local committees, private sector actors, farmers’ associations to enable the beneficiaries themselves to clearly identify their current needs and gaps, establish selection criteria for target beneficiaries, and find solutions to address their constraints. Women participation in identifying their and their families’ needs is essential in consultation processThe sense of ownership of the targeted beneficiaries will be ensured through an active participation, from the beginning of the Action throughout its whole execution. Proposed activities:- Raise awareness among communities especially among young people on enterprise development - Market mapping and establish linkages between communities and input suppliers (Gender analysis, HEA, VRA, EMMA).- Set clear criteria to select market actors willing to start input supply businesses and identify successful candidates- Provide in-kind support for machinery and raw materials to private traders to boost input supplies- Provide community enterprises in kind support on nurseries, green houses for seeds and compost production; at least 50% women.- Train farmer on production planning and natural resource management- Provision of gender sensitive productive toolkits- Support setting-up 60 greenhouses, and 1000 conflict affected households with kitchen garden toolkits; at least 50% women- Provide small livestock assets as immediate support to 1000 vulnerable livestock breeders; at least 50% women - Vocational training and provision of in-kind support; at least 50% women and young women- Provide support to local food enterprises (35 in-kind supports; at least 50% women - Mapping and identification of water sources to link with renewable energy sources- Drill 3 new solar powered boreholes including headwork for community use- Rehabilitate 3 borehole for crop diversification- Set-up water user committee for maintenance and sustainability. Equal participation and presence of women and men is essential.- Establish 3 committees and strengthen 3 existing committees: of which women and young people committees - Training on improved livestock husbandry practices and local regulations- Train and equip groups of veterinarians- Organize annual learning events for women, men and young people - Organise multi-stakeholder seminars on environment friendly technology use for agriculture in Syria; equal participation for men and women , adolescents and young people (M&F). - Measurement of impact through participatory mid-term review and end line participatory evaluation- Quarterly partnership review workshops with partners and stakeholders. The review will look into gender and age equalities throughout project cycle (including women participation in decision making and address inclusively people’s needs).

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Inequality in distribution. Mitigation Measures: Promote Establishment of adequate management plan. Promote involvement communities in distribution planning and feedback on package for improvement; distribution plans should address equal and equitable distribution for women and men. Risk :Physical and psychological harm arising from harassment, violence and coercion,Risk of theft of assists which may be attractive to groups inside Syria Mitigation Measures: Agencies provide feedback and accountability mechanisms to usersWork closely with all partners on the ground. Raise awareness and apply Oxfam safeguarding three policies to prevent and response to abusive and violence behaviours. Utilize referral pathway in coordination with protection sector and Oxfam gender team. Risk :Stress negative Gender power dynamics (roles and responsibilities of women and men). Mitigation Measures: Apply Oxfam gender minimum standards. Harmonise and standardise gender friendly assets among the sector members and indicate quantity and instruction for use. Project gender sensitive analysis and community effective participation expected to provide clearer understanding on systemic influencing factors including power dynamics, shifting gender roles and responsibilities and intra-community cohesion. This would adequately support process of adopting activities to the deferential needs. Risk :Misappropriation from armed groups or Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion. Mitigation Measures: Agencies are aware and apply best practice . Agencies involve communities in organizing joint planning and avoid duplication ,Agencies assist people to claim their rights, access available remedies and recover from the effects of abuse through Oxfam safeguarding focal point. Risk:Unequal distribution of services inadvertently empower or strengthen the position of armed groups or other actors.Mitigation Measures: Establish strong links with authorities and operators to promote equity and “do no harm principle” all the time; Organise joint planning culture and agree on minimum standard Include community and authorities in targeting. Ensure sensitive information is handled with care. Risk: Risk of theft of assets by groups and used in conflict activities.Mitigation Measures: MoUs to be agreed with local authorities/councils to ensure local ownership of materials. Risk :Possibility of explosion and human casualty and asset destruction. Mitigation Measures: Promote cost recovery systems for longer term ownership to assets to promote community ownership Selection of distribution location to ensure availability of safe place nearby. Agree with partner on mitigation measures to comply when required. Ensure proper storage of supplies with clear provision of care and safety consideration.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk :Escalation of conflict resulting in insecurity in the vicinity of project locations which may limit Oxfam and its partners’ abilities to achieve project objectives.Mitigation Measures: The issue will be addressed as per Oxfam’s security management protocol. These include coordination with agencies, UNDSS, INSO, relevant ministries and security networks. Development partners will be informed and updated on situation and way forward.Risk Difficulty in accessing communities for data collection in order to assess their actual needs and/or measure impact.Mitigation Measures: 1) Oxfam has developed new relationships with the Ministry of Social Affairs and the Ministry of Local Affairs to help aid in data collection and assessment at the local level. 2) To reach vulnerable groups in hard-to-reach areas, coordinate and collaborate with private contractors and partners with enhances capacities to access these areas. 3) Adoption of remote reporting techniques (short SMS, mobile videos, simple verification check-lists, etc.) to allow for data collection and reporting.Rapid increase of beneficiaries in targeted locations. Mitigation Measures: Oxfam will work with other actors to meet expanding needs. This will minimise duplication and appropriate and efficient use of available resources. Risk Increased tension between IDPs, returnees and host communities. Mitigation Measures: Oxfam will encourage involvement and representation of all conflicted affected from inception to completion. To enhance social cohesion conflict impacted vulnerable households from IDPS, returnees and host communities will participate in the action.Risk: Limited equipment and supplies available in the Syrian market due to instability, sanctions and limited imports delays or prevents the implementation of project activities.Mitigation Measures: Work with other international humanitarian organisations to see what other options exist to get needed supplies and equipment in country while remaining in compliance with applicable sanctions and counter terror legislation. Purchase materials through regional or international tenders. Risk Delays in approval on partnership with local NGOs, and project activities due to Syrian government bureaucracy.Mitigation Measures: Capitalise on local partners’ existing rapport with concerned line ministries to seek approval. Submit joint application as consortia for approval. Advocate with government. Risk of compromised impartiality and independence.Mitigation Measures: Needs are verified through triangulation of data through various sources such as Oxfam’s site engineers, sector and actors. Oxfam has redlined on advocacy, impartiality and independence, and safety and security. When either of these is at a risk of being compromised, Oxfam conducts a review to identify the best way to proceed. Risk Exchange rate fluctuations between the Euro and the SYP. Mitigation: Regular monitoring, Notifying Donors, Payments to suppliers in Tranches.
