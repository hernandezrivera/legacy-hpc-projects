Project Summary

Save the Children will deliver an inclusive, gender responsive & comprehensive healthcare services and will reach around 90,277 beneficiaries through 8 HP and 1 PHCC & SARI ITC over a 12-month period in 8 camps of Ukhiya and Teknaf, Cox’s Bazar to ensure that diverse Rohingya refugees and host communities have equitable access to essential healthcare services. This Health facilities will deliver primary health care service with special focus on 24/7 emergency care, sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support and noncommunicable diseases in line with the MoH and Health Sector recommended Minimum Package of Essential Service Package. Along with regular health consultation, PHCC will offer IPD service with 15 beds (5 child, 5 male, 5 female); laboratory; PMTCT; BEmONC service with 5 bedded maternity unit & SGBV services (CMR, IPV and Domestic Violence). All this health facility will offer integrated services with Nutrition (IYCF-E), WASH(IPC) and Child Protection by ensuring coordination and referral in between, establishing a child friendly corner considering Do no harm policy. To ensure IPC measure and safe disposal practice to avoid chance of infection, a dedicated IPC focal will work closely with facility level IPC committee and provide necessary guidance aligned with IPC WG and MoH. Considering living with COVID-19 strategy, SCI PHCC & SARI ITC will merge to manage COVID-19 cases through its 20 COVID-19 dedicated beds along with regular PHCC operation. Overall, this center will act as a multifunctional outbreak center where other communicable diseases (AWD, Measles, Diphtheriae, etc) will be managed along with the COVID-19. As per the guidelines, health facilities are staffed by Doctors, Midwives, Medical Assistant, Nurse, Dispenser, MHPSS officer, clinic aides, IYCF-E counsellors and MCHN promoters. Clinical services will be complemented with health promotion and community engagement activities through a robust community health workers(CHWs) to encourage timely access to healthcare and address existing challenges in the community level. SCI CHWs will work in their catchment area of 8 different camps in Ukhiya, Teknaf and its surrounding host communinty as organized by Community Health Working Group(CHWG) to avoid service overlapping with other partners and CHWs will visit around 17546 House Hold. Community based surveillance(CBS) will be continued by CHWs to identify COVID-19 patients in the communities. All staff and volunteers will be trained on CP, child safeguarding and PSEA. To enhance clinical excellence, team will be trained on prevention & management of communicable & non-communicable disease, IMCI, IPC and on other identified needs. There will be 2 Mobile Medical team (MMT) aligned with Emergency Preparedness Response Working Group (EPRWG) will be providing services in any emergency health response following any natural or manmade disaster. All the health facilities will be accessible and environment friendly for all age group and also for PWD by ensuring ramp, disable friendly latrine, triage area, signage, lighting and skilled workforce to prioritize and manage geriatric population and PWD.
SCI has a robust MEAL system and according to the Program’s Logical Framework, develop a MEAL plan including Monitoring Plan that specifies indicators, data disaggregated by sex, age and disability etc. Data will be managed internally using SCI’s web-based Information Management System (IMPACT) and externally with EWARS and DHIS2. In regards of Accountability to Affected Population(AAP) , SCI is applying multiple channels for Feedback Response and Reporting Mechanism i.e Hotline, Partner Led Data Collections, Help Desk & Exit Interview.

1 - Identified Needs

The essential health needs of Rohingya and host community continue to be critical. The key health needs of both communities are very similar; prevention and treatment of communicable diseases, ANC & PNC services, emergency obstetric care services, reproductive and child health, and SGBV related clinical case management. According to MSNA, 2021, though there are only 9% household (HH) expressed Health as priority need, it’s shown multiple segregated need of older population and person with disabilities. 11% of individuals reported as having had a health problem and needing to access health care and 86% of them sought treatment for it (J-MSNA, 2021, RR). About 1/3rd of them went into debt to pay for health expense privately, and procured medicine from outside the camps. In camps, 44% of HH reported having experienced or expecting experiencing barriers when needing to access health care like long waiting time/overcrowded, unavailability of specific/right medicine, service needed. About 66% of individuals under the age of 2, by reported location of birth was at home, 30% at NGO clinic, 3% in Govt. clinic and 1% maternity ward and 20% of HH from both Camp & host communities reported at least one child born at home after August 2017. About 24% of individuals from host community reported as having had a health problem and needing to access health care and 58% of them sought treatment for it (J-MSNA, 2021, host Communities) and majority of the respondents went into debt to pay for health expenses. According to REACH BGD Factsheet (May 2021), In Rohingya Camp, the prevalence of persons with disabilities (aged 2 and above) was 12% & there was no difference in disability prevalence between male and female individuals. However, findings suggest an increasing disability prevalence with increasing age. 17% & 35% of HH reportedly had at least one older person and at least one PWD respectively. Among adults, 20% of individuals were identified as PWD. Among the 5 to 17 year-olds, 3% & Among the 2 to 4 year-olds, overall 2% were identified as PWD. Overall, more than half the PWD had reportedly not received any assistive devices in the year prior to data collection despite needing them. This proportion was highest among female older persons with disabilities. 76% of persons with disabilities aged 15 and above reportedly face difficulties moving around camps. Findings in End Line Evaluation of Save the Children (SC) (September 2021), Unavailability of Blood donation bank for critical patients, adequate specialized service like eye care, dental care, advanced skin disease and orthopedic treatment etc. is also decrease the standard of overall health care services for both Rohingya & Host. Additionally, the need to scale up structured and effective MHPSS services remains urgent. The SRH needs for high-risk and special-need populations (LGBTQI), including survivors of GBV & adolescents, are yet to be addressed. Married and pregnant adolescent girls still need to be targeted through facility and community support groups to reduce their high risks of maternal mortality and morbidity. There is a need to strengthen SRH care, including BEmONC, access to safe FP, maternal and new-born health services. Unsafe home delivery and delay in the uptake of PNC follow-up also put newborns and mothers at risk of avoidable complications. Analysis of data from SC’s facilities shows that up to 10% of newborns present with medical complications during PNC follow-up. Reasonable male engagement in health services yet not achieve, thus affect health-seeking behavior due to unequal power exercise in make the decision on SRH for their spouses. GBV is under-reported, and clinical staff have limited capacity to manage its medical and psychosocial impact. There are multiple communicable & other disease outbreaks such as chickenpox, measles, diphtheria and Dengue, confirming the understanding that the current setting is a “perfect storm” for disease outbreaks

2 - Activities or outputs

1.Provide accessible quality integrated health care services, with focus on Maternal and neonatal & Child Health, Adolescent Health, Communicable disease, non-communicable diseases, infection prevention and control and mental health and psychosocial services through Health facilities. 2.Under two years of children and pregnant women from camps and host communities immunized through fixed site vaccination center. 3.Provide quality SRH services (e.g., ante-natal care, post-natal care, family planning, treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STIs)/Reproductive Tract Infections (RTIs) in a gender responsive environment with adequate privacy from three health posts. 4.Provision of Comprehensive medical services to SGBV survivors with ensuring adequate privacy and confidentiality for clinical management and refer to protection/mental health support services . 5.Maintain with Responsive Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan with have the capacity to detect out-break/epidemics and initiate a timely response to control the out-break in align with the support of health Sector contingency planning for emergency Health response to other adversity e.g., Cyclones, Monsoons, mass displacements through deploying Mobile Medical Teams (MMTs). 6.Health Facility provided with essential primary healthcare supplies, family planning commodities, medical and non-medical supplies in a gender responsive manner. 7.Capacity building initiative of frontline health workers and volunteers, relevant health personnel to ensure quality health care services with the effective coordination of the ministry of health and Health sector. 8.Provision of IYCF-E services to Pregnant and lactating women and Girls (PLW/G) and mothers of children <2 years received nutrition counselling including screening and referrals of malnourished children. 9.Maintain hand washing facilities, water networking and supply system and hospital waste management and IPC measures in health facility level. 10.Provide community and facility based MHPSS services (including mhGAP) addressing Mental Health conditions and related impaired function. 11.Strengthen the local Health Systems by increasing the functional health service delivery outlet such as Community Clinics and Union level centers (Health Sub-Centers and Family Welfare Centers) as part of local health system strengthening. 12.Provision of preventive and promotive community Health activities through conduction of topic-based awareness sessions, distribution of gender sensitive IEC/BCC materials, demographic surveillance including CBS by trained Community Health Workers (CHWs).
