Project Summary

AHF will maintain provision of life-sustaining and curative nutrition services through the stabilization center in Sarmada children’s hospital which is considered the pediatric referral hospital in Idleb governorate, 3 OTP centers in Dana Subdistrict, Sarmada, Atareb and Salama (this locations according to the severity scale considered 4 which is considered high priority according to the nutrition response prioritization strategy for 2024-2025. In addition to 14 RRTs will fill the gaps in 6 subdistricts in Idleb (Dana, Jisr- Ash-Shugur, Ariha), & in Aleppo (AlBab, A'zaz, Atareb) covering more than 39 communities & 49 camps high severity. and according to PIN and severity scale all these locations considered high severity 4 on the severity score, while AHF will ensure these RRTs covering the camps and communities are located far away from any health services to be in align with services minimum package. Also, providing CVA to 244 PLWs and CU5 according to the guidance.
all these as considers the severity of geographical areas, distinguishing between prevention and curative interventions. 2 RRTs Jisr-Ash-Shugur (38,105), 2 RRTs Ariha (26,177), 4 RRTs AlBab ( 42,702), 1 RRT Azaz (131,938), 1 RRT Atareb (51,985) while the total number of disabled population in these 6 subdistrict 365,303, the number of targeted CU5 194,940, 90,850 PLWs. The staff of the centers and the RRTs are qualified and well-trained in many topics in the nutrition cluster (CMAM& IYCF), Also, PSEA and GBV.
The nutrition services include: - Outpatients therapeutic Program Centre (OTP), 3 centers in Sarmada, Salama and Atareb run by 1 pediatrician, 2 Nutritionists, 1 Nurse, and 1 data Entry cover OPD services on a daily, providing assessment and management of outpatient pediatrics with malnutrition including SAM, MAM patient follow up, routine assessment and treatment for MAM and SAM without complication, receive referrals from RRTs to OTP Centre. - Stabilization Centers in Sarmada children’s hospital and planned Afrin hospital: this is run by 2 pediatrician, 1 Nutritionist, 4 nurses (1 nurse every 8 hours, 3 shifts), and 1 data Entry, 3 cleaner cover inpatient services daily 24/7, providing daily assessment and management of inpatient pediatrics with SAM with complication. - RRTs: 14 RRTs, each team includes: 2 Nutritionists, 2CHW, and 1 data Entry, covers outreach services: Routine assessment and treatment for MAM and SAM without complication, screening, referral to OTP center and follow-up CU5, pregnant and lactating women who are malnourished, Providing emergency IYCF counseling for mothers with moderately malnourished children,distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and pregnant, lactating women.
IYCF-E recommended feeding practices, , Support health/nutrition education and promotion. In addition distribute 244 CVA to PLWs and CU5, CVA will be provided as Preventable measure for who not receiving GFA for pregnant in the first trimester women and lactating women for children under two (pregnant: 45. Lactating 45) with 55$ for 8 rounds. Also, providing CVA for children who have malnutrition cases to facilitate access to health services to improve treatment outcomes, with 30$ as an estimation of the transportation costs for minimum 6 rounds until recovery and discharge. During the project implementation, the M&E team will regularly collect the required data to help make enlightened decisions. Monitoring and evaluation of the quality of the service provision through analyzing the collected data based on the Log-Frame, the project will be monitored and evaluated through three levels: 1) The operational, for which the project manager is responsible. 2)The technical, for which the technical team is responsible. 3) The objectives, for whichthe MEAL team is responsible. Monitoring tools: • Performance Indicator Tracking Tables(PITTs), Satisfaction surveys and Project Evaluation Report,by the end of the project term.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

According to the HNO 2024 5.7 million people (3.9 million female, 1.8 million male) in need. which 65% of them 3.7M are children in dire need of nutritional assistance in Syria, the prevalence of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) has surged threefold in the past five years, escalating from 1.7 per cent in 2019 to 4.7 per cent in 2023 across Syria.AHF will work on recruitment, provide the salaries to the staff, and the operational costs, and provide capacity building program covering many topics (IYCF& CMAM), PSEA and GBV. Through 2 SCs, 4 OTPs, 14 RRTs and 244 CVA. AHF will provide: - Screening, referral and follow-up children 6-59, PLWs who are malnourished. -Provide emergency IYCF counseling for mothers with MAM children. -Disseminating IYCF-E messages among mothers of CU5 - Registering all screened cases and reporting. - Distribute micronutrients for preventing anemia and malnutrition among children and PLWs Disseminate messages (IEC/BCC) on IYCF-E. - Nutrition Supplements distribution. - Disseminate messages (IEC/BCC) on IYCF-E. - Referral CU5 with dangers signs, referral PLWs with Medical problems. - Referral of medical cases to nearby PHCs, As AHF has been developed SOPs for Referral TOR to distinguishing the PWDs which was distributed and explained to the teams - Treatment MAM in CU5 by OTP and the RRTs. - Treatment SAM uncomplicated in CU5 by the OTPs. -Treatment SAM with complications in CU5 by the stabilization centers. - Identifying the abused cases among boys and girls 6-59 months, pregnant and lactating women to be referred to child protection partners to take an action. -Monthly 4Ws reporting to the cluster, In addition distribute 244 CVA to PLWs and CU5, CVA will be provided as Preventable measure for who not receiving GFA for pregnant in the first trimester women and lactating women for CU2 (pregnant: 45. Lactating 45) with 55$ for 8 rounds. Also, providing CVA for children who have malnutrition cases to facilitate access to health services to improve treatment outcomes, with 30$ as an estimation of the transportation costs for minimum 6 rounds until recovery. Risks assumptions and mitigations will be considered, full coordination with the cluster, local communities and other NGOs, availability of the qualified staff. Timely provision of services. Achieving the planned targets. And depends on the AHF solid experience the common risks are the number of IDPs increased and the capacity of the project and services are not enough. To mitigate AHF will coordinate with the cluster and the partners providing the same services on the ground to fill these gaps, also AHF will work on secure an additional fund to expand services. Also, Cholera transmission within the project staff. To mitigate AHF apply strict IPC measures after train all the staff on IPC, and daily disinfect surfaces and sterilize periodically.
