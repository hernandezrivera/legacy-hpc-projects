Project Summary

Community Development Association (CDA) is a Non-Government Development Organization registered with the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare and the NGO Affairs Bureau, Bangladesh and have been working with the Landless Rural Poor people, Disable, Ethnic Minorities for their poverty reduction and empowerment since 1986. And HumaniTerra International, France is an international INGO providing surgical support to Bangladesh with different NGO’s from 2000. CDA/HTI in partnership have opened a surgical clinic in Ukhiya, December 2019 and provided high quality 24/7 emergency obstetrical care and general surgical services, mentored by visiting international surgeons, anesthesiologists to both the Rohingya refugees and the host communities especially focus on CEmONC services. From December 2019 this Surgical clinic bridge the gap in surgical intervention and provided 24/7 services. The project addressed both Rohingya and Host communities. And we have provided General surgery around - 300 cases, cesarean section – around 100 and screened for general elective surgery – 1400 beneficiaries.
As there is a gap in Comprehensive SRHR services, referral, CEmONC (C-Section) and surgical services there need to bridge these gap areas by our past experiences, ready OT, trained surgeons and by the support of other stakeholders in Ukhiya and Teknaf as referral secondary surgical facility.
This project will address these gaps by
• Upgrading and improving the health facility structures to ensure space for safe and Comprehensive SRHR services by Surgical interventions
• Provision of essential maternal healthcare services as per minimum standards including focus on CEmONC and elective general surgery for the beneficiaries of Ukhiya and Teknaf. For specialize 24/7 emergency services 3 sets of skill OT staff will be ensure.
• Improve the health seeking behavior of Beneficiaries through increased engagement of the community
Target- 9000 WRA

1 - Identified Needs

Bangladesh is experiencing one of the worst humanitarian crises in its history, due to the recent influx of hundreds of thousands of Rohingyas coming from Myanmar. The UN estimates that as of the end of September, more than 680,000 Rohingyas have fled to Bangladesh since the latest round of violence started on August 25. Other estimates put the number closer to 800,000 making it difficult for the Government, NGOs, and local inhabitants to arrange essential shelter, food and water, medicine, sanitation, and clothes for the Rohingya people.In addition, the population of Cox Bazar district especially in Ukhiya and Teknaf is highly in need of medical surgical assistance, especially C-section in CEmONC services, Comprehensive high quality 24/7 SRHR - emergency obstetrical care and general surgical services to both the Rohingya refugees and the host communities especially focus on CEmONC services in Ukhiya and Teknaf areas. The needs of the population are changing from acute emergency to more regular health needs the situation remains very critical and major gaps remain evident and unaddressed. The majority of health facilities are temporary structures and mostly providing OPD services, some PHCs are providing normal vaginal delivery, few organizations are providing CEmONC and other surgical services are rare in both Ukhiya and Teknaf areas. The availability and accessibility of 24/7 health services remains a challenge and very few agencies are providing 24/7 services in the camp and the host community. Furthermore, health services across the camps and NGOs are not standardized and fail to adhere to the essential primary health care services packages (ESP), such as sexual reproductive services, non-communicable disease services which results in patients receiving varying degree of quality health assistance. The access to services is overall more restricted for women, one of the reasons may include limited number of female staff and that services are not segregated. Women’s mobility is generally controlled by men and they require a man’s permission to visit a health center. It is therefore likely that access to family planning is still controlled by men. low utilization of sexual and reproductive health services is observed and is reflective of challenges in access and availability of services, Women continue to deliver at home instead of facility-based deliveries. Regarding obstratical data January and June 2020 were a result of PPH (10%) followed by Severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia related cases (6%). On the other hand the majority of FP visits were from first time visitors (58%). The most preferred FP method was the Oral contraceptive pill (56%) followed by injectable contraceptives (33.6%) and condoms (8.7%). Also, needs for high-risk and special need populations including sex workers, survivors of gender-based violence and adolescents are yet to be addressed. The violence experienced by the refugees prior and during the exodus and the emotional distress of being forcefully uprooted from their home and separated from family has resulted in the need MHPSS. Comprehensive SRHR services and surgical intervention facilities for 24/7 is very essential for FDMN and host community in Ukhiya and Teknaf areas, FP services, non-communicable disease care such as hypertension and diabetes, including diagnostics, treatments and continuum of care. The program needs to be strengthened and expanded to protect the population from potential outbreaks. COVID-19, Hep-C, HIV continues to be a challenge.Bangladesh is a land of natural disaster due to its vulnerable global location. And surgical facilities for post disaster victims are still absent in the camp. This project will fill the gap areas especially surgical interventions to the post disaster victims. Adolescents knowledge on SRHR and services and confidential counseling are also emerging health challenges and need to be addressed.
