Project Summary

IRC intends to target crisis affected communities with livelihoods activities to cushion them, the affected communities in various states needs to recover from the shocks, the crisis and the economic collapse, The IRC team intends to target men and women in host communities to be able to produce staple foods, and other cash crops to boos economic status of small scale farmers whose value chain has been affected by the ongoing crisis in Sudan. IRC through its interventions intends to improve the agricultural productivity of the affected population. The purpose of the food security support is to take most vulnerable populations from further deteriorating into severe food insecurity, to make sure that the IPC levels of affected communities' is reduced. To improve the food diversity and dietary diversity by most persons at all the time. The country is facing shortages of food stocks and this has caused many to be in poverty, and worse malnutritional levels. IRC will work with livelihoods partners to improve the seed systems of affected persons so that they can produce. All the targtetd persons who can produce will be trained to do smart agriculture. Conservative farming. IRC believes the target will be food secure in the long and short season, also that the communities will be able to sell surplus and look after their families as before. IRC believes that the communities will recover from the shocks if they are empowered to practice farming and look after their livestock. In some areas some veterinary trainings will be done to preserve the loss of livestock due to diseases or other animal health problems. Sudan is rich in livestock and cropping activities as a result these should be upholded at all costs by livelihoods practitioners. In The FSL interventions IRC intends to partner with local non governmental organisations and other cluster members to carry out assessments to the target group and see what they need most. IRC will also use desktop reviews to come up with a response plan in hard to reach areas especially in contested areas with a target majority of farmers and small scale farmers. The needs being analayzed it will be easy to reach the targets and to scale up the food security support in the chosen areas. The affected communities needs agricultural inputs and equipment so that they produce for domestic consumption and go to markets with surplus. IRC has been in these areas on intervention and because of its presence field teamd have so established good relations with local leadership and other value chain systems. This will enable IRC livelihoods team to effectively implement without any bottlenecks. IRC teams have now institutional memory that will build up the needs of the affected communities and recommend any food security support interventions in the chosen geographical locations. Despite the ongoing crisis, IRC teams will work in these areas and taking caution in security , also the team will feed in other ICCG meetings at state levels and share notes on project interventions and share challenges in any.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

IRC takes protection mainstreaming as the core at its design and implementation. These issues are taken serious and women , girls, men and boys concerns are taken care of at design and implementation levels. A core principle of the IRC’s work is the protection and promotion of rights. The project will integrate four key elements of protection mainstreaming: (1) meaningful access to assistance and services; (2) prioritizing safety, dignity and doing no harm; (3) accountability and participation; and (4) empowerment of beneficiaries. By facilitating family discussions, GBV risks which may arise as a result of the cash transfers will be mitigated against. Where possible, the project will connect with protection programs to ensure referral mechanisms and support services are available for survivors or those at risk. All IRC staff undergo an induction on the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) and are required to sign the IRC’s Code of Conduct, the IRC Way, which mandates reporting any possible PSEA cases. The IRC implements and monitors programs through the inclusion of clients and other stakeholders in the feedback mechanism on its delivery performance. Clients will be instructed on how to report any IRC related misuse of resources, lack of effectiveness, timeliness or responsiveness. A communication structure, the Complaints Response Mechanism, is in place and includes guidance on how to handle complaints and report them to the IRC Management. The IRC will identify and target households and individuals that are most in need of economic support for inclusion in the program. The project will give special attention to women, widows, people with disabilities, etc. In addition, it is understood that the improved income realized from cash transfers will mitigate the negative effects of strategies that households use to cope with emergencies. Whilst children will not be direct beneficiaries of food security and livelihoods programs, their survival, food security, nutrition, protection, health and education will be key considerations in eligibility and targeting criteria for livelihoods packs at the household level. In the same vein, the IRC will also ensure ample and priority support is given to households that have people living with a disability and the elderly. The design of FSL projects will also be informed by adequate consultations with this group to ensure that their safety is considered in the design and location of in kind collection points.

4 - AAP & PSEA
4.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback? Please select as many as relevant.
4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

The project will use community feedback to improve in some gray areas, also the feedback to be used to check the quality of the work that is implemented, if there are concerns they are picked up very early and addressed before something big happens. Also feedback protect all by getting risk concerns on time. To ensure vulnerable women and children have safe access to the feedback mechanisms and IRC support, the IRC will consult with women, girls, boys and their caregivers who may be isolated in their homes. The IRC will systematically collect feedback and concerns from clients through a combination of proactive and reactive channels when making programmatic decisions. By utilizing inclusive and accessible Client-Responsive programming and feedback mechanisms, the IRC aims to address the barriers faced by persons with disabilities and advance their access to humanitarian services on an equal basis with other clients across all sectors.To strengthen accountability to affected people, the IRC will routinely collect and analyse data on beneficiaries’ perspectives, complaints and feedback on the quality and relevance of project interventions. The IRC will maintain and strengthen existing complaints and feedback mechanisms, develop applications to digitize the data, create awareness through already existing and trusted community leaders’ structures, design and disseminate information, education and communication materials, and conduct dialogues with at risk groups. A gender sensitive complaint and feedback mechanism (CFM) will be ensured by equal numbers of female and male members to provide friendly channels for women to voice their issues (including GBV and abuse related, threats and grievances) through fellow women leaders. The IRC will routinely include gender awareness sensitization messaging during training of project community-beneficiary selection committees (CBSC), project monitoring committees and CFM members.Following IRC Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) feedback and complaints will be responded to within the agreed timeline, based on the urgency of the request. The IRC will facilitate monthly client feedback committee meetings to reflected on progress of the project and identify hindrances to access and service delivery for our beneficiaries, this will ensure coherence with the AAP principles and promote inclusivity of vulnerable groups, fostering equal opportunities for all. Feedback will guide decision-making and inform course-correct interventions in order to achieve better, more sustainable outcomes for beneficiaries in line with their priorities and preferences. Under its Client Responsiveness and Accountability (CRA) division, the IRC offers ongoing accountability for programming through proactive, reactive, and anonymous channels. Proactive feedback channels include focus group discussions, client satisfaction surveys, or meetings with community members or key beneficiaries, which are utilised to gather feedback from beneficiaries (girls, women, boys, men, older persons, with and without disabilities). Additionally, reactive feedback channels available for beneficiaries may include strategically placed suggestion boxes, a hotline, SMS, WhatsApp messages, walk-in hours and other mechanisms to be adapted as identified by different client groups e.g. children. Each piece of feedback received is handled in a way that adheres to the IRC’s client confidentiality policy and the IRC Way.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The IRC will work closely with other agencies and address the concerns in an open fora. Issues of sexual exploitation at the country level will be dealt with seriously and investigated to see why some issues are happening that way. All issues of sexual exploitation will be documented and shared at inter agency meetings. Resolutions and Action taken against perpetrators will be documented too and shared and some lessons learnt will be shared. The IRC will work with other partners and come up with a robust SOP to be shared with partners and in this SOP there will be information on how sexual exploitation offenders will be dealt with.
