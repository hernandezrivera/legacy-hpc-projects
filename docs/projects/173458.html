Project Summary

Even before the refugee influx Cox's Bazaar had one of the highest levels of malnutrition and food insecurity in the country. One in every five households already had low and borderline food consumption patterns. The COVID-19 pandemic declared in March adds to and reinforces this global extreme poverty situation.
This action seeks to save the lives of children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and reduce the number of children that become severely malnourished. This will be done by implementing a comprehensive WASH program that will improve the FDMN community's and host community’s ability to keep the children alive and healthy, building their resilience to withstand the dire chatter condition they find themselves in. Safe water provision will be ensured by installing/improving, and maintaining water points to address the needs. Regular water samples will be tested to check microbial contamination and analysis to inform safe WASH programming in the ACF targeted blocks. ACF will work in vulnerable communities, community clinics, and educational institutes in the host community. To deduce 14%, open defection ACF will collaborate with the vulnerable communities, community health clinic, and educational institute in the host community. ACF will continue constructing sustainable and environmentally friendly latrine and washroom facilities and the rehabilitation and Operation and maintenance of WASH facilities. ACF will work for proper solid waste management in the community clinic and educational institute. ACF will keep this improvement in the long term both practices on knowledge considering most risk behavior. Regarding Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM), ACF will consider the MHM kit to minimize the gap (88% received). ACF will also support by providing MHM kits, WASH in NUT kit, and NFI considering health and hygiene issues in the host community.

1 - Identified Needs

The limited essential services available before the influx are under severe strain due to the massive increase in people in the area. In some of the camps sites and even a part of the host community that has spontaneously emerged, water and sanitation facilities are limited or low quality, with too high density raising the risks of an outbreak of disease. With cramped and unhygienic living conditions, inadequate food security has led to disturbingly high levels of malnutrition amongst the Forcedly Displaced Myanmar Nationals (FDMNs). Improved Water Services:Even though 47% of refugees in the camps are now accessing chlorinated water through piped distribution networks, most of the refugee population has access to water from the tube wells/hand pump (61%) compared to the piped water system. 12% sufficient, safe water gap is reported considering quantity standard (MSNA-2020 report). There are some issues (62 %) for the household to secure water access, e.g., water point/tap stand functionalities, water collection distance, and equitable access to water. The tube wells are prone to contamination, where still a remarkable percentage are shallow tube wells. Access to Sanitation:Despite the fact that 99% of households reported using pit latrines in the camps and acceptable quantitative standards reached 88% for the toilet in the ACF responsible blocks (currently 28 persons per latrine-WASH sector GAP analysis, 2020). The functionality of the latrines remains low, households (including disabled and aged) are facing problems accessing or using toilets (Latrines are full -61%, No separation between men and women’s latrines-61%, Latrines don’t have sufficient lights -52%, walk to a latrine further away -61% -NPM-IVR-R-2). Still, 65% gap for bathing facilities considering quantitative standards. (Average 67person) in the ACF responsible blocks. High numbers of refugees (especially females) using makeshift bathing spaces inside their shelters; sometimes, it's associated with laundry, and dishwashing places are informal and often unhygienic. Challenges remain in achieving full coverage of fecal sludge management (FSM), but it can ensure that all latrines are regularly emptied and safe to use. Solid Waste Management (SWM) remains a challenge in the camps; only 47.4% of the refugee households reported access to household bins or shared garbage bins. This is also lead to making the barrier to ensure the functionality of the drainage system. There is still a significant need for further construction/improvement/ renovation and maintenance of sanitation and hygiene infrastructure to bring coverage up to minimum Sector standards. Hygiene Promotion and Community EngagementDue to COVID-19 fear and prevention effort, appropriate hygiene practices remain low due to progress has been observed partly, focusing on handwashing promotion-98% in the refugee and host community. The still scary part is handwashing after handling a child's stool/cleaning a child's bottom-5%, before feeding children-14%, before cooking/meal preparation-23%. 95% HHs reporting having access to soap. Despite the significantly less hygiene promotion intervention in the host community, households can mention three critical times but still need to focus more on the community, considering risky practice (Before breastfeeding -1%, Before feeding children-5%).
