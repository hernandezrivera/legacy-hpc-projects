Project Summary

Project Objective: The resilience and the productive capacity of vulnerable households are restored/enhanced and livelihoods protected in Gaza, drawing from the following Country Strategic Objectives and FSS activity:
• Country Strategic Objective #3: The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.

1 - Needs

In the oPt food security continues to be affected by the protracted nature of the crisis, repeated shocks, continued restrictions on freedom of movement, constrained productive capacities and a lack of economic opportunities. Female-headed households, pregnant and lactating women are particularly vulnerable to food insecurity and malnutrition. The level of food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Gaza, revealing the long-term impact of the disruptive shocks of recent years, where an estimated 62% of households are severely or moderately food insecure. The situation in Gaza Strip continue to decline. Farmer communities in the Gaza face challenges in their protection, livelihoods and resilience due to ongoing crisis, blockades and climate change shocks. In addition to what is already reported in the FSS HNO narrative, October 2019 and the Strategic Objectives (CSOs 2019) Humanitarian Response Plan and in reference to the HPC 2020 FSS Needs/Response Framework, and in Line with the Gender Guidance for the HPC 2018-2020. Gaza is still suffering from a chronic electricity deficit that has undermined its already fragile living conditions. As of May 2017, the electricity deficit has become the worse rolling blackouts of 16-20 hours per day. The significant shortages have left 2 million residents of Gaza with just a few hours of power a day, turning many aspects of ever women, men, boys’ and girls’ life miserable and raising additional deterioration to the already impoverished Gazan. This has had a direct impact on the delivery of services, including water supply, sanitation, health, education and agriculture. The overall guiding principle for CARE WBG’s theory of change is adopting an impact driven approach, acknowledging that our impact groups (women, girls, boys and youth) require emergency and early recovery supports, however CARE will utilize interventions that address both immediate needs, as well as the underlying drivers of those needs. CARE focuses on restoring the capacity of vulnerable farming communities and livestock holders, and enhancing the ability of women and men farmers to recover from the protracted crises and cope with the environmental shocks. Based on CARE various reflections and learning form, the gender transformative programming, female Gazans negative experiences of the crisis are compounded by social norms which inhibit their access to food, inputs, finance and livelihoods’ services, as well as lower control over income and household decision making (CARE, 2014, validated 2019) where the economic space for women is normally con ned by what is culturally acceptable for women to engage in. Women in agriculture are particularly disadvantaged, despite being a significant unregistered portion of the workers in this sector. This is reflected in limited economic opportunities, restricted mobility, and limited technical preparation. Farmers and herders are particularly vulnerable considering their direct exposure to the effects of the conflict and their dependence on small-scale productive activities to meet their basic food and income needs. The intervention builds on CARE and its partner (MAAN) various and comprehensive needs assessment, baseline/endline surveys, constraints and opportunities analysis, gender in agriculture research (CARE 2017 targeted 100% women) and context analysis. Findings indicate that, the destruction due the conflict heavily impacted the agricultural sector; about 17,331 dunums of vegetables plots were destroyed. There are still significant needs in the poultry farms which recently remain unmet (MoA 2019). Most of the interviewed framers indicated constrains accessing to irrigation water due to damaged wells. The lack of possibilities to rebuild these agricultural infrastructures leaves households unable to provide neither for their own consumption, nor for income generation.

2 - Activities

This intervention will aim to restore and enhance the resilience, food security, productive capacity of vulnerable households in Gaza to protract their livelihoods taking into account the local impact of climate change. As such, the project aims to support affected communities in Gaza by rehabilitation and provision of productive assets and inputs, for small scale women and men farmers, breeders and water wells including promotion of technical capacities and improving their production and using the BBB (Build Back Better) approach for increased resilience. The activities are designed based on standards adopted by technical working groups of the FSS, and in line with the accountability to affected populations (AAP) with respecting the rights, dignity and protection of all segments of affected communities. The activities are closely coordinated with FSS, MoA, stakeholders, local CBOs, while being aware of what is offered in the targeted locations, as per the FSS coordination mechanisms 2019. The activities will be implemented in partnership with MAAN who will be responsible of the technical implementation at the field level. CARE will be responsible of the overall quality management and will act as the main contact for donors and stakeholders. The project activities will be as follows: Activities for O #1: Lands Rehabilitation 1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end beneficiaries. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying their needs by gender, age, and vulnerabilities. 3: Cleaning & ploughing, and installing protective fence and the local irrigation system. 4: Distributing farming manual tools, accessories, local fertilizers, seeds and seedling. Activities for O #2: Poultry Farms Rehabilitation including provision of renewable power supply 1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end beneficiaries. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying their needs by gender, age, and vulnerabilities. 3: Rehabilitating the poultry farm at targeted HHs. 4: Installing solar system for the targeted poultry farms (1Kw) as per their outlet capacity needs. 4: Provide extension services for the targeted poultry farms in close coordination with MoA. Activities for O #3: Maintenance/Rehabilitations of Water Wells. 1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end localities and beneficiaries. 2: Conduct needs assessment to identifying the localities and damages. 3: Rehabilitating the wells as this will improve access to water for many farmers who don’t have access to water and will be able to get the water after the wells’ rehabilitation. Activities for O #4: Installation of renewable power supply for Irrigation water wells and collective ponds. 1: Conduct a baseline assessment and selection of end localities and beneficiaries. 2: Conduct need assessments to identifying the areas of damages. 3: Installing the solar system for the water wells (25Kw) and ponds (6kw) as per their outlet capacity needs. We would like to confirm that all extension services to farmers in all activities will be conducted in strict and close coordination with MoA and FSS.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

During the needs assessment phase, several meetings with local committees and different stakeholder were conducted. The content of these discussions formed the basis on the projects scope, approach and trend of damages, which help us to develop our vision of the project’s design. As well, the project has its local committees, which were formed to represent and facilitate the access to farmers in the field. The design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of targeted communities, based on a number of key sources, including comprehensive assessments conducted for similar project implemented by CARE/MAAN (AYADI Project), various and comprehensive need assessments conducted by CARE & MAAN in Gaza Strip, HNO 2020/FSS, coordination with FSS and with Ministry of Agriculture (MoA), Gender Transformative Insights (CARE WBG 2019), a series of consultation workshops/ FG were held by with representatives of farmers/ herders, women's CBOs & local councils in targeted locations to revalidate their needs; with taking into consideration the gender, age groups’ and disability needs. All of the designed activities reflect the needs realized at the field level, and which also are in line with what has been identified from HNO 2020. Most of the interviewed framers/herders indicated high constraints in accessing to irrigation water due to damaged/ deteriorated water wells, as well as high needs to rehabilitate of agriculture lands and maintenance of poultry farms through the need assessment phase. people with specific needs were also addressed in our need assessment and are included within CARE projects. Beneficiaries have contributed to the design of this action through local committees. In addition, beneficiaries will be involved in the project monitoring and evaluation process during and after the implementation in order to provide recommendations to maximize the benefits and highlight lessons learned. Beneficiary knowledge of this system has been promoted through a number of field assessments and monitoring visits. In addition, a paragraph will be included in each agreement with beneficiaries explaining the steps to report any complaint through the beneficiary feedback mechanism. CARE/MA'AN will publish the project’s interventions. Numbers of Orientation workshops for the targeted communities will be conducted to announce the project and give an idea about the project’s plans, budgets, selections criteria and other details of the project intervention. In addition, advertisement tools will be distributed in public places such as: mosques, municipalities, and CBOs, to ensure that the information about the project would reach most of the potential beneficiaries. CARE/MAAN have successfully created a coordination and cross checking mechanism with other actors such as (MoA), food security sector (FSS), local and international NGOs, along with the local committees in order to verify and identify the neediest and affected areas and beneficiaries.
