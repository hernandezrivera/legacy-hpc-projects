Project Summary

Children in Syria are disproportionately impacted by this crisis, with 7.48 million children in need of humanitarian assistance in 2024. In Government of Syria (GoS)-controlled areas, the most prevalent child protection risks are related to boys and girls facing school dropout, engagement in child labour, child marriage, and street-involved children. According to MSNA 2023, children - especially boys - are starting to drop out of school at 11 years of age, and child labor has seen a 20% increase across Syria compared to 2022, with notable spikes in Deir Ez-Zor and Aleppo, respectively reporting that 54,73% and 59.63% of households have a child under 18 engaged in labor. Voices from Syria indicated that adolescent girls are at higher risk of child marriage and girls and women are exposed to sexual harassment on the streets, and sexual exploitation by employers when working. Safe education spaces are scarce, with more than 3700 schools in need of rehagilitaiton/repair. Mental health and wellbeing of children and their families continue to decrease, and violence in the family and towards children is widespread, fuelled by multiple trauma, negative coping mechanism, and economic distress of caregivers.
• Intervention and implementation of activities: The proposed project offers an integrated response to needs identified by the Child protection, WASH and Education- Sectors. The intervention will strengthen community-based child protection mechanisms and foster a protective environment in the communities through MHPSS for children, parenting for caregivers, case management, and supporting child protection committees. Recognizing the central role of schools for identifying and responding to CP risks, the intervention will also focus on activities in the schools, including rehabilitation of WASH facilities and classrooms, hygiene awareness and hygiene kits provision, CPMS training for teachers and MHPSS provision. Through strategic partnerships with national and international education and protection actors, these activities will complement non-formal education provision and entail a strong referral system.
- M&E: The MEAL Team will conduct field visits during the project cycle to monitor activities. Data collection will occur monthly, with disaggregation based on age, sex, disability, status, and location, following data protection measures. A Project Follow-Up tool will track progress, and the MEAL Team will oversee MoV. Specific M&E per activity include: post-kit survey 2 wks post-delivery, classroom survey & teacher interviews after 1 mo., pre/post tests for training.
- Risk: Lack of Trust: Children and families may be reluctant to engage with child protection services due to mistrust of authorities, fear of consequences, or cultural barriers. Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. Mitigation: Build trust and rapport with children and families through transparent communication, empathy, and cultural sensitivity. Involve community members, religious leaders, or trusted individuals who can vouch for the credibility of child protection services. Risk: Security threats or risks in the operational environment L: Low. I: High. Mitigation: Conduct thorough security assessments and implement appropriate safety measures to ensure the physical safety of participants and facilitators during awareness sessions. Coordinate with local authorities, security providers, and community leaders to mitigate risks and respond to emergencies effectively. Risk: Economic crisis, inflation and impact of the sanctions. L: High. I: Medium. Mitigation: Close collaboration with the key stakeholders in the country to ease the impact of the sanctions and have a smooth implementation. Risk: Limited availability of goods. L: Low. I: Medium. Mitigation: Extensive network of suppliers. Risk: Community backlash or retaliation. L Low. High. Mitigation: Conduct community consultations and engage with local leaders and stakeholders to build support.
