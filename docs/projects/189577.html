Project Summary

This IOM Protection project aims to contribute towards the achievement of the Protection Cluster Objectives 2.4 and 2.5.
The project seeks to build resilience among the most vulnerable i.e. women, children, persons with disabilities and the elderly among others. Building the resilience of the most vulnerable against the surrounding crisis including subnational violence and climatic shocks will help to reduce their vulnerabilities. The building of the resilience will be done through mitigating the risks and addressing the violations experienced by the vulnerable members of the community. The project will focus on providing cash assistance or individual protection assistance (IPA) to those facing protection risks or those who have survived protection violations. The cash assistance will target the vulnerable in areas affected by subnational violence and climatic shock hotspots such as Warrap, Jonglei, Central and Eastern Equatoria. The counties to be targeted include Twic, Tonj North and South, Wau, Jur River, Rubkona and Mayom. The project will also continue raising awareness on protection and conducting protection monitoring in hot spots of conflict and climatic shocks.
IOM aims to contribute to strengthening the humanitarian response to GBV through building national capacities to implement GBV prevention and response programming based on the Interagency GBV minimum standards.
The HLP Rights component seeks to continue addressing the contextual situational analysis, legal environment and judicial system as the potential tools to better lives of persons faced with HLP related challenges and or issues. It’s evident that the law and legal institutions are charged with the mandate of protecting the dignity of Property Holders and in doing so, fortify those most vulnerable to HLP issues.
The target group consists of IDPs, Host Communities, Returnees and Vulnerable groups in various categories like widows, PWDs, paralegals, elders, regulatory and supportive institutions staff who can help escalate and advocate for the upholding of the dignity of the right holders especially the vulnerable groups and PWDs as the most affected by the impact of continued discrimination in society and violations of their HLP rights as entitled to.
IOM also intends to particularly target Regulatory and Supportive Institutions so that they can represent cases of HLP rights violations among the most affected and vulnerable groups.

1 - Needs

South Sudan has been affected by many years of conflict exacerbated by natural disasters such as flooding and famine. While there appears to be progress towards a transition to peacebuilding and a cessation to violence, there are still pockets of sub-national violence in parts of the country necessitating the provision of protection assistance that will cater for emergency and reduction of protection risks, provision of targeted protection assistance to survivors of violence, abuse, exploitation, and neglect as well as strengthening structures and systems that foster a protective environment.Gender based violence is one of the protection concerns bedeviling the country. It is structurally underpinned by a patriarchal social system, connected to the political economy of bride wealth (Luedke, 2019). The availability and quality of multi-sectoral GBV services across the country is low, and access remains a challenge for women, especially in rural communities. protection risks affecting women and girls include forced marriage, IPV, and CRSV. support (PSS), case management, and legal assistance. Due to an absence of trained service providers in Tonj East and inadequate personnel in Tonj South, there is a barrier for women to access GBV services. Aweil South will also be targeted to build on 2021 programming as there are few GBV actors in the location. Furthermore, HLP rights continue to be a key protection issue across urban and pre-urban areas in this country. Land ownership in and control in urban areas remain a complex and precarious issue in absence of functional legal avenues for IDPs, returnees and host communities to claim ownership of land before fragile and dynamic status (Wau-HLP TWG-Joint Assessment Report 2021).

The project has not conducted a market functionality assessment as the money will not be utilized to purchase consumable goods but rather to access basic services such as HLP, Health, Education and Justice.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

Protection and GBV: IOM works with national partners, many of whom have limited internal capacities on PSEA. When IOM initiates a partnership a PSEA capacity assessment is conducted, and where partners agencies have capacity gaps, IOM supports by providing training. To mitigate risks of exclusion of persons with disabilities, older persons and young persons especially girls, IOM ensures that consultation and assessment process specify a requirement to consult with marginalized group, staff are trained on inclusive communication methods. This also includes providing accessible information to marginalized and different age and gender groups to ensure they can access CFMs, as well as conducting monitoring and providing technical guidance to partners on ensuring functional CFMs. IOM implements an institutional data protection policy and follows GBV case management data protection protocols, including capacity building and monitoring implementing partners on their application. IOM works with the GBV sub-cluster to ensure GBV response partners are trained on use of GBIMS and information sharing protocols, as well as ensuring the data collected from beneficiaries access project activities (including their image) is based on informed consent HLP:Donors and HLP Partners can support the National and Sub-National land institutions-led initiatives that recognize ownership of user rights, providing technical assistance and establishing emerging conflict resolution mechanisms to support the land administration process.
