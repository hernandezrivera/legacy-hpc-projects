Project Summary

The project will target 1000 food insecure families in the Gaza Strip to improve their access to diverse and safe food. This will be done through the distribution of E-vouchers for food (vegetables, groceries, protein source). The project also will build the capacity of 3 vulnerable communities in the Gaza Strip to undertake innovative community-led collective actions/initiatives to address community-based humanitarian needs, foster preparedness for emergencies and reduce potential risks and threats.

1 - Needs

Through its 6 family centers in Gaza strip, DCA/NCA partner, MAAN, has been providing the case management services to 115 children/family on monthly basis (about 50% are females). Different services have been offered to the vulnerable families including female headed HH’s by different actors who are part of a specific referral and coordination mechanism. Food insecurity and poor living conditions have been recognized as of the main problems threatening the well being of the target vulnerable families with no adequate response to be tackled. In March 2019, MA’AN has conducted an assessment to better understand the needs of families who are receiving the case management services. The assessment targeted 308 families (2449 individuals: 498 men, 479 women, 1443 children, 29 elderlies) and was intended to examine the socio-economic conditions and food insecurity levels for the targeted population and to better understand the needs of MAAN’s family center beneficiaries. The assessment has revealed alarming vulnerabilities across all sectors that are driven mainly by lack of income. Among the assessed population, 95.1% are abject poor and other 3.6% of the families were classified as poor. Furthermore, 21.2% of the targeted families have reported no access to any source of income and that they are totally dependent on aid. The assessment has found that the majority (64.2%) of the targeted families are moderately food insecure, while 28.7% are severely food insecure, and 7.2% are mildly food insecure. 54.4% of families have reported significant (more than 50% of their food) reliance on unreliable sources to access their needs of food including debts and irregular aid. The inability of the families to purchase adequate quantities of food often forces them to detrimental coping strategies. This includes reducing the number of meals, buying food on credit, reducing consumed food quantities and purchasing low quality market leftovers. In September 2019, an assessment has been conducted to address the nutritional health situation for children under 5 years and the socio-economic vulnerabilities of the children’s families in Biet Lahiya (Al Amal) and Rafah (Al Shouka). The assessment found that 16.7% of the children are malnourished (sever and moderate wasting/stunting/underweight) which is much higher than the nationally reported figures and the internationally accepted figures (2.5%). The prevalence of Anaemia is 31.5%. The deteriorated nutritional status in the surveyed localities is mainly attributed to poverty (95% of the families are considered as abject poor), limited access to food, inadequate food habits, deterioration of sanitary and environmental conditions and exposure to parasitic infestation. The assessment has also revealed that 81% of surveyed families have reported no access to any source of income and are totally dependent on aid. The bad socioeconomic situation and food insecurity have negative reflections on the social relations of the targeted families and undermine the psychological wellbeing of women. Women have the responsibility of taking care of children’s feeding and preparing food for family. Therefore, supporting the access to nutrient food is a priority for women to enjoy well-being and improve nutritional health situation for children. The assessment has revealed that the most affected groups by the food insecurity are women (especially lactating, neonates and pregnant), children especially those under 5 years and elderlies. Women and elderlies sometimes reduce the no. of meals per day to make food available for other family members. Considering the recurrent crisis in Gaza, it has been clear that there is a need for a stronger focus on emergency preparedness to build resilient communities that are prepared to cope with emergencies, with specific focus to address their access to food.

2 - Activities

The project will target 1000 vulnerable families from the Gaza Strip. The target group will include poor and food insecure families nominated by MA’AN’s family centers across the five governorates of Gaza strip and families with malnourished children from Biet Lahiya (Al Amal) and Rafah (Al Shouka). Food insecurity and poverty will be the main criteria for targeting. Female headed HH’s, families including children under five years, pregnant and lactating women will be prioritized. At least 12% of the targeted HH’s will be female headed. The proposed project will include the following activities: The 1000 households will receive a total of 15 electronic food vouchers, including fresh vegetables, grocery, and protein vouchers over a period of 15 weeks. Locations of the distribution points and times of distribution will be determined upon consultation with the beneficiaries, in particular women to ensure safe and accessible distribution points are identified. The diversity and content of vouchers will be identified in consultation nutritional expert and with beneficiaries, in particular women as they are responsible for cooking and are more aware of the family preferences and children needs. Voucher content will address needs of most vulnerable groups including lactating, neonates and pregnant women, the children under 5 years and elderly. The targeted families will benefit from nutritional awareness sessions to help them enhancing their national status. The project will include a complementary component to promote the capacity of 3 vulnerable communities to undertake collective actions towards reducing the potential risks and threats of the most pressing hazards. In the past three years, DCA/NCA and MAAN have conducted capacity building activities to strengthen the community-based emergency response mechanisms including the partner CBOs, community-based committees and groups of volunteers where women were very well represented. However, DCA/NCA and MA’AN have recognized that the targeted vulnerable communities still lack the capacity to lead humanitarian response and deal with the potential hazards and threats in proactive manner. In June 2019, DCA/NCA has conducted training/co-design workshop with its partners including MA’AN Development Center on a new approach for community-led response called “Survivor and Community Led Response (SCLR). This approach is based on supporting the vulnerable communities to plan for and implement actions that aim to reduce the negative impact and mitigate risks of future hazards as well as to address current humanitarian needs at the community level. This project will address protection threats through promotion of diversified and participatory community responses addressing the needs of women, men, girls and boys. The community-based structures will be empowered to address communities’ humanitarian needs but also to contribute strengthening the long-term resilience. Participation of different community groups including women and youth will be ensured in the composition of the community groups. The intervention has been coordinated with Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and Ministry of Agriculture. Further coordination with MoSD for beneficiary selection will be ensured. Special attention will be made by a well-trained female staff to receive GBV related complaints, address female needs, monitor and report on GBV incidents, where detected cases will be referred to specialized services through referral pathways of the protection cluster/CPWG.The project fit with the cluster response plan & complement other clusters’ actions.

8.6.c - How will feedback be used?

MA’AN and DCA/NCA will pay a specific attention to ensure engaging beneficiaries in addressing the expected risks and challenges, and empowering them to participate in the monitoring and evaluation process of the project. This project was built based on consultations with the project key stakeholders and beneficiaries and have considered the past experiences, emerging needs, and expectations of the beneficiaries in the design of the project activities. The complaints and feedback mechanism will be introduced to the project beneficiaries at the beginning of the project to ensure their understanding for the mechanism and encourage them to share feedback. All project beneficiaries will have the right to raise concerns; share thoughts and opinions; and submit complaints to MA’AN using different means including writing, phone, SMS, or direct verbal communication with the responsible staff. The received complaints and feedback will be documented and analyzed by MA’AN’s M&E and program staff to feed into the decision-making process and inform possible corrections during implementation but also the design of other projects. The complaints and feedback will support for increasing the efficiency of the adopted work system and procedures and will allow for adjusting the work mechanisms and approaches in a way that ensures a better response to the needs of the vulnerable groups. MA’AN will conduct focus group discussions (FGDs) during the project implementation to collect feedback from the project beneficiaries and measure their satisfaction. The collected information will be used to inform decision making by searching for possible areas of improvement and revisiting the implementation approaches and tools.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

This project contributes to addressing the food needs of vulnerable and food-insecure families in the Gaza Strip and enhancing emergency preparedness capacities for vulnerable communities through proposing a set of integrated activities and adopting approaches that respond to the identified needs while maintaining the dignity, safety, and security of the beneficiaries. The project adopts gender-sensitive and culturally responsive approach to implement the project activities which will highly contribute to addressing the social and cultural risks that might undermine the participation of the beneficiaries, especially women in the project activities. Other risks such as safety and security risks were also identified and considered, and the mitigation measures will be further developed to ensure that these risks are addressed during implementation. For instance, the E-voucher modality will allow beneficiaries to redeem their vouchers with flexibility in terms of the time of redemption and the amount per each transaction. Beneficiaries will be able to choose the food items they need from shops close to their residency place which fosters their safety and protection. Further, beneficiaries will be oriented about how to redeem their vouchers and on their rights and duties to protect them from the risk of abuse and fraud. Distribution of food will be done through local vendors in the community that contributes to support the local economy and build the capacity of private sector. The selection of vendors will consider their ability to upscale capacity to absorb additional number of customers’ needs to avoid any shortage of the services that might occur in the local market as a result of the intervention, hence, ensure that other community members-non-beneficiaries- will not be affected negatively.The monitoring process for this activity will include regular field visits and random calls to check if beneficiaries are satisfied with the services provided to them and that they are treated with dignity and respect. The community led response component will build the capacity of vulnerable communities to take lead in emergency response and to undertake collective actions to mitigate the threats and risks of potential hazards. This will contribute to enhancing the overall safety and security of the targeted communities and will enhance their protection in emergency and non-emergency contexts. The training and capacity building activities will be implemented at CBOs that are located in safe places and are well-known to the beneficiaries. MA’AN and DCA/NCA will also keep close monitoring for the security situation in the Gaza Strip and will advise the donor and partners in case any emergency indicators arise. MA’AN and DCA/NCA will also adjust timeframe and activities in case any emergency happens to avoid security and safety risks

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

This project aims at improving the resilience, alleviating the suffering and enhancing the preparedness mechanisms of the most vulnerable people in the Gaza Strip, realizing the principles of impartiality, independence, and equality in the targeting process and in the delivery of assistance to people. DCA/NCA and MAAN adopt Anti-discrimination policy that criminalizes all aspects of discrimination and guides the implementation of interventions in light of the humanitarian principles. An assessment will be conducted to select the project beneficiaries based on clear selection criteria related to poverty and food insecurity. The assessment will be conducted by trained field workers, who have the required skills and expertise to collect precise data, under the supervision of the M&E team at MA’AN. The collected data will be analyzed and scored following an accurate statistical methodology and using Excel to ensure the accuracy of selection. The final selection results will be also verified and approved by MA’AN’s management and DCA/NCA. All selected beneficiaries will be treated equally, impartially, and transparently, with particular attention to gender issues, considering the extra vulnerability inflicted on women in the society. In addition, further technical criteria will be applied to select the beneficiaries of community initiatives. The selection criteria will be communicated with the target groups from the beginning of the project through orientation sessions, brochures, and feedback sessions, as relevant, to ensure accountability. The project applies human rights standards and gender-segregated data at all levels of intervention. DCA/NCA and MAAN apply a needs and rights-based approach to their work within humanitarian response and community mobilization investments. The intervention will improve the access of the targeted communities to equitable and accountable humanitarian assistance in accordance to safety and dignity, hence, mitigate the protection risks and potential health hazard resulting from the low access to sufficient nutrient food. The intervention addresses the right of target people to food, where, everyone is entitled to food that is sufficient, safe, acceptable, accessible and affordable. The voucher-based system will be adopted through the intervention as a dignified approach, where assistance will be provided considering the needs of the different vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities. The following actions will be considered to ensure providing equitable access to the basic services for men, women, boys and girls: 1. Ensuring significant involvement of women through the participation in the community engagement committee.2. Having safe procedures to guide the handling of gender-sensitive complaints through the complaint’s mechanism3. Orienting the project team on gender policy and GBV incidences. 4. Ensuring that the developed baseline tools address the perceptions and concerns of women.5. Ensuring that the baseline information contained gender segregated data
