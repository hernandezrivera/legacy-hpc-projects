Project Summary

The Phase 2 of project aims at supporting the emergency response to the Syrian humanitarian crisis though a combined humanitarian and stabilization intervention, divided in 2 connected components in the Health and WASH in health. The health sector response aims at supporting the provision of emergency, primary and secondary health care services, including primary care activities in Deir ez-Zor, ar-Raqqah, Aleppo and al-Hasakeh Governorates and to Covid-19 case management response in preselected hospitals.
The WASH component aims at consolidating and where needed establishing the safe system of healthcare waste management in several healthcare facilities in the Governorates of Deir ez-Zor, ar-Raqqah, Aleppo and al-Hasakeh. This will allow to strengthen the Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) activities carried out in all targeted health facilities and it is part of the Covid-19 response in NES. This action will also contribute to promote safe and sustainable human settlements for Syrian IDPs and local population in North-East Syria (SDG no. 11).
With the overall aim of reducing morbidity and mortality among affected populations through coordinated and timely emergency health and WASH services, the action has 2 specific objectives:
- SA1: Guaranteeing access to emergency and primary health care services to Syrian IDPs and locals in NES, with particular attention to the most vulnerable cases, including Covid-19 preparedness and response.
- SA2: Consolidating and increasing availability of health waste management and IPC services in healthcare facilities in Deir ez-Zor, ar-Raqqah, Aleppo and al-Hasakeh.
The Phase 3 of the project aims to target in-camp and out-of-camp high-need/neglected areas with a comprehensive and integrated package of primary health care services, specialized assistance for PWDs, health waste management, and community outreach.
The modality of response will focus on strengthening the existing referral mechanism among partners and towards external services and other humanitarian actors providing assistance in targeted areas. IPC and Health Waste Management (HWM) activities will be mainstreamed in several UPP/KRC healthcare facilities, in continuity with Phase 2 and previous projects and in coordination with other WASH and Health NES actors. Major focus will be on implementing partner’s capacity building, with the aim of consolidating and strengthening the functionality of the already installed systems of HWM.

4 - Needs

With the proposed intervention UPP wants to support an inter-sectoral comprehensive program that aims at addressing the negative outcomes of the disruption of health services in rural and remote areas of NES, with special territorial focus on Deir ez-Zor, ar-Raqqah and Southern al-Hasakeh Governorates. Below specific negative outcomes addressed by the intervention:• the concentration of the resources in cities and town,• the lack of connection and coordination among the different health care facilities (HCFs),• the need to further mainstream IPC and covid-19 preventive measures across HCFs,• the lack of safe systems of hazardous health waste management.

5 - Implementation of activities

The Wash in health / IPC objective of the program is to consolidate and increase the availability of health waste management and IPC services and in healthcare facilities in Deir ez-Zor, ar-Raqqah, Aleppo and al-Hasakeh ; sought via the following activities:o IPC and capacity building on health waste segregation inside HCFs - ensuring that at least 11 UPP supported HCFs have a safe system of medical waste management in place and functional - specialized trainings on HWM for medical staff and waste agents (cleaners for outside the HCF and workers of onsite HWZ) will be provided, along with equipment for correct waste segregation. o Organization of health waste transportation and disposal in Health Waste Zones, by ensuring the maintenance of the existing HWZs across NES or construction of new HWZs through a mix approach that of direct intervention of UPP-KRC and with WASH partners support, and provision of the means of transportation (HW vehicles or equipment) adapted for HW transportation. - UPP aims at supporting the implementing partner to identify and prepare the locations for expired pharma safe disposal – possibly one in Hasakeh/Deir Ezzor and one in Raqqa/Menbij areas. A preliminary assessment of the locations will be led by UPP international and national technical experts in coordination with Wash and Health WGs. UPP will support with technical design, SOPs, trainings, supervision and monitoring and, based on project budget availability and partners’ capacity to support, with construction works.The action targets the catchment area of the health facilities and HWZs (634,000 indirect beneficiaries).

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

In line with Wash WG PRA Analysis for SWM activities, UPP identifies the following risks: • health risks associated with unsafe disposal of health waste: o health risk for the workers manipulating with waste – as mitigation UPP with the partner ensures the provision of PPE for the workers transporting the HW and operating the HWZ facility, and provide training and SOPs to the workers for safe waste handling and operation of HWZ. o health risk for the population living in proximity of open dumping sites, where the medical waste is disposed off if a HWZ is not available or the waste collection – disposal system is not well established. This poses a risk of injuries and infection, especially for extremely vulnerable groups of waste scavengers (often women and children). The mitigation is the core of the action - increasing the availability of safe HW disposal in various locations by establishing the system of safe HWM from the point of waste generation at HCFs to treatment/disposal of the HW, coordinating with health care providers, local authorities and other actors and by regular monitoring ensuring the sustainability of the mechanism. • health risk of environmental pollution by the disposal of the hazardous waste > partners mitigate the risk by conducting feasibility assessment with consultations of local stakeholders, considering the distance from populated areas, water sources and agricultural land as key criteria, together with adaptation of the designs to eliminate the risks that could affect the public health and livelihoods.• Child labour – UPP requires its contractors to abide to UPP Good Business regulations, The work sites are regularly monitored by UPP technical team, who report the breaches.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

- Presence of Covid-19 cases in NES, inducing loss of humanitarian access, loss of part of the health workforce, evacuation or quarantine of UPP and impl. partner's non-medical staff; shortages of medical supplies; shortages of pharmaceuticals > priority mitigation measures/needs identified are: Strengthening of all COVID-19 measures already in place with particular focus on IPC, finding additional financial resources to support dedicated COVID-19 facilities; redirect resources from other activities to the preventions and mitigation measures. Improve coordination at camp level to protect the vulnerable.- Financial instability in the region, especially in Lebanon, Iraq and Syria, impacting timely payment, allocation of resources on direct costs, shortages of medical commodities > monitor any change in financial regulations, maintaining regular contacts with financial authorities and banks personnel in order to minimize loss due to additional taxation and/or restriction to the use and transfer of foreign currencies- Turkish forces expand “Peace Spring” operational theater eastward and westward to counter Kurdish presence in NES > maintaining access also unstable areas and negotiate access to accessible western territories with relevant authorities and military councils, revise project action plan and establish alternative operational procedures to restart service delivery as soon as possible in line Humanitarian principles. Move to remote management modalities where possible, developing specific Remote Management SOPs
