Project Summary

The project will build the resilience and provide livelihood opportunities to vulnerable women and youths who have lost their productive assets and capital and are struggling to produce and generate income in Urban and rural settings. The focus will be given to IDPs, returnees and host population. Firstly the project will embark on conducting Period and Adhoc Livelihoods Market Assessment that will focus on understanding the early recovery context and labor opportunities and identifying existing vocational training structure from the Government, INGO and private sectors that are fully or partially in use. Secondly, it will Support Youths (Women and men form the age of 18 to 40 for employment and self-employment opportunities. It will also support School drop out to gain vocational skills through enrollment to private sector enterprises as apprentice. Thirdly, it will enrich youths with entrepreneurship skills, support them with conditional grants through the use of business plans development. It will also support rural communities with Village and Loan saving opportunities and finally support communities to rehabilitate their infrastructure damaged during the crisis trough cash for work schemes. The activities proposed here will target 28,500 youth aged 18 to 40 years (70% women and 30% men)

Needs

Violence in the northeast has caused massive displacement and at the same time restricted movement for people and trade. The people affected by violence in Yobe state are in need of livelihoods intervention. The 2019 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) estimates an approximate 5.5 million people in urgent need of early recovery and livelihoods assistance. findings from the just concluded multi-sector needs assessments and Livelihoods and Economic Recovery Assessments by REACH indicated that an inadequate levels of access to income and livelihood opportunities exist, and increased resource demands, leading to societal tensions between IDPs, returnees and host communities.The destruction or loss of access to productive assets has decreased livelihoods and employment for many especially the youth. The youth account for many of those adversely affected by the conflict in the region as it has disrupted their access to livelihood and employment opportunities. There is high demand amongst affected populations for income generation opportunities, yet restrictions, especially for women and girls, on freedom of movement, including in and out of IDP camps and settlement, are an inhibiting factor that exposes them to many protection risks, including sexual exploitation and abuse. Meaningful and contextualized livelihood activities also need to incorporate re-integration needs of women and youth formerly associated with armed groupsThe insurgence have affected market functionality which have inhibits trading and affects household income in petty trade which is also important aspect of their livelihoods. In many locations, market infrastructure was destroyed by insurgent attacks including marketplaces and storage facilities. On the other hand, savings mechanisms have been undermined by the insurgency through the dissolution of savings groups and through the closure of many banks and MFIs due to threats and insecurity. The Livelihood Market Recovery Assessment in the northeast illustrate the various sources of savings of most rural farmers. About 36% are part of savings groups 31% save money at home, 22% use commercial banks, and 2% use the Microfinance Bank. Many do not participate in the formal banking sector, as the insurgency destroyed most financial institutions.

Activities

•Period and Adhoc Livelihoods Market Assessment: The IRC is aware the Northeast context is fluid/volatile, hence the need to continuously monitor the context that will inform our early recovery project design and implementations. The IRC propose to initiate/conduct Livelihoods and market assessment through collaboration with the early recovery working group and will ensure participation from other partners for a broader scope and credible findings. It will focus on indicators that will help in the restoration and functionality of the market and understanding of increasing income of farmers through value chain analysis. It will map out existing Government and private sectors vocational training centers, assessment the possibilities of accessing such facilities and identify gaps that will limit functionalities of such facilities during it use. • Establishment of Livelihoods creating employment/self-employment opportunities: these centers are mostly applicable to urban centers for which the IRC will establish Livelihoods centers that will focus on Employability Skills Training, Career/Job Counselling, Internship Programs/Job Placements, and Job fairs/Networking Events and Access to job information and Employment Policy Advocacy. The centers will also cater for Support to entrepreneurship skills that will allow trainees develop business plans; the viable business plans will benefit from startup conditional grant, followed by mentorship sessions, apprenticeship with private sector enterprises and linkages with micro finance institutions. This part of the project will also cater for school dropout through support to vocational training exercise (carpentry, Mechanics, bread/soap making etc) and support to start up training kits. • Support to Small business enterprises making people self-employed: In rural settings, the IRC will support women and men with existing business complementing their challenges in maximizing more profit. It will conduct a feasibility study to assess the most profitable IGA. Following that conditional grants will be given to the supported households followed by business and financial skills. Establish Village Savings and Loans Associations (VSLA): The village Savings and Loans Associations (VSLA) activities will support communities through the provision of training and support kits to village savings groups from vulnerable household who are motivated to successfully imbibe in savings culture. The programme will allow people to improve their purchasing power, reduce negative coping strategy, improve food consumption and maintain/increase their productive assets. The IRC will set up VSLA group with maximum of 25 person for a period of 9 to 12 months to contribute shares and distribute at the end of the VSLA cycle. Rehabilitate community infrastructures through execution of Cash for Work schemes: The cash for work schemes are meant to support communities rehabilitate community’s infrastructures and well as increasing income of targeted individual or households. The focus will be for households having limited or no income opportunities and communities whose infrastructure were damaged during the crisis. The IRC will facilitate the process by allowing communities identifying the most effective schemes. The schemes will be implemented in a four consecutive months with 10 days of work. Payment will be aligned with the government daily wage and will be paid at the end of each work period.
