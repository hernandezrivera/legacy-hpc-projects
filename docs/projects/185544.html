Project Summary

The current anglophone crisis plaguing the two English speaking regions of Cameroon have posed a tremendous negative impact on the civilians. Over 215,000 displaced people are being hosted within communities in the North West and 162,000 within communities in West region (OCHA Cameroon Situation Report, 5 Aug 2021), placing additional strain on already limited social services and economic development.
We believe lack of perspective, unemployment and inequality are the drivers for conflict, polarization, extremism and migration, as was recently shown in a research by MSF and recognized by the Cameroon government in their National Youth plan. En-power creates perspective and brings employment opportunities to the doorsteps of young people and reduce their vulnerability to engaging in illegal activities such as gambling, theft, drugs, prostitution or being recruited into armed groups.
H4BF seek to boost the capacity of her skill training centers (which is registered under MINEFOP) in Nkambe and Bamenda in the North West and Baffousam in the west region to create perspective by providing a blended vocational training program for 300 displaced and Vulnerable Youths (age 16-25) in Renewable Energy (solar and Biogas), Agri-business, Fashion and Designing and Computer knowledge (Website/apps building, Graphic Design and Data Management). Main focus is on educating the vulnerable youth of Nkambe and Bamenda in the North West and Baffousam in the West at our skill training centers. This program will train them with skills needed in an upcoming market with growth potential. Out of the 300 young people to be trained 250 are inserted into the job market, among them at least 50 will be employed in new jobs created (formal/informal). The center will also provide GBV case management to guide beneficiaries and others be in their full potential.
Local businesses in the sector will be directly involved as the training can only be successful and sustainable in close collaboration. While the training program evolves a local EN-Power hub will be established to encourage strong co-creation between educational institutes, businesses and local communities.
With this project which was launched in Baffousam in 2020 and 55.01% applications coming from North West, we seek to strengthen the capacity and create another training hub in Bamenda. EN Power has the ambition to grow into a national ecosystem of businesses, education institutes and civil society organizations in the sector. Best practices and lessons learned in existing centers will be disseminated within the global knowledge platform of H4BF.
Impact goal 1: To create perspective by enhancing chances for employability providing skills training in the renewable energy and communication technology (ICT) industries.
Impact goal 2: To reinforce and empower the MSMEs in these industries so as to increase and upgrade local employment opportunities
Impact goal 3: Bring together international public and private partners with local stakeholders to ensure the impact of the project is long lasting and has global outreach.
Impact goal 4: To improve crisis affected youth’s decision making, stress management and behavior based on understanding past behavior to better predict future behavior through GBV case management.
We know as our data proofs that over 95% of our beneficiaries have been through trauma due to loss of their source of livelihood and or psychosocial torture from either the crisis in the North West and South West or some form of Gender based Violence. As a result, we will in place trained psychologist and GBV experts to help them improve their decision making, stress management and behavior based on understanding past behavior to better predict future behavior. Our training also ensure we apply the do no harm principle by training staffs on sexual exploitation and abused (PSEA) and also ensuring our beneficiaries are safe by practicing safe measures on COVID 19.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The current anglophone crisis plaguing the two English speaking regions of Cameroon have posed a tremendous negative impact on the civilians. Over 215,000 displaced people being hosted within communities in the North West and 162,000 within communities in West region (OCHA Cameroon Situation Report, 5 Aug 2021), placing additional strain on already limited social services and economic development. • Girls and boys, women and men are distinctly affected by the violation of their rights. In the two English speaking regions, protection risks are often different, depending on gender, age and diversity. Beyond the popular belief that girls and women would form the vast majority of those facing security challenges, both halves of the population are truly affected. For example, for the two Southwestern and Northwestern regions, for the period from January to June 2019, 55.3% of the victims of violations of their rights were female, compared to 44.7% of boys and men.• IDPs are the most vulnerable category, with girls and women being the most affected because of their lower social and economic status, compounded by displacement: they form the majority of victims of sexual violence (89% of registered survivors in June for South West and North West were girls and women43)).• Many women have become widowed as a result of the crisis. Thus, they represent 31% of households in the South-West and 38% of households in the North-West, which represents an increase of about 10% compared to 2017. They are at risk of gender-based violence, particularly the risk of sexual abuse andexploitation.• Girls, as well as women who became heads of family because of the enlistment or death of their husbands, sometimes use survival sex to provide for their families. In some cases, these sexual relationships are in exchange for the release of husbands and sons. • Adolescent boys are particularly at risk of forced recruitment by armed groups.• In NWSW, young men and men are more likely to face arbitrary arrests, beatings, torture and extrajudicial executions. They are also at greater risk of being targeted by armed attacks and are more at risk of injury or death directly related to the conflict.• Men, because they enjoyed a privileged access to property over women before the crisis, are directly affected by the spoliation of their land and their home.• Women, men, boys and girls who have experienced violence and have seen loved ones abused and killed, sometimes suffer of psychological trauma. Lack of perspective, unemployment and inequality are the drivers for migration, as shown in a research by MSF and recognized by the Cameroon government in their National Youth plan. In Cameroon it has become clear that there is an education and training gap in the working-age population. A large share of the population (43%) has either no formal education or an incomplete primary education, and 67 % of the working age population has received no additional training at all. Unemployment is significantly higher among youth as compared to older demographics across all levels and types of education, pointing to persistent education and training gaps. One way to address this is to create more technical schools and training that will endow young people with a specific job or skill so that they find work right when they finish. Highlights from H4BF 2021-2022 training program indicates;•429 students applied from 8 regions of Cameroon with 52.45% being female and 47.55% male •66.67% registered students are internally displaced •34.97% registered for renewable energy course •27.74% registered for computer studies•25.41% registered for Fashion Design•11.42% registered for smart agriculture•8.39% registered students are physically challenged •55.01% registered applicants are in Bamenda, and cannot afford attending training in Baffousam due to high cost of living.
