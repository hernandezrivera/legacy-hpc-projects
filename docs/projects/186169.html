Project Summary

WFP is implementing a nutrition response frame with stronger focus towards prevention
of malnutrition. The programme focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most
vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with focus on the first 1000days of life. The programme targets all children aged 6-23 months including
those children suffering from moderate acute malnutrition-MAM) as well as for children
24-59 months with MAM in the most food insecure departments presenting highest GAM
rates in the Far North, North, Adamawa, and East region, while in the North west and south
west region the programme targets children aged 6-23 months and pregnant and lactating
women and girls in food insecure household. The programme activities include:
1.Screening for malnutrition and referral of severe cases for treatment;
2.Promotion of infant and young child feeding practices
3.Distribution of specialized nutritious food to children aged 6-23months and Pregnant
and lactating women and girls(PLWGs)
4.Social and behavioral change communication in favor of good nutrition and health
practices
5.Promotion of the use of the blanket feeding platform or provision of complementary
services including immunization, micro-nutrient supplementation, deworming, malaria and
other childhood illnesses prevention, family planning etc
WFP in 2022 intends to target about 43150 beneficiaries in the Far North region

1 - Besoins / Needs

The results of the latest SMART nutrition survey conducted in November 2019 by UNICEF and the government of Cameroon classified the Far North regionEast and North regions in a poor nutrition situation with global acute malnutrition rates of 5.2percent, 5.5percent and 6.2percent respectively. Stunting remains a public health concern in Cameroon. Stunting remains a public health problem in all 4 regions with prevalence exceeding the WHOserious threshold of 30%. The Far North has the highest prevalence at 38.2%. Regarding health situation, low immunization coverage, limited access to essential health care services (ratio between the population and the number of doctors remains far below the norm) and essential drugs increased risks of epidemics.The prevalence of food insecurity is 11% in the Far North region (CFSVA February 2018, including 1% in severe food insecurity. The proportion of households whose food consumption is poor remained relatively high in the North (9%). In this context, indicator related to infant and young child feeding practices are among the poorest in the country, especially for the Far North region with exclusive breastfeeding rate of 30.8%, a timely introduction rate for complementary foods of 33.3% and a low dietary diversity (dietary diversity score of 19.7% and and minimum acceptable dietary intake of 32%). It is estimated that more than 300,000 children and Pregnant and lactating women and girls in in emergency need of nutrition services across all 6 regions WFP’s program focuses on preventing malnutrition amongst the most vulnerable populations through strengthening supplementary feeding with a focus on the first 1000days of lifeminimum acceptable dietary intake of 32%).
