Project Summary

Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through prepare and distribute 1,200 daily cooked meals and 320 bread bags to the displaced families affected by the conflict and hosted in the transit and reception center in Albab, with the focus on the most vulnerable communities/camps especially female headed families. The project will create 20 job opportunities for the field staff.
19,200 IDPs are supported with Daily cooked meals. This number is roughly made according to a previous experience in managing the reception centers as the IDPs received are on daily movement in/out the center so this number will vary according to the IDPs tracked and based on emergency situations.
the kitchen will be operated in response to massive displacement in case of deteriorated security situation that may result such a need.

Needs

According to HNO 2019 draft version: An estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity, from the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies. There is an urgent need for multiple actions to provide food and livelihoods assistance as per the most feasible modality to the People in Need and at risk as established by the sector.Syria’s dependence on food assistance is expected to continue throughout the coming year and access to a diversified diet will remain a key need to ensure adequate nutritional status of the acutely food insecure, there are 6.5 million acute people in need, and 4 million risk people in need. QRC has been closely and continuously assessing the IDP displacements and collecting data on locations including available economic activates, products and services.

Implementation of activities

The project will provide 1,200 daily cooked meals provided for the IDPs in the reception centers and the settlements , this will continue to provide service based on needs and in case of emergency after tracking the IDPs in coordination with the CCCM and FSL clusters. 320 of bread bags provided for the IDPs in the transit center in Albab or other centers, on daily basis, the activities will be:1. Recruitment the project staff with concentrating to hire female staff, procurement, and kick of the project2. Coordination with local councils and other organization that work in the same location to avoid the overlapping.3. Determining the actual number of Beneficiaries and their location in the camps and open areas.4. Distributing cooked meals and bread bags 5. beneficiaries satisfaction survey6. Complaint ObservingThe project will continue providing services in more than 10 months, the number of meals will vary according to the actual number of IDPs displaced and received in centers, camps and other settlements. Based on QRC previous experience in operating mobile kitchen this project will provide more than 400,000 meals (about 1,200 ones a day) on emergency situations to target those vulnerable groups of newly IDPs.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Low Capacity coupled with challenges in recruitment and retention of appropriately skilled staff at all levelsMitigation measure: - Bridging the staff gap- implementing a capacity building and training program for staff at various tiers and implementation responsibilities2. Lack of coordination and synergies with representatives of target communitiesMitigation measure: involve focal persons from the local council to coordinate with the beneficiary elders 3. Identification of beneficiaries and appreciation of project achievements will be weak because they do not hear, read or see enough of project activities and positive impactsMitigation measure: - Encourage field visits by community elders with appointed focal persons to see the results and hear from beneficiaries directly- Develop a communications strategy to guide constituency building through internal and external communications with Project stakeholders- Consider Media communication and mobile applications 4. Grievances and complaints of expected direct beneficiaries are ignored by the implementing partner with all the negative consequences this will haveMitigation measure:- Establish a comprehensive Grievances Redress Mechanism (GRM) with well-defined guidelines for complaint acknowledgement and redressal. The GRM to be publicized externally as part of each project communication's strategy- staff are provided with the necessary training to report unaddressed complaints 5. Insufficient supervision and poor-quality control will cause faults and deficiencies in project deliverablesMitigation measure: periodically carry out spot checks, certify quality and report back to QRC 6. Continuing risk of insecurity and potential escalation in insurgency related violence with direct impacts on project activitiesMitigation measure:- train field staff on MOSS compliance (Minimum Operational Safety Standards)- Use of alternative measures for project supervision including Remote Asset Monitoring to carry out spot checks in the target areas 7. Non-transparent procedures of recruitment and procurement processes may lead to project getting a bad reputationMitigation measure: Establish a wider and transparent dissemination of project procurement and recruitment advertisements 8. Sustainable functioning of distribution or project implementation not well ensuredMitigation measure:- Contribution of identified and planned capacity building by trainings - Encourage communities to provide their feedback on the distribution process or project deliverables- Ensure M&E efficacy- Qualified staff hired
