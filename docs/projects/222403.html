Project Summary

According to humanitarian needs and response plan 2024, approximately 5.6 million people required WASH assistance in 2024. A significant portion of the population (41 per cent) lacks access to improved water sources and 37 per cent need to travel over 30 minutes to reach their primary water source, posing particular hardships for persons with disabilities and making women and girls more vulnerable to GBV. Access to sanitation is even more limited with prevalent open defecation, as 88 per cent of the population lacks access to improved latrines, according to the 2023 FSNMS data.
In the proposed project, Impact Health Organization seeks to implement integrated WASH interventions to reduce the prevalence of malnutrition and WASH-related morbidity and mortality, mitigate the risks of gender-based violence, and promote the inclusion of people with disabilities.
The proposed interventions will target 3 counties (Aweil East, Aweil South and Aweil North Counties) which have several WASH related challenges. The purpose of the intervention is to improve the WASH conditions in these areas, which have been severely affected by conflict, flooding, and displacement This will contribute to improving the health, well-being, and livelihoods of the affected communities and setting the foundation for sustainable development in the three counties.
The intervention involves a range of activities, including the provision of standard kits to children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and their caretakers, the construction and rehabilitation of latrines and boreholes in nutrition facilities, and the promotion of hygiene practices. The project also aims to improve access to water and sanitation for women and girls, construct and rehabilitate flood-resilient WASH facilities, and design disability-inclusive sanitation facilities. The project will also conduct hygiene promotion and other WASH related community outreach activities using multiple formats to ensure accessibility.
The proposed interventions aligns well with the South Sudan WASH cluster objectives 1; Contribute to reducing WASH- related morbidity and mortality in priority counties through WASH integrated programming with Nutrition, objective 2: Provide access to basic WASH services in emergencies and/or WSC 4 and 5 counties, objective 3: WASH specific activities for GBV mitigation and objective 4: Community members exposure to environmental hazards is reduced through the construction of disaster- resistant structures and infrastructure. . In addition, water management committees will be established and trained to maintain water points, fostering community ownership and sustainability.
Impact Health organization targets a total of 17,450 beneficiaries (7451 Male, 9999 Females) across the three counties. 9764 Aweil East , 4423 Aweil South and 3263 Aweil North. These beneficiaries include children, adults, and the elderly, with a specific focus on those most vulnerable. The estimated cost to deliver the proposed WASH interventions is USD 700,000.

1. Needs

The Republic of South Sudan has been grappling with significant humanitarian challenges since the outbreak of conflict in December 2013. According to humanitarian needs and response plan- 2024, approximately 5.6 million people required WASH assistance in 2024. A significant portion of the population (41 per cent) lacks access to improved water sources and 37 per cent need to travel over 30 minutes to reach their primary water source, posing particular hardships for persons with disabilities and making women and girls more vulnerable to GBV. Access to sanitation is even more limited with prevalent open defecation, as 88 per cent of the population lacks access to improved latrines, according to the 2023 FSNMS data. These challenges have escalated considerably, particularly in the counties of Aweil East, Aweil North and Aweil South, which are among the most affected areas, especially in the Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) sector. Vulnerable Girls, Boys, Women and men including Disabled person in the targeted counties are at heightened risks of shocks and stresses. The likely-hood of water-related disease is extremely high couple with the higher number of returnees, Refugee, IDPs due to sub national conflict and Sudan crisis. As of October 2024, more than 810,000 people have been recorded crossing into South Sudan from Sudan (UNCHR -October 2024) some settling in the targeted counties putting more pressure to existing limited WASH facilities. Climate change impacts are being felt every year now in South Sudan, with major flooding occurring annually since 2019.According to the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) Acute Food Insecurity and Acute Malnutrition Analysis for September – November 2024, released on 18th November 2024, Aweil East County and Aweil South were classified as Emergency (IPC Phase 4) while Aweil South County classified as crisis (IPC3) indicating a severe crisis. A multi-sector rapid assessment conducted by Impact Health Organization (IHO), Anglican Relief and Development Fund (ARDF), Water and Rural Transformation (WART), and Humanitarian Emergency Logistics & Preparedness (HELP) in Northern Barh Ghazal State in 2023 revealed a dire WASH situation. The assessment found that 89% of the total population practices open defecation, and over 33% use water from open sources for household use due to a lack of boreholes. Furthermore, the assessment discovered that individuals use less than 10L of water per day and travel 5km to reach the nearest water point. Poor handwashing practices, limited access to soap, Jari canisters, water purification supplies, and high morbidity from diarrhea and vomiting are among the significant challenges related to poor WASH conditions. Limited access to clean water and sanitation services contributes to the high prevalence of malnutrition, water-borne diseases, poor menstrual and hygiene management, risk of GBV at water points, absenteeism from school, and school dropout. Women and girls have specific WASH risks and needs, for example, they may face increased risk of GBV when collecting water and using communal latrinesWith this project, IHO plans to undertake timely integrated WASH interventions to meet the needs of 34900 beneficiaries in the three locations. These interventions will address the escalating humanitarian crisis in the targeted locations, particularly in improving the WASH conditions. The project targets a total of 17,450 beneficiaries (7451 Male, 9999 Females) across the three counties. 9764 Aweil East , 4423 Aweil South and 3263 Aweil North. The beneficiaries include children, adults, and the elderly, with a specific focus on those most vulnerable. The project aims to provide these individuals with improved access to water, sanitation, and hygiene facilities, and to promote better hygiene practices. 2. Project activities

Impact Health Organization (IHO) plans to undertake the following key activities based on the indicators: 1. Providing Standard Kits to SAM Children/Caretakers: IHO will provide standard kits paired with hygiene promotion to 200 children suffering from Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) and their caretakers. 2. Constructing/Rehabilitating Latrines in Nutrition Facilities: IHO will construct or rehabilitate 4 latrines in nutrition facilities. 3. Improving Access to Water: IHO will work towards increasing the number of people having access to an improved water source, with a target of reaching 6000 individuals. 5. Enhancing Sanitation Facilities: IHO will focus on increasing the number of people with access to sanitation facilities, with a target of reaching 4300 individuals. 6. Promoting Hygiene Practices: IHO will reach out to 9540 individuals with hygiene promotion. 7. Improving Access to Water and Sanitation for Women and Girls: Following a safety audit, IHO will work towards improving the access of 240 women and girls to water and/or sanitation. 8. Constructing/Rehabilitating Flood-Resilient WASH Facilities: IHO will construct or rehabilitate 15 water points and 10 latrine stances with a flood-resilient design. 9. Provision and distribution of water purification tablets Aqua tabs (1-67mg tab/10ltrs) or PUR sachets(4mg-1/10ltr) in the communities, School, OTP/SC, and Health facilities. 10.Training of water management committees on management of water points 10. Designing Disability Inclusive Sanitation Facilities: IHO will design 1 disability inclusive sanitation facilities. IHO will also integrate with health at both community and facility level (technical support) to contribute to reducing WASH-related morbidity and mortality. IHO will mainstream protection with a focus on GBV mitigation (safety audits) and disability inclusion. Mitigating GBV is recommended in all WASH programs, with a minimum package that includes assessment, analysis and planning, resource mobilization, programming, and communication and information sharing. The project will also focus on disability inclusion, which requires WASH partners to consult people with disabilities and collect disability-disaggregated data. This will allow the identification of specific needs/vulnerabilities, risks, and barriers which prevent people with disabilities from accessing WASH services on an equal basis with other people.
