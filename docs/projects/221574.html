Project Summary

The propose health intervention aims to provide critical, life-saving health services to vulnerable populations affected by climatic shocks, conflict, displacement, and humanitarian crises. Its primary purpose is to address urgent health needs, reduce mortality and morbidity, and strengthen community resilience. The intervention targets women, children, and other marginalized groups in hard-to-reach and conflict-affected areas, ensuring access to essential services such as maternal and child health care, immunization, disease prevention, and treatment of common illnesses.
Key activities include conducting 75,000 outpatient department (OPD) consultations, facilitating 3,643 normal deliveries, providing 52 Early Warning Alert and Response System (EWARS) and Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response (IDSR) reports, administering PENTA-3 vaccines, offering maternal healthcare services to 9,686 women, delivering child healthcare services to 18,160 children, providing psychiatric treatment and psychosocial support to 202 individuals with mental health conditions, offering community-based mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) to 13,500 persons living with disabilities, managing uncomplicated diarrhea cases, treating malaria cases, addressing respiratory infections, reaching 121,067 individuals with health education and promotion, conducting monthly community meetings, screening pregnant women, facilitating joint supportive supervision visits, strengthening linkages with Boma Health Initiatives, providing care for physical injuries and clinical management of rape (CMR), and implementing active epidemic disease surveillance and control.

1. Needs

According to IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition Snapshot, September 2024 - July 2025, in the harvest/post-harvest projection period of December 2024 to March 2025 1.71 million people are projected to be in Phase 4 and between April to July 2025, the food security situation is expected to deteriorate with the arrival of the lean season. It is likely that an estimated 7.69 million people (57 percent of the population analyzed) will be in Phase 3 or above. This will include 2.53 million people likely to be in Phase 4 and 63,000 people likely to be in Phase 5 (Catastrophe). According to Health Cluster (HC) People in Need and HPC Target analysis for 2025, the PIN in Guit County is 27,187 people and target is 20,390 people. In Mayendit County, the PIN is 34,343 with a target of 25,757 people. The PIN in Rubkona County are 211,802 and the HC targets are 158,851 individuals. Pibor County PIN is 157,649 people and HC targets are 118,237 individuals and for Panyikang County, the PIN is 53,286 and HC targets is 39,965 individuals. CASS will be directly targeting about 60,534 people. Children under 5 years are 9,080 children (4,631 boys and 4,449 girls), children above 5 years to 18 years of age are 21,186 children (11,016 boys and 10,170 girls) and adults above 18 to 65 years are 24,214 people (10,896 men and 13,318 women) and persons above 65 years (the aged) 6,054 people (2,724 men and 3,330 women). Among them are 3,127 persons with disability (1,720 males and 1,407 women).The IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition Snapshot, September 2024 - July 2025 projected that Guit, Rubkona, Mayendit, Pibor and Panyikang Counties will remain at Phase 4 (Emergency level) from post-harvest season 2024 to lean season 2025 and an findings from Integrated Public Health Rapid Assessment of Internally Displaced Persons in Rubkona County, conducted by REACH in July 2024 indicate that most households experiencing food consumption gaps indicative of IPC AFI Phase 4 (emergency), with some possible pockets of IPC AFI Phase 5 (catastrophe) conditions. An interagency Rapid Needs Assessment Report (IRNA) conducted in Mayendit County between 26th to 30th August 2024 reported that about 3,659 HHs (21,963 individuals were affected floods. Similarly, OCHA publication, South Sudan: Flooding Situation Flash Update No. 5 (25 September 2024) reported that the number of people displaced by conflict and flooding in Tonga, are 5,707 individuals. In addition, on the 29th July 2024, OXFAM reported that people in Pibor County resort to eating wild vegetables as hunger crisis deepens including 12 recorded deaths due to starvation. Acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition have severe and interrelated impacts on individual health, particularly among vulnerable populations such as children, pregnant and lactating women, and the elderly, exacerbating health emergencies and creating cyclical vulnerabilities leading to increased cases malaria, diarrheal diseases, and acute respiratory tract infections.The plan response will provide quality health services to people affected by health events, including maternal, child, adolescent, sexual and reproductive health, common and chronic diseases, neglected tropical diseases, MHPSS (Mental Health and Psychosocial Support) and GBV (Gender Based Violence). The response will also improve sexual reproductive health services of EmONC (emergency newborn and obstetric care), family planning, PMTCT (prevention of mother to child transmission), Clinical Management of Rape and supplies for vulnerable women and girls.CASS is having active ongoing projects in the selected counties with funding from SSHF (Pibor and Rubkona), CERF (Mayendit), BHA (Mayendit), USAID through Core Group Partners Project (Panyikang, Pibor, Malakal, Manyo, Fashoda, Renk and Pochalla)

2. Project activities

The programmatic activities are designed to align with the Strategic Objective SO 1 and Strategic Objective SO 2 and Strategic Objective SO 3. It is further aligned to Specific Objective 1.1, Specific Objective 1.3, Specific Objective 2.2, Specific Objective 3.1 and Specific Objective 3.2. The activities are also in line with Health Cluster Objective 1, Cluster Objective 2 and Cluster Objective 3. The key activities are:Activity 1: Conduct 60,000 OPD consultations in static facilities/mobile clinics Activity 2: Facilitate 1,4000 normal deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants Activity 3: Provide 52 timely and weekly EWARS/IDSR reports from each of the 3 focus Counties Activity 4: Facilitate 2,421 children with 3 doses of PENTA-3 vaccine Activity 5: Provide maternal healthcare services to 4,843 women of child bearing age of IDPs, returnees and host community including FP, ANC, PNC, IPT 1 and 2, de-worming and TT immunization. Activity 6: Provide child healthcare services (routine immunization, Vit A supplementation and child growth monitoring) to 9,080 children (4,631 boys and 4,449 girls), including accelerated mass measles and polio campaigns. Activity 7: Provide psychiatric treatment and psychosocial support to 202 mentally ill persons Activity 8: Provide community-based level 2 and level 3 (non- specialized) MHPSS to 13,500 persons living with disabilities (PLWD's), including referrals for specialized services from other partners. Activity 9: Manage 9,870 uncomplicated diarrhea cases with ORS and Zinc Activity 10: Manage 16,300 uncomplicated malaria cases with ACT Activity 11: Manage 8,450 uncomplicated respiratory infection (pneumonia) treated with AmoxicillinActivity 12: Reach directly and indirectly 121,067 people with health education and promotion to prevent disease outbreaksActivity 13: Reach directly and indirectly 121,067 with awareness raising community engagements on GBV prevention, prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse, drug abuse, peace building and peaceful coexistence.Activity 14: Conduct 12 monthly community meetings in each target county to provide accountability to the communities (AAP strategy).Activity 15: Screen 4,843 pregnant women, lactating mother and children under 5s for malnutrition and refer them to for treatment/management.Activity 16: Facilitate 12 monthly joint supportive supervision visits to increase quality of services Activity 17: Strengthen linkages with Boma Health Initiatives (Boma Health Workers) for community-based disease surveillance and referral Activity 18: Provide care to physical injuries and CMR including level 2 and level 3 MHPSS to survivors of GBVActivity 19: Provide active epidemic disease surveillance, prevention and control including outbreak detection, investigation and respond within 48 hours. Activity 20: Provide IPC in health facilities through regular assessment using score cards, provision of supplies and protocols and continuous monitoring of WASH in health facilities
