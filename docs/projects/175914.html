Project Summary

The project aims to provide sustainable and equitable access to safe water, sanitation, and hygiene to ameliorate the suffering of 25,000 displaced persons and vulnerable host communities in Yirol East County of Lakes State and Gogrial East and West Counties of Warrap State. Focus will mainly be on women and children who constitute the bulk of the displaced persons and at-risk host families, and will provide lifesaving assistance to households who have children suffering from malnutrition and preventable communicable diseases due to poor access to safe water, hygiene, and sanitation facilities as a result of the impact of flooding and widespread inter-communal violence in Lakes and Warrap States particularly Yirol East and Gogrial East and West Counties of Warrap, where this project will be implemented. The affected population includes internally displaced persons and vulnerable host communities including but not limited to poor female headed households, households with persons living with disability, those with low income, and the most food insecure households suffering with malnutrition cases, particularly pregnant and lactating mothers and under ﬁve children. Targeting will be based on humanitarian principles of humanity and impartiality to save lives and alleviate suffering and aid will be provided based on need alone. CAFOD protection and safeguarding framework is central to our work and will ensure safe, meaningful access, dignified and inclusive programming. A participatory and consultative approach will be used to agree on targeting criteria ensuring the most vulnerable individuals/households voices are heard and are at the heart of our intervention. This will be implemented by building on past targeting methodology used in previous CAFOD response and recovery interventions. The project plan will allow CAFOD to provide immediate lifesaving assistance as per the needs and this will be delivered through repair and rehabilitation of critical broken boreholes, house-to-house hygiene promotion through project volunteers, training of water users committees to aid sustainability of access to safe drinking water and refresher training for handpump mechanics which will be conducted by CAFOD humanitarian staff. To support girl-child education, the project will provide sanitary kits for adolescent girls in primary school to curb menstrual hygiene-related absenteeism from school and mediocre performance. The project will also train women, girls and boys on the making of re-usable menstrual pads (RUMPS). This will be a source of income generation for the concerned individuals. Children being social agents for change, the project will establish and train school hygiene clubs to pass on good hygiene practices to their peers in school and eventually to the entire community. The main messages would revolve around personal, food, environmental hygiene, safe water chain and safe disposal of solid and human waste amongst others. Hygiene campaigns in public places such markets, health facilities, village centres and schools would also be conducted to enlighten the communities on environmental and public hygiene. COVID19 Infection Prevention and Control messaging will be mainstreamed in every activity throughout the project cycle. The project will address the drivers of vulnerabilities and underlying structural issues across the targeted counties. This project will be complimented with other CAFOD projects on protection, food security and livelihoods funded from different sources. As with this project, FSL projects prioritise areas of high malnutrition and diarrhoeal cases to address the food insecurity emergency. The project will collaborate with and complement the work of other stakeholders in the area to address WASH in Nutrition and WASH for GBV mitigation.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in South Sudan is projected to worsen in 2021, driven by cumulative effects of years of recurring conflict and violence, further aggravated by subsequent economic and environmental shocks. Continued macroeconomic crisis, the low preparedness capacity of national and state authorities, COVID-19 pandemic, low oil prices, and consecutive years of flooding are exacerbating existing vulnerabilities and humanitarian needs of people, while pushing more people downward spiral into crisis and reliance on humanitarian assistance. An estimated 5.6 million people will be negatively impacted by lack of/or inadequate access to clean water and improved sanitation and hygiene practices according to HNO 2021 overview. The main drivers of WASH needs include limited physical infrastructure, sub-national violence, and recurring flooding in many parts of the country. These shocks have led to the continued displacement of people and restricted their access to already limited and functional WASH infrastructure. Increased displacement also places an extra burden on existing WASH infrastructure in displacement sites. Only 36 per cent of households reported having access to an improved water source in under 30 minutes without facing any protection concerns according to the FSNMS Round 25/ 26. The quantity of water available per day is also below standards with water consumption per capita of 12.81 litres and 8.96 litres for Yirol East and Gogrial West counties respectively according to CAFOD KAP survey November 2020. Poor access to clean water, hygiene and sanitation services combined with high levels of food insecurity compromises people’s health and nutrition conditions across South Sudan and contributes to one of the highest child mortality rates in the world. Communities in Gogrial and Yirol cite lack of access to water as leading to tension and conflict between communities... Access to sanitation remained low in Gogrial West and East counties, with only 15.6 per cent of households reporting owning a latrine in their compound according to CAFOD KAP survey November 2020 conducted in Gogrial West. Only 10% of the population countrywide have access to improved sanitation (UNICEF). Community hygiene awareness and practices require continuous improvement to help in gradual behaviour change on hygiene practices. This is even more important in the context of COVID-19. Poor access to WASH services combined with high levels of food insecurity continue to have a detrimental impact on the health of children, as seen through the high prevalence of malnutrition and water-borne diseases. Counties reporting high GAM rates have also high WASH needs which include Yirol East and Gogrial West and East. This project will address the identified WASH gaps, coordinating with other actors in the targeted locations such as health and nutrition actors HELP and CUAMM in Yirol East and ACF and NCA in Gogrial Counties respectively.

2 - Project activities

CAFOD targeting will be in line with the WASH cluster’s HRP targeting guidance. We will focus on both hardware and software activities to provide a full package of WASH services to targeted communities. The project plan will allow CAFOD to provide immediate lifesaving assistance to 25,000 individuals as per the needs. This will be delivered through repair and rehabilitation of critical broken boreholes. As part of rehabilitation of boreholes, pump mechanics will be trained and equipped with appropriate tools and training of water users’ committees. Households targeted for hygiene messaging will be mobilised to construct and use their own latrines. Hygiene Promotion Volunteers will be trained to carry out house to house hygiene messaging. To support girl-child education, the project will provide sanitary kits for adolescent girls in primary school to address menstrual hygiene-related absenteeism from school. The project will also train women, girls and boys on the making of re-usable menstrual pads (RUMPS). This will be a source of income generation for the concerned individuals. Children being social agents for change, the project will establish and train school hygiene clubs to pass on good hygiene practices to their peer in school and to the entire community. The main messages would revolve around personal, food and environmental hygiene, safe water chain and safe disposal of solid and human waste amongst others. Hygiene campaigns in public places such as markets, health facilities, village centres and schools would also be conducted to enlighten the communities on environmental and public hygiene. Messaging on COVID19 Infection Prevention and Control practices will be mainstreamed in every activity throughout the project cycle. At the same time, the project will engage strategically to address the drivers of vulnerabilities and underlying structural issues across the targeted counties. The project will target the IPC 4&5 counties (Yirol East and Gogrial West) as per the WASH targeting guidance. Areas of high malnutrition and diarrhoeal cases will be prioritised in which our food security and livelihoods programme will be implemented to curb the alarming hunger situation. Cost-recovery mechanisms will be put in place through users’ fee contributions and linking the pump mechanics with spare parts traders. Construction quality of WASH infrastructures will be monitored and maintain. It will also integrate protection mainstreaming, conflict sensitivity and specific activities such as GBV case management and psychosocial support services to survivors of SGBV. Community consultations in the design infrastructures will be undertaken to ensure the project is gender and disability sensitive. A multi-sectoral approach is key in this project implementation and an integrated response is very crucial. The project will collaborate and complement the work of other stakeholders in the area to address WASH in Nutrition and WASH for GBV mitigation.

The skills in CTP were horned by carrying out an actual market analysis by CAFOD in Yirol East, Rumbek and Gogrial in 2020 in preparation for its food and NFIs distribution project which was successfully carried out involving traders in Wau and Yirol East. Consultations during the market analysis were held with communities, local chambers of commerce who mobilised the traders, other local vendors, consultations with other NGOs, and government officials. This was to dig out and unpack information to streamline processes before it rolled out its cash-based response. As mentioned above, a market assessment and cash feasibility analysis were undertaken which informed CAFOD to embark on the commodity voucher programme. Based on that experience CAFOD will build on lessons learnt from that experience in shaping the CVA approach for of this project. In 2021 CAFOD has already embarked on market analysis for supply of various commodities such as WASH NFIs and crop and vegetable seeds in which this project will benefit from this analysis. There is also ongoing market analysis on handpump spare parts supply chain in which CAFOD will engage local traders in supply of handpump parts in the local market for easy access by the community. To ensure beneficiary choice and flexibility especially as regards the types of WASH, hygiene items and the mode of assistance, there would be consultations with the participating households whose lives are affected. An important secondary aim has been to stimulate local business and inject money into the local economy to strengthen the market system.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

CAFOD implemented commodity voucher for food and non-food items in previous projects in Yirol East in 2020. The venture into CVA was after comprehensive market assessment and feasibility analysis which informed the decision to undertake CVA in both Yirol East, and Gogrial East and West counties. The market assessment to ascertained the market potential to support the approach. Things considered in the assessment included functionality and access to the market by the affected population, availability and source of goods and services, and traders’ potential to supply the market. Cash transfer programming (CTP) refers to all programs where cash (or vouchers for goods or services) is directly provided to beneficiaries. It is an approach that builds upon linkages, capacities, incentives, and relationships to encourage effective market recovery. The logistical burdens and risks that are usually experienced by agencies in transporting in-kind supplies to end beneficiaries are thus transferred to the vendors or suppliers. The use of vouchers or cash reduces overall costs of the assistance and thus more people can be reached with limited resources. The engagement of local vendors in supplying items to the beneficiaries is intended to support the local market dynamics. Through this approach, the cash or vouchers given to beneficiaries and exchanged with local vendors for supplies would advance financial linkages in the local markets as well as boosting the purchasing power and local economy. Cash and vouchers assistance (CVA) is globally felt to be a more dignified form of assistance for humanitarian aid recipients because it promotes dignity of the participants. Individuals accustomed to supporting themselves and their families suddenly become dependent on aid and charity. While their sense of dignity cannot be easily and fully restored, cash and voucher transfers offer some choice and a small degree of control in a situation where many feel they have none. The affected populations preferred the flexibility that cash offered them and the choice it offered them to prioritise their own needs. We have realised that use of cash and vouchers empowers participants through ownership of their needs and the opportunity to purchase locally preferred supplies. Since CAFOD intends to provide WASH NFIs through vouchers this will further boost the local economy and potential of local traders and trigger market growth.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

CAFOD has strict policy on PSEA which guide all staff in their daily interactions with beneficiaries. All staff and volunteers, consultants hired by CAFOD must adhered to CAFOD’s PSEA policy and staff sign staff code of behaviour and safeguarding policy. Staff are inducted (trained) on PSEA and code of behaviour and safeguarding policies within 3 months of recruitment. Volunteers and contractors are also oriented and expected to abide by the policies. Programme participants are made aware of the policies and what to expect from CAFOD’s staff. CAFOD has safe and confidential channels for reporting SEA concerns directly to the Country Representative or focal person in UK or through ethics point website, helpdesk, hotline, information kiosk and other channels which is regularly checked, and timely responses provided to the complainant. CAFOD carries out regular community awareness on the existing channels of airing out their complaints and feedback as well as making them understand that it is their right to complain. In addition, there is a policy on whistleblowing which protects the whistle-blower.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Every CAFOD project is required to establish meaningful and accessible complaint and feedback mechanisms. To establish these mechanisms, we will follow CAFOD’s guidelines. For this project, we will establish complaints and feedback mechanism through community consultations to identify appropriate channels most preferred by the community in general and particular focus on people with specific vulnerabilities such as people living with disabilities, elderly, IDPs, etc. CAFOD has standard operating procedure to receive and respond to both sensitive and non-sensitive complaints. A flow chart indicating the process and decision making at different levels has been developed and in use. When a complaint is received by the focal point person within the organisation, it is logged. Depending on the nature of the complaint (sensitive and non-sensitive), it’s referred to the appropriate person for actioning. For sensitive complaints, it’s referred to senior management who will review and may decide to carry out investigations and further actions within the prescribed time limits. Likewise, on sensitive cases are referred to the relevant staff members for actioning and resolutions. In any case whether sensitive or non-sensitive, feedback is given to the complaint within prescribed timeline. In the event the complainant is not satisfied with the response or feedback, there is room for appeal in which the case will be handle by different person. In addition to the preferred community channels for communicating complaints or feedbacks, there are safe channels such as CAFOD hotline, emails, online CAFOD’s ethics point. CAFOD has created women and girls’ friendly spaces where they can give their feedback and complaints in a safe and dignified way to trained staff who know how to handle such issues. This has cultivated confidence in the vulnerable people coming forward to air out their views. Focus group discussions are conducted with different categories of vulnerable persons and key informant interview with service providers who work with vulnerable groups such as people living with disabilities, elderly and women and girls. CAFOD works with local partners who are embedded in the local communities. They employ local staff who understands the culture and languages of the communities. This improves understanding of the local context and cultural structures in the areas of operation which is important for access and acceptance, and in-depth analysis of the needs of different vulnerable groups. Some project documents are produced in local language e.g., posters, IEC materials, and information on complaint and feedback mechanism.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

Complaints received/logged will regularly be analysed by the focal person and timely responded to and the programme adjusted on real time to ensure gaps identified are urgently addressed. The project carries out regular monitoring, project reviews, evaluations, community consultations, which are used to inform the project decisions. For example, the project carries out various project distribution monitoring to assess the satisfaction levels of various aspects of the project. CAFOD carries out regular community awareness on the existing channels of airing out their complaints and feedback as well as making them understand that it is their right to complain. In addition, there is a policy on whistleblowing which protects the whistleblower. CAFOD has clearly laid out procedures on handling and responding to feedback and complaints. Complaints are categorised and serious, sensitive complaints such as SEA are handled by senior management with clear investigation and disciplinary protocols it must be resolved within set time limits and feedback provided to affected persons. On risk 8.4, on no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors CAFOD has robust protection response and prevention activities in most of its projects. This leads to design of projects that meet the specific needs of groups of people.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The proposed intervention will contribute to the collective outcomes on food insecurity by improving access to basic services in emergency, mitigate GBV and protection and build WASH resilience. It will Improve level of WASH services for both IDP and host community in Yirol East, Gogrial West and East Counties which falls between severity levels 3 to 4. WASH is a critical complement in enabling reduction in morbidity and mortality. The close correlation between malnutrition and diarrhoea due to poor WASH services is a well-established cause of morbidity and mortality amongst children under five years old. The undernourished are more likely to suffer higher incidences and increased mortality rates from diarrhoeal diseases, while on the other hand those suffering from diarrhoea are more likely to suffer from wasting, stunting, or other complications resulting from abrupt dehydration and loss of valuable nutrients and electrolytes. Furthermore, recurrent parasitism due to faecal-oral contamination associated with poor hygiene and sanitation leads to repeat cyclical infections, diarrhoeal diseases and malnutrition. This intervention will complement CAFOD's ongoing response in the locality and will improve community’s access to safe drinking water as well as increasing adoption of good hygiene practices thus, reducing incidences of diarrhoea and other hygiene and sanitation related diseases and building resilience of communities to shocks and stresses.

During needs assessments and meetings, separate focus group discussions will be conducted for women and girls to ensure they feel comfortable fully express their views on sensitive issues. 12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

This project will contribute to address the underlying root causes of malnutrition in children under 5. The insufficient WASH services disproportionately affect women and girls and the long travelling distances to access water expose women and girls to risks of sexual gender-based violence as well as limiting their coping capacities. Consumption of unsafe water, poor sanitation and hygiene practices is one of the major contributors of malnutrition, high morbidity and mortality especially amongst the under five-year in Yirol East. The close correlation between malnutrition and diarrhoea due to poor WASH services is a well-established cause of morbidity and mortality amongst children under five years old. The undernourished are more likely to suffer higher incidences and increased mortality rates from diarrhoeal diseases, while on the other hand those suffering from diarrhoea are more likely to suffer from wasting, stunting, or other complications resulting from abrupt dehydration and loss of valuable nutrients and electrolytes. Furthermore, recurrent parasitism due to faecal-oral contamination associated with poor hygiene and sanitation leads to repeat cyclical infections, diarrhoeal diseases and malnutrition.
