Project Summary

The coordination and common services (CCS) aims to support an efficient coordination systems enabling and effective and coherent response for people with humanitarian needs in crisis affected Counties in the States of Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria and Central Equatoria States of South Sudan. The project locations are considered for reasons being presence of IDPS in Fashoda and it borders Sudan- Southern Kordofan with the signing of revitalized peace agreement, there is likelihood of returnees coming in large number who will require humanitarian assistance in the Counties of Nasir and Longechuk bordering Ethiopia, Meanwhile, counties like Juba,Magwi and Terekeka receive IDPS every season from neighboring States due to flooding and inter-communal flighting and returnees. Therefore, Inter-cluster coordination remains the relevant coordination body at national and subnational levels, with a needs based approach informed by multi-sectoral joint needs analysis and strategic response planning underpinned by quality programming and a data driven prioritization.
UNKEA will lead and support inter-cluster coordination mechanisms at State and County levels in facilitating the development of context-specific and responsive Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for the humanitarian needs in these States and assures a coherent and coordinated approach to planning and operationalizing the shared strategic objectives , informed by multi-sectoral joint needs assessments, analysis, and strategic response planning. Quality inter-sectoral data will be of special focus as it is critical to informing evidence-based prioritization, decision-making, and quality programming and implementation throughout the Humanitarian Program Cycle (HPC) to achieving the shared strategic objectives and improve humanitarian outcomes.
This project proposes to; -
 Strengthen joint needs analysis informing strategic response planning for effective and coordinated humanitarian action.
The sectoral objectives are linked to the inter-sectoral specific objectives of; -
 Strengthening coordination and contextual analysis of needs conducted at the national and sub-national level
The project will be implemented in the States of Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, and Central Equatoria. Strengthened engagement with the local–duty bearers and partners will aid in strengthening inter-sectorial coordination mechanisms and ensure effective and sustainable humanitarian service delivery. The States of Upper Nile,Eastern and Central Equatoria are selected based on protracted history of inter-communal flights and flooding which displace thousands of IDPS in the Counties as well as the returnees coming to the Counties following signing of revitalized peace agreement as well as formation of State governments which will ensure relative peace in those counties,
bureaucratic impediments to enable timely ,effective humanitarian access in Counties of Upper Nile, Central Equatoria and Eastern Equatoria. The strategic objective of the CCS is to enable people to recover from crisis, seek solutions to displacement, support the freedom of movement and freedom to choose a place to live or stay and for safe, dignified voluntary and gender-and-age specific returns and build resilience to acute shocks and chronic stresses through targeted programming to support coping capacities and live livelihoods in three States.
The project will further training of community leaders on community risk reduction strategies in conflict and flood prone Counties in order to minimize impacts of the events on the livelihood of the inhabitants, engaging key staked holders on community accountability of the humanitarian assistance. The project targets 12000 beneficiaries in the States and Counties respectively

1 - Needs

Humanitarian service delivery to people in South Sudan has multiple access challenges , such as limited access, persistent insecurity including deliberate targeting of humanitarian assets and a constant threat to the security of staff, intrusions on humanitarian space, ranging from violence to bureaucratic impediments, and impact of Covid-19 restrictions and measures . Though the recently formation of government on revitalized peace agreement gives hope to South Sudanese for peace and stability, the general situation remains dire in many parts of the Country. The cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence and destroyed livelihoods have left more than seven million people or about two thirds of the population in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection (WHO September, 2019). Therefore, as humanitarian aid agencies are delivering humanitarian assistance in term of food, education, WASH, health and nutrition service, to these crisis-affected populations who have very weakened community governance structures or none at all, with many civilians who are in possession of guns, humanitarian aid workers had never been able to delivery services freely. According to report on humanitarian access in South Sudan, 2020, it was reported that South Sudan is one of the most challenging operating environments in the world, due to security concerns and logistic challenge. Access to the crisis-affected population is particularly challenging in disputed areas. Of recent, it was reported that about 1.5 million people are still living in areas where armed hostilities, violence against humanitarian aid workers and assets, and other access impediments render humanitarian activities severely restricted, or in some cases impossible (UN-OCHA report, 2020). Many at times there are issues to do with revenge killings targeting national humanitarian aid workers, harassment of humanitarian aid workers, vandalization of boreholes, health facilities and school structures and looting of aid supplies by army youth civilians/fighting forces. This culture of vandalization of boreholes, schools and health structures by army youths or people who are or whose relatives are the main beneficiaries of such facilities has been making humanitarian aid agencies to incur un necessary costs. Similarly, the culture of revenge killings and harassment of humanitarian aid workers brought an adverse effect where the wave of these attacks have prompted the humanitarian aid workers to threaten pull out of the all the services if the revenge attacks and other forms of harassments continue. In light of this context, there is need to set up and reinforce community management/governing structures to relook at the issue of revenge killing, harassment of humanitarian aid workers and vandalization of boreholes, health and school structures from a critical perspective through empowerment of community participation and involvement in the work of humanitarian aid workers. Crisis-affected people and other stakeholders should be enlightened on recognition of humanitarian work, and commitment to upholding the safety and security of humanitarian aid workers, including by providing a feedback mechanism platform that is relevant, feasible and sustainable for both humanitarian aid actors and the local community.

2 - Project activities

UNKEA will strengthen joint needs analysis informing strategic response planning for effective and coordinated humanitarian action by the end of the year 2021. Under this outcome, UNKEA will carry out the following activities;- Facilitate State level inter-agency coordination meetings;  Conduct State level inter-sectoral needs assessments and analysis; Disseminate the findings of State level inter-sectoral needs analysis through the various appropriate coordination forums, including the Inter Cluster Coordination Group (ICCG), Needs Analysis Working Group (NAWG) and Information Management Working Group (IMWG).Support establishment of humanitarian hubs at subnational levels in Central Equatoria, Eastern Equatoria and Upper Nile. UNKEA will ensure humanitarian hubs are established in the three States for effective and coherent response by humanitarian actors in the prioritized Capital States of each state
