Project Summary

The proposed action is based on the 2018-2020 HRP specific objective of WASH Sector: “Ensure equitable, affordable and sustainable access to safe and sufficient WASH services with dignity for the underserved, underserved and most vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation in Gaza and the West Bank.”
Based on recent ACTED assessments, extensive consultations with key stakeholders, and following the recommendations of the Palestinian Water Authority, Coastal Municipal Water Utility (CMWU), the project will target 200 vulnerable households of Wadi al Salqa in Middle Area governorate.
The aim of this project is to restore access to domestic and drinking water for most vulnerable households of Wadi Al Salqa (Middle Area, Gaza Strip), by ensuring their access to drinking water supply, increasing water storage capacity and improving their sanitation facilities (bathrooms and kitchens). This project will also raise awareness and build capacity on good hygiene practices.
By improving access to drinking water, basic sanitation and hygiene awareness, this project will also prevent further deterioration of the households’ conditions of living due to high temperatures and harsh summer weather. This action will benefit to at least 1,120 households members.
The project will be implemented by ACTED in partnership with CESVI and Natuf NGOs. CESVI has been working in Palestine since 1994 and has develop a strong expertise in WASH (South Hebron Hills – adequate access to water in terms of both quantity and quality, introduction and testing of innovative technologies for water, sanitation and hygiene). Natuf NGO has been implementing projects in Gaza since 2010, in particular in the sectors of Food security, WASH, Shelter and alternative energies (solar panels).
The project will be coordinated with relevant stakeholders, such as the WASH cluster, Palestinian Water Authority, Coastal Municipal Water Utility (CMWU), and the Ministry of Social Development.
Eventually, the project will be supported by the mainstreaming of an Integrated Protection Approach (IPA), with specific focus on women and vulnerable groups, and a strong Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) component. In particular, beneficiaries will be heavily involved in the action through community based empowered committees, and a comprehensive Complaint Response Mechanism will be deployed. Gender principles will be mainstreamed during the action and Committees will be trained on referral mechanisms of protection cases with a particular focus given to women and children issues such as related to GBV. An ACTED protection officer will be focal point of the committees to receive and follow-up on cases.

Needs

The Gaza Strip faces a dire situation in terms of access to quality water, sanitation and hygiene services. Due to decades of blockade, Israeli restrictions, succession of wars, existing WASH facilities have been severely affected and are non or partially operational. With 20 hours/day of electricity shortage, water facilities rely mostly on generators to ensure minimum of critical services. Due to shortage and inflation of fuel, households are reliant on private water trucking to access domestic and drinkable water; desalination and wastewater treatment plants cannot complete treatment cycles, thus respectively resulting in the delivery of undrinkable water and release of partially-treated wastes in the sea. As consequence, the number of Liters per Capita per Day (LCD) has decreased from 84 to 53 LCD between 2016 and 2017, while WHO’s recommendations are set at 100 l/c/d (WASH cluster report on Electricity Crisis, 2017). Today, more than 2 million people have no access to safe WASH facilities and receive water supply only 4 to 6 hours every 3 to 5 days (“Gaza electricity crisis leaves more people without water than in aftermath of war”, article published on ReliefWeb based on Oxfam report, August 2017). Only 10% of Gaza’s population has access to safe drinking water, compared to 90% in the West Bank or about 85% in the MENA region in general (“Alarming water situation in Gaza”, World Bank, November 2017).Following previous ACTED assessments and identified needs, ACTED and MAAN conducted, in September 2018, a multi-sectorial needs’ assessment to address WASH, Shelter and Protection needs in Wadi as Salqa. In coordination with local municipality, Ministry of Social Development, and local committees, 198 HHs were visited and confirmed the lack of access to safe and sufficient WASH services in the area. The assessment results raised that:- 50,5% of visited families reported no access to any source of income and are totally dependent on aid- 49% of visited HHs reported difficulties to access drinking water, difficulties illustrated as following due to high cost of water (for 50% of assessed HH) far distance to water sources (for 26% of assessed HH), lack of water trucking (for 14% of them), or other reasons such as the weather or closure of public standpipes (for 10% of visited HH). - 25% of HHs do not have potable water tanks- 73% of HHs reported the unavailability of public sewage network in their area, whereas the remaining families (28%) reported the availability of public sewage network in their area but for 78% of them, their HHs are not connected to the network.- 57%of the HHs reported they are not connected to the public water network and they use private wells, relatives’ wells, agriculture wells or purchase wells to access water.- 48% of HHs reported that they lack or face bad condition of toilet/shower, while 2 % of families are living without toilets- 52% of HHs reported that they lack of or face bad kitchen condition, while 5 % families do not have kitchen- 48% of HHs reported that their houses are dark and gloomyIn addition, a CESVI WASH specialist carried out a field technical assessment to identify the needs of Wadi Al Saqla people at community level in term of water quality and sanitation hygiene. The majority of people rely on poor and unsafe cesspit tanks that cause untreated wastewater over floods which highly impact the health of children and other vulnerable people such as pregnant and lactating women, or elders. The poor water quality delivered to HHs members is highly at risk of waterborne diseases.

Activities

Activity 1 - Establishment and capacity-building of WASH committees2 Community-Based WASH committees will be established. They will be involved throughout the whole project to ensure a solid engagement and ownership at community level and to guarantee a better compliance and respect of safety and privacy of each individual, especially the most vulnerable groups. WASH committees will be responsible for the distribution of hygiene kits, as well as to help for the upgrade of basic water and sanitation materials and facilities. ACTED will provide capacity-building to the committee members about WASH, SPHERE and protection standards. Activity 2 - Provision of drinking water and installation of Household Water Treatment StorageACTED and CESVI will provide targeted households with Household Water Treatment and safe Storage (HWTS) unit with Gravity Driven Membrane (GDM) technology to minimize the risk of cross contamination before consumption. Dedicated practical trainings will be carried out in the 200 identified HH. The project team along with WASH Protection committee members will test the quality of water at the supplying point, treated water and at the point of use (at the cup). ACTED and Natouf will distribute 200L of potable water tanks to 200 HHs in order to ensure that every HH has enough storage capacity. ACTED will also distribute a water storage maintenance (incl. cleaning) and repair kit to the WASH committees. The kit will include a saw, glue, and spare piping and connectors, boots, gloves, chlorine tablets or granules, bleach, and scrubs. The WASH committee will be trained on how to use the repair and maintenance kit and will be responsible for ensuring that the items are properly used and maintained in the long term.Activity 3- Rehabilitation and upgrade of sanitation facilities at households and neighborhoods’ level 200 HHs will receive conditional cash assistance to upgrade sanitation facilities (upgrade of kitchen and bathroom/toilets, connection of drinking water tanks to a sink equipped with water saving supply, and connection of their domestic water tank to the water network inside the house). Work implementation will be monitored through visits conducted by ACTED to each household to review progress made by beneficiaries and decide whether to trigger, postpone or cancel installments. Monitoring visits will also be used to identify any technical issues beneficiaries could be facing and solve them on the spot through complementary training and guidance. ACTED will provide drainage to 150 HHs in critical need of sewage drainage; pits the drainage will be carried twice a month during 9 months. At the project onset, ACTED team will start directly contracting sewage drainage service providers in order to provide immediate intervention. It is expected that 2000 6000 m3 of sewage will be drained/pumped to closed sewage network in coordination with authoritiesActivity 4 – Promotion of hygiene practices and protection principlesAt the beginning, baseline survey will be implemented by CESVI to assess WASH practices and knowledge within the 200 targeted households. TBaseline's outcomes will be used to adjust WASH promotion materials. CESVI will then implement awareness-raising sessions. In total, 4 members for each of the 200 HHs targeted by Activities 2 and 3 will benefit from this campaign. Specific sessions of hygiene promotion will target mothers, children and supported by the distribution of 200 hygienic kits, incl. both long-term items and consumables such as: detergent, sanitary pads, shampoo, soap, a soap box, adult toothbrush, child toothbrush, toothpaste, a towel and a washing line. Kits distribution will be carried out by ACTED and CESVI with the support of WASH Protection committees. At the end of the project, CESVI will implement an endline survey to measure progresses in regard to WASH KAP among the targeted population.

Indicators

Indicators are selected based on clusters' indicators. Project implementation will be supported by a comprehensive monitoring system enabling to gather all relevant data to steer the action, maximize its impact, measure its effects, anticipate and mitigate any challenge. The ACTED Appraisal Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU), independent from the Program Department, will be in charge of measuring project outcomes and progress indicators through a variety of approaches and methodology. AME activities will enable gathering and reporting detailed gender and age disaggregated data on beneficiaries and to measure the project outcomes of different targeted groups (vulnerable farmers, female-headed households). Eventually, satisfaction surveys, coupled with a comprehensive Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM), both managed by the AMEU, will enable to identify and solve any issues reported by both beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries and steer the project to maximize its impact. Accountability to beneficiaries and communities will be ensured through the Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) complying with HAP standards and ACTED’s SoPs. The mechanism will feature several channels, including a dedicated hotline and frequent visits from CBAC members, ACTED protection officer and independent monitoring staff from Natuf, to ensure that feedback is received from beneficiaries, and, when preferred, confidentiality is ensured and respected. During the project, the Community-Based WASH Protection Committees (CBWPC) will be responsible for monitoring the works and ensuring that protection standards are upheld and concerns are addressed or referred to the ACTED protection officer. Following the completion of the project, the committees will be responsible for monitoring, identifying and reporting any technical or protection issues in regard to rehabilitation of land and assets to ACTED as well as the relevant local service and maintenance providers, to ensure the sustainability of the project’s outputs and outcomes. In addition, ACTED protection officer, with the support of the CBWPC, will be in charge of monitoring the dissemination of protection principles as well as their concrete application during the implementation process. In particular, ACTED protection officer and the committees will regularly visit all beneficiaries to ensure that protection concerns are being respected and addressed through the works. Were any lack to be identified, the Protection officer will offer guidance and complementary training to beneficiaries. Finally, ACTED Project Development Department (PDD), Finance Department and Audit Department will closely follow-up project activities and ensure the action is implemented in conformity with the HPF guidelines, international humanitarian principles and standards, as well as locally defined shelter cluster recommendations and standards. Using ACTED’s global Project Cycle Management tools, ACTED and Natouf Project Management teams will report monthly to the PDD, on project implementation, outputs and data to ensure that all indicators are on track, delays are prevented and any difficulties are identified and recorded. Similarly, the Finance and Audit department will ensure a constant follow up of all procurement, expenses and transactions. Those monitoring activities will ensure the highest level of compliance and transparency, as well as the production of comprehensive and detailed reports.
