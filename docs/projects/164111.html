Project Summary

Through this project this NGO will implemented an integrated health and mental health response in Northeast Syria. Primarily the project will support the provision of critical health services in areas where needs and gaps have been identified and the population largely depend on supported facilities for daily health services. Primary and secondary health services will be provided through four hospitals and six primary healthcare clinics (PHCs) in Aleppo (Ain al Arab), Ar Raqqa (Raqqa City, Al Qahtaniyeh, Akirishi, Abo Qobeih), Deir Ez Zor (Kesrah, Al Harmoushiyeh, Jazra Bu Hamayid) and Al Hassekeh (Al Malikeyeh, Tal Baydar). Health services will include provision of basic primary health services, including treatment of common acute and chronic illnesses and injuries, management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, including local endemic diseases and health education. It will also provide basic SRH services such as ANC and PNC, family planning services focusing on women and girls of reproductive age, in addition with closely coordination with WHO will provide vaccination services for children under five. PHCC’s will also provide a referral system to enable referral of cases that need further services to secondary and tertiary levels. Through the Kidney Centre at Raqqa National Hospital, the project will provide critical lifesaving hemodialysis services to huge catchment area in NES. The project will increase access to more than one million people including IDPs and host communities.
Mental health needs continues to be highly unmet due to shortage of qualified work force and resources, even though the needs continue escalating across the globe. The prevalence of depression, anxiety, PTSD, psychosis and trauma continues to be high in NES. Massive displacements a due to a protracted period of war, has put pressure on already unavailable resources. The clients will be provided with mhGAP care using trained practioners, whom will also be provided with psychotropic medications. mhGAP intervention is suitable in this kind of setting due to the shortage of psychiatric doctors and comprehensive mental health care. This will also be supported with awareness of common mental health conditions and mhGAP community toolkit which shall raise level of awareness and consumption of the services.

4 - Needs

Access to quality healthcare services remains one of the main gaps for conflict affected population. According to WHO Health Resources and Services Availability Mapping System (HeRAMS), in 2019 only 54% of Syria health facilities are fully functional, with remaining 46% either non-functional or only partially functional, with non-communicable diseases remaining among the most prevalent health issues in Syria. Limited availability of health services, particularly in non-government of Syria controlled areas, is one of the main factors that is hindering access to most vulnerable conflict affected populations. The situation in NES is further exacerbated following the recent Turkish led military operations displacing nearly 200,000 civilians. Healthcare is reported to be the top priority need in NES. There have not been "outbreaks" in 2019. However, communicable disease incidence does remain elevated and the potential for oubreaks remains high. WHO estimated that the prevalence of mental disorders (depression, anxiety, post-traumatic stress disorder, bipolar disorder, and schizophrenia) is 22·1% at any point in time in the conflict-affected populations. The report estimated that more than one in five people (22·1%) in post-conflict settings has depression, anxiety disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, bipolar disorder, or schizophrenia and that almost one in ten people (9·1%)in post-conflict settings has a moderate of severe mental disorder at any point in time. Integrated and responsive mental health and social Care services in community-based settings; strategies for promotion and prevention in mental health has been recommended. As part of the provision of affordable and accessible mental health care WHO recommends Mental Health gap Action plan (mhGAP) which enables doctors who are not specialists to offer care to patients which includes provision of psychotropic medication.

5 - Implementation of activities

Secondary/specialized health care services will be provided through support to all departments at Kobane Hospital, 6 in Raqqa Hospital including Raqqa Kidney Center; 4 in Kesrah Hospital and 2 in Al Malikeyeh Hospital. Primary health care will be provided at Al Qahtaniyeh, Abo Qobieh and Akirishi in Raqqa; Jazrat Abu Hamayid and Al Harmoshiyeh in Deir Ez Zor; and Tal Bayder in Al Hassekah. Services include treatment of common acute and chronic illnesses/injuries, management of communicable/non-communicable diseases, as well as basic SRH services such as ANC and PNC, family planning services. Support entails essential medicines, consumables and medical equipment, salaries for health and support staff, health-related training and supportive supervision, and covering running costs. Mental Health Services will be provided at selected health facilities that include mhGAP consultations by trained doctors who will provide assessments and treatment to patients with symptoms of MNS conditions at two facilities (Raqqa and Kesrah Hospital). Outreach workers will provide household monitoring to ensure adherence to medications. PSWs will provide structured support group activities for interested patients. Training and supervision on mhGAP HIG will be provided to General doctors to develop a pool of qualified mhGAP trained health practitioners. The organization will pay close attention to the needs of vulnerable groups and individuals in its provided services including means to facilitate access to persons with disabilities. The organization will participates in various UN, WHO, other INGO and local administration coordination groups; including the Northeast Syria Health Working Group, and MHPSS Working Group, while also collaborating with the local health committees and NGO Office. M&E of the project will include monitoring health facility services, shipments and conduct quality assessments. Community feedback mechanisms will ensure accountability to affected populations.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risks• Beneficiaries and humanitarians face a real risk to their physical safety and security due to ongoing and unpredictable conflict incidents and targeting by different groups and criminal elements• Working with children, women and girls encompasses risk of potential sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), physical abuse and harassment, gender based violence and trafficking from a range of actors including their own family members, persons in positions of power including local officials, service providers and NGO's own staff and staff of other NGOs• Frontline service providers risk developing post-traumatic stress for dealing with violence in their daily lives and supporting complex cases Mitigation Measures• The NGO has developed an extensive security management structure, encompassing a security manager, as well as security officers, assistants, analysts and guards who establish valuable community based contacts enabling acceptance and a steady flow of information. The organization undertakes full Security Risk Assessments (SRAs) of all activity locations identify threats, estimate impact, and assess risks• The NGO maintains a ZERO TOLERANCE policy towards Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and follows internationally established protocols and standards for child safeguarding. Staff will receive SGBV training and will know how and when to report suspected cases of exploitation. For community activities MHPSS team will employ an equal or similar number of male and female staff, so that beneficiaries will have a choice of the gender of case manager-community mobilizer that they feel more comfortable receiving services from. All facilities and community centres have clearly visible signs with guidance on how to register complaints and feedback including reporting SEA concerns anonymously• The NGO will provide staff wellbeing activities to its field staff, in order to help them cope with stress at the workplace and in their daily lives

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• The NGO continues to have approvals from local authorities to provide services in targeted locations; maintain access to targeted locations to deliver the assistance• The security situation remains stable to implement the programs on a day to day basis. Risks:• Delays in procurement and delivery of large shipments of essential pharmaceuticals and medical consumables, medical and non-medical equipment essential for health facility rehabilitation and operations; leading to delay in start-up of services for beneficiaries or cause of stock outs and shortage of medicines.• Program locations are taken over by Government of Turkey or allied forces or Government of Syria; leading to restrictions in access or implementation of activities without specific approvals.• Program quality assurance due to semi-remote management model• Aid diversion and/or fraud by those who carry out refurbishment of facilities Mitigation Measures:• The physical security of the organizations facilities to be regularly assessed and upgraded when needed. Close security monitoring, security training for staff which would hopefully limit/lessen exposure, contingency and evacuation/withdrawal plans in place, alternative implementation methodologies depending on situation. Duty of Care, families of killed or injured staff, severance pay for sudden closure of activities resulting in termination of staff.• The NGO has multiple sources for procurement and maintains procurement plans and processes to mitigate delays, coordination with other humanitarian actors and agencies continues to reduce impact of unpredictable delays.• Complaint and Feedback Mechanisms to receive complaints and feedback regarding the services provided. This will be managed separately by MEAL team and communicated to senior program staff to address.• Reporting mechanisms available at several levels for staff and beneficiaries in case of suspected diversion or fraud. Independent supervision of rehabilitation.
