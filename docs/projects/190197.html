Project Summary

The conflict between the Ethiopian federal government and Tigray regional forces that began on November 4, 2020, has had a profound impact on the lives of households (HHs) throughout Tigray, many of whom were already vulnerable prior to the conflict. More than 2 million people (432,358 households) are displaced from all corners of the region and these IDPs have been hosted in 308 sites, more than 67 thousand taking refugees in Sudan and more than 5.2 million people needing humanitarian support to meet their basic needs. The number of people in need is expected to increase significantly to more than 6.5 million people.
The conflict has decimated the livelihoods of rural populations in Tigray. As a consequence of the conflict, households in all the six zones of Tigray have lost both stocks and assets, including food stocks, seeds, agricultural tools, and livestock. Many households have lost their ploughing oxen. Agriculture sector has been affected in the past three cycles: i) production of 2020 meher season was not fully harvested when the war brock-out; ii) the 2021 belg season was also insufficient in three zones (Eastern, South Eastern, Southern), affecting local food production, and iii) the 2021 meher season was major opportunity for most farmers in relatively safe and secured areas of Tigray, but crop production performance was not as good as it can be mainly due to limited access to seed and fertilizer, low level of land preparation – due to lack of ox/en for ploughing and security concerns in the peak land preparation season, and shifting from long cycle crops to short cycle crops due to shortening of land preparation period. Therefore, as indicated in the multi-agency meher assessment report, crop production was only about 33 % of the expected level of production (taking pre-war production performance and performance during reference years) This production may be cover for three months food consumption for people living in rural areas.
Livestock are central to the livelihoods of many households in rural Tigray. Since the start of the conflict, 15% of Tigray’s 17 million life-sustaining livestock were reported looted or slaughtered. Displaced families have had to leave their livestock behind. Households who still own livestock have insufficient access to feed and veterinary services because of limited input provision and the absence of government veterinary services. Keeping these animals alive and productive is critical to households’ livelihoods and nutrition. Access to livestock feed is a major challenge as the main sources of feed/pasture were in the Western and Southern Zones. There is also limited capacity to manage livestock disease outbreaks. 80% of veterinary clinics were destroyed or looted. Animal health services, especially vaccines, are urgently needed to contain current disease outbreaks. In addition to this, off-farm economic opportunity has been severely hampered with wage employment becoming non-existent. The non-functionality of markets, banking services, public transport, extension support is also affecting livelihood situation of war-affected people in Tigray.
Therefore, this proposed action will support 40,000 households in 8 Woredas of Tigray by increasing access to key production input: seed, farm tools, restocking, and ‘green’ IGA start-up support. This will increase food production at household level and availability of diverse food in local market, and increase household income – to increase access to diverse food. The project will be implemented in an environmentally sound manner: selecting varieties/breeds that are locally acceptable and agro-ecologically compatible and supported by technical training and guidance on proper use of these inputs.
Project Woredas:
1) Eastern Zone (three Woredas): Kilte Awlaleo, Gulo Mekeda, and Tserea Womberta, and
2) South Eastern Zone (five Woredas): Samre, Seharti, Hintallo, Wajerat, and Degua Tembien

1. - Projects activities

Activity 1. Provide support to farmers to rebuild/take up sustainable, climate smart agricultural LHs. The Action will provide cereal and pulse seeds and fruit seedlings to conflict-affected, vulnerable HHs with access to land. 8,000 HHs will receive about 2,000 quintal of different cereals and legume, allowing them to plant a minimum of 0.25 hectare of land. Seed types and varieties and their relative share in the HH seed package will be decided after the rapid assessment of the specific agro-climatic conditions of each intervention woreda. Seeds will be distributed through in-kind voucher redeemable with local cooperatives and/or suppliers, if they are active, functional and required seeds are locally available. 5,000 farmers whose farm tools were lost or destroyed during the conflict, will receive replacements (in kind distribution). Priority will be given to HHs directly impacted by the conflict, those who did not have land for crop production in the past, and FHHs. Activity 2. Provide support to livestock holders to rebuild/take up sustainable LH activities. The project will distribute livestock, e.g. poultry, beehives/bee colonies, and small ruminants as well as corresponding equipment (sheds, houses for chicken, bee keeping equipment, etc.). The project will use an in-kind voucher system to distribute 45-day old chickens with start-up feed to about 1,000 HHs (25 chicken per HH); recipients will receive training on chicken management and guidance for the construction of appropriate poultry house from locally available materials. Chickens will be primarily distributed to FHHs, persons with disabilities, unemployed youth and others without access to agricultural lands. The project will also provide about 160 unemployed youth with trainings and up-to three modern beehives per person and offer complementary training and technical support. Beehives and bee colonies will be distributed through in-kind vouchers. The project will work with government structures and private providers to facilitate access to animal health service and drugs (Ox tetracycline, Albendazol, and Ivermection) for about 30,000 cattle and 50,000 shoats (two rounds). Animal drugs will be distributed through in-kind vouchers. Severely affected HHs and HHs who cannot afford essential inputs and services are prioritized.Activity 3. Provide support to women and youth to rebuild/take-up sustainable off-farm “green” LHs. For 2,000 conflict-affected individuals (mainly FHH, youth) without access to land or experience/interest in livestock rearing, the project will provide support to rebuild/initiate off-farm LHs. Such as production and/or sales of fuel-efficient stoves, bio-gas, solar businesses as well as agro-dealerships. Women and youth will be provided with business skill training and cash grants that will allow them to purchase the necessary inputs and assets to start their IGA, as detailed in the business plan they develop. Beneficiaries will be organized in VESAs to facilitate cross-learning and VESAs also function as a platform for members to convene and discuss issues that are relevant for them within the community as well as to receive information and awareness raising messages through the project. VESA will receive necessary equipment (saving boxes, notebooks etc.) and follow-up support. Members of VESAs will be encouraged to apply for the off-farm LH support scheme and vice-versa. Activity 1.4. Support local cooperatives/associations involved in the supply chain to reinitiate their activities. To facilitate availability of essential inputs and assets for on-farm LHs, both for the project and for the community at large, the Action will support 16 cooperatives or associations impacted by the conflict and currently not (fully) functioning with priority given to those involved in relevant supply chains, e.g. for seeds, chicken, combs/beehives and the project support could include rehabilitation of office spaces or replacement of equipment.
