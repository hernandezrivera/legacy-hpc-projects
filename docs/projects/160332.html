Project Summary

This proposed project will address short- to-midterm WASH concerns of conflict, emergency and disease prone affected populations by providing integrated WASH services to vulnerable populations. This will improve water supply, sanitation and hygiene behavior for the affected populations and host families and reduce waterborne diseases. The project therefore intends to contribute to the reduction of avoidable mortality and morbidity through the provision of lifesaving integrated WASH services to vulnerable populations, especially women, girls, men and boys through a flexible, responsive and synergistic approach.
This project will be targeting 40,000 individuals (Male: 4,800, Female: 9,600, Boys: 11,200 and Girls: 14,400) in Tonj East, Tonj North, Yirol East and Awerial
Prior to project implementation, CCOSS will conduct, focus group discussions (FGD) with women, men, boys and girls at targeted community settlements will be conducted to get feedback on design, implementation, and monitoring of sanitation facilities, ensuring safety and mitigation of Protection / GBV risks, especially for females. These FGDs will also be utilized to trigger community action towards ending open defecation.
The project will be achieved through the following interventions
• Construction of two stance block latrines at key health facilities, market places, and crowded settlement areas.
• Rehabilitation of non-functioning boreholes
• Clean- up campaigns to improve environmental sanitation at schools, health facilities, market centres and settlement areas since these are key areas where people gather and transmission of infectious diseases such as cholera is at a heightened risk.
• Conduct hygiene awareness campaigns each month of implementation to improve hygiene practices and health-seeking behaviour for WASH-related infectious diseases. The awareness shall also provide knowledge on appropriate behaviours to prevent and mitigate WASH related diseases and encourage good hygiene practices.
• Train community hygiene promoters (60% of them women) on hygiene awareness and promotion. These hygiene promoters will be identified and recruited through participatory approach with the community members playing a central role.
• Assess and identify at least 2000 most vulnerable households in Tonj East, Tonj North, Yirol East and Awerial for distribution of WASH non-food items (NFIs) such as jerry cans, buckets, PUR tabs and soap. A needs assessment will be conducted at the beginning of the project implementation phase

1 - Needs

Tonj North and Tonj East counties broadly faces myriad of WASH related challenges emanating from accessibility challenges by partners, influx of IDPs as a result of forced displacement due to cattle raids from neighbouring Cuiebet that spills over the Warrap State boarder into Manyangok Payam. Majority of residents drink water from unprotected sources, almost no existence of pit latrines thus open defecation, while Cholera and AWD are a main health challenges. Open defecation is a common practice of disposal of human excreta and the community knowledge on good hygiene practice is very low. This situation has exposed the populations to high level risk of vulnerability to water related illnesses. During rainy season, most counties in Warrap are affected by heavy and flash floods. In September 2019, heavy down pour and over flow of River Lol and Kuom caused flooding in low-lying areas of Gogrial West, Gogrial East, Twic and Tonj North, and causing massive displacements of more than 325,000 people. There were many crowded people in particular locations leading to overstretched use of existing social services. Cases of diarrhea and other infectious diseases were reported due to lack of clean drinking water. There were reported cases of high admission malaria especially in health facilities due to lack of enough mosquito nets. Because of lack of clean drinking water, many people suffered from dehydration. Stagnant water, severe overcrowding, inadequate drainage and a dire shortage of functional latrines, all create ideal conditions for the spread of diarrheal diseases including cholera and skin infections. Spikes in inter and intra-communal violence (ICV) alongside economic and climatic shocks in Lakes State have reportedly driven large-scale displacement and restricted access to food for host communities (HCs), returnees and internally displaced people (IDPs). Humanitarian needs have persisted throughout Greater Bahr el Ghazal (GBeG) in 2019. Protection, shelter, water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH), education, and health concerns, remained constant, while there have been increasing reports of poor food access in assessed settlements leading to negative coping mechanisms, some of which are causing long-term livelihood depletion for affected populations in Yirol East and Awerial. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) has continued to be a challenge with almost one quarter (23%) of affected population reported being unable to access their preferred water source year round. Only 38% of affected population use latrines. The most common reasons for this among assessed settlements without latrines was that there are no latrines (59%). Lack of latrine use and 73% of affected population reporting not using soap to wash their hands likely contributed to assessed settlements reporting health issues resulting in diarrhoea (21%) and typhoid (12%) as their main causes of death. Jur River appears to have the most severe water and sanitation issues with only 10% of assessed settlements reporting latrine use and only 27% reporting functional boreholes.

2 - Activities

a) Provide timely, equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations b) Increase knowledge of appropriate hygiene practices among vulnerable and affected populations, to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. c) Enable vulnerable and affected populations to practice safe, sanitary and Hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate sanitation services d) Provide a timely WASH minimum package of interventions on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community e) Set-up of WASH services, training of health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within hand washing with soap at critical times at their household level

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

The rights of the beneficiaries will be legally recognized through various means; ensuring there is a referral pathway established for GBV survivors, creating awareness to traditional authorities about the rights of beneficiaries, incorporating GBV messaging during the response. In 2010, the General Assembly and Human Rights Council recognized “the right to safe and clean drinking water and sanitation as a human right that is essential for the full enjoyment of life and all human rights”. The project will provide safe and clean drinking water and sanitation for the displaced and affected population.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

The project targets displaced persons and returnees, and thus will have access to and benefit fully from the project. They are the central theme around the project design and implementation, and thus are the main project beneficiaries.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The government through the Ministry of Electricity, Dams, Irrigation and Water Resource (MEDIWR) in its long-term vision commits itself to "to achieve universal access to water and sanitation, through an oriented action towards the most in need, hence, MEDIWR will prioritize the delivery of basic services to the poorest rural districts with lowest access to WASH services, as well as to the poor urban dwellers, especially the ones in informal settlements". Though very little has been done towards achieving this broad long-term vision, this project is aligned to the government plans of ensuring there is sanitation and water for all.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

The project works with the returnees and displaced persons, thus is aligned to the government Framework for Return, Reintegration and Relocation of Displaced Persons: Achieving Durable Solutions in South Sudan (2017). The Framework's main goal is to "Creating conditions enabling safe, voluntary and durable solutions to internal displacement in South Sudan. This framework will guide the Government’s response to current and any future situations of IDPs, specifically during armed conflict or natural disaster". This project works with internally displaced persons and returnees all aimed at contributing to the reduction of avoidable mortality and morbidity through the provision of lifesaving integrated WASH services to vulnerable populations.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Though there have been incidences of inter-communal violence arising from cattle raids and revenge killings, there are humanitarian agencies operating in these locations, especially the National NGOs. CCOSS as a National NGO has also been operating in these locations and has established a good community goodwill owed to the fact that, as a humanitarian agency, it doesn't take sides with either sides of the warring communities. The interventions are designed in a way that protection issues will be key to project success. As such, safety audits and gender analysis will be conducted prior to any activity implementation.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

CCOSS will put in place measures geared towards sustainability of this project right from its design throughout the implementation. Prior to project initiation, CCOSS will conduct meetings in the community with beneficiaries to explain to them about the project period during community engagement sessions, to adequately prepare the affected population about the project duration. Towards the end of the project, CCOSS, through community engagement activities, will emphasize the following to the community:• Explain to the community what knowledge the Hygiene Promoters have, the roles they played throughout project implementation and how the community can continue to benefit from their knowledge after the end of the project. • Explain to the community what knowledge on shelter design and establishment volunteers have and how the community members can continue benefiting from their experience after the end of the project.• Explain to the community what knowledge enumerators have and the role they played, describing how the community members will benefit from their engagement after the project with CCOSS ends.• CCOSS will urge affected populations to continue practicing the proper hygiene according to what they have learned from CCOSS’ hygiene promotion, including hand washing (using ash or tippy-tap), purifying water using PUR or collecting from the repaired boreholes, solid waste management, and burying of human feces, among other topics.• CCOSS will hand over the hygiene promoters to the local authorities (RRC) who can continue utilizing their skills in the community when need arises• CCOSS will also hand over the project to other partners in the locations, especially the Hygiene Promoters, Pump Mechanics and Water Management Committees), including sharing lessons learned The design of this project is also aimed at being sustainable after the phase-out stage. The rehabilitation of water infrastructure will be self-sustaining due to rehabilitation of boreholes.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Complaint /feedback mechanism will be agreed upon and established prior to commencement of project. Monitoring of the complaints and remedial action will be documented during the project implementation cycle. This will be done with the goal of promoting community engagement and accountability. The community participation in the planning, implementation and monitoring of the post distribution will also be solicited. For instance, the community will be involved in the identification and selection of community volunteers to help in distribution among others. After the completion of distribution, a monitoring team will be sent to visit selected samples of beneficiaries to confirm the actual quantity received and the quality of the relief commodities, as well as to collect further feedback from beneficiaries. Once complaints are received, CCOSS will look for the most appropriate way to respond to the complaints Complaint and Feedback desks will be set in strategic places in the community. Some of these desks will be manned by some community members who will assist other community members about the issues they want to raise. Illiterate community members will be assisted with those who are able to read and right by documenting their complaints and feedback. Households with members with disabilities will be visited and their complaints and feedback received.
