Project Summary

In 2021, Relief International (RI) will continue to support and improve the food security of the most vulnerable households in Somalia’s Hiraan, Banadir Gedo, and Middle Shabelle regions who are currently classified at Crisis levels (IPC 3) or worse. RI will continue its ongoing intervention aimed at meeting immediate food needs, while ensuring a recovery of household’s productive capacity to ensure their progressive recovery and medium-term self-reliance. RI will support agro-pastoralists with drought and disease-resistant seeds, appropriate tools, trainings on good agricultural practices (GAP) and restocking of livestock to boost livelihoods, land preparation-ploughing activities, as well as livelihoods skills training and business cash support.
Additionally, RI will provide cash transfers to the most vulnerable households to ensure their access to sufficient and nutritious food based on the recommendations of Somalia’s Cash Working Group. RI will also consider cash for work activities to provide income during the lean periods in 2021. RI’s activities will be closely aligned with the strategic objectives of the Food Security Cluster.
The proposed activities will complement and build on RI’s 2020 intervention aimed at strengthening households' productive capacity and increase their adaptive abilities and supply chain connection. In 2020 and 2019, RI established farmer cooperatives, installed solar irrigation systems along the Shabelle River, provided direct agricultural and livestock inputs such as seeds and productive goats, and trained cooperatives on mitigation measures against drought and pests.
With the proposed intervention, RI aims to contribute to the mitigation of the impacts caused by multiple shocks Somalia is currently facing: 1) The socio-economic impacts resulting from the COVID-19 pandemic combined with 2) an erratic Gu rainfall season, 3) displacement due to flooding and conflict, and 4) the desert locust infestation. These shocks have increased the number of people facing food insecurity and exacerbated already high food insecurity levels across Somalia. RI’s target locations are among the areas most affected by displacement. According to OCHA, the 2020 Gu floods displaced 412,000 people, nearly 30 percent of whom were displaced from Beletweyne town in Hiiraan, one of RI’s target areas. Floods damaged irrigation infrastructure in riverine areas and inundated an estimated 54,000 hectares (ha) of farmland in Hiiraan, Middle Shabelle, Middle Juba, Lower Juba, and Gedo, which is equivalent to roughly 21% of the 1995-2019 average area planted (FEWSNET Outlook June 2020-January 2021). Further, it is estimated that approximately 30,000 ha of planted crops were destroyed.
According to FSNAU, households (especially IDP and urban) are estimated to currently face a 20-30% decline in income due to social distancing measures, rising staple food prices, the decline in external remittances, and other negative COVID-19-related impacts that suppress business activity. All these factors combined with climatic/environmental shocks and conflict-related displacement are dramatically worsening food security levels.
RI’s food security intervention in 2021 will assist affected households who are struggling to meet their daily food needs which in turn is impacting their health and nutrition outcomes and posing an increased risk of malnutrition. As of September 2020, 1.3 million people are in IPC 3 and 4 and have food consumption gaps even in the presence of humanitarian assistance. This number is expected to increase to 2.1 million by the end of 2020 (Somalia Stakeholder briefing 30 September 2020). Therefore, RI’s intervention will be critical in the target areas in 2021 with a forecast of below-average Deyr rainfall from October to December 2020 which is expected to decrease the availability of food, increase prices of staple food, and further worsen the food security outlook through at least early 2021.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Somalia’s market system is well integrated which indicates the appropriateness of cash and voucher assistance. Looking at the prospective of Somalia 2020 Post Gu Food Security and Nutrition Outcomes and Projections, the availability and price of staple food is likely to stabilize mainly due to the decrease of fuel prices as well as the availability of import products, which will compensate for the expected slight decrease of the locally produced cereals. Somalia has a strong functioning FSP ecosystem and RI has experience working with several different cash and voucher delivery mechanisms. Most recently, RI worked with mobile money providers which demonstrated a high level of adaptability to COVID-19 constraints. RI’s recent experience in May/June 2020 includes an emergency flood intervention in which RI provided multi-purpose cash assistance to 1000 flood-affected households in Hiiraan (benefitting 6,000 people). The amount used was aligned with the Somalia Cash Working Group Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB). The post-distribution monitoring (in which RI surveyed 300 of the 1000 households) showed that 90% of the recipients interviewed used the cash received either partly of fully to purchase food. 94% of the respondents expressed satisfaction with the cash assistance and the only request made by the affected households was for continued monetary assistance due to economic hardship currently experienced.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

RI ensures that market functionality in target areas is continuously monitored, particularly in light of recent COVID-19 restrictions. RI’s previous experience in the target locations suggests that markets are generally functioning and that cash and voucher assistance is appropriate, however, RI will continue to follow and regularly consult the national and sub-national cash working group analyses and recommendations, consult the affected community and will conduct its own context-specific market analysis prior to conducting any cash assistance.

Mitigation Measures

1. To mitigate the risk of violence, RI will ensure safety during distributions and activities by 1) Conducting project activities away from checkpoints/military presence/targeted buildings; 2) Avoid sites that require beneficiaries to endure long travel times, take diﬃcult routes through check points or areas of town/a district that highlights their presence (i.e. IDP in Host area with animosity, women travelling through checkpoints stationed with only males); 3) Ensure distributions occur to both host and IDP communities, avoiding disturbance/competition/tension between different settlement groups; 4) consult the affected population in the selection of the locations. Further, cash and voucher assistance will be provided in a safe manner ensuring recipients are not exposed to any risks. 2. RI's proposed intervention is actively seeking to provide emergency assistance to people in most need - including women and children of IDP settlements. RI will mitigate this risk by ensuring that vulnerable and marginalized groups will be 1) assisted and consulted through needs assessments; 2) included in the project also in conjunction with relevant authorities, and 3) represented by capturing their diverse needs (age, genders). 3. To mitigate this risk, RI will 1) select safe distribution points for all in consultation with the community; 2) conduct distributions in daylight hours and at convenient times for the most marginalised; 3) ensure that program participants do not have to travel long hours to access services; 4) ensure marginalised groups are engaged and the local community including government and other stakeholders are made aware of the intervention.
