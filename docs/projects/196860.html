Project Summary

The organization seeks to reduce, alleviate and mitigate the accidents related to the presence of explosive ordnance (EOs) in contaminated areas and increase the protection of at-risk persons through clearance and Explosive Ordnance Risk Education (EORE). The Clearance component includes: Non-Technical Survey Teams (NTS) collect data in order to identify and mark potential EO -Clearance teams (TS) render safe and dispose of the EO contamination through Application of the Land Release Process- Marking & Demarcation- Demolition work. Prioritization of tasking should be done from 2023 in coordination with NES MAO. Currently, the organization implements clearance activities in the Ar-Raqqa governate and since October 2022 expanded to Deir Ezzor. The EORE component includes three key activities as follows: 1. EORE Sessions’ aim that the at-risks persons, living in contaminated areas, have the necessary information to be able to adopt safe behaviors and reduce the threats posed by the presence of explosive hazards. Based on context, access and needs analysis, this is conducted either through direct EORE sessions at households’ level, community group sessions, public information activities (indirect EORE activities) or through Training of Trainers to strengthen the skills of key local actors and ensure the proper dissemination of the messages through a sustainable approach.2. Contamination Impact Surveys (CIS) which aim to collect information to identify the level and type of EO contamination, the areas identified as hazardous and the need in the community. 3. Accident and Victim surveys which aim to identify the areas in which the accident occurred, and the type of explosive device that is suspected to be the cause of the accident. During the provision of EORE activities, people who need complementary services are internally or externally referred. In addition, safety briefings are provided to humanitarian actors (local and international NGOs) and for public and private sectors to raise their awareness about risks and the consequences of explosive hazards and to minimize the risk of accidents among staff and aid workers. Finally, the organization's advocacy team continues to lobby for the ban of the use of Explosive Weapons in Populated Areas and produce evidence-based advocacy documents to mobilize and influence local, national, regional and international stakeholders by raising key humanitarian and protection issues for populations and humanitarian actors in Syria. Advocacy efforts during the MA donor meetings and within the SIRF Policy and Advocacy WG will be oriented towards more access and permits for mine action actors for risk education, recognizing that humanitarian mine action is a prerequisite to any immediate or long-term recovery, and ensure that humanitarian mine action activities are more strongly integrated into other sectors in Syria. Moreover, monitoring of quality of all project activities will be ensured by INGO`s Qualitative Monitoring Framework (QMF). One QMF has been devised for each thematic area to assess the delivery of quality of services. This framework will utilize a mixed method approach to assess various quality aspects. Each phase will have a set of indicators that assesses the quality of interventions; however, if indicators are phased and graded, it will establish a detailing approach on assessment of quality.

3 - Needs

One in three communities is reportedly affected by some form of EO. Access to agricultural fields and roads continues to be compromised by contamination, followed by access to private properties, local markets, schools and other public buildings. Farming/herding is the most common reported activity at the time of incidents, followed by travelling/moving and playing. The location and nature of activities related to incident explains why men and boy are more exposed to explosive threats due to gender roles. Based on 2022 victim reports (849) and accidents reports (685),85% of victims are males. Children 31% of victims while adults were 64%. Based on accidents reports, 55% of victims killed and 45% injured. 68% of survivors remained with a permanent disability. Landmines represented the highest cause of accidents (35%). EO threat continues to be major protection concern, as community-based assessment shows that the presence of explosive ordnance is a safety concern for 23% of IDP households (2022 MSNA). Contamination by EO poses threats to safety of civilians, exacerbates vulnerabilities and hampers safe access to humanitarian support, and prevent safe returns. Low level of knowledge on risk also confirmed: 76% reported to have not participated to EORE awareness in the previous 3 months. Victims face barriers to access assistance and services, knowing most needed services are related to economic support, medical care, prosthetics services and provision of assistive products.

4 - Implementation of activities

The intervention will count on teams, each team in NES consists of 2 EORE/CL/NTS agents (6 teams in RAQ and 2 in DEZ), in NWS, each team consists of 4 EORE agents (2 teams in Aleppo and 4 in Idleb). The EORE team’s intervention starts at community level by gathering relevant information on EOs related accidents, victims, and presence of contamination trough Contamination Impact Survey. They will then conduct EORE direct awareness sessions, either through direct individual or group sessions, public information activities or training of community volunteers. The EORE field teams will be able to identify record and refer EO victims to appropriate services. All activities are inclusive to Disability, Gender and Age (DGA). Persons with disabilities will participate through conducting activities, or identification of activities linked to barriers accessing information. All IEC materials will be easy-to-understand and culturally appropriate. HI also conduct analysis on its internal and secondary data, share relevant data with actors, and produce evidence-based advocacy factsheets highlighting protection concerns on use of explosive weapons. In NES 10 additional teams will be deployed in Hassakeh, Raqqa, and Deir ez Zor governorates as part of an HMA consortium to specifically conduct Non-Technical Surveys and analyze data on suspected and confirmed hazardous areas and EOs to support prioritization for clearance.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

INGO is keeping low visibility to avoid risk to partners, staff and beneficiaries, the INGO does not conduct MA activities in GoS areas. • Clearance representing a highly at-risk activity for the staff, close monitoring on SOPs compliance trough QAQC is determinant. •Conflict sensitivity approach, the INGO will conduct prioritization assessment of land/property or areas to be cleared based on local priority, land/property ownership, contamination evidence, and density. •Facilities safe closure and protecting data, field staff trained on safe withdrawal/evacuation including ways to protect and/or destroy data. •Applying PSEAH policy for staff, partners and suppliers. •Presence of explosive hazards limit access and posing a threat to beneficiaries, mitigated by delivery of RE training to humanitarian personnel to increase awareness of the explosive threat and including first aid component in RE sessions and TOT level. •Creation of beneficiaries' wrong expectations, who could wrongly attribute to risk education teams' ability of removing EO, this is mitigated by clear explanation of intervention in NWS and by implementing the HMA comprehensive approach in NES. •Not reaching all vulnerable groups: mitigated by involvement of community focal points and inclusion team in planning, adaptation and delivery of inclusive RE sessions •Lack of information on accessibility to services facilities mitigated by collection of information from health sector and stakeholders.

INGO Partners are involved in actions of abuse or harassment against beneficiaries: Any proven allegations of such practices shall result in follow-up of the case and termination of agreement unless the partner commits to and effectively ensures a radical change in behavior.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In NES and NWS: •Loss of access and border closure mitigated as the INGO developed an access strategy that is regularly reviewed and adapted. •Lack of acceptance mitigated through positive relations with the community. The INGO and its partners make sure to keep close coordination with the local authorities and maintain positive relations with the community. The INGO will also focus on increasing the community’s participation. •Loss of funding is mitigated by close communication with various donors, and direct negotiation for continuous funding. •The available budget reduction due to exchange rate loss, managed by continuous use of USD value for money transfer as well as, close monitoring the local market flow. •Spread-out of COVD-19 or Cholera. To mitigate this risk: i)use alternatives remote modalities ii) ensuring PPE and hygiene items are available; iii) mainstreaming Cholera/COVID-19 messaging in activities; iv) monitoring the epidemic situation. •Inability or delay to get HMA supply: anticipate needs, coordination inter-HMA NGO and with Coalition forces. Enhancing networking in KRI and NES for supply. •Border closure impact the HMA operation as, in the absence of medical evacuation, capacity clearance operation cannot be carried on. •Authority interferes in HMA activities: No clear agreement on the insurance/taxation policy and request of suspension of activities. Advocacy effort supported by NES Forum, HMA INGO, donors and international actors.
