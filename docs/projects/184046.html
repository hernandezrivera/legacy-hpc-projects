1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian crisis in Sudan continues to impact the already fragile health system and reducing the capacity to provide basic health services and respond to the multiple emergencies affecting the country. The annual cycles of floods, disease outbreaks, civil unrest, border conflicts, and ongoing economic crisis have further impacted the accessibility to health services. The decline in service provision by the public sector is forcing the population to seek health services at the private sector that is dominating the scene with 69.3 per cent of current health expenditure. According to the 2021 multi-sectorial needs assessment MSNA 72.3 per cent of the population had to pay in full for their health services despite a reported coverage by the national health insurance fund of 67 per cent. During 2021 the availability of emergency medicines declined steadily to reach 43 per cent compared to 57 per cent during 2020. http://www.nmsf.gov.sd/wafra .Since the first COVID-19 first case was reported in Sudan in March 2020, until the first week of September, a total of 37,873 laboratory confirmed cases were reported across the country (Sudan outbreaks dashboard). Despite the steady decline in reported cases throughout the year 2021, the case fatality ration stayed at 7.5 per cent recording one of the highest globally. the COVID-19 vaccination coverage is standing at around 2 per cent of the population with challenges facing the national vaccine deployment plan that aims to reach 20 per cent coverage by the end of 2021. Measles vaccination coverage declined by end of 2020 to reach 67 per cent with 29 localities across the country reporting coverages of less than 50% mainly in South Darfur and South Kordofan. By end of August 2021, 4 states have reported measles outbreaks: River Nile, White Nile, South Darfur and East Darfur. The total reported suspected cases reached 1,252 cases including 21 deaths and a case fatality ratio CFR of 1.7 per cent. The availability of qualified health cadre and healthcare workers is still a challenge in Sudan According to figures by the federal and state ministries of health, there are only 5,457 doctors, 12,601 nurses, and 17,343 midwives working in the public health system (0.76 doctors + nurses+ midwives per 1,000 population, against the 4.45 per one thousand population WHO target as recommended in the WHO Health workforce requirements for universal health coverage). The lowest ratios of medical professionals per 1,000 people, were reported in White Nile, West Kordofan, East Darfur, Northern, and Central Darfur. Even though over 70% of the population resides in rural areas, 70% of health workers work in the urban areas with 38% in the capital, Khartoum. Moreover, 67% of the staff works in secondary and tertiary care creating a significant gap in primary health care provision (the 2019 FMOH annual statistical report).In addition, 1,156 cases of hepatitis E were reported across the country mainly in refugee camps and hosting communities. Despite the effort the facility-based disease surveillance system only covers around 30 per cent of health facilities with significant disproportionality between urban and rural coverage coupled with low reporting rates especially at PHC facilities (reporting rate is 72.5% from hospitals and 60% from PHC facilities).During 2022, the health needs are expected to remain high due to projected increase in inflation and economic crisis, poor investment in infrastructure, inter-communal conflicts, and potential new refugee influx.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Provide comprehensive primary health care (PHC) and reproductive health (RH) services through static health facilities and mobile medical units (MMU).The Comprehensive PHC services include, Mother, Neonatal and Child Health (MNCH) services; through provision of Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI) that address the common childhood diseases, and provision of Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC), which address maternal and neonatal health issues, through provision of Antenatal Care (ANC), Postnatal Care (PNC), Family planning (FP), Deliveries and prevention and syndromic treatment of sexually transmitted infections (STIs)). Management and prevention of communicable diseases, management of non-communicable diseases, including trauma care. Health education and behavioral change communications at health facility level. Referral of gender-based violence (GBV) survivors will occur, with their informed consent through focal points who are trained in Clinical care of Sexual Assault Survivor (CCSAS) and are aware of confidentiality as well as health and protection needs of survivors. A roving gynecologist will coach and mentor healthcare providers on CCSAS. Care will provide essential medicines, medical supplies, consumables and equipment for management and prevention of communicable diseases at the supported health facilities and mobile teams. Care will provide training to health workers and CHWs on various health topics related to prevention and management of communicable diseases including but not limited to cholera and diarrheal diseases. Care will support surveillance system of communicable diseases through strengthening the Early Warning, Alert Reporting System (EWARS) at the supported health facilities, MMUs and communities. Care will also focus on preventive measures to decrease incidence and morbidity associated with non-communicable diseases by providing support for non-communicable disease treatment through the provision of basic medicines for hypertension and diabetes and medicines/consumables for injuries and trauma. Care will contribute to the promotion of behavior to prevent communicable diseases which include pneumonia, diarrhea, ARI, measles, STDs, TB, leishmaniasis, Dengue, Rift Valley and Chikungunya. Care will also ensure screening for COVID-19 at all supported facilities and MMUs, timely referral of suspect cases through existing COVID-19 Rapid Response Teams (RRTs). The support includes, provision of medical supplies, essential medicines, consumables, equipment, furniture, stationery, cleaning materials as well as provision of basic and refresher trainings to midwives and health workers on basic emergency obstetric and neonatal care (BEmONC), and minimum initial services package (MISP) of reproductive health. Care’s support will enable these health facilities and mobile teams to provide antenatal care (ANC), post-natal care, family planning including short term and LARCs (long-acting reversible contraceptives), and management of sexually transmitted infections through syndromic approach. Care PHC services are adapted to COVID-19 response through enhancing Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) measures at all proposed static facilities and MMUs. These measures will include rehabilitation of toilets and washrooms at static facilities, addressing sanitation needs at mobile facility operation points, and establishment or rehabilitation and operation of Health Waste Zones where needed.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE will take extra caution in analyzing the protection of at risk groups, including children and girls. Efforts will include: no creation of new risks, address discrimination, analysis of the most vulnerable, safety and dignity in service delivery, orientation and familiarization on humanitarian principles and rights, and promoting empowerment. Furthermore, CARE and partners will exercise principles of protection mainstreaming: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of the communities about the best way to deliver the services and considerations for vulnerable groups, accountability mechanisms and Field visits. CARE and partners will empower communities by training & capacity building & awareness raising about key protection concerns and risks, consulting them in decision making process, identifying volunteers and establishing and training community committees. Strengthening community protection mechanisms and improving access to services through information dissemination on available services, good selection criteria and identification of the most vulnerable and referrals will be prioritized. Do no harm analysis will be carried for each sector and the activities will be screened not to enhance risks for all especially women, girls and children. Facilities such as latrines and water points, income generating activities and trainings will be analyzed and discussed with at risk groups to ensure that the beneficiaries are protected and that the services are easily accessible and safely usable by all. Protection risks faced by women and girls, and in particular S/GBV risks, will be the specific focus of capacity building for staff and partners to ensure that they are able to detect cases of abuse. Staff will also be oriented in order to make sure that programming does no harm and that they are able to respond safely and appropriately to protection cases when they are reported, using a confidential and survivor centered approach to disclosures of violence. An internal mechanism for reporting complaints and ensuring that required measure are in place will be promoted throughout the implementation period

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARE will be working and coordinating with all interagency partners to ensure accountability maintained for the affected people including SEA and abuse complaints. CARE has Measurement in place to protect beneficiaries and communities from sexual exploitation and abuse as the following: - all CARE staff has signed letter that explicitly prohibit SEA of beneficiaries by staff and staff of affiliated entities (such as contractors, vendors, suppliers, etc.). and CARE has policies and training package that have been disseminated to staff and well understood amongst staff; all CARE staff Has received training and orientation on Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse; CARE has also appointed PSEA focal persons at country and state level, whom are doing, regular in person support and follow with the team members. CIS has reporting mechanism in place for reporting PSEA, at field level, CARE Medical assistants in charge of the clinic are the focal person at the field level, whom work closely with all his/her departments staff to ensure PSEA policy are implemented and any cases related to PSEA are reported timely to CARE focal person at state level.
