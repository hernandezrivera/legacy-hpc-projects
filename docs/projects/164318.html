Project Summary

Breeding milk cows is an important livelihood for a considerable number of the agropastoralist area of Idleb, which is the target area for the proposed project. An estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Protracted conflict, inflation, high food and commodity prices; and poor purchasing power among the population including livestock keepers signifies the dire situation and vulnerability of people. Majority of vulnerable host communities and IDPs, among them men, women, boys, girls, the elderly and the disabled that are highly dependent on animal source foods (meat and milk) for their nutrition are at risk of hunger and malnutrition, while livestock assets are at risk of diseases
Because of the needs and rationale above, Nasaem is requesting funding to conduct the following:
- Provision of technical guidance for breeding and feeding milk cows.
- Provision of fodder and other dietary supplements to male and female cow breeders to
protect their assets.
- Provision of trainings on establishing community-based cooperatives for male and female cow breeders.
Nasaem's team will discuss and agree on selection criteria with communities' actors and communities. Following that, Nasaem will identify and register eligible vulnerable
households who have one or two cows to be supported. Each household out of 1000 eligible households will receive 14 fodder bags, each bag contains 50 kg of concentrated pellet fodder, in addition to that the cow breeders (men and women) will receive 10 kg of minerals supplements and vitamins (premix) with 30 kg of mineral salt block. The total amounts that will be provided from these nutrients adequate for one cow for about 150 days. The fodder and other dietary nutrients will be distributed to cow breeders in two batches; the first batch will be distributed in February, the second in March 2020. The quantity provided of fodder will cover at least 50% of the fodder needs for one cow during the winter season. This quantity has been determined to first ensure coverage of the maximum number of vulnerable households and at the same time avoiding creating dependency.

4 - Needs

According to HNO 2020, an estimated 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity. From the assessed households, Some 70 per cent of the assessed households reported that food accounted for more than 65 per cent of their expenditure. More than 37 per cent of food-insecure households reported having outstanding debt and 57 per cent of food insecure people had adopted negative coping strategies (HNO 2020). Unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods leading to irreversible coping strategies. The HNO 2019 indicates that 200,000 breeders’ households are suffering from the loss of productive assets (livestock) and damaged infrastructure (land, irrigation infrastructure, facilities, etc.) which is affecting both farmers and breeders. Sector assessments indicate that several population groups are particularly affected by food insecurity, including host and resident population particularly headed by vulnerable members are more food insecure. The estimated ratio between urban and rural People in Need in the sector is 55:45. With the winter coming, it is expected that populations will need money to cover heating costs and livestock breeders might not be able to afford feed for their livestock. Likewise, during winter, there are few vegetables grown and people will rely more on milk and dairy products to enrich their diet. Nasaem Team was conducted key informant interviews with livestock specialists and members of the FAO-led Livestock Technical Working Group in Gaziantep. Interviewed specialists have advised that provision of winter fodder for vulnerable cows breeders (most of these are women as it is mostly women who look after cows within the household) for the following reasons: - Cows produce abundantly.

5 - Implementation of activities

Meeting all stakeholders to inform them about the aim, the main activities of the project and selection criteria for beneficiaries.- Nasaem will distribute the concentrated pellet fodder, minerals supplements and vitamins (premix) with mineral salt block to 1000 of the most vulnerable breeders. The indicator for this activity is # of Quantity of animal feed distributed.- Cow breeding trainers will tailor the technical guidance training through conducting focus group discussions with the beneficiaries to better understand their concerns and problems.- Conducting the training in each location. The indicator is # of targeted herders assisted. The indicator is % of participants out of all beneficiaries.- Nasaem will announce for tendering, contracting and procurement of fodder and other dietary nutrients. - Agreeing with beneficiaries on times and locations of distributions ensuring that these are as safe as possible and inspection of fodder quality by Nasaem's technical staff. The indicator: the amounts of fodder and other supplements distributed.- Selecting active cow breeders who show interest in collaborative work and who have the minimum necessary qualification.- Identifying training and agreeing on the training materials in collaboration with FAO and Gaziantep Livestock Working Group. - Conducting the training.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Risk: A breach of ethics. - Mitigation measure: Nasaem will train the staff on the code of conduct, child safeguarding, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies, and ask them to sign them. - Risk: Staff may find sensitive protection cases. - Mitigation measure: Nasaem’s field staff will receive training on the referral of cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to the available service providers in the area based on the updated service map.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption: Cow breeders' interest is maintained. Access is still available. Security situation permits operation. Fodder and dietary nutrients are accessible. Access to the project location is maintained. Security situation permits operation. Cow breeders show sufficient interest. Access is maintained. Security situation permits operation.Risk: There is a potential risk of tensions between IDPs and host communities. Mitigation measure: To avoid creating tensions among host communities and IDPs as not all of them will receive assistance, Nasaem will discuss the project with the local councils and will also explain the project’s goal and vulnerability criteria to all communities in the target areas, besides, to ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs. Nasaem will make efforts in cooperation with the S/NFI cluster to target % of vulnerable host residents especially for those who have greatly affected by the crisis.Risk: Complaints may arise from the beneficiaries. Mitigation: Nasaem will establish a robust feedback mechanism to submit complaints in case there is variance in the quantity or quality of items received. The feedback procedures will be communicated in an appropriate language and in a clear format to allow the beneficiaries to know how to access and use the feedback mechanism. Nasaem will hire male and female to participate equally in all project phases.
