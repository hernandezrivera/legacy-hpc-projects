Project Summary

UNICEF will provide support to ensure equitable quality and gender sensitive maternal, newborn, child and adolescent health and immunization services as well as provision of sexual and reproductive health (SRH) services including counselling and referral for GBV and support to pregnant and lactating women (inclusive of children with HIV) with inclusion of people with disabilities. The project will focus and ensure adherence with the Minimum Package of Essential Health services for primary health care in PHCs and HPs, and the advance newborn care services in the Field Hospital in the Rohingya refugee camps. UNICEF work with other partners both in health sectors and other sectors to ensure integrated health services are provided. The project will target 424,831 direct beneficiaries (284,446 refugees and 140,385 host communities). The project will be implemented in 13 health facilities in the camps (5 PHCs and 8 health posts) and strengthen advance newborn care and child health services in Hope Field hospital in camp 4, and 1 Diarhoea Treatment Centres (DTCs) as well as strengthening government health system and the health facilities in adjacent host communities in Ukhiya and Teknaf (2 union sub centres and 16 community clinics). Following the 2022 Rationalization exercise in the health sector, UNICEF will observe, explore, and consult regarding the possibility of relocation or closing of HPs in the camps with the transition period at least 6 months to provide sufficient time in consulting with the CiC and awareness of the communities, and upgrade the HP at Camp 16 to be a PHC by midyear as per gap analysis. A special focus will continue to support well functional Newborn Stabilization Unit (NSU) with Kangaroo Mother care, across all the Upazila health complexes and 65 bed Special Newborn Care unit (SCANU) at the district hospital with crucial human resources, maintenance of the equipment, capacity building of the health workers, regular review and follow up, including following up the discharged patients. UNICEF will lead the role in newborn care in the camps and working with other agencies/organization to improve newborn and child health. In addition, we will support the MPDSR to reduce maternal and perinatal (stillbirth and newborn) deaths. UNICEF will continue to play her leading role in supporting vaccine logistic and supply and ensure vaccine availability in all service provision sites in all the camps and host communities in Cox's bazar district. UNICEF will continue to provide technical support, supply and transportation of vaccines and logistic and enhance cold chain capacity, advocacy and social mobilization for strengthening immunization services across all the camps. UNICEF will continue to strengthen the Health Management Information System (HMIS/DHIS2) through deployment of technical staff to the office of the Civil Surgeon and build the capacity of service providers on DHIS2 and support the government in rolling out the e-trackers for improve data collection and monitoring on immunization and maternal health services. Review meeting and constructive feedback will be conducted regularly (monthly and quarterly). UNICEF has developed emergency response plan to ensure better preparedness and response to emergency and disasters including strengthening Infection Prevention and control measures in all the supported health facilities and support the scale up of COVID-19 vaccination program in the camps and host communities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya crisis due to influx of Rohingya refugees from Myanmar into Bangladesh shifted to protracted crisis. UNICEF and other partners have been supporting the government of Bangladesh specifically the Cox's Bazar district to provide integrated essential health services in line with the Minimum Package of Essential Health Services. Despite the collaborative efforts being made to ensure equitable access to quality health care services, there are still issues that need more efforts and attention. In the first half of 2022, there has been reported 143 stillbirths, 134 neonatal deaths and 31 maternal deaths among the refugees in the camps. The ANC 4 coverage is very poor at less than 18 per cent, the MR2 coverage was 37 per cent. Some of the challenges include availability of well-trained health workers, poor quality of care, high home delivery and weak referral linkages especially at night which require to be strengthened. Data quality and use of data for actions are other gaps in the response that require more support and there is a need to strengthen community mobilization and engagement to improve on positive health seeking behaviours.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The project will ensure effective collaboration with health and other sectors especially Nutrition, WASH, social and behavioural change, education, child protection and GBV, PSEA and child safeguarding to promote integrated intervention that also address the other determinants of heath and synergy among the partners in the camps and host communities. UNICEF will ensure effective coordination among partners under the leadership of the government and improve on the relationship with the government, refugees and other stakeholders at all level of the health systems both in the camps and host communities. Following the rationalization in the health sector, UNICEF will gradually reduce the operation of the health posts in the coming years and maintain the quality and accessible PHCs providing Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Care (BEmONC) with essential and advance newborn care in the case of challenge in the referral outside the camps. PHCs have integrated health and nutrition services, including the screening of nutrition status and referral of malnutrition cases to integrated nutrition facilities.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

UNICEF adheres to the protection principles and ensures access to health services to all and avoid discrimination, ensure safety and dignity of the beneficiaries, meaningful access, being accountable, and ensuring participation of all groups. The human right of all the beneficiaries is protected and system are put in place to ensure that none of the population is abused, neglected, or exploited while seeking health services through training, orientation of service providers and monitoring. UNICEF work together with partners, within the sector and across sectors to prevent and respond to GBV, recognizing the systemic exposure to GBV, while helping to ensure that support is available for all survivors of sexual violence (including violence against children). UNICEF has zero tolerance for SEA and SH. UNICEF maintains environment that prevent and protect from sexual exploitation and abuse, and provide timely, confidential, and effective investigation, assistance, and support to survivors.

3.2 - Gender

Due to physical condition in the camps, women and girls are still at risk and prone to GBV, sexual harassment, violence, and assault. Rohingya population is also a conservative population. Better access to WASH, nutrition, and other facilities improved the situation but it significantly affected by the pandemic the last couple years. Since the Rohingya refugees' influx in August 2017, some gender and age-based analysis have been conducted and more inclusion of gender as cross-cutting issue and intersect with other dimension e.g. age, disability, social status, education, income, legal status, and religion. Despite some development, several gaps remain e.g., in understanding shifting in gender norms, practices among the Rohingya population, and specific population to which often overlooked e.g., male and boy, people with disabilities (PWD), people with diverse gender identity, and host communities affected by the protracted crisis.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

UNICEF has rigorous recruitment system in place and UNICEF staff and partners have thorough background check in line with child safeguarding policy. All staffs who will be involved in the project will be trained /oriented on the principles of child safeguarding and monitoring system established to monitor compliance. The health facilities have no age restriction and are accessible to all, and the services accommodate different approaches to different needs according to age and urgency. Triage is in place for different priorities groups (elderly, severely ill, PWD, etc.)

3.4 - Diversity

UNICEF allocates and delivers assistance based on needs and without discrimination. All staffs on the project will be trained and oriented to respect and appreciate other people irrespective of their difference in terms of nationality, sexual orientation, ethnicity, race, sex, language, disability, religious belief, gender identity, political or other opinions. A workplace culture embraces diversity and there is zero tolerance for discrimination. The health facilities and services are accessible to all, and some actions have been taken to improve access and service to the people with disabilities. For example, all health facilities supported by UNICEF have ram in the infrastructure with the availability of wheelchair. Actions have been taken to improve the latrine to be accessible (despite the difficult terrain). Community health workers have been orientated to identify and approach the PWD for further support when needed.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

The project will ensure community members are provided adequate information on the type of health services available at the various health facilities. Appropriate feedback mechanism/system already established in all health facilities for community members to provide their feedback about the services and all concerns and complaints will be adequately and timely addressed. In UNICEF supported facilities, addressing feedback from communities is crucial, thus we established complaint and suggestion box, exit interview, and linkage with Information Feedback Centres (IFCs) in the camps. In addition, feedback from communities also taken from communities meeting led by communities/religious leaders e.g., Majhi, Imam etc. All feedbacks collected were processed (registered and reviewed) during quality improvement meeting every month. All complaints are then discussed, and the facilities will prioritize the issues to be addressed and take action.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

UNICEF child-centred approach to reduce disaster and climate risk and building resilience in development and humanitarian setting, both for and with children and young people. DRR and climate change mitigation measures are embedded in the programme and UNICEF ensured that partners trained and incorporated these in programme planning and implementation. UNICEF allocated funds with partners for contingency to which they can use when significant event occurred e.g. cyclone, flood, landslide, fire. This is applied since the beginning of the response to the crisis to strengthen and maintain the resilience. All facilities have waste management in place and the workers received regular training as part of infection prevention and control.
