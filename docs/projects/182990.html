Project Summary

Decades of poor governance, harmful monetary policies, corruption, and political instability have led to the most severe economic and financial crisis in Lebanon's modern history. Poverty rates have soared amongst Lebanese, migrants and members of the refugee community. Imports of goods, including essential goods such as fuel, wheat, and medication are at their lowest levels due to the government’s shortage of USD and the massive spending on subsidies which are set to be stopped at any time. The economic crisis has extended to the health sector in Lebanon leading to decreased access to, and availability of, critical emergency health services, and essential medicines. The situation in Lebanon has also impacted the mental wellbeing of many across the Lebanese territory.
Due to multiple crises, the health institutions in most of the governorates have become unable to provide essential health services to vulnerable people in need. An IRC health feasibility assessment, done in November-December 2020 and still relevant today, identified the imminent need for primary health care and mental health services. This assessment was followed by field assessments and focus discussion groups to design a response. Findings show how the COVID-19 pandemic has impacted routine immunization coverage for children; this was highlighted and confirmed by the Ministry of Public Health (MoPH). Additionally, the continuity of electricity to PHC centers has been impacted, affecting the vaccine cold chain.
In the proposed project, the International Rescue Committee (IRC) will contribute significantly to prevention of preventable morbidity and mortality rates which have been on the rise due to the health care system’s collapse. IRC, in coordination with health partners in Lebanon including the MoPH, Primary Health Care Department, National Mental Health Program, UN agencies and NGOs will focus activities on institutional capacity building enabling the MoPH to provide responsive Primary Health Care and Mental Health services.
Proposed activities will focus on meeting the needs of the vulnerable population groups, specifically Lebanese and migrants, across Lebanon and will include support with essential medicines, cold chain, mental and reproductive health services, extended PHC hours, infection control, health messaging and protection efforts. The project will also deploy outreach volunteers from the targeted communities to help with the awareness raising and referral guidance.
Through this project, IRC will support three health facilities with PPE and improved access serving 14,000 people with extended hours; provide quality, essential medicines and medical supplies to 5,000 people; support three health facilities to ensure cold chain for non-COVID vaccines; reach 45,000 people with integrated and accessible health messaging through outreach volunteers and community health workers; and support 12 outreach workers. Community health workers will be linked to health facilities and will visit the communities where they will supervise the outreach volunteers and ensure proper referrals. IRC will train 60 healthcare workers on GBV safe identification and referral, subsidize the cost of health services at three health facilities, and train six frontline health workers on culturally sensitive approaches to improve health access for migrants.
IRC will select PHCCs that are part of the MoPH PHC network, are not supported by any other INGO, and who serve a vulnerable catchment area with large migrant and Lebanese populations. Contracts with the selected PHCCs and NGO’s will be finalized once the funds are secured. IRC has started looking for international procurement options for the procurement of medicines and the national and regional IRC supply chain staff are working with MOPH to ensure compliance.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

To mitigate the perception of discrimination against a certain group, IRC conducts service mapping to ensure communication with communities can be proactive and informed and prioritizes consultation with communities and other service providers to refer beneficiaries. To mitigate the risks of GBV, abuse and exploitation IRC ensures that partners have strict policies and regulations on child protection, gender, diversity, sexual harassment, bullying, and discrimination. IRC also ensures that all staff are trained on code of conduct and that a confidential complaint mechanism is established. To mitigate the risk of internal conflict, or hostilities IRC ensures staff have access to security information and that all staff are trained on preventive security measures. To mitigate the risk of Covid-19 transmission strict measures are applied for the protection of the staff and clients and requires all staff to be trained on IPC measures and practices.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

IRC’s MEAL team ensures that feedback from beneficiaries is mainstreamed throughout the project cycle. The MEAL team collects and analyses feedback from beneficiaries on a regular basis to inform project adaptations. IRC also utilizes a national feedback and response mechanism (FARM) for adults and children. Feedback communication channels (hotline, WhatsApp, feedback boxes, email, approaching Feedback Officer, help desk and SMS) and materials are used by beneficiaries to voice their suggestions, feedback, and complaints to an independent accountability team. Accountability to affected population will be ensured through regular and active involvement of local communities and target beneficiaries. Additionally, a monitoring framework that includes Third Party Monitoring will be in place to monitor and evaluate the program implementation, quality and effectiveness.
