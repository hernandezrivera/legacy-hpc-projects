Project Summary

Please note that for all activities implemented in partnership with a Lead Partner INGO (INGO), that Lead Partner will input into the HPC separately. This submission only covers our activities, beneficiary targets and budget.
This project seeks to protect and enhance food security (FS) and livelihoods (LLHs) of conflict‑affected highly vulnerable IDPs and host communities in North West and North East Syria. This will be achieved through an integrated programme of assistance including repeated mixed‑modality life‑sustaining food assistance (monthly assistance to the most vulnerable HHs) and emergency lifesaving FS assistance in addition to LLHs support. The action priorities areas ranked highly in severity of FS needs. The complementary package of LLHs support will increase HH resilience through the promotion of entrepreneurship and the development of small businesses along with the improvement of agricultural production and increasing the ability of targeted HHs to meet food needs. See Section 5 - Implementation of Activities, for more details on FS assistance.
FS: 19370 HHs thru the provision of repeat FS assistance either in-kind or cash-based food vouchers w/ modality confirmed thru market capacity and access assessments.Emergency RTER FS assistance will be provided to 1000 HHs with MPCGs to 12400 HHs affected by sudden shocks.
In line with CWG and FSL guidelines, 70% of MPCGs est. for use of FS needs.
With reference to LLHs, funding is sought to support the Olive Value Chain (OVC) to improve the LLHs of 1,500 HHs in Maaret Tamsrin and Idleb sub-districts. A comprehensive program of assistance is planned to benefit 750 small scale olive farmers and create income generation opportunities for an additional 750 acutely vulnerable IDP and host families through Cash for Work (CfW). The project will support self reliance, protect and build productive assets and increase access to inclusive LLHs. Proposed activities are complemented by our existing FS programming in Idleb gov. which provides repeat, in kind food assistance to the most vulnerable HHs, and by our ongoing Emergency Response (ER) interventions which will continue to provide timely life saving assistance to new arrivals. Funding is also sought to promote entrepreneurship and Small Business Development in Idleb and Ar Raqqa governorates. We propose to support entrepreneurship and the growth of small businesses through the provision of small grants and business skills training packages targeting 500 HHs to support LLHs recovery and regeneration.

4 - Needs

According to the Food and Agriculture Sector (FAS) 2021 HNO, an est. 14.2 mil people/68.6% of the Syrian population are in need of some form of FAS assistance. A combination of factors including currency depreciation, rises in food prices, displacement and a lack of income opportunities increased the number of PIN, from an est. 9.8 mil in 2020 to an expected 14.2 mil in 2021. Targeted areas are considered high severity of need and are informed by recent FSL cluster gap analysis. Project design is informed by a RNA completed by us in Apr 2021 in Idleb & Aleppo govs. HH and KI interviews confirmed food to be a priority need while observation and interviews with food vendors confirmed markets were safely accessible and able to support a cash-based response in many areas. Interviews were conducted with 327 HHs, of which 87% IDP. Of these, 87% reported no source of income, rising to 90% of female respondents. All HHs interviewed stated that access to income generation opportunities decreased since the start of the conflict, with 72% of respondents also stating they were less able to meet food needs as a result of COVID-19. Food Consumption Scores (FCS) were ranked as poor or borderline for 83% of the HHs interviewed. This was considerably higher for female respondents for whom FCS was poor or borderline for 87%. 71% of respondents referenced not having sufficient food to meet needs in the past 7 days, a figure which again rose to 82% of female respondents. Buying food on credit from local markets was reported as the most common source of food, again indicating market functionality and safe access. However, 78% of HHs reporting being in debt, rising to 85% of female respondents.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Established FSL SOPs are adhered to by all staff and designed to reduce the risk of protection concerns. Our CRM, DM & PDM will monitor for potential protection risks, including SEA and GBV. Any protection cases identified will be referred to specialized organizations offering case management services in the project area. SOPs include measures to ensure dignified delivery of assistance to men, women, the elderly & people living with disabilities. These include separate distributions lines for male and female BNFs, the presence of female staff at all distributions, the option for BNFs with access issues to nominate a relative to present at the distribution in their place (with a copy of the ID of the named BNF head of HH and a letter of delegation) and finally home distributions to BNFs who would struggle to attend a distribution for either cultural or physical access issues. These measures are designed to ensure access issues or gender considerations do not present barriers to the delivery of humanitarian assistance. The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex & age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. BNFs will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cycle, with feedback collected through DM, PDM & the CRM used to systematically inform delivery of the action. All our staff and project stakeholders sign our code of conduct (CoC), which includes the obligation to report abuse by staff or BNFs, including SEA and misuse of assets or funds.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

We have strict protocols re to PSEA and Child Protection (CP) specifically with refresher trainings completed regularly with all staff on our PSEA and CP Policies & staff responsibilities as these relate to conduct, monitoring & reporting. Gender balance in our FSL & M&E teams will continue to ensure that activities are responsive to the particular needs of men, women, boys & girls with female staff present at all distribution points and responsible for all M&E activities completed with female BNFs. Our Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) will continue to be widely promoted, through information displayed prominently in public places, at all distribution points, printed on in-kind food baskets / food vouchers and through face-to-face interactions with project BNFs & key stakeholders. The recent RNA completed by us found no protection risks associated with accessing markets in targeted areas. Monitoring by our staff will continue to assess for any protection concerns related to food voucher redemption. Targeting criteria will continue to mitigate protection concerns by actively targeting the most at risk groups, including female-, child- and PWD-headed HHs. Monitoring and routine PDM, completed by male and female staff with a representative sample of both male and female BNFs will also continue to monitor for any SEA risks or concerns. The design of LLH activities places particular focus on gender-appropriate and sex-disaggregated LLH activities plus add'l mitigation measures.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Security-related suspensions prevent field access. Mitigation: Maintain contact with stakeholders and BNFs thru WhatsApp, monitor the security dynamic to inform activity planning & scheduling including option to deliver assistance door-to-door. Should access not resume, consider reprogramming assistance to more stable areas. Risk: C-19 pandemic. Mitigation: All activities adhere to established C-19 Guidelines, incl. use of PPE, adequate sanitization & social distancing. Risk: Inclusion/exclusion errors: Mitigation: 100% verification in line with well-established SOPs, triangulation of BNF lists with relevant community leaders, promotion of CRM to report potential inclusion/exclusion errors. Risk: Fund Misuse. Mitigation: Follow mitigation measures outlined in established SOP for FSL programming, Finance & Procurement policies adhered to at all times, DM & established internal control measures, continue to ensure safe, transparent & fully documented assistance delivery.Risk: GBV / SEA concerns. Mitigation: Codes of conducts signed by all staff & stakeholders, regular refresher training on PSEA & CP delivered. DM, PDM and CRM used to monitor for GBV/SEA risks, female staff present at all distributions, CRM hotline (including PSEA) widely promoted. Risk: Supply chain breaks. Mitigation: FWAs in place with a range of tested suppliers, option to move to cash-based assistance should in-kind no longer be viable. Cont'd from Section 5 Acts.:MEAL activities will include DM & PDM to monitor implementation, BNF satisfaction and relevance of assistance to needs and project impact against key FS indicators including FCS, HHDDS and RCSI. Impact will also be evaluated thru comparison of baseline & end line FS indicators.
