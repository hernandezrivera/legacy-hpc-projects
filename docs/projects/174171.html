Project Summary

This agency-specific plan seeks to contribute immensely to the protection of conflict-affected men, women, boys and girls heavily impacted by the ongoing conflict in Northeast Nigeria specifically Borno state, which unfortunately is entering it's eleventh year while disproportionately affecting women and children. Overall, and within the context of widespread erosion of protective structures within communities and IDP camps CHAD intends to support and seek opportunities for effective and innovative community-based control, ownership, and partnership in combating and addressing child protection and gender-based violence risks affecting children and women, and caregiving responsibilities by caregivers, and other relevant community-based stakeholders including women and youth groups. Our Community based ownership and partnership approach to protection and mental health and psychosocial support recognizes affected boys and girls, their families and accessible community resources as active agents in addressing emergency humanitarian and protection needs for the purpose of appropriate and sustainable solutions.
Key interventions include
Child Protection
- Provision of case management to unaccompanied and separated children
- Provision of family tracing and reunification services to separated children
- Provision of alternative care arrangement to children without parental care or whose current caregivers have been assessed to lack the capacity to provide meaningful care to vulnerable boys and girls
- Capacity building and livelihood support to vulnerable caregivers
- Community-based Reintegration for boys and girls associated with armed groups
- Provision of critical child protection prevention and response messaging to communities where CHAD will be operating.
- Support the development and operationalization of multisectoral referral pathway.
Gender-Based Violence
1. Strengthen the capacity of women-led community-based organizations in Bama, Ngala and Mafa to recognize, prevent and respond to incidents of Gender-based violence especially sexual exploitation, child marriage and domestic violence, while contributing to structural reforms promoting the rights, resilience and protection of women and girls
2. Galvanize positive behavioural change and livelihood opportunities amongst adolescent boys and girls aged 14-17 that better protects and mitigates the impact of GBV especially early child marriage, intimate partner violence, sexual exploitation and abuse through a peer-support initiative and locally-appropriate livelihood opportunities.
3. Support survivor-centred care for 600 adolescent survivors of gender-based violence especially survivors of sexual violence and assault, and domestic violence through case management and direct services
4. Support GBV mainstreaming across the humanitarian response by supporting key sectors, local actors, and key government personnel on developing locally-appropriate GBV risk mitigation strategies for their responses.

1 - Needs

CHILD PROTECTIONAn estimated 1.7 million people (1.6 million children and 100,000 caregivers) need child protection services; this includes an estimated 21,000 children and caregivers living with disabilities. In 2021, the Child Protection Sub-Sector will target 937,394 children (492,966 girls, 434,427 boys) and 72,874 caregivers (38,698 women, 34,175 men), bringing the total number of persons targeted with child protection services to 1,000,269 people. Children’s vulnerabilities to protection risks are also exacerbated by limited or lack of access to basic services and goods such as food, education, health and nutrition and limited access of caregivers to livelihoods. An estimated 17,528 girls and boys (28% girls, 72% boys) are in need protection child protection case management and other specialized services to help prevent or address exposure to abuse, exploitation, neglect and violence. Girls and boys in north-east Nigeria continue to face multiple protection risks and violations of their rights. The threats and risks posed by these protection risks have been exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic which has not only negatively affected delivery of life-saving child protection services but has also weakened the capacities of families and communities to care for children. The COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the situation of children living without parental care, especially those for boys who had been sent away by their families for religious education purposes as well as the situation of children on the streets.Gender-Based Violence Though over 1.5 million people including women, men, boys and girls are estimated to be in need of GBV services, 1.3 million people are being targeted for humanitarian assistance in 2021 by the GBV sub-sector. Women and children are the face of the crisis. Women and children constitute 81% of the overall crisis population and 87% of the new displacements. Thea gendered dimensions of displacement is having a significant impact on the status of women and girls especially within the context of mobility, fragmentation of households, demoralization and trauma (OCHA, HNO Nigeria 2020). Service provision gaps continues to affect the quality of care available to GBV survivors especially in locations within hard to reach areas. Unethical data management on reported GBV incidents, very limited case management capacity and a general lack of adequate MHPSS services targeting adolescents are areas in dire need of strengthening. There is an urgent need to strengthen existing services, coordination as well as referral mechanisms to ensure survivors access to comprehensive services is facilitated.It is equally to important to note the pervasive nature of GBV even before the inception of the crisis. Nigeria ranks 158th in the global gender inequality index developed by UNDP. Gender norms especially in the northeast region of Nigeria continues to affect women and girls especially in the area of child and maternal mortality, sexual and reproductive rights and education for adolescent girls. There remans a need to address the historical relegation of women through gender transformative programming seeking to redress gender inequalities. LocalizationWhile there have been well-meaning capacity building initiatives by the international humanitarian community targeting local organizations, like the PLRCAP initiatives and efforts to support local leadership in coordination structures, experience has shown that local organizations continue to partake in these iniatiatives without knowing exactly where the road leads nor ends. This is clear indication that externally driven and designed initiatives continue to demonstrate limitations in supporting self-sustenance for most local NGOs especially in the areas of strategic indepence and a sense of ambition. CHAD will continue to advocate for approaches that respects and recognizes local actors as active agents in solving their own problems.
