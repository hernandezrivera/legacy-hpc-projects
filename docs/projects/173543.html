Project Summary

Based on recent assessments conducted by COOPI multi-sectoral teams in two localities (Camp Kilo 26 in Khashm Girba in Kassala State and Al Lait in North Darfur State) in the framework of the humanitarian response to floods, the project aims to address significant challenges experienced by refugees (South Sudanese in Al Lait and Eritreans in Kilo 26) and reduce their vulnerability to recurrent natural disasters by improving their general living conditions and promoting a culture of Disaster Preparedness and Disaster Risk Reduction measures which will enhance the capacity of the target beneficiaries to cope with multi-hazards including natural disasters. In particular, the project aims to tackle structural weaknesses identified in the assessment processes which highlighted serious shortcomings and damages as a consequence of recurrent natural disasters as follows:
- Access to adequate, safe, durable and community-managed water supplies;
- Access to adequate, safe and durable sanitation and hygiene facilities;
- Promote and support community-developed and community-led Disaster Preparedness and Disaster Risk Reduction measures as well as contingency planning and pre-positioning of first-response emergency supplies targeting communities/households most exposed to recurrent natural disasters;
- Enhance livelihood opportunities for refugees' farming and pastoralists communities with particular focus on vulnerable and disadvantaged refugees' groups such as women, youth and people with disabilities.
The project will also include a component for the expansion of COOPI's emergency response to the Ethiopian Refugees' crisis in Kassala and Gedaref States focusing on WASH.

1 - Needs

A post-flood technical assessment by COOPI in Camp Kilo 26 (Kassala State) identified these needs: (1) High exposure to floods of resident in camp zones 4, 5 and 7. The flood event of 2020 affected 110 HHs in Zone 4, close to the main road. The area is located in an underlying position and the canal's banks and the surface is concave and tends to collect water due to lack of drainage. The U-shaped 6Km terrace protecting the camp perimeter disappeared due to lack of maintenance. (2) Hygiene and Sanitation. Only 40% of community members have HHs' latrines and the remaining 60% opt for open defecation. There is only 1 functional latrine for both gender as a second one collapsed at the health centre. The 2 schools located in Zone 3 have insufficient latrines without hand-washing facilities and running water. (3) Access to Water. The areas of the camp far from the Water Station (Zone 7, 3 and 6) do not have water supply. Residents purchase polluted canal water from donkey-carts and seasonal cases of Acute Watery Diarrhea and outbreaks of typhoid fever are reported. (3) The 2 main water stations in the camp use slow sand filters. The two water stations are old (1982 and 1985) and both use solar energy. The water stations serve the camp as well as 2,000 additional people in 3 surrounding villages. The older station is damaged in many parts and today the station is working directly without sufficient filtration. The station can approximately pump 150m3/day. The other station is in good condition but there is no cover for the filtration basin which protects from external contamination. This station can currently pump 150m3/day but the quantity can actually increase significantly if rehabilitated. The majority of the water points in Zone 3 and 7 are not working (approximately 1,090 HHs affected) are old, broken and rusty.A preliminary assessment conducted by COOPI in Al Lait (North Darfur) after the flood event 2020 indicated the following needs: (i.) Some areas are not covered by water points. Moreover, some of the existing water points have been damaged by the recent floods. (ii.) In general, the existing and functioning water points serve a population which must come from long distances to access water at big costs. (iii) There is a very low level of awareness in the communities when it comes to hygiene and sanitation practices. Open defecation is common. (iv.) Recent flood events have destroyed HHs and institutional pit latrines. (v.) A number of refugees and host communities have not yet been reached by an emergency response and they are still in need of basic emergency supplies such as: tents, blankets, plastic sheets, clothes, floor mattresses and jerry cans (vi.) Refugees have limited access to farming (seeds are normally loaned by local traders with very high in-kind interest rates). (vii.) Vulnerable refugee women, youth and people with disabilities have limited livelihood opportunities. (viii.) Post-harvest losses due to poor storage facilities are quite common. (ix.) There are outbreaks of livestock diseases in the area. (x.) Refugees and host communities share limited natural resources and this generates tensions and occasionally conflict.As far as the Humanitarian Response to the refugee crisis, COOPI intends to meet needs as follows: (a) construction of 72 additional emergency trench latrines with plastic slabs (b) Construction of 3 blocks of VIP latrines (c) Installation of 30 HWFs “hands-free model” (d) Construction of 72 emergency shower and washing facilities (e) Provision of laundry basins and essential washing supplies (f) Solid waste management/disposal at Hamdayet (g) Provision of additional large tents/shelters and enhanced shading for Reception and Waiting Areas
