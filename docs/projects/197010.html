Project Summary

INTERSOS health program follows an integrated approach which is aimed at providing life-saving and life-sustaining community and primary healthcare assistance. In cooperation with the Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) and Ministry of Health, INTERSOS is committed to build resilience and capacities of communities affected by the crisis in Syria. INTERSOS will implement multi sectoral actions where health will be integrated with components of Protection (including child protection and GBV, see HSYR23-PRO-197346-1), education (HSYR23-EDU-196630-1) and ERL (HSYR23-ERY-196628-1) interventions. and Education. Health projects aim to achieve the following main results: strengthening skills and capacities of health personnel on PHC and diseases outbreaks (including COVID-19 related topics - IPC, Triage), enhancing access to essential health services in remote areas, raising awareness on good health behavior and practices in Hama, Idleb, RD and Deir-ez-Zor. The action applies a combined mobile and static approach whereby static health facilities are enhanced and equipped alongside with the deployment of mobile health teams in hard-to-reach areas with no health clinic available. As a final result, access to quality PHC services for vulnerable communities in hard-to-reach areas will be increased and expanded. The mobile units will provide primary health care (including ANC/PNC and pediatric consultations), nutrition-integrated services (screening and IYCF), support to immunization, risk communication and active prevention awareness (for communicable and non-communicable diseases) A comprehensive capacity building program will contribute to strengthen Health Workers and first-line responders’ skills to decrease health risks, protect and assist vulnerable individuals with high-severity needs. Specific, tailored, technical training and capacity building sessions will be provided to SARC health staff and volunteers/community mobilizers on relevant COVID 19, health topics, PHC, common childhood illnesses, basic emergency obstetric and new-born care and health education and promotion. INTERSOS will facilitate and monitor the delivery of health services to beneficiaries through SARC health workers and INTERSOS medical staff to ensure continuity and availability of PHC services through diagnosis and treatment of common illnesses among children, PLW, adolescents, PwDs, elderly, adults according to the national protocols.

3 - Needs

The HNO 2023 estimates more than 15.3 M people in need of health services in Syria, meaning an increase by around 3.2 M compared to 2022. Functioning health facilities are overstretched and struggle with limited human resources, lack of medical equipment/drugs/supplies, absence of reproductive health care services in rural areas, and disease outbreaks (lastly, Cholera). These shortcomings have direct consequences on vulnerable communities and patients, increasing morbidity and mortality across Syria. INTERSOS RMNA conducted in Hama, Idleb and Rural Damascus (May/June 2022) highlighted the multiple challenges faced by local communities to access health services, including decreased availability of medicines, increasing cost of medical treatment, inability to afford transportation costs to reach the closest HF, and lack of reproductive health services for PLW. In rural areas across the 3 governorates, the overall lack of emergency ambulance services was reported, while their cost remains a key barrier for many individuals where these are available. Mental health care services as well as specialized services for PwDs and older persons are also very limited. Access to specialized mental health services is limited due to shortage of qualified mental health professionals, while PwDs and older persons cannot access basis health services due to physical (distance, non-inclusive facilities, lack of specialized staff, etc.) and attitudinal (discrimination, stigmatization) barriers.

4 - Implementation of activities

INTERSOS will continue focusing on the provision of PHC through mobile approaches-targeting hard-to-reach areas, while expanding support to static HFs. INTERSOS will thus support the provision of the minimum package of quality and free care based on national and international standard and comprehensive of: general consultation (CDs including IMCI and NCDs, trauma); sexual and reproductive health (ANC, PNC); referrals to guarantee the continuum of care; community-based health and hygiene promotion; provision of medical commodities; health data management and support to the EWARS; technical capacity building for health workers (IPC, C-19 and Cholera, IYCF, malnutrition surveillance, IMCI, RH, MhGAP, disability inclusion, health awareness) and volunteers (IPC, C-19 and Cholera awareness, RCCE). INTERSOS will monitor the service provision through health databases/quality checklists and the knowledge of the HWs and CHVs will be evaluated through pre&post test. Following an integrated approach, HWs will be trained on safe identification and referral, CP and GBV. INTERSOS will ensure referral pathways and provision of medical in-kind assistance to vulnerable cases identified during case management. INTERSOS is reinforcing integration with EiE by training school personnel and teachers on health and hygiene related topics (IPC, first aid, C-19 and Cholera prevention), conducting health and hygiene education (such as menstrual hygiene management) and sessions on exam stress management.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Risk of discrimination, perceptions of discrimination/exclusion of vulnerable groups, lack of communication and engagement of target groups. Mitigation: Ensure that consultations with communities, in a way to tailor and adapt the intervention according to needs; Continuous involvement of community throughout the project, in line with the principles of accountability to affected populations (AAP); Adopt and enforce a lean and effective Complaint Response and Feedback Mechanisms, Production and dissemination of key messages, inclusive and accessible to wide audiences (e.g. RCCE campaigns). Risk: GBV victims and survivors lack safe, dignified and stigma-free access to PHC and support. Mitigation: Ensure Health Workers are trained on GBV principles and response strategies and are enabled to identify GBV cases and provide safe referrals; include PSS and GBV prevention and response activities in health interventions; Secure protection mainstreaming in all health programs. Risk: SEA. Mitigation: strict PSEA policy and SOPs are in place; Provide inception and refresher training to INTERSOS and SARC staff; ensure a proper reporting system, data protection and referral pathways are in place. Risk: Increase of tensions between IDPs, Returnees and HCs due to unequal provision of assistance. Mitigation: Secure that a community mapping and conflict-sensitivity analysis are performed at the onset of the project; consultations and transparent communications to foster social cohesion


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R: INTERSOS/implementing partners (IP) staff, suppliers (etc.) have prior conviction for SEA related offenses; IP, staff member, suppliers, do not comply/accept PSEA policy; IP does not have a strong PSEA system in place; SEA perpetrated by staff/service providers; PSEA allegations are not timely/effectively addressed; survivors have limited access to safe assistance/remedies; lack of PSEA reporting mechanisms; communities not aware of PSEA risks/reporting channels. M: INTERSOS has a Global PSEA/CP Policy that applies to all personnel in addition to a country level Strategy to ensure SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP Policy Declaration, attend a PSEA/CP Policy induction and one refresher training per year. One PSEA Referent and two PSEA Focal Points are appointed to receive complaints/reports about alleged acts of SEA and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Complaints are referred to the in-country PSEA Referent and are subject to investigation. An assessment about IP commitment and capacity to prevent/respond to SEA is conducted; efforts are made with IP to harmonize PSEA strategies/reporting procedures to the best extent possible. Training is offered to partners to strengthen the identification of SEA risks/adoption of related SEA mitigation measures; when possible awareness raising on PSEA risks/reporting channels is conducted at community level.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

INTERSOS Syria has developed a Contingency Planning Process (CPP) with risk analysis and related mitigation measures, whose key components can be summarized as follows: Reputational Risk: unable to deliver on-time, breach of sanctions and/or do not harm principle. A detailed SOPs on procurement of medical supplies and drugs is in place and into force, thorough market research and due diligence exercises are conducted for verification of quality standards and legal requirements, strong communication, reporting mechanisms and robust monitoring strategy are followed; Security and Access: unable to reach and implement in targeted areas. INTERSOS relies on security assessment and protocols, combined with SARC guidance, supervision and networks in the field; Revisions of target communities, work-plan and scheduled activities might be envisioned if necessary. Gender discrimination and exclusion: risk of reinforcing gender stereotypes by excluding women from participation. ; Financial Risk and Accountability: exchange rates fluctuations affect achievement of targets; lack of transparency; diversion or misuse of project financial resources. INTERSOS has an internal system and SOPs to secure compliance and accountability: careful, systematic and rigorous monitoring both of project activities and project spending is carried out; financial controlling rules and processes are in place; regular communication with the donor in case of economic shock and re-alignment of targets.
