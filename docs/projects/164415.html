Project Summary

This project aims at promoting cohesion and recovery by reducing shelter vulnerabilities of residents, host communities, returnees and IDPs for both inside of camps and outside of camps as follows:
1- Providing rehabilitation to people living in substandard shelter conditions in (Al-Bab, Qabasine) sub-districts and Salqin sub-district in Aleppo and Idleb governorates by providing rehabilitation to 700 houses, for 700 HHs (3500 individuals, 52% female - 48%), each house approximately costs 1000$ to be adequate housing based on accurate and time-specific information pertinent to the shelter conditions of the beneficiaries, including specific vulnerability categories by provision of HLP-related information and improve community governance and control of the shelter projects within targeted communities to ensure results-based management and efficient response to shelter needs throughout CWGs participate in selecting beneficiaries and implementation monitoring.
2- Provision of emergency shelter (tent installation in camps with associated infrastructure, distribution of emergency shelter materials and kits), this activity will be conducted by selecting a suitable land
and conducting a topographic study for rain drainage to prevent a flood.
In order to construct a planned camp, we are planning to pave the roads, gravel the tents and provide them with all the necessary facilities in the three lands as following:
- Al Ghazala community in Drkush sub-district by establishment a camp for 300 HHs (1500 individuals, 52% female - 48%), Sarmada in Dana sub-district by establishment a camp for 500 HHs (2500 individuals, 52% female - 48%), Atma in Dana sub-district by establishment a camp for 350 HHs (2000 individuals, 52% female - 48%), and Jarablus in Aleppo by establishment a camp for 700 HHs (3500 individuals, 52% female - 48%), and installation 2000 tents for newly IDPs in northern of Syria (10000 individuals, 52% female - 48%).
ATAA will collaborate to identify needs for targeted rehabilitation. These discussions will be driven by FGDs and key informant information interviews done by previously made projects with both elderly and female community members, ATAA will ensure their involvement in all phases of the project.
Conducting the PSEA/GBV awareness-raising sessions to the beneficiaries to protecting them from sexual exploitation and abuse.

4 - Needs

According to HNO/2019 "While the crisis in Syria has left no one untouched, it continues to impact population groups differently. Some segments of the population are likely to be more acutely affected than others, six broad population groups have been identiﬁed as those generally facing the most acute needs across sectors, two out of these six groups are as followed: Newly-displaced populations and Overburdened communities including women-headed households. Based on HNO/2019 ﬁndings "there are increased needs related to the repair and rehabilitation of damaged shelters and infrastructure as people seek more durable solutions. It is likely that these needs will further increase in 2020, as well as a large proportion of IDP sites in Syria are informal settlements, especially after the recent huge displacement, a lot of these camps are needed to gravel and site improvement. According to RNA conducted by ATAA for (houses rehabilitation): the number of inhabited and uninhabited sheltersand the scale of damaged shelters according to the thresholds of damage set by ATAA depending on the scale of the damage (minor damage: less than 500$, moderate damage: between 500$ and 1000$ and major damage: more than 1000$) for each sub-districts are as followed: 1- Al-Bab: the number of minor damage houses: 1100, the number of moderate damage houses: 700, the number of major damage houses: 150 2- Qabasin: the number of minor damage houses: 1000, the number of moderate damage houses: 500, the number of major damage houses: 150 3- Salqin: the number of minor damage houses:900, the number of moderate damage houses: 500, the number of major damage houses: 300.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project was designed in a manner that benefits all of the categories, including women, children, people with disabilities and the most vulnerable categories.Equality will be achieved in the recruitment process and personnel selection.Also the participation of the women will be enhanced in the project design The risks:1- Sexual exploitation and abuse and child abuse by ATAA personnel, its partners, services providers/suppliers toward the beneficiaries especially women and girls. The mitigation:- Mainstreaming PSEA policy, in addition to CSG policy including training ATAA`s personnel.- requiring ATAA`s personnel, partner, services providers/suppliers to sign a commitment of the policy.- Activate the complaint mechanism and penalty procedures.- Awareness-raising sessions to the beneficiaries about sexual exploitation and abuse, and aware them about complaints mechanism.- Activating the mechanism of referring survivors of SEA/GBV to the specialist services2- Community tension due to houses selection processleading to a feeling of discrimination. The mitigation: - Clarify the criteria and procedures for choosing houses according to: Ensure beneficiary selection criteria is uniform/agreed from CWGs.- HLP training will be mitigate this issue Raise awareness about HLP through engaging with community leaders taking into account not to evacuate any families from homes, while maintain cooperation with shelter sector members and all operating parties to find solutions to HLP issues with respect to people right of having a home.- Establish complaint mechanism at community level.3- Lack of required documentation (property or / and civil documentation) to prove property/occupancy rights. The mitigation:-According to HLP, we will work together with the local authorities, partners and communities to establish a mechanism to deal with such issues at local level. Work together with protection partners and referral for legal assistance.

Mitigation 2: Rehabilitation will begin during the period from May until the end of the year. Risk 3: Community tension due to houses selection processleading to a feeling of discrimination. Mitigation 3: Clarify the criteria and procedures for choosing houses according to Ensure beneficiary selection criteria is uniform/agreed from CWGs. Establish a complaint mechanisms at the community level. Risk 4: Lack of required documentation (property or/and civil documentation) to prove property/occupancy rights.
