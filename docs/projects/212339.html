Project Summary

"The Syrian crisis has impacted everyone; women, men, boys and girls. Each group was impacted in a different way and had lived a unique experience.
Children, especially boys, face different kinds of child labour. Women and girls face different protection risks too, including several forms of GBV such as sexual exploitation (more often for single households, widowed, separated, divorced...) early and forced marriage.
In addition to protection risks, in crisis, common mental disorders tend to increase such as depression, PTSD – from 5% to 10%. But also, there are lots of symptoms that can impact a large range of people affecting their daily functioning, and they are not clinically diagnosed.
People inside Syria have witnessed violence, lost family members, were obliged to move several times and experienced other traumatic events.
In the CFS project in Rural Damascus (Jaramana) and Daraa, through protection activities we will increase more equitable access for boys and girls to protection services, and through different activities we will try to raise protection awareness in the community, help people have better coping strategies and prevent severe mental health conditions.
Through this protection program aims to respond to the critical needs of the affected population while also building resilience in key underserved in targeted areas which were disproportionately impacted by war over the last decade.
In targeted areas communities will have improved access to protection services, particularly for women and children. During this project will be focusing on children in vulnerable households including low-income households, children with disabilities, girls, separated/unaccompanied children, children associated with armed forces/armed groups, and those who are marginalized, stigmatized or discriminated against.
The project will establishment two safe space for children in the Rural ( Jaramana ) and Daraa city. Through these spaces, protection services will be provided for boys and girls by providing psychological and social support activities for children, raising awareness for caregivers, and case management services.
PSHEA related feedback will be collected through PSHEA specific survey, email, and hotline. Data will be
collected by EPDC based on the beneficiary’s choice of reporting. EPDC will handle the complaints jointly. PSHEA, Child Safeguarding, will be signed by all staff, volunteers and
contracted service providers. While Code of Conduct will be signed by EPDC staff and volunteers.
