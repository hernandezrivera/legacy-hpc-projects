Project Summary

This is an urgent emergency response to reinforce the capacities, resilience and protection of vulnerable Palestinian communities mainly in Area C, and East Jerusalem from natural and manmade hazards. The project will support also quarantine/isolation centers in Area A and B, an emerging issue related to the COVID-19 pandemic, flagged as most urgent by the MoH especially in winter.
The action includes the rehabilitation and upgrading of shelters and the provision of capacity building to reduce the exposure of targeted beneficiaries from man-made/natural disasters and weather conditions, to manage and adapt internal space in case of COVID isolation, enabling them to live with dignity and improving their preparedness. The action will mitigate the threats against communities and support resilience building to protracted threats and vulnerability, while responding to their needs in terms of shelter and protection, and indirectly WASH. Providing adequate shelter will contribute to mitigate the effects, vulnerabilities and risks associated with the coercive environment, especially for women, children, elderly and PWDs. Shelter rehabilitation and upgrading activities, coupled with the implementation of the community participation methodology will allow the fulfillment of the objectives of empowering communities in terms of coping capacities, ownership and expertise as well as. The action is fully in line with 2021 HRP Strategic Objectives 1, 2 & 3, it complies with the minimum shelter cluster standards for adequate housing, gender, and adequate access to housing by PWDs. This integrated approach ensures that communities are sufficiently protected and equipped with tangible assets, in order to prevent their forcible transfer. The beneficiaries of this action are among the most vulnerable households in terms of socio-economic and protection vulnerabilities, which are connected to their physical and political-related situation: families with PWDs who needs shelter/WASH facilities adaptation and families who suffers settler’s violence; residents of communities highly affected by settler and IF violence, lack of access to basic services, inadequate shelter, and movement restrictions among many other hindrances. The action will target 24 communities in six governorates, including EJ inside and outside the wall, and four quarantine/isolation centers in four governorates. Shelter rehabilitation will be obtained through different interventions: (Area C) rehabilitation of concrete shelters with composite roof, rehabilitation of concrete shelter with asphalted roof with internal renovation works, provision of PVC cover and rehabilitation of composite shelters, C-19 measures adaptation; (EJ) roof sandwich panels, ventilation, insulation, flooring, windows, internal walls, toilets, sinks and kitchens, disabilities access if needed. Interventions do not need municipality permits since no new constructions will be executed, solely rehabilitation and in some cases rearrangements of existing housing units. Capacity building will be provided to the communities on self-implemented house renovation techniques to continue improving the shelter conditions. The provision of material support will be backed-up with activities and sessions to contribute to self-recovery; reduced risk of GBV and other protection-related risks; increased privacy and PWD minimum living standards; WASH facilities and hygiene. All interventions will be implemented in coordination with the PA. The action is part of a multi-year action to cover the humanitarian needs of the communities with a NEXUS prospective, the action responds to communities’ needs and looks beyond the mere material needs to the broader issues of safety and dignity. Responsive and preparedness supplies, will be delivered to quarantine/isolation centers to assure safe and dignified accommodation for infected people and medical staff.

1 - Needs

WW-GVC conducted a multi-sectorial assessment for 22 quarantine centres in West Bank in April 2020, information was updated in July 2020, and updated lately in September 2020 during distribution of humanitarian supplies (kitchen and hygiene kits) by different initiatives, the assessments were disseminated to inform the WASH, Shelter and Health clusters about the most urgent needs, moreover the results of the assessment were cross-checked with “COVID-19 Advocacy Priorities and Key Messages” document which was launched and updated regularly by ICCG.As part of the Community-based Protection Approach (CPA), in the framework of the ECHO West Bank Protection Consortium, WW-GVC has been collecting quantitative and qualitative data in the targeted communities since 2015 to gather, through a full community engagement, protection related information on vulnerabilities, threats, capacities, and coping strategies. The last round of assessments, carried out in February 2020, highlighted the persisting difficult environment, continuously challenging protection threats, restriction of movements, and restricted access to basic service (including shelter). Finally, WW-GVC has participated also to the shelter vulnerability assessment led by the Shelter Cluster, completed in July 2020. These data, along with the joint assessment carried out by WWGVC and PHC during elaboration of this plan, highlighted the following issues: presence of old shelters in urgent need of rehabilitation -including severe electrical damage, persistent dampness, leaking and caving roofs, and many other critical needs-; incapacity of the shelters to guarantee proper insulation and proper hygiene conditions, which become even more urgent with COVID-19 pandemic; need for installation of doors and windows to prevent the entrance of animals, as well as to mitigate the harassment from Israeli Forces and settlers; need for privacy; unhealthy and unhygienic WASH conditions due substandard insulation and old sanitation systems in their shelters, which leads to infection by bacteria and germs; presence of snakes, scorpions, and centipedes inside most shelters; continued restraint to construction works on the site by Israeli DCL, citing security reasons. The assessments show that the inability of the community to improve structures is mainly due to economic deprivation. Data analysis conducted by WW-GVC show also that specific groups are extremely vulnerable and exposed to these risks, particularly new couples, households lead by women, and pregnant women, as well as children, elders and PWD. Women represent a vulnerable group, given the patriarchal culture that stresses women’s role in the domestic sphere, they specifically suffer from improper and unsafe living conditions and they are more vulnerable to poverty and diseases; the same applies for children, elders and PWDs. During the project design all the different needs of men, women, boys and girls, collected though the above assessments and related with the rehabilitation of the shelters, were taken into account. The identified activities will be further appraised through technical assessments and community consultations in line with: the applicable humanitarian standards, the parameters of the Shelter Cluster, and any new COVID-19 prevention measures recommended by Shelter cluster and WHO, also factoring social acceptance to insure gender and age-appropriate solutions.Furthermore, social teams will study the situation of the shelters with all family members specially women and girls to define the main problems related to health, hygiene and women/girls privacy, and transfer this information to the technical staff, to define the suitable and accepted solutions with the family members and access to PWD. These considerations will be taken into account in the final dignified design, drawings and BoQ to make sure to adapt them to the indications of the end-users. Women will be involved in designing the rehabilitation of the houses, based on the needs they expressed through the brainstorming meeting with field staff. The project will place particular attention on the vulnerable families (families with people with disabilities, families headed by women or by elders).

2 - Activities

The action will entail provision of upgrading and protection assistance to vulnerable households at high risk of exposure or affected by natural hazards (e.g. COVID pandemic, weather extremes, flooding), or manmade hazards (Exposure to settler violence); shelter upgrading through providing cash or materials assistance and technical training; shelter and WASH facilities adaptation for PWDs; protective assistance to shelters exposed to settlers violence. AGD interviews and focus groups discussions will be conducted to assure involvement of men, women, boys and girls as well as PWDs in analysing their needs, designing and implement project activities, female staff will make specific interviews and FGDs with women to analyse their needs and transfer them to engineers, then technical assessments will be conducted prior to the intervention to design interventions, specifying the materials and labor needed for each shelter. Based on the Community Implementation’s methodology, rehabilitation/upgrading will be completed by the beneficiaries themselves, whom will establish local project committees, one in each community committee (at least 30% of the members being women), that will be in charge of the implementation of the activities. To ensure beneficiaries’ involvement in the project implementation, MOU signature and handover is foreseen with the local committees in Area C, while individual MOU in EJ (due to special situation in EJ inside the wall). Rehabilitation of shelters will guarantee: proper ventilation, decreased water leakages, improved hygienic conditions and privacy as well as safety, in compliance with shelter cluster standards. Activities will include proper flooring, plastering and painting of the shelters, ensuring proper and dignified accessibility for people including PWD, adequate insulation, ventilation, prevention of rain-water leakages, and more resistance to climate change. The renovation work will include also, when needed, WASH infrastructure in the shelters, to improve health and water conditions, sanitation and hygiene management. The project will provide technical support and supervision to the shelter upgrading through weekly field visits, detailed BoQs and drawings in Arabic and English languages, conducted by WW-GVC and PHC. The project will also ensure constant coordination with the Shelter Cluster and relevant stakeholders, Governors offices (based on signed coordination MOU), village councils, OCHA and legal aid providers. The specific approach selected for each community will depend on several factors (primarily the land ownership status) and will include: rehabilitation of concrete shelters with composite roof, which are comprised of a steel frame covered with insulated metal sheets and include a wooden wall framework treated with fireproof material; rehabilitation of concrete shelter with asphalted roof and internal renovation works; provision of PVC plastic covers (supporting the structure of metal bars that the community members already own); caves rehabilitation. Rehabilitation cost per shelter will vary according to its location and type rehabilitation. Average cost for shelter rehabilitation and upgrading in Jordan Valley is 2,400$; average cost in other area C locations is 5,000$, average cost in East Jerusalem inside the wall is 8,300$.WW-GVC and PHC will conduct capacity building to the community members on self-implemented house renovation and techniques to improve the shelter conditions, including protection from natural hazards and extreme weather events, and to ensure privacy and safety, female staff in WWGVC and PHC will assure the participation of women in communities through technical field visits and capacity building documents. WW-GVC and PHC will target communities who suffer from inadequate and overcrowded shelters with activities and sessions that contribute to self-recovery, to mitigate risk of infection of COVID-19 among family members, to reduce the risk of GBV and other protection-related risk, and to sensitize the communities about privacy inside shelters for all of children, women and men, PWD minimum living standards, the trainings will be done by WWGVC and PHC social teams based on RCCE materials First aid trainings and kits were provided to the targeted communities under previous projects with support of Palestinian Civil defence, new first aid kits will be provided to all HHs under risk of settler violence, crawling insects or any other risks during the coming projects as well.COVID-19 protection measures (wearing face masks, gloves, hand sanitizers, physical distances) will be followed by WWGVC and PHC teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread.

3 - Indicators

The purpose of this action is to address, by assisting families in restoring their shelters, the communities’ humanitarian and protection needs, while improving their living and health conditions and contribute to safety, dignity and resilience. Specifically, the action will lead to the achievement of the following indicators: 100% of assisted HH living in upgraded unit in accordance with minimum standards; 60% of assisted families protected from settler violence; 100 % of people in quarantine facilities and/or housing units have improved shelter and hygienic conditions; 4 public facilities are partially /fully upgraded for use as isolation/quarantine centres. As part of the monitoring system, specific assessments are designed to capture indicators related to the shelter sector, which will be collected giving consideration of the impact on women, children, and elders. The interventions are guided by protection analyses. Context-adapted responses and accountability to affected populations (including gender-age sensitivity) are ensured thanks to active beneficiary engagement throughout all stages of the project cycle; female staff from WWGVC and PHC participate in all project field visits to assure participation in comfortable environment of women and children in all phases of the project. The community protection approach is specifically designed to reduce bias and to support adequate beneficiary participation and to involve groups traditionally excluded from decision making (women, children, PWDs). Specifically, the achievement of the indicators will be measured as follows: a technical assessment is conducted prior to the technical implementation to investigate thoroughly each of the community’s protection, through focus groups conducted with men and women, girls, boys, children, elders including PWD (separated for males and females) to evaluate indicators (such as ventilation, number of rooms, separation between girls and boys, size and reason of seasonal movements) related to shelters conditions, baseline and after the completion of the intervention; an overall evaluation will be conducted, including quantitative data collection (Pre & Post Specific surveys) and qualitative analysis through separated focus groups discussions (women/girls and men/boys) focusing on Displacements / Seasonal displacements / Family Separation / Safety / Dignity and Integrity. A feedback mechanism is assured through concrete community engagement in decision making related to intervention’s design and direct implementation, which enhances the ownership of the activity by the community. The action responds to communities’ needs and looks beyond the mere material needs to the broader issues of personal safety, dignity and protection. The new shelters will ensure: an improvement of the privacy of all family members, especially women and girls; good ventilation and subsequent improved health conditions and more resistance to COVID-19 and other diseases of all family members especially children and elderlies during cold and hot seasons; increased security and safety of all family members, especially of women and girls who will be able to carry out their duties in a safe place. The proposed shelter intervention will significantly decrease the risk of displacement for the most vulnerable Palestinian populations, especially women and children. Reduced seasonal displacement will provide protection for vulnerable families (families with people with disabilities and families headed by women). Improved shelter standards will enhance vulnerable families’ economic, health and social situation and will be allowing for pregnant women to spend more time at home before and after their delivery, decreasing separation from their families and community. The separation of spaces for boys and girls will allow increased sense of privacy and dignity for men and women. Specific monitoring tools will be designed to measure the impact of assistance delivered to COVID-19 quarantine/isolation centres, participation of staff and related stakeholders in the assessment phase will be also assured.

9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Response is carefully designed to ensure that interventions will not impair beneficiaries’ safety or expose them to further risk. Through the Community Protection Approach all community members will be involved in all the project phases. A thorough assessment will be conducted with each community to further investigate and understand the needs, risks and vulnerabilities of the diverse social groups, assuring the compliance with the Do No Harm principle, so that the renovation works are based on the preferences and needs expressed by the members of the HHs, considering the different threats that affect girls, boys, women and men of all ages including PWD. Additionally, interventions in the shelters will be designed to comply with protection principles, such as meaningful access and accountability towards beneficiaries, taking into account the diverse needs, roles and dynamics of women and men, age and disability status, in each community. Furthermore, household members will work in their shelter rehabilitation, so the sense of dignity and ownership will also be improved. The field team participated in the protection cluster GBV training for improving their capacities in detecting and referring cases. Moreover, the field team also participated in a training with War Child to better detect and refer children violence. Complying with the “Do No Harm” principles, communication between the PA’s legal counterparts and NRC ensures that legal awareness sessions are coordinated to strengthen the target communities’ knowledge of their rights, risks, and procedures to be followed. WWGVC is now mainstreaming the AGD and PWD toolkits in the community based protection methodology.COVID-19 protection measures (wearing face masks, gloves, hand sanitizers, physical distances) will be followed by WWGVC and PHC teams during all project activities to prevent the disease spread.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The Community Based Protection Approach (CPA) is a participatory methodology based on WW-GVC operational interpretation of the IASC definition of protection. An environment fully conducive to protection is obtained when multi-sector humanitarian and development needs of individuals and communities are met in a dignified and safe manner. As such, multi-sector needs are identified and ranked on the basis of the contextual protection risks of communities and individuals. A right-based analysis is used as entry point to elaborate local protection strategies to be then used as a programming framework for the definition of needs-based responses. The CPA was developed according to IHL, Humanitarian, Do No Harm and the following fundamental principles: 1. the safety of affected population (both of groups and individuals); 2. restore the dignity of affected population putting a stop to emerging or established patterns of violations (and/or alleviating their immediate effects); 3. place the empowerment of communities at risk at the core of any action to enable self-protection and realize their rights. The use of focus groups, random interviews and transect walks, ensures the involvement of the population in terms of location, age, gender, ethnicity, diversity, profession and power. According to the access to the population, the level of trust built and other do not harm considerations, multiple specific techniques will be applied to get deeper insight (bias reduction and/or data gap filling) of a particular group, a specific situation, a particular household. Throughout the assessment and planning phases, the needs of all community members will be taken into consideration. The proposed shelter and protection intervention address the urgent need of the targeted communities, accounting for non-discriminatory practices and considering the needs of every age, gender, and level of ability. Providing a voice for each member of the community, and requesting their direct action in the project’s implementation, preserves the dignity of each beneficiary and fosters community empowerment. The distribution of Complaint Response Mechanism (CRM) cards in the communities will ensure the accessibility to all beneficiaries to provide their feedbacks and complaints about the implemented project.Outputs of the assessment conducted in quarantine/isolation centres were verified with the health cluster and ICCG documents related to priorities in health sector; continuous coordination will be kept with these centres and related stakeholders to update the collected data and to respond to the most urgent NFI needs before designing and delivery of any response.
