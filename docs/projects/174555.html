Project Summary

For the past 4 years, Cameroon has seen a socio-political crisis emerge in the North West and South West Regions of the country known as the Anglophone Regions. The unrest which started in 2016 has progressively escalated into an armed conflict between secessionists and government security forces, disruption of basic services and public infrastructure, and subsequent large displacement of population which has led to increased food insecure homes. 10 % of IDPs are living remote areas, often in farm huts, bushes and the forests. (MSNA September 2019).The onset of covid19 pandemic in March 2020, introduced new threats to children and vulnerable communities worsening the situation in the NW/SW regions.
Morbidity (malaria, cough and diarrhea), poor sanitary conditions remained accelerated in 2020. Access to health care still remains a challenge due to insecurity and persistent attacks on healthcare as this constituted a huge threat to the availability of essential health care to the populations. 71% of the facilities are functional with majority (58%) of IDPs not able to access health care due to distance (45%) and financial constraints (17%). Furthermore, weak disease surveillance system has put the population at risk of epidemics with late detection and limited response capacity with repeated pockets of cholera outbreak have been reported in SW (MSNA, 2020). 26 % of persons affected by the NW/SW crisis are going.
SHUMAS intervention will be designed to reduce morbidity and mortality of children under 5 years, adolescent boys and girls and pregnant women affected by the crisis in 4 health districts in the NW/SW for 12 months through consultations and treatment of 3 killer diseases in children (Malaria, Diarrhea and Pneumonia), provision of Vitamins and deworming medications, vaccination coverage, provision of baby and delivery kits and psychosocial support.
The aim of this project is to contribute towards the reduction of morbidity and mortality rates among children under 5 years, adolescent boys and girls and pregnant women in the affected 6 districts in the NW (Kumbo East, Kumbo West, Ako, Ndu and Nkambe) SW (Mamfe) regions of Cameroon. This will be by improving access to basic curative and preventive health care services for vulnerable IDPs and host through provision of primary and secondary sexual and reproductive, mental health services, epidemiological surveillance and psychosocial support for 12 months.
And the specific objectives are;
• To improve timely access to basic curative and preventive health care services in children less than 5 years (3000M, 5000 M), pregnant women (1000) affected by crisis in 6 health districts in NW (Nkambe, Kumbo East, Kumbo West, Ndu, Ako) and SW (Mamfe) region for 12 months through consultation and treatment for 3 killer disease, distribution of LLINs, sensitization on Essential Family Practices.
• To strengthen epidemiological surveillance/ Vaccination coverage from below 50% to 80% in children 6-59months, adolescent boys and girls, pregnant women affected by crisis in 6 health districts in NW(Nkambe, Kumbo East, Kumbo West, Ndu, Ako )SW (Mamfe) region for 12 months through measles vaccination, outbreaks and search for loss to follow up children.
• To improve access to Sexual Reproductive Health services (Assisted deliveries by skilled personnel/ management of pregnancy related complications) in adolescent girls (500) and pregnant women (500) affected by crisis in 6 health districts in NW (Nkambe, Kumbo East, Kumbo West, Ndu, Ako) SW (Mamfe) region for 12 months through provision of baby and delivery kits, ANC cards.
• To provide mental health and psychosocial support to trauma victims affected by crisis in 6 health districts in NW (Nkambe, Kumbo East, Kumbo West, Ndu, Ako) SW (Mamfe) region for 12 months through referral and counter-referral of GBV survivors.
