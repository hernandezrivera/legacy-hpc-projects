Project Summary

The UNICEF Child Protection programme provides support to the extremely vulnerable, conflict-affected girls, boys and women in South Sudan who are exposed to child protection risks and gender-based violence. The programme includes providing case management and family tracing and reunification services, psychosocial support, reintegration interventions for children associated with armed forces and groups (CAAFAG), mine risk education (MRE), monitoring of grave child rights violations through the monitoring and reporting mechanism (MRM), gender based violence interventions, and critical, frontline emergency services, through integrated rapid response mechanisms (IRRMs). Within the programme budget $12m is needed for CPiE, $2.5m for GBV and $1m for MRE.

1 - Needs

Children continue to be disproportionately affected by the ongoing conflict, with high levels of population displacement and family separation affecting over one million children, causing severe psychological distress and leaving children vulnerable to exploitation, abuse and neglect. More than 20,000 children have been registered as unaccompanied, separated or missing since December 2013, with more than 8,600 cases still open. With the expansion of comprehensive case management (CCM) services in South Sudan in line with the newly endorsed standard operating practice (SOP) for CCM, upgrading and transiting from the current CPIMS database to CPIMS+, roll out of the CCM guidelines and coaching and supervision training for all implementing partners, further capacity building is still needed on identification and registration, FTR and interim care arrangements to ensure the continued protection of children. Since 2014, some 3,700 incidents of grave violations against children have been documented and verified by the UN. The CTFMR has recorded 651 incidents of recruitment and use of children which resulted in the number of 5,988 verified cases of children recruited and used in armed forces and armed groups between the first quarter of 2014 up until the second quarter of 2019, creating a high need for disarmament, demobilization and reintegration support. More than 3,200 informally and formally released children are receiving support through a 3-year community-based reintegration programme to fully rehabilitate and integrate them back into their communities. Girls are less likely to access school and PSS activities due to their traditional responsibilities in the home. UNICEF estimates that over 1m children are estimated to be suffering from profound psychosocial distress. Therefore, PSS activities in communities and schools are vital to help children deal with their experiences, and efforts are ongoing to strengthen community-based support mechanisms. While GBV remains underreported due to barriers to reporting and access to services, the level of GBV is very high across the country, with women and girls being exposed to rape, gang rape, sexual slavery, child marriage, conflict related sexual violence and other forms of GBV. The breakdown of traditional and formal protection mechanisms due to conflict, violence and displacement has significantly increased their vulnerability to various forms of GBV. For GBV incidents 2019: 98 per cent were women and girls and incidents included physical assault by an intimate partner 37 per cent, sexual violence 18 per cent, and emotional abuse 25 per cent. Increased risks, coupled with gaps in GBV service provision, necessitates the urgent need for strengthened GBV prevention, risk mitigation, and response services. Women and girls are particularly vulnerable to exploitation and abuse due to their low social and economic status and have reported exposure to sexual assault near PoC sites and when walking long distances to collect water, firewood, go to school or access distant services. The conflict has destroyed many support systems for survivors and impacted access to services; as such, there are high needs for continued support to GBV prevention and social norms transformation, risk mitigation and response. In communities, there is a need for women and girls to have safe spaces for sharing their experiences and accessing support. GBV interventions need to be integrated into all sectors of the humanitarian response as well as engage communities in harmful social norms transformation to address barriers and drivers of GBV.Girls formerly associated with armed groups and forces require additional support beyond the standard GBV interventions in order to re-integrate into the community. Such needs include specialized clinical management of sexual assault services, specialized mental health and PSS and economic empowerment including skills building and livelihood support.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Beneficiary rights will be legally recognized during the UNICEF project cycle. To ensure beneficiary rights in the intervention UNICEF uses different tools such as AAP, PSEA, rights based programming and many other tools and approaches to ensure that beneficiary rights are respected and legally recognized.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

To include perspectives of displacement-affected people, UNICEF, in collaboration with partners, will not interview or use any information without consent of any affected population including children and women themselves. The affected population, including children, youth and women, will be part of planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project. UNICEF also conducts perception surveys and KAP studies to better understand the population’s perception of dignity and whether there has been any improvement in that area during the intervention. UNICEF also conducts reviews and evaluation exercises on its goods and services such as critical CP supplies to ensure they are relevant, sustainable and serve the purpose of affected populations and their needs.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

The project is directly linked with ongoing policy systems related to protection and prevention from violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. The project is also directly linked to communities. UNICEF has conducted a detailed situation analysis through community based consultations including INGOs and NNGOs. Annual reviews, mid-year reviews and end country program reviews were conducted in 2018 as preparation for the next country program which covers three years, 2019-2021. UNICEF's annual work plan is approved by the Government, there are also state level action plans and child protection working groups where issues raised by communities can be resolved. Community based child protection committees also provide a mechanism for consultation with communities. Focus group discussions are another means of consultation used in child protection projects.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

UNICEF works closely with the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfare when planning and implementing its projects and ensures that its work is in line with national objectives and policies e.g. on child marriage, or children without appropriate parental care. The project is also mainly focusing on child protection system building during humanitarian action targeting community resilience and promotion of long term durable solution to host community, IDPs and returnees' population. The project is focusing on social work force strengthening in humanitarian action, establishment and strengthening of comprehensive case management system inline with CPIMS+ release and reintegration of children who are associated with armed forces and groups through community-based child protection networks.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Since the signing of the Peace Agreement there is better access to hard to reach areas, and improvement in security conditions in locations such as Central and Southern Unity, Upper Nile and Jonglei. However, the protection situation in the country still needs to be improved and a lot of technical operational support is needed to work with authorities and communities. To assess perceptions of improved safety, UNICEF, with partners, will conduct focus group discussions (FGDs), key informant interviews, KAP surveys, direct observation methods and other protection tools with communities to better understand their safety, security and protection risks and any improvement during the intervention. FGDs and other proposed methods are especially used in protection related humanitarian actions and focus on the physical safety, security and protection of accessing services. UNICEF does not interview or use any information without consent of any affected population including children and women. UNICEF supported the roll out of the IASC Guidelines on GBV in Humanitarian Action and has supported selected clusters to incorporate risk analysis, safety measures and strategies to mitigate risks to GBV. UNICEF worked with Cluster Coordinators and GBV focal agencies to provide technical support to Nutrition, WASH, Education and Child Protection AoR/Sub Cluster. UNICEF leads GBV risk mitigation awareness raising and training for selected Nutrition, WASH, and Child Protection actors. A GBV guide on risk mitigation and guidelines on Women and Girl Friendly Spaces (WGFS) have been rolled out. Safety audits are conducted, GBV safety perception surveys assess beneficiaries’ feelings of safety and community-based complaint mechanisms respond to beneficiaries’ complaints. UNICEF Health and Child Protection sections with Ministry of Health (MoH), GBV AoR/ Sub Cluster and UNFPA trained medical personnel from selected agencies as trainers of trainers on clinical management of rape (CMR).

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

UNICEF will ensure comprehensive community structuring, ownership and participation in the project activities to ensure that they can actually independently carry out activities and understand how to give such assistance as counselling, referral to the appropriate agencies, confidentiality of identified cases and proper procedures of handling cases of abuse or exploitation. UNICEF will also include the local authorities in the different training sessions to ensure that they can follow up on the CP protection practices, resolutions and minimum standards and hold to account any identified perpetrators of child abuse or exploitation.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

The CPiE and GBV programmes work with women and girls who are at risk of GBV on prevention and mitigation as well as response. The progamme consults boys, men, women and girls and minority or marginalized groups when planning implementing and giving feedback on interventions. The programme also works on mine risk education so although people are living in areas exposed to mines we raise awareness about them. We also raise awareness about availability of services including GBV services and orient communities about complaint and feedback mechanisms that they feel able to report. We are expanding the activities that we offer to youth/adolescents enabling them to have a voice in discussions and decision making.
