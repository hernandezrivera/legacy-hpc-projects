Mercy-USA is seeking to ensure the continued operation of these health facilities through sustained staffing, provision of medical supplies, data management, and reporting, finance, governance, training, monitoring, and oversight of program quality, and infrastructure maintenance support.
These facilities will provide obstetric and gynecological services, treatment for diarrhea, respiratory tract infections and skin conditions for boys and girls under five, treatment for chronic conditions including hypertension, asthma, cardiac ailments and psychosocial
support (PSS). The project, therefore, aims to continue to increase access to appropriate and impactful health services to address these urgent needs.

4 - Needs

Since 1st of December in just two months, more than 520,000 people have been displaced from their homes in Idleb, the vast majority of them being women and children.last month two separate attack on health care claiming 10 lives and injuring 30 people, with total 85 attacks on health facilities (health cluster) in 2019- 61 health facilities affected.- 12 health providers killed.All of that could be led to see outbreaks of measles, meningitis, and diarrhea. According to HNO the Health Sector Vulnerable Groups in northwest Syria :- Children Under 5: 1.43 million- Women of Reproductive Age (15-49): 3.22 million- Elderly (over 65): 336,369- Displaced Populations: 5.54 million- Persons living with a disability (estimation): 3.07 million.Disability according to the last assessment has found up to 27%.The last multi sectors need assessment survey has shown the need for improved supply of medicines and services within PHCs, particularly for non-communicable diseases (NCDs).Mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) needs are critical: prolonged exposure to conflict is believed to increase the prevalence of mental health conditions above the WHO global prevalence of 15 per cent26, yet stigma and shortages of trained health workers are treatment barriers. Where health structures are damaged or none exist, mobile teams are needed to fill gaps while repairs are addressed. Referral capacity, including ambulances, is needed to overcome gaps in secondary care and connect patients to humanitarian life-saving care. Specialized services are needed for physical rehabilitation, assistive devices, tuberculosis, dialysis, in-patient treatment of severe acute malnutrition with medical complications, and burns.Surveillance data for epidemic-prone diseases indicate that influenza-like illness, acute diarrhea, cutaneous leishmaniasis, and suspected hepatitis are the most common morbidities.

5 - Implementation of activities

Mercy-USA will be responsible for implementing the program in coordination with the health cluster and stakeholders. Mercy-USA will apply the Essential health package to effectively and efficiently deliver comprehensive quality health services that are equitable, accessible and sustainable, ensuring the timely delivery of cost-effective, integrated and standardized health services tailored to meet the priority health issues faced by the majority of the population. The quality and accountability will be monitoring by Mercy-USA to ensure meet the standards furthermore Mercy-USA will be responsible for all program and relevant financial report to the donor and to the cluster ( 4Ws ) and other related reports. All health staff will be trained on different health topics. The program will be monitored and managed by the program coordinator who will work directly from the Turkey office with the field’s staff to ensure the program is being correctly implemented. The selected health facilities will start when the fund is received. The health facilities will be divided between the health officers, each medical officer will supervise two health facilities providing administration and technical supervision. Mercy-USA supply chain department in coordination with our team in Syria and turkey will ensure all needed medical equipment and operational supplies are delivered on time. the Information Management officer will work with data entries and our health team to produce monthly information on the progress of the program which includes caseload information desegregated by cases/age, sex, gender, and disability. We provide services regardless of race, color, religion, creed, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, ancestry, age, and disability

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection /Risk/Threat: Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers Likelihood: High Impact: High Mitigating Measure: - Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare. Monitoring: Incidence Report Resources Required: security officer. Protection /Risk/Threat: Gender-Based Violence GBV Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigating Measure: - Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package. - Trained specialized health staff on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. - Safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - Recruit female staff in health facilities will increase access of women and girls to health services. Monitoring: Case Management / Reports Resources Required: Train the specialized health staff Protection /Risk/Threat: Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: Likelihood: Low Impact: High Mitigating Measure: -Mercy-USA maintains a ZERO TOLERANCE policy towards SEA in accordance with internationally-established protocols and standards for SEA protection. 7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: - Cross border access from Turkey is available. - Easy and smooth Logistical access. - Cooperation from local councils - Facilitation and acceptance from major regional stakeholders/actors (Turkish Government, OAG groups, etc.) - Timely fund dispersal - Targeting of most vulnerable beneficiaries - Recruitment of female staff within distribution teams will be considered. Risks: - General daily security threats including robbery and looting - Macro-level conflict-related threats including aerial bombardment, advancement of hostile actors, shifting alliances, border closure, and chemical attack. - Danger to staff and beneficiaries including the conflict between beneficiaries. - Delays in fund dispersal -Fire hazard and/or conflict-related activities happen during the ongoing implementation. Risk Mitigation: - Following Mercy-USA safety and security guidelines and established safety protocols. - In the event of major macro-level conflict-related threats, Mercy-USA will take appropriate action that is commensurate with the scale of the threat. This may include: - Suspending operations temporarily -Shifting resources and operations to more secure locations - Clear steps will be taken to mitigate risks between staff and beneficiaries including gender based violence. - Project staff may be dismissed at any time in the event of endangering beneficiaries or other staff, and this will be clearly outlined in each employment contract. - Contingency stock will be in place for shipments to avoid any impact if Bab Alhawa borders become closed (for items purchased from Turkey) - Gender balance and equal opportunities will be taken into consideration when hiring staff for a different position. -All workers will be given the proper needed instructions ahead of any deliveries. -All necessary precautions will be taken to prevent a fire incident at the health facilities.
