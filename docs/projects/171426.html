Project Summary

A rapid assessment conducted by NCA in February 2019 revealed critical gaps in 18 schools supported by NCA and partners in Belet Hawa, Dollow and Luuq. About 80% of these schools are in rural and hard to reach areas and have up to 3,500 children (49% girls) enrolled in schools. While numerous inequities exist within these locations, the most socially excluded groups are rural children (particularly those from pastoralist communities), from households with the lowest wealth quintiles (including ‘urban poor’), children with disabilities and children from IDP households.
A Gender Analysis conducted by NCA (Dec 2018) found that Education is highly gendered and boys and girls do not receive the same priority for education (gender parity ratio of 0.9). Boys are usually prioritized over girls and this is exacerbated in times of hardship and crisis where boys will most often be prioritized over girls for education. Girls continue to face more barriers to education than boys including protection related risks for those who have to walk long distances to and from school, some girls face ridicule for attending school especially when they reach puberty, menstrual hygiene management is not a part of the curriculum and latrines and WASH related needs are not adequately met for adolescent girls in schools and don’t consider inclusion aspects for children with disability. The gender analysis further indicated that perceptions of what work women can do is highly gendered and translates to a lack of meaningful work for women and limited space to fully exercise economic empowerment. This is clearly demonstrated in the few number of female teachers present in most schools in Somalia (data from 10 schools assessed by NCA in 2020 reveal teacher gander parity ratio of 0.3). This in turn impacts on girls’ retention in schools as they lack a female role model they can look up to for support and advice.
The rapid assessment indicated that 65% of the assessed 18 schools had temporary learning spaces that needed major repairs to ensure a conducive learning environment. Additionally, all the assessed schools needed additional learning spaces to reduce overcrowding; and learners in these schools did not have frequent access to learning materials which are critical for improved learning outcomes. About 10 of the assessed schools did not have adequate gender segregated latrines compromising the health and safety of learners and teachers and also lacked disability friendly infrastructure to enable easy access for children with disabilities. Various protection related issues such as harassment of girls in and out of school, lack of awareness on PSEA among students and teachers as well as child friendly complaints and response mechanisms hinder these schools from being safe and protective learning environments. Children living with disabilities within school settings face discriminatory treatment both consciously and unconsciously from other students as well as teachers. This manifested in ways such as social and educational exclusion, psychological harm and increased risk of experiencing violence.In many cases, children reported being reluctant to attend school, or dropping out.
Through this project, NCA will provide life-saving education response for boys and girls in rural and IDP settings. The project will seek to address the above-mentioned education needs and gaps in 18 schools across three districts namely Luuq, Dollow and Belet-hawa assessed by NCA and which host a significant number of children from most vulnerable and hard to reach rural areas. A total of 3,500 school aged girls and boys (49% girls) among which 433 are children with disabilities(219 boys: 214 girls)from affected populations will be supported to access safe and quality education opportunities; the capacity of 180 teachers in supported learning institutions will be enhanced to deliver quality education; and 90 education committees strengthened to effectively manage schools

Project Activities

The proposed project will respond to the following Objectives in line with Education Cluster Response Strategy 2021:1. Ensure emergency and crises affected children and youth have access to a safe and protective learning environment• Conduct quarterly social mobilization campaigns in Belet Hawa, Dollow and Luuq on importance of education with a focus on girls and children living with disabilities in four districts• Rehabilitate of 54 temporary learning classrooms in targeted 18 schools benefiting 3,500 school children (49% girls of whom 12% are children with special needs• Construction of 18 blocks of gender segregated latrines with 4 stances per block in each school benefiting 3,500 school children (49% girls of whom 12% are children with special needs• Conduct Mapping of the child protection services in three districts (Belet hawa, Dollow, Luuq)• Establish referral mechanism in 18 school for children in need including children with disability to access protection services.• Establish child friendly and accessible Complaints and Response Mechanism in each of the 18 targeted schools 2. Ensure vulnerable children and youth are engaged in lifesaving learning that promotes personal well-being and social cohesion• Provision of teaching and Learning materials to 3,500 learners (47% girls) including 12% children with disability• Provide monthly emergency incentives for 100 teachers (40% female)• Connect water in 10 rural/IDP schools 3. Strengthened capacity to deliver effective and coordinated education in emergencies preparedness and response within the education system• Conduct training for 180 teachers on inclusive, protective and socio-emotional teaching & learning; psychosocial support, PSEA & COVID 19 prevention and response• Conduct training for 90 CECs on school management, child protection, PSEA, School safety and COVID 19 prevention and response• Conduct training for 20 staff and partners on emergency preparedness and response planning and coordination

Delivery Mechanism

Cash - Mobile

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

This Project will have a CTP component focusing on Teachers' incentives payment for 100 teachers that will be engaged in 18 targeted schools. The payment will be done through Hormuud mobile cash transfer. This will minimize the risk of loss of funds or funds being paid to wrong beneficiaries. NCA is currently using this modality to pay teachers and other beneficiaries in Education programming and this has proved to be an effective way as the funds are used for the intended purpose.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The use of mobile cash transfer modality through Hormuud is a common and contextual friendly modality because it is accessible to the local population and most community members in Gedo have access to phones and use Hormuud tarrif for communication and money transfer. Additionally, teachers are able to redeem the cash in hormuud outlets that are available in the market.
