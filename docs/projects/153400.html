Project Summary

This project will improve the capacity of vulnerable farmers in Budi County to address food insecurity in the county through provision of seeds and tools, support to livelstock keepers, treatment of livestock, provision of model Fish pond, training of farmers and vegetable growing farmers to addrsss food insecurity. The project will target 15,000 beneficiaries as to improve food security situation in the County.

Needs

Budi County is one of the bread baskets of Eastern Equatoria. It is however unfortunate that the current conflict has disrupted the livelihoods of the people and particularly agricultural activities coupled by inter communal conflicts and cattle rustling. The Budi County is inhabited by two ethnic communities Buya and Didinga but surrounded by Tenet and Lotuho of Lopa/Lafon and Torit in the North, Logiri in the Western part and Southern part is the Lawroma and Dodoth of Uganda. The Eastern part and North East of the County is bordered by Toposa of Kapoeta South and Kapoeta North Counties. the County is also bordered by Kenya and Uganda. The population projection of the County for 2018 is put at 116,119 people with over 33,000 Households. The People of Budi are Agro-pastoralist and Pastoralist. The geographical locations are categorized into two the high lands and the lowlands where the former is where agricultural activities and gold mining are practiced and the later though farming activities take place have been pone to drought and occasional rainfall delays. The Highlands of recent have been affected by poor rainfalls due to human destruction of forest for farming activities. Last year the area had a serious rainfall delays and farm produces were completely reduced in yields. The WFP General and Conditional food distribution intervention support 37000 for last year and this year 28,000 was not sufficient enough to close the gap. According to the IPC Key Messages update report (28 September, 2018), household food security remains primarily dependent on subsistence agriculture and livestock rearing. Food security in South Sudan has further deteriorated due to armed conflict, economic crisis, and below-average harvests that were exhausted well before the on-going lean season. An estimated 6.1 million (59% of the population) people are expected to be severely food insecure in January -March 2019, compared to 5.5 million (45% of the population) people in May 2017; the on-going conflict has worsen humanitarian situation with thousands of people forced to neighbouring countries and POC centres. However, returning IDPs and refugees, as well as resident communities, are facing limited availability of agricultural inputs and services to support crop production and livestock rearing. This is compounded by poor quality healthcare and education, inadequate infrastructure and fragmented markets clearly, the restoration of livelihoods has had setback. In the recent FSNMS round 22nd back Office report in July 2018 by team1 indicated that malnutrition among less than 5 years in Budi is alarming. The dilapidated rainfall Patten and poor traditional farming practices have reduced yield and food security situation needs could rise. On- going fighting amongst tribes and sub-clans, and the resultant displacements, destruction and deaths continue to hinder efforts to early recovery process. Communities are faced with crop failure, limited access to food and basic services with escalating food and fuel prices. Cattle raiding in Eastern Equatoria have made livelihoods unbearable. The majority of farmers have to maintain backyard gardening in fear of attacks and targeted killing. The project will address these needs of food insecurity through support to the affected farmers who have not been able to meet some of their production needs through a set of activities to diversify their livelihoods and food insecurity. The project will be linked with other existing project such as FAO Emergency distribution of seeds and tools, DanChrchAid Addressing the Root Causes Food Security and Peace Building project and other community initiatives in addreessing food Security and Livelihoods. CDSS will ensure working with the groups and other stakeholders projects to meet the objectives and outcomes of this intervention through a number of activities that are stipulated in this proposal.

Those who have email address also provide the information through a designated focal point person who have access to the e-mail address alone and record the information on a 'Protected' complaint data sheet. Where telephone communication is workable, the complainer can also call the focal person directly and share his or her complaint and feed back is provided accordingly as may be prefered by the complainer.
