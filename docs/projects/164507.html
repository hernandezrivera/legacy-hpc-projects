Project Summary

Resulting in ADRA’s presence in the targeted areas of Homs, Dera’a and Deir Al Zour, severe protection concerns and gaps in the humanitarian response have been identified, particularly regarding people with special needs, and in the newly accessible areas of Deir Al Zour. Based on these identified needs, ADRA will implement a variety of protection activities in line with the protection sector objective 1, targeting a total of 32,000 individuals, 12,000 of whom have special needs. ADRA will implement the project in close cooperation with the Ministry of Social Affairs and the Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) in the target areas.

4 - Needs

The humanitarian crisis has had a devastating impact on the civilian population with more than 8 in 10 people living below the poverty line. Prolonged conflict and multiple displacements have resulted in high unemployment rates (estimated at 75%), a loss of livelihoods, school dropout, wide scale infrastructural damage and, with the trend to return to one’s area of origin, increased pressure on local community resources.DeZ for example is composed of highly vulnerable groups with some areas consisting of 60% of the households identifying as female-headed and over 50% of the population as returnees, many of whom have experienced multiple displacements (ADRA Syria Dec 2019, Key stakeholder interviews). Large-scale protection concerns were identified across DeZ, Homs and Deraa governorates (ADRA Syria, Nov 2019, key stakeholder interviews), including in former hard to reach areas where extremist occupation caused frequent SGBV (particularly for women, girls and boys), forced recruitment into the armed forces (men and boys), forced marriage to ISIS members (women and girls) and rape. This has contributed to up to 60% of households to be female-headed across DeZ governorate as combatants fled the country following changes in control, and breadwinners perished in combat or were left with permanent disabilities. The rise in number of people with disabilities throughout the crisis was also caused by UXOs, posing significant threats to vulnerable groups such as children who play outside and in uncleared areas as well as untreated injuries and health concerns, especially for children and older adults. Women, men, boys and girls of all ages are all at risk of UXOs during water collection, particularly in rural areas where the ordinances are often hidden in grassy areas of piles of rubble or debris. A lack of street lighting was reported to lead to increased levels of SGBV, particularly among women, boys and girls, as reported to ADRA Syria field staff.

5 - Implementation of activities

The proposed activities fall within Objective 1, the protection of population is improved through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions. Within that objective, ADRA Syria plans to rehabilitate disability centres, distribute assistive devices to persons with speciﬁc protection needs and provide street lighting in high-risk areas. These activities address the extensive destruction of infrastructure and services and will promote safer and more inclusive humanitarian response in areas of origin and help reduce the incidences of SGBV (Protection HNO 2020). ADRA Syria will target an estimated 12,000 individuals with the rehabilitation of three disability centres, in Homs, Deraa and Deir Al Zour and provide 250 people with disabilities with assistive devices. People with disabilities are not always adequately taken into account in programming, and the planned assistance and centers will reduce the barriers in accessing assistance. The Ministry of Social Affairs who will be responsible for the management and running of the centers following rehabilitation. The centers will provide a variety of services including education activities, PSS activities, health activities, vocational training and life skills activities as well as counselling services, recreational activities and child therapy activities. These services will address the lack of services for people with disabilities, and provide individual support. ADRA will coordinate the selection of beneﬁciaries with SARC and the newly rehabilitated centers who has close community links and a detailed understanding of individuals protection needs within the targeted community including the inclusion of children, adolescents, adults, and older adults, both male and female. Streetlights in Deir Al Zour will reduce the protection risks particularly for women, boys and girls for 20,000 individuals in selected areas of high female headed households, lack of street lights and high population movement.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

There is a risk of inter or intra community tension due to services or activities offered if one community and a neighboring community not served/cannot access or if information offered is not consistent between communities as there may be perceptions of discrimination or favoritism. (Likelihood: Low; Impact: Medium) Mitigation: Before a decision is made to establish a new service, ADRA staff will make use of existing tools/ mapping and coordinate at local level with other protection actors to ensure information sharing with community members on services available and balanced coverage responding to needs; avoid overlapping or concentration of activities if possible and unless motivated by needs; consider focusing on underserved/rural areas as well as projected return areas and work with national actors and community based groups to ensure sustainability of the intervention. There is a risk that community / individual tensions may occur if the demand for assistive devices is larger than the available amount. (Likelihood: Low; Impact: Medium) Mitigation: ADRA will work closely with local stakeholders to ensure a clear selection criterion for the selection of assistive devices and that the most vulnerable will receive the assistance available. Referral mechanisms will also be established where possible to provide additional support where possible. There is a risk that beneficiaries or staff will be exposed to sexual exploitation or abuse. (Likelihood: Low; Impact: High). Mitigation: All ADRA staff are required to sign the code of conduct, as well as undertake training in protection principles. Staff will go to the field in gender balanced teams to ensure accessibility, accountability and prevent any issues arising. In addition, an established complaints mechanism is available and will be provided to beneficiaries to report any concerns or issues with special care taken to ensure the inclusion of children, adolescents, adults and older adults.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Several potential risks have been identified that may impact the success of the project. One is the changing security situation. To mitigate this risk, ADRA Syria will monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct security assessments. ADRA will also choose locations which have been stable for some time and unlikely be directly impacted by renewed conflict. If needed, ADRA will negotiate with the donor to confirm the flexibility to change the project locations as required. (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes). On the other hand, as the proposed project is mainly community-based level, and targets mostly people with disabilities, it is unlikely to create protection risks of project beneficiaries. However, these is a slight risk that by reopening disability centres, individuals will be exposed to protection concerns either on the way to and from the centres. ADRA will select centres in safe locations that will not result in increased exposure to danger for the beneficiaries. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes). Delays in obtaining the necessary approvals. To mitigate this ADRA will maintain strong connections with all levels of stakeholders to follow-up on necessary permissions and approvals and discuss any delays with the Ministry of Social Affairs or Local Administration and Environment (Likelihood: Medium; Acceptable level: Yes). Also, another risk is that the procurement of supplies and materials could be subject to corruption or conflict of interest, to mitigate this ADRA Syria has developed and follows a clear procurement policy under which all purchases and contracting takes place. The policy includes a clear segregation of duties, levels of authorization, and conflict of interest declaration to help prevent fraud, corruption and conflict of interest. (Likelihood: Poor; Acceptable level: Yes).
