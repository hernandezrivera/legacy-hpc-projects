Project Summary

1) Basic Services and security (first layer):
- Setup a system of identification and referral to the designated services of mental health, child protection and GBV related cases; monitor the effective care needs; observe possible existing gaps in the overall response mechanism and report them to the designated institution/actors/platform? (Cluster, coordination meetings…)
- Encourage humanitarian actors to address the aforementioned gaps in order to offer services that promote basic mental health and psychosocial well-being of the population;
2) Community and family support (second layer):
- Enhance community and relevant stakeholders (leaders) sensitizations on child protection, GBV, psychosocial and mental health problems and care. This activity would aim not only at preventing the occurrence of such incidents but also at creating an environment in which these issues, usually stigmatized, could be addressed and explained. conductive continue providing recreational activities and life skills trainings – especially for children and adolescent – as a tool for processing, catharsis, strengthening skills, building resilience and empowering them in in their daily life;
- Reinforce local coping mechanisms for individual and mutual support in the community.
- Promote a community referral system for people in need to the adequate services available in the area.
- Create/rebuilt DIAP- and train 60 Community Protection Workers (CPW) that will provide to the children a protected space in which it will be possible to identify child protection related cases (in particular separated and unaccompanied minors and children presenting psychological distress) and refer them to the designated institution/actors/services;
- Establish a Community protection system that will provide a sustainable community psychosocial response mechanism. This system will be implemented by the community protection workers (4 per each DIAP:H&F) that will work both in the DIAP and within the community to sensitize, identify and refer to the designated services child protection, GBV and mental health related cases;
3) Focused non-specialized support (third layer):
Build and consolidate a basic psychosocial response mechanism and a reference system focused on psychological, child protection and GBV related cases. This mechanism will be implement by:
• Psychosocial Mobile Teams (including 6 Protection facilitators working in the DIAPs for each team?) that will be activated in emergency situations (e.g., sudden new displacement) to provide psychosocial emergency support;
• Health workers of primary health centers trained on mental disorders detection and drug administration that will make available - in short term and as the only feasible and sustainable measure at the moment – an immediate emergency response to the psychosocial and mental health problems caused by the current acute crisis.
4) Specialized services (fourth layer):
- Continue the integration of mental health care into primary care by integrating – as a temporary emergency measure – 4 psychologist officers in the targeted health centers to train the staff and offer an emergency psychological response to severe cases.
A psychiatrist will help in the training and supervision of the health workers (medical doctors, nurses and midwives) mental health disorders detection and prescription of psychotropic drugs and of MHPSS Specialist (public mental health expert who is also a clinician)
The project will also benefit from technical expatriates’ staff, in particular. He will be providing direct technical support in terms of trainings, activity plans, supervision, case managements, project management, and, in collaboration with the head of mission, will act as link with the other actors involved...

1 - Besoins, Activités et Resultats Attendus

The humanitarian gaps in protection services have been strongly stated by OCHA that underlines the centrality of the access of affected populations to life-saving assistance in the Lake Chad region.COOPI’s observations of the intervention during the previous phases show that the beneficiaries of the Lake province have experienced and continue to experience difficulties and traumatic situations, such as physical violence, murder, kidnapping and prostitution, and that a large majority of the displaced persons face symptoms of psychological distress In this environment and following radical changes in community structures, loss of family’s member and means of subsistence., the mental health and psychosocial problems of the populations are permanently at risk of deterioration. Children are exposed to even higher risk of mental health and psychosocial problems, such as PTSD (Post Traumatic Stress Disorder), depression, general anxiety, sleep disturbances, or learning difficulties, attachment disorders, inappropriate or anti-social behavior, etc. In line with the previous phases, during this extension the multi-component approach will go beyond basic psychosocial care activities and will require a continue provision of specialized mental health support. The totality of the community actors will be called upon to support the program in the successful establishment of an effective community-based approach that commits everyone to get involved in the dynamics of care for all levels.The data collected during the previous phase recorded 571 individuals (of which 357 were females) requiring a form of psychosocial support and 104 individuals (of which 65 were females) suffering from mental disorders and the first quarter of this phase lead us to believe that the MHPSS needs are still considerable. According to WHO in a population affected by crisis, severe mental health problems can reach up to 10% of the population and moderate to mild mental health problems 20%. These data supports the prediction that mental health needs in the province are at their peak. In fact, the psychological consultations provided during the past phases showed that symptoms related to PTSD as well as depression and anxiety are the most commonly reported and represent respectively 42.5% and 28.1% of the reported cases of mental health conditions; whereas convulsive disorders represent 23.5% and psychotic disorders 7.2% . Moreover, although epilepsy is a neurological disease, its occurrence can be aggravated by repeated exposure to traumatic circumstances. Despite the efforts and results of the program, there is still much to be done in terms of filling the treatment gap for mental health problems in the Lake Province, especially considering the lack of a National policy.Since the start of the Boko Haram crisis, the humanitarian situation in the province in terms of security and infrastructure has been in perpetual deterioration; that of IDPs is more precarious in general and in particular for children who are called upon to face new challenges on a daily basis. According to the data collected in 2019 from COOPI’s projects in the three departments (Mamdi, Kaya and Fouli), COOPI has identified and supported 462 (226 girls) children with specific needs (EBS). The Population movements continue, especially in the northwestern part of Baga sola, in the department of Kaya as well as in the department of Fouli. Considering the high concentration of displaced people in these two departments (84% of the Province) and given that IDPs constitute OFDA’s priority in Chad, this 4th phase will focus its intervention in Kaya and Fouli,. the two departments which register the largest number of displaced persons including population movements.Thousands of internally displaced persons have been distributed in the communities of the Lake Chad region since the start of the crisis in 2015. And IDPs figures are still increasing: according to the last UNHCR report in October 2019 there was 133,338 internally displaced people while, according to the International Organization for Migration (IOM), this figure stands at 169,003 living (November, 2019). In this context, the department of Fouli has the largest percentage of displaced persons is 48%. According to the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA)’s last assessment (14 February 2020, OCHA) of the informal site of Malmari, Malmari village and Fourkouloum reported 11,300 IDPs.Protection issues such as gender-based violence (2 210 cases in2019) , EBS are increased and exacerbated due to multiple attacks and military operations. COOPI's action during this extension aims at strengthening the resilience of displaced people within a new site in order to improve their psychosocial well-being as well as their mental health. COOPI will focus more on sites less served in terms of humanitarian actions A total of 1,181 protection incidents were recorded in the 202 lake sites between January and December 2019 . At the same time, out of 88 protection incidents reported during February 2020, armed groups were allegedly responsible for 54 % of them, especially for kidnapping and homicide cases.It is important to note that the 20% of incidents concern homicides and 19% kidnappings, and that 92% of homicides and 60% of kidnappings concern men. 90% of the incidents perpetrated concern IDPs. This high percentage of homicide cases emphasizes the need for an extension of this project, in particular with regard to the mental health, since the population is living of communities, families and individuals. This extension will specifically focus on a totally community-based approach to mental health.In the extension, we will continue to target 11 health facilities (1 in Mamdi's department; 4 in Kaya’s department; 6 in Fouli’s department). To date, for the first quarter of this 3rd phase, our medical partners have already identified and continue to take care of 104 new cases. These results already achieved need to be consolidated in order to reach as many beneficiaries as possible and provide good quality service.Children, defined as persons under the age of 18, are exposed to higher risks in crisis situations due to their age and developmental stage. Children may be more vulnerable in a crisis situation, like the current BH triggered crisis, due to disruptions of normal care giving practices, schooling, or other factors. Children living with disabilities are doubly vulnerable and particularly exposed to violence, exploitation, and abuse. Disasters may also introduce new threats or exacerbate existing threats to children. The threats in the area continue in particular in the 2 departments where the activities of this phase are oriented (Fouli and Kaya). Following the latest attacks on positions of forces loyal to Bohma in the sub-prefecture of Kaiga Kindjiria, the DTM report of March 26 2020 reported more than 2,000 displaced persons, including 40 children separated from their families at the Yakirom site. This situation has only worsened the already difficult situation of the population and its movements in the sub-prefecture of Ngouboua where both the OCHA evaluation report and the humanitarian actors registered +/- 11,300 IDPs in the sites of Malmari, Fourkouloum and Malmari village. That is why the protection of these children is a predominant aspect of the intervention COOPI proposes to carry on. Health care cases that required specialized services are referred to the community health centers or main hospital in Bol or Baga-sola by the International Medical Corps (IMC) or the International Rescue Committee (IRC), depending on the case’s location. The World Food Programme (WFP) is responsible for food distribution 1 time every 3 months. IDPs are carrying out fishing activities in the waters around them at the exception of the area where fishing and the sale of fishery products are prohibited and many have engaged in other income generating activities such as sheep and poultry rearing. They have erected their shelters from local materials such as elephant grass stocks, locally made mats and ropes made out of palms leaves. Dedicated mental health legislation does not exist in Chad however; some efforts are being made at National level. During this extension, COOPI will continue its advocacy with the Ministry for the adoption of a law on the protection of people suffering from mental disorders and on the dissemination of monitoring tools. In this extension phase of the program, COOPI will multiply contacts with the National staff to participate in training for the implementation of the mhGAP-HIG and in the supervision of field activities. Legal provisions concerning mental health are still not covered in other laws (e.g. welfare, disability, general health legislation etc.). Mental disorders are grouped as ‘other disorders’, there are no special programs for the population, regardless of their group identity (IDPs or victims of BH crisis) Prescription regulations authorize primary health care doctors to prescribe and/or to continue prescription of psychotropic medicines but with restrictions. The majority of primary health care nurses have not received official in-service training on mental health within the last five years. Officially approved manuals on the management and treatment of mental disorders are available in the majority of primary health care clinics. Official policy does not permit primary health care nurses to independently diagnose and treat mental disorders within the primary care system. Official referral procedures for referring persons from primary care to secondary/tertiary care do not exist. Referral procedures from tertiary/secondary care to primary care do not exist.According to WHO, mental health in Chad is not part of the primary health care system. Actual treatment of severe mental disorders is not available at the primary level. Very few psychotropic are included in the essential drug list and treatment is difficult. Regular training of primary care professionals is not carried out in the field of mental health. There are no community care facilities for patients with mental disorders. Only traditional treatments are available at community level. • Total psychiatric beds per 10 000 population: 0.02• Psychiatric beds in mental hospitals per 10 000 population: 0.01• Psychiatric beds in general hospitals per 10 000 population: 0.01• Psychiatric beds in other settings per 10 000 population: 0• Number of psychiatrists per 100 000 population: 0.01• Number of psychiatric nurses per 100 000 population: 0.01• Number of psychologists per 100 000 population: 0.01 The above data demonstrate the gap that needs to be filled in the Chadian Mental Health Sector. Other data, for e.g. on the expenditures for medicines for mental and behavioral disorders at country level or information / referral system, informal resources (family and users associations) are not available. That stresses the non-prioritization of the sector, in spite of the needs (made obvious by partners – including COOPI – above mentioned field analysis). This justifies the action that COOPI would like to realize, especially in regards of the components related to large-scale sensitization, training of health workers and authorities, provision of drugs and establishment of referral systems. In addition, bearing in mind the community approach considered by COOPI as a priority in the field of mental health COOPI will initiate and consolidate regular dialogue with traditional healers In the previous phase we have formed 37 health workers, 4 of whom were doctors and 7 managers (men) on mhGAP-HIG module. This module, established by the WHO that, aims to fill the gap- in conformity with the specificity of the country and/or region of the intervention- concerning mental health.During this training, the Mental Health Team (MHT) structure of the health delegation, the health district and health centers has been established In order to act as a Mental Health Focal Point in the Province.During the extension phases, we will continue training its members and will add more health workers from the 3 additional health facilities. The main objectives of these trainings are:• Supporting the effective transfer of skills in order to ensure the proper execution of Mental Health intervention and identify the areas that need further trainings;• Ensuring that the necessary administrative procedures (referrals and follow-up) are executed;• Ensuring that the supply of psychotropic drugs and other support systems are operational;• Encouraging the respectful, non-judgmental attitudes and ethical treatment of individuals;• Providing support and monitoring the healthcare providers experiencing stress;• Boosting the implementation of mental health program into the Chadian health system and preparing the MoH in managing this program in the long term.COOPI will now extend training to other key actors of community-based health systems in Chad, specifically COGES and Community Health Workers (RECO) and establish dialogue with traditional healers. The following therapeutic drugs are generally available at the primary health care level of the country: carbamazepine, phenobarbital, chlorpromazine, diazepam, haloperidol, levodopa. The present intervention proposes to provide Health Facilities and Bol Hospital with additional drugs that are quoted as relevant by experts in the context (see list attached) (WHO, 2008). Because of the budget limitations, we will not be able to help the MoH in providing secure places for severe cases with mental health disorders (There is no space for hospitalizing severe cases with acute reactions), however by providing equipment to the targeted Health Facilities, they will be able to provide temporary assistance to patients needing hospitalization. In the facilities targeted by COOPI, data registration of mental health cases is being integrated in the MoH official registers. However, a correct and efficient system of patient’s registration and follow up does not exist and its establishment will serve as a key tool in the integration of mental health within the primary health package. Also, COOPI will continue to interact with the MoH to support them in the adoption and implementation of a National Mental Health Policy.
