Project Summary

This project aim to provide treatment for 5785 children < 5 years of which 2066 SAM, 2389 MAM boys and girls and 1330 PLWs through outpatient and therapeutic supplementary feeding Programme (TSFP). It is intended to scale up nutrition intervention to reduce nutrition related mortality and morbidity among children < 5 years and PLWs in Talodi, Elliri, Abu karshola, Umdorein and Heiban localities in South Kordufan state.
It will provide immediate lifesaving nutrition services to affect population and those living in newly accessible areas which will include Treatment for SAM and MAM among children < 5years and PLWs through MUAC screening and referral OTPs centers and stabilization center for SAM with complication while support in training, rehabilitate OTP centers, provided needed equipment and supplies., support 10 NIPP and 10 IYCF groups, community mobilization, awareness raising events, networks, community based child protection, GBV referral from the OTPs/TSFP center and during the MUAC screening and referral cases to specific service providers (Psychosocial support, medical treatment etc).
It is expected that the described project activities are expected to ensure that children < 5 years with SAM and MAM access to treatment and have improved household child nutritional behaviorial practices and reflected in the daily lives of children < 5 years and PLWs and prevent future reoccurrence of the acute malnutrition, change dietary behavior through improve of dietary diversity, achieve sustainable improvements in family, health care and feeding practices through upgrade positive behavior address and mitigate the risk towards food insecurity and reduction of malnutrition problems in the target families.
The project will respond specifically to children < 5 years and PLW, and family members including men, girls and boys, elderly and people with special needs. ASSIST will set a benchmark to collect, analyse and use sex, age disaggregated data, 60% of staff will be women, 80% of staff will complete basic gender training and learning sessions, and 100% of the project reports will have specific gender results and lessons learned.
Secondly, we will ensure that 100% of the project activities have resource allocation to achieve IASC Gender with Age marker code 4(M) by the end of 2020. ASSIST will also have financial tracking mechanism to quantify funds disbursement for promoting gender and women empowerment.
3% of the project grant will be cash based disbursement to SAM with medical complication, PLWs and NIPP groups as Unconditional and unrestricted transfers as it gives them the chance to chose and prioritize their needs. especial SAM cases with medical complication referred for medical treatment and ensure mothers and care givers prioritise their daily needs that meet child treatment concerns.
Through the project ASSIST will ensure functioning complaints and feedback mechanisms as an accountability to affected beneficiaries who receive assistance or participate in the project implementation, gender and age dimensions are integrated into 86 percent of Country Strategic Plan, and in 63 percent of ASSIST guidance manuals

1 - Needs

This concept note is bases on S3M II 2018 findings, Inter-agency Assessment report finding conducted in Talodi, Elliri,and Abu karshola and HNO 2020 baseline data for Um-dorein and Abujebaha localities in South Kordufan state from October 2018 – 27 June 2019. In Talodi, Abujehaba and Um-dorein locality there are 16 health facilities integrating with nutrition service but only 6 health facilities (37.5%) are working. The referral system is not functioning, in additional to lack of regular supply in routine medicine for U<5 years. S3M II results indicated that GAM rate by MUAC measurement stood at 18.6%, which are above the emergency threshold of 15% according to the WHO criteria. The community outreach activities are lacking, absence of trained volunteers to mobilize and refer SAM cases to OTPs and there is no regular MUAC Screening in villages where IDPs, returnees and host communities settled. CMAM services are not accessible in most villages in Um-dorein,Toladi and Leri due to the long distance to the Health facilities as they were far from their residential settlements with a walking distance of 3-10 Km, there are limited infant Young child feeding activities and most PLWs practice poor feeding practice contribute to high GAM, SAM and MAM rates among children under 5 years and Pregnant lactating mothers (PLWs). While in Um-doerin, Elliri and Toladi locality, the result indicates that nutrition situation remains poor and people dependent on humanitarian assistance to meet their basic food needs. Most of the interviewed households and PLWs complained of lack of adequate food at household level and shortage of critical nutrition supplies. There are very few trained volunteers among the IDPs to undertake nutrition services; only 2 out of 5 (40%) of areas had volunteers from host community doing nutrition activities. No IYCF and Mother support group’s activities, high cases of premature weaning among infants are fed semi-solid and solid food, which is not recommended. According to FEWS NET September 2019, Food security outcomes are expected to deteriorate to Crisis (IPC Phase 3) in parts of South Kordufan States especially in SPLM-N areas, in Umdorein, Abujebaha, Toladi and Alliri localities as people can’t meet minimum food needs with severe coping strategies ie. many households reduced meals from 3 to 1 per day, PLWs are poorly feed that have led to extreme food consumption gaps result in high levels of acute malnutrition especially for the most vulnerable families and children. According to MUAC conducted in Abu Karshola from Jan-May 2019 a total of 15,255 children under 5 years were screened using MUAC. 13,061(85.6%) were found to be Normal, 2,039(13.4%) were moderately malnourished (MAM) 155(1%) were severely malnourished (SAM) there is a shortage of nutrition supplies to cover basic OTPs needs during the rainy season, no stabilization centers (SC), no IYCF activities implemented in PHC and most children under 5 years and PLWs have limited access to OTP. Based on S3M II 2018, Um-dorein, Abujebaha, Talodi, Elleri and Abu Karshola localities have; • GAM rate are 17.80%, 7.10% and 7.66%.• SAM rate of 5.01%, 1.04% and 6.45%• MAM rates; 12.78%, 6.02% and 1.31%) MAM• Stunting of 10.76%, 16.51% and 25.74% of children U5, • Wasting rate of 1.55%, 0.44% and 0.19% among U5 children respectively. While HNO 2020 baseline data for Umdorin and Abujebaha show PLWs GAM 8.35, Under 5 GAM 8.13, U5-SAM 0.93, Stunting 36.8, ranking 5 and Nutrition sector severity 3. In summary Toladi, Elliri, Abukersola, Abujehaba and Um-dorein have U5 SAM 265, 139, 85, 524, and 421, U5 MAM 2781, 3490, 1286, 5228 and 4198, PLWs Moderate Acute Malnutrition of 303, 931, 1415, 1952 and 1567 respectively.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

We will integrate the environmental concerns into this nutrition project programming which will include formation of (NIPP) groups and involve them home gardening practice to grow vegetables, fruits which provides households with sources of income instead of tree cutting firewood, introduce the fuel efficiency stove (FES) to reduce use of firewood, teach mothers to pre-soak beans before cooking, introduce environmental awareness raising session in the OTPS/TSFP centers with leaflets messages, while for OTPs rehabilitation/reconstruction we will promote the use Stabilized Soil Blocks (SSB) or Cement Blocks (CSB) to replace the use of burnt bricks which will discourage tree cutting and environmental pollution.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

4=Fully in place

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

During design and implementation we will ensure consideration for protective spaces for women and children to provide a safe environment where recreational activities for children, hygiene and nutrition promotion, food demonstration, and psychosocial support can take place and finally, a confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system has been designed and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents of rights violations The day, time, and location of the distribution has been discussed with different community members (age, gender, disability) to determine when/where would be the safest, Working hours during which services are provided are appropriate for all persons, including women, children, and the elderly, the day, time, and location of the distribution has been clearly communicated using a variety of approaches to all distribution recipients, including those who may have difficulty accessing information points.Adequate levels of female staff with skills and experience working with women and with children exist, vulnerable individuals who may need assistance during distributions eg. pregnant women, chronically ill, disabled) have been identified and prioritized by placing them first on distribution lists or placed in separate lines, Measures are in place to distribute supplies to vulnerable individuals and those who are unable to travel to distribution points, Access roads between where beneficiaries live and distribution points or where services are provided are safe, If the distribution point is far away from the shelters, arrangements have been made to assist people to transport their goods so they are not forced to pay for transport themselves, All recipients know how to use and are able to use the materials they have been provided, e.g. Proper use of RUTF/RUSF. Confidentiality and privacy are respected in consultation, counseling or personal information sharing with beneficiaries, Breastfeeding corners should be available in nutrition centers to offer a private place for women to breastfeed in privacy, Outreach services are available for areas that are far from Health facilities or in geographically difficult or in insecure areas for beneficiaries to access. A system is in place for regularly finding out about new nutrition needs (e.g. through IYCF/NIPP committees, elders, or women’s groups etc), Monitoring teams are in place to assess, for example, whether service delivery locations are adequate and can be used in a dignified way. All Nutrition Staff have signed a code of conduct agreement or zero tolerance policy for sexual exploitation and abuse, a confidential complaints and protection reporting mechanism system is in place and beneficiaries know where to refer or report incidents of rights violations. Feedback on the complaints should be regularly be provided to the beneficiaries, beneficiaries have been clearly informed of the cost, if any, of the services or that the services are free
