Project Summary

According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), more than 4 million people are in need of food security in Sudan. The key driver of humanitarian needs in Sudan is conflict which has led to displacement of both IDPs and refugees. The impact of conflict is new and protracted displacement, and access to basic services. It also disrupts the livelihoods and food security of people in need. In 2019, UNHCR estimates 1.2 million refugees and asylum seekers will be living in Sudan and in need of humanitarian assistance. Disease outbreaks, AWD in particular, and floods also cause humanitarian needs. Acute malnutrition in children under the age of 5 is above emergency thresholds. Access to formerly inaccessible areas in Jebel Marra (Central Darfur), South Kordofan and Blue Nile remains key. Effective and timely delivery of humanitarian assistance will require a coordinated response that brings together all relevant humanitarian stakeholders to agree on needs and priorities based on coordinated inter agency assessments, access, and the vulnerability of the affected populations. To meet the needs of these populations, the HNO identified priority needs around the three outcomes of the MYHS: saving lives when new needs arise either due to natural or manmade disasters, responding to protracted displacement and addressing malnutrition and resilience.

Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan has improved with greater opportunities for internally displaced persons (IDPs) and returnees to determine their choice of return, resettlement, or reintegration. Despite this improvement, humanitarian needs persist for vulnerable people who have been living in protracted displacement for over a decade; people affected by recent localized clashes in Darfur’s Jebel Marra area; people affected by food insecurity, malnutrition and protection risks; as well as people displaced due to inter-communal violence and conflict. An estimated 5.7 million people in Sudan need some formof humanitarian assistance. Further the situation is exacerbated by the reduced household purchasing power caused by increasing prices of key commodities and medicines. At the same time, the country continues to be a major host of over one million refugees and asylum seekers, the majority of whom are largely dependent on humanitarian assistance. Food insecurity, Malnutrition, Displacement and refugees, and Protection concerns are the key drivers of needs in Sudan. Deterioration of food security is expected, according to FEWSNET’s latest outlook, January 2019, the prices of sorghum and millet continued increasing in December and January - atypical of the harvest period. This is due to the delayed harvest in semi-mechanized sorghum-producing areas, as well as currency depreciation and the impacts of macroeconomic conditions on the cost of production and transportation. Sorghum and millet prices are on average 150 percent above last year and over 270 percent above the five-year average. Food prices are expected to further rise and food access among poor household will be much lower than normal during the 2019 lean season when poor households are reliant on markets to access food. Malnutrition will likely continue with a potential increase in GAM and SAM rates, particularly for children under 5 years. Food inflation is estimated at 87 per cent (December 2018) which is directly impacting household nutrition security triggering negative coping strategies including their dietary diversity. The recurrence of communicable diseases, such as AWD and measles is likely to remain. However, due to preventable measures in put in place for AWD, the number of reported cases has dramatically declined in the last year and could remain low. Preventive measures against chikungunya and dengue are essential to avoid the spread of these diseases. UNAMID is downsizing and is preparing to leave the country in the next two years, in part due to the improvement of the security situation in areas of Darfur. However, as the root causes of the conflict remain unaddressed and long-standing grievances and competition for resources between communities continues, clashes and tensions may arise.

Activities

Provide support to the HC and HCT to enable effective leadership in strategic coordination, including the integration of key cross cutting issues such as gender, environment, and accountability to affected people in humanitarian actions2. Lead the inter sector coordination mechanisms at national and sub national levels in accordance with IASC guidance and support sectors in the preparation of sector response plans and their strategic monitoring indicators including those that directly fall under CCS sector3. Establish and maintain dialogue with affected people, including in IDP settlements in Darfur, to ensure provision of basic services to vulnerable people. This includes evidence based liaison with sector coordinators at state and Khartoum level to address any gaps reported.4. Support the HCT in developing the Humanitarian Response Plan informed by evidence based analysis of assessed needs with clear measurable objectives5. Ensure that inter agency risk analysis is undertaken and minimum preparedness actions and advanced prepared actions are routinely carried out in accordance with the Emergency Preparedness Framework6. Develop and publish on a regular basis communication and information products using a standardized and integrated approach. This includes the coordination of humanitarian public information and advocacy efforts7. Analyze and provide access reports to guide and inform decision making by the HCT and advocacy, and negotiate for access8. Promote the adherence to humanitarian principles by humanitarian actors to ensure principled interventions and in line with the HCT Minimum Operating Standards
