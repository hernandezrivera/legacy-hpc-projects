Project Summary

The goal of this project is to strengthen socioeconomic resilience of IDPs and returnees in communities in Gwoza and and Mafa LGAs where resettlement has occurred by training and providing start-up support and enhance social cohesion through community dialogue and awareness creation.

1 - Needs

The Boko Hara insurgency in the northeast of Nigeria has rendered a lot of persons depending on humanitarian aid for their survival for over a decade now. The multi-year Humanitarian Response Strategy (HRS) (2019-2021), stated that the violent conflict has resulted in deaths, displacement of about 1.8 million people (440,000 women, 364,000 men, 614,000 girls, and 516,000 boys) loss, and destruction of property, deterioration of living conditions, disruption of livelihood, accumulated stress, weakened resilience, and heightened vulnerabilities with The conflict has had disproportionate impacts along gender lines. Recently, the Borno State Government (BSG) has closed three IDP camps in Maiduguri and is looking forward to closing more by the end of December 2021. Also, in order reduce over dependency of returnees on humanitarian aid and to build their resilience in their communities of resettlement, the BSG as restricted the distribution of food and none food items in the communities of return. There is thus urgent need for early recovery interventions, including Livelihoods stabilization and income recovery, especially for female headed households, for returnees, IDPs in Host Communities, along with members from the Host Communities as well as restoration and or enhancement of social cohesion among these groups.

2 - Activities

Act1. Carry out assessments to determine preferred and innovative context-demand-driven income-generating activities for women, men and youth, including persons with disabilities. Act2. Provision of skills and vocational training with start-up kit/capital for women, men youths especially female headed households and link them to have access to IGAs to boost their socio-economic capital and self-reliance. Act3 s provide women, men youths especially female headed households with start-up kits to establish small-scale businesses. Act4. Organize and support women, men and youths to form groups of Village loans and saving associations (VLSA). (25 members per group) made up of those provided with start-up kits/funds. Act5. Provide women, men youths with 2 cycles of 3 days training course in basic business skills, including bookkeeping, saving, and basic business management skills. Act6. Carry out discussion sessions on Peacebuilding, social cohesion, Sexual exploitation and Abuse (SEA) and gender-based violence (GBV) prevention including community leaders, community security officers, returnees, IDPs and members of the host community. Act 7. Sensitize 1000 community members on gender equality, peaceful co-existence and human rights. Act8. dissemination of peace and reconciliation messages within target communities.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

Sirri Care will work with community members including women, men, girls, and boys, particularly female-headed households, and people with disabilities to establish gender-sensitive and appropriate compliant mechanisms in project target communities. The established complaint channels will provide for women and girls, particularly female-headed households and people with disabilities that are directly affected by the project a means to raise complaints about the quality of program and staff behavior. Sirri Care will also make provision for a complaint booth where issues of GBV and SEA can be reported. Also, there will be complaint and feedback desks and boxes at strategic areas in the project communities to ensure that community members are able to provide feedback and complaints about the project.
