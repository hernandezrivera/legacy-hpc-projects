Project Summary

Conflict and insecurity have forced hundreds of thousands of people to flee their homes in 2021 and are expected to remain key drivers of displacement in 2022. Conflict-induced shocks exacerbate the humanitarian situation of both IDP and host communities, with increased numbers of children married earlier as a coping strategy. Children in Somalia continue to face acute humanitarian needs due to conflict and environmental hazards such as drought, conflict and flooding. COVID-19 pandemic has compounded the challenges exacerbating vulnerability of the crisis-affected populations across the country. The crisis in Somalia has negatively impacted the health system and most affected are the vulnerable pregnant and lactating women, girls and boys, including children with disabilities with no access to health and nutrition services. Fear of conflict, insecurity and heightened tensions have triggered massive displacement in Guri Ceel, Galgaduud region over the last few days. Decades of conflict, recurrent climate shocks, disease outbreaks and increasing poverty are devastating the people of Somalia. The country is forecast to experience its third consecutive season of below-average rainfall, which has already resulted in a sharp increase in food insecurity, especially in rural areas. Without humanitarian assistance, nearly 3.5 million are expected to face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse outcomes through the end of 2021.
In this project, NWO will improve the access and utilization of Emergency integrated Nutrition services for women and children living in the most affected areas in L/Shabelle and Galgaduud regions using both static and mobile sites. The static nutrition sites will be strengthened by integrating with WASH and health Services, provision of nutrition supplies and the improvement of staff capacity. Remote villages will be reached through mobile clinics; community mobilizers and CNWs will be recruited and trained. Among the activities that NWO will be providing include: Immunization to under 5 children and Women of child bearing age (WCBA); Treatment of complicated and uncomplicated malnourished cases; Promote the adoption of good health and hygiene practices to the affected communities; Breast feeding promotion and support for infant and young child feeding; Identify and assist in the training of community volunteers on nutrition promotion and education; MUAC screening and identification of malnourished children; Distribution of standard IEC materials; MUAC screening for all children 6-59 months and pregnant and lactating women (PLW); Treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition cases with/without medical complications; Under this project, NWO will scale up its intervention through fixed integrated Nutrition sites and mobile services targeting 20,500 drought/conflict affected people IDPs and Host communities. The project will target all gender groups such as Pregnant Women, Boys and girls will be able to use the services of the nutrition facilities. Community Nutrition Workers and project staff to be trained will be composed of male and female. Men and women will be given equal opportunities when recruiting staff for the project. All social groups, including, youth (male and female), community elders, representatives of minority/marginalized groups and people living with disability will be involved in the project activities. Persons living with disability and members from marginalized communities will be given special consideration in staffing. NWO will promote accountability and provide information to line ministries and donors about the progress of the project in terms of project activities. NWO will work with the donor to ensure public trust and establish a system of transparency, community participation in conducting monitoring and evaluation. NWO will facilitate a dialogue between New Ways Organization and its affected populations over information provision.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

New ways will collect and use data dis-aggregated by sex, age and diversity to effectively inform programming. - New ways will ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups - New ways will ensure lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services - New ways will ensure the location, distance and access routes to the nutrition facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. - New ways will introduce special arrangements for persons unable to access nutrition facilities by providing mobile nutrition services, transportation/taxi services, facilitate accompaniment arrangements (e.g. youth groups assisting the elderly/persons with disability; community networks). New ways will ensure the location, distance and access routes to the nutrition facilities and services are accessible to all members of the community, including the most vulnerable groups. - New ways will make infrastructure adaptions to the nutrition facility so that it is accessible to persons with impaired mobility (e.g. persons with physical disabilities and older patients). - New ways will provide stabilization centres services by a trained staff. Where these services are not available in the nutrition facility, NW will make sure that nutrition workers are aware of nearest stabilization centre. - New ways will ensure that beneficiaries know of available nutrition services and assistance where and how to obtain it, through various means to ensure communication reaches the broader community and consider literacy levels (eg. health community workers, door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms). - New ways will set up mechanisms for submitting feedback that do not require the beneficiary exposing themselves to project staff. - New ways will respond to complaints, regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. - New ways will organize awareness raising sessions so that people know how it works. - New ways will consider a joint feedback mechanism with other sectors to minimize confusion. - New ways will make sure nutrition staff and nutrition committees will be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, persons with disabilities) and all staff and members will receive protection mainstreaming training. - New ways will ensure all nutrition staff and volunteers working with affected populations understand, sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect and foster humanitarian standards and the rights of beneficiaries, including the confidentiality of patients.Children and people with disabilities are among the most vulnerable, marginalized groups within Somali society because of the attitudinal, environmental, and institutional barriers they face.
