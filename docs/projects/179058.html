Project Summary

This intervention takes a participatory, community-based approach to recovery programming, ensuring market-sensitive livelihoods is provided to vulnerable Syrian households. Increased income, assets and consumption hold the key to promoting an increase in individual economic well-being. Social well-being stems from human capital and positive social relations. The vocational training and on-the-job training or apprenticeships are provided to persons with disabilities, youth and women, who will be offered a selection of enterprise and employment pathways. For those seeking to set up their own small/micro enterprise, a further package of business skills training and business grant will be provided based on their business proposition. Moreover, vocational training graduates are supported to seek employment with small-medium businesses. All skills trainings are identified based on a rigorous assessment of labour market to ensure addressing local sectors of economic growth, gaps in skills, barriers facing persons with disabilities accessing livelihood opportunities and women role in the market. Small and micro-business owners whose business has been negatively affected by conflict are supported to relaunch their businesses through a combination of skills, business grants & coaching. Business trainings are provided, particularly for those new to labour market such as youth, women & PWDs. Moreover, Cash for Work schemes are identified to provide benefit to the entire community. Community acceptance for targeted livelihoods activities is ensured through community participation in selection of types of businesses needed in community, beneficiary selection and monitoring project progress. All activities are mainstreaming COVID-19 Key messaging in activities package according to WHO Guidelines.
Inclusion of persons with disabilities mainly women with disabilities will be ensured through using twin track approach, the logic of intervention will combine a package of activities aimed at empowering PWD and building more inclusive and accessible working environment at community level. Activities will be based on assessment of barriers faced by PWD. While INGO will conduct several actions to overcome barriers such as awareness-raising on PWD rights, tailored case management support, and intensive coaching support. Participation of women will be supported through awareness and advocacy with their families, provision of female trainers and skills training for home-based businesses. Complementary services such as Mine Risk Education (MRE) and Psychosocial Support (PSS) will be provided based on community needs & vulnerability assessments.
The organization provides technical support to other livelihoods agencies to ensure the design and implementation take an inclusive approach and that they provide persons with disabilities with equitable access. This support also includes guidance on the meaningful engagement of older persons and women in activities.

4 - Needs

The severity and complexity of humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain extensive. This is the result of continued hostilities in localized areas, new and protracted displacement, increased self-organized returns and the sustained erosion of communities’ resilience during close to ten years of crisis. Across Syria, according to the HRP 2021, an estimated 14.2 million people are in need of Food Security and Agricultural assistance. The spread of COVID-19 and the application of the Caesar act sanctions, in June 2020, brought an unprecedented increase of market prices coupled with a reduction of households’ incomes and livelihood opportunities. The deterioration of economic conditions has been reported as the main driver of displacement, according to HNAP data. 71% of people surveyed declared that the monthly income gained is insufficient to cover household basic needs. This is turn affects their resilience capacity at 3 levels: i)creating new protection concerns (4% of HH are recurring to child labour and 3% are selling their house and/or land); b) reducing their productivity capacity (28% of HH are selling assets and goods and 9% their means of transport); c) reducing their resilience and ability to deal with future shocks (71% of HH are borrowing money and 41% are spending their savings). With a Gender Age and Disability (GAD) perspective, key concerns resulted from the surveyed persons. Such as i) the community attitude and cultural behaviors limiting PWDs and women from the involvement in livelihood activities ii) accessibility barriers related to inaccessible transport, built environment, and infrastructures; thirdly attitudinal, information, and communication barriers.

5 - Implementation of activities

Cash-for-Work participants are the mostly vulnerable households including at least 25% of women and 15% of people with functional limitations (15%) will be ensured. After discussion with the community cash for work schemes. It may include infrastructure rehabilitation projects such water infrastructure and accessibility of marketplaces.Labour Market Assessments (LMAs) will be conducted in each selected community in order to establish local-level economic growth sectors, new economic opportunities, and skills in greatest demand. This data will feed into a response options analysis for livelihoods programme design in line with the needs of the local labor market. LMAs will also identify specific barriers faced by women, men, young & older people and persons with disabilities in accessing employment and make recommendations for addressing them. Provision of business skills training, grants & coaching for small & micro business recovery for re-establishing businesses will be offered to eligible applicants. Provision of vocational skills, life skills and business skills training will be offered to eligible applicants. Vocational skills trainings will be based on the LMA findings of sectors of economic growth and skills in demand in the local market. Vocational skills training (VT) will be certified where feasible, and include an apprenticeship or on the job training and establishing links to local employment opportunities. Young people and women will be prioritized for VT & apprenticeships. Beneficiaries graduated from the VT and/or apprenticeship will have the opportunity to apply for the micro-business grant scheme.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

• Protection of Field staff and partners: keeping low visibility to protect the identities of staff. In case of accident, the INGO activates duty of care policy and provide field staff with psychological support.• PSEA: applying PSEA policy for staff, partner staff and suppliers. Proper accountability measures are ensured through investigation and follow up of reported cases. Also, raise awareness on the organization’s policy on PSEA and complaints and feedback mechanisms to follow up. As part of inclusion mainstreaming strategy, training to organizations’ staff and partners to highlight specific vulnerabilities faced by PWDs is implemented. • Community tensions arising from beneficiary selection: The INGO will share and discuss vulnerability criteria & weighting with community through participatory processes. The INGO outlines clear processes and guidelines for beneficiary selection to partners’ staff.• Participation of Women in livelihood activities within vulnerable household causing intra-family tensions. The INGO will closely monitor family dynamics and conduct awareness sessions for families. • Exclusion of persons with disabilities: Inclusion workers will be embedded within projects to provide specialized support to PWDs to fully enjoy and participate opportunities, in parallel with community and HH awareness-raising of rights and capacities of PWDs. Providing guidance and support for service providers on skills to interact with PWDs.• Location of livelihood services can have threats of violence. Conduct participatory security assessment involving community members, including representatives of gender, age and disability groups and Ensure security risk assessment as part of programming.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

• Loss of access to intervention areas: Livelihood programming will be prioritized in relatively stable areas, in communities with severity raking 3 or below. The INGO also continuously monitors the security situation in its intervention areas and adapts its access strategy to the regular conflict analysis and how it affects its operations.• Lack of local community acceptance :The INGO and its partners are well-perceived on the ground and make sure to keep close coordination with the local authorities and positive relations with the community. The INGO will also focus on increasing the community’s participation in its programs. • Intended beneficiaries are psycho-socially not ready to participate in productive activities. People in need of psychosocial support can be identified during the vulnerability assessments and will be supported through the following: Basic Psychological First Aid and the group Psychological support sessions are integrated into livelihood activities.• The eventual spread-out of COVD-19 pandemic and its implications on the operational capacities, as per prior experience in 2020. To mitigate this risk, the organization and partners will ensure the following: i) continue exploring alternatives remote modalities of intervention ii) ensuring PPE and other hygiene items are available in targeted locations; iii) mainstreaming COVID-19 messaging in the overall package of activities; iv) monitoring the epidemic situation through the health cluster.• Loss of funding and budget reduction due to exchange rate loss is managed by the organization continuous negotiation with the donor and the use of USD value for money transfer and payment as well as, close monitoring of local market flow.
