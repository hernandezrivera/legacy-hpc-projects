Project Summary

Project Goal:
To ensure education to the deprived Rohingya community children who are forcefully displaced from their ancestral land.
Core objectives:
1. Providing quality education to the FDMN children residing in 7 different camps as per Education Sector’s guideline and materials;
2. Provide the teaching and learning through digital method;
3. Capacity building of the teachers, staffs and volunteers in order to cope with the Education Sector standard;
4. Rehabilitation of the learning centers in the camp areas;
5. Providing a child friendly environment inside the learning centers;
6. DRR contingency planning for the monsoon.
The project aims to provide a structured and qualitative education to the children from Forcibly Displaced Myanmar community currently taking refuge in Cox’s Bazar. To achieve the goal this project targets to provide education through Myanmar Curriculum to a total of 5,000 children for the time period. To harmonize the project with gender inequality the targeted population ratio has been divided equally where 50% of the total targeted population will be girls. To accommodate the number of learners with adequate education facilities this project will use 50 well-built learning centers (LC) in an orderly manner at seven distinctive Rohingya camps. In order to provide Education Sector authorized curriculum, this project has been designed to follow all the guidelines from Education Sector Cox’s Bazar taking from LC constructions to teaching and learning. This project is ongoing since July, 2018 and since the project is following all the guidelines by the Education Sector and as a continuity, the project has already started the Myanmar Curriculum Scale up in all the learning centers.
By keeping the ongoing education system in concern this project thought of bringing something new to the table for its learners which will also be very supportive to distant education system during pandemics like Covid-19. Teachers from both host and FDMN communities will deliver all the lessons through multimedia system in animated and interactive ways. Alongside going with the Myanmar Curriculum Scale up teaching and learning, this project also provides the learners with different types of life-oriented lessons which will help them keeping their community a better place.
Mukti is one of the oldest members of Education Sector. Individuals from Mukti always attends every kind of meetings from the sector. Such as monthly coordination meeting, camp focal point meetings, Education Sector Information Management Working Group meeting and TWiG meetings. As a result, Mukti is committed to follow the policies and instructions coming from the sector. Mukti is well aware of the importance of including different types of protection mechanisms in the system. As a part of this process, the project has its own complaint and feedback mechanism which is strictly controlled from the head office. To ensure the protection issue, Mukti has its own Gender Focal, PSEA Focal and Protection Officer. This project has budget for addressing the DRR issues as it needs to repair its LCs in every monsoon. For this the project follows the instructions from the Sector. In order to increase the community awareness, Mukti will involve vulnerable communities, mainly by parent’s meetings to elicit their views and perspectives on programme priorities. Besides, Mukti will work in a similar manner with the community leaders like Majhis, religious leaders and CiCs through different meetings & orientations. As a part of the social cohesion, the project always tries to observe the important events or days in the life of the Rohingya community like, Genocide day on 25th August. Also the project plans to organize yearly sports and education fair in order to engage its targeted population in different types of peacebuilding activities.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

The Rohingya people have faced systematic disenfranchisement, discrimination, and targeted persecution in Myanmar’s Rakhine State for decades. Widespread influxes have occurred following violence in Rakhine State since 1978 to 2016. By far the largest refugee influx from Myanmar into Bangladesh began in August 2017. As of 01 January 2022, approximately 918,841 Rohingya refugees/FDMNs are registered in Bangladesh as part of the Government-UNHCR joint registration exercise, residing in 33 extremely congested camps in Ukhiya and Teknaf Upazilas of the Cox’s Bazar District, as well as on the island of Bhasan Char.It has been found that 481,765 (Girls- 234,744; Boys- 247,021) out of the total population is below 18 years. Women, girls, and boys, who make up more than 75% of the refugee/FDMN population, are particularly vulnerable to risks of abuse, exploitation, and gender-based violence. Children make up more than half the refugee/FDMN population and have been disproportionately affected by the crisis due to physical, psychological, and social vulnerabilities.Among this population the most vulnerable population group was elderly and young citizens. After providing the mass population with emergency fundamental needs there was need of engaging children in education. Although the primary unrest has been resolved with the help of Bangladesh govt. and other global humanitarian organizations. Leading humanitarian organizations like Unicef and UNHCR came forward with education support in all the camps. But there are still a huge number of children out of education. As education has always been a fundamental right of the children, the right deprived Rohingya Children are also among them. This project is targeting 5,000 learners to get education through 50 LCs in different camps. Due to the unavailability of internet in Camps there were no way for the community or learners to access web or facilities like multimedia system. Along with the rest of the world these learners also need to get introduced to digital teaching and learning process. So keeping that need in mind this project plans to bring out live versions of all the printed lessons through different types of interactivities. The system has already been tested in targeted areas and the anticipation was more than satisfactory. This system will also open new windows to distant education as the materials will be easily accessible from any kind of multimedia supported device from projector to multimedia bar phones. During the Covid-19 pandemic we came to know the importance and possibilities of providing remote supports. Live and interactive lessons alongside workbooks and copies can be a way of distant education if the learners can catch up with the system or idea. As the targeted population is children and they are forever vulnerable to sexual exploitations and abuse. Also the FDMN community is looking for their children’s safety inside Learning Centers. Mukti Cox’s Bazar has already been in a partnership with UNFPA to provide Gender Based Violence support to victim females/girls. Now to cope up with the changing scenarios Mukti Cox’s Bazar has introduced its own PSEA wing to ensure reporting link, justice and support for victims and their families.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

As the recognized partner of the Cox’s Bazar Rohingya response, and in line with rationalization exercise and its organizational mandate, Mukti has been coordinating with all relevant parties that are under the Education Sectors and its system. Mukti tries to avoid overlapping of services and ensure that there are no current actors providing similar service within the targeted area. Mukti has been one of the active partners in the inter-sectorial coordination mechanism since the beginning of the influx and always maintained respectful partnership and networking with relevant stakeholders and government officials. Moreover, Mukti has been prioritizing the needs of the sector to ensure continuity of interventions and bringing out the optimal positive impact of the programme. Simultaneously, Mukti’s recruited individuals are also equipped with the skillsets, and institutional capacity to operate at the field level. Given its mandate and policies on transparency and accountability to affected populations and donor communities, Mukti has been actively working with the Rohingya Community since the beginning of the response. Mukti knows the importance of information and so ensures that information is accessible to the donor and the Sector. Also Mukti has always been the JRP partner and participating in the current JRP process and meet the minimum PSEA related requirements while being active members in the Cox’s Bazar PSEA network, all of whom have appointed focal points and Child Safeguarding Focal Points.

Gender equity and protection is highly reflected in Mukti’s zero tolerance policy. Also the project is designed with all-female teachers’ policy. Also various awareness issues like health and hygiene issues, hand washing, child marriage, sexual exploitation and abuse, gender-based violence, etc., is a part of Mukti’s teacher training, staff training packages, and key agenda for parents’ meetings. Mukti works proactively to ensure that girls and special needs children are identified and given priority to enroll them. Mukti believes in women empowerment and focuses on female empowerment by ensuring their active participation in parent’s meetings, different trainings so that they can share their learning with their family as well as the respective community. The partner will ensure that its field staffs and teachers/ instructors also attend training and that this is closely monitored in the field level implementation. To ensure the mentioned actions, Mukti has its own PSEA focal and Gender focal in the head office level who work closely with the programs. There is already a reporting system introduced in Mukti for those who suffer from such exploitations and abuses.As Mukti is a partner of Unicef and UNFPA for quite a long time, it's policy related to protection and child safeguarding has evolved highly in recent years. Each and every recruited employees needs to sign different code of conducts prior to the joining. It is for ensuring that everyone is aware and will abide by the policies. Mukti has its own PSEA and child safeguarding focals in the headoffice who is responsible to deal with such issues if occurs. Teacher and field staffs are regularly being refreshed about the child safeguarding issues in monthly meetings and workshops. Mukti has its own reporting mechanism in order to help and support the victim at the earliest.

3.3 - Age & Disability

The project aims to support the education of 5,000 FDMN Children and 1,200 Host Community children who are under 18 years.Mukti will prioritize inclusiveness to enroll girls and children with disabilities in LCs under this Project. Mukti will support women’s empowerment by ensuring their active participation in parents’ meetings, teacher training and staff training so that they can share their learning with their family as well as respective community. In order to do so Mukti will train it’s teachers and staffs to help the program dealing with disability and inclusion. The disability trainings will help the field teams to find out children with disabilities during the surveys and screenings. Children who responded to the Child Functioning Module in a way which indicates they may have a disability will be referred to the partners who works with inclusion in the selected camps. They will technically guide and support Mukti if they require a specific assistive device, which is measured and is tailored to that learner. To support equity in teaching and learning and improve data administration education technology will be deployed, including classroom radios and teacher tablets, for interactive learning, teacher’s professional development and strengthened record keeping on attendance, learning and assessment results.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

Overall, an alarming 33% of parents only report attending parents’ meetings, partly due to the fact that these interactions largely appear to be structured as a one-way flow of information from staff to parents, with less space left for the community members themselves to take any initiatives. In order to increase the community awareness, Mukti will involve vulnerable communities, mainly by parents meetings to elicit their views and perspectives on programme priorities. Besides, Mukti will work in a similar manner with the community leaders like Majhis, religious leaders and CiCs through different meetings & orientations. Mukti plans to come up with a new mechanism with the target to bring social & behavioural changes through well planned and target oriented community engagement. Major activities related to the community engagement to ensure AAP are given below:• Parents group discussion (4 sessions per learning centre in a month)• Community Dialogs under each learning centre (community engagement & Feedback mechanism)• Information Sharing Session in all the learning centres.• Introducing reporting mechanism in all the learning centres for the affected population in need.

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

The project is designed to address the DRR/DRM issues as Cox's Bazar is one of the most cyclone prone areas in Bangladesh. Also the rohingya camps are situated in hilly terrains where the shelters and learning centers are established on top of hills which can be risky for the learners and community as well. During every monsoon the learning centers are affected by the rain, flash flood and landslide. So there has always been a budget for repair and reconstruction of the learning centers every year to ensure continuous teaching and learning. Also the project takes regular mitigation measures like tie down of the roof, land filling to reduce the land slide risk, making the access way to learning centers child friendly.
