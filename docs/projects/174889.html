Project Summary

Street children live in exceptionally difficult circumstances and are a highly vulnerable group. They are especially at risk of abuse, discrimination, and stigma as well and other human rights violations, including lack of access to education, healthcare, food, and adequate living conditions.
The number of street children in Pakistan is estimated to be between 1.2 million and 1.5 million, meaning that the country has one of the world's largest street children populations. Although, this number remains anecdotal since it was cited over ten years ago.
Pakistan is a signatory to the UNCRC Child Rights Convention, Article 20 of which clearly defines the rights of street children: “A child who is temporarily or permanently deprived of his or her family environment, or in whose own best interests cannot be allowed to remain in that environment, shall be entitled to protection and assistance provided by the state.”
District Dadu district is in the west of Sindh province. Dadu is the district that absorbed thousands of the migrants and flood-affected persons from almost four districts of Sindh province. Most of them returned after the recession of floodwater and as many as equal to half of the Dadu city population did not go back and settled in the Dadu city or around the Dadu city. In broad terms, the street children phenomenon exists all over the Sindh province and the situation is not much different from the other Asian countries. In Sindh, street children can be defined as those who earn their livelihood on the streets and stay there, for most or all, of the day. Dadu is facing same the issue as Street Children. There are different opinions that came in front during FGDs conducted by NDO in Dadu with different groups. The root cause of the phenomenon of street children is disclosed as poverty. A large number of garbage pickers in the city hail from the Baghri (minority) community. Shoe polishing, garbage picking, begging, and selling things are the major sources of income for street children. Children come on the streets after disputes in their homes, after natural disasters, internal disturbances, terrorism, family loss, poverty, psychological problems, and the bad influence of other children. The problems faced by street children are very common and well known. Many of them have no guardian who can keep a check on them, and so they are exploited by people who use public spaces. Once on the streets, these street children then become even more vulnerable to other abuses including drug-addiction, trafficking, and sexual abuse. Some of the risks faced by street children include homelessness, malnutrition, physical and mental abuse, and marginalization from mainstream society. These children easily become victims of organized criminal gangs, drug pushers, and begging mafias who take these children under their wings and use them in criminal activities. Street children are also found to be involved in risky behaviors including commercial sex and drug abuse. Enough children are roaming in the streets in Dadu. They are often involved in begging, rag-picking, drug peddling, and only a handful of them are working as cobblers, paperboys, waiters, and flower sellers. It is believed that their lifestyle makes them vulnerable to anti-social behavior. Participants recommended establishing a system whereby an increasing number of street children could be stopped in the province and to facilitate these street children and give them food, shelter at daytime, and awareness about various social and medical problems as to rehabilitate the existing street children. So far, NDO has designed this project to establish drop-in centers where these street children could have better living during the daytime, psycho-socio support, food, health, informal education, and skill development training.
