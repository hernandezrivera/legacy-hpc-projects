Project Summary

Based on the South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) (2021), an estimated 2.7 million people, including 2.3 million children, are at risk of violence, exploitation, abuse, and neglect and in acute need of immediate child protection in 2021. Children continue to be disproportionately affected by crisis and exposed to multiple protection risks including recruitment by armed groups, psychosocial distress, family separation, violence and exploitation in 61 most affected counties. In this project, GREDO will provide critical child protection services and assistance to separated, unaccompanied and children associated with armed groups in Pibor, Budi, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta East and Bor South Counties. The intervention is participatory, integrated and right based and will be implemented for a period of twelve months. To ensure critical provision of child protection services, GREDO will develop existing child protection referral pathway ensuring safe and secure identification, documentation, referral to service providers and case follow up of all cases of child protection. GREDO will train 50 Case Workers on minimum child protection standards and support them in managing CP cases. Two Child and Youth Friendly Spaces will be established in each location. Structured activities targeting at risk children will be conducted within the child friendly spaces and will include provision of psycho-social support, registration of unaccompanied and separated children followed by family tracing and reunification as well as a youth resilience program. Young girls of reproductive age will be provided with Menstrual Hygiene Management training and hygiene kits. Caregivers of unaccompanied and separated children and former CAAFAGs will be supported through restricted Cash Based Intervention Cash Voucher system for them to be able to obtain essential supplies and basic needs such as food. To mitigate Child Protection risks, GREDO will establish one Children Based Protection Mechanisms in each county and train them on Child Protection in Emergency, protection awareness and messaging, preparedness and Child Protection Risk Protection mitigation. To integrate the response, GREDO will work with Nutrition partners during Nutrition outreach activities and in stabilization centres on Child Protection Messaging targeting women. Children with protection concerns and malnourished will be referred to Nutrition Service providers for supplementation feeding and treatment. Education partners will be engaged in enrolment of at risk and out of school boys and girls. Injured children or those suffering from extreme trauma will be referred to Health Providers for specialized care. To ensure accountability to affected persons, a Complaint and Feedback Mechanism (CFM) will be activated on contact with target communities. Community communication and sensitization will provide sufficient information to affected persons on how to submit complaints and receive feedback. In this intervention, GREDO will reach 300 UASC who will be identified, documented, and reunited with their families. On the other hand, 150 UASC (75 boys and 75 girls) will be supported through Cash Based individual protection assistance as part of case management and referrals. Similarly, 600 cases comprising of 300 girls and 300 boys will be supported through case management and referrals services and 15,000 women and men will receive PSS, parenting messaging and participate in parenting sessions. Additionally 35,000 girls and boys will receive focused and non-focused MHPSS and other related services such as recreational activities, livelihood and parenting skills. Finally, 200 persons (150 Females/50 Males) will be trained on CPiE while another 54,000 persons will be reached with child protection awareness activities.

1 - Needs

South Sudan children and adolescents continue to face a multitude of risks, including abduction, GBV and other forms of violence, recruitment into armed groups and separation from families. These risks are further challenged by COVID-19 with reduced access to schools and the disruption of environments where children grow and develop. Economic pressures from COVID-19 have forced some families to rely on negative coping mechanisms, including child labor and early marriage. According to South Sudan IPC report (December, 2020), Pibor, Kapoeta North, Bor South are classified in Emergency (IPC phase 4) acute food insecure counties with the food insecurity levels remaining elevated due to insecurity, the effects of COVID-19, persistent poor macroeconomic conditions, and the impact of flooding on livelihoods. High level of stress, loss of friends and family members are also taking a toll on mental health and emotional development of children, further exacerbated by the COVID-19 outbreak. Studies suggest that at least one in three respondents met the criteria for a mental disorder. A recent FSNMS assessment found that 30 per cent of children had behavioral change, showing signs of distress due to repeated exposure to conflict and shocks. Since the outbreak of violence in December 2013, 26,18 (12,214girls and 13,970 boys) cases of unaccompanied and separated children (UASCs), missing and other vulnerable children have been documented in South Sudan and only 6,348 reunified, with up to 4,000 children yet to be reunified. A report recently released by United Nations has documented 109 abductions, 453 recruitments, 72 cases of sexual violence, and 125 incidences of killing. In rural and urban parts of the country, protection concerns including child protection continue to ravage several states in the country. Other factors driving protection concerns among children include; Lack of alternative pathways for adolescent girls due to limited education particularly for girls who have dropped out of school, education related expenses making it difficult for IDPs and returnees to retain children in school, patriarchal gender norms and cultural practices that restrict girls options, the practice of bride price, restriction to girls mobility and participation in activities outside the home, weak community protection structures and poverty and limited options for conflict affected families leading to forced marriage in exchange for cattle to replenish family animal assets. GREDO therefore aims to aims to prevent and respond to immediate life- saving child protection services to 54,000 vulnerable children and their families in Pibor, Budi, Kapoeta North, Bor South and Kapoeta East counties.

2 - Project activities

GREDO CP staffs will conduct sensitization sessions with Community Leaders, Community Members, Chiefs, Local Authorities, Parent Teachers Associations, Teachers and Religious leaders to provide information on the project, receive inputs from the affected community and seek their support. Information provided to affected communities to include; Project aim, project objectives, expected results, project activities, project lifespan, beneficiaries’ selection criteria, modality of intervention, role of the community in project implementation and role of project staffs. Also, information on Complaint and Feedback Mechanism will be provided with reporting and feedback mechanism being articulated. GREDO Protection staffs will work closely with chiefs, community leaders, teachers, local authorities and parents to map child protection risks prior to commencing project activities. Report generated from the audit will be utilized by project staffs while deciding locations for Child Friendly Spaces, Sites for recreational sports and to create awareness among staffs and community members of risk mitigation measures. GREDO will establish ten Child Friendly Spaces using locally available materials. CFS Managers will oversee activities within the safe spaces ensuring safety of children and supervision of PSS and structured activities.Though 50 caseworkers, GREDO will provide Comprehensive case management and referrals including CBI: The exercise will involve identification, documentation, family tracing and reunification of unaccompanied and separated children. UASC headed households, Caregivers of UASC will be supported with Cash Based Individual Protection assistance as part of case management and referrals. GREDO will facilitate training and support of 50 Case Workers conducting case managementGREDO will provide psychosocial support services to UASC boys and girls and caregivers of UASC. Specialized PSS will be conducted by trained Case Workers and will be supplemented with Life Skills training, recreation sports, and training on beadwork, leatherwork, painting, and other livelihood skills. GREDO will conduct awareness and prevention messaging through community mobilizers, case workers and Community Based Protection Networks targeting women, men, boys, girls, teachers, chiefs, community leaders, youth leaders, women leaders and religious leaders on the rights of children.GREDO will facilitate in the formation, training and support of five Community Based Protection Mechanisms (one in each County). CBPMs will be trained on Child Protection in Emergency, child protection risk mitigation, preparedness and early warning. CBPMs will remain active in their respective communities advocating for the rights of children, mapping, monitoring and reporting protection risks likely to impact on children. GREDO will conduct Menstrual Hygiene Management training targeting reproductive age girls. Girls will be provided with menstrual hygiene kits in order for them to manage menses.

3 - Project indicators

The success of this project will be measured through the following indicators:• Number of girls, boys, women and men provided with individual, group, specialized PSS and other related services (e.g. recreational activities, livelihood services, parenting skills) • Number of women and men who receive psychosocial support & participating in parenting sessions • Number of girls, boys, women and men with protection concerns supported through case management and referral services• Number of girls, boys, women and men supported with cash based Individual Protection Assistance as part of case management and referrals • Number of unaccompanied and separated girls and boys identified, documented, and receiving family tracing and reunification services (UASC)• Number of girls and boys released from armed forces and groups and provided with care and reintegration service • Number of girls, boys, women and men reached through awareness raising activities • Number of women and men trained on child protection in emergencies (CPiE) including Case management and PSS

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

GREDO has a means for communities to provide feedback on programmes andto submit complaints, and to ensure that they receive a timely response.Beneficiary participation is mandatory for project executed by GREDO, whichhas enabled affected populations to play an active role in decision-makingprocesses that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines andpractices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the vulnerable arerepresented and have influence. GREDO will actively seek the views of theaffected populations to improve programming, ensuring that feedback andcomplaints mechanisms are streamlined, appropriate and robust enough todeal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from)complaints about breaches in plans and stakeholders dissatisfaction. Accountability will be taken into consideration during the proposed action to ensure that the points of view of the affected population and returnees are considered. They will be involved all along the project, from the assessments, to the monitoring of activities, including beneficiaries’ selection and composition of kits and implementation of activities. GREDO staffs will be well trained to ensure the needs of the children are assessed. Before the intervention, GREDO will organize a minimum of 3 focus group discussions per location with girls, boys, women, men, elderlies and people with disabilities for the community consultations to ensure community inputs. This feedback will be used to adapt the activities, improve the mobilization and participation of the population, monitor and improve the quality of the work. This approach guarantees safety, dignity and protection of affected people. For each location, GREDO will also create an implementation committee and a complaint committee with members of the community. They will be responsible for the link between affected households and GREDO. They will be able to spread messages, explain the selection criteria, report needs from the community, etc. GREDO will set up a specific complaint/feedback mechanism adapted to the local context to collect the complaints and feedbacks from the beneficiaries and take appropriate action. GREDO will organize regular feedback desk within each location. In this way, GREDO will be present to listen and register the complaints, to collect information and to answer the questions. GREDO already has large experience of accountability to the communities and complaints responses mechanisms, the AAP for this project will be also aligned with the standardized community-led complaints and feedback.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

GREDO will strive to gain acceptance from local authorities and stakeholders; follow strict logistics and financial procedures; streamline security risk assessments at all stages of program design; gather up-to-date security alerts from various formal and informal sources and adjust our actions. Community awareness of the principles and arrangement of the program, with Community Feedback and Complaint Mechanism. (CFCM) are in place. GREDO staff are equipped with the capacities to negotiate access with the authorities. Access negotiation is based on a strong commitment on the respect of humanitarian principles. Specific trainings of the key people involved in access negotiation are conducted. In addition to the risks detailed above, GREDO will mitigate the specific risks such as risks linked to the important variations of the population size in the areas of intervention. Mitigation measures will include advocating with other partners to complement GREDO interventions when necessary. Another risk to take into account is the exclusion of women in project activities. GREDO will ensure increased participation of women in project activities from the start to the end. Community members will be engaged in project planning, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.
