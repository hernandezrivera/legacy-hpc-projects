Project Summary

This project will provide immediate and short time live saving emergency CCCM response for 35,013 (14,370 males, 20,643 female) Internally displaced persons with 9,790 IDPs (3941 M, 5849 F) in Jur River County, 9995 IDPs (4287 M, 5709 F) in Lainya County, and 15228 IDPs (6142 M, 9087 F) in Yirol East county in 13 collective sites with 6 sites in Jur River (Kangi, Juarjena, Marial Bai, Rocrucdong,Udic), 5 sites Yirol East (Adior, Malaek, Nyang Town, Pagerou,Tit Agau), and 2 sites in Lainya (Kenyi and Lainya).
Out 8.3 million people estimated to be in need of humanitarian assistance, 1.62 million IDPs are in dire need (OCHA Snapshot, Jan 2021), more than 80% live in collective sites and in informal camp-like settings or with the host community mainly located in hard-to-reach areas. The major triggers of displacement are the on-going intercommunal conflicts in Lakes and Western Bahr el Ghazal State, and sporadic clashes in Lainya, County (Central Equatoria) which remained unresolved. Inadequate coordination and management structures at IDP sites and in informal camp-like settings remains a challenge as IDPs do not receive adequate and timely essential information on how they can access humanitarian services and advocate for their needs in these settings. Therefore, the IDPs are likely to remain in this displacement sites since conditions for their voluntary return remained unresolved.
The project is aimed at improving coordination and monitoring of CCCM response, enhanced capacity, and participation of IDPs to ensure community participation, inclusivity, local ownership, self-governance and self-reliance through CBI for improving household’s income to reduce dependency, site care and maintenance improvement to minimize protection risks and support them through cash-based initiatives by participating in activities concerning their access to services. Through this project, AFOD will respond to the needs of IDPs in collective sites and informal camp-like settings or within the host community located in these areas to ensure dignified access to basic services. This project will be implemented through static and mobile outreach response approach for cost effective response for vulnerable communities. The project contributes to; ensure equal access and needs based assistance to improve quality of services for populations affected by displacement; ensure outreach response to newly displaced and un-reached populations; Strengthen inclusive community participation to ensure local ownership, self-governance and self-reliance; and improve engagement with vulnerable populations with priority in addressing protection gaps and building resilience. AFOD will ensure women, men, boys, and girls are fully consulted, involved, and participate at different stages of project cycle; and IDP leadership will be empowered to monitor, report, and represent the needs of the community, and coordinate with humanitarian agencies and other stakeholders. The beneficiaries will be involved through participation in community meetings, election of site committees, feedback sessions on services provided, cash for work initiative, community site maintenance, and through participation in site leadership governance.

1 - Needs

It is estimated 1.62 million people are in need of camp coordination and camp management services; most affected are children (54%), women (50%) and people with disabilities (13%). This includes 1.28 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in protection of Civilian sites (PoCs), collective sites, as well as camp like settlements who are the primary focus of CCCM and will continue to rely on humanitarian assistance despite on-going efforts by humanitarian actors to address their needs. Out of 1.28 million IDPs in dire need of camp coordination and management services, more than 80% live in collective sites and in informal camp-like settings or with the host community mainly located in hard-to-reach areas. The major triggers of displacement are on-going intercommunal conflicts in Lakes and Western Bahr el Ghazal State, sporadic clashes in Lainya, County in Central Equatoria which remained unresolved. Therefore, the IDPs are likely to remain in this displacement sites since conditions for their voluntary return remained unresolved. This IDPs live in precarious conditions where their basic needs are unmet due to lack of service provision. The critical needs of the IDPs which requires urgent attention include insufficient access to community participation structures mostly for women, girls, and persons with disabilities who are the most vulnerable groups and continue facing protection risks. Therefore, to mitigate these potential risks and ensure equitable access to services, assistance and protection, these groups need representation in community governance structures to enable them to decide how they want to receive assistance. Inclusive governance structures lead to stronger identification of needs and more efficient assistance delivery based on the feedback of displaced communities. AFOD will identify existing structures, include groups who are currently under-represented and train these members on coordination, communication with communities and humanitarian principles and develop an exit strategy to enhance their self-governance and self-reliance skills. To reduce community dependency, AFOD will devise a strategy to support vulnerable groups like women, youths and persons with disabilities through CBI. They will be identified, trained and supported with cash to undertake income generating activities with support of AFOD technical team. Similarly, majority of IDPs live in informal camp-like settings where the living conditions are poor with limited access to services due to lack of service providers. This can consequently lead to the adoption of negative copying mechanisms which can increase gender-based violence and other safety and security risks. AFOD will identify gaps and needs by conducting rapid assessments taking into consideration protection concerns of the different groups, create site profiles to highlight and communicate the key gaps and needs, collect feedback by enhancing complaints and feedback mechanisms, conduct essential care and maintenance interventions that will improve the living conditions of the displaced population and mitigate any protection risks for vulnerable groups based on the feedback from the community following protection guidelines as well as conduct post monitoring assessments. AFOD teams will support the site and care maintenance interventions led by the community. Additionally, AFOD will ensure beneficiaries benefit from cash-based initiatives by participating in community activities concerning their access to services. Lastly inadequate coordination and management structures at IDP sites and in informal camp-like settings remains a challenge as IDPs do not receive adequate and timely information on how they can access humanitarian services, advocate for their needs in these settings. AFOD will establish community-based complaint mechanism to enable communities provide feedback. AFOD will conduct sensitization to IDPs and stakeholders.

2 - Project activities

AFOD through this project will conduct the following activities in all collective sites in Wau; 1. Identify spontaneous settlements or new arrivals in collective sites and report to appropriate clusters 2. Conduct site profiling and mapping of service provision and share appropriate information with beneficiaries. AFOD will conduct monthly site profiling, mapping and develop 5Ws in all collective sites to identify gaps and share the report/information with the relevant clusters for decision making. 3. Strengthen complaints and feedback mechanisms and engage the site committee to address issues identified. AFOD will continue to strengthen complaint feedback system including protection mainstreaming to ensure equal access and needs based assistance is provided to affected populations, strengthen engagement with the IDPs and continue to build inclusive community participation to ensure total local ownership, self-governance and self-reliance. 4. Monitoring of service provision at collective sites and at out of camp like setting. AFOD will conduct safety audits and beneficiary satisfaction survey to inform decision making and service improvement. 5. Conduct site maintenance activities to support minimum living standards and protection. AFOD will ensure all collective sites have specific services for People with Special needs (PSNs). AFOD will continue to implement activities of site maintenance & rehabilitation with a specific focus on PSNs and improving referral pathways. 6. Establish referral pathways for all services available in collective sites (GBV, health, education, protection, nutrition, WASH and NFI) 7. Conduct community engagement, mobilization and sensitization. AFOD will carry out monthly community mobilization and sensitization to promote coexistence of the IDPs with host communities and durable solution for safe return of IDPs are formulated and strengthened through stakeholder engagement and dialogues. 8. Support self-managed structures and community led site management in camp-like settings through site maintenance & rehabilitation and promoting IDPs participation. 9. Train staff in CCCM on humanitarian response. 10. Train IDP site leaders and site committees on CFM to strengthen site management and accountability. AFOD will enhance the capacities of site management committees to be able to self- governed the collective sites including working camp administrator, who shall provide oversight and supervise the activities in camps and camp-like situations 11. Organize and conduct site level coordination meetings. AFOD will strengthen collaboration with other partners through information sharing, regular site level coordination meetings. 12. Contingency planning. AFOD will lead process of contingency planning in all the collective sites and ensure contingency plans are updated regularly and shared with cluster members and stakeholders. 13. Select beneficiaries to be supported with Cash based initiative through cash for work selected collective sites. AFOD together with site committees identify individuals to benefit from cash for work modality

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

AFOD will ensure through the complaint feedback mechanisms, relevant information on protection against sexual exploitation is disseminated to the IDPs to ensure exploitation of beneficiaries is reported on PSEA. Site committees and staff will also be trained on PSEA issues to be able to identify and report exploitation between the beneficiaries and any authority or partners. AFOD shall roll out its whistle blower policy or guidelines, conflict of interest policy and ensure all staff sign and adhere to the organization code of conduct. Hence, no tolerance to exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Transparent complain feedback mechanisms will be established and strengthened in all collective sites. Priority will be given to the most vulnerable persons to provide feedback on the services they receive AFOD shall ensure two-way feedback platforms will promote IDPs to share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. AFOD will continue to strengthen the complaint feedback mechanisms/desk to receive feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries about the services, and the feedback and comments of the beneficiaries shall be referred and managed by the respective clusters and partners. During Community engagement, mobilization and sensitization, any feedback shall be transmitted to the IDPs, the project will promote harmony among the IDPs and services will be provided to every beneficiary regardless of race, tribe and ethnic dimensions. The site committees will be empowered to monitor, report and represent the needs of the community and will be facilitated to meaningfully participate in coordination mechanisms with humanitarian agencies and other stakeholders through support from the Camp Management. Further, the beneficiary satisfaction survey will be conducted which allow for a quantitative and comprehensive impartial approach to accounting for the wide population's voice independent of site governance committees.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Transparent feedback and/or complaint mechanism system will be established and strengthened in all collective sites. AFOD shall ensure two-way feedback platforms are created/improved so that IDPs can share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. AFOD will continue to strengthen the complaint feedback mechanisms/desk to receive feedback and complaints from the beneficiaries about the services, and the feedback and comments of the beneficiaries shall be referred and managed by the respective clusters and partners. During Community engagement, mobilization and sensitization, any feedback shall be transmitted to the IDPs, the project will promote harmony among the IDPs and services will be provided to every beneficiary regardless of race, tribe and ethnic dimensions. The site committees will be empowered to monitor, report and represent the needs of the community and will be facilitated to meaningfully participate in coordination mechanisms with humanitarian agencies and other stakeholders. Further, the beneficiary satisfaction survey will be conducted routinely which allow for a quantitative and comprehensive impartial approach to accounting for the wide population's voice independent of site governance committees.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

AFOD will ensure feedback from beneficiaries is compiled and analysed to inform decision making. The analysis of the feedback from beneficiaries will be used to inform or guide any changes or improvement required in the IDPS collective sites and camp like settings. The beneficiaries will be continuously encouraged to use the feedback mechanism established to give feedback about the services provided and through their committees, appropriate solutions will be sought collectively. Project changes or modification will be done with reference to the feedback received from the beneficiaries.
