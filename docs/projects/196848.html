Project Summary

Sudan's ongoing food insecurity endangers people's lives, means of subsistence, and stability. A large portion of Sudan's population is at risk of food insecurity as a result of the protracted conflict, environmental degradation, and other disasters such as drought and floods, in 2023, there would be an estimated 11.7 million individuals in need of FSL assistance in Sudan.
This project is designed to improve the food security and livelihood status of 18,000 (9000 Female, 9,000 male) vulnerable agro/pastoral individuals directly, as part of the project activities, 9,000 individuals (3,000 males, 6,000 females) will receive agricultural assistance, 9,000 individuals (4,000 males, 5,000 females) will receive livestock assistance, and 1,000 (500 males, 500 females) will receive cash or vouchers to support their livelihoods 8000 , as well as their families indirectly. The action is expected to reach up to 120,000 people including children, youth, adolescent girls, people with disabilities, women-headed households, IDPs, and Returnees in East Darfur State. The project interventions will allow households to grow their own reliable food for family consumptions while earning some money from the sale of the surplus. This will be conducted through improving farmers and pastoralists awareness on good agricultural practices, provision of improved seeds and essential agricultural hand tools, distribution of milking goats to enhance the availability of milk and meat for home consumption and conducting a livestock vaccination campaign to maintain the households' animal assets in the targeted communities and vouchers for goods or services will be provided directly to affected people to meet one or more of their basic humanitarian needs and save their life and the new displacement regarding to current crisis ongoing response targeting 20,000 to receive Cash /voucher based transfer( for food full ration 10,000 and Half ration 10,000

1 - Needs

Sudan is experiencing a serious humanitarian crisis and a complex development situation. Since the military coup of 2021, the country is facing an increasing humanitarian caseload due to political fragility, economic deterioration, violence, floods and displacement. These factors have resulted in an increase in poverty levels and unemployment rates among citizens. Droughts across 115 localities in 14 states have affected 5.6 million people. The increasing prices of agricultural inputs led to a decrease in overall harvest size by around 35% from the previous year. According to the Sudanese Humanitarian Aid Commission (HAC), 349,000 people were affected since September 26th (2022) by seasonal rains and floods in 16 out of 18 states. 24,860 homes have been destroyed and 48,250 damaged, further impacting food security. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview report (HNO 2023) the level of acute food insecurity increased for the third year, to a record 11.7 million people (49% women 51% men). From the affected population 48% are children and 15% are persons with a disability. Women and girls, particularly adolescent girls, face additional challenges having significantly lost control of and access to vital resources such as land, livestock, income and farm inputs, severely affecting their decision-making ability and participation in the household (HNO, 2023). Additionally, women and girls face the double burden of having to carry out household work as well as livelihood activities to support their families. Many have been forced to sell their livelihood assets for income and cultivation is low, thus increasing their vulnerability to disasters and shocks. East Darfur: Food insecurity in East Darfur increased by 18%, from 38% in 2021 to 56% in 2022. The prevalence of severe food insecurity also increased from 4% to 14%. The localities with the highest prevalence of food insecurity were She-areia (90%), Abujabra (80%) and Assalya (75%). Eldeain has the highest level of severe food insecurity in East Darfur at 59%. With a semi-desert climate, East Darfur is prone to droughts and low rainfall, and has a geological system that is unfavorable for groundwater storage. Late rains last year resulted in crop failure and a poor harvest in the 2021-22 season. Sorghum production in the traditional rained sector was 90% below the five-year average . and land disputes, with over 300,000 IDPs and more than 350,000 South Sudanese refugees. in east Dafur state sharing the community in general serveses

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

African Relief Committee (ARCO) will prioritize safety and dignity to ensure no harm is caused and will seek to identify, prevent and mitigate any unintended negative effects or risks during the intervention to girls, boys, children, young people, and project participants. ARCO will target the most vulnerable without discrimination and ensure that all participants are aware of and has access to necessary and relevant services. ARCO implements ‘Do no Harm’ measures to ensure the safety and dignity of children and young people that interact with its staff, associates or volunteers. It has zero tolerance against all forms of abuse and exploitation as articulated in its policy on safeguarding for children and young people. ARCO will continue to engage with the local authorities to ensure that activities are context appropriate and will orient all third parties working with and for ARCO and keep signed acknowledgements of the safeguarding policy thereafter. Activities will be underpinned by human rights principles and will strictly adhere to the core humanitarian standards and maintain the principles of humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence. ARCO maintains high standards of safeguarding and preventing sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse PSHEA, especially in emergency situations. ARCO’s Safeguarding Policy and procedures are implemented during the design and implementation of project interventions with vigilance. Safeguarding and PSHEA risk analyses and assessments of project interventions will be conducted with mitigation measures/contingency plans identified for each risk. ARCO will work to ensure staff, partners, project participants and communities are aware of the safeguarding and PSEA policies, its minimum codes of behavior, and requirements to uphold high standards of professional behavior. Project participants, especially children and young people will be respected, and their safety and protection will not be compromised when obtaining and using information on and images of them. ARCO will identify groups at risk of unsafe livelihoods practices and priorities provision of alternatives. ARCO will identify the power dynamics (who can access assets and inputs and who can influence decisions) and will monitor and respond to potential exploitative practices or abuses. Information will be communicated through various means to reach the wider community and account for different literacy levels. All layers of the affected population, especially those with intersecting vulnerabilities, will be consulted in identifying and responding to FSL needs to collect accurate information about specific requirements and preferences. ARCO will encourage communities to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes to promote a sense of ownership, build self-esteem and improve the sustainability of the response. Collective livelihood projects will be promoted contribute to community protection and resilience.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

African Relief Committee (ARCO) maintains high standards of Safeguarding and PSHEA (preventing sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse), especially in emergency situations. ARCO’s Safeguarding Policy and procedures are implemented during the design and implementation of project interventions with vigilance. Safeguarding and PSHEA risk analyses and assessments of project interventions will be conducted with mitigation measures/contingency ARCO identified for each risk. ARCO will work to ensure staff, partners, beneficiaries and communities are aware of the safeguarding and PSEA policies, minimum codes of behavior, and requirements to uphold high standards of professional behavior. ARCO is a member of the AAP/PSEA network and mechanisms and interacts with UNCT/UNOPS call centers and AAP focal points in other organizations that is set up by the humanitarian actors in Sudan. ARCO will contribute to enhancing technical standards and AAP and PSHEA mechanisms and approaches based on lessons learned and experience. ARCO has robust training and capacity building manuals that can enhance AAP and PSHEA roll out, reporting, and responding. ARCO will also ensure to participate in needs/information assessments on AAP and PSHEA as initiated by the AAP network as appropriate. ARCO is committed to mainstreaming principals of accountability throughout its intervention in accordance with the Core Humanitarian Standard on Accountability and Quality Management. ARCO will that target groups and communities have access to information that is relevant to their needs by creating opportunities to persons of concerns and served communities (including girls and boys, adolescents and youth) to participate in programming decisions and to provide feedback on the project interventions using existing and established community feedback and response mechanisms (CFRMs). The CFRM system has multiple entry points, designed with communities to be accessible to all community members regardless of gender, age, mobility, socio-economic status, or any other differentiating factor. Entry points include telephone calls, suggestion boxes as safe entry points to allow target beneficiaries to share their feedback/complaints, feedback forms, community meetings as well as making visits to the Plan office. The feedback/complaints received are collected by the CFRM focal point, classified, and analyzed and confidentiality is ensured throughout the process. Complaints and feedback are addressed in line with Plan’s internal procedures and responses are made to those who filled the feedback/complaint. Feedback/complaints concerning other project stakeholders (government staff, partners etc.) working in the same areas will be referred to their senior management as appropriate for the concerned organization to take the necessary actions.
