Project Summary

The provision of basic essential lifesaving health services project focusing on major causes of morbidity and mortality in Nasir, Ulang, Pochalla, Eastern Equatoria and Gogrial East will improve access to integrated basic health care and avert excess morbidity and mortality among 30,000 women, children and men. The project targets the most vulnerable population whose right to access to health care services is in jeopardy following the continued intercommunal violence, floods and the Covid 19 pandemic. Of the targeted 30,000 people, 12,000, 8,000 and 10,000 will be IDPs, returnees and host community respectively.
The project will support provision of critical lifesaving interventions aligned to the CERF criteria and the South Sudan Basic Package of Health and nutrition through a network of 12 PHCUs, 7 PHCCs, 1 Hospital, and 8 mobile clinics in hard to reach locations. The project activities will focus on prevention and case management of epidemic-prone diseases such as malaria, pneumonia, acute watery diarrhoea, cholera, and measles through both static and outreach services; provision of essential medicines and medical supplies accessed through the core pipeline; capacity building of staffs through on-job mentorship, disease surveillance including EVD and COVID 19, and routine monitoring of health information through strengthening the EWARS/DHIS2 system.
The clinical package will include; Health education and promotion, curative outpatient and inpatient services, routine immunization including supplementary immunization activities focusing on measles and polio, Maternal New-born and Child Health Services (MNCH), and HIV/TB services including e-MTCT. The project will integrate Clinical Management of Rape (CMR) at PHCCs and Hospital; management of severe cases of malnutrition with medical complication at the stabilization centres; identification, and referral for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS); and Water Hygiene and Sanitation (WASH) at the health facilities.
This project will contribute to addressing the needs of the 30.000 of the estimated 4.9 million women, children and men that will need basic essential health services in South Sudan in the year 2021 and hopefully contribute to a reduction in high maternal mortality rates (789 deaths per 100,000 live births) and under-five mortality rates (90.7 deaths per 1,000 live births) in women and children and mitigate the transmission of vaccine preventable and other infectious diseases.

1 - Needs

Despite the signing of the revitalized peace agreement two years ago, South Sudan remains in a state of a complex emergency (Humanitarian Needs Overview, 2021). An estimated 8.3 million people will need assistance in 2021 of which 5.2 million will require health interventions of which 50% are mainly females and 52% children. The deteriorating humanitarian situation is attributed to the effect of intensified conflict and sub-national violence, a second consecutive year of major flooding, and the impacts of COVID-19 (Humanitarian Needs Overview, COVID 19 Bulletin, Flooding situation report). The health sector faces serious challenges because of worsening food security, high levels of malnutrition, an outbreak of preventable diseases such as malaria, and measles; and reduced access to life-saving essential health services caused by the fighting and floods (HNO, FNSMS). This might worsen the health situation in a country with one of the highest infant mortality rates, maternal mortality rate. In 2020, outbreaks of measles were recorded in some of the counties. According to South Sudan Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response System, Malaria remains the top cause of morbidity (64 percent) and mortality (45 percent).These multiple shocks compounded peoples' psychosocial distress increases the risk of suicides, domestic violence, and GBV and yet specialized mental health services are scarce and limited. Low immunization coverage due to weak health systems and access challenge will expose millions of children to vaccine preventable disease outbreak.States such as Upper Nile, Jonglei, Warrap, and Eastern Equatoria are amongst the most affected in South Sudan. Only 35% of children in Upper Nile received Penta 3 with a dropout rate of 12%, 3281 children are partially vaccinated. Ulang and Nasir are amongst the counties with the least vaccine coverage (EPI Bulletin, December 2020) putting at risk the lives of many children. There are severe health gaps and critical needs in Pochalla, Gogrial East, Nasir, and Ulang as a result of flooding, and inter-communal fights (IRNA Pochalla August 2020, IRNA Ulang October 2020, IRNA Nasir September 2020, and IRNA Gogrial East January 2021). According to the IRNA assessments, over 50,000 people were internally displaced and the recurring health challenges are; increased morbidity, inaccessibility to health services including labor and delivery, shortage of basic medicines, overcrowding, and destroyed WASH facilities. Access to cost-effective medicines such as ACTs, ORS, and Zinc has been disrupted putting the lives of many children at risk. Similarly, uptake and utilization of reproductive health services in all the counties are very low. Only 317 women out of the expected 1,920 delivered at the health facilities supported by UNKEA (DHIS 2, January 2020 to December 2020). The target counties are among counties reporting high GAM rates and poor WASH indicators yet South Sudan has reported cases of COVID 19 in all the states.Without strengthening COVID 19 risk communication and community engagement initiatives, there is a risk of increased morbidity and mortality in the target counties given the low capacity of the health system to absorb any increase in numbers (COVID 19 weekly Epidemiological Bulletin). This project will contribute towards reducing excessive morbidity and mortality in the six counties (Kapoeta East, Magwi, Torit, Pochalla, Nasir, Ulang, and Gogrial East) in the four states of Upper Nile, Eastern Equatoria, Jonglei, and Warrap.

2 - Project activities

UNKEA will prioritize the delivery of life-saving health services in line with the CERF criteria. The focus will be on providing primary health care that is aligned to the South Sudan Basic Package of Health and Nutrition through a network of 38 PHCUs (9 Nair, 1 Ulang, and 12 Gogrial East), 9 PHCCs (4 Nasir, 1 Ulang, 4 Gogrial East), 1 hospital (Nasir), and 7mobile clinics (1 in each of the locations). The clinical package will include;(a) Conduct Emergency preparedness and response: UNKEA will buildtrain and mentor health workers to effectively implement infection prevention and controlin the facilities, perform early reporting of suspected cases, collect samples and facilitatepreliminary isolation of suspected highly contagious cases including COVID 19. UNKEA will ensure that supported health workers compile quality data for submission in the EWARS/DHIS platforms. (b) Provide case management for common causes of morbidity. UNKEA will train, equip and facilitate health workers to provide static and mobile curative outpatient and inpatient servicesfocusing on common causes of morbidity such as malaria, pneumonia, diarrhea, andmeasles. Support will include the provision of incentives, medicines, medical equipment, HMIS tools, and job aids. (c) Provide routine and supplementary immunization activities. UNKEA will support the health workers to provide both routine and supplementary immunization activities targeting vaccine-preventable diseases. Support will include training on Immunization in Practice, developing and implementation of micro plans, requesting and management of vaccines, and monitoring of uptake of vaccine services.(d) Support management of SAM with medical complications. UNKEA will train clinical officers at the supported PHCCs and hospitals to manage cases of SAM with medical complications at the established stabilization centers. The project will work closely with the nutrition departments and ensure the establishment of a robust referral and follow-up mechanism.(e) Strengthen WASH in health facilities. The project will support the construction ofgender-disaggregated pit latrines, construction of incinerators and placenta pits, provisionof handwashing facilities, training of health workers in Infection Prevention and Control,and provision of waste segregation buckets. In addition, handwashing will be promoted atall health education sessions. UNKEA will also integrate assessments for WASH at health facilities in its implementation activities.(f) Strengthen sexual and gender-based violence services through training in CMR: UNKEA will ensure that all clinical staff especially the clinical officers are able to provide CMR services. The health workers will be provided with CMR kits received from UNFPA, and GBV registers for easy documentation. (g) Provision of Mental Health and Psychosocial Services (MHPSS). UNKEA will train health workers in the identification, referral, and management of patients with mental health issues. Health workers at the lower facilities (PHCUs) will focus on identification and referral, while those at the PHCC and hospital will manage the referred cases.(h) Scale-up COVID 19 response. UNKEA will ensure that the health workers conduct COVID 19 risk communication and community engagement activities through providing on job mentorship and Information Education and Communication (IEC) materials. In addition, UNKEA will orient the health workers in the management of COVID 19 that includes strengthening Infection Prevention and Control measures at a health facility, wearing of masks, availing and use of PPE, and isolation and referral protocols.(i) Monitor uptake of services by special groups. The project will ensure that special groups such as old people, women, and people living with disabilities are reached and are using services. Tools such as the disability tracking tools will be used to monitor service uptake. j) Conduct health awareness and promotional activities

3 - Project indicators

The success of the project will be measured using the indicators adopted from the health cluster and collected using the recommended platforms (DHIS 2 and EWARS). The project will also integrate both qualitative and quantitative assessments during project implementation. The key indicators that will be routinely monitored and measured are aligned with cluster strategy including;-Coverage of penta 3 among children in < 1 year old age immunized in the project locations-Number of clean deliveries attended by skilled birth attendants-Malaria incidence rate among children under five and adults segregated by sex or case percentage change-Number of health facilities with functional Basic Emergency Obstetric care-Measles coverage among children 6-15 years of age-Average population per functioning health facility by type(PHCC,PHCU and Hospital)The project will be measured at the output and outcome level. The data will be captured using the routine South Sudan HMIS tools from the available registers. These data will be reviewed weekly, monthly and quarterly against the set targets.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All UNKEA staff and contractors are expected to sign a code of conduct and UNKEA's PSEA policy. At inception meetings, UNKEA will categorically inform the participants of the PSEA policy. At the health facility, the health educators will include sessions on PSEA while the community focal persons will take lead in disseminating information on PSEA. The project will also print abridged versions of the policy in the local language and have the policy displayed at strategic locations at all service delivery points. In all the project meetings both for staff and the community (monthly feedback meetings), dissemination and discussion on PSEA will be included on the agenda.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

The project will purposely ensure that people living with disabilities, people who are not literate, women, and girls participate in the project inception meetings, joining monitoring visits, and community feedback meetings. The project team will ensure that meetings are provided at locations accessible by all persons. In addition, the community resource persons will include all the different categories of people. Information both at the meetings will be provided in both English and the local language thus ensuring that information is received and understood by the participants. The project will instruct the focal persons to receive information both verbal and written and in both English and local language. The translation to the local language, inclusion of women, girls, and people living with disabilities in the community feedback structures will ensure that the needs of all people are catered for and their voices are heard. UNKEA selection criteria for community feedback groups clearly include representation of the special groups.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

The project will use various platforms to provide beneficiaries with information about the project, feedback mechanism, and complaint mechanism. At project inception, meetings will be conducted in the communities targeting key stakeholders including women representatives, youth, local authority, representatives from IDPs, and people with disabilities. UNKEA staff will disseminate the project objectives, activities, and monitoring plans. At this forum, UNKEA will disseminate the feedback/ complaint mechanism whereby the contact person and associated telephone numbers will be shared. The project will also identify and assign community feedback focal persons that will include women, youth, and people with disabilities. On a monthly basis, the project will convene meetings to seek feedback from the focal persons. Also, there will be a focal person at the health facility to seek feedback. UNKEA will ensure that the name of the focal person is displayed and also how to contact the person. Whenever possible, a suggestion box will be placed at strategic points of service delivery. However, the project will adapt the feedback mechanism to lessons learnt along with implementation.

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

UNKEA has built a wealth of experience in using beneficiary feedback to adapt project assistance. At project design, community feedback is incorporated in the selection of project locations, operating times, and service packages. On a quarterly basis, the project will synthesize the beneficiary feedback for emerging themes and recommendations. This feedback will be used to guide the outreach locations but also the mix of service providers. For example, UNKEA used the feedback from the beneficiaries to change the location of the outreach site in one of its projects which resulted in the easing of access to services by women. Also, feedback (client satisfaction surveys through clinic exit interviews) on the quality of services will be used to guide the subsequent mentorship and support supervision. As a result, the services provided will meet the needs and expectations of the beneficiaries.

8.11. Mitigation Measures

The project will ensure that all staff is screened, all staff will sign the code of conduct, and that the PSEA policy is well disseminated. UNKEA protection specialists will monitor the implementation and adherence to the policy. The project will provide services at locations that are safe and close to the communities. The use of chaperones will be encouraged. The project will train health workers and non-clinical staff in the clinical management of rape and GBV service provision. This will promote the identification of clients for timely service delivery. In addition, the project has integrated the design of the referral pathways that will be linked with the county GBV referral pathways. This will ensure that beneficiaries will access services and those requiring referral referred in a timely manner.The project plans to implement a robust community feedback mechanism and will purposely ensure that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, boys, girls, and women are included. 12.1.4. Quality, timely and inclusive protection services (including medical care, legal support, safety and mental health psychosocial services to children, women at risk and GBV survivors

The project will contribute towards promoting quality, timely and inclusive protection services through training of clinical health workers to provide Clinical Management of Rape (GBV) and non-clinical staff in Provision of Psychosocial First Aid. The trained health workers will be equipped with CMR kits and registers. In addition, a robust referral system will be implemented. The referral system will link to the county GBV referral pathway under the leadership of the protection cluster or the county health partner. The referral pathway clearly indicates the available services at the health facilities and in the community and the contact focal persons. The project staff will also participate in the monthly GBV meetings thereby further strengthening coordination and avoiding duplication of services. Whereas the project will not provide outright medical care for clients with mental health challenges, the health works will be trained in identification and referral for those cases. All clients requiring referral will be facilitated by the project referral process that has often involved the use of a boat or hire of the canoe.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The project will provide curative services for common morbidities such as malaria, acute watery diarrhoea and acute respiratory infections that affect the targeted populations. By ensuring that the population is healthy, it is anticipated that they will live active and productive lives that will translate into the ability to access and provide for their families food. The linkage between health and nutritional status is well documented. People that suffer from acute health conditions and frequent infections are more predisposed to being malnourished. By the project addressing the health needs through curative services and health education should result in improved nutritional status of the target beneficiaries.

12.1.3. Equitable access to cross-sectoral basic services to vulnerable populations (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disability and older persons)

The project will provide equitable services to all gender, age, disability, and population groups without discrimination. The services will be tailored to the different needs of the different groups including women, children, persons with disability, older persons, and the elderly. The service packages will include Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI), Integrated Management of Adult Illnesses (IMAI), GBV/CMR services, identification and referral for Mental Health and Pyschosocial Services (MHPSS), and Maternal Newborn and Childhood Health services (MNCH) such as BemONC, and immunization services. The service package is tailored to different age groups. In addition, the Project indicators have been selected to capture the output and impact of the project to each of the different groups to ensure equitable delivery of services and satisfaction of all the targeted vulnerable people. All the data shall be disaggregated according to gender, age, and disability to ensure no group is marginalized. The data of persons with disability accessing services will be captured using Washing Group of Short Questionnaire for South Sudan(WGSS) to ensure persons with disability are not marginalized. All the constructions of WASH facilities like latrines will be disaggregated for both males and females and will be properly labeled with provision for persons with disability and the elderly to avoid their inhibition from use.

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The project has integrated health and nutrition interventions in its design of activities to be able to contribute to decreasing acute malnutrition. The project will offer treatment for SAM with medical complications at PHCCs and Hospital levels to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality from severe acute malnutrition. All the children under five years and pregnant mothers accessing services delivered during the project implementation will be screened for malnutrition at all points of contact and proper procedure will be followed in the management of confirmed cases. A proper referral system will be set up from the community level and lower levels of care to PHCCs and Hospitals where management of cases will be done. The project will offer health education on proper nutrition in the health facilities and offer behavior change communication to the communities to promote the adoption of good nutrition practices to reduce the burden of malnutrition. The project will offer curative consultations and preventive services for the common causes of morbidity especially among children under five which are one of the leading causes of malnutrition. Implementation of the above comprehensive packages will contribute to the reduction of acute malnutrition in the targeted locations that will translate into low Global Acute malnutrition rates.
