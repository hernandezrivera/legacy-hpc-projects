Project Summary

Through this project, which is a continuation of the intervention started in August 2021, SARD intends to support unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) and children at risk of separation through the continuation and extension of its specialized and unspecialized child protection services, which specially target sensitive issues such as family separation, child marriage, child labor, neglect, and violence in two locations; A’zaz and Idleb.
This project will be two-fold: prevention and response to the separation of children from vulnerable families. The preventative part is ensured through case management of children at risk of separation, and living with vulnerable families, through case management and after a thorough assessment of each case, a strategy is identified based on individual needs, including health assistance, reintegration in formal or non-formal education, involvement of children in leisure time activities, PSS activities, etc. To boost the sustainability of the intervention, we will adopt a household approach and work with caregivers, organizing positive parenting sessions and ensuring follow-up of recommendations.
Response to separation will be carried out by the services provided through an Interim Alternative Care Center (IACC) for UASC. The critical physical and psychological situation of referred and/or identified children, who generally have mostly been living in the streets for a period of time included between 3 months and 2 years, require immediate attention and is a real life-saving intervention, encompassing all aspects of the child’s life, from personal care, to emotional care, to enrolment or re-enrolment in (accelerated) education, to structured and unstructured PSS activities. The objective is to create a nurturing space out of the IACC for children.
While providing temporary accommodation, suitable foster families will be identified: first of all, kinship care will be preferred in case members of the extended family are available. If this option is not possible, finding a foster family will be explored. Due to the high sensitivity of the matter, lack of clear legal framework, and lack of cultural acceptance of this practice, SARD can count on the services of a lawyer, who will provide legal and technical assistance. Beyond that, working in close collaboration with the local council, the CP AoR and with partner organisations will be paramount to overcome a challenge for all the international community.
To address the needs of the foster families and families of children at risk of separation, SARD will also adopt a multi-sectoral approach by providing SNF and referral to other departments (internal) and actors (external) in the area.
Last but not least, even after eleven years of humanitarian interventions, the need to strengthen awareness on child rights persists: as an example, children from the age of 13, are considered adults, and are deprived of their rights, including their right to development, education, well-being, and a proper adult care.
To monitor and evaluate the project, SARD has established multiple approaches to pin-down this matter. First, internally, program team organize weekly meeting to monitor the progress of the activities and provide technical support for the team. As well the weekly and monthly reports are used to monitor the progress. Second, M&E team has its own monitoring and evaluation tools to track the progress and quality of the CP activities. They carry out random field visits to monitor the activities at the ES. As for monitoring the CM work, the M&E conduct random phone calls to check the clients' satisfaction. Program team utilize the results from the M&E team and internal report for learning purposes.
Reporting, in addition to the above-mentioned reporting tools such as weekly and monthly reports, also include reporting on the 4Ws and CM reporting tool of the Child Protection Area of Responsibility AoR.

3 - Needs

Needs are identified based on 3 data sources: SARD’s current CP project, a Yearly Needs Assessment (YNA) by SARD and WHH (Nov 22) and the draft of the HNO 2023. According to the HNO 2023, more than 6 million children, are at heightened risk of violence, abuse, and exploitation. Based on the data from SARD’s ongoing CP project, 10% of the cases are UASC, 12% have child labor as the primary risk, 30% of the cases are out of school, 35% have health issues, 13% have neglect, and 5% have domestic violence as the primary risks. Almost all children targeted through case management in the community or hosted at the IACC have experienced psychological distress due to past traumatic events; especially, UASC display behavioural problems, loss of hope in the future, and lack of personal and social skills. From all sources, a significant gap in the child protection system is obvious. Furthermore, the 2022 YNA highlighted that in Azaz and Idleb, 48% of households reported they don’t send their children to school, 47% of boys are working to help their family with the income. KI identified child labour as the top major risk impacting children, followed by early marriage and physical violence against children. During FGD discussion, youth identified early marriage as the highest risk for girls and child labour for boys. In this line, 53% of the respondents to the 2023 HPC GBV analysis 2023 in Aleppo said that child marriage is very common for girls aged 15-17, and 41% in Idleb.

4 - Implementation of activities

A child-rights based approach will be adopted in all phases of the project cycle: design, implementation, and M&E. Children aren’t seen as mere recipients of humanitarian aid in general, but through this project, their participation is fostered, starting from an open dialogue and the formal consent from the child to open the case. We aim to empower children and adolescents temporarily hosted at the center to become “ambassadors of child rights” within their communities during events such as the International Child’s Day on 20th of November.SARD serves cases through various channels: SARD's CM team, the Protection cluster, local authorities, other humanitarian actors, and self-referrals. Following referral/case identification, SARD's CM Team studies each case and sets a tailored plan. The PSS team, and the Interim Alternative Care Center unit, work to provide harmonised CP services.The main activities that will be provided for the cases are: family tracing and reunification or alternative care arrangements/foster placement; structured and unstructured PSS; school enrolment; legal assistance (including, issuing civil registration); livelihood support for caregivers; and Emergency Case Fund (ECF) for high-risk cases in need of adequate services, which are not provided for free in the area by other actors/service providers.In situations where protection services are outside SARD's capacity, such cases are referred to other humanitarian actors for proper management.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Perpetuating stigma for children and their families while providing CM and/or parenting program or by providing low quality service and risk of physical safety of CP workers if perceived to be interfering with family concerns.Likelihood: Medium - Impact: HighMitigation Measures: SARD team is trained with materials that are in line with CP minimum standards and uses service maps that includes only quality services.Risk: Breaching the confidentiality of the UASC at the IACC.Likelihood: Medium - Impact: high Mitigation Measures: SARD sensitizes the staff and partners regularly on the accessibility and confidentiality policy of the IACC and holds data confidentiality to the highest standards.Risk: Existing traditional practices conflicts with CP principles and the socio-cultural context is a barrier to identify and work with unaccompanied and separated girls.Likelihood: Medium - Impact: MediumMitigation Measures: SARD ensures consultation and mobilisation of key persons in the community to increase self-referral, conducts AR on child rights and provide culturally adapted information on key CP issues and responsibilities of different people in the growth of children. Risk: Foster families/families exploit the fostered/reunified child for aid.Likelihood: Medium - Impact: HighMitigation Measures: SARD engages the families in AR prior to foster placement/reunification, conduct M&E regarding the family’s mindset, and conducts a parenting program.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

In 2023, strengthening external knowledge and compliance with safeguarding policy and PSEA is an institutional objective at SARD, also by organising surveys and trainings of associated service providers. During implementation of activities, the main risk is that child or adult beneficiaries may be targets of SEA by a staff member or associate. To mitigate it, SARD is reinforcing the CRM to facilitate reporting of abuse (including by making the system more child-friendly), including referral pathways; staff undergo regular training on PSEA; awareness raising on safeguarding policy (including PSEA) will be conducted for both, adults and children.In addition, to build resilience from any form of sexual misconduct, sex education sessions will also be organized at the IACC (based on the International Technical Guidance on Sexuality Education by UNESCO – 2018), to make boys and girls more conscious of the difference between consensual intercourse and /harassment.Another form to promote gender equality include awareness raising, which will help to challenge gender stereotypes and patriarchal norms and reinforce AAP. Finally, SARD works with the whole households and has access to a space where power dynamics are clearly in favour of men, taking this opportunity to enhance women and girls’ agency by adopting a gender-based approach, collecting sex and age disaggregated data informed by gender sensitive lens that captures women and girls’ views and allows to adapt the answer.

Services should not be provided by any staff who have not received and demonstrated proficiency in training modules.3. Include a practice element to training modules that can be overseen through remote mentorship arrangements to allow for practice prior to service provision.4. Service mappings should only include services that have been determined to provide safe and quality services.5. Establish consultation with communities and ensure feedback mechanisms are in place.6. Child Protection messaging to non-Protection actors.
