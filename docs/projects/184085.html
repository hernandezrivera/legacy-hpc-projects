Project Summary

In response to significant health, nutrition and WASH gaps in Gedaref and East Darfur states, ZOA in partnership with Medical Teams International proposes an integrated health and WASH response in Um Rakouba and Tenedba camps. In South and East Darfur, ZOA will address critical WASH groups in 6 localities (Bahr el Arab, Assalaya, Abu Karinka and Abu Jabra in ED; Damso and Bileil in SD).

1 - Needs

Sudan is now one year into the Ethiopian Refugee Response, a humanitarian crisis triggered by a massive military campaign by the Ethiopian government toward the Tigrayan People’s Liberation Front (TPLF) in the Tigray region. This sudden onset conflict caused mass displacement into Sudan’s eastern states (Kassala, Gedaref) and recently, to Blue Nile state as conflict continues to spread throughout the Tigray, Amhara, Afar and Benishangul-Gumuz regions in Ethiopia. There are now 55,785 Ethiopian refugees in eastern Sudan, with 87% of them in Gedaref and Kassala states with Um Rakouba and Tunaydbah camps established, and another new camp to be opened. Eventually, border sites were established at entry points to initially screen refugees, then transfer them to two newly established camps in Gedaref state (Um Rakouba and Tunaydbah). These camps are both at capacity now and currently, UNHCR and the Sudanese government are leading the search for a new camp, based on new planning assumptions of upwards of 63,00 new arrivals until the end of 2021 due to a recent massive increase in fighting in Ethiopia. Growing tension between host communities in Gedaref and the refugee populations is also becoming more apparent due to the poor economic situation in Sudan (evidenced during the Al Fao locality flooding incidents). WASH (particularly sanitation) gaps are alarming, with high prevalences of vector and water-borne diseases, such as the recent Hepatitis E outbreak in Um Rakouba camp. East Darfur hosts the highest number of South Sudanese refugees in the country, at 93,414 total. The selected localities (Bahr el Arab, Assalaya, Adila and Abu Karinka) hosts 65% (60,405) of the SSR population (UNHCR Dashboard August 2021). Kario camp in Bahr el Arab in particular hosts 45,430 South Sudanese refugees, with ongoing high sanitation needs. The 2022 WASH HNO shows high percentages of populations drinking from surface water and high rates of open defecation, signalling potentially catastrophic health outcomes. Most of the targeted localities show poor sanitation and access to water sources. Bahr el Arab, Bileil and East Galabat show particularly poor WASH outcomes. In Bahr El Arab (ED), approximately 20% of the population are disposing of human faeces in open spaces. In Bileil (SD), 44% and in East Galabat (GED), 19%. In Bahr el Arab (ED), approx. 16% are drinking from surface water, Mellit - 25% and East Galabat – 20% leading to potentially catastrophic health outcomes, particularly during/after the rainy season. According to the Ministry of Health, in Sudan, some 2.2 million children suffer from malnutrition annually (Global Acute Malnutrition) out of which over 573,000 suffer from Severe Acute Malnutrition. In Sudan, the Global Acute Malnutrition and Severe Acute Malnutrition rates are 14.1% and 4.5%. Vitamin A supplementation coverage is estimated at 39.87%. The age-appropriate dietary diversity is only 24.11% indicating that malnutrition is not just caused by lack of food. The health systems in Darfur remains weak and inadequately prepared to provide basic life-saving health services and respond to outbreaks. In Darfur, it is reported that 26% of the health facilities are not functional. The coverage of measles vaccination is only 55.9% while only 38.3% of children are fully vaccinated. Only 54% of the clinical OPD cases are managed according to the protocol despite training and supportive supervisions, which signals the need for strengthening support and follow up to ensure adherence to protocols. Health promotion, disease surveillance and referral remain poor due to low coverage of community outreach activities. Health facility delivery rate is also low (11.5%) which requires an increased effort on identification and referral of pregnant mothers to give birth at facility level. 2 - Activities/Outputs

Ethiopian Refugee Response Since March 2021, ZOA and Medical Teams International (MTI), have been implementing an integrated health, nutrition and WASH response in Um Rakuba camp and support a newly constructed PHC unit (which serves +/- 200 patients/day from refugee and host communities) in Zone 2 and community health activities in Zone 2 and 4. The proposed response will maintain delivery of existing health services currently implemented by ZOA/MTI, and complement the response with a larger scale latrine intervention. CHWs will work with clinical teams to follow up with cases at home and support in defaulter tracing and identifying those who have not accessed services. Output 1: Primary healthcare services provided through (1) construction/rehabilitation of health facilities and (2) delivery of integrated package of health interventions incl. medical consultations, immunization, antenatal care and delivery, neonatal and child health and epidemiological surveillance. Output 2: Secondary Health Care Services Provided through establishment/maintenance of prompt and effective referral system to secondary/tertiary health care and delivery of multi-layered mental health and psychosocial support incl. Output 3: Nutrition Services Provided through effective treatment of acute malnutrition (CMAM) and effective prevention of undernutrition and micronutrient deficiencies. South Sudanese Refugee response Building on ZOA’s previous refugee response in East Darfur (cash programming and provision of access to water in Kario), ZOA will scale up WASH activities in Bahr el Arab, Assalaya, Adila and Abu Karinka localities. Activities will include: WASH Install 2 new mini water yards, with solarization. Rehabilitate 2 water yards including solarization. Construction of 6 communal latrines of 4 stances at 30 persons/latrine. Rehabilitate 6 shared household/communal latrines (for multiple households, in markets schools, health facilities etc) - ND (4), ED (4), GED (4). Provision of 3,000 dignity kits in SD and ED. Conducting hygiene promotion campaigns in communal set ups (all locations). Education Construct 5 primary school classrooms in SD. Rehabilitated 5 primary school classrooms in SD. Construct 20 gender-segregated and disability-friendly latrine blocks at schools in SD. Construct permanent or semi-permanent hand washing stations in SD.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

As part of protection mainstreaming, ZOA and partners will ensure the following during the project’s design and implementation: Gender sensitive latrines and water cleaning points separate for boys and girls will be constructed in schools while in public social places, separate latrines will be constructed for men and women. Basic facilities including lighting will be installed in public places to ensure safety to women and children when using them even at night. Further, latrines constructed in public places through this project support will include special ones with ramps for safe and dignified access by PWDs especially those who are physically challenged. Latrines in public places and schools will be labelled to clearly demarcate those for females from males. In the water infrastructure rehabilitation and development activities, the project will ensure to put in place the following protection measures: Ensure that the water points will be at points determined unanimously by the community to avoid any tensions between different segments of a community; the water points are at a central place easily accessed by most users; and the timing for access; if regulated, is convenient for majority of users. Further, water pumps will be well positioned so that even children, the elderly, and pregnant women can easily use; separate watering points for animals;

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As part of accountability, ZOA has a complaints reporting mechanism in place in all its programming that is adopted by all implementing partners. The reporting mechanism is part of a broader three-pronged organizational integrity framework (IF) made up of i) code of conduct (coc)signed by all staff and consultants; ii) complaints reporting mechanism for staff, partners and beneficiaries and iii) Addressing complaints. Under the CoC are the child protection policy, PSEAH and fraud & corruption, among others. Each new ZOA project develops a beneficiary reporting mechanism that is shared with beneficiaries at the beginning of the project through sensitization so that they know their rights on where and whom to report to incase they have complaints.
