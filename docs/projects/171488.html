Project Summary

Somalia is experiencing a large-scale humanitarian crisis due to the compounding effects of protracted conflict and drought. Poverty, elevated environmental shocks, widespread use of harmful traditional practices and weak rule of law, have created overlapping protection concerns that disproportionately affect the safety of women and girls and, undermined individuals’, families’ and communities’ abilities to cope. This is particularly true for Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) who are extremely vulnerable to sexual exploitation and other forms of abuse due to the lack of purchasing power, exposed living conditions and limited clan protection. Among the IDP population women and girls are at greater risk, especially those from minority clans, female-headed households, and female elderly persons. Moreover, the recent COVID-19 pandemic, floods and the locust invasion in 2020 have exacerbated GBV risks due to economic impact on household livelihoods. Evidence suggests that food insecurity is linked to an increase in GBV and may also lead to conflict over scarce resources and generate violence. GBV is known to be under-reported in Somalia due to stigma and fear of retaliation from a survivor’s own family members and/or perpetrators. Furthermore, GBV Information Management System (GBVIMS) continues to show high prevalence of sexual violence, physical assault, and other forms of GBV among women and girls, particularly those living in the IDP settlements. Many protection concerns stem from negative and hazardous coping mechanisms applied by destitute and severely food-insecure families. Limitations on access to reporting mechanisms among affected populations, due to social exclusion and marginalization, including gender discrimination, heightens the vulnerability of the affected populations to sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA). To address these, WV seeks to provide disability-sensitive quality services in 2021. WV will address GBV needs through a prevention, mitigation and response perspective in Somaliland, Puntland, South West State and Jubaland where the organization has physical presence and ongoing interventions on Child Protection, Health, Nutrition WASH and FSL. The prevention interventions will focus on increasing awareness on GBV prevention through IEC materials, radio and direct messages to registered beneficiaries through the adapted toll free line. WV will leverage on existing structures and strengthen capacity and knowledge on GBV prevention and existing laws that prevent GBV. Central to prevention interventions, WV will engage communities and create platforms that enable dialogue with specific groups (such as men, women, boys and girls) on GBV prevention to strengthen awareness at community level. Through Channels of Hope Gender training, attitudes, practices and behavior change will be addressed in a bid to curb violence and gender inequality. Mitigation interventions will focus on safety audits and safety perception mapping of the most vulnerable households. WV will also distribute solar lamps to IDPs to improve safety, particularly for women and girls and, channel efforts towards mitigation of GBV incidences. The response interventions will focus on provision of all services necessary to respond GBV after it has occurred. WV aims to ensure access to safe, timely, confidential, quality coordinated GBV specialized services through integrated referrals. WV will provide individually specialized services through case management. For hard to reach areas, WV will provide GBV mobile outreaches to ensure equal reach of vulnerable households including those with disabilities. WV will provide material assistance to GBV survivors through cash programming to enable these beneficiaries have greater choice and dignity on prioritized individual needs. WVI will work with disability persons organizations for enhanced capacity building on GBV prevention and response including the reporting and referral pathway.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

In line with the standards for infectious disease outbreaks response, cash and vouchers will be used as a means to prevent exposure to COVID-19 through in kind distribution. Cash and voucher assistance directly to beneficiaries will prevent delays in access to essential commodities due to logistical hurdles that could arise as a result of another COVID-19 state and country level lock down leading to delays in procurement and distribution. Cash and voucher assistance enables target beneficiaries have greater choice and dignity by allowing them to have purchase power and choice on prioritiesed individual needs, while at the same time stimulating local economy and improving the market cash flow by promoting local businesses and providing a source of livelihoods which is a factor in prevention of GBV. WVI will prioritize cash transfers to channel material assistance to GBV survivors for procurement of supplies and unique gender needs as opposed to providing a standard in kind package.

Mitigation Measures

1. Risk of no access to or awareness of protection assistance or referral services for survivors of protection incidents (i.e. GBV, child protection, evictions, explosive hazards, etc.). This risk will be addressed by factoring in mobile outreaches to far and hard to reach locations to ensure timely access to services. A mapping of the available GBV service providers alongside the telephone of their focal points will be displayed in health facilities and other social amenities like schools in the Somali language. 2. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities.The project has considered working with disabled persons organizations for consultation and make a provision for service delivery tailor-made to reach marginalized communities and PWD e.g. community outreaches, remote service delivery e.g. for MHPSS and individualized follow up. School clubs will be used to reach children with GBV prevention, mitigation and response activities including the creation of women and girl-friendly spaces. 3. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms.WVI already has an existing community response mechanism which will be integrated in this project to increase access to provide feedback and complaints. The toll free number 364 will be displayed in all service delivery sites in the Somali language. Monitoring of the response to complaints in this system usually forms part of the senior leadership team monthly updates until the issue raised is resolved. Suggestion boxes will continue to be used in schools alongside the setting up of help desks where boys and girls can walk in and share concerns about WVI service delivery. 4. Risk that insecurity prevents access to beneficiaries or monitoring of the project.Somalia experiences periodic insecurity incidences, particularly in South. WV staff has a full-time security team (composed of a Security Advisor and field-based Security Officers) who continuously monitor the security context, liaising closely with International NGO Safety Organization (INSO) Somalia and the local security apparatus to keep abreast of the evolving context and make informed decisions. Staff weekly updates on the security situation. 5. Risk of under reporting of GBV cases due to sensitivity of issues related to GBV and SGBV.The project management team will be deliberate and actively participate in existing regional and central GBV cluster and coordination meetings aimed at promoting identification, reporting, referring and addressing GB incidents that occur in the project target locations. With regards to persons with disabilities, WV will be deliberate in including them as project participants through sensitization of the community to provide a representation slot in structures such as the GBV response committees, IDP camp management, water committees etc. WV will support and include the families of persons with disabilities and Disabled Persons Organizations (DPOs) in GBV prevention awareness raising and subsequent training on the reporting and referral pathways. This will require collaborating with local DPOs to support with identifying the most vulnerable persons with disabilities. In addition to address barriers of access to services because of structural and mobility challenges, WVI also proposes to provide mobile outreaches. Remote PSS will also be used to reach PWD to address mobility constraints. Direct messages to their phones will also be sent ahead of meetings and trainings to address information gaps. The faith leaders will also be equipped to address stigma around disability through including disability as a vulnerability factors during emergency situations. This will be integrated in already planned for community engagement sessions and through IEC materials as well.
