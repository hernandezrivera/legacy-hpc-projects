Project Summary

The scale, severity and complexity of the humanitarian needs of people in Syria remain extensive. This is the result of continued hostilities in localized areas, new and protracted displacement, increased self-organized returns and the sustained erosion of communities’ resilience during close to eight years of crisis. Across Syria, 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity, with certain population groups facing particularly high levels of vulnerability. Whilst there has been a reduction in violence in many parts of the country over the past year, the impact of present and past hostilities on civilians remains the principal driver of humanitarian needs in Syria (HNO Syria 2020).
The project aims to increase the resilience of ID Households who had to leave their houses due to the deterioration of the security status which have had intensified in the past four months, as well as targeting a percentage of the host communities who match IYD’s selection criteria. The project will provide food rations on monthly basis for six months to 5,000 families; 2,500 families in camps of Dana Sub-district in Idleb Province and 2,500 families in Afrin sub-district (off camps) in Aleppo province, 50% of the targeted audience will be from the host communities. Furthermore, the information provided to IYD indicated that distributed food baskets must include various items that provide nutrients to the body needs.
In addition, the project will distribution bread to beneficiaries every other day as a part of the general food assistance, which covers the minimum need.
Because they mostly displaced in hast under aerial or artillery bombardment, the majority of IDPs were not able to carry their basic personal or livelihood items, and therefore, have no living resources or any means for purposes of exchange. Even those who came with limited cash or livelihood assets have exhausted their resources because of the prolonged conflict in Syria. Therefore, the IDPs are wholly dependent on humanitarian assistance for their daily needs and require a general distribution of food baskets to meet their daily caloric nutrient requirements. In addition, the host communities in both provinces Aleppo and Idleb, are agricultural communities with very limited financial and livelihood resources, and the IDPs because liability on the host communities because of this lack of livelihood, this issue is clearer in Afrin sub-district due to the limited number of NGOs which provides humanitarian support.
Consultation with several potential beneficiaries have been made to ensure their full understanding of their entitlements and when these are to be delivered. This consultation is intended to continue by IYD’s team to reach all beneficiaries.
IYD's MEAL plan will be done by regularly monitoring the implemented activities and evaluating the objective achievements, to ensure that the project meets the required quality and time frame, to tackle challenges faced and summarize lessons learned.
The IYD’s MEAL team will prepare a post-distribution monitoring report; this will be prepared in participation with the program team. The next step will be data analysis and issue the report with key findings.
IYD will send the 4Ws report to appropriate clusters in order to find the gap if any and coordinate more on the cluster level. And at the end of the project, IYD will prepare a final narrative and financial report.
IYD, will pay particular attention to this role and to ensure a high standard of recruitment.
IYD will coordinate with camps management committee “in Idleb Province only” (A civilian body works to coordinate the response in the camps), and with local councils in Afrin sub-district in Aleppo province. This coordination will facilitate IYD’s work especially in terms of data collection and verification, assist in solving any disputes and problems that might happen during the distribution and others.

4 - Needs

To have a factual and tangible gap analysis for the proposed project areas, IYD has conducted a needs assessment covered all the project areas.Through this study, we considered that the minimum daily need for bread is 2 loaves per person; providing that a loaf of bread weighs 0.142 kg whereas the current daily distributed bread covers the beneficiaries’ minimum need. In terms of priority, provision of safe shelter came first for IDPs, while the provision of nutritional programs for lactating women and young children was the top priority for the host communities, provision of extra quantities of subsidized bread was ranked second, and the need for food baskets was ranked third. The IDPs requested the provision of appropriate fuel for cooking; it is stated that the IDPs resort to using cheaper yet more dangerous means of cooking, which causes fires and injures of many IDPs due to the bonfire of cooking stoves usage. According to the needs assessment, the food gap has effectively affected the studied communities. In the same context, the studied HHs has reported that 86% of the families have no food stock for more than one month. As a result, the HHs especially women heading households found themselves obliged to face the harsh circumstances of the lack of assistance especially food one. The coping strategies of the studied families vary between relying on less preferred and less expensive food items, reducing the number of daily meals, buying food items on credit, sending the family members to beg, borrowing food, keeping the whole day with no food, and sending the children to eat with neighbors. In relation to the food items which the studied communities prefer are: cereals, bread, sugar, beans, oil, ghee, meat, egg, tea, coffee, fruits, and vegetables.

5 - Implementation of activities

IYD field team will distribute General Food Assistant GFA on a monthly basis to 5,000 HHs for six months; 2,500 families in camps of Dana Sub-district in Idleb Province and 2,500 HHs in Afrin sub-district (off camps) in Aleppo province. The implementation will go through•Registration: IYD team will visit the potential beneficiaries and collect their data, and according to a set of selection criteria, IYD team will select the beneficiaries of the project. •Distribution: IYD team will coordinate with local authorities to determine a food distribution point “FDP”, where the beneficiaries will receive their rations. In the FDP, the beneficiaries are requested to provide an ID card to prove their identity. After validating the beneficiaries’ IDs, IYD team will deliver the ration. The elderly and pregnant women will be given priority during the distribution process. As for the bread in the camps, IYD will contract a bakery which will transfer the wheat flour from IYD warehouse to the bakery’s storage, bake the bread, and deliver it to the camps, where IYD team will distribute the bread every other day to beneficiaries for free of charge. Besides, there will be a queue for males and another for females to prevent any harassment cases which might happen if only one queue is available.•Post Distribution monitoring: IYD MEAL team will conduct PDM with beneficiaries to verify that the delivery of commodities is done according to IYD and donor distribution methods and policies. •Finally, it is worth to mention that each benefited HHs, regardless of its size, will receive one food ration of 60 kg per month. In camps’ distribution only “Idleb Province”, and due to lack of bakeries and baking equipment inside the camps, IYD will contract bakeries to bake and deliver 2,500 KG of bread every other day. This means that each HHs will receive 15 bundles of bread on a monthly basis. On the other hand, the selected 2,500 BNFs in Afrin will receive the wheat flour as part of GFA.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The project aims to reduce the impact of the mentioned above strategies that were conducted by the community before the project, such as early marriage vulnerable, child labor, and school dropping. These actions have a harmful effect and impact on the groups in the community. Unfortunately, acts of gross misconduct are sometimes recorded among humanitarian workers. These include cases of demanding money in return for assisting, or even asking for sexual services, etc. IYD, within this project, will seek that the following factors are highly considered: • Providing distribution sites with shaded areas so that beneficiaries would be protected from different climate and weather conditions.• Sick people and pregnant women will always be given priority while distribution is taking place. • A queue for females and another for males should be formed in the distribution site to prevent any harassment cases which might happen if only one queue is available.• The distribution should be done on equitable bases with no discrimination irrespective of the gender, age, skin color, religion and others• The relations between the beneficiaries and IYD’s teams will be based on mutual confidence, consent and respect, while IYD’s team will be highly aware of the behaviors or acts which might cause resentment among the beneficiaries. • Ensuring that no abuse of any kind will take place (especially aid in return for money or sexual services) through: Conducting regular training for IYD staff in relation to abuse through Code of Conduct, accompanied by a clear, enforced, and well-communicated disciplinary system. It essential that the new staff recruited to be well-educated on these issues. Establishing a complaint mechanism in order to respond to the complaints and feedback submitted by the target community. A separate investigation will be conducted if any abuse by one of IYD’s staff against a beneficiary happened or believed it has happened.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: Full support from the local authorities and host communities to implement the project’s activities smoothly. Risk: Disputes between the host communities and the IDPs might take place in terms of the benefiting from the project’s activities. Cross border closure between Turkey and Syria. Steal or loss of food rations. The fuel price is a risk for bakeries which is changeable according to the exchange rate, IYD will sign a fixed-price contract with the bakeries to have a stable cost during the implementation. Attempts of interference by military groups might take place. Mitigation: IYD will conduct FGDs on the selection criteria, as well as distributing leaflets give more details on the selection criteria. IYD will check for an alternative from the local markets if the border closed for a long time. The food commodities will be stored in a well-placed, safe and guard warehouses. The warehouses will be locked and monitored 7/24.
