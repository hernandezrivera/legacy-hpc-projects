Project Summary

The IRC will be implementing Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH) activities to support refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and host communities across Gedaref, Khartoum, Blue Nile, White Nile, and Jazeera states. The IRC aims to improve the availability, quality, accessibility, and reliability of clean drinking water sources by strengthening the maintenance and operation of drinking water sources. Additionally, the IRC will promote the adoption of positive hygiene behaviors and practices, especially regarding the safe water chain activities from collection to consumption of clean drinking water.
The IRC will determine and execute project interventions in Blue Nile's Wad al Mahi locality and White Nile's Kosti and Rahad localities with the highest unmet needs. The IRC will also administer WASH activities in other areas that can be safely accessed by project staff. To ensure long-term sustainability, the IRC will primarily utilize local methods and materials to complement the impact of water supply infrastructure activities.
The IRC will form Water Management Committees (WMCs) to support projects. The members will be selected based on coordination with community leaders and will consist of approximately 5 to 6 members. The selection criteria will include individuals from the targeted community, those over 18 years of age who can read and write in Arabic and are active participants in community work. Those with technical skills or trusted human resources such as masons, teachers, community health volunteers (CHVs), and religious/customary leaders will be given preference. Women will be specifically targeted to be trained and selected to take on leadership and management roles within the WMCs, and the IRC will advocate to ensure women make up at least 50% of the WMC members.
Moreover, WASH volunteers will operate within the project's three target locations. The IRC anticipates having at least two CHVs (segregated by gender) for every 1,000 individuals within the target communities. Women, in particular, will be empowered and trained to take on the role of CHVs to serve as agents for change within their respective communities.
Lastly, the IRC will respond to the emergency by supporting the affected people from the ongoing conflict in Khartoum, Jazeera, and the IDPs in Gedaref, Blue Nile, and White Nile states by providing WASH services at the health facilities and community levels.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The IRC's WASH project will involve the active participation and feedback of target beneficiaries, particularly women, through focus group discussions (FGDs), and their inclusion in the WUCs and as hand pump mechanics. The IRC will also increase the registration and identification of persons with disabilities and older people to ensure that their unique needs are adequately addressed while strengthening existing community support structures for these groups, such as surrogate water collectors. Hygiene promotion activities will include information on services available for survivors of abuse and sexual violence. Beneficiaries will be made aware of the IRC's Code of Conduct and opportunities for reporting to prevent and reduce the risk of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The proposed project will not involve drilling new boreholes but instead focus on repairing existing water sources to reduce local tensions around new borehole sites. The IRC will engage with the targeted communities and local authorities at the village and locality levels to explain its principles of impartiality and provide opportunities for complaints and redress through focus group discussions, community leader meetings, and suggestion boxes within supported health facilities. At the start of the project, the IRC will conduct a needs assessment to identify further households with people with disabilities and older adults to ensure the successful delivery of interventions, particularly regarding water collection and hygiene awareness. The IRC will also identify and strengthen existing cultural and behavioral practices that promote inclusive behaviors for vulnerable groups, such as collecting water for them. Community Health Volunteers (CHVs) will also conduct house-to-house visits to identify people with disabilities or special needs within their respective communities, conduct follow-ups, and mutually agree on ensuring their unique needs are continuously and adequately addressed.

4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

IRC Sudan will work closely with community structures throughout the project by training them to identify and discuss issues that may result in changes or adaptations in implementation. This ensures accountability to the affected population, as they are involved in finding solutions that will bring about change.In addition, the IRC Sudan uses Project Cycle Meetings (PCM) to enhance its project management approach in a rapidly changing environment. The PCM guarantees that the right people have access to the right data, allowing them to make timely and effective decisions. IRC teams use the latest MEAL (monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning), client feedback, security, and risk analysis data to make informed decisions about project adjustments and improvements. These decisions are documented and shared with clients and donors whenever they require major changes in project approach or deliverables.A key component of PCMs is a "project dashboard," an MS Excel-based tool used to analyze project data, assess project performance, and identify and plan actions to improve project delivery. The project lead is responsible for updating and monitoring the tool and convening regular meetings to discuss any issues or obstacles that may impact implementation and demand decisions. The tool includes sections for project progress, performance indicators, project spending, and an action tracker, as well as major aspects of the supply chain, staffing, client responsiveness and feedback, context and security, partnerships, donor compliance, and reporting.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

The IRC will conduct regular feedback sessions with women and girls within the community to ensure activities and services are adequately responding to their needs. Women, girls, and parents will be involved in the decision-making regarding the location of the latrines, contents of the MHM Kits and water points. Client satisfaction surveys will also be conducted with survivors of WASH on a voluntary basis. The IRC team will engage with women, girls, children, and caregivers from crisis-affected communities in planning and implementing strategies to prevent, mitigate and address the protection issues with WASH intervention. Throughout implementation, the IRC will leverage its Meal and Evaluation (M&E) and feedback systems to ensure community involvement in the design and re-design of the action. The IRC will conduct outreach and awareness to ensure that beneficiaries are aware of the IRC Code of Conduct, Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse policies, Child Safeguarding policies, process for providing feedback, and the principles of beneficiary feedback (including confidentiality, non-retaliation, and accountability). The IRC will provide multiple channels of feedback to beneficiaries including FGDs, key informant interviews (KIIs), post-distribution monitoring (PDM) surveys, KAP survey, feedback boxes, and a hotline. Moreover, the IRC has zero tolerance for bullying, harassment and sexual exploitation and abuse. The IRC has the following policies in place: Child Safeguarding Policy; Beneficiary Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse Policy; Harassment Free Workplace Policy. All allegations and investigation information received is maintained in a confidential centralized case management system. Cases are evaluated and prioritized when received considering, among other things, whether the alleged conduct is ongoing or historical. The IRC will ensure that all new staff hired for IRC Sudan are trained on and understand the seriousness with which the IRC takes such issues. The IRC also has a neutral department called the global Ethics & Compliance Unit - ECU, responsible for all major complaints, including SEA and abuse complaints.
