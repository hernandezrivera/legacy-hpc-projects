Project Summary

As showed FSNAU report The 2019 Gu rains (April-June) have dismally failed, resulting in a second consecutive below-average rainy season while Somalia is still recovering from the impact of the prolonged 2016-17 drought. Except the 2018 Gu (April-June), every rainy season since late 2015 has been below average, leading to increased vulnerability and decreased coping ability. The 2019 Gu is the third driest on record since the early 1980 and has resulted in widespread crop failure and accelerated decline in livestock productivity. Consequently, the number of people in crisis and emergency levels of food insecurity or worse is expected to reach 6.3 million by December 2019. This is more than 40 per cent higher than January this year. Malnutrition, drought-related diseases and displacement are on the rise.
The September 2019 Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) estimated that 1.2 million people face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher) and an additional 3.6 million are Stressed (IPC Phase 2) between July and September 2019, in the presence of humanitarian assistance. Without sustained humanitarian assistance, 2.1 million people will face Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher) outcomes between October to December 2019, including an additional 4.2 million people expected to be Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people facing acute food insecurity to 6.3 million.
The most vulnerable men, women, boys ,girls and the elderly will be prioritized during the beneficiary selection process. However, acute food insecurity is prevalent among populations who lost most of their animals and who became destitute during the 2018-2019 severe drought, those affected by flooding in April and May 2018 and others who were affected by large-scale and protracted.
To ensure sustainability, Somali Children Development Association (SOCDA) will develop a revolving kitty that will provide livelihood assets to affected families to enhance household nutrition. The affected families will be provided with seeds and a goat which they will be expected to repay in kind so that other families can also benefit in future. This will motivate especially the male members to get involved in this project and also enhance sustainability. In addition, the campaigns and outreach educational activities using communication for behavior change will enhance the male behavior towards Food Security to promote project sustainability.

Project Activities

Activities 1 - Improve households’ immediate access to food through provision of conditional and unconditional assistance depending on the severity of food insecurity phases, vulnerability and seasonality of the livelihoods. 2 - Protect and restore livelihoods, related food and income sources, through provision of seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and technical support in rural and peri-urban settings. 3. - Support household and community resilience to shocks, through rehabilitation and/or restoration of productive assets, enhance livelihood strategies and disaster preparedness. 4.Farm inputs: A- Provision of good quality seeds - sorghum, cowpeas, sesame. B-provision of tractor for land preparation to selected vulnerable households . 5. Restocking: Purchasing of goats and distributing to vulnerable HH who lost their livestock and willing to re-start their normal occupations as agro-pastoralist. 6.Provision of two cycles of unconditional cash transfers to 2,100 IDPs and Host Communities (US$ 75 each cycle). 7. Provision of conditional cash transfer to reconstruct/rehabilitate community assets such as canals, water catchments and public sanitation for 900 households in Burdhubo Town.8.Gender Based Education Sensitizing of community members on gender violence problems associated with FGM Meetings and dialogue with elders on issues affecting children, women and youth Education and sensitization of community members on the need for girl-child education.

Mitigation Measures

2a. - Identify groups at risk of unsafe livelihoods practices (eg. child labor, survival/transnational sex) and priorities provision of alternatives for these groups - Collect and use data dis aggregated by age, gender and diversity to effectively inform programming. - Ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable - Identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalized groups - Apply a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries – otherwise vulnerable persons, such as older people, may easily be excluded as other more mobile and vocal population groups are registered. - Ensure lack of documentation does not exclude individuals from accessing services 2b. - Communicate information through various means to reach the broader community and to account for different literacy levels (eg. door-to-door, poster, radio, social media, use of pictograms). - Identify the power dynamics in the area. Who has access to food, assets and inputs? Who is able to influence decisions on food, agriculture and livelihoods? - Consider the gender balance of distribution teams and committees to ensure that there are enough women available as a contact point for women in the community - Ensure that both men and women have equal and fair access to work opportunities(including cash/food for-work schemes), micro-credit, cash transfers, agricultural inputs and skills training and that they receive the same benefits for their input and work. - Ensure that marginalized and disenfranchised groups have equal and fair access to livelihood/agricultural assets ( 4b. - Consult both displaced and host communities about food security and livelihoods needs and assess whether assistance could cause tension, harassment or conflict between the two communities. - Ensure that marginalized and disenfranchised groups have equal and fair access to livelihood/agricultural assets - Be transparent with the affected populations by providing them with accessible and timely information on selection criteria for targeted assistance, organisational procedures and processes that affect them - Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improve programming, understand community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct. Eg. place feedback surveys in food baskets, feedback box at distribution site, phone lines - FSL staff and relief committees should be representative of all groups within the community (e.g. gender, age, ethnicity, socioeconomic group, disability) and all staff and members should receive protection mainstreaming training
