Project Summary

The crisis in Somalia is characterised by prolonged conflict and recurrent drought and flooding. These have resulted in large displacements over the years and the loss of life, assets and livelihoods. An estimated 2.6 million Somalis are displaced within the country.
According to the UNOCHA Humanitarian bulletin for September 2020, Seasonal hagaa flooding since July has resulted in the displacement of nearly 342,000 people, inundated 294 villages and destroyed property and about 15,000 to 20,000 hectares of assorted crops. Most of the displaced people live in IDP settlements who mainly rely on humanitarian assistance. Secondary displacements require shelter and NFI assistance each time they move to a new location. Lack of such assistance makes them more vulnerable to further shocks regardless of the displaced location. Shelter and NFI items remain unaffordable to many and people are resorting to coping mechanisms such as scavenging for NFIs, borrowing cash, living with others and moving from one location to another.
Distribution of shelter kits and NFI has been one of the main interventions to address the immediate lifesaving needs of the affected population. CPD has been running various interventions addressing the needs of IDPs in four (4) states (Galmudug, South West, Hirshabelle States and Benadir Region) in South Central Somalia. CPD in close coordination with other humanitarian actors has also been conducting rapid assessments and focus group discussions to feed into ongoing interventions as well as design/plan for new projects.
According to a Joint Rapid Assessment carried out in Galmudug State in May 2020 by the government and humanitarian partners including CPD, five IDP settlements hosting 2,000 households (12,000 people) have partially or completely lost their shelters following a heavy storm in Adado town of Galgadud region. The affected IDPs settlements include: Badbaado, Kulmiye, Karama, Daryeel and Xeeb. Moreover, 700 vulnerable households (4,200 people) from the host community also lost their shelters and household items.
The make-shift shelter of the existing IDPs has also been affected by tear and wear and needs replacement. CPD has identified IDP camps across 4 states of South-Central Somalia (Galmudug State - Adado, Abudwak, Dhusamareeb, Galkayo South Districts), South West State (Afgooye, Marka, Qoryooley Districts), Benadir Region (Daynille, Dharkinley Districts) and Hirshabelle State (Jowhar District). In some areas, IDPs are living with the host communities who provide them with makeshift shelter.
CPD therefore plans to provide Shelter Kits and NFI for 21,000 people (3,500HH) in Galmudug, South West, Hirshabelle States and Benadir region as per the Shelter and NFI kits guidelines which will guide the appropriate processes for distribution and the key considerations to observe throughout the project cycle. The organization will target IDPs, host communities and refugee returnees. Newly displaced, evictees, IDPs affected by fire incidents and IDPs living in sites without long term security of tenure and target areas with high severity and people in need will be prioritized. CPD will establish complaint and feedback mechanisms for Shelter and NFI projects implemented and conduct and analyse PDM and use the findings to improve the quality of future projects.

Mitigation Measures

Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities • Ensuring that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable by Identifying and prioritising the most vulnerable groups in the community and prevent discrimination or exclusion of marginalised groups. • Applying a systematic outreach process in registering beneficiaries. • Ensuring that the location, distance and access routes to the water points, latrines and distribution sites are accessible to all members of the community, including the disabled and most vulnerable groups. • Making infrastructure adaptions within the camps accessible to persons with disability. • Establishing mechanisms to give priority to those who cannot stand in line for long periods such as persons with disability. Risk that beneficiaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms: • The project will set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms e.g. place feedback surveys in NFI kits, feedback box at distribution site, provide phone lines e.t.c. • This risk will be mitigated by ensuring that beneficiaries know how to obtain shelter and NFI assistance, where and when the distributions will take place, what they will get and for how long it should last and how to use the items. • The mechanisms for submitting feedback will not require the beneficiary exposing or identifying themselves to project staff.• Complaints would be responded to immediately regardless of whether corrective measures can/need to be put in place. Risk of unsafe and unethical management of data, especially disclosure of sensitive personal data on beneficiaries: • The project will keep lists of beneficiaries and personal data confidential and safe from unintended use by applying effective data protection measures. • It will also ensure shelter/NFI staff sign and adhere to a Code of Conduct stating their commitment to respect the rights of beneficiaries. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking inter-community conflict over access to resources: The project ensure this risk is mitigated through:• Avoiding community tensions, by providing the local/host community with access to camp services and assistance in situations where they are experiencing similar shortages of non-food items. • Providing clear information to distribution actors and affected population mostly women, girls and people living with disabilities explaining that beneficiaries do not have to pay or provide services/favours to anyone in exchange for receiving shelter or NFI assistance. • Engaging the community and committee representatives to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect them, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area: • The project will take into account the land tenure arrangements of IDPs, including statutory/legislative and customary access rights to land and inheritance rights. • It will also reach out to the land and property owners and local authorities to avoid eviction or disproportional rising in rent. • The project will also obtain permission before using or building on any land or property, in writing, where possible.
