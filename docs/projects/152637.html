Project Summary

According to the South Sudan health cluster report (2019 HNO released in October 2018), it is estimated that 4,058,392 South Sudanese women, men and children will need humanitarian healthcare services in 2019 and of which 829,949 in Jonglei State accounting to 20.4% and number estimated for Pibor and Twic East counties stand at 70,489 and 54,521 respectively. Due to high disease burden resulting from endemic, epidemic, pandemic and neglected diseases the population targeted for 2019 humanitarian healthcare services is 2,272,700 and of which 464,435 (20.4%) is for Jonglei and 39,474 and 30,532 for Pibor and Twic East Counties respectively. The health needs for Jonglei is as huge as the state has suffered a significant destruction and displacement during the armed conflict between Juba government and opposition forces in the past five years.
The Rapid Results Health Project (RRHP) with financial support from World Bank for Jonglei and Upper Nile is facing significant financial gaps, 211 out of 318 facilities are now being supported and no real surety for continuity in 2019 (RRHP 2018). From the September 28, 2018 released IPC result by South Sudan Bureau of Statistics, 65.2% of Jonglei will be IPC phase 3, 4 and 5 in January to March 2019 and Pibor and Twic East Counties in IPC phase 4.
United Networks for Health response focuses mainly in Pibor and Twic East Counties of Jonglei State. These counties are hard to reach, very vast, prone to floods and displacement due to inter-clan and tribal conflicts. Twick East is now managed by the County Health Department that has limited capacity, no health partner and most services are concentrated at county headquarter with no adequate lifesaving health services for the vulnerable populations in dire needs. Former Pibor County is so vast with poor transport networks and difficult to access during rainy season. Most of the vulnerable population live in the rural (very hard to reach) areas with no access to lifesaving healthcare services, EPI coverage is limited only in Pibor headquarter and Boma health facility. UNH has a strong networks in Pibor and Twic East and will work together with the County Health Departments, health partners on the ground and other clusters (nutrition, protection and food security) to ensure synergies. UNH will scale up response and increase access through establishing 2 mobile clinics (1 in Pibor and 1 in Twic East), strengthening and supporting 2 primary health care centers (1 in Pibor and 1 in Twic East), 1 primary health care unit in Pibor. The response will targets the IDPs and vulnerable host communities; women/men and girls/boys of all age groups without discrimination in the two counties; 10,000 beneficiaries (5,645 in Pibor and 4,355 in Twic East) will receive healthcare services of which 2,560 IDPs and 7,440 vulnerable host communities. Generally the whole community in the catchment areas will benefit from the response directly and indirectly.

Needs

The health care system of South Sudan depends mainly on Donor funding, most of the health facilities were vandalized and looted during armed conflicts that started in Juba mid-December 2013 spreading to Upper Nile Region swiftly and now in most parts of the country. An economy continuing to contract, protracted conflicts and challenges associated with delivery of humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable population (women/men and boys/girls of all age groups), lack of water quality control, infection prevention and control measures, healthcare waste management, expansive malnutrition and a looming threat of viral hemorrhagic disease including Ebola, necessitating a need for holistic integration of health responses to ensure the necessary health securities to mitigate disease spread and enhance beneficiary experience.According to the South Sudan health cluster report (2019 HNO released in October 2018), it is estimated that 4,058,392 South Sudanese women, men and children will need humanitarian healthcare services in 2019 and of which 829,949 in Jonglei State accounting to 20.4% and number estimated for Pibor and Twic East stand at 70,489 and 54,521 respectively. The population targeted for 2019 humanitarian healthcare services is 2,272,700 and of which 464,435 (20.4%) is for Jonglei and 39,474 and 30,532 for Pibor and Twic East Counties respectively. The health needs for Jonglei is so huge as the state has suffered a significant destruction and displacement during the armed conflict between Juba government and opposition forces especially in 2014 to 2017 and there is continued inter-clan and inter-tribal conflicts, most parts of the northern Jonglei is still under SPLA-IO. During the armed insurgency most of the health facilities were destroyed, vandalized and looted and most health partners pulled out and now couple with significant funding gaps the situation will continue to deteriorate if emergency health lifesaving is not scale up. The Rapid Results Health Project (RRHP) with financial support from World Bank for Jonglei and Upper Nile is facing significant financial gaps, 211 out of 318 facilities are now being supported and no real surety for continuity in 2019 (RRHP 2018). The situation for Jonglei is also complicated as 65.2% of the population will be in IPC phase 3, 4 and 5 in January to March 2019 (IPC result released on September 28, 2018 by National Bureau of Statistics). Pibor & Twic East Counties will be among the 5 counties of Jonglei that will be in IPC 4 in the first quarter of 2019 and may deteriorate further during the long spell of lean season. UNH response focuses mainly in Pibor and Twic East Counties of Jonglei State. These counties are hard to reach, very vast, prone to floods and displacement due to inter-clan and tribal conflicts. Twic East is now managed by the County Health Department that has limited capacity, no health partner and most services are concentrated at county headquarter with no adequate lifesaving health services for the vulnerable populations in dire needs. Former Pibor County is so vast with poor transport networks and difficult to access during rainy season. Most of the vulnerable population live in the rural (very hard to reach) areas with no access for lifesaving healthcare services, EPI coverage is limited only in Pibor headquarter and Boma health facility. UNH has strong networks in Pibor and Twic East and will work together with the County Health Departments, health partners on the ground and other actors (nutrition, protection, WASH and food security) to ensure synergies and scale up response and improve accessibility mainly through mobile services. It is now imperative that emergency healthcare services be channeled to Pibor and Twic East counties to minimize morbidity, mortality and improve quality of lives of the IDPs and most vulnerable groups (women/men, girls/boys of all age groups).

Activities

Proposed Activities• Establish 2 Mobile clinics (1 in Pibor and 1 in Twic East)• Strengthen and Support 2 primary health care centers (1 in Pibor and 1 in Twic East)• Strengthen and support 1 primary health care unit in Pibor• Participate in RRMs activities• Identify and refer trauma cases to the hospital for management (SGBV, CeMONC )• Support IDSR and EWARs activities• Preparedness and response to epidemic prone disease outbreaks (Malaria/Measles/ cholera/Meningitis and HepE)• Integrate WASH response at all our run health facilities• Increase demand for uptake of mental Health & Psycho-social services and for SGBV services • Provide targeted response to disabled and vulnerable groups • Management of SAM with medical complication in the primary healthcare centers• Capacity building of Nutrition actors on standard procedures and guidelines for screening and referral of SAM children with medical complications (Malaria RDT training)• Water quality testing and Monitoring in health facilities to mitigate disease spread• Integration of Health education messages across sectors on risk communication • Collaboration with protection on Mapping of SGBV services and referrals

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

United Networks for Health approach of operation normally focuses on a person as a whole by providing medical/physical, emotional, economic and social support. There is normally accountability and feedback mechanism in place to ensure that needs of the affected people are taken into consideration while developing and implementing a project. United Networks for Health had a number of consultative meetings with the leaders and the affected people (women/men, girls/boys of different age groups) of Jonglei State most notably Pibor and Twic East Counties. The design of this project is in accordance with the needs and preferences of girls, boys, women and men of different ages so that no activity will be carried out in isolation or fragmented manner as the proposed project aims to adopt a comprehensive approach based on participation of all stakeholders.Once a project is approved and funding available, all necessary authorities will be informed, documents will be shared and meeting will be conducted with community leaders and representatives of all age groups and sex from the affected population to be informed about the project and during implementation information will be passed continually during monthly review/feedback meetings.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The designing of this project followed a number of community consultative meetings conducted to identify needs of affected population, all age groups and sex were consulted and the activities and resources requested are adapted to the needs and preferences of girls, boys, women and men of different ages. The targeted actions address the needs and priorities as well as the discrimination faced by specific groups of women/girls/boys/men.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Sexual exploitation and abuse have two dimensions, from the community or by the implementing partners. UNH has a clear code of contact and policy on sexual exploitation/abuse, all our staffs are always oriented and provided with the policy document and any offender will be punished accordingly.UNH will be working with protection actors on the ground to mitigate barriers to health care for SGBV survivors, mapping of existing services and referral pathways available to SGBV survivors and orienting health care workers on the needs and services of SGBV survivors.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Our approach of operation focus on a person as a whole by providing medical/physical, emotional, economic and social support.There is accountability and feedback mechanism where people can complain and be heard, there is a confidential complaints process that considers gender and age, and responds with changes to the way services are delivered hence improving quality of service delivery.UNH always encourages the participation of the affected population in management and implementation of the project, all our team on the ground are South Sudanese nationals and some are recruited from the affected population. The team meets and discusses with the Payam administration, local areal leaders/chiefs, youths and representatives from the communities (women/men, boys/girls of all age groups) monthly about the progress, challenges, lessons learnt and the way forward.This project will deliver different messages through appropriate media according to the gender analysis.Our approach ensures transparency in our work, and is shared with the affected populations as per the organizational procedures and policies so that they can make informed decisions.
