Project Summary

Silk Road aims by implementing this project to contribute to strengthen education process in the affected and vulnerable areas and provide a safe educational environment for approximately 7060 students aged 5-17 years in 17 schools in Ariha and Harim sub-districts through focusing on a response to needs that are the improvement of access to equitable education and learning opportunity as well as promoting quality and relevance of formal and non-formal education within a protective environment in the targeted schools.
Silk Road aims by implementing this project to contribute to strengthen education process in the affected and vulnerable areas and provide a safe educational environment for approximately 7060 students aged 5-17 years in 17 schools in Ariha and Harim sub-districts through focusing on a response to needs that are the improvement of access to equitable education and learning opportunity as well as promoting quality and relevance of formal and non-formal education within a protective environment in the targeted schools.
This project is a humanitarian action for emergency education needs in a protracted crisis. The project will work with the local education authorities to ensure local ownership of the project and the sustainability of the project's investments. The project will use existing school buildings that are under the authority of the local education authorities. The project will improve these structures so that they can provide a more protective learning environment for children beyond the project period. The project will build up the capacity of the targeted educational centres and its personnel which will increase their influence in the community, increase the value of the schools in the community and increase education enrolment rates year after year. Training teachers will support the sustainability of education beyond the life of the project since those teachers will commit to teach in Syria and will support their colleagues by sharing their learnings with them. The project team will also strive to support cross-learning between education actors by sharing resources, knowledge, reports, and lesson learned with the community served and education actors. The project will be implemented in 8 located in Ariha and Harim subdistricts which are Bazabur, Korin, Berj Hab, Maarzaf, Motaramm, Kafr Najd, Ariha and Ariba.
7060 Students aged 5-17 years will benefit from:
1. Schools with equipment (blackboards, chalks etc)
2. Rehabilitated classrooms
3. Schools provided with furniture and/or safety equipment
4. Learning opportunities (catch up classes)
5. Schools with the operational and maintenance cost
6. Transportation to schools for disabled children
7. Learning materials.
8. Psychosocial support using the below curricula:
- For younger children (5-9 years) using PSS Palestine guide
- For older children (10-16 years) using the Children's Resilience Programme: Psychosocial support in and out of schools (Save the Children).
19. Referral pathways to specialized services such as child protection, gender-based violence and nutrition
10. Recreational materials
11. Provide textbooks to all children (Standard curricula of the Syrian Interim Government)
Also, 200 Teachers and education personnel will benefit from:
1. Provided professional development opportunities (Continuous training).
2.Provision of incentives for teachers and education personnel.
3.Provision of teachers with teaching materials (kits for each one).
4.Training on ASER assessment
Feedback/Complaint mechanism: The provision of complaint boxes that can be seen and accessed easily inside the school. Use of social media through our pages on Facebook and the mobile application (A directly associated phone number associated with Project Manager. Also, the project team coordinates with the local councils/local authorities to replay complaints and explain any reasons behind inquires. All feedbacks will be archived in the database and analysed in the evaluation.

Needs

Idleb Governorate has been deeply affected by the Syrian conflict, and as the conflict enters its eighth year, Idleb like much of the country continues to experience significant population displacement, reduction of livelihood opportunities, badly damaged the situation of Educational process; access to quality of education has been significantly hampered by the deterioration of the humanitarian situation in the last years of conflict, and decreased market access, all of which place disproportionate pressures on families.However, Idleb’s geopolitical location means that the combination of characteristics and challenges in the governorate are unique compared to other parts of the country. Idleb hosts a constant influx of internally displaced persons (IDPs), predominately from Aleppo, Hama, and elsewhere in Idleb, as well as smaller proportions from Homs. Last year of conflict have badly damaged the situation of the Educational process; access to quality of education has been significantly hampered by the deterioration of the humanitarian situation in the last years of conflict, when aerial bombing has increased significantly, children dropping out due to security reasons and the unsafety of the educational facilities; Furthermore, teachers, children, and their families experienced and have to cope with a level of increasing violence that worryingly on the surface has become normality but is causing several consequences such as fear, panic attacks, anxiety, loss of motivation, increased aggressively. Amidst this backdrop, local organizations remain committed to finding resources and ways to support communities in need.As outlined in the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) whole of Syria education data: 853,292 School-age children (5-17 years) and 17,777 Teachers in need in Idleb Governorate. Furthermore, the severity scale in these communities as follows:Bazabur 4, Berjhab 5, Kafr Najd 3, Korin 4, Maarzaf (Ariha) 3, Motaram 4, Ariba 3, Besnaya- Bseineh 5 and Harim 4.Silk Road had conducted a survey through its field teams in collaboration with Local Councils, the survey included schools in Bazabur, Korin, Berj Hab, Maarzaf, Motaram, Kafr Najd, Ariha and Ariba communities by field visit to the schools, and it included the number of children, teachers in the school, state of the school, and it indicated that there are different needs in each area. According to need assessment conducted by Silk Road team in these communities, the most common reasons for non-enrolment of children aged 5-17years in school were worsening of the economic situation is leading to migrating the teacher who is searching for the good living and needs to education reservation. Moreover, damaged or destroyed schools building, the need to work to support the family income and meagre absorptive capacity in schools are constituted additional reasons impeding the educational process.Otherwise, we estimated 150 students with disabilities lost their educational opportunities due to disability and inability to access schools. Furthermore, provide protection services for students in the targeted location is considered as a critical need for all schools, where no protection services provided and no training for the teaches as well. The results show that most of the teachers with a low percentage of qualification and improve the performance of teachers and administrators in schools and conduct training courses are highly required.

Implementation of activities

The project will start at the beginning of April by starting to establish safe learning spaces for 7060 students in formal settings through the main activities which will be started by rehabilitating 215 classrooms informal settings for 7060 children including repairing wooden and iron doors, installing galvanized metal water tank in each school, installing handrail for disabled children’s access, installing wash basins and squat toilets, and repairing drainage systems and drinking water pipework in each school, will be provided, moreover we will provide these schools with new desks as well as old desks will be repaired, in addition, the project team will provide each targeted school with First aid kits (1 per school) and Fire extinguisher (3 per school). These activities will take three months and will be implemented in the first three months of the project from April till the end of Jun then BTL campaign will be launched at the beginning of July and this campaign will include the provision of training for 300 women on the importance of children’s education, particularly for young girls. Also, the project team will recruit 4 Community outreach workers in order to facilitate this campaign. These Community outreach workers will deliver weekly awareness sessions to teachers, and students within the activities of the campaign. The campaign will end at the mid of August and after that catch-up classes will be launched for three months and these classes will be held in one of the classrooms in each of the targeted schools by the teachers in these schools in the first three months of the project (student-teacher ratio will be 30:1).In parallel with the launch of catch up classes our team will start providing the targeted schools with equipment (whiteboards, white pens etc.), then at the beginning of the school year, we will start providing students with learning materials (stationary, pens, notebooks) in-formal settings as well as recreational materials and the teachers will be provided with teaching materials and Silk Road will provide 7060 students in the targeted schools with 7060 set of textbooks will be provide to 7060 students in formal education. Teachers and education personnel in the targeted schools will be provided with incentives and continuous professional development including training on pedagogy, modern teaching methods, DO-NO-HARM principle and child protection policy and needs and rights of children in crisis contexts.From the beginning of the school year, children with disabilities will be provided with transport for commuting in order to ensure that each student will have the opportunity to get into the school, moreover, all students in the targeted schools will be provided with psychosocial support through assign, child protection facilitator (one in each school) who will provide PSS activities for students, conduct recreational activities for students, provide referral pathways to specialized services such as child protection, gender-based violence and nutrition and conduct Awareness sessions or other activities to students' parents and teachers as well. On the other hand, Silk Road will conduct pre- and post- assessment in order to measure students’ education level before and after the programme intervention and Silk Road will provide the needed and required training for teachers (Train 215 teachers on ASER assessment) and Silk Road will provide a logical analysis of the results of the assessing that will be conducted during the first two months and at the last month of the projectFor winter season Silk Road will provide operational and maintenance cost which include heaters and heating fuel for the months of December. Add to the above the members of this project team maintain a good working relationship with the local community in the project area, the project was discussed with the local community during the design phase and the project team expect the communities to continue their support of the project

Protection Risk Analysis

This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and crossfire. Mitigating Measure:1-1 Closely monitor the security situation in coordination with partners and relevant stakeholders and conduct security assessments. They will ensure that staff members and teachers are trained in safety and security best practices, including mine risk education1.2 If and when the local security situation deteriorates: a) Immediately inform internal emergency focal point on the situation; 1.3 Coordinate with partners, cluster and hibernate/suspend the operations if necessaryProtection Risk/Threat 2 Likelihood is Medium and the Impact is Medium ): 2- Child safety incidents in school due to bullying and overburdened teachers Mitigating Measure:2.1 Examine the possibilities of adopting different modalities to implement the project. 2.2 All important documents will be saved as soft copies so that they will not get lost due to an attack. 2.3 Decisions are taken in advance whether hard copies will be destroyed or not.Protection Risk/Threat 3 Likelihood is Medium and the Impact is high)Attacks on schools.Mitigating Measure:Communication with donors to accept flexibility and support relocation of activities to safer spaces whenever needed.Protection Risk/Threat 4 ( Likelihood is high and the Impact is high): Security situation affecting implementation capacity negatively (e.g. denial of access) or /and Border Closures Mitigating Measure:4.1 Previous projects already built relationships with local leaders, communities and authorities.4.2 To keep nurturing these relationships with regular information and support that is sensitive to the structure of incentives will be a central part of this project that will mitigate this risk.4.3 Projects implemented with a degree of flexibility;
