Project Summary

55% of the population in the targeted locations need food and do not have cash to buy food items, and due to continuous shortage of food, the HHs would be unable to absorb economic shocks, such as loss of a livelihood source. The overall objective of the proposed project is to increase the ability of vulnerable conflict-affected populations in NES through addressing the most acute humanitarian needs and enhance their capacity for socio-economic resilience. Over 1,400 HHs (50% Female headed HHs, 8,000 individuals) to be supported. This 12-month project focuses to enhance the availability and promote access for food sources through a multidimensional approach for poverty reduction through adopt a hybrid modality (value-based e-vouchers and Commodity transfer ration /Average Voucher transfer value 66 USD (6persons*11$) per round per BNF) to provide regular food assistance over an eight - month period to 1,000 households (6,000 individuals). In addition, provision of structured support to 400 small businesses of the ultra-poor beneficiaries engaged in the “Monthly Food Assistance” focusing on nutrition-sensitive agriculture activities to enhance Agro-food products availability in local markets and promote access of the local population to stable food sources, enabling participants to re-store, increase Agro-based livelihoods and become more food secure. So, enhance self-sufficiency and improve nutrition status at the household level, as well as create income generation by selling the surplus

3 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protection crisis compounded by over ten years of hostilities, protracted displacements, and prolonged consequences, including destruction of civilian infrastructure and violation of international humanitarian and human rights law. According to MSNA data for WoS, by Aug 2022, the average HH expenses exceeded the average HH income by 60%, compared to 49% in 2021. The increase in the income‐expenditure gap has resulted in widespread utilization of increasingly desperate coping mechanisms. The reduced access to electricity and fuel shortages are having a crippling effect on the population, eroding LHs opportunities and severely limiting access to essential services.RNA carried out in Kasara and AlKarama, were 50% of the population are PIN of food and do not have cash to buy food items, and due to continuous shortage of food, the HHs would be unable to absorb economic shocks, such as loss of a livelihood source. Majority of the population relied upon agriculture sector, where the wheat and vegetables, along with livestock breeding were widespread. Similarly, the Syria government was the leading supervisor in all Agri-activities providing subsidies, thus supporting their production and income generation. Later, this support was interrupted due to the conflict. The basic agri-related infra. has been disrupted and in urgent need to be rehabilitated to enhance the Agri-production aspects and promote the availability of food in local markets

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

R1:Potential tensions between BNFs, and between HCs and IDPs, who will receive the food assistance L(H), I(H) M1:Bahar will post brochures that specify the list of criteria that people should meet to benefit from the food assistance, and will further explain these criteria to BNFs inquiring about this assistance. The M&E team will monitor the BNFs’ selection process and will ensure strict verification measures R2:Cash modality to targeted survivors put their safety and confidentiality at risk L(H), I(H)M2:Bahar will provide cash assistance at the WGSS to ensure the safety and confidentiality of survivors, and the cash distribution will be complete in the daytime, which will allow the BNFs to redeem their commodities with limited travel necessary and return home during daytime to avoid any security-related risks R3: Potential BNFs excluded from participation in the selection process, Lack of substantive leadership opportunities for women and youth L(H), I(H)M3:the staff will clarify the selection criteria, identify who in the community meets the criteria, and then submit the recommendation to a group representing community diversity such as IDPs, HCs, females, ethnic (Arab, Kurd), and/or other local factors to confirm the recipientsR4:Insufficient attention given ensuring inclusion of HHs with especially vulnerable members (gender, age, disability) L(M), I(H)M4:prioritizing HHs when assessing vulnerability raises their profile, and they become a contributing members.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Clear standards of conduct, technical and behavioral competencies for all staff, consultants: Bahar will ensure that staff have the technical knowledge and skills, and behavioral competencies to deliver commitments to affected population, and understand what accountability looks like in practice, and to abide by Bahar’s Code of Conduct CoC which includes: Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, Prevention of Conflicts of Interest and Fraud, and Child Protection. All staff members are obligedto follow Bahar’s CoC as outlined in their job contracts and measures will be taken should they be found in breach of it. However, malicious complaints will be identified as such and our staff protected from false allegations. Bahar will consulate a PSEA field officer who will make appropriate recommendations to management on enhancing prevention strategies. This could include collecting and analyzing information on actual/potential risk factors for vulnerability to sexual exploitation and abuse andelaborating measures to address them. Make sure that PSEA materials and posters are visible and in place in all locations. Receive complaints, reports, and questions about alleged acts of SEA committed by the organization’s personnel. And track all PSEA-related activities and ensure that all information on PSEA-related activities is shared with relevant persons in the organization
