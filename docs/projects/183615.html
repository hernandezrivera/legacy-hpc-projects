Project Summary

This project aims to improve the access of vulnerable cancer patients in Gaza Strip to essential health services, through provision of essential medical supplies, mainly chemotherapy and palliative medications, and raise awareness about the importance of prevention and early detection. The proposed intervention is in line with strategic objective 2 and Cluster Objective1.
In addition, the proposed intervention is in line with SDG3: Good health and wellbeing and meets SDG3, target4: Reduce mortality from non-communicable diseases and promote mental health.
QRC will provide chemotherapy medications for 1000 cancer patients, medications used as part of palliative care plans for 1620 patients (including 120 children) and will help raise the awareness of the community, mainly women, on the importance of early detection of the breast cancer. This intervention is expected to improve the well-being, quality of life and the 5-year survival rate of people with cancer through: contributing to maintaining uninterrupted treatment plans, contributing to controlling pain for patients at advanced stages with life-threatening illnesses and during chemotherapeutic doses, and enhancing prevention and early detection practices.
Lists of needed medications will be obtained from MoH, reviewed and a tender process will be followed to secure the medications. QRC will receive lists of beneficiaries, will be holding field visits and carrying out all necessary M&E activities. In addition, QRC will carry out the awareness raising sessions at the community level. In addition to the supply of medications, and raising awareness activities, QRC is keen on providing comprehensive services to patients with cancer. QRC, through its continued efforts to support specialized health care services at health facilities, will make sure oncologists who have received specialized training outside Gaza are available and compensated for their work to provide advance oncology services at health facilities. This will be done through QRC contribution.
The intervention mainstreams gender through its activities and allocates an entire activity to women and girls. Awareness raising activities particularly target females and aim at sensitizing the community towards the importance of prevention and early detection of breast cancer in order to increase effectiveness of other interventions related to the disease. In addition to gender mainstreaming, the intervention by default mainstreams other cross cutting issues including protection, age, equal access to aid and doing no harm.
While cancer is one of the main chronic diseases that affect all members of society, it affects different genders and ages differently, where colon cancer is most common among men and breast cancer the most common among women, while leukemia is the most common cancer among children. Access to medications will be equal to all groups, including men, women, children and the elderly since the MoH will be responsible for distributing medications through its set protocols of service, which provide service with no discrimination. In addition, more focus will be given to women and girls through health awareness services, which will help education people in general, and mainly women, on the importance of early detection of cancer.
810 women, 690 men and 120 children will benefit from palliative care, while 500 women, 426 men and 74 children will benefit from chemo. Medication and thousands will benefit from awareness activities.

During implementation and for M&E, focus groups, field visits will be used to assess project progress and get feedback from the community. 1 - Needs

The protracted humanitarian crisis in Gaza Strip has recently escalated after the 11 day May flare up, which came to aggravate an already deteriorated situation due to the persisting Covid-19 pandemic, over 15 years of blockade, political divide and a weakened, overstretched and fragile healthcare system in Gaza Strip along with limited humanitarian and financial resources.The healthcare system of the Gaza Strip is facing a real challenge to deliver the essential health care services with the limited resources and 15 years of access restrictions. According to the 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview “HNO’, about one million population is in need for health-related humanitarian assistance in 2022.Cancer disease is one of the main causes of morbidity and mortality in the world. Cancer patients in the Gaza Strip are considered the most affected patients from the current deteriorated situation. Ministry of Health “MoH” annual report in 2020 indicated the followings: -Cancer disease represents 10.3% of the total number of deaths in the Gaza Strip. –8644 cancer cases were registered in the last 5 years, in which 54% were females and 46% were males. -Breast cancer is the most common cancer occurring with 17.9% (1533 cases) and the first most common cancer in women with 32.8%. -Colon cancer is the first most common cancer in men with 14% (1106 cases).According to the HNO 2022, cancer patients who need access to essential health services within the Gaza Strip are considered as a priority and stated that all cancer patients are vulnerable and in urgent need of health intervention.Referring to the MoH drugs & medical disposables report in September 2021, it is found that the shortage of essential drugs is continued to be a chronic problem, where 34.9% of cancer medications were reported at zero stock levels in the Central Drug Store (CDS), and 14.3% were reported to be sufficient for less than 3 months.Many types of oncological services are not available in the Gaza Strip including the special types of chemotherapy, radiation therapy, PET/CT scans, and some phases of diagnosis, treatment and evaluation of the disease. Cancer patients who need referrals to access the essential health care are required by Israel to get permits to travel. 2020 MoH report showed that oncology referrals (35.7%) accounted for the highest percentage of the overall referrals’ specialties. Furthermore, WHO reported that one in three referrals applications from the Gaza Strip during the period from January to July 2021 were unsuccessful.Palliative care for patients at advanced stages of cancer disease and during the chemotherapeutic doses aims to improve quality of life for patients and their families and reduce suffering for persons with life-threatening illnesses, Donors’ conference held in 2021 clarified that palliative care in the Gaza Strip is not yet available except for primitive self-supported trials at Al-Rantisi Hospital with limited experience and drug and equipment shortage. Nevertheless, oncologists try to effectively reduce cancer patients’ pain through painkillers, which often run out of hospitals. In spite of the efforts spent in increasing awareness towards cancer screening in the Gaza Strip, in 2021, UNFPA published that 70% of cases discovered are in the advanced 3rd stage of the disease, making it difficult to treat and resulting in serious physical, psychological and social impairment. This intervention responds to the Health Cluster Objective no.1: “Ensure the availability of acceptable and quality essential healthcare services to vulnerable communities in oPt”, this links to Second Strategic Objective: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under international humanitarian law”.While cancer is one of the main chronic diseases that affect all members of society, it affects different genders and ages differently, where colon cancer is most common among men and breast cancer the most common among women, while leukemia is the most common cancer among children.

2 - Activities

Through this project, QRC aims to improve the access of vulnerable cancer patients in the Gaza Strip to essential health services and enhance prevention and early detection practices. This intervention will improve the quality of life of cancer patients in the Gaza Strip and improve the 5-year survival rates. these aims will be reached through the following:1. Supplying MoH with cancer medications, specially the zero-stock items, to ensure the continuity of provision of cancer treatment. This activity will benefit a total number of 1000 cancer patients, covering their medicine for one year. MoH will distribute the medications to the needed patients through Al Rantisi Hospital and European Gaza Hospital (EGH). This activity will contribute to alleviating the shortage of medicines in the hospitals of the MoH in the Gaza Strip. Accordingly, this will mitigate the financial burden on cancer patients and their families by providing cancer medicines locally, saving them the hassle and costs of traveling to receive treatment abroad.2. Supporting pain management by supplying MoH with painkillers items used in palliative care to reduce the suffering and improve the quality of life for cancer patients at advanced stages with life-threatening illnesses and during chemotherapeutic doses. This activity will benefit about 1500 adults and 120 children with cancer disease, covering their needs for one year.As the Ministry of Health is the main health care provider for cancer services through Al Rantisi Hospital and European Gaza Hospital, the aforementioned activities will be implemented in full coordination with Ministry of Health, starting from holding meetings to present the project and explaining the aim and activities, preparing the cancer medication list, according to the ongoing needs covering the targeted number of beneficiaries, participating in the tender process, receiving the medications, distribution of medications to the targeted patients and following up and ensuring the delivery of service to the beneficiaries. QRC and MoH will ensure that specific needs of patients with disabilities, as well as age and gender sensitive concerns are addressed during the executing of the intervention.It is worth mentioning that QRC has a well-experienced staff in procurement process and an extended program that has been going on for years to support cancer services in the Gaza Strip with the coordination of MoH.3. Enhancing breast health awareness, as it is an integral part of all early detection strategies which ultimately leads to improving survival, lowers morbidity and reduces the cost of care. This activity is at the community level and will include public health and professional medical education on the risk factors and symptoms of breast cancer and the importance of seeking medical evaluation for breast concerns, through conducting organized health educational sessions at the community level and other awareness activities that include disseminating brochures. This activity will benefit mainly 1000 people through 50 awareness sessions and 3000 people from brochures that will be distributed in public spaces. Moreover, in the context of COVID-19 pandemic, QRC will provide hygiene and sanitation materials to prevent spreading the virus protecting people from infection. In addition to the supply of medications, and raising awareness activities, QRC is keen on providing comprehensive services to patients with cancer. QRC, through its continued efforts to support specialized health care services at health facilities, will make sure oncologists who have received specialized training outside Gaza are available and compensated for their work to provide advance oncology services at health facilities. This will be done through QRC contribution. Community will be engaged in all stages of the project including design, implementation and M&E. during the design phase, QRC held interviews with oncology expert and reviewed MoH 2020 report to decide types of medications, mechanisms of implementation and other needs and indicators. During implementation and for M&E, focus groups, field visits and surveys will be used to assess project progress and get feedback from the community. Focus groups will be held prior to health education sessions in order to get community feedback on best approaches for conducting the health education sessions.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

QRC in coordination of MoH through the organizational policies- based on international and local protocols will assure the maximum benefits and the minimum harm of the beneficiaries. Those policies emphasize on the safety of the beneficiaries as a top priority including the safe service delivery and minimum threats. The distribution of the cancer medications will be in MoH hospitals ensuring equitable access for all needed cancer patients especially women, children and PWDs. MoH staff including men and women through hospital policies will assure meeting gender special needs and concerns. Moreover, the awareness raising activities will be conducted at the level of the community in public places to guarantee the accessibility and taking into consideration the PWDs needs. The working staff will assure the privacy while conducting project activities specially for women. In addition, QRC will highly consider the adaptability and the cultural accessibility to respond to the cultural backgrounds of the beneficiaries. Furthermore, there are clear lines for the privacy and confidentiality that are shared between the working staff; there are private areas for therapy, and there are clear rules for data collection that include; not collecting unneeded information nor sharing information with any except the working staff. Importantly, QRC and relevant actors in the context of COVID-19 pandemic- follow strict precautions to prevent the spread of COVID-19 and decrease the possible complication. Furthermore, they encourage beneficiaries and the working staff to take the vaccine to protect him/herself and the community. QRC follows strict rules to ensure that the items procured are what is needed and reject items that don’t match the technical criteria and may cause any harm to the beneficiaries.All medications that will be provided through this project are registered in MoH lists, stated in the local guidelines and protocols, with assured effectiveness and already used in hospitals. No new medications will be introduced so no harm of unknown effects of drugs.Patients’ well-being is the primary consideration of the intervention. One of the main components of our intervention is providing cancer patients with items that support pain management in order to reduce and/or control their pain specially during chemotherapy sessions.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

QRC is neutral and doesn’t lean toward any group over the other in Gaza context. It is impartial and provide the aid to those who need it regardless of any other consideration such as gender, age, religion, etc. QRC is independent organizations and not affected by commercial nor political powers. It is humanitarian and provide aid to those vulnerable and suffering all the time. QRC follows strict rules to maximize the geographical, financial, and cultural accessibility especially for women, children and PwDs.The working staff consider providing the beneficiaries with enough information about their rights which is crucial in the service delivery. It uses both verbal and written information to overcome intellectual differences and barriers. QRC monitors service delivery and procurement at beneficiary levels to ensure all items are made available. Due to the community spread of COVID-19, QRC tends to utilize all alternative resources to implement theactivities in a highly effective and efficient manner including distributionof medications, hygiene kits, distancing during the awareness sessions, etc.
