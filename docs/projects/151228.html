Project Summary

The proposed project will be implemented in 4 districts in Bakool (xudur,wajid,rabdhuure and elbarde), 3 in banadir (Kahda,hodan,abdiaziz) ,1 in Lower shebelle region (Afgooye) , 2 districts in Nugaal region(Garowe and eyl) and 1 in Woqoyi galbeet to cover nutrition needs of internally displaced people and host community women, men, boys and girls.
The project seeks to reduce emergency levels of acute malnutrition through integrated multisectoral response in line with 2019 strategic objective 2 for Somalia. The primary targets of the project are U5 boys and girls and pregnant and lactating women in 11 districts in Somalia. This will be achieved through facility and community based management of acute malnutrition including 4 stabilisation centres that are operational 24hr/7days , promotion of optimal maternal ,infant and young child nutrition and linkages with other sectors such as WASH, food security and protection. The program’s approach involves both men and women while acknowledging their respective roles and needs, and fostering mutual awareness and partnership. Recognizing that child care in Somalia is mainly considered a female role, the project will take into account the socially constructed roles for each gender ensuring that women do not spend so much time in the project at the expense of their other children who are not malnourished, while also seeking to influence them. The project will also target men to encourage them to take an active role in better child care practices, while also improving women’s knowledge on infant feeding and care. The project seeks to enhance the capacity of community and Ministry of health staff to provide life saving services and build community resilience . The activities will be implemented in line with Ministry of health/WHO/UNICEF guidelines. A total of 65439 persons including 13023 women of child bearing age ,25653 boys and 26699 girls, 80 key community representatives (40 men and 40 women), 40 staff (24 men and 16 women) will be targeted for a period of 12 months.

Needs

The humanitarian needs in Somalia are still high despite recent improvement.FSNAU 2018 post Gu assessment estimates that 1.5 million people in Somalia are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse through December 2018.In Bakool, 101,000 people are reported to be in IPC phase 2 and 3 which is an improvement from GU last year. The report indicates that food security situtation improved due to average to above average rains combined with sustained humanitarian effort. The assessment also shows that acute levels of malnutrition have improved nationaly to serious with a median GAM of 14.0 percent compared to critical levels in Gu 2017 which recorded a median GAM of 17.4 percent. Despite this improvement, pastoral communities will continue to face acute food insecurity as they are yet to rebound from the livestock loses they faced during the drought season. Action against hunger SMART surveys done in March 2018 Showed that GAM prevalence among Garowe and Mogadishu IDPs were 15.1% and 16.3% respectively.This is within the same range as 2018 Post Gu assessment that recorded a critical level of 16.7% for both camps. The Survey also showed high disease burden among children U5 where one in every five children was reported to be ill .Mortality rates among IDPS in mogadishu are at emergency levels(1.06 %) . The situation is aggravated by constraints of food availability, economic resources, caregiver’s time, knowledge, availability of quality health services and hygienic environment. 16 % of the acutely malnourished children identified suffered from measles an indicator that malnutrition increases children susceptibility to measles. Hygiene practices were also found to be poor across the IDP camps with only 6.1% washing hands at the 4 critical times of hand washing.There is therefore a need for an integrated approach to tackle underlying and immediate causes of malnutrition.

Activities

• Continuous screening and referral of U5 boys and girls to Nutrition treatment programmes including use of mother-led MUAC approach.• Treatment of SAM and MAM ( 25653 boys and 26699 girls) through provision of RUTF/RUSF at fixed and decentralized mobile outreach sites • Provision of Vitamin A supplements to 25653 boys and 26699 girls admitted to IMAM programme as per the national guidelines (Total 52352)• Conduct Nutrition ,health and hygiene education sessions targeting 52352 caregivers (41882 women and 10470 male) of malnourished children admitted to the IMAM programme.• Training of 70 staff (40 male, 30 female) on IMAM and IYCF . Two cycles of training will be conducted, 1 initial training and 1 follow up refresher training.• Conduct SQUEAC assessments in Bakool, Banadir and Nugaal regions to measure coverage across all demographic segments including disaggregation of access by boys, girls and women. • Sharing of nutritional data based on programme trends through the Nutrition Clusters 4W matrix as well as coverage and SMART surveys.• Participate in regional and national cluster meetings and working groups and coordination mechanisms such as AIMWG and SAG.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed intervention is aligned to the Health,WASH ,Food security and Nutrition cluster HRP priorities to focus on basic life-saving activities and community resilience-building in all high GAM/SAM and IDP areas. The regions where the interventions will be implemented are characterized by enormous environmental challenges related to poor waste disposal, deforestation, land degradation, increasing aridity and water scarcity . By increasing access to nutrition services through use of mobile teams and community focused approaches,the project reduces abnormal migration of human to other regions where they inflict significant environmental damages by exerting pressure on exisiting services . In the absence of latrines, in the new areas of displacement, open defecation would increase posing serious risks to the environmental wellbeing. Portions of land are usually left open to be used by the displaced population for human waste disposal. This poses serious health risks due to the spread of faecal matter, especially during the rainy season when there is seepage into water wells used for drinking water. This has been partly responsible for the periodic outbreaks of Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) in IDPs and villages in Somalia which increases the risk of high morbidity and mortality rates among vulnerable groups. The project will use an integrated approach with WASH to Provide latrines with hand washing facilities that will ease the level of public health risk posed by providing safe places and consequently check the spread of human waste. Whose location will be far from the water wells to check the risks of contamination. Public health will be ensured through proper disposal and management of medical equipment, supplies and waste, including the use of incinerators at the health facility. A component of nutrition training will also include lessons on environmental awareness with the objective of providing skills to the beneficiaries on better waste management, which doesn’t endanger their immediate environment. The project will jointly with line ministry representatives support the establishment and capacity building of community groups ( Community Health Committees, camp management committees, Mother support groups) to ensure resilient community systems are in place. The immediate/emergency components of the project will be linked to ACF’s resilience program in the country and handovers to the capacity built community resource committees will ensure that the resources established are maintained and contribute to building resilient communities. The services and commodities planned for transfer to beneficiaries under this project adhere to government policies and have been designed jointly with community user groups. The transfer will be witnessed by local authorities to ensure it fits within the existing legal framework at district level.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

The main target population for the project are IDPs and the surrounding host communities. Displaced populations, youth, women and the elderly, most notable those in camps lack nutrition services. In addition, poor access and long distance to nutrition services expose them, especially women and young girls to sex and gender based violence. Moreover, access to basic services and opportunities is further compounded by the pervasive marginalization and exclusion of IDPs and certain sections of the host communities mostly attributed to clan dynamics. IDP and host community families from weaker clans or minorities face extra vulnerabilities and protection threats including economic exploitation, trafficking and discrimination. To mitigate against the risks identified, ACF will ensure protection sensitive data collection and analysis to identify the specific protection challenges of the target population at project implementation stage. Targeting of project locations and beneficiaries will be guided by need with specific attention to households that may be marginalized due to family and clan factors. Engagement at community level will emphasize representation of all groups/clans in the target locations. ACF staff are already trained and sensitized on PSEA and have signed a code of conduct that commits them to strictly adhere to humanitarian principles when providing services. This is expected to curtail any cases of humanitarian staff exploiting the beneficiaries. Nutrition programs will employ approaches that reduce distance to treatment/consultation sites. This includes use of mobile teams that visits villages where the most vulnerable are located. This will minimize exposure to potential distance related risks. Proper consultations will be done at the inception of the project with all the key stakeholders in the camp to ensure that those who benefit from these interventions are not put at a risk.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The nutrition projects mainly target children under 5 years of age and women actively in the reproductive stage regardless of their age. lack of knowledge on infant and young child feeding may among women of reproductive age exposes the under 5 children ( both boys and girls) to malnutrition. Communities in hard to reach areas due to insecurity and poor infrastructure suffer from lack access to nutrition services.Due to prevailing social norms, male caregivers of SAM and MAM children may be excluded from targeting. Moreover, male and female caregivers from marginalized communities may miss opportunities to access nutrition services due to lack of information or reluctance due to their social status. The roll out of mobile outreach services will ensure that remote but accessible villages are reached with nutrition services. This will include engagement of community health workers who represent the various clan profiles, who can increase reach to all groups in the target communities. Moreover, the project will engage with all major groups to ensure that they are aware of the availability of services and their entitlements. The Project will also engage Traditional Birth attendants and improve their capacity so that they can provide services closer to the population. The project will also pro-actively engage male caregivers so that they don’t miss on services. Data collection and analysis will be designed in a way that allows ACF to better understand age and gender needs to ensure inclusivity.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

ACF works in areas where there is active conflict between state and non-state armed actors or between clans. The key risks include Service delivery being appropriated by different sides to the conflict against the others, aid services being diverted to the most powerful groups, retaliatory attacks or victimization of those who seek services by hostile groups, attack on beneficiary on their way to service delivery points and attacks on humanitarian staff ACF applies conflict sensitivity approaches in its project implementation and this will be mainstreamed in the project. All volunteers engaged in this action will be sensitize on the implication of this engagement in line with power dynamics at the household and community level. Deliberate action will be taken to discuss also the implication of this action on individual beneficiaries at the community level and if they are concerns, proper mitigation measures will be instituted. ACF local teams are well trained and experienced in handling sensitive community issues, especially if related to power dynamics and protection of all involved in the successful completion of this project. Additionally, beneficiary information will be protected and treated as confidential with consent being sort if any information such as pictures are to be shared. ACF will also be strictly guided by humanitarian principles, key among these principals being neutrality when providing services. Strict security protocols will be employed to ensure staff are not put in harm’s way, for example by ensuring that staff are back from the field stations early enough. Mobile outreach services will also target the remote but accessible areas, regardless of the community dynamics.
