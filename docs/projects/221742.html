Project Summary

The project, "Improved Access to Equitable Integrated Primary Health Care Services for Refugees, Returnees, IDPs, and Vulnerable Host Communities in Upper Nile State, South Sudan," aims to enhance equitable access to life-saving, comprehensive quality health care services. It focuses on maternal, child, and adolescent health; sexual and reproductive health; treatment of common illnesses; Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS); and Gender-Based Violence (GBV) and health promotion services. The initiative targets crisis-affected populations, including refugees, returnees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), and vulnerable host communities in Upper Nile State, South Sudan.
In Renk and Maban, Relief International (RI) will focus on responding to Sudan's crisis by supporting returnees and refugees. In Maiwut and Longechuk, RI will serve as the health-implementing partner under the Health Sector Transformation Project (HSTP), providing services in targeted facilities. Additionally, RI will reach people in hard-to-reach areas in Nasir, Ulang, Maiwut, and Longechuk by deploying Mobile Medical Units (MMUs).
RI will strive to provide health services integrated with WASH and nutrition interventions in all locations and responses, with protection principles mainstreamed into all activities. RI will work closely with local organizations and County Health Departments (CHD) to support nationalization agendas while collaborating with other partners to create synergy and avoid duplication of efforts. This integrated and coordinated approach aims to address the multifaceted needs of the target population, ensuring holistic and inclusive support.
The project is set to run from January 1, 2025, to December 31, 2025, under the implementation of Relief International, with additional implementing partners to be determined.

1. Needs

The humanitarian crisis in South Sudan remains dire, with 11.3 million people projected to need humanitarian assistance in 2025, including returnees. Upper Nile State, home to 1.57 million individuals, bears a significant share of the burden, accounting for 7% of national healthcare needs. Of these, 939,249 people require health services, with 509,841 in seven priority counties: Renk, Maban, Melut, Maiwut, Longechuk, Nasir, and Ulang (HPC 2025). Malnutrition and food insecurity are widespread, with 56.9% of the population classified as IPC AFI Phase 3 or worse by mid-2024. Projections for 2025 suggest 65% of the population will face severe food insecurity during the lean season, with 10,000 in Malakal County at IPC AFI Phase 5 (Catastrophe) (IPC 2025).Economic challenges worsen the crisis. In 2024, South Sudan’s economy deteriorated, with rapid devaluation of the South Sudanese Pound (SSP) and soaring prices for food and goods. In Renk, prices tripled within a year, severely undermining humanitarian operations. These pressures have reduced the effectiveness of critical interventions, leaving vulnerable populations without sufficient support.Health needs are acute, with 5.7 million nationwide requiring health services, including 939,249 in Upper Nile. Malaria is the leading cause of illness and death, and cholera, Hepatitis E, and measles outbreaks persist due to weak health systems and poor immunization (IPC 2025). Inadequate sanitation—74% of households practice open defecation—exacerbates disease transmission and malnutrition. Maternal and child health indicators remain critical: only 39% of pregnant women receive antenatal care, skilled birth attendance is just 14.7%, and the maternal mortality ratio (MMR) is 1,223 per 100,000 live births. Infant and under-five mortality rates are equally alarming at 75 per 1,000 live births and 10%, respectively (aho.afro.who.int).The protection needs of women and children are urgent. Nearly 50% of married women aged 15–49 experience intimate partner violence, and women and girls face heightened risks of sexual violence and exploitation, especially when displaced. Food insecurity and poverty force families into harmful coping strategies, such as child labor and early marriage, further exposing women and girls to gender-based violence (GBV). Despite these risks, GBV services remain inadequate, leaving survivors without essential support HNRP 2024).Relief International (RI) has tailored its response to meet community-identified needs in health, nutrition, water, sanitation, hygiene (WASH), and protection. RI adopts a community-centered, client-focused approach, ensuring inclusive programming that addresses the unique needs of women, men, boys, and girls. Community participation is central to all project phases, with disaggregated data by age, sex, and disability guiding adaptive management and decision-making.RI’s accountability to affected populations aligns with Sphere Standards and includes training on conflict sensitivity, protection principles, and the prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). RI maintains robust feedback, complaints, and response mechanisms, aiming to address immediate needs while building resilience among vulnerable populations.

2. Project activities

1. Access to Essential Health Services:• Primary Health Care: RI delivers life-saving health services through fixed health facilities and Mobile Medical Units (MMUs) to ensure that vulnerable populations in remote and conflict-affected areas have access to medical care.• Reproductive Health Services: RI provides antenatal care (ANC), postnatal care (PNC), skilled birth attendance (SBA), family planning, and Basic Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (BEmONC) to improve maternal and newborn health.• Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI): RI treats common childhood illnesses like malaria, pneumonia, and diarrhea to reduce under-five mortality.2. Support for Pregnant and Lactating Women (PLW) and Children Under Five:• Nutrition Interventions: RI screens and treats malnutrition in children and PLW through outpatient therapeutic care and supplementary feeding programs. Community health workers track defaulters and provide follow-up care.• Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI): RI vaccinates children under one year against preventable diseases, including measles and polio, to reduce morbidity and mortality.• Micronutrient Supplementation: PLW receives iron, folic acid, and other supplements to address anemia and nutritional deficiencies.3. Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Response and Prevention:• Case Management and Clinical Services: RI provides clinical management of rape (CMR) and psychosocial support for survivors of GBV.• Community Awareness: RI raises awareness about GBV prevention and response, particularly for women and girls who face heightened risks in refugee and returnee setups.4. Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS):• Counseling Services: RI integrates MHPSS into health programs, offering individual and group counseling to address trauma, particularly for women and children.• Capacity Building: RI trains health workers and community volunteers to provide psychosocial support and identify mental health needs in vulnerable groups.5. Integrated WASH Services:• RI ensures access to clean water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) facilities in health centers and communities, reducing disease transmission risks for vulnerable populations.• Special considerations are made for people with restricted mobility to ensure equitable access to WASH facilities.6. Implement Integrated disease surveillance and response:• Early Warning Alert and Response (EWAR)• Surveillance and early warning• Outbreak detection, investigation, and early response• Line listing 7. Community-Based Interventions:• Boma Health Initiative (BHI): RI works with community health workers to deliver basic health services at the grassroots level, ensuring access for those in remote or inaccessible areas.• Care Groups: RI engages mothers and caregivers in health and nutrition education to improve household health practices and reduce child morbidity.8. Emergency Response:• RI deploys rapid response teams during crises, such as the Sudan crisis, to address the immediate needs of newly arrived returnees and refugees, including health, nutrition, and protection services.• Overcrowded transit centers and reception areas are targeted with integrated services to prevent disease outbreaks and malnutrition.9. Support for People with Disabilities:• RI ensures inclusion by integrating disability-friendly services in health care delivery and providing specialized referrals when needed.Conclusion:Through these targeted and integrated interventions, RI supports the most vulnerable groups in South Sudan, addressing their health, nutrition, WASH, and protection needs. RI’s emphasis on community-based approaches, gender-sensitive programming, and multi-sectoral integration ensures that these groups receive comprehensive and sustainable care, even in the most challenging environments.
