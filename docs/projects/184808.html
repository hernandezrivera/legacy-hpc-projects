Project Summary

Across Sudan, GBV continues to be a life-threatening concern, exacerbated by a variety of factors including armed conflict, tribal clashes, forced displacement, and recurring disasters such as floods. It is estimated that there are 2,940,705 people in need in 2022 for GBV prevention, mitigation and response, including mainly women and girls from localities that are prone to conflict and tribal clashes, hosting refugees as well as disasters, with a high percentage of households engaged in negative coping mechanisms. This also includes 209,366 refugees without access to core GBV services. The severity of protection needs is very critical in 94 localities across 12 states in Sudan. UNFPA intends to target 80% the GBV subsector target of the PiN in severity ranking 5, 60% in severity ranking 4 and approximately 30% of PiN of the GBV SS target in severity ranking 3.
The project pursues the following 3 objectives:
To provide life-saving specialized quality GBV services including CMR, case management and psychosocial support and reinforce referral pathways
To enhance strategies for community resilience through empowerment of women and girls and strengthened community based interventions
To promote GBV risk mitigation into all aspects of the humanitarian response
To strengthen GBV Coordination mechanism at national and sub-national level and strengthen data collection and information management

1 - Needs

Across Sudan, GBV continues to be a life-threatening concern, exacerbated by a variety of factors including armed conflict, tribal clashes, forced displacement, and recurring disasters such as floods. It is estimated that there are 2,940,705 people in need in 2022 for GBV prevention, mitigation and response, including mainly women and girls from localities that are prone to conflict and tribal clashes, hosting refugees as well as disasters, with a high percentage of households engaged in negative coping mechanisms. This also includes 209,366 refugees without access to core GBV services. The severity of protection needs is very critical in 94 localities across 12 states in Sudan. According to the first nation-wide, qualitative GBV assessment, “Voices from Sudan 2021”, physical and sexual violence are prevalent both inside and outside the home, and sexual violence is more pervasive in rural and conflict-affected communities including refugee camps. Intimate Partner Violence is not considered as a crime, and archaic laws such as the Personal Status Law (1991) mean that incidents that occur in the home are viewed as private issues to be resolved within the confines of the family, thus women tend not to seek legal redress.The findings from the Voices from Sudan also indicates that distance to basic facilities, food insecurity and negative coping mechanisms, especially due to continued harsh economic conditions, compound the problem, including for refugees. Women are forced to adapt their behaviour to try to protect themselves from violence by staying home, avoiding certain locations and not travelling alone. People with disabilities are disproportionately affected by violence including that perpetrated by family members, and child marriage remains a prevalent negative coping mechanism. Access to services remain particularly weak in conflict-affected and refugee hosting areas of South/North Darfur, and West Kordofan, White and Blue Nile as well as East Sudan states. Refugees continue to be hosted in many states including Khartoum, where women and girls continue to face numerous protection risks exposing them to GBV. Adolescent and young girls are particularly affected by early marriage and other forms of harmful practices. Sudan has one of the highest prevalence in harmful practices globally. Among girls aged 20-24 years, 60.2 % were first married or in union before the age of 18, and 87 %of women aged 15–49 years have been subjected to Female Genital Mutilation (FGM) (UNICEF, S3MⅡ 2018). The farming season which coincides with the migration of armed nomadic herders from the north registers high attacks on IDPs, including rapes, in the ensuing tensions over land. Sexual violence and harassment was also reported to have taken place in the crackdown on protests, especially in Khartoum and other state capitals. Most respondents to the VOICES from Sudan survey reported violence against men and boys to be rare, though underreported. Most reports concnered sexual violence against children. Particularly boys in low-income informal jobs and children, including boys, in Qur’anic schools were reported to be at increased risk. Limited respondents also mentioned members of LGBTQ+ community as particularly vulnerable. Boys are also affected by forced marriage. Violence against men and boys goes underreported, key barriers include the belief of it being a sign of weakness and lack of masculinity. GBV survivors have inadequate access to services and reporting mechanisms. Quality specialized life-saving GBV services, such as clinical management of rape (CMR), psycho-social support (PSS), legal aid, safe houses, case management and referral mechanisms are still unavailable, in over 80% of localities, according to the Service Mapping conducted by GBV Sub-Sector in May 2021. 7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Accountability is core in the UNFPA response and improved through continuousconsultation with the affected population on implementation modalities as well as totheir satisfaction with services provided. UNFPA is an active member of the nationalPSEA technical and coordination team and leads the survivor support task team andtogether is working with the team members to ensure that GBV referral mechanismsare accessible to survivors of PSEA. UNFPA will equip implementing partners andtargeted communities with information regarding the PSEA, and how to report andaccess services including health, legal and psychosocial support services. UNFPA’spartners and beneficiaries play an active role in the decision-making process in regardsto the planning, implementing, and evaluating of activities. This is done by conductinga focus group discussion with women and girls in women centers. The project willsupport the development of self-protection capacities and aim at improvingbeneficiaries’ daily lives through improved formal and informal protectionmechanisms. This is considered the most effective way of tackling gender inequalityas the root cause of GBV
