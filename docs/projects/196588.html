Project Summary

On July 12, 2022, the UN Security Council (UNSC) renewed the Cross-Border UNSCR 2642 authorizing UN agencies to continue providing aid from Türkiye into Northwest Syria for six months. The renewal averted immediate catastrophe. But Russia’s insistence that the mandate be cut from 12 to 6 months created significant challenges and uncertainties around the UN cross-border aid effort. While the situation in Northwest Syria remains critical following 12 years of conflict, where there are 4.1 million people in need in Northwest Syria, 3.1 million people are food insecure, and 1.7 million people are internally displaced and living in camps. In the middle of winter, when Syrians’ needs will be at their height, aid agencies and communities will be more desperate waiting for the UNSC negotiations to extend the resolution forward another six months to July 2023. As there is a risk of non-renewal, and each year the risk gets higher.
It will be extremely challenging to find adequate alternatives for the UN cross-border aid mechanism, accordingly the UN's localization efforts by building the capacity of implementing partners became a must to accelerate planning for this scenario and prepare to provide humanitarian aid as best as possible to the people of Northwest Syria. That said, local NGOs and other civil society organizations must play a central role in formulating any such plan that requires well capacitating those NGOs to lay the groundwork for a more sustainable aid strategy in Northwest Syria by strengthening their capacity for early recovery and localization efforts in the response to be able to intervene and providing the immediate needs.
To enable local NGOs of the provision of humanitarian aid in Northwest Syria, there is a need to provide more capacity development activities on technical and cross-cutting topics, building on the exerted efforts in 2022 of the vested interests in the improvement of NGOs’ internal structures through implementing HR consultancies aimed at adopting the humanitarian principles, HR policies, and standardized operating procedures (SOPs), the implementation of Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) workshop with focus on protection, GBV and other vulnerable groups with the aim of building advanced knowledge and technical expertise in response analysis processes and better analyzing and planning response options. While cash became an essential tool for humanitarian aid, implementing Cash Grant Programme for IPs with the aim of strengthening systems of accountability and quality assurance to improve IPs institutional capacities was an essential programme. To restore and improve access to key basic services IOM implemented number of workshops in NWS, namely in Idlib and Azaz, in collaboration with the Access working group attended by frontline humanitarian actors aimed at improving humanitarian staff knowledge and skills on humanitarian access and negotiation. IOM also implemented Compliance and Governance Consultancy to IPs with the focus of advancing compliance and governance areas and allowing IOM contracted IPs to improve their organizational policies and procedures to ensure quality response while conforming to Turkish laws and regulations to sustain their operationalization.
In 2023, IOM will implement more trainings, consultancies and workshops with the focus on Disability inclusion - how to create and implement programs that account for, engage and center persons with disabilities, stress management including secondary trauma and compassion fatigue, monitoring and evaluation, Accountability to Affected Population, research methods and ethics, including on sensitive issues such as GBV, Health, Health, Safety, Security and Environment, Early Recovery, Humanitarian Negotiations for practitioners, community health in NWS in response to various outbreaks, epidemics and pandemics, as well as emergency WASH interventions.

3 - Needs

Based on a comprehensive Capacity Assessment conducted by IOM received for implementing partners in 2022, with IPs requesting more training and coaching sessions on topics such as Disability inclusion - how to create and implement programs that account for, engage and center persons with disabilities, stress management, including secondary trauma and compassion fatigue, monitoring and evaluation, accountability to Affected Population, Research method,s and ethics, including on sensitive issues such as GBV, Health, Health, Safety, Security and Environment, Early Recovery, Humanitarian Negotiations for practitioners, community health in NWS in response to various outbreaks, epidemics, and pandemics, and emergency WASH interventions.

4 - Implementation of activities

IOM is proposing to develop humanitarian partners’ organizational capacity on topics such as disability inclusion - how to create and implement programs that account for, engage and center persons with disabilities - stress management, including secondary trauma and compassion fatigue, monitoring and evaluation, accountability to affected populations, research methods and ethics. including on sensitive issues such as GBV in collaboration with the Protection cluster targeting 125 protection caseworkers, Health, Health, Safety, Security and Environment in collaboration with Shelter cluster targeting 50 Cluster members, and Early Recovery targeting 30 beneficiaries. Furthermore, Humanitarian Negotiations for practitioners' training will target 150 frontline humanitarian actors, and community health workers in NWS in response to various outbreaks, epidemics, and pandemics - in close collaboration with health cluster targeting 40 frontline health workers, and Emergency WASH interventions in coordination with WASH Cluster, targeting 75 cluster’s members. IOM will continue to deliver trainings, host face-to-face sessions when it is feasible to do so. IOM has years of in-house expertise from working in the cross-border context and will recruit technical experts to deliver specific trainings when needed. IOM will continue to liaise with the relevant clusters and working groups to ensure proper coordination.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

IOM will ensure that Protection is mainstreamed through all of the proposed capacity development efforts. Protection risks in capacity development activities include the limited participation and representation of females in the trainings conducted and hence limited inputs on protection mainstreaming from participants. To overcome this limitation and to increase the meaningful participation of women, IOM started coordinating with the Gender advisor for NWS and women groups to circulate its call for participations for trainings, whilst protection topics will be integrated training curriculums across all trainings and coaching sessions.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

In the event IOM uses service providers and consultants to deliver capacity development activities, IOM will encourage female applicants to apply and will ensure that service providers and consultants are trained on PSEA and sign mandatory IOM HR PSEA agreements. During the revision of policies and procedures of the implementing partners, IOM will closely coordinate with the PSEA network to ensure the integration of PSEA in the policies, SOPs, and procedures. Each training aims to have gender-balanced participants, with 50% females and 50% males. IOM will also gather feedback from participants on women's participation in order to improve the participation of women in future capacity development activities.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

To ensure that priority needs are being met, IOM, in coordination with the relevant Custers, will continue to assess the capacity development needs of IPs through regular needs assessments and will prioritize training based on the highest impact. To avoid duplication efforts from different organizations, IOM will liaise with the PWG and the relevant Clusters to capitalize on existing resources and promote localization. The identification and recruitment of qualified service providers for capacity development needs will be done through a transparent and clear recruitment process, which will ensure that the most qualified candidates are selected to deliver capacity-building activities. To mitigate against rapid changes in security which may impact the ability to deliver capacity development activities inside NWS, IOM will ensure that trainings are organized within a sufficiently flexible timeframe so that activities can be conducted when there are relatively stable conditions. IOM will strive for 50/50 involvement of both male and female participants in all capacity development activities and encourages qualified female trainers to deliver capacity development activities. Lastly, IOM will continue utilizing various capacity-building activities using all the effective tools including face-to-face, online platforms IOM e-campus and videos
