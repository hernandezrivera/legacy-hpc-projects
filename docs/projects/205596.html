Project Summary

Children and women in South Sudan continue to experience the devastating effects of malnutrition. According to the 2023 FSNMS and SMART survey’s findings, the national Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) levels remain above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, with 2.5 million children and women at risk of acute malnutrition in 2024 and will need urgent treatment. The recent SMART survey 2023 and FSNMS round 29 findings, reveals serious and critical nutrition situations in the 5 counties that IRC operates in. The SMART Survey 2023 reveals GAM rates of 14.3%, 19.2% in Yirol west and Aweil South counties, respectively. According to the FSNMS round 29, Panyijiar county has alarming proxy GAM rate of 28.2%, Rumbek north and Rumbek East counties have Proxy GAM rates of 12.1% and 11.6% respectively. The key drivers of malnutrition are high morbidity among under five children, high household food insecurity resulting in sub-optimal infant and young child feeding practices, poor water, sanitation and hygiene. This underscores the pressing need for multi-sectoral response and coordination mitigate the impact of malnutrition on children and PLWs.
The objectives of this action will be: To increase equitable access and utilization of quality nutrition services to prevent malnutrition and build resilience among children, adolescents, and women. To increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of wasting for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW. To enhance nutrition situation and program monitoring, analysis, and utilization of nutrition information. To strengthen coordination & integrated programming with health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors for equitable access to services that address key drivers of malnutrition among vulnerable groups in Aweil South, Rumbek East, Rumbek North, Panyijiar and Yirol west by the end of 2024.The proposed intervention aims to increase coverage and access to the most vulnerable community by providing equitable and accessible nutrition prevention treatment services. The nutrition services will deliver by scaling up the emergency nutrition responses through 62 static OTP/TSFP (10 in Aweil south,10 in Panyijar, 23 in Rumbek east, 7 in Rumbek North, and 12 in Yirol west), 5 Stabilization center (1 inPanyijar, 1 Aweil south, 1 in Rumbek east, 1 in Rumbek north, and 1 in Yirol west) and through 4 RRM mobile tam (2 in Aweil south, 2 in Panyjar,). In addition, the IRC will implement integrated CMAM and MIYCN in all sites. Promotion of optimal nutrition and prevention of malnutrition among young children (0-23 months through the implementation of MIYCN to ensure vulnerable children have optimal growth. IRC will strengthen community participation using existing community structures and active engagements of the affected community. IRC will conduct meetings with MtMSG, regular community sensitization sessions and scaling up of the existing Nutrition response to ensure timely service availability. Scale up family MUAC to ensure early detection of acute malnutrition. An estimated 21,223 SAM children, 52,069 MAM, and 37,064 PLW MAM will be reached through this action across all implementation areas in OTP and TSFP, respectively. IRC will continue with the capacity building of CHD, primary health care service providers and nutrition staff on CMAM and MIYCN to ensure timely nutrition services. A rapid response team will be deployed to the hard-to-reach area to provide nutrition services that are accessible and vulnerable groups. Integration of nutrition activities with other sectors will also be implemented, including integration of health and nutrition education messages, Malaria testing and treatment, promotion of hygiene and sanitation, training on GBV and CP for nutrition staff. Similarly, with food security, establish kitchen garden, cash programs and train CNVs on agronomic and fish preservation techniques.

1. Needs

South Sudan (SS) continues to face overlapping crises, including floods, conflict, and food insecurity – all contributing to high malnutrition levels, mainly affecting children under five and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). 2024 projections reveal an exponential 15% increase in the number of people in need compared to 2023, approximately 1.6 million children under the age of five in South Sudan will likely suffer from acute malnutrition in 2024, including 484,502 severely malnourished and will need treatment. The recent SMART survey 2023 and FSNMS round 29 findings, reveals serious and critical nutrition situations in the 5 counties that IRC operates in. Recent IPC 2023 findings reveal high levels of food insecurity classified as IPC phase 3 and above, in the five counties. Between December 2023 and March 2024, it is projected that 473,000 people (55% of the population) will be classified in Phase 3 (crisis) and 4 (emergency) in the five counties: 114,000 in Rumbek East; 44,000 in Rumbek North; 99,000 in Yirol west; 132,000 in Aweil South and 84,000 in Panyijiar. April to June 2024 projections further reveals a 12.5% uptick with 540,000 people classified as phase 3 and 4 compared to December to March 2024 projections: 149,000 in Rumbek East, 48,000 in Rumber North; 108,000 in Yirol West; 144,000 in Aweil south and 91,000 in Panyijiar Counties. The SMART Survey 2023 reveals GAM rates of 14.3%, 19.2% in Yirol west and Aweil South counties, respectively. According to the FSNMS round 29, Panyijiar county has alarming proxy GAM rate of 28.2%, Rumbek north and Rumbek East counties have Proxy GAM rates of 12.1% and 11.6% respectively. Additionally, IPC food security classification in the 5 counties is reported as IPC3 crisis and IPC4 Emergency and is projected to deteriorate in 2024. Analysis shows that the key drivers of malnutrition include high food insecurity, poor water and hygiene conditions, high child morbidity rate especially; diarrhea, malaria or upper respiratory infections, gender-based violence concerns, and poor child feeding and caring practices. These drivers contribute to worsening of the nutrition situation in the counties, as the humanitarian needs intensify due to displacements and loss of livelihoods as a result of conflicts, climatic and economic shocks. In the counties suboptimal practice of MIYCN remain prevalent in South Sudan especially complementary feeding practices as shown by low prevalence of MDD, MAD and MDF. According to the FSNMS round 29 report the Minimum dietary diversification remains low, in unity MDD is at 11.6%, Lakes 12.9 % and NBEG is at 13.5 %. Similarly, Minimum acceptable diet in all states remains extremely low, reported at 2.2 % in unity, 4.7% in NBEG and 6.8% in Lakes. Under this proposed action IRC intend to reach through these proposed actions. IRC targets 21,223 SAM children, 52,069 MAM, and 37,064 PLW MAM will be reached through this action across all implementation areas in the 62 OTP and TSFP centers respectively. IRC will reach 108,771 primary caretakers of children 0-24months and women with Key IYCF messages, and IRC will engage in national immunization days; an estimated 154,092 and 126, 899 children will be reached through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming, respectively, across the targeted counties.

2. Project activities

The proposed response will focus on addressing the immediate nutritional needs and addressing the underlying causes through integrated response. First, to increase equitable access and utilization of quality lifesaving nutrition services for early detection and treatment of acute malnutrition for girls and boys under five years of age and PLW affected by acute malnutrition. IRC will establish robust community mobilization screening and referral mechanisms for early detection and treatment through community outreach activities. Family MUAC will be prioritized. The MtMSG, CHW, and CNV will be trained in MUAC screening and provided with MUAC tapes. Support and strengthen static 62 OTP and sites: IRC will provide the timely provision of quality nutrition therapeutic through 62 OTP/TSFP sites in all counties (AS:10, Panyi=10, RN;7, YW;12 and RE;23). Additionally, five stabilization centers (SC) will be supported and ensure 24/7 treatment for SAM children with medical complications needing inpatient services. All nutrition sites will have at least two community nutrition workers. IRC will establish 4 Rapid response mechanism teams in Panyijar and Aweil south to serve hard-to-reach areas within the counties and improve program coverage. Secondly, staff capacity building is a critical strategic part of the emergency response mechanism contributing to sustainability. IRC will capacity build and share capacity with the MOH, State MOH, county health department, community members, and IRC nutrition team. IRC will train staff on CMAM, MIYCN, promotion of hygiene and sanitation, GBV and Child protection. IRC will provide monitoring tools and national guidelines for the nutrition sites; conduct quarterly on-job mentorship and supervision of nutrition workers by qualified nutrition officers; facilitate joint (SMoH, CHD, UNICEF, WFP, and IRC) support supervision in all the counties. Establish feedback/complaints mechanisms at all nutrition sites in form community meetings and surveys; timely and regular nutrition information reports submission from all the nutrition sites into cluster nutrition dashboard; actively support and facilitate national surveys (FSNMS). Thirdly, the IRC will promote and support safe and appropriate maternal, infant and young child nutrition practices focused on preventing malnutrition through behavior change and communication strategies. IRC will establish and support 310 MTMSG linked to nutrition centers and conduct community nutrition education sessions. IRC will reach 108,771 primary caretakers of children 0-24 months women through Key IYCF messages, and IRC will engage in national immunization days; an estimated 154,092 and 126,899 children will be reached through Vitamin A supplementation and deworming, respectively, across the targeted counties. Fourth, to increase equitable access to nutrition sensitive interventions from health, WASH, FSL, Education and Protection sectors through enhanced coordination and joint programming targeting vulnerable groups. IRC will establish kitchen gardens and conduct cooking demonstration at the nutrition centers. IRC will train community nutrition volunteers on agronomic and fish preservation techniques. IRC will refer TSFP beneficiaries to GFD. Similarly, all service points at the PHHCC/U will screen under five and PLW for malnutrition at MCH, EPI, OPD, and strong referral linkage. Conduct prevention and treatment of Malaria. IRC will refer mothers and children for ANC follow-up and immunization. Providing WASH kits for SAM clients attending nutrition sites. IRC will continue to integrate cash programing for children discharged cured for SAM. IRC will also ensure all gender and age groups are equally able to access and receive high quality nutrition services.
