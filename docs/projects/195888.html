Project Summary

This project aims to provide FSL services through supporting the barley value chain and women groups in food processing training skills.
The project will support 375 farmers and 900 animals breeders in Mhambel, Jisr Shughur, Sharan, and Jindiries sub-districts as follows:
- Selecting 375 barley farmers who have access to 5 donum planted with barley. And supporting them through:
- Distributing Urea 46% fertilizer (75 kg per farmer).
- Holding extension field visits to provide the best agricultural practices.
- Distributing cash E-voucher with a value of 50$ for pesticides.
- Distributing cash E-voucher with a value of 50$ for harvesting.
- Purchasing 70 % of the final product of barley grains (337.5 tons) and straw (337.5 tons). And keeping 30 % of products for planting next season.
- Selecting 627 animals breeders (327 sheep breeders and 300 cow breeders), and supporting them through:
- Distributing 270 tons of barley grains to 327 sheep breeders. Each breeder will receive 825 kg of barley grains to feed 5 sheep for 5 months. Where each sheep need 33 kg/month, which is the milk production period.
- Distributing 67.5 tons of barley grains to 300 cow breeders (225 kg per breeder).
- Distributing 135 tons of barley straw to 300 cow breeders (450 kg per breeder).
The fodder will be distributed to animal breeders by in-kind modality. IHR technical committee will test the quality of barley on the field during harvesting to confirm its eligibility to be consumed by animals. IHR will rent trucks and distribute the fodder to breeders in their houses in the same targeted community. where the fodder will be filled with clean bags with weight 50 kg, and have information about the kind of fodder.
The selection criteria of farmers are (having access to at least 5 donum of land planted with barley crop, having no access to fixed monthly income, not benefiting from similar support, pledge to sell 70 % of the final products to the IHR). High priority will be given to smallholders.
The selection criteria of animal breeders are (sheep breeder has from 5 to 50 sheep, cow breeder has from 1 to 3 cows, have no access to fixed monthly income, are not benefitting from similar support). high priority will be given to breeders who own fewer animals.
The project will support 200 women by establishing 20 women groups (10 members per group) and supporting them to able of producing the dairy products as follows:
- Distributing 20 kits of food processing tools for 20 groups with a value of 200$ per kit. Those assets will be used to produce dairy products daily.
- Distributing cow milk to women groups for four months to enable producing the dairy products and marketing it.
Milk purchasing will be from the targeted 300 cows breeders by purchasing 30 % of their daily products with a subsidized price through the E-voucher system. Every day about 200 liters of milk will be available for each women's group with an estimated cost of 1 $ per kg.
That process aims to provide small grants to women by providing raw materials (milk) to start their special businesses. And link women with milk producers and markets to ensure a successful and sustainable capital cycle.
The 20 kits of food processing tools will be handed over to the group's members to be used after finishing the project period. Where they will receive capacity-building training to enable them to work together and manage the small project.
The selection criteria of those women are (women with age 18 to 50 years, have experience in producing dairy products, have no access to fixed monthly income, are not participating in similar training projects, have the willingness and the ability to participate in the training sessions). High priority will be given to female-headed households.
The required fund for this project is planned for the year 2023. Since it is not secured and IHR is planning to secure the fund through advocacy with possible donors.

3 - Needs

After eleven years of crisis during which the country, most Syrians face a context of continuing humanitarian decline. According to HNO 2023, the humanitarian and economic indicators continue to deteriorate, basic services are collapsing, and an ongoing cholera outbreak and climatic shocks compound an already dire situation.As a result of these challenges, in 2023, 15.3 million people will require humanitarian assistance, including 2.1 million IDPs living in last-resort IDP sites, with needs that keep increasing across all sectors.12.1 million are food insecure. PWDs in Syria face particular challenges and have specific needs that too often remain unmet. Where 1 in 4 people is PWDs.These findings match the findings resulting from the need assessment conducted by IHR in communities of Ariha, Jisr Shughur, Jindieris, Shran and Afrin sub-districts. The assessment results indicated that women are the most crisis-affected category. In general, they lost breadwinners and did not have access to sources of income. So, they need to be supported sustainably through training them and building their capacity.Moreover, the agriculture sector is affected due to the high prices of inputs and fuel for irrigation. in addition to the low quality and less availability of those agricultural items like seeds, and fertilizers.As well as the livestock breeders were affected due to the high prices of fodder the related of low production of fodder crops and focusing farmers on wheat.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. Likelihood: High.Impact: High.Mitigating Measure: Promote the Establishment of an adequate management plan; Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on the package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code conduct among partner staff.Risk 2: Risk of theft of NFIs (food processing equipment).Likelihood: Medium Impact: High Mitigating Measure: Agencies provide feedback and accountability mechanisms to different groups of users; Work closely with all partners on the ground; Promote distribution stock between multiple warehouses in the target regions.Risk 3: Cholera outbreak among people.Likelihood: Medium.Impact: Medium.Mitigating Measure: Spreading awareness among individuals through awareness sessions that include all segments of society, especially women and children. In addition to distributing brochures and videos through the communications canals, to show the necessary personal hygiene methods as a basic means of protection against infection with Cholera.Providing referral services in the targeted area for potential injuries by transferring them to hospitals for treatment. Advocating for supporters to provide the necessary support to households affected by cholera.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Based on IHR PSEA policy and before starting project interventions, IHR will conduct a SEA risk assessment to identify all potential SEA risks such as :1- Risk of SEA act by non-PSEA trained staff while providing the support to BNFs, 2-BNFs donot report SEA cases for IHR staff, which results in the IHR being unable to investigate these cases and provide the needed assistance to them.Mitigation: After an in-depthassessment for these risks, IHR through collaboration with PSEA focal point will bedesigned the mitigation plan for all these risks as follows Mitigation measures:1- IHR will ensure that all its staff had signed a code of conduct to prevent acts of SEA by its staff and all recruited staff should attend PSEA training before any contact with BNFs.2- Beneficiary awareness-raising (Beneficiaries must be aware of: what SEA is, what their rights are in regard to SEA, what they can do if SEA occurs.3- Establish safe and accessible community reporting channels to report SEA complaints tool will also be available (e.g. complaints box/telephone hotline to deal with these cases)
