Project Summary

This project will be jointly implemented by three partners Ard El Insan (AEI), We World GVC (WW-GVC) and Qatar Red Cresent Society (QRCS) targeting families of children 6-59 months old via house hold interventions, where 1400 children will be referred to AEI centers for treatments of moderate to severe wasting and iron deficiency anemia.
The main goal of this project is to Contribution to the provision of life saving health and nutrition services for children less than 5 years old in Gaza by providing accessibility for health and nutritional services for children less than five years who are suffering from life threatening malnutrition illness specially when accompanied with other medical illnesses .
The project will target children whose their morbidity and mortality associated with moderate to severe malnutrition or anemia ,will be significantly high specially when exposed to virulent micro- organisms epidemics such as Covid 19 virus which is considered currently as a high risk threat all over the world ( WHO updated reports on website)
AEI and its partner will provide health and nutrition care services to children < 5 years. The project will ensure accessing communities of vulnerable areas in providing essential life saving health interventions including nutrition, hygiene and sanitation services for the beneficiaries, towards making incremental changes in their situation as reflected in project outcome.
The project targets 1400 children( direct beneficiaries) selected based on vulnerability criteria ; children in vulnerable areas according to their location, status, protection risks and needs severity. Beneficiaries are selected from most affected locations in Gaza North (Jabalya Camp, Jabalya, Izbat Beit Hanoun,); Gaza ( Gaza City) Deir ALBalah , Khan Younis and Khan Younis camp . Rafah and Rafah camp.
The project applies right based approach and “do no harm” principle, currently it is reflected by providing least contact to reduce risks of caching the Covid -19 virus which is spread globally. Its activities are:
screening 2036 H.H at the vulnerable areas and admission 1400 children who are detected to moderate to severe wasted or moderate to severe Anemia. They will be provided with proper medical and nutritional treatments with provision of micro nutrient supplements and follow up visits and individual counseling sessions beside that’s provision of wash and essential services to 327 H.H. including at least 47 women headed H. in addition of provision hygiene kit for 1400 families .
The project will follow updated approaches to achieve resilience of beneficiaries, participatory monitoring, gender/age differences, inclusion of women and children with disabilities, environmentally neutral, protection mainstreaming, security and full access to project services

1 - Needs

Gaza Strip continues to face acute crisis since the imposition of the Israeli blockade in 2007. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2020, poverty is one of the main determinants of food insecurity. According to the latest available data, around 29 per cent of Palestinians in the oPt live below the poverty line. The gap between the West Bank and Gaza is substantial, with 53 per cent of the population below the poverty line in Gaza (expected to raise to 64% post COVID-19 according to estimates by the World Bank), compared to 14 per cent in the West Bank (estimated to raise to 30% after the COVID-19). Another reason of increasing poverty drastically in the coming period due to expected economic recession following the declaration of State of Emergency COVID-19 on March 5, 2020 with tight closure and movement restriction. Due to the increase in the level of poverty (at 64%) and unemployment (more than 45%) in Gaza, many families cannot afford the cost of food nor basic needs including clean water and hygienic material, which increases the health risk exposure particularly among children. The dire humanitarian situation means that over 70% of Gaza’s population is dependent on some form of aid. The level of food insecurity remains alarmingly high in Gaza, revealing the long-term impact of the disruptive shocks of recent years, where an estimated 62 per cent of households are severely or moderately food insecure, compared to 9.2 per cent in the West Bank. Female-headed households in Palestine show a level of food insecurity of 31 per cent, compared to 26 per cent for male-headed households. The Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) declared that an additional 81,000 HHs in Gaza would be in need for assistance with food distribution following COVID-19. These numbers are for newly vulnerable negatively affected by COVID, not included in other lists, and are in need of humanitarian assistance.AEI, QRC and WW-GVC conducted a complementary field survey targeting 650 H.H. for Malnourished children ( severe and moderate wasting and Anemic children) at vulnerable areas to assess the shelter and WASH conditions in particular (in terms of water, sanitation, hygiene and general shelter conditions), considering the different needs and expectations of women, men, girls, boys, the elderly and people with disabilities. The assessment identified 650 vulnerable HHs requiring: -Rehabilitation/construction of bathroom and kitchen - 327 HHs; -Increase of water storage capacity -165 HHs.The assessment also identified the most vulnerable areas that lack access to the essential WASH requirements, including hygiene materials and lack of hygiene awareness.The proposed intervention aims to enhance the resilience of these vulnerable women, men, girls and boys, considered some of the most vulnerable across the whole GS, by improving their hygiene awareness and conditions. Further, it also aims to develop and adapt a suitable environment for people with disabilities that serve the community to incorporate all “inclusivity” principles. Women especially will benefit from this project since, according to local culture, they spend more time inside the house as they are in charge of all housework. Poor nutrition among children under the age of five years as a result of several reasons such as poverty, lack of access to nutritional foods, and poor feeding practices, have led to a rise in cases of wasting, stunting and anaemia in children aged 7-24 months . Malnutrition is also strongly linked to recurrent infections such as gastroenteritis and respiratory tract infections. Water, sanitation and hygiene conditions (e.g. quantity and quality of drinking water availability, presence of waste disposal facilities) are vital to ensure that all health and nutrition interventions integrate key elements of water, sanitation and hygiene e.g. promotion of handwashing, provision of soap and latrines. Due to the increase in the level of poverty, many families cannot afford the cost of food nor basic needs including clean water and hygienic material, which increases the health risk exposure particularly among childrenThe outbreak of COVID-19 exposes children and their caregivers to potentially massive disruption to their healthcare, access to basic needs and services like food, protection and social interaction. The capacity of the Palestinian health system to cope with an expected increase in COVID-19 cases is severely impaired by longstanding challenges and critical shortages. The situation is particularly severe in the Gaza Strip, where the health system has been undermined by the longstanding Israeli blockade, the internal Palestinian divide, a chronic power deficit and shortages in specialized staff, drugs and equipment and with massive casualties from the ongoing “Great March of Return”. Various preventive measures have been put in place by the government and other actors to mitigate or slow down the spread of the virus into the Gaza Strip. A major concern is for children being treated for malnutrition, including anaemia cases, is that they may not be able to access their treatment services or that may suffer severe consequences if they get infected with the Corona 19 virus.

2 - Activities

Activities to be conducted by AEI:AEI will provide access to healthcare and nutrition that prevent excessive maternal and newborn morbidity and mortality by ensuring essential newborn care services are available at all times through:Activity 1: Provision of Basic nutritional supplies (micronutrient supplements, therapeutic treatment of moderate and severe wasting ,will be procured and provided to AEI nutrition centers that will join and support the project. These will be used to provide the full support for the 1,400 targeted children identified with specific nutritional needs and support(quantities are estimated based on AEI database and are subject to change upon actual admission of cases) • High Nutrient Biscuits, (20,520 KG).• Multivitamin Syrup 200 ml, (5,677 Bottle).• Vitamin (A&D) Drops 10 ml, (5,814 Dropper).• Iron drops 15ml (50mg elemental iron per ml) supplements as prophylactic, (5600 Bottle).• Anti-biotic, (513 Bottle).• Anti protozoal and Anti helminthic (855 Bottle).• Anti-fungal (1,026 Tube).• Anti-pyritic (684 Bottle).• Folic acid , (56,760 tab) .Activity 2: management of malnutrition children with moderate, severe wasting and anemiaChildren 6 to 59 months with signs, symptoms of moderate or severe wasting and or anemia discovered through screening and assessment from HH beneficiaries will be managed at AEI Health Nutrition care centre to start malnutrition management and treatment process.Activities to be conducted by QRCS:Activity 4:Provision of appropriate hygiene kit for 1400 H.H.QRCS will prepare the list of contents of a hygiene kit to help protection from COVID 19 infection for the whole family members of 1400 targeted children. They will be responsible for procurement and distribution of the kits. Activities to be conducted by WW-GVC:Activity5: Pre/post needs assessment. Conduction of a rapid assessment in order to verify and monitor the exact needs including: technical survey for planning and design; HHs field visits and gender separated focus groups to collect feedback and incorporate them in the action.Activity6: Improvement to WASH and shelter essential services for 327 HHs including at least 47 women headed HHs.6.1. Consultation with HH members, especially women, to collect feedback and prioritize the rehabilitation methodology, ensuring to increase their comfort and privacy.6.2. Bills of Quantities, technical specifications and tendering process, to meet basic humanitarian principles and a signed commitment to comply with Special Measures for Protection from any kind of abuse and harassment.6.3. Rehabilitation works, including mainly WASH and minor shelter interventions.a. Rehabilitation/ upgrading of bathrooms (toilets, hand washing/ shower facilities, partitions, windows and doors, tiling, plastering and painting works, sun water heater, access for disabled people)b. Rehabilitation/ upgrading of kitchens (dishwashing sinks, counters, tiling, plastering and painting works)c. Rehabilitation/upgrading of general shelter structure (access ramps for PwDs, house roofing)d. providing infection prevention and control measures to break the transmission of COVID19. e. Basic intervention aiming to improve water storagef. Basic intervention aiming to improve solid waste management (distribution of cleaning kits)The repairs/upgrade will ensure women’s and men’s physical and climate protection, privacy and dignity; improve access to HH WASH services, safety and security and reflect gender and protection.ACTIVITY 7:. Hygiene promotion and environmental health activities The activities will be implemented through a participatory community approach, tailored to the specific needs and behaviors of women, men, girls and boys, with the aim to raise awareness on sanitation, hygiene and environmental health, especially in the situation of COVID19 outbreak, as well as the adoption of life-saving hygiene behaviors through:a. At least 98 Hygiene awareness promotion sessions targeting women, men, girls, boys, the elderly and PWDs. A pre-intervention KAP survey will set up the baseline, while a post KAP survey will measure the impact and effectiveness of the campaign, to increase the awareness in IPC protocols.b. Procurement and distribution of 327 hygiene kits, considering feedback from women/girls and men/boys separately, and adapting the kits to their specific needs.c. At least 16 Public environmental health campaigns involving the Behavioral Change Communication (BCC) approach to enhance the protection and safety of the surrounding environment, in collaboration with AEI.The partners will play an important role in supporting all phases of the campaigns, mainstreaming gender, with the purpose of avoiding the perpetuation of stereotypes and socially imposed roles for women and men.
