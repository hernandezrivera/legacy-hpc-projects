Project Summary

Maram has designed this proposed to provide the Shelter and NFI assistance for the most affected people in NW Syria to upgrading their living condition through:
- Distribution of essential NFI items.
- Improving accessibility and drainage systems.
- Rehabilitation damaged houses.
1- Distribution of essential NFI items: MF plans to distribute 5,000 NFI kits to target 25,000 newly IDPs with different ages and vulnerable cases PWN (23 % men, 25 % women, 26 % Boys, and 27% girls ) taking into consideration Gender and age diversity through NFI kits distribution in Darkosh Sub- District in 3 communities ( Darkosh , Zarzur , Mreimin ), where Maram Foundation has full access to the mentioned area and is implementing Emergency Response right now in it.
2- Improving accessibility and drainage systems:
The activity aims at upgrading the living conditions of the targeted IDP sites by improving their infrastructure, this will be achieved by implementing graveling rehabilitation activity for the roads and tents of the targeted sites, establishing ramps for elderly people and PLWD and establishing drainage systems inside the sites to avoid any damage to the tents and property of the IDPs during rainy seasons and thus protect the camps from the consequences of the floods.
Maram Foundation will provide graveling for the roads and ground insulation for the family tents for 10 IDPs sites in Idleb were selected after Maram teams conducted a detailed rapid needs assessment.
The total target is thus 25,000 IDPs (4925 men, 5475 women, 7800 boys, 6800 girls).
3- MF will implement this activity to target both the host community and IDPs in Suran sub-district, serving the populations of 5 communities (Dweibeq, Mreigel, Albil, Suran, Ehteimlat) through shelter rehabilitation activities and rehabilitate shelter for 1,100 households (HHs), which include 5500 individuals (1183 male, 1254 female, 1509 boys, and 1554 girls) living in 1,000 damaged houses in Northern Aleppo governorate. MF will provide them with shelter repair and rehabilitation activities. This project includes 59% host communities and 41% IDPs.
All activities within the projects will be according to the Shelter cluster objectives and will be designed according to the standards of SPHERE.
Furthermore, the planned intervention will mainstream protection principles especially “Do no harm” throughout the project cycle by considering the different needs of men, women, boys, and girls.
During this project, Maram will consult with the community and individual IDPs taking into account the speciﬁc needs of each group. Maram Foundation also will involve the women and girls through Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) during the community outreach plan.
Summary, this project aims to reach 40,500 individuals targeting most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls and prioritize family members with high vulnerabilities according to the beneficiary selection criteria (female/single/child-headed households, IDP status, no source of income and support, people with disabilities, elderly,...etc.).
Maram Foundation always seeks to high-level coordination with all parties involved in any project (SNFI cluster, Donors, local authorities, other NGOs, and other related parties) to avoid any duplication. So for the activities included in this project and during the designing phase, Maram has coordinated with all the relevant parties and selected the locations according to the gaps, which means that there will not be any duplication during implementing the proposed project activities.

4 - Needs

A further intensifying of the crisis in north-west and north-east Syria resulted in significant levels of displacement to already fragile areas, with little capacity by humanitarian partners to respond appropriately, due to funding, situational and access constraints resulting in significant numbers of IDPs without shelter or basic means of survival, with IDPs expected to continue increasing into 2020. Based on CCCM Cluster report which cover January 2020 “620,867 IDPs displaced for Sothern and Eastern Idleb countryside to North Idleb and to the areas near to Turkish border “. the majority of IDPs settled in communities, camps and informal settlements. The large displacement movement led to establish many new informal settlements through the local communities and councils. According to the CCCM Cluster and SNFI HNO 2020, displaced people remain in need of multi-sectorial assistance and face increasingly complex and interlinked protection needs, the two most urgent needs and challenges reported by the newly displaced populations are non-food items (NFI) and shelter. So, MF seeks to save lives, uphold rights and promote access to safe and more privacy shelter through the provision of Shelter and NFI assistance in particular, which include distributing NFI items for the new arrivals, providing ground insulation of their tents in addition to improv the accessibility and the drainage systems in many IDPs sites that affected from flooding during winter seasons, and rehabilitate the houses for those who live in a damaged houses where there is no privacy and not protected from the external living conditions.MF is currently implementing Gravelling and Tents insulation project in 27 camps and informal settlement in northern Idleb Countryside, in adition, MF had implemented Shelter rehabilitation project in partnership with Global Communities, where rehabilitated 128 damaged houses and 6 collective centers in Idleb Governorate.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions, risks, and mitigations: Risk 1: Organizations Acceptance by beneﬁciaries, communities and other stakeholders: CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION MEASURE: Maram Foundation will prepare a community outreach plan, will attending and conduct coordination meetings with target community members and the local authorities to explain project activities, targets, and purposes. Risk 2: Access prevented (Armed groups occupying the area; active conﬂict, disgruntled locals; access/transport routes blocked by huge number of IDPs ﬂeeing to north of Idleb) likelihood: Likely impact: critical mitigation: Avoid checkpoints whenever possible.Conduct a safe approach to the checkpoint. Deal with main actors. Try to comply. follow policies and procedures/ SOPs etc/ staff training. Risk 3: Front Line Shifting likelihood: Likely impact: critical mitigation: Avoid targeted locations Prepare for evacuationRisk 4: Exchange rates change signiﬁcantly and therefore the budget is insuﬃcient to reach the targeted number of beneﬁciaries CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION MEASURE: - MF will maintain a regular dialogue with its donor in the event of a signiﬁcant change - MF conducts regular price monitoring, utilizing existing information where available - MF will contract with the contractor in the dollar to avoid the risk. Risk 5: MF unable to maintain access to the most vulnerable populations despite changes in conﬂict dynamics inside Syria CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION MEASURE: MF has adopted a Whole-of-Syria structure in order to provide multiple entry-points to vulnerable affected populations and to enable a more ﬂexible response. Risks 6: HLP clear documents CONTINGENCY / MITIGATION: MF staff will verify all the HLP documents available through the local council and actual community members.
