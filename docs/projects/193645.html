Project Summary

According to UNHCR at September 2022 estimated that Sudan hosts about 1.14 million refugees, of whom 43 per cent are children, 52 per cent are adults and five per cent elderly. UNHCR estimates that this number will be reduced to 926,000 refugees in 2023. This is still Africa's second largest refugee population.
The project aim to enhance intervention and expand life for Ethiopian and South Sudanese refugees in Blue Nile, South and West Kordofan over a period of 12 months (Jan-Dec 2023).
Through protection component (CP, GP and GBV) HA-UK is proposing to deliver comprehensive child protection and GBV services targeting vulnerable IDPs and host communities in Blue Nile at (Al Kurmuk, Ar Rusayris and Wad Al Mahi localities), South Kordofan (Abu Jubayhah, Abu Kershola and Dilling localities) and West Kordofan at (Lagowa and Keilak localities) to 18,200 . The aim of this response is to reduce the suffering of boys, girls, women and men including children with disabilities who are at risk of experiencing violence, abuse, exploitation and neglect through provision of specialized/lifesaving child protection and multi sectoral services.
HA will use a multi-sectoral approach and strengthen coordination to leverage referral pathways to ensure the needs of the survivors are addressed and considered with respect and confidentiality. This approach includes prevention through awareness raising and community-based education and information campaigns, capacity development for partners, monitoring and reporting, and provision of services for the survivors by Conduct protection assessments for communities, targeting Persons with Specific Needs (PSNs), CBCPMs are strengthened and incorporated to CPCs CAPs through the development of risk reduction action and monitoring plans by CP focal points, who advocate for children’s rights in CPCs, Psychosocial support services* (PSS) are available for emergency affected children (girls and boys) through the establishment of safe healing and learning spaces throughout the areas of operation, PSS are provided for primary caregivers to improve the care and positive coping mechanisms for managing trauma and stress in themselves and children through Parenting Skills Sessions, Lifesaving, age appropriate case management and PSS are available to women and girls, Women and girls face reduced risks and benefit from an improved protective environment through community engagement, advocacy and risk mitigation, Minimum actions to respond to GBV and ensure women’s and girls’ safety are integrated.
Through Food Security and livelihood component HA-UK aim to reach out 20,400 persons supporting them for improving their livelihoods and economic inclusion in Blue Nile, South, West Kordofan states.
Overall component objective: economic inclusion, Food security and livelihoods for refugees, asylum seekers, promoted through the proposed activities, HA will ensure a timely preposition of assorted crop seeds—in particular sorghum, maize, cowpeas and groundnuts—and tools, providing beneficiaries with the means to reestablish their own production and help them address the threat of hunger. Also, HA will ensure the beneficiaries receive timely agricultural inputs in addition to in collaborate with state ministry of agriculture to train beneficiaries (in groups) in Rain-fed agriculture and vegetable production including land preparation, agronomic practices, fertilizer application, pest management and control, harvesting, storage, post-harvest loses, seedling production to support environment conservation, seed bank system and marketing. Training will ensure that participant understand and are able to implement basic agronomic practices that will assist in emission reduction of greenhouse gases and enhance carbon sequestration.

1 - Needs

According to UNHCR, Forced displacement within Sudan and into neighboring countries has continued to increase since an armed conflict erupted between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) in mid-April 2023 in multiple states across Sudan, including Khartoum, Kordofan, Darfur and North. The current fighting has already displaced over 800,000 people inside Sudan. In addition, over 220,000 refugees and refugee returnees have left Sudan. The conflict has worsened the situation for all families, but especially for vulnerable groups, such as refugees, they live in overcrowded conditions and face repeated emergencies Conflict and violence are anticipated to continue, leading to widespread human rights abuses, protection issues and GBV, as well as damage to infrastructure and housing. As well, amid increasing violence and deteriorating conditions, the situation in South Sudan has escalated to a full-blown humanitarian emergency. The total number of South Sudanese refugees has now passed 2 million and it is the largest refugee crisis in Africa. After Syria and Afghanistan, it is the third largest refugee crisis in the world. Sadly, 63 percent of South Sudanese refugees are under the age of 18. UNHCR reported The majority of those fleeing South Sudan are women and children. They are survivors of violent attacks, sexual assault, and in many cases, children are traveling alone. Sudan is both a destination and transit country for asylum seekers, refugees, and migrants from at least ten countries. These include refugees from Eritrea, the Central African Republic (CAR), Ethiopia, Chad, Syria, and Yemen. Refugees in Sudan are settled in camps, out-of-camp settlements, and urban areas across 18 states. According to UNHCR at Sep 2022 estimated that Sudan hosts about 1.14 million refugees, of whom 43 per cent are children, 52 per cent are adults and five per cent elderly. UNHCR estimates that this number will be reduced to 926,000 refugees in 2023. This is still Africa's second largest refugee population. The largest group of refugees is from South Sudan, with an estimated 811,000 South Sudanese refugees in the country by September 2022, of whom 52 per cent are female. From January to August 2022, more than 36,000 South Sudanese crossed into Sudan, mainly into White Nile State, East Darfur, West Kordofan and South Kordofan.Despite progress made significant gaps remain & exacerbated by funding gaps, economic crises. Over 30% of the refuges are living in camps where they relay on assistance provided through humanitarian partners. Although progress has been made to achieve minimum assistance standards in camps, gaps remain across sectors. There is a high food insecurity, this compounded by limiting access to income generation opportunities and prevalence of child labor, early marriage and other child protection issues as well as high risks of SGBV. Generally, They are expose to protection risks and exploitation including SGBV by the criminal networks who are active Sudan and involved in human smuggling and people trafficking. Also, in BN, SK and WK thousands of refugees are living in poor situation, seeking for livelihood opportunities and humanitarian assistance. In 2023, this number is expected to be increased significantly. In Early November 2020 armed conflict erupted in Ethiopia in Tigray’s region. Accordingly, Sudan received big influxes of refuges who arrived in poor health situation, non or less personal possessions and frighten seeking safety, security and live saving humanitarian assistance. There is gap in all sectors and there is high need to provide humanitarian live saving assistance of all sectors particularly FSL and protection.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

HA adheres to the five Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) defined by the IASC Task Force As part of its strong commitment to AAP, HA is engaging refugees and other persons of concern in the programme cycle through formation, training and strengthening of community-based structures and networks who take leading role in project implementation. HA has distinctive community based and AGD approaches that promotes the active participation of targeted population particularly girls, women, disability and minority and ensures engagement of all people of concern across the interventions levels. The people of concerns and other stakeholders are also able to share their opinions and complaints through feedback mechanism. This ensures greater accountability to people of concern and support the project sustainability. The opinions and feedback are regularly collected, analyzed and processed to inform project adjustment and improvement. This has largely reflected in building trust with the people of concerns and host communities as well as led to client satisfaction. Through its presence and capacity support to counterparts, HA has strengthened partnership with the local authorities and line ministries including HAC and COR. This helps for smooth and timely delivery of services to the targeted people of concerns in the hard to reach areas. HA has open access to all areas where refugees are hosted and able to deliver uninterrupted services.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

HA-UK has established multi-sector Community Feedback and Response Mechanisms (CFRM) in all its operational sites including in the proposed project location (South and West Kordofan states) through which the beneficiaries will be informed on their rights to launch complaints or give other forms of feedback in regard to the implementation process and the quality of services they are receiving from the HA-UK. The existing complaint and feedback mechanism will be used during the project period with beneficiaries being sensitized on beneficiary accountability standards. Communities will also be informed about the focal point in the HA-UK to handle any compliant report and feedback. The reporting procedures will be displayed in a visible place in the project location and complains and feedback boxes will be placed at WGSS and project site offices for those who are literate to use. Views and feedback will be collected from women, girls and community beneficiaries including those living with different disabilities and the minority, authorities from the start of the project to the intervention and monthly thereafter. Feedback and complaints will generally be collected using FGD’s with groups of men, women, boys, and girls; during community sensitization and awareness sessions; and through the use of suggestion boxes. HA’s community feedback mechanism allows for a range of complaints to be received and managed, including a mechanism for confidentially fast-tracking concerns of a serious nature such as Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. Through this discussion, the information and the feedback obtained from the beneficiaries of the HA-UK will explain its procedures, decisions and allow women and girls to hold HA-UK accountable. Awareness raising on existing PSEA Networks or Focal Point(s) will be included to ensure that beneficiaries are well informed of existing reporting mechanisms.
