Project Summary

The project covers two sub-clusters activities including Child Protection and Mine Action.
Child Protection: The recruitment of children to the conﬂict remains widespread with 47% preventing children from school and causing family separation and other negative effects. So, the project seeks to increase access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions to prevent and respond to all kinds of violations against boys and girls. The project aims to establish three child-friendly spaces in three locations:
-Idleb city
-Atarib town in western Aleppo
-Jarablus city in northern Aleppo
Activities:
Because of the crisis in Syria and its consequences of displacement, shelling, and loss of the family members, many people, especially children, suffer from psychological pressure, sometimes resulting in high levels of risk that could lead to suicide. So, there is a great need to conduct (PSS) Psychological Support activities for children suffering from stress and pressure.
-So we will provide PSS services for (250 boys, 250 girls).
-We will also provide alternative education for (60 boys, 60 girls) from children dropping out of school including children with disabilities, CAAFAG, UASC, and other vulnerable children.
Adolescents & Youth: Adolescents and youth who are about 56 % of the population, have also been severely affected by the conﬂict.
Activities: We will provide adolescents and youth with the following activities.
-Life skills and vocational training in computer skills, accounting, and English for 300 adolescents and youth (150 males, 150 females) (from 14 to 25 years)
-Parenting programmes for (260 men, 400 women) from headed household youth.
-Specialized child protection services (case management) and follow up case management services for 660 children (330 boys and 330 girls) for vulnerable children including CAAFAG (Children Associated with Armed Forces and Armed), UASC (Unaccompanied And Separated Children), children with disabilities.
The activities will be in the CFSs (child-friendly spaces), where children can receive recreational kits and other creative activities.
The case management activity also includes the following services:
-Family tracing services and reuniﬁcation with families for (30 boys, 30 girls) UASC children.
-Reintegration services for (60 boys) CAAFAG, including alternative education, life skills, and vocational training in (computer skills, accounting, and English).
-Referral (60 boys, 60 girls) from children with disabilities to physical treatment centers in the areas of operation according to the service mapping.
-The project also provides capacity building activities for 60 people (30 males, 30 females) from Afaq staff, humanitarian actors and local authorities to improve the quality of protection services to strengthen the ability to prevent the violations against children.
Mine Action: The threat of explosive hazards in contaminated areas puts the lives of millions of people at risk. These risks have been exacerbated due to the lack of access to education and child protection services. So, there is a great need to reduce the impact of explosive hazards and injuries by mines or UXOs.
Afaq's mobile teams will provide awareness-raising sessions on the dangers of explosive remnants of war in schools targeting students and teachers in the following locations:
-Idleb city.
-Atareb town in western Aleppo.
-Jarablus city in northern Aleppo.
We will conduct awareness-raising sessions on ERW (Explosive Remnants of War) for 10000 children (5000 boys, 5000 girls) and 500 adults (250 men, 250 women).
We target teachers as adults beneficiaries. However, they can deliver awareness messages to their students later.

Needs

Child Protection: Based on the "Protection Sector DRAFT HNO 2019 chapter (2019) v.3 – 09-12.2018", children throughout Syria continue to face pervasive violence and a range of protection risks. The ongoing or recurrent conflict environment experienced by many boys and girls over the past seven years has profoundly affected their psychosocial well-being, weakening their sense of security, purpose, control, and significance, or exercising social agency, and causing profound distress. However, as Afaq has been providing child protection services in Idleb and western Aleppo for more than three years, we found that there is still a big need and gap for specialized services for children including case management, psychological support, reintegration, alternative education, and vocational training. It is estimated that more than 2.6 million children remain displaced and separation from caregivers is a reported child protection issue in all 14 governorates of Syria putting children at risk of exploitation, abuse or neglect "2019 MSNA". In 46% of assessed communities, respondents reported the occurrence of family separation and events leading to family separation indicate how related protection and child protection issues increase the vulnerability of affected boys and girls, with death or disappearance of caregivers, a disappearance of children, separation due to displacement or conflict, child marriage and child recruitment among cited reasons for family separations. The conflict and its impact on children and families have fuelled negative coping mechanisms such as child labor, and child recruitment, putting adolescent boys and girls at particular risk. Child labor is reported to occur widely in all 14 governorates of Syria. Child labor was the protection issue most frequently cited by respondents, in 81% of assessed communities. The issue emerged in over 90% of assessed communities in 8 governorates; over 80% in 12 governorates; over 50% in all 14 governorates". Child labor interferes with children’s schooling by depriving them of the opportunity to attend school, obliging them to leave school prematurely, or requiring them to attempt to combine school attendance with excessively long and heavy work.Child labor is a result of the lack of formal education for years because of the war, where children missed several years of study and they are now unable to return to formal education in the appropriate grade of their current age. So we have to provide alternative education for children to compensate them for the information they missed and help them to return to formal education and continue their studies. Mine Action: Based on current population estimations, 10.2 million men, women, boys, and girls live in the 1,980 communities reporting explosive contamination in the last two years. The impact of explosive hazards on communities is immediate and devastating, with the loss of community members, an increased number of people with permanent injuries and loss of access to properties, basic services, and livelihoods.Provision of risk awareness on these hazards is crucial to provide civilians with basic safety messages to reduce the daily risks posed by contamination.As Afaq has been providing risk education in Idleb city, rural Idleb and western Aleppo areas for three years, we found that there are a big need and demand from the population in the areas of implementation to continue providing the awareness-raising sessions on the dangers of ERW (Explosive Remnants of War). Explosive hazard accidents can result in serious injury and death, leading to long-term physical and psychological trauma for survivors and their families. Based on available data from where the collection has been possible, on average 1.5 people are killed and 2 are injured in explosive hazards accidents, with approximately 1 in 3 survivors suffering at least one limb amputation.

Protection Risk:- Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members especially against girls in the case management activity. Mitigating measures:- Provide regular training to staff and service providers for code of conduct and PSEA. Likelihood: High Impact: High Resources required:1. Human resources to analyze data on community tensions related to services.2. Access to sector messaging guidance.3. Access to information on services available by the community.4. Mitigating measures:-Self-care strategies as part of programmes and organizational HR policies. Likelihood: High Impact: High Resources required:1. Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The risks that are beyond the project’s control might be the physical security risk due to conflict activity, in addition to barriers for certain groups in access centers (limitations on movement). The risks might also be that services provided in centers not adequate to address the scale of protection issues and sustainability of centers given the current funding environment. The project would mitigate these risks by collaborating with key members on accessibility mainstreaming of centers and assessing the impact of community centers through baseline and end-line surveys, in addition to advocating with donors for direct funding to Syrian organizations to ensure continuity of centers. The project will also ensure center staff is trained on PFA and first aid. Moreover, there is a risk that is beyond the project’s control, which is targeting the centers by airstrikes during providing the services.The project would mitigate these risks by community engagement from the onset of the project, from the assessment phase and throughout all project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders. The mitigating measures might also be safety measures for low visibility and underground centers so that the centers are safe from the airstrikes. Risk of inter or intra-community tension due to service delivery locations or types of services offered if one community is served and a neighboring community not served. The project will mitigate this risk by ensuring a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed, in addition to ensuring consultation with communities and neighboring communities related to the location of services to understand perspectives of communities are all understood and addressed. Ensuring safety audits for access are conducted for each fixed site or mobile service location.
