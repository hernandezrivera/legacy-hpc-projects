Project Summary

Since October 2016, the political conflict and security crisis in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon has led to the displacement of more than 705,000 people internally and over 61,000 people to Nigeria. Conflict related displacement and violence have led to the deterioration of basic services in the North West Region coupled with rising protection, health and nutrition needs among the affected population. In response to this crisis, International Medical Corps proposes to support the Ministry of Health (MoH) in providing integrated health (including mental health) and nutrition services for IDPs as well as host communities affected by conflict in the Mbengwi and Njikwa Health districts.
In response to the ongoing crisis in Cameroon since 2016, International Medical Corps through Bureau of Humanitarian Assistance (BHA) funding, has been supporting the Ministry of Health (MoH) to provide integrated health including mental health and nutrition services for IDPs as well as host communities affected by conflict in the Mbengwi and Njikwa health districts. This has permitted the provision of health and nutrition services to over 30,000 IDPs and host population members, relieving suffering and preventing maternal and neonatal deaths.
IMC’s intervention has supported the collapsed health system to meet the needs of IDPs and host populations in Mbengwi and Njikwa since 2021. IMC proposes to continue providing health and nutrition services where there is a growing need for assistance and increasing concerns surrounding the health and nutritional status of both IDPs and host communities. Building on its existing program, IMC proposes to assist an additional 38,328 beneficiaries, expanding into two additional districts of Fudong and Akoh in the health areas of Konene and Akwaja. These areas host a large number of the IDPs in the region, with 8,437 and 5,000 respectively. IMC will also continue to support the four health facilities covered under the current award including Oshie Medicalized health center, the Konda integrated health center (IHC), the Njah-Etu IHC and the Bome IHC.

1 - Besoins / Needs

For over a decade, Cameroon has faced a series of complex humanitarian crises specifically: the Central African Republic (CAR) crisis in East and Adamawa regions where 300,0002 refugees were forced to flee into Cameroon; the Boko Haram crisis in Nigeria which has spilled into the Far North of Cameroon; and the ongoing political crisis in the Northwest and Southwest (NWSW) regions. The crisis in the NW/SW region has been classified consistently on the list of the world’s most neglected crises since 2018.3 With violence perpetrated by non-state armed groups (NSAGs) and the crackdown by the Cameroonian military forces, there has been a direct impact on the local population which has led to displacement in large numbers. As per IOM’s Multi-sectoral Needs Assessment that took place between 7 August and 30 August 2021, more than 55,607 IDPs (16% of the total number of registered IDPs) were displaced between January and July 2021. IMC’s current intervention in the NW region was based on a needs assessment carried out in 2020, leading to the selection of Mbengwi and Njikwa health districts. For this intervention, IMC has carried out a multisectoral needs assessment targeting areas that were desired for expansion: Akwaja in Ako District; Konene in Fundong District and Bamunka and Bambalang in the Ndop District - in the Northwest region. The assessment also included a review into IMC’s past performance and the needs of the community were reassessed at IMC’s currently supported health districts (Mbengwi and Njikwa). Quantitative and qualitative data were collected through a desk review of secondary sources, a community survey, focus group discussions with community members, and key informant interviews with health district authorities and community leaders. Additionally, an assessment of 03 health facilities in two health districts were carried out using a health facility checklist. The major needs identified by sector are as follows: health care services are major need for 49% of the community within the two health districts of Ako and Fudong. It was found that only 32% of the surveyed population were able to seek health facility services. Reasons for this included, the high cost of care (48%), insecurity (39%), unavailability of drugs (31%) and geographical access/ transportation costs (29%). None of the assessed health facilities provided any child health activities at community level, which was due to insecurity and access difficulties in addition to the low number of community health workers (CHWs). On average four CHWs were available per facility (compared to local standards of 1/500 persons). For example, Konene IHC operates through 4 CHWs and are serving a community of 11,946, while the need is 25 CHWs to be within the local standards. The Akwaja health facility has no working CHWs at all. The absence of an ambulance system and the poor quality of roads continue to make referrals to higher level facilities challenging. Providing free transport using motorbikes has proven to be successful under IMC’s current program and IMC will continue to provide this service through 2022 to 2023 specifically for urgent referrals, and this will therefore also be extended to newly supported health facilities.
