Project Summary

On August 4, 2020 Beirut the country capital and house to over 900,000 people was hit by a tragedy as a result of a massive explosion that rocked the Port of Beirut, killing 180 people , injuring over 6,500 and rendering 300,000 people homeless while destroying the port and causing wide damage in residential and commercial areas.
The housing sector is one of the most impacted and face many challenges including lack of construction materials and rising prices which hinders repair. According to UNOCHA, within a 3km radius of the explosion 291,000 individuals have damaged houses of which 29,770 have light damages and 17,100 with moderate damages. Many people do not want to leave their homes out of security concern. Three days after the explosion it rained in Beirut and in couple of weeks the change of season it may start to rain again on houses lacking basic protection including windows and doors.
The UN Special Coordinator Mr. Kubis stressed on the need for international community to provide assistance for rehabilitation of the damaged homes and basic water and electricity infrastructure in the neighborhoods destroyed by the blast. Hence the urgent need before winter to ensure shelter for the homeless especially by restoring and rehabilitating effected houses.
Since the blast, RMF has formed an Emergency Response Unit to rehabilitate Beirut comprising of RMF partners Dar Al Handasa (a leading international consulting organization in engineering), MAN Enterprise (one of the largest engineering and rehabilitation companies in Lebanon) both of which will be contributing their assessment and recommendation services for rehabilitation free of charge. RMF is has already started rehabilitation of 10 houses and assessed over 100 houses.
Under the scope of this project RMF with the collaboration of Dar El Handasa, MAN aim to conduct essential repair and rehabilitation of 250 affected homes with glass failure and 100 homes with moderate damages with 15% RMF contribution of overall budget as part of the overall humanitarian response & early recovery through a comprehensive approach including: Selection of most vulnerable localities based on UNOCHA and MoSA vulnerability criteria; Conducting structural assessment to ensure safety; Conduct full Damage Assessment; Defining basic repair interventions; Procurement of contractors through open tenders and best procurement policies and procedures; Support cash for work of local population; Conduct rehabilitation and repair focusing on rehabilitation of glass failure and basic infrastructure so they can be livable dwellings again; Ensuring safety for Covid-19 in works; Collaboration with concerned decision makers and stakeholders; Monitor works and ensure quality control in rehabilitation and delivery
RMF has over 25 years of experience in managing highly complex project and its development and humanitarian footprints are a result of a sound organizational, operational and financial management.
• At the organizational level, RMF’s structure, management and policies and procedures in HR, finance, procurement, code of conduct, documentation and filing, security, IT to ensure efficiency, transparency, accountability.
• At the operational level, RMF has 220 staff members with experience in various field, facilities, tools, equipment and materials that can be mobilized towards achievement of objectives. RMF has a team of 20 engineers that design, implement and oversee infrastructural development projects following the highest local and international standards.
• At the financial level, RMF advanced financial department and RMF accounts are audited by KPMG and its financial and narrative reporting meet local and international donor requirements. RMF is currently managing 12 projects with a total value 40 million USD RMF’s main contributors include: UNHCR, UNDP, EU, Kingdom of the Netherlands, OCHA, USAID, UNICEF.

1 - Needs

Since October 2019 Lebanon has been witnessing an unprecedent economic, financial, political, social crisis that is having detrimental effects on the country. The Covid-19 pandemic and lockdown has deepened the socio-economic situation and disparity challenges faced by vulnerable communities. Before that Lebanon had been suffering from the impacts of the Syrian crisis with over 1 million refugees across the country putting pressure on scare and inadequate resources and causing tension. The impacts of these crisis are reflected in:- Disruption of hundreds of thousands of living-incomes: 400,000 job losses in 1 year- Increased poverty: over 50% of population rendered poor.- Currency devaluation currently at 100% with reduced purchasing powerOn August 4, 2020 and according to UN OCHA, in 15 seconds over 15 years of development gains have been lost due the explosion of Beirut. Over 180 people were killed, 6000 injured and 300,000 people have been rendered homeless. Homes have been left without windows, doors, water or electricity as a result of the blast, and some have suffered substantial structural damage. According to an assessment conducted by ACTED one of the most pressing needs is reflected in the need to secure shelter/accommodation. According to the Syndicate of Engineers an estimate number of houses that have been affected is 200,000 with around: 40 building destroyed, 900 houses with major structural walls collapse, 3,000 houses with serious damages, 18,000 houses with limited damages and 20,000 houses with typical pressure for glass failure. One of the main challenges to rehabilitation and reconstruction of affected houses is reflected in the affordability and availability of construction materials especially with the country context explained above. Many of the owners or tenets living in the affected houses live in vulnerable and poor neighborhoods and are characterized as vulnerable segments with little or no means to rehabilitate their homes. Even in partially damaged houses or those with glass failure people did not leave the homes for fear of security and no alternatives being provided for long term shelter. The situation will become worse with the winter season as home will become inhabitable resulting in increased worsening situation of the affected population. Many of the affected houses are located in vulnerable localities and zones that so not have the means to conduct rehabilitation. As such there is an urgent need to rehabilitate as much as possible the damaged houses especially that have medium scale damage or having glass failure where immediate interventions can alleviate the tragedy and support returning back to livelihoods and shelter. The objective is to render the houses and businesses habitable before the beginning of winter. RMF will choose houses located in zones identified by their socio-economic vulnerability according to the map developed by MapAction through data sources from Open street maps, Lebanese Red Cross, OCHA, UN Habitat. The map divides the affected area into zones to help coordinate and collaborate relief efforts where the zones also indicate level of poverty and vulnerability. Already RMF engineering team have conducted preliminary assessment in the neighborhoods of Gemmeyzeh, Mar Mikhael, Karantina, Nabaa and others. Houses interventions on average and based on preliminary costing and assessment costs on average 4,500USD for glass failure and 8,000 for moderate repair that can include: rubble removal, windows and doors repair, paint, electrical, sanitary, other basic interventions

2 - Activities

Output 1: Assessment and rehabilitation of 350 damaged houses (250 glass failure repair and 100 medium repair) in the most vulnerable localities affected by the explosionActivity 1.1 Identification of most vulnerable localities within vulnerability map Activity 1.2: Selection of most affected localities based on specific criteria related to vulnerability, poverty of affected population, number of households per house to be an average of 5, houses with limited damage or glass failure, urgency of intervention before winter impacts people to stay home, impact of intervention Activity 1.3:Conduct safety of the house and rehabilitation assessment with the development of reporting for each house. Houses that need demolition or that have serious or major house structure failure will not be regarded as part of the project. As such houses with limited damages and glass failure will be assess and development of bill of quantity for intervention conducted: MAN and Dar El Handasa free of chargeActivity 1.4: Conduct open public tender for the rehabilitation of the selected affected houses where the average intervention per house for glass failure will be 1,500USD, for moderate affected homes 4,500USD. RMF procurement policies are based years of experience in similar projects with USAID through BALADI through construction and development project, WFP construction of irrigation canals and UNHCR. As such RMF has a network and list of contractors who have excellent deliverables, with profiles and data to suppliers who are willing and able to provide the needed materials. These contractors will be invited to submit through the open tender. The selection of contractor through a joint steering committee between RMF, Dar El Handasa and MAN where at least top 3 finalists who have cost effectiveness and quality assurance will be evaluated and transparency conducted for the selection. Activity 1.5: Ensure participation of at least 50% Lebanese’s and affected population through cash for work mechanism while ensuring Covid-19 safety measures in line with the Ministry of Public Health and Ministry of Labour requirements.Activity 1.6 Monitor implementation of rehabilitation works as part of quality control through MAN engineering and consultancy firm as well as ensuring overall project implementation by RMF project management team respecting the highest level of quality assurance,
