Project Summary

CARE in partnership with Palestinian Medical Relief Society (PMRS)/Local Health NGO will respond to this situation with a comprehensive intervention that combines elements of direct health service delivery to the identified vulnerable communities in Area C and H2, with a special focus on women, children, elderly, adolescents and people with disabilities (PWD), those in need of reporting and referral of S/GBV, PSEA cases, and the injured as a result of the Israeli settler and military force violence, and it also responds to the ongoing COVID-19 crisis through increased community awareness and full coordination with Ministry of Health offices (MoH) in order to support their vaccination process as well as the logistics processes, if and when needed. CARE will continue working with trained community representatives (focal points) and project staff to report/refer these violations using the agreed upon mechanism.
The proposed intervention will be targeting 42 vulnerable communities in Area C, H2 and North Jordan valley in the West Bank that were identified by CARE/Partners/ MoH/ and the HNC during the recently conducted HRP 2022 workshops and via mobile clinics working group. All identified locations have serious access issues and lack any type of health services. The identified locations will be targeted through 4 mobile health clinics (10 health teams) that will be visiting these communities regularly to provide the needed health services as per the agreed upon program/plan with the Health and Nutrition Cluster (HNC) and MoH. The 42 locations are distributed as follows: 10 locations in Bethlehem Area, 12 locations in Hebron Area, 9 locations in North Jordan valley, and 11 Bedouin communities in Jerusalem and middle Jordan valley area.
CARE/partners, in close coordination with MoH centrally and at the governorates’ level, will provide life-saving services in targeted communities in the WB through the use of mobile clinics/teams that offer basic curative and preventive Primary Health Care services (PHC), including women’s healthcare/ SRH (ante and post-natal care, obstetric care, adolescent health, elderly and post-menopausal syndromes. Midwives will be added to health teams to enhance health awareness activities and quality of services and referral mechanisms, etc.), general practice care (GP), health awareness with focus on communities’ needs and priorities, including COVID-19 control, prevention and referrals, basic lab tests, home visits, children health and psychosocial support. The team will also be provided with emergency kits that will enable them to respond to emergency incidents in these communities while working in the clinics to ensure best case management and lifesaving before transferring to the hospitals. Additionally, the teams will train selected community focal points (mainly females) on First Aid and provide them with First Aid kits to better respond to emergencies in their vulnerable and isolated communities. The package of services was designed jointly with UNFPA, HNC, the communities and MOH to synergize and unify the services all while building on the long operational and programmatic experience in working with the targeted locations.
The project is designed fully in coordination with other actors, mainly MoH, with which CARE/partners conducted a number of coordination meetings during September, 2021 at MoH offices around the West Bank, and along with other INGOs (UNRWA, UNFPA, CISP, MAP) community leaders, focal points, beneficiaries, and community representatives. CARE/partners ensured that the existing data are updated, as well as indicators, that the stakeholders’ feedback is reflected in the proposal, with activities that meet and fulfill the community needs, and that any possible duplication of services and overlap in locations are avoided.

1 - Needs

Please describe the needs your projects aim to addressThe design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of target communities, based on a number of key sources, including the data and mapping exercise led by the HNC needs analysis of the most vulnerable locations that are in need of health services. CARE and partners were among the group who worked with the HNC to develop and update the matrix of the locations all over West Bank. CARE has conducted an internal needs analysis that included several meetings and discussions with targeted community members, as well as MoH oﬃces in the targeted governorates. The assessments took into considerations all new needs/implications of the COVID-19 crisis on the services provided in the identiﬁed locations/communities and/or the capacity of the health providers (mainly MoH) to provide PHC in the vulnerable communities. According to the HNO-2022 “It is anticipated that the COVID-19 caseload will continue to burden the healthcare system and based on 2021 trends, it is estimated that the incident rate could be on average be around 78 per 1,000, but could be as high as 108 per 1,000. (1) CARE has also looked into different relevant documents/studies such as the latest Multi-Sector Needs Assessment, which showed that 23% of the HHs reported facing access barriers to health services. CARE also looked at the oPt response plan for COVID-19, the COVID-19 Global HRP, COVID-19 related social stigma, IASC MHPSS Guide for COVID-19 responders, HPSS IASC guidelines for COVID -19, and the UN Women’s study “The UN Women Gender Alert (4) (2021),” which further highlighted that the women’s mental health is disproportionately more affected by the situation than the men’s.According to all assessments, access to primary health care in the WB is problematic largely due to various restrictions on movement and access imposed by the Israel authorities, including the separation barrier, settler’s violence, checkpoints, permits regime and the isolation of East Jerusalem, in addition to the overburdened health system that has been further weakened by the ongoing COVID-19 crisis, and the lack of adequate ﬁnancial resources, shortages in basic supplies and the structural limitations as a result of the political situation. The Mobile Clinics Working Group (6), reiterates “about 150,000 Palestinians in marginalized locations in Area C, East Jerusalem, Hebron/H2 and the closed area behind the Barrier, continue to suffer from limited, or no, access to fixed healthcare services and are therefore in need of mobile clinic service provision. Israel’s restrictive planning system and its associated demolition orders remain a barrier to accessing health in these communities”. These communities face severe restrictions in accessing primary healthcare, particularly women, children, adolescents, elderly and PWDs. Without the humanitarian intervention of the mobile health clinics, there are no alternative accessible PHC services. In September 2021, CARE reviewed and updated its mapping exercise of health services through Focus Group Discussions and meetings with stakeholders, including MoH, health care providers, local community representatives, village councils, focal points, beneficiaries, and the mobile teams, taking into consideration the gender needs and varied age groups in the identiﬁed communities of targeted districts in the WB. The mapping revealed that approximately 42 communities are lacking any type of health services, the finding of which was also conﬁrmed in the HRP 2022 process. Within these communities, poor health outcomes are a result of compound effect of lack of quality care, shortages in medical supplies and lack of health education and awareness. As a result, we see the spread of infectious diseases and complications in chronic diseases, such as diabetes as well as complications related to pregnancies and post deliveries. From previous work in these communities, data shows that approximately 50% of the women’s health needs were related to ante and post-natal care, however, the remaining 50% were distributed as follows: 25% acute illnesses, 15% gynecological diseases, 10% puberty and post-menopausal related problems. Diﬃcult access to PHC combined with the deeply entrenched stigma and norms of the traditional and conservative culture in many of the communities tend to affect women in particular. Inadequate treatment of RH problems, for instance, is common, often leading to complications.The incidents of GBV, SGBV, PSEA, settler violence, demolitions attacks, forced Immigration and imminent threat of eviction, violations of IHL, IRHL and the resulting lack of security, in addition to the difficult access to the health services, are major causes for our interventions that have prompted great needs upon which the proposed project design is based.

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

CARE’s ‘Do No Harm’ principles are embedded in all approaches and interventions by CARE, from the ideation process of a project, throughout its duration and after the project is finished. CARE has identified risks, their potential impact and mitigation strategies, and all activities proposed are designed based on numerous discussions with community members, partners and other stakeholders, as well as on needs analyses (i.e. rapid / gender needs assessment) that are sensitive to the complexities of the local context to ensure that people are not exposed to additional risks through our action. CARE is committed to protection and ensuring safety and security of participants, dignity and accessibility of services; CARE’s activities are inclusive, and designed with the community members’ feedback, and the selection criteria for activities are transparent; the data collection processes are sensitive to the context, power dynamics in communities, and also the management of sensitive information is conducted with caution and care.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

PWD are considered as one of the main targeted vulnerable groups in the proposed project, as they were identiﬁed by HNC as one of the vulnerable groups who needs special consideration, for that the project team will map out, with the communities’ representatives and actors all cases of PWD in the targeted communities and will provide the necessary medical services to them. Referral will be also an option for those who need to be referred to advanced or specialized services, which will be coordinated with other health providers as needed. Additionally, the social worker, who will be part of the medical team, will work with them on the psychological aspect. PWD will have special attention when they come to the clinics, and those who are not able to access the clinic will be visited at home. Elderly and the bed ridden patients also have special needs as a target group. For the elderly, the project will provide the medical treatment and the social worker will work with them on special needs, follow up with them, so they could prevent serious medical problems. Home visits will also be planned for those who can’t access the clinic.
