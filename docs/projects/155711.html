Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to improve access to life-saving SRH and GBV interventions for male and female in four tribal districts of KP – Orakzai, Khyber and Kurram.

Needs

The Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) is one of the most-underdeveloped region of Pakistan. Its population has been caught up in a protracted crisis since 2008 that has seen millions of individuals temporarily displaced. Its unique governance arrangements, poverty and violent militancy have compounded the regions problems. In 2015 security was stable enough to start large scale return. Returns continue to FATA, with 438,657 families having been facilitated by the Government and humanitarian partners to return since 2010 to date. Although the Government initially planned for all people to return by the end of 2016, conditions within FATA, including the difficulties of returns during winter, meant that 32,469 families remained displaced and are yet to return. There is also evidence of a substantial number of displaced people who are not registered for official assistance. The FATA Sustainable Return and Rehabilitation Strategy, initiated in 2015, provided a plan for the return of displaced families to their areas of origin following the completion of security operations in 5 agencies. Return has been accompanied by short-term assistance for relocation, resettlement, the revitalization of livelihoods and the economy, reconstruction of housing and infrastructure. Similarly, there was an attempt to coordinate the humanitarian development nexus through the “Operational Coordination Mechanism (OCM)” and the Thematic Working Group at Peshawar which evolved into the FATA Transition Task Force (FTTF) co-chaired by the Govt. On 2 March 2017, the Federal Cabinet adopted a historic package of FATA Reforms. The most significant decision has been on the mainstreaming and integration of FATA with KP within a transition period of 5 years. The 24-recommendations package represents a new political compact for FATA and the border region, and an unprecedented extension of constitutional rights and governance structures to millions of the poorest people in Pakistan. The international community is now developing a transition plan to link the ongoing humanitarian action and the growing development investment more closely together and to align both with the emerging government strategies for the long-term stabilization and development of the region.According to the vulnerability assessment, the population in Tribal Districts are mostly scattered with the average distance to the nearest health facility being 11 Kilometers, thus reducing people’s inclination (explicitly among the females) to seek health care, and negatively impacting on the health status of the population. Preliminary findings from FATA Vulnerability Assessment exercise conducted in August/September 2017 revealed that 64% of the population in FATA Agency and FR Areas faced difficulties in accessing health care facilities. The mean distance traveled by the population to the nearest health facility in FATA Agency is 7 km and in Frontier Region (FR) Areas is 19.5 km. Out of the women who had a child in the last three years, 24% reported that they didn’t seek any medical help or services during their pregnancy. A significant proportion (40%) reported delivery at home, mostly with the assistance of traditional midwife (Dai). Around 20% of the households from FATA and 15% from FR areas reported that their eligible children were not vaccinated during routine EPI. Key reasons include the unavailability of relevant staff, service and financial constraint. Over 80% of health infrastructures in return areas are severely damaged by the crisis and remain non-functional. Many of the health staff who were displaced especially the female health workers have not resumed work. This has negatively influenced the provision of Reproductive Health, Mother & Child Health care services in FATA and FR Areas. In addition, only 11% and 22% respectively of responders in FATA and FR Areas have ever been visited by Lady Health Workers during the last three months.

Activities

Conduct GBV service mapping, assessments and coordination meetings• Establish and support the delivery of SRH and GBV information and psychosocial support services in Women Health Friendly Spaces• Strengthen the GBV coordination mechanism led by government in Peshawar • Train CSO partners on GBV Coordination, Minimum Standards and Survivor Centered Care• Support the implementation of GBV Referral Mechanisms and orient case officers on GBV Minimum Standards and Standard Operating Procedures• Conduct learning sessions and experience sharing among GBV case workers in implementing the GBV referral pathway• Organize case management conference (bi monthly) to address gaps in case management and develop actions to resolve recurring operational issues• Train health providers in the Clinical Management of Rape Survivors and Health Sector Response • Train service providers on Essential Service Package
