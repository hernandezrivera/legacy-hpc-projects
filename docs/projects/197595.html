Project Summary

According to the HRP, Bahar will ensure access to quality, lifesaving, and life-sustaining health services at all levels of the health system - community, primary, secondary, and tertiary - through its supported health facilities (1 general hospital, 1 BEmONC facility, 3 specialized treatment centers (1 for TB, 1 hemodialysis center, and 1 stabilization center for SAM management), 3 PHCs, and 1 MMU. Surgical and trauma services, reproductive health, and child health - including immunization and nutrition services, mental health, diagnostic services, and specialized care for people with disabilities, those with communicable and noncommunicable diseases, and children with severe acute malnutrition with complications - are all considered essential services provided throughout Bahar's facilities. Bahar aims to ensure the provision of primary health care services per the WHO Essential Primary Health Care Package, which includes six pillars: child health, reproductive health, communicable disease, NCD, MHPSS, and nutrition, as well as secondary health care services, including specialized reproductive health care services BEmONC and CEmONC services following international guidelines and the UNFPA. Furthermore, neonatal, vaccination, and reproductive health care, including care during pregnancy and childbirth and the performance of normal and C-section deliveries, will be provided, particularly in maternity and SRH facilities. Bahar will provide staff salaries, operational costs, and medical commodities such as medicines, consumables, and equipment to the health facilities, as well as recruitment support, capacity building, and ongoing medical supervision to ensure the target HFs are staffed with qualified medical personnel and comply with standard case management protocols. Furthermore, Bahar will continue to train HFs on a variety of health topics by conducting training or providing training opportunities through inter-agency collaborative efforts. Management of communicable and non-communicable diseases, pharmaceutical supply management, patient data management, SRH treatment protocols, and gender sensitivity and protection are among the services provided. Bahar will collect and circulate health data from the HFs on a monthly basis. Bahar health facilities have always been a core component of the referral pathways supported and organized by WHO, the health cluster, and partners to ensure the quality of healthcare services and refer needy cases to higher care. A network of CHWs will conduct outreach activities in the communities surrounding the health facilities and will be linked to the health facilities in order to improve community health and synchronize trends and knowledge requirements with the messages distributed to the communities. Due to the COVID-19 and Cholera outbreaks in NW Syria, Bahar will continue to actively participate in the AWD/Cholera Task Force, which was formed to follow the Cholera outbreak in Syria and will ensure that the protocols and guidelines in both COVID and Cholera case management are followed under WHO's pillars. Bahar health facilities will be provided with the necessary PPE and disinfectants to keep frontline staff safe and operational. Beneficiaries will receive risk communication and community engagement messages while seeking healthcare services. Furthermore, surveillance measures will be strengthened in order to detect Cholera and/or COVID-19 cases early and refer them to appropriate health facilities. In addition, IPC measures and tools will be secured and implemented to ensure adherence to WHO standards and guidelines and to keep the health facility from becoming a potential source of infection.

3 - Needs

According to HNO, 35% of the available hospitals were either non-functional or only partially functional. In terms of public health centers (PHC), 44% were either non-functional or only partially functional. In the absence of consistent and dependable electricity, health facilities are forced to rely on generators and solar energy systems to meet the bare minimum of power requirements, raising concerns about the continuity of health services such as surgery, cold chain storage, sterilization, safe blood storage, and timely and accurate diagnosis. During power outages, essential items such as lights, anesthesia machines, autoclaves, and radiology equipment become inoperable. In some areas, limited access to potable water continues to have an impact on people's health as well as the quality of services provided in health facilities. The majority of operational health facilities do not meet WHO health worker benchmarks (benchmark >22 HWs per 10,000 population). In addition, the number of hospital beds per 10,000 population (inpatients and maternity) (benchmark >=10 beds for 10,000 population) is not met in any of the communities. According to HNO, the health situation worsened as a result of the COVID-19 outbreak, which strained the capacity of health facilities even further. While non-COVID outbreaks continue to erupt, with continued leishmaniasis cases in northern Syria and increases in proportionate morbidity for acute watery diarrhea (suspected cholera).

4 - Implementation of activities

Bahar will ensure the provision of integrated primary, secondary, and tertiary health services, as well as nutrition and protection services. This will be implemented at all levels of the supported health facilities: 1 general hospital, 1 BEmONC facility, 3 specialized treatment centers (1 for tuberculosis, 1 hemodialysis center, and 1 stabilization center for SAM management), 3 PHCs, and 1 MMU. All supported health facilities will provide preventive and curative services at no cost. Bahar will ensure that primary health care services are provided in PHCs in accordance with the WHO's essential Primary Health Care Package, which consists of six pillars: child health, reproductive health, communicable disease, NCD, MHPSS, and nutrition. Secondary health care services, including specialized reproductive health care services BEmONC and CEmONC, will be provided through the ER, OPD, IPD, department, radiology, and laboratory. Furthermore, neonatal, vaccination, and reproductive health care, including prenatal and postpartum care, as well as vaginal and C-section deliveries, will be provided, particularly in maternity and RH facilities. Further to that, the specialized treatment centers will provide TB services as well as hemodialysis services for patients with end-stage renal disease (kidney failure) and children with SAM. 7 - Protection Risk Analysis

If a service is provided to specific groups or individuals while excluding others, there is a risk of discrimination. As a result, Bahar will follow the risk analysis and mitigation measures conducted and developed by the cluster,sub-cluster, and technical working groups; ensuring comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed; providing consultation with communities related to the location to understand relevant community perspectives, and taking care to ensure that the specific needs of the most vulnerable people are met. Overcrowding and a lack of privacy can exacerbate the risks of GBV. The absence of doors and partitions for sleeping and changing clothes in multi-family tents can increase exposure to sexual harassment and assault. Early marriage is also on the rise. Bahar will engage the community throughout the project cycle. It includes meetings with religious and community leaders and adaptation of GBV messaging to ensure context and cultural sensitivity. In addition, Bahar will advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided, sensitization of women, girls, and boys will be conducted, and Bahar will hire localized and gender-balanced staff, who will be trained on GBV core concepts and have a referral mechanism to refer any GBV survivor upon disclosure.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

In accordance with PSEA standard policy and procedures, Bahar is committed to protecting beneficiaries from sexual exploitation and abuse. New employees will be required to sign the PSEA network's CoC and will be fully informed about PSEA and the available complaint mechanism. The full illustration of available compliant mechanisms will be used to raise community awareness about PSEA. PSEA focal points are responsible for mainstreaming PSEA into all departments and projects by developing measures and practices, including feedback and compliance mechanisms, in collaboration with concerned departments. Posters and banners promoting PSEA will be displayed in all facilities. The network's videos will be constantly displayed on Bahar's social media accounts (Facebook, Youtube). New PSEA awareness-raising methods, such as sending text messages and videos to beneficiaries, online training, and Webinars, may be implemented. The project ensures community and beneficiary participation in project design and planning from the start, through need assessments and so on during the subsequent phases, and by targeting adolescent girls with socially acceptable activities including life skills. There will be multiple channels for reporting feedback and complaints privately and confidentially, including a hotline number, WhatsApp...etc.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: The Turkish-Syrian border remains open for humanitarian aid flow and personnel movements. There has been no change in the in-power forces in the region, and the project locations remain free of armed conflict. The security situation is stable, and there will be no movement of IDPs. Risks and Mitigation Measures: Overlap in services provided: Bahar will ensure coordination of any new services added or removed with the health cluster and partners to avoid any duplication in services provided. Furthermore, the Bahar technical team will actively participate in health cluster meetings and share updates as needed. Bahar will ensure that any changes to services are guided by a need assessment conducted by the Bahar MEAL team or other health cluster partners.Lack of health service quality: Bahar has a health technical team that will monitor the quality of health services provided in health facilities and connect the staff to the most recent international guidelines and protocols. Furthermore, a training plan for staff will be developed in collaboration with health facility teams and staff needs. A complaint mechanism is already in place in the health facilities, and patients can raise any issue regarding the quality of health services, which will be registered and forwarded to the appropriate department.Failing to renew the Cross-border Resolution:Mitigation: Increasing advocacy about the importance of cross-border operations and the drastic impact on non renewal
