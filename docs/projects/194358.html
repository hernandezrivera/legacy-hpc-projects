Project Summary

The proposed project intends to provide food assistance and livelihood support to 38,664 foods in secured population people in Greater Kapoeta (East, South, North and Budi) of Eastern Equatoria Counties. The project intervention activities will contribute to famine prevention and improve food consumption, dietary diversity and coping strategies of target beneficiaries in IPC 4 and 3. The project will also seek to enhance emergency food production and contribute to the reduction of dependency on food and agricultural inputs. Organization for Peoples’ empowerment and Needs (OPEN) will adopt a mix of intervention approaches including but not limited to unconditional, cash transfers, complementary livelihood support, capacity building tailored on agronomic practices, and resilience building at the household level. The proposed project will also be implemented in synergy with other sectors implemented by OPEN in the target locations such as health, nutrition, WASH, gender and protection related programming

1 - Needs

Eastern Equatoria State population in greater Kapoeta Counties are most pastoralist communities and the trends of movement is seasonality drives with reported cattle rustling, Food insecurity and the food security situation projection FSNMS results at state level show that the age of the head of household has a strong link to food security with projection is estimated 578,000 people (51.3% of the population) by April- July 2023 for Counties of Kapoeta East, Kapoeta North, Kapoeta South, Budi and Ikotos likely to be in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity classification. Food insecurity will mainly be driven by low or no food stocks, high food prices, poor market functionality because of the degradation of roads during the rainy season, insecurity that will likely result in displacement and affect agricultural activity, and the seasonal increase in the prevalence of livestock pests and diseases that will affect livestockproduction and productivity according to South_Sudan_IPC_Key_Messages_-_October_2022_to_July_2023.pdf As part of the 2023 HRP, Organization for Peoples’ empowerment and Needs (OPEN) aims to reach the most vulnerable affected IDPs and host populations in Eastern Equatoria State with food assistance, emergency food production through support of agricultural, support and strengthen households’ ability to absorb shocks. This support will improve households’ food insecurity incidents, macro-economic crisis, and the effects of COVID-19 and also EVD outbreak threats. The food security situation in the targeted counties is very critical (IPC 3 & IPC4) in terms of food consumption, dietary diversification, reduced coping strategies, level of hunger and livelihood coping strategies. The IDPs, returnees, and host communities are particularly vulnerable and many of them switched to negative coping strategies including both selling their assets and decreasing the number of meals per day per person. Organization for Peoples’ Empowerment and Needs in partnership with Diakonie has been implementing Conditional Food and Cash distributions in the target locations but huge food gaps still exist and the communities need immediate support. Cash Based Intervention will be done in proper manner and more so, community cohesion and mobilization, empowerment of women and youth development is a priority in addressing tensions between IDPs and host communities that influence IDPs-host community relations, limiting the local protection space and opportunities for interdependence. Such tension results from use of land, natural resources, firewood collection, access to medical, educational and infrastructure facilities, family disputes, overstretched absorption capacity of the conflict affected communities

3 - Project indicators

OPEN uses a results-based management to deliver project results. The project results will be closely monitored through a dedicated monitoring and evaluation system, consisting of tested tools such as Indicator Tracking Tables, Detailed Implementation Plan, and project tracker. This tracking is done on regular bases (monthly, quarterly, and annually) with results measured and quantified. The following key indicators, among others, will be closely monitored: - Number of beneficiaries receiving food, non-food items, cash transfers and vouchers - Number of beneficiaries receiving agricultural inputs - Number of people trained in improved agronomic practices - Number of livestock vaccinated and treated During reporting, the project outputs and outcomes will be measured based on the above indicators whose values will be tracked on a rolling basis.

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

OPEN recommends the use of cash because using cash revives the local economy, promotes beneficiary dignity, and empowers the household to make choices that best suit them. Before embarking on the actual modality selection, OPEN will conduct a quick market assessment for actual modality recommendation. Moreover, OPEN is not recommending cash for all counties but for counties where the market is functioning well enough to support the right quantities and access to commodity/service needs of the affected populations. Currently, OPEN uses the same modality (cash and voucher) in its operation areas using funds obtained from different donors.

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The Project will support both Cash or Vouchers assistance to increase the financial capacity of the most vulnerable to diversify house holds needs.

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

The specific risks identified above will be addressed as follows: Registration and distribution points will be thoroughly assessed and located at the safest points in the village.Distributions will be done at appropriate times in the day to allow closure before the dark: Women, girls and boys will be sensitized on protection concerns, prevention, and where to get psychological first aid in case of violations. When doing assessments, information sharing, and awareness sessions, the team will ensure that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and older persons, women, boys and girls, participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project by directly involving them in the discussions. The project will ensure that complaint and feedback mechanisms are accessible to all members of the community through increasing access points, diversifying feedback channels, etc. Overall, the project will be based on a protection risk analysis considering the specific needs of different population groups. The project will do protection risk analysis by identifying various possible risks and ascertain their level of severity, likelihood and impact. Barriers to access for different groups will be identified and addressed. The team is mindful the barriers to access could be social, distance, or security concerns. These barriers will be identified and addressed as appropriate.
