Project Summary

The proposed project- Education Support Programme aims to improve school enrollment rates and gender parity at secondary education as means to bring peace, normalcy and stability in Temporary Dislocated Peoples (TDP) return areas of Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) currently named as Newly Merged Districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP). The programme serves dual purpose of ensuring education for children while supporting the development of the human capital in the long term. Peace and stability will be at the center of the assistance as every additional year of schooling contributes to develop emotional intelligence among children through knowledge, understanding and management of emotions, enabling children to bring peace in themselves and in society. Likewise, parents with adequate means of predictable income (like cash stipends provided under the programme), will be less-likely to engage their children in bread-wining roles.
Under the proposed project, boys and girls of grade 1-5 and adolescent girls of grade 6-10 will be assisted with cash stipends conditional upon enrollment and retention at schools. The programme will focus on the families whose children were drop out of the school due to conflict, displacement or are out of school due to poverty and un-affordability in the areas of TDP return.
The community will be mobilized to enrol and retain their children in schools and will be informed of programme modalities and conditionality. To ensure uninterrupted educational activities at schools, cash stipends will be transferred to parents/ authorized guardians of the eligible students. The programme also exclusively targets girls from grades 6-10 to help to reduce the considerable gender gap between girls’ and boys’ education at the secondary level which is highly prevailing in the area.
The programme will be jointly implemented with the Directorate of Education FATA/KP and Benazir Income Support Programme (BISP)- two government entities, significant for the continuity and sustainability of the results produced by the programme. Besides cash stipends, awareness on nutrition, basic health and hygiene will also be provided to schoolchildren, mothers and communities to promote dietary diversity and healthy life style (improved basic health and hygiene).

Needs

WFP believes efforts for saving people's lives are more effective while an invest is also made in changing people's lives. This investment is even more significant and much needed during recovery phase after an emergency. Education is believed as one of the most effective strategies and long-term investment to bring positive change in peoples' lives and provision of education during emergencies is even more important while targeting the most vulnerable segment of population. In the context of FATA, education is an urgent priority and of great importance for all, particularly for returnee children, because, schools provide a safe space for children, where they are protected from physical harm, child labour, early marriages and being recruited as child soldiers. It also brings routine, stability, structure and hope to children lives, and improves psycho-social well being. Similarly, schools are also a key vehicle through which children can learn about hygiene, nutrition, preventable diseases, and other life-saving topics. Therefore, WFP designed a context-specific educational support programme to increase access to education in Erstwhile FATA.The overall objective of the Education Support Programme is to encourage return and resumption to education in Erstwhile FATA. The specific objectives are to: 1. Increase enrollment and retention in schools2. Reduce gender disparity in secondary education 3. Increase awareness on nutrition, personal health and hygiene and to promote dietary diversity. The Programme will target the most vulnerable population-poorest of poor & returnees by supporting families whose children were out of school due to conflict, displacement, poverty or un-affordability. The programme modalities include cash stipends for students (as safety nets), nutrition education (promoting dietary diversity) and community mobilization (as social behavior change communication) and capacity strengthening of education department (strengthening Government systems). The assistance package includes, the cash stipends for boys and girls of primary schools and adolescent school girls of poorest of poor families. The stipend amount for primary school children is PKR 750/quarter while it is PKR 1,500/quarter for adolescent girls, conditional upon enrollment and 70% attendance per month at schools. A total of 70,000 children (primary schoolboys: 40,000; primary schoolgirls: 25,000 & secondary school girls: 5,000) will receive cash stipends. However, all school children (regardless of whether they are receiving cash or not) present at schools, mothers and communities will also receive nutrition education and awareness on basic health and hygiene. The programme will be implemented in collaboration with Directorate of Education Erstwhile FATA, and BISP . A memorandum of understanding and annual work plan agreement will be singed among the partners to formalize the programme implementation mechanisms. This partnership would be effective in strengthening Government systems and mechanisms and thus would be helpful to sustain the programme results.WFP and its partners will constitute Education Support Programme’s Steering Committees at provincial and federal levels to ensure effective coordination, supervision and control of the programme activities and resources. The committees will provide strategic direction, supervision and control of the programme activities and will be comprised of the senior management and programme teams of all three agencies- DOE, BISP and WFP. The committees will be co-chaired by DOE Erstwhile FATA and BISP while WFP will serve as a secretariat of both committees. An effective coordination with other humanitarian partners operating in the area will be ensured. WFP will also develop programme SOPs, joint monitoring framework and programme implementation guidelines in collaboration with partners to ensure that programme is implemented as per approved design and achieving required scope

Activities

1. Community mobilization for enrollment of children in schools and community groups formation (as a tool to bring out of school children in schools and to ensure community participation and effective role in maintaining enrollment and retention in schools). 2. Cash stipends for children of returnee and poorest of poor families in areas of TDP return (Primary school boys and girls (grade 1-5) and secondary school girls (grade 6-10) of eligible families will receive cash stipends conditional upon enrollment and 70% attendance per month in schools. 3. Nutrition education/awareness at schools and community levels for improved diet diversity, basic health and hygiene 4. Teachers’ and Education Directorate Staff training on programme modalities, monitoring and reporting. 5. A complete schools’ capacity assessment will also be carried out to ensure that schools have enough space to accommodate new enrollment as well as basic infrastructure is available. (In the case of unavailability of required school infrastructure, Government and development partners will be taken on board for provision of such facilities). 6. Joint monitoring and evaluation of programme activities by all partners. A database of assisted beneficiaries under cash will also be developed and maintained.
