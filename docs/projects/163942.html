Project Summary

Medair’s proposed intervention will provide life-saving WASH interventions at community and household-level to increase water supply that currently barely reaches or in most cases are under the Sphere Standard and address a dire situation in community sanitation. The proposed response will reach approximately 148,000 beneficiaries through:
i. Water network rehabilitation:
Medair will rehabilitate up to 10 community level water systems. Medair’s activities will include the upgrade/installation of water pumping equipment, equipping wells, replacing generators, repairing distribution lines and installing new components for safe drinking water systems. Alongside these activities, Medair will promote water conservation, and also teach proper operation and maintenance (O&M) techniques to Water Economic Unit (WEU) water system operators responsible for systems rehabilitated by Medair.
ii. Water storage solution:
As part of FLER particularly for recently displaced HHs in hard to reach (HTR) and newly accessible areas, Medair will provide safe water storage solutions (WSS) to approximately 2100 households for domestic use. This may be done either through the installation of community water tanks (CWT) or through the provision of HH water storage tanks (HHWT). In case of particularly vulnerable households (female headed households, elderly), HHWTs are preferred to prevent protection issues such as lack of access or harassment at communal water points.
iii. Sewage network rehabilitation:
Medair will rehabilitate one sewage network, the aim of the intervention is to ensure that waste can be adequately transported away from the HH and public spaces where it may pose health risks through a functioning piped network free from leaks and blockages. Medair’s planned sewage network rehabilitation will focus on Deir-ez-Zor city to prevent further discharge of raw sewage into the open and to restore the previous sewage network and connectivity to the main sewage line.
Medair’s proposed intervention will target vulnerable households and communities in Government Controlled areas within Homs, Aleppo, Deir-ez-Zor City, Idleb, Quneitra or Quneitra enclave (geographically within Rural Damascus Governorate), where Medair already has presence and possibly in Hama and Al- Hasakeh, subject to approvals and identified needs. Ar-Raqqa is also included as a potential area of intervention (areas under GoS control), pending context development and access. Implementation will be done through Medair’s area bases in Damascus, Aleppo, and Deir-ez-Zor.

4 - Needs

Water and sewage infrastructure have been severely damaged especially in hard to reach or newly accessible locations in Medair’s target governorates due to sustained hostilities.Most of the communities assessed by Medair in the first half of 2019 didn’t meet Sphere standards for safe drinking water (15 l/p/d), let alone the minimum needs based on Medair previous intervention experience of 35 l/p/d. Population movement to locations with intact infrastructure and better humanitarian conditions has led to the overburdening of host communities with competition for safe water supply, making it necessary for the already vulnerable population to rely on high-cost and unreliable alternatives. Safe water storage solutions for HHs is an important basic need, without which particularly vulnerable HHs will continue to resort to high risk water storage options which increase the likelihood of contamination, predisposing them to an additional burden of morbidity. Medair has identified that local WEUs generally need additional technical training to increase members’ expertise in water system operation and maintenance to increase intervention impact and sustainability. Additionally, communities at large require improved awareness of water conservation, especially noting the gradual depletion of water resources in-country. MoWR and local authorities in DEZ city (where IDPs and returnees make up >60% of the population of >227,000 as of December 2018 and where 65% of the population are PIN), confirm rehabilitation of the damaged sewage infrastructure as one of their main priorities in addressing public health concerns.Damage to the sewage network has resulted in flooding of houses or streets with wastewater, a breeding ground for disease-transmitting vectors. Health surveillance sites in DEZ report a disproportionately high communicable disease burden. High rates of acute diarrhoea, dysentery and acute jaundice cases are likely to be linked to poor sanitation (EWARN, Nov 2018).

5 - Implementation of activities

Medair will rehabilitate 10 water systems to restore adequate water supply to communities. Works may include upgrade/installation of water pumping equipment,equipping wells, replacing generators, and renewing/repairing/replacing components for safe drinking water systems. After rehabilitation, projects are handed back to the WEU, after which O&M training is provided to WEU staff to ensure sustainability. Where possible, Community Awareness-Raising Workshops on water conservation and safety are organized for key local leaders. The leaders will be encouraged to spread the messages to their communities. The WEUs are responsible for water quality and testing on a regular basis. Medair will liaise with the WEU to ensure pre and post intervention water testing. As part of FLER to communities that are either newly accessible or with recent or sudden people movement, Medair will provide community or HH water tanks up to approximately 2100 HHs, based on strict vulnerability criteria. Medair provides HHWT directly to each HH, delivery of HHWTs is coordinated with a beneficiary and local community committee. Installations are carried out for select HHs that are particularly vulnerable to ensure that they are able to timely benefit from the assistance. Water safety in CWTs will be monitored through testing at key points in the water supply chain by Medair facilitators or in cooperation with SARC. Medair will rehabilitate one community level sewage network in DEZ, which is assumed to serve 12,000. The selection of the sewage project site will happen based on strict vulnerability criteria, taking into account the public health impact. After rehabilitation, the system, is handed back to the sewage company and local Municipality. M&E mechanisms (by Medair or potentially 3rd-party monitors) are in place to assess the program’s benefits and challenges and document lessons learned for future scale-up and to inform adjustments during implementation.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

ASSUMPTIONS: - There is no significant change to the political and security situation inside Syria - The security situation remains stable enough to allow project and support functions to be set up and to continue - Market systems remain relatively stable - There are no excessive fluctuations of the Syrian Pound, and any inflation/deflation will be able to be offset against hard currency (USD) - Acceptance and participation of authorities,local leaders and communities during interventions - Equipment in specifications listed by Ministries will be available at the time of procurement RISKS: - Medair cannot secure approvals from ministries within the timeline outlined in the work plan, delaying project delivery - Significant injury to team members within Syria due to security incidents-Central Bank imposes regulations or restrictions which stop or markedly constrain Medair’s ability to maintain adequate cash flow to pay programme expenses - Medair staff or contractors commit fraud or do not comply sufficiently with procurement guidelines. MITIGATING MEASURES: - Conduct clear, robust, regular context analysis as part of security management - Continue with keeping security planning up to date and training a crisis management team for each location - Daily, careful monitoring of security situation and trends (INSO, UNDSS, National Staff etc.) including close liaison with local stakeholders on security issues and a clear safety briefing to all staff and volunteers - Maintain good contact with SARC, Ministries, local authorities, communities, UN agencies and other stakeholders - Balance activities for IDPs, returnees and host population to avoid unnecessary tensions, noting that lead factor for beneficiary selection is vulnerability, not status - Complete training for new staff on procurement guidelines,conduct systematic internal and external audits to assess adherence to financial and logistic guidelines and spot check for any fraudulent activity.
