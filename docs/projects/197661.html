Project Summary

In line with HRP SO2, DDD’s project for 2022-2023 aims to strengthen access to lifesaving and life-sustaining essential primary health services, protection referrals and COVID-19 response for the most vulnerable people with high-severity needs and limited access to regular services in underserved urban settings and camps in Aleppo and Idleb. They will receive comprehensive Essential Health Service Package: PHC, SRH, and MHPSS through 10 fixed clinics. There will be increasing focus on outreach services, including health promotion activities and home-based specialized medical care focused on people with chronic conditions, people living with disabilities (PwD), and at-risk pregnancies. In-demand MHPSS services will be expanded, with improved MHPSS consultations, dedicated safe spaces for women and children, and strengthened referrals to third-parties through dedicated referral staff. The services will be complemented by the medical waste management for DDD facilities and other providers in Idleb. A total of 274,798 individuals are expected to directly benefit in 2022 and 288,541 in 2023. An estimated 392,247 catchment population will indirectly benefit from the intervention.
The program will support 10 PHCCs:
- 3 PHCCs in Afrin (Afrin PHC 9!B0203070; DDD Jalma comprehensive PHC!B0203087; Jandairis PHC 3!B0203069)
- 9 DDD PHCCs in Idleb (Qah village clinic/ Qah village!G0703049; Morek Alaez camp PHCC/ Morek Alaez Camp!G0703051; Dier Hassan camp PHCC/ Dier Hassan Camp!G0703088; Alsalam camp PHCC/ Alsalam Camp!G0703101; Aldoaa camp PHCC/ Aldoaa Camp!G0703047; DDD Kelly PHC! G0700164; DDD Sarmada PHC! G0703184)
The project has been developed to respond to needs identified by the community itself through DDD’s engagement mechanisms and strong links with local communities, as well as its MSNA in 2021. DDD's MEAL framework, based on Medecins du Monde (MdM) Network standards, will continue to ensure results-oriented implementation, measure impact and adjust activities for improved quality. The MEAL plan includes direct supervision and monitoring at field level through 2 offices in Qah and Afrin; monthly monitoring visits by coordination staff based in Antakya; constant tracking of consumptions of drugs and consumables; monthly reporting of situation, workload, morbidities, beneficiaries reach, and indicators progress; and data disaggregation by gender, age, host/IDP and disability. Annual program evaluations are also conducted to assess impact and any changing needs.
DDD’s close coordination with relevant structures and actors in the region has also informed the project design, to ensure that services are well-integrated and relevant. In the 2022-2023 period, the established coordination is planned to continue with Health, CCCM, Protection, Logistics and Nutrition Clusters, as well as COVID-19 Taskforce, MHPSS WG, NCD WG, the Access WG, the NGO Forum, AFAD meetings, SRH Technical WG, and Afrin and Harim PHC networks. In addition, activities will continue to be supported by cooperation with other partners, including Mentor Initiative Organization for leishmania diagnosis and treatment, Shafak Organization for nutrition services, SRD for referrals, UOSSM for MH-GAP, Horan Foundation for ambulance services, Syrian Immunization Group for COVID-19 vaccination, and GIZ for MHPSS supervision.
DDD, as part of the MdM network, is one of the largest providers of health care services and one of the few NGOs providing PCR testing in NWS. MdM has been responding to the health needs of IDPs and host communities in Dana sub-district since 2012 and in Afrin since 2018. Support for these 10 facilities in 2023 is essential to maintain availability of services to these populations in need.

3 - Needs

NW Syria (Idleb and Aleppo) still the largest increase of health sector needs (PiN - HNO 2022); Afrin and Jandairis have critical gaps. Needs include: Access to free PHC services including NCD, child immunization and MUAC. CDs remain a priority with infection risks such as Leishmaniasis. Demand for home-based PHC/SRH is a priority, particularly for vulnerable groups such as women, WRA, elderly, and PwD: 21% of DDD HH have one PwD while women account for avg. 53% of DDD beneficiaries. COVID-19 is additional barrier for these people. -SRH access: Increased aid dependency and disrupted healthcare system has decreased the wellbeing of WRA: 30% of DDD respondents in the 3 locations require SRH services (incl ANC, PNC), while access to obstetric and new-born health care continues. -MHPSS: 75% of DDD HHs in target locations reported MH issues but only 8% accessed MHPSS professionals; avg 53% cannot access safe spaces for women and children and ongoing need for GBV referrals and specialized medical services. -Increased COVID-19 testing and vaccination with 3% of NWS population fully vaccinated. -Community outreach to increase beneficiary knowledge of health, COVID-19, and services must be improved. The pandemic is occurring amidst dire health conditions, infrastructure and high-risk factors for the CD transmission. -PHC services must be accompanied by the safe removal and management of medical waste in Idlib, where it is burned close to HHs.

4 - Implementation of activities

Essential Health Services Package (SRH, MHPSS, MUAC, NCD, CD, Community Outreach, GBV first-line support, referrals) in addition medical waste management and PCR testing. Services are directly implemented by DDD staff in Afrin and Idleb PHCc and Labs. Syrian Program Coordinator based in Istanbul (with support staff) oversees all Syria staff and NWS activities. Field offices in Qah and Afrin are managed by field coordination team in Antakya. PHC services are provided by qualified health care staff (doctors, paediatrician, gynaecologists, midwives, nurses etc) and PCR tests by trained lab technicians all overseen by the Medical Coordinator. Referrals are made by case managers to protection actors for GBV, CP, and other specialized care needed. Community Health Workers (CHWS) inform beneficiaries of available health care services, health issues and COVID-19. Two incinerators will be operated for enhanced waste management in Idlib and be available to safely dispose of waste for DDD and partner facilities. All PHC equipment, medical consumables and PHCC supplies are procured and shipped into Syria from Turkey. Regular crossborder monitoring is conducted to monitor activities; monthly collected data is disaggregated by gender, age and disability to improve services. Monthly exit interviews facilitate beneficiary feedback, satisfaction, and challenges for service access. Complaints Mechanisms are accessible to beneficiaries, stakeholders, and staff.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

1 Safety and dignity. Protection Risk (PR) 1: GBV, particularly if women are direct assistance recipients. Medium Likelihood, Medium Impact. Mitigation Measures (MM): Eligibility criteria to identify IPV/GBV based on context, community inputs, evidence, and objectives of transfers. Service mapping and referral pathways established. Medical staff are trained on providing survivor-centred support, CMR, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals in line with the internal SoP’s. 2 Access PR 1: Discrimination or perceptions of discrimination among targeted communities for DDD services. Medium Likelihood, High Impact. MM: DDD has a transparent set of criteria and SoP`s for who is or is not eligible for our services and why . Communities are informed in CFRM and the MEAL Team provides continuous feedback to the communities. PR 2: Staff cannot provide referral to specialized GBV and Child Protection support services. High Likelihood and Impact. MM: DDD is active in relevant working groups and collaborates with service providers, updates service mapping, established referral pathways. 3 Data Protection/ beneficiary privacy. PR 1: Interagency referrals are done via hard copy at the field. Sharing personal data of refugees, IDPs and others with third parties puts them at risk of violence, detainment or discrimination. Medium Likelihood. High Impact High. MM: DDD has a sensitive data protection guideline and staff stores the data in locked cabinets.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The power unbalances between DDD staff and our beneficiaries are exacerbated by the disappearance of the traditional protective monitoring mechanisms and weakened formal services in charge of ensuring protection. This might lead to safeguarding risks. DDD uses safeguarding as an umbrella term for a set of procedures and policies: Code of Conduct, PSEA Policy, Whistleblowing Policy, Gender Policy, Child Protection Policy. Following are the mitigation and prevention methods DDD employs for safeguarding risks: 1. DDD continuously trains its staff, partners and communities we work with regarding Safeguarding and complaint and feedback mechanisms. 2. DDD is an active member of the NWS PSEA Network and attends meetings regularly and provides input regarding its achievement, challenges and lessons learned. 3. DDD has a Safeguarding Action Plan for 2022 where all relevant DDD staff contributes to achieving the safeguarding goals. 4. DDD recently increased its staff capacity and recruited two staff members to provide specialized PSEA services.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

DDD implements robust risk management and contingency planning measures, closely following context evolution and developing projects to prevent duplication in coordination with OCHA, Health Cluster, COVID Taskforce, local stakeholders, and other partners. DDD collaborates with Mentor Initiative to receive Leishmania treatment and WHO for NCD kits. DDD conducts periodic needs assessments, health committee meetings and exit interviews to ensure services meet vulnerable peoples’ needs. DDD’s FCRM is accessible to beneficiaries, community members, stakeholders, partners and staff. Key mechanisms in place to mitigate risks to DDD beneficiaries, personnel, assets, and operation: - Security SoPs and staff trained to act in insecure situations. Security context continuously monitored in close coordination with local stakeholders and other partners. - COVID-19 mitigation measures (triage, IPC, and PPE) and staff trained on COVID-19 protocols and prevention. - Contingency Plans updated on continuous basis. - Duty of care policy implemented for staff inside Syria; psychiatrist available for health facility staff to aid in managing stressors. - Coordination with key stakeholders in Turkey and Syria ensures acceptance for DDD operations and safety of staff and beneficiaries. - Active participation in Access Working Group. - Strict financial management controls in place to mitigate risk of fraud.
