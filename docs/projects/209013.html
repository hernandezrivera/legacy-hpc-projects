Project Summary

Sudan has been in crisis for years, and in mid-April 2023, fighting between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) escalated into a conflict of alarming scale. The dispute began in Khartoum and quickly spread to other areas across the country, resulting in over 12,000 fatalities. The conflict has caused massive displacement, with over 6.1 million people forced to leave their homes in search of safety elsewhere. Of these, 4.85 million were internally displaced, and over half were children under 18. Sudan currently faces the most significant internal displacement crisis in the world, with 3.8 million IDPs.
This project will aim to provide lifesaving and sustenance, targeting the most severely food insecure population groups through unconditional in-kind food and cash transfers and essential life-sustaining agricultural livelihood support. The project targets 77500 people who are identified to be in need, facing extreme severity levels classified in the current IPC Phase 3+ in Kassala, Gedaref and Blue Nile states.
The project will support FSL strategic objective one (SO1), which is to provide safe, timely, conscientious and gender-responsive lifesaving humanitarian assistance to the most vulnerable groups (including those displaced, directly impacted host communities, non-displaced people in conflict areas and refugees) to reduce mortality and morbidity in regions with high severity of need or at-risk of a further deterioration of critical humanitarian needs. The project target is 77500, disaggregated as follows: 38750 children & 4650 older people, 38750 women and 11625 PwDs representing the most vulnerable, including the newly and protracted displaced population, refugees and host communities. The project will be implemented in three states, covering ten localities.
The project activities are providing a general food distribution half–ratio for 6,500, most of whom need HHs. Provide and distribute agricultural support (drought-resisting crops and vegetable seeds and seedlings package, farmers' schools, horticultural diversification and agroecology production, post-harvest reduction techniques, community-based women and men agricultural extensionist training) to 4000 small-scale farmers. Provision of agricultural support services [crops, vegetables, legume seeds and associated inputs to 4000 farmers. Provision and distribution of livestock support (establishing livestock migration routes, support vaccination campaigns, poultry raising establishment, livestock health awareness services, livestock watering and migration routes security) to 1000 pastoralists. These activities will be delivered in collaboration with all relevant stakeholders.
Practical Action has a long presence and great legacy in working with communities in the project operational areas. Besides, it has the capacity and capability to deliver multiple projects.
Need Analysis:
Fighting and attacks on producing centres will lead to below-average harvests, reduced income opportunities, and high staple food prices. Around 17.7 million people in Sudan are experiencing high levels of acute food insecurity between October 2023 and February 2024 due to intense conflict, organized violence, and an ongoing economic decline. About 4.9 million people are in Emergency (IPC Phase 4), and almost 12.8 million are in Crisis (IPC Phase 3). According to the last Sudan IPC, Blue Nile state has 1,416,233 people, with 41% in Phase 2, 15% in Phase 3, and 6% in Phase 4. Kassala state, with a population of 2,886,457 people, is 43% classified in Phase 3 and 8% in Phase 4. In Gedarif state, 19% of its population is classified in phase 3; about 542,886 out of 2,788,911 people live there.
It is worth noting that Practical Action has extensive experience in developing the organizational, institutional, and technical capacities of civil society organizations and government institutions through different projects.

3 - Protection mainstreaming
3.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Practical Action ensures understanding and commitment to the safeguarding policies package by all employees, partners and contractors, which states Zero tolerance to all types of sexual exploitation and abuse especially against children, women and vulnerable adults. Complaints system will be put in place to ensure safe and effective communication channel is active for all beneficiaries to raise their concerns. The system includes different tools including hotlines, emails, complaints boxes and others. As communities will be well-oriented and engaged in initial consultations and need assessments, Practical Action will ensure inclusion of the needs of different community groups including women and disabled people to assure that no one is left behind! Doing No harm principle will also be maintained to ensure no negative implications will arise from the project interventions that violate people safety, security, dignity or create damage to the resources. The project will enhance communities’ protection through provision of market-based livelihoods and income generating activities that will reduce tensions and competition over natural resources, raising the public awareness on conflict resolution and peaceful co-existence.

4 - AAP & PSEA
4.1 - How will the project share information and collect community feedback? Please select as many as relevant.
4.1.1 - How will the project use community feedback to improve and adapt activities? (open-ended/free text, required)4

To improve and adapt activities, the project will use community feedback in the following ways:1. The project will work with communities and humanitarian responders to identify the barriers that prevent feedback from being listened to and acted on. This stage is essential to understand where the problems lie, including lack of staffing, poor communication channels, non-working technology, inadequate data management, and confused information flows.2. The project will build on existing staff capacities and work with established services to ensure sustainability. The project will identify which activities are already underway, build on these, and identify which community members, partners, and staff they can work with.3. The project will engage with communities through channels they like and trust. The project will conduct an Information and Communication Needs Assessment to identify which channels different groups and individuals prefer.4. The project will have clear commitment and agreement on roles and responsibilities, both internally and inter-agency. The project will draft Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), which indicate the agreed commitments, follow-up timeframe, and different agency/individual roles.5. The project will design its mechanism to collect structured data that enables it to make decisions and take action at the right time.The project will also engage in a dialogue and joint analysis with people about the feedback they provide. This is essential in identifying solutions and managing their expectations about what program adjustments can be made.

4.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints?

To support Inter-Agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints.The project will also adopt agency mechanisms that feed into and support collective and participatory approaches that inform and listen to communities, address feedback and lead to corrective action. The project will establish and support the implementation of appropriate mechanisms for reporting and handling Sexual exploitation and abuse complaints. The project will plan, design and manage protection and assistance programs responsive to the diversity and expressed views of affected communities.
