Needs

The vast majority of the IDPs in Somalia amount to 2.6 million people who live in informal settlements, classified as critical shelter structures most of the structures are buuls, unfinished or poorly adapted buildings. Some of these IDPs have the lowest level of ongoing humanitarian programs and field presence, rendering many IDPs under serviced and in need of basic assistance. only indicative of the general situation due to the limited number of sites assessed, highlight the stark gaps and needs within informal settlements. The findings demonstrate a clear need for site management and coordination assistance that can bolster overall humanitarian response through the identification of referrals and service requests, and the establishment of reliable site representative structures. A rapid assessment conducted by WRRS showed that:0% IDP representatives in settlements had any kind of CCCM or site management training 0% of settlements had any kind of coordination meetings with service providers 0% of settlements have any kind of IDP committees or governance structures 85% of settlements do not have fire prevention material available, e.g. fire extinguishers 92% of settlements have not received fire prevention training 75% of settlements have not received infrastructural repair/maintenance support 50% of settlements were flood proneThrough collaboration with other clusters, WRRS aims to ensure that the specialized concern of women, children and other vulnerable groups are better responded to and gaps in the provision of basic services are decreased.

Activities

Regular site visits to informal settlements to monitor and identify needs and gaps. Develop and/or deliver partner-prepared awareness raising and sensitization material to target population on life saving issues Document and transmit service requests, protection referrals, site level and HH level information (disaggregated for sex and age) to relevant specialized partners and/or coordination bodies. Facilitate community meetings with relevant stakeholdersIdentify, establish and train individuals to be incorporated within an accountable IDP site representative network, aiming to support women representation (25%). Provide/distribute basic communication materials (e.g. phone credit) to site representatives Facilitate fire prevention training and if necessary, distribution of fire prevention related items, e.g. fire extinguisher and/or smoke detector. Through the provision of multi-purpose cash transfer or in-kind NFI distribution, address basic site repair and improvements that respond to safety and physical risk concerns. Through cash-for-work, pre-identified skilled and unskilled IDPs are able to repair and maintain basic infrastructures that underpin a safe and risk-free environment. Provide technical assistance and guidance in establishing or reinforcing existing community representative/committee structures as necessary (including women’s representation) Support and help facilitate IDP governance meetings. Support and help facilitate site coordination meetings with relevant partner’s/service providers. Basic training on internationally recognized CCCM training modules and complementary trainings/workshops on other relevant themes filling gaps identified through assessments

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The project will contribute to durable solutions efforts and especially in Kismayo where the government together with UNHABITAT are working on site planning and settlement management in the existing IDP settlements. The support by the government offers an environment that if well utilized will see many durable solutions achieved. In line with the CCCM Cluster strategy, the proposed project will aim to provide specialized assistance for the most vulnerable and undeserved temporary settlements, enabling a livable environment for IDPs in which they receive timely and effective comprehensive response to their needs, in accordance with international humanitarian standards and aligned with the specific needs of women, men, girls and boys.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Risk: Under or non-representation of minority or marginalized communities in consultative meetings or amongst Community structures. - Likelihood: High; - Impact: high - Mitigation Measures: Hold an initial open mass gathering and introduce project objectives, selection criteria for various components, organizational procedures and make clear the entitlements for all. Set up accessible, confidential and well understood feedback mechanisms and ensure the information reaches all. Emphasize on inclusive subsequent meetings and possibly hold separate meetings among households clusters. Undertake random physical consultations in the settlements or if need be outside the settlements. Condition implementation to on equal and direct access with all communities and make this very clear to gatekeepers - Risk: Forced Evictions and Government unwillingness to provide land for sustainable infrastructure development for the displaced - Likelihood: high - Impact: High - Mitigation measures: Align project to government’s resilience recovery agenda to gain support and sustainability for project Advocate for legal rights of the IDPs in relation to the due process to be followed to relocate with dignity Sensitize and support the government on developing policies and enforcing laws on land to protect the displaced

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Risk: gender-based violence and discrimination against women and girls as they access facilities Likelihood: High - Impact: Very high - Mitigation: - Involve women and girls in siting locations for communal facilities considering their security and safety . Ensure the location and access routes to facilities and distribution points are safe. Where possible avoid central distribution on NFIs, Hygiene kits to avoid long ques and exposure of items to evil doers but rather use mobile money cash transfer for beneciary to purchase items at own convenient time. Ensure lighting in basic facilities to reduce women and girls overcrowding, waiting too long at the ques and getting caught in darkness. Avoid community tensions, by providing the host community with access to basic services where they are experiencing similar needs for services and facilities. Work in coordination with protection actors to put in place measures to prevent and respond to physical/sexual violence arising from use of or lack of basic services. Risk: Rigid infrastructure that is difficult to be used by persons with reduced mobility (eg. persons with physical disability, older persons) Likelihood: medium Impact: medium Mitigation: - Make infrastructure adaptions to structures to make them accessible to this category of people (proportion of latrines with ramp and side rails, handlebars, toilet seats, Ensure that beneciaries know about the programme and how to obtain items, where and how much they cost.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

One of the major risk of the Somali environment with a potential high impact is the worsening of the security situation due to clan conflicts, terrorism and political controversies that can limit the movements, resulting in a restriction of access to facilities, increased demand for health care services, population displacement, increased morbidity and mortality with a possibility of halting the intervention. As a mitigation measures WRRS has operational safety procedures that include clear risk assessment procedures, evacuation plans and suspension for the staff and it collaborates with local authorities and with community to guarantee acceptance, access and security of properties and staff. Regular contacts with the authorities at the regional and national levels will be maintain to ensure that valuable supporters and local authorities remains engaged and supportive of the programme.
