1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The project aims at support the students and teachers affected by the humanitarian crisis in Cabo Delgado. The Action will aims to improve the access to safe and quality education for girls and boys in order to create a sustainable system. From the need assessment and lessons learnt, AVSI elaborated the following logic of intervention with community-based and school specific sustainable strategic actions which are interwoven and interrelated to Improve access to safe and basic education for children displaced affected by the humanitarian crisis. The target groups will be students, teachers and schools (including the management) divided as per follow:- 8,000 students targeted; % of teacher demonstrating children retention approaches; Comparative attendance and retention rates. - 560 primary teachers trained on rehabilitative skills for out of school children- 1,000 of out of school children re-integrated back to school; - 300 education stakeholders trained on education promotion; % caregivers and parents mobilising out of school children back to schoolThe project will be implemented with a strong involvement of the local community to ensure its sustainability. The objective is to work on different possible scenarios given the complexity of the context.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

The specific activities are: • Establishment of TLS for the children affected by humanitarian crisis in order to guarantee a safe and quality learning spaces• Teachers training on key thematics to guarantee PSS and PFA assistance and to identify the most vulnerable children. The training will focus: class management and implementation of hygiene protocols; Positive discipline in schools; Child protection and Psychosocial support; Multi grade system of teaching approaches to cater for newly enrolled learners with various learning needs; Age appropriate teaching/learning materials preparation; PSS Rehabilitative Non-formal education program; Vocational training for overage and alternative education; Short-term vocational courses;• Distribution of learning and recreational materials amongs cyclone IDPs students lost their materials;• Distribution of teachers kits amongs the teachers needs additional support• Teachers training of specific thematic with a modules dedicate to the PSEA and GBV principles• Higiene and sanitation intervention promoted between the schools with a distribution of higiene kits • Awareness campaing on back to schools and reinforcement the distance learning schools for the students still at home• Training on access to school for out-of-school children. Engagement of the excluded, the most vulnerable and hardest-to-reach children, and their families, in awareness raising and motivational activities designed to support enrollment in schools. Training takes in consideration: Adolescent girls’ education promotion; Prevention of Child labour; Legal provision on education; Community based education promotion.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Even though school/centre environment is supposed to be a protected and safe environment for the correct development of the child, in particular in an emergency context, focusing only on the mere access to school does not conduct directly and immediately to better outcomes in terms of child protection. School is meant to be a protected environment: providing psychosocial support in emergency and the rehabilitation of school buildings (included approaches to make safer the way to school) are two key actions to be planned in all the interventions. Combination between hard and soft actions are preferable. Moreover, this project will be implemented jointly with the AVSI-UNICEF child protection programme. The project, which takes place in the districts of Pemba, Ibo, Montepuez and Metuge, has a staff of 8 people and 260 activists who reaches more than 6000 children. Case Management activities allow the identification and management of the most vulnerable cases. Through a mapping of the districts, AVSI is able to have a clear picture of households and their residences. Child protection activities, integrated with education activities, ensure that all possible risks to children can be avoided during the project.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people?

In line with the Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS), AVSI will employ its Accountability Guidance to establish a Beneficiary Communication and Accountability (BCA) mechanism. Information derived from this mechanism, as well as output monitoring data will be validated through regular field visits to project site after which a field monitoring report will be produced. Monthly project reports will be produced and will include sex- disaggregated monitoring and evaluation data, including key findings from field monitoring, PDM and the BCA mechanism. These reports alongside other project data will be shared and discussed during monthly project management meetings with project and key AVSI staff. This process will inform adaptive project management decisions to enhance project effectiveness and responsiveness. Any significant issues will be shared with the donor to ensure transparency and seek collective solutions that can best address the needs of beneficiaries. Humanitarian Inclusion standards will be used to ensure that MEAL systems follow recommended key actions

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

Inclusive activities are mainstreamed in all AVSI projects. Teachers are trained on safe identification and referral and according to the needs, specific topics are tackled concerning physical or mental disability. In some cases, special educators are hired to provide continuous support and mentoring to teachers on the inclusion of children, from needs assessment to progress monitoring, as well as coordinate the work of specialized service providers. Activities can be implemented in different context (non-formal, formal, or informal). In different projects we engaged also specialized services – speech therapy, psychology, psychomotor therapy, mobility assistant for the visually impaired children, etc both for the assessment of child’s needs and for the provision of support services. Referral pathways are always mapped and shared. The engagement of parents for these activities, in the parental meetings to discuss the child’s progress and problems is fundamental.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The proposed activities will adopt an environment-friendly approach by trying to increase awareness in the affected communities. With a strong community engagement and participation; on rehabilitation activities, specific attention regarding the site selection and sourcing of materials, favouring the use of local resources, like the locally sourced building materials, sun-cured soil bricks produced in affected communities and the limiting of the use of timber wherever feasible, for all rehabilitation and construction. WASH activities will be managed with a special care for the selecting sites and constructing latrines to ensure that there is no overflow or infiltration into ground water, a safe access to potable and clean water, and proper location of sanitation facilities, that will ensure that environmental harm is reduced or eliminated and environmental benefits are maximized. Finally, health and hygiene education will have a component of key messages regarding the environment.
