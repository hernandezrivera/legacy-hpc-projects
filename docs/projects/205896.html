Project Summary

The International Medical Corps (IMC) will continue to implement GBV prevention and response program targeting returnees, IDPs and host community with the aim of empowering. communities and build resilience through promoting social and behavioral change to address the root causes of GBV in the country. IMC will work closely with Hope Restoration South Sudan (HRSS) to ensure that wider communities are reached and that the partners capacity is build.
GBV prevention efforts will focus on behavior and attitude change interventions through engaging men and boys, working with community structures and local activities as well as women and girls. Response activities will focus on strengthening survivor cantered case management services and psychosocial support. Further IMC and HRSS will strengthen the linkages to ensure that survivors receive necessary information through integrating services with health, nutrition and mental health and Psychosocial support (MHPSS).
IMC and HRSS will take advantage of its vast experience and success and continue to implement GBV prevention and Response activities in Upper Nile (Malakal PoC and Malakal ton Renk, County), Unity (Nyal County), Panyijar ( Leer and Mayendit) (Canal/Pigi County, Kurwai,
Fangak).

The interventions will focus on the provision of individual support in line with the Inter-Agency GBV Case Management Guidelines for at-risk women and girls as well as provision of group psychosocial support activities within Women and Girls Safe Spaces (WGSS.) in line with IMC/IRC WGSS global toolkit (2017). IMC UK and HRSS community engagement activities will focus on long-term social and behavior changes strategies which aims at addressing the root cause of violence. These strategies will include use of SASA! Community mobilization tool which aims at encouraging the communities to rethink use of power, promote gender equality and ensure balanced and safe societies SASA!IMC and HRSS will work with men and boys through Engaging Men in Accountable Practice (EMAP) which is a primary prevention model, which aims at men’s gender equitable attitudes and reducing violence against women and girls. In addition, IMC will work closely with community structures including community leaders, Payam administrators and camp leadership. The afore mentioned groups will be trained on GBV key concepts, PSEA, Psychosocial Fist Aid (PFA) and safe referrals. Similar training will be conducted with other stakeholders including the Ministry of Gender Child, and Social Welfare (MOGCSW), Women Association (WA) and Police. Targeted capacity building of the partner HRSS will be done upon capacity gap assessment. The project aims at reaching 22483 direct beneficiaries with services

1. Needs

GBV continue to affect men, women, boys and girls, but it disproportionally affectswomen and girls in South Sudan. This has been Gender inequality, exacerbated by armedconflict, severe drought, massive flooding, and ethnic conflicts. Data from GBVIMScollected by IMC within the context of service provision in their case managementcenters in upper Nile, Unity state, Western Barh el Ghazal and Canal Pigi. shows that 98%of the GBV cases were reported by women and girls with denial of resources being thehighest cases reported followed by physical assault. In addition, Assessment done byDanish Refugee Council (DRC)in May 2023 in Renk indicates that the arrival and presenceof the returnees and spontaneous refugee population have greatly affected the price ofcommodities in the market, in some cases up to three times the original price of theitems. This is the similar situation in Malakal. The returnees have limited access to basicneeds such as food and hygiene items with limited means of procuring them with thehiking market prices. This has greatly affected women and girls who have limited accessto dignity kits including menstrual hygiene materials, which puts them in a risk of SEA.IMC is providing integrated GBV prevention and response services in Renk and Malakal inresponse to the Sudan crisis. Report from the safety audit conducted jointly by IMC,HRSS and Protection working group partners indicates in 2023 in Upper Nile, indicateincreased vulnerability as a result of reduced coping mechanism due to ethnic conflict aswell as increased numbers of returnees from Sudan Crisis. At the reception center inMalakal, the returnees are arriving in an average of 700-1000 individuals per day. Theassessment showed increased numbers has resulted to limited shelters leading to morethan one family sharing one shelter which has reduce their privacy. Further, they have towalk away from their shelters at night which increased their vulnerability to protectionrisks. Lack of basic needs has increased women vulnerability to sexual exploitation andabuse (SEA). Safety audit conducted in Western Barh el Ghazal, Wau, Unity state andupper Nile Panyijar, Korwach and Old Fangak, show reports of GBV specifically physicalviolence, Intimate Partner Violence, forced marriage and sexual assault, where Womenand girls are exposed to this forms of GBV in their routine activities in their communitiessuch as water points, latrines, distribution areas (food and non-food items), marketsand/or firewood collection sites, among others. In Renk, Safety audit exercise conductedjointly by IMC , HRSS and UNHCR led Protection working group in Renk in August 2023shows increased protection risks affecting returnees in both locations. Issues identifiedin the audit included: Teenage pregnancies, risks of physically violence, sexualexploitation and abuse as well as risk of child labour. Help-seeking and reporting ofprotection incidents is highly affected by cultural barriers. Adolescent girls who areattending activities within Women and Girls Safe Space (WGSS) run by IMC in Renk haveexpressed fear of risk of forced marriage as their studies have been interrupted by theconflict. IMC has provided interventions tailored toward adolescent girls within the WGSSwhich include information sharing to improve their levels of awareness on GBV andservices available including creating awareness in local language on topics whichaddress harmful cultural norms. The situation is similar in Canal Pigi county where forcedmarriage and IPV is reported to be prevalent. In addition, reports from IMC GBVIMS inCanal Pigi shows that Physical assault has accounted for most cases throughout theyear, while psychological and emotional abuse accounted for the second highest numberof GBV cases perpetrated and reported. That is, 39.34% physical assault, 34.21%emotional/psychological denial of resources, and 17% of cases were forced marriage.

2. Project activities

Provision of specialized GBV response services which include case management andpsychosocial support. IMC and HRSS will provide survivor-centered case managementand psychosocial support focused on healing, empowerment and recovery andpsychosocial support services within WGSS to promote healing, well-being, andempowerment. These services will be provided within the WGSS by a trained GBVcaseworker. Rape cases will be referred to clinical management of rape (CMR) providedby IMC midwife. In addition, IMC will provide emergency cash assistance to GBVsurvivors for the purpose of meeting individualized needs and facilitating casemanagement processes. Psychosocial support activities will be implemented withinWGSS where women and girls will participate in group psychosocial support activitieswithin WGSS which ensure that women and girls access crucial information about theirrights and services available for GBV survivors. Implement GBV prevention and riskmitigation activities, through holistic community engagement, such as Engaging Men inAccountable Practice (EMAP) and Start, Awareness, Support, and Action (SASA!)activities. Awareness raising activities will be conducted through IMC trained communitystructures who are comprised of GBV taskforce as well as SASA community activists.Provision of Dignity Kits to the Most Marginalized and Vulnerable Women and Girls ofReproductive Age. IMC UK and HLSS will procure and distribute dignity kits to the conflictaffected women and girls. The dignity kits will include sanitary items, as well as otheritems explicitly tailored towards the local needs of women and girls at risk. The kits willhelp women and girls maintain their health and dignity during humanitarian crises.Conduct safety audit. IMC will conduct joint-participatory safety audit assessments withpartners and local authorities to identify multi-sectorial concerns that expose women andgirls to the risks of GBV. IMC UK will share the findings and recommendations withrelevant partners, donors, and other stakeholders for their action. Enhance capacities offrontline workers, key community leaders and community-based organizations on GBVprevention, mitigation and response. IMC will continue to provide various targetedtrainings to key GBV actors – the frontline responders such as the GBV case workers;community leaders, social workers from the Ministry of Gender, Child and Social Welfaretrained women’s committee members, among others. The frontline workers andcommunity leaders will be trained on GBV basic concepts, psychological first aid (PFA),quality GBV prevention and safe referrals while GBV staff will be trained in line with casemanagement, prevention and risk mitigation as well as Psychosocial first aid. This willinclude GBV, nutrition and health staff. The GBV staff will be trained on GBV key concept,psychosocial first aid (PFA) and case management while health and nutrition team will betrained on GBV concept, PFA and referral pathways. Provision of fuel efficiency stove IMCUK and HRSS will IMC facilitate the training on fuel efficient stove to women and girls'part of GBV mitigation measures. Women and girls have the primary role to care for thefamily including looking for firewood and fetching water to cook for the families. In mostinstances they are forced to move to far distances to fetch firewood where they are atrisk of rape, sexual assault and Intimate partner violence especially during theemergencies. IMC and HRSS will ensure that women are provided training on use andhow to make the stove locally with locally. This will save the time that women and girlswill be using to travel for long distance to fetch firewood which can be used for otheractivities and reduce the risk of GBV
