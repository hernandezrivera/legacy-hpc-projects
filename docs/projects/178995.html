Project Summary

The program’s overall objective is to strengthen social cohesion amongst conflict affected communities in North Syria and contribute to long-term peace and stability of Syria. Strengthening cohesive communities is vital to supporting the stability that is finally emerging in North Syria, and reinforcing post-conflict economic recovery. The combination of improving inter-communal relationships and reducing resource-based tensions will serve to prevent the escalation of some economic-based disputes into violence – for example between host and IDP communities over livelihood opportunities or social service provision – and reduce the ability of political actors to manipulate ethno-religious divisions and mobilise violence. To this end, and in close collaboration with local and neighborhood councils, Mercy Corps will implement community service projects to support local economic development and address resource-based tensions. Mercy Corps social cohesion approach is grounded on a sound context, conflict and conflict sensitivity analysis that supports the identification of viable entry points for strengthening social cohesion within and between communities on different sides of the conflict divide. Mercy Corps supports community members with the knowledge, skills and confidence they need to analyze opportunities and impediments to strengthening social cohesion; and to formulate and implement options for strengthening social cohesion at the community level. Through participatory methodology including community mobilization, dialogue, interest based negotiation and the use of sports and creative arts; Mercy Corps will encourage positive interactions between and among community members from across the conflict divide. Mercy Corps will also support conflicting communities to address drivers of conflict through the joint implementation of projects that address resource based conflict drivers. Mercy Corps community mobilization methodology multi-pronged approach to social cohesion Conflict sensitivity is at the heart of all social cohesion activities. The project will develop a conflict sensitivity strategy, guided by various conflict sensitivity analysis tools including the do no harm matrix and stakeholder analysis matrix, building off the findings of context and conflict analysis in the areas of implementation. Mercy Corps social cohesion project will ensure the meaningful participation of all sections of the community with an emphasis on women and youth (male and female).

4 - Needs

The conflict in Syria has contributed to the fragmentation of Syrian communities. Micro-level tensions and conflicts are prevalent, driven by unresolved historical and emerging grievances, population movements (displacements and returns), dissatisfaction with issues of governance, competition over control and access to resources, limited access to formal and informal dispute resolution and a deterioration in economic wellbeing amongst other drivers. Conflicts and tensions often manifest along identity lines including political, ethnic, displacement (host vs IDPs vs returnees) and tribal identities. Displacement, gross human rights violations, forceful conscription, radicalization, tactics of war such as altering demographic composition of populations along ethnic lines and other impacts of the macro-level conflict in Syria damaged cohesiveness of micro-level Syrian communities. The lack of cohesiveness in-turn limits opportunities for transforming micro-level conflicts across the country and presents challenges for durable solutions for the people of Syria. However, opportunities for building and strengthening social cohesion pertain, as evidenced by emerging micro-level initiatives by various local and international organizations working across Syria. These initiatives highlight the possibility of achieving gradual and incremental transformation for Syrian communities through carefully designed and context appropriate interventions that facilitate constructive interactions amongst within and between identity groups – ultimately contributing to social cohesion.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and Mercy Corps has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. Mercy Corps is committed to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Child Safeguarding through appropriate policies (Code of Conduct) which are enforced through on-boarding and training to all staff, suppliers and partners. Distribution are carefully planned to ensure they are done at times and in places that are safe and accessible to women and girls, as well as the elderly and people with disabilities, and that any barriers to access as a result of gender norms or physical ability are taken into account when planning for the distribution. Forceful conscription into armed forces: Adult men (above 18) are at risk of forceful conscription by armed forces and governing authority across Syria. This project will target youth and project activities could be exploited as an opportunity to forcefully conscript male youth. Threat to physical safety and security of participants: The program could challenge harmful community cultures and norms. This could place project participants at risk of harm by other community members who believe that challenging harmful cultures and norms is a betrayal and threat to the larger community.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Safety and security of program participants and staff could be negatively affected: The ongoing conflict in Syria presents a constant threat to the safety and security of individuals and groups across Syria, including prospective project participants and staff members. To mitigate this risk. Mercy Corps will conduct ongoing context analysis including security and risk assessments to inform decisions on location, routes and timing of program activities, ensuring such decisions are in the best interest of the safety and security of participants and staff. Social and cultural norms of targeted communities will be observed during all phases of the program implementation to ensure safety risks associated with real or perceived violation of norms are mitigated. Project staff will complete a mandatory safety and security training; and participants will also be sensitized on issues of safety and security so that they are able to recognize threats to their own safety and security and take action to protect themselves from such threats. Implementation delays due to COVID 19: Program implementation could experience significant delays due to the COVID-19 pandemic, occasioned by lockdowns and curfews. Mercy Corps will draw on experiences adapting program implementation over the last one year to ensure that as many activities as possible can be implemented. Simultaneously, opportunities for pursuing and strengthening remote program implementation capacities will be explored and harnessed. Loss of access to target communities: Shifts in territorial control or major security incidents could temporarily or permanently impede access of Mercy Corps organization to project participants. Mercy Corps will continuously monitor the situation.
