Project Summary

The project aims at reducing vulnerability and improving physical and mental well-being of crisis-affected population with particular attention to specific vulnerabilities linked to Disability, Gender, and Age (DGA). The INGO action focuses on providing multidisciplinary rehabilitation services tailored to the needs of people with physical impairment and their caregivers through direct intervention or through the support of local partners in North-West, North-East and GoS-controlled parts of Syria. The intervention includes provision of multidisciplinary rehabilitation services and psychosocial support services to physical rehabilitation beneficiaries and their caregivers. Furthermore, to ensure continuity of the tailored treatment plans, remote follow up services are provided through telerehabilitation/tele-PSS sessions in case of movement restrictions, due to COVID-19 or existing gender norms. These services help in recovery of physical rehabilitation beneficiaries and support the caregivers through a holistic approach in order to alleviate stress, fatigue and other psychological impacts related to their responsibilities. The project improve access to services through referral, accessibility work in health structures, training of (female) rehabilitation staff and integration of the community through Self-Help Groups, awareness raising sessions and mobile outreach to increase access for IDPs, underserved communities and persons who are incapable of reaching the service centers. The INGO also mainstreams COVID-19 key messaging in the activities package according to WHO Guidelines. In GoS- controlled areas and NWS the intervention includes the provision of mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services to people suffering from psychological distress without having physical rehabilitation needs. MHPSS services include psychological first aid, psycho education, and individual and/or group session therapy. Across whole of Syria including GoS- controlled areas, the INGO focuses on developing rehabilitation services in areas where services are not available. Concerning DGA mainstreaming, all activities and delivered services are prepared based on a rapid assessment to study the sensitivity of the context and ensure inclusion according to DGA. This includes the promotion of equal opportunities for people with different needs and abilities. Conditional cash for transportation is also provided to patients and caregivers according to individual assessed needs based on pre-defined criteria. The INGO closely coordinate with the Cash working group in the targeted hub to align the common standards and guidance. Conditional cash will be provided to patients and/or caregivers based on individual assistance and the INGO criteria taking into account socioeconomic, travel, and clinical factors. The health staff assess the needs case by case based on fixed criteria. The cash is conditioned to the attendance of patient to the treatment sessions aiming to ensure the finalization of the treatment plan. Acknowledging the dire economic situation in WoS, where possible close linkages with other actors are envisaged to support beneficiaries accessing unconditional cash and support actors to enhance inclusion within CVA programs. The INGO provides support to its local health partners through technical, financial and managerial support, to improve the quality of services and ensure the sustainability of impact of the project. The capacity building support is tailored to the needs of partners. The Inclusion specialist support the health clusters, by guiding through several session on how to implement DGA inclusion principles ,how to identify vulnerability risks of persons with disabilities, setting indicators on data for identification of persons with disabilities. In addition to trainings related to understanding needs of persons with disabilities including women.

4 - Needs

An estimated 12.2M people need health assistance (HNO/HRP2022). Syria ranks among the worst affected countries in the Middle East and North Africa region from COVID-19. Hostilities are the direct cause of death and injury, displacement, damage to property and destruction of schools, hospitals/health points. In 2021, OHCHR documented 1,874 civilian casualties of airstrikes, ground-based shelling, armed clashes in NWS, and explosive ordnance incidents, including improvised explosive devices, and landmines. An overall lessening in hostilities reduced population movements since March 2020, there is some evidence that the increasingly dire economic situation overall, combined with the often-minimal basic services available in areas of return, affected households’(HH) decisions to move back home, especially IDPs with disabilities (HNAP 2021). These factors prompted pre-emptive movement towards camps and sites where there is more likely to be humanitarian assistance. Access to health was reported among top priorities by 60% of HH with at least one family member with disability. Main barriers included lack of existing services, or available but too expensive, lack of finances to cover transportation. For mental health, crisis continues to affect psychosocial well-being, exposure to violence, loss of income and reduced access to basic services, touching the youngest in particular. The psychosocial support has been reported as the 2nd protection priority need (50% NWS and 43% NES).

5 - Implementation of activities

Deliver multidisciplinary rehab services, through gender-balanced teams, including physical and functional rehab sessions, provision of assistive devices, prosthetic and orthotic devices, education sessions and/or complementary PSS sessions (also to caregivers). MHPSS will be provided to persons affected by psychosocial and psychological issues caused by conflict, displacement and multiple losses. Persons in needs are identified through outreach teams and other services (presence in health facilities, coordination fora, partners).Identification and needs assessment of persons with injuries and/or disabilities in hospitals, rehab centres, IDP camps, communities. Health services will be provided with support of 4 rehab centers in GoS, 8 Hospitals/rehab centers in NES, 7 hospitals and 2 rehab centers in NWS, 2 mobile teams in NES and 15 in NWS. 10 community workers in NES and NWS. Health services are aimed at prevention of longer-term impairment and promotion of independence for people with disabilities, to promote inclusive access to other services. INGO will strengthen national technical capacity especially on identification of barriers people face accessing services and participating, production of inclusion assessment report with practical recommendations and action plan, training, coaching and follow ups. MEAL role through regular field visits and remote monitoring tools and Knowledge, attitude and practices (KAP),satisfaction surveys, and Feedback Response Mechanism.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

•Protection of field staff and partners, INGO keep low visibility to protect identities of staff. In case of an attack on health care, activates its DoC policy and provide field staff with psychological support to prevent burn out. •INGO applies PSEA policy for staff, partner staff and suppliers. Proper accountability measures are ensured through investigation and follow up of reported cases. Also raises awareness on its policy on PSEA and complaints and feedback mechanisms to follow up. Part of inclusion mainstreaming strategy, training to staff and its partners to highlight vulnerabilities faced by persons with disabilities, person of older age and girls and women (GBV, sexual exploitation, etc.) is implemented. The INGO has protection task force, to ensure protection mainstreaming is optimized. Referral task force works towards improving protection referrals and processes.•Healthcare Provision for vulnerable groups and persons with disabilities and equitable access to health services; staff regularly trained on how to interact, communicate and engage with persons with disabilities. •Safe closure of facilities and protecting data: Field workers are trained on safe withdrawal and evacuation measures including ways to protect/destroy data.•Working through implementing partners: before becoming an implementing partner, each actor goes through a thorough assessment and vetting process. Reporting is required, monitoring visits, feedback and response mechanism are in place.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

The INGO expresses its determination to combat the sexual exploitation, abuse and harassment of children and vulnerable adults. Also implement measures to reduce risks on programs. •Sexual Exploitation and Abuse: PSEAH and Child Protection policies and Code of Conduct is effective; Mandatory and refresher PSEAH training and other safeguarding policies for staff and partners; reporting mechanism is effective •New employee is not aware of policy: all candidates seeking employment shall undergo recruitment process and submit background checks. Staff signing permanent or fixed-term employment contract, accompanying family members, trustees, consultants, interns, volunteers shall be informed of PSEAH policy. •Beneficiaries not aware of rights of services: information session will be provided for beneficiaries, as well as complaint number and process. Country Manager is responsible for information on policy and practice, and for monitoring matters related to abuse within the INGO network.•Sensitive cultural and beneficiaries refuse services of opposite genders: To address cultural sensitive and risks with GBV and CP, INGO will ensure gender balance of staff, with the aim of increasing number of female staff. •Partners are involved in actions of abuse or harassment against beneficiaries: Any proven allegations of such practices shall result in follow-up of the case and termination of agreement unless the partner commits to and effectively ensures a radical change in behavior.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Loss of access and border closure mitigated as the INGO developed an access strategy that is regularly reviewed and adapted. •Lack of acceptance is mitigated through positive relations with stakeholders, community and staff. INGO and its partners are well-perceived on the ground and ensure to keep close coordination with local authorities to maintain positive relations with community. The INGO will also focus on increasing the community’s participation in programs; setting in place community satisfaction surveys to measure satisfaction of the community. •Loss of funding is mitigated by the INGO’s close communication with various donors, and direct negotiation for continuous funding and adapting programs accordingly.•The eventual spread-out of COVID-19 pandemic and its implications on the operational capacities, as per prior experience in 2020. To mitigate this threat, the INGO and partners will ensure the following: i) continue exploring alternatives remote modalities of intervention ii) ensuring PPE and other hygiene items are available; iii) mainstreaming COVID-19 messaging including DGA; iv) monitoring the epidemic situation through the health cluster •Logistic and fund transfer issues and supply chain interruption, mitigated by assessing transfer partners and enhancing coordination with INGOs and prepare contingency stock. •Mismanagement for cash related issue is mitigated: 1) individual monitoring of beneficiaries, 2) FRM and anti-fraud corruption policy applied.
