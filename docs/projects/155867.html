Project Summary

In line with the HRP Strategic Objective 5: Increased and more equitable access for boys and girls to quality child protection interventions in targeted locations in line with the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action. The project aims at tri-fold objectives, ensuring activities will be sustained as plans; 1) rehabilitating existing nurseries, provide furniture for CFS, children desks and chairs, teachers and management equipment, WASH internal maintenance, and fridge at each nursery, 2) provide capacity enhancement to the staff therein, and 3) conducting awareness campaigns focused on child protection, caregivers and parenting, women empowerment, mitigation and prevention of GBV, and early marriages. Under these objectives, the project addresses direct needs for 825 registered children in nurseries operating under the supervision of the Rural Damascus Education Directorate (RDED). However, the project will raise the community awareness on early marriage, taking that at least 20% of the caseload are parents under 18 years of age. The assurance of the safety for their children will enhance their confidence to avoid leaning to negative coping mechanisms, or fear that they would be forced to leave their children in order to provide income but rather to work. The project will carry out awareness campaigns about child protection, child rights, and early marriage. These activities including capacity enhancement of the caregivers and nurseries’ staff will be conducted by the Syria Trust. Therefore; the project will eventually impact on the direct needs of 7500 parents because it will encourage them to go to work and register their children when the protective environment status of the spaces is upgraded, and become healthy and secured, the registered children expected to increase to 1,500. Staff will receive capacity enhancement on child protection, PFA to be able to detect cases for referrals to different services. Staff will join caregivers during participation in FGDs and public awareness campaigns on child rights, consequences of early marriages, parenting and promotion of the available services. The campaigns will include printing materials on child care and protection, and early marriages to be distributed at the nurseries addressing the children’s families. The project will enhance women empowerment by FGDs and social community awareness campaigns. Rescate will carry out the rehabilitation dividing the targeted nurseries to LOTs each is 2 nurseries at a time, to ensure implementation is concluded while children are hosted in the nearby nurseries. The project rehabilitation implementation will be concluded in 8 months, 2 nurseries per 2 months stage ensuring continuation of service without any extra burden on the parents when they have to move their children to a neighboring nursery during the rehabilitation works. The capacity enhancement and awareness campaigns will be carried out during the project cycle to reach to most population in the areas/localities of intervention. The prioritization of this response is solidly based on needs and priority actions are accepted by all segments of the communities.

Needs

Based on field assessment in the areas in East Ghouta in addition to requests from the Education Department, and Rural Damascus Governorate, the project aims to respond to the urgent need to build a protective environment via rehabilitation of 8 nurseries to be suitable, healthy and safe as daytime care facilities for children at the age of 3-5 years. These nurseries were under the mandate of the Women Union in Syria, they accommodate children during daytime while parents are at work. Services will be sustained as plans, but not structures, to return to their main function as nursery facility in addition to provision of capacity enhancement to the staff and adding awareness campaigns targeting women, caregivers, and families in general. There are dangers of rodents, and other insects that may affect children’s health and safety, not to mention the workers at the nurseries. The population in the areas consist of returnees and IDPs who are in dire need to work to support their families’ living needs. The intervention is a Community-based child protection project ensuring the availability of CFS/CPU and other static structures for girls and boys in prioritized locations. The project will greatly contribute to: - Provide direct daytime care to some 825 children (direct assistance to 16 children with disabilities among them) – Carry out planned recreational activities, -Increase enrolled children after upgrading (675 on waiting lists), consequently will provide more chances for livelihood opportunities for parents. - Limitation of high rate of infectious diseases and pandemics among students. - Assist 7,500 individuals (1,500 families) in the 8 areas of intervention. - Limitation of the reluctance of many citizens to work, especially women, outside their homes. – Carry out awareness campaigns addressed to mothers, parents, in mitigation of GBV as the caseload assessment revealed the existence of ~20% of the direct beneficiaries are GBV survivors been victims of child marriages who need support in empowerment, enabling them to work to mitigate the risk of leaning to negative coping mechanisms. A contract will be signed with a local contractor to carry out the rehabilitation works, while Rescate will directly carry out the procurement of the furniture and equipment for the nurseries’ operation.

Implementation of activities

Ensure a comprehensive service mapping is undertaken so that risk mitigation and communication with communities can be proactive and informed. Carry out training for the nurseries’ staff on Child Protection enabling staff to resume and ensure the sustainability of the program plans to return the nurseries to their function as nursery facilities, ensure that the staff are trained on child protection in line with child protection minimum standards. Linking protection intervention with existing services within targeted communities to enhance the system approach. Capacity building training and coaching of nursery staff to be delivered by professionals with special emphasis in complying with the "do no harm principle". Assess impact of the trainings introducing appropriate methodology and base lines. Link with other capacity building initiatives to ensure synergy in all the nurseries under the project. Encourage follow-up, mentoring and coaching initiatives. Carry out awareness raising and community events to prevent and respond to child protection issues. Carry out awareness raising campaigns and community events targeting women empowerment, prevention of GBV and early marriage, to prevent women leaning to negative coping mechanisms. Awareness session directing men and women to referral pathways to legal and social aide and services. The staff of the nurseries will be trained on case detection and connection with case managers (MOSAL) in the communities for further referrals to specialised services. The project will enhance support to national actors and community-based groups to ensure sustainability of protection intervention. Rescate will carry out procurement procedure for contractual agreement for rehabilitation in due course of time. Carry out procurement procedure for contractual agreement for provision of furniture and equipment. Will include monitoring of access to services by sex, age, host/IDP status, and by people with reduced mobility. And will ensure appropriate supervision by relevant authorities is in place upon final delivery of the premises. There will be an monitoring and evaluation officer in place to manage the 8 nursery sites, with regular visits and regular monitoring of the progress of the activities; as well as engaging the communities in designing, providing and evaluation of the services. This community based evaluations were the basis of the needs identification of this project , as well as the response planning.

Protection Risk Analysis

Rescate will ensure mitigation measures are taken in consultation with other partners from the sector so nothing will go wrong during the implementation of the project. Rescate will provide the services in consultation with stakeholders and we will coordinate with relevant partners to ensure do no harm. Staff training on PFA to be able to conduct referral pathway to different services in detected cases among the children. Rescate will actively work with the direct nurseries' caregivers, and families of the children beneficiaries targeting the most vulnerable from the returnees and original inhabitants with focus on FHHs, PWDs, and families with need to secure daytime care for their children. Rescate will also ensure active involvement at all times of beneficiaries and the wider community, ensuring that the activities are accessible to everyone for examination and inspection and will assist beneficiaries to actively involve throughout the project’s life cycle. Rescate will ensure to take clear information about needs if required will be directed to those in need such as, children with specific needs (there are 16 already registered at these nurseries) and FHHs who are considered among the most vulnerable groups. The implementation of post-implementation monitoring questionnaires. This information assists in providing essential data to gain a better understanding of the living conditions of affected populations by the crisis, in addition to monitoring the relevance, effectiveness, efficiency and impact of the provision of the services.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination if a service is provided to specific groups or individuals at the exclusion of others. This is mitigated by community-based approach, Rescate had received the request for this project from the communities in the targeted areas. Risk of doing harm or perpetuating unintended consequences to girls, and boys (and their caregivers) through the provision of specialized child protection services if service providers are not adequately trained/ services are of low quality, the risk of creating or perpetuating stigma for girls, boys and their families if staff are not appropriately trained or if client data is not managed appropriately, and the risk of inappropriate respond to protection issues faced by children due to limited number of qualified personnel among partners and national actors. Risk to the wellbeing of child protection service providers due to exposure to violence, Risk to physical safety of child protection service providers if perceived to be interfering in family-based concerns, Impact of activity is minimal on children's protection, Limited knowledge among stakeholder responsible to make key decisions regarding the protection of children. The above risks will be mitigated via providing capacity building to the staff at the nurseries ensuring that the activities are sustained as planned and the staff are trained on child protection and PFA for referral of cases to the available services. The Syria Trust for Development will provide specialized trainings for Child Protection at its nearest Community Centers in the area, while ensuring the training packages are in line child protection minimum standards. In order to ensure protection mainstreaming, Rescate applies the “Do No Harm” approach in all its interventions.
