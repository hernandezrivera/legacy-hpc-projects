Project Summary

The project aims at responding to the health life-saving needs across Sudan by providing access to regular malnutrition screenings, paediatric primary / secondary health services to children aged 0-14, and maternal and reproductive health (SRH) activities. It will take place in Mayo area (Jabal Aulia district, Khartoum), Port Sudan (Red Sea State) and Nyala (South Darfur).
In Mayo, EMERGENCY is running a primary health centre (PHC) which provides paediatric services to children under the age of 14, as well as maternal and reproductive health (SRH) assistance to women living in the catchment area of the facility. The Centre is working five days per week and is composed of an observation ward, a pharmacy, a laboratory, an isolation room, a vaccination area, and a midwifery room. In Mayo, EMERGENCY intends to strengthen the referral services with therapeutic feeding centres (TFC) for cases of severe malnourished children (SAM), and with higher-level health facilities for children with medical complications. Additionally, EMERGENCY will ensure maternal health care, addressed to women in reproductive age living in Mayo area, through a comprehensive set of reproductive health services such as ante/post-natal care and family planning.
The Paediatric Centres in Port Sudan and Nyala are primary health facilities with integrated secondary health services. Both hospitals have a triage department, outpatient rooms for consultations, follow-up and auxiliary services such as laboratory and pharmacy departments. Patients with medical complications and severely malnourished children will be hospitalised in the wards, each of them with 18 beds available. Patients that are out of the Centre’s admission and treatment criteria will be stabilised and referred through an ambulance service to public facilities and therapeutic feeding centres (TFC). The provision of maternal and reproductive healthcare assistance will be kicked off in Port Sudan throughout the course of 2021.
In order to contain the spread of COVID-19 and ensure continuity of health care, EMERGENCY has introduced a set of preventive and control measures within its health facilities since March 2020. Such measures have been comprised in formal protocols and they include the set-up of isolation spaces either outside (isolation tents) or inside (dedicated rooms) the premises for suspected COVID-19 cases; the screening of adults and children at the entrance in pre-triage areas, according to MoH scoring; installation of additional hand-washing points; and a strict compartmentalization of the whole facilities (e.g. reorganisation of patients’ flows; wards to be re-designed to allow enough space between beds). These preventive measures are shared with patients visiting the clinic, to ensure accountability and compliance. Additionally, medical and non-medical personnel receive regular training sessions and refreshers on infection control and prevention based on internal, national and international protocols. The procurement of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) has been ensured; both patients and staff members are informed on their correct use according to WHO dedicated protocols. All COVID-19 IPC measures and risk communication activities undertaken within EMERGENCY facilities can be shared with CSO partners, as well as with local and national authorities, to maximize capitalization of good practices in view of a gradual handover and to tackle any potential outbreaks in the future. Such information sharing and knowledge transfer can render health systems more resilient and prepared to cope with COVID-19 potential next wave(s) and other outbreaks of endemic diseases.

1 - Needs

Sudan is expected to remain in the grip of a protracted humanitarian crisis in 2021 due to the cumulative effects of the economic crisis, food insecurity, health emergencies (COVID-19 and endemic disease outbreaks), conflicts and flooding all of which have destroyed livelihoods and access to basic services. The current drivers will negatively affect people’s living conditions, especially women and children. While some processes, including implementation of the Juba Peace Agreement, are expected to offer new opportunities to improve people’s lives and reduce vulnerability, this may take time to materialize. This is particularly the case for improvements in the economic situation, availability of government-provided basic services, and a reduction of pressures placed on communities hosting IDPs and refugees.This status of large protracted humanitarian crisis has a significant impact on the already fragile health systems’ capacity to respond to the needs of the people and the multiple emergencies affecting the country. Health needs are constantly exacerbated by the deteriorating economy, conflict, civil unrest, floods, and disease outbreaks. This assessment lead to WHO’s classification of grade 2 acute health emergency in September 2020. According to data gathered in need assessments and meetings held with health ministries and local authorities in Khartoum, Red Sea and South Darfur, major gaps have been identified both in the provision of paediatric, nutrition and SRH services, as well as in the availability of comprehensive data sets. Recent macro-economic and social shocks are making the population increasingly vulnerable and forcing the local communities to adopt harmful coping mechanisms, such as reduction in health expenses, to withstand shocks. Currently, paediatric and maternal health services are critical, including the provision of essentials drugs, vaccinations as per the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI), malnutrition screenings and referrals. Further, health and nutrition education activities are needed to promote positive behaviours that can reduce malnutrition and the risk of vector-borne and waterborne diseases.The nutrition situation in Sudan is currently characterized by persistent high levels of household food insecurity and undernutrition, as well as of child stunting and national prevalence rate of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM). According to data available within the latest S3M II (2018), 36.6 % of children less than five years of age are stunted (short for age). The national prevalence rate of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) was recorded at 14.1 %. There has been substantial attention to the provision of treatment services in conflict-affected states over the years. However, most Sudan’s acutely malnourished children (52 %) live in nine non-conflict affected states. This has been confirmed by official documentation and statistics collected at our centers in Khartoum, Red Sea and South Darfur. All above mentioned areas are experiencing a concomitant rapid urbanization, with an impact on nutrition levels and inclusive economic growth. On average, only 15.1 % of between 6 and 23 months receive a minimum acceptable diet, with stark disparities between Eastern and Northern States. The high prevalence of poor hygiene practices, including open defecation, inadequate treatment and storage of drinking water, and suboptimal hygiene practices, contribute to the high incidence of diarrhoeal diseases and malnutrition among children. Only 1/3 of households in Red Sea have access to improved water sources, a problem which is reflecting also in Nyala and in such area of Khartoum State as Mayo. From the Mayo area, EMERGENCY is confirming a number of deficiencies, such as absence of infrastructures, unpaved roads, partial electricity cover, open defecation practices and overcrowded makeshift shelters as accomodation. Hygienic and nutritional conditions keep deteriorating due to the lack of proper waste management.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Children from under the age of 14 have access to primary and secondary health services. The beneficiaries will be girls and boys under the age of 14. Outpatient activities will include malnutrition screening through MUAC checks, triage, visits and provision of medications. The laboratory service for diagnostic tests will be available for all patients. Patients classified as severely malnourished without medical complications will be referred to TFC. Community Health Promoters (CHPs) will make sure to follow-up with them once they are discharged from the hospital/feeding centres. EMERGENCY will also provide immunization services to children in order to boost their immunity against public health preventable diseases. 2. Guaranteed access to maternal and reproductive health services (antenatal, post-natal care, and family planning services) in Port Sudan Hospital, as well as maintained within Mayo Clinic. EMERGENCY will target Pregnant and Lactating women (PLW) through the provision of comprehensive Antenatal Care (ANC) services. Nutritional supplementation, prevention of endemic diseases (i.e. anaemia due to warms) and contraception will be prescribed and distributed free of charge to women. Referrals will be available for women in need of emergency obstetric care. Laboratory services for pregnant women will be available with regard to STDs, and provision of TT (Tetanus) vaccination will be guaranteed. Additionally, ultrasound for foetal heart rate measurement will be carried out. Post-natal care will be provided to all new-born and mothers at both facility and household levels. Women who receive ANC services will be advised on the importance of postnatal care for the health of their children. Family Planning (FP) services, including counselling, evaluation and provision of short and long-acting FP methods will be ensured. FP counselling at facility level will be addressed to pregnant and non-pregnant women in childbearing age and women who will request the service. 3. Carried out health education sessions and malnutrition screening activities. Health promotion aims to prevent diseases by means of advising communities on good health, hygiene and nutrition practices, and encouraging parents/caregivers to comply with prescribed treatments for their children and with scheduled follow-up consultations at the health facilities. They will focus on critical health areas such as communicable and non-communicable disease prevention, hygiene practices and nutrition. Specific messages will target pregnant PLW. The sessions will be held at the health facility level every day, twice per day. During the outreach session, EMERGENCY will carry out malnutrition screenings through MUAC checks and ensure referrals as per identified needs. Treatment and prevention of moderate malnutrition will be maintained in Mayo, where EMERGENCY has been carrying out cooking classes sessions to raise awareness to how to prepare healthy and nutritious food for children with affordable and locally available products. 4. Ensure equitable coverage and accessibility to quality referral services. EMERGENCY will strengthen its referral network in Mayo to provide response to the demand created from referrals at the primary level of care. In Port Sudan and Nyala, EMERGENCY will create a referral network with primary health facilities delivering services in hard to reach areas where referrals possibilities are not sufficiently available. 5. Trained health staff members. National staff working within EMERGENCY facilities will receive regular on-the-job and theoretical trainings on topics such as integrated management of childhood illnesses (IMCI), maternal and child health, disease surveillance and reporting, community management of acute malnutrition (CMAM), and IYCF.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Within all its facilities, EMERGENCY ensures proper medical waste management to protect patients, communities and the surrounding environment. This measure has been particularly strengthened over the current COVID-19 pandemic, with the adoption of specific protocols and workflows. EMERGENCY facilities aim at being innovative and environment-friendly from a design perspective: for instance, energy consumption are tentatively minimised by using heat mitigation ventilation and thermal solar panel plants for cooling the buildings.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The provision of high quality and free-of-charged essential health care is itself a way to strengthen the resilience of vulnerable communities by protecting them from adopting harmful coping strategies that increase their vulnerability to exploitation and abuses (especially women and children). Additionally, the presence of safe spaces and skilled staff members that are able to respond to health threats such as increased morbidity or potential outbreaks are also meant to enhance protection. Specifically, the presence of well-educated staff members is key to empower beneficiaries in claiming and exercise their rigths to be protected / receive adequate healthcare. AAP and PSEA policies implemented at both HQ and country levels are additional tools to maximise compliance with the Do No Harm principle and mainstream protection across the whole intervention.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

As of June 2020, a set of dedicated policies have been renewed and approved at HQ level to ensure maximum accountability, ethical resource management, and continuous improvement of provided services. The policies deal with cost allocation methodologies, fraud handling, HR management and ethical recruitment, whistleblowing and PSEA. A Compliance Officer and Policy Implementation Manager are working towards bridging HQ and country levels with regard to the implementation processes (e.g. by means of adapting specific SOPs for each project). Dedicated committees are already in place to handle complaints. The setup of a dedicated hotline for registering complaints, the construction of complaint and response boxes (to be placed in areas of transit with enough space to ensure privacy for the complainers), and the selection of a national complaint focal point in charge of reading the messages / confidentially handling complaints are currently underway with an ad-hoc roadmap. In order to ensure long-term sustainability, the whole intervention will also focus on capacity building of national health staff. Health and nutrition promotion sessions in the facilities and in outreach will ensure long-lasting behavioural changes at local level and boost community engagement in building socially and culturally appropriate practices to improve beneficiaries’ general health. EMERGENCY remains committed to an implementation strategy that maximizes community engagement and acceptance at all stages of the project. As per standard practice, EMERGENCY has introduced the main project objectives and the implementation strategy to local stakeholders, e.g. local authorities. The health promotion activities were adjusted according to the feedback received from the beneficiaries. EMERGENCY adopted a participatory approach to tailor and prepare the training to the team of community health promoters (CHPs). EMERGENCY aims to ensure that the different needs of women, men, girls and boys are addressed appropriately through the activities implemented. As such, general and COVID-19-related health promotion sessions were implemented in a gender-sensitive manner e.g. being held by female health promoters, focusing on household hygiene and child nutrition during the pandemic, etc. Within all Paediatric Centres, separate spaces for toilettes, as well as private rooms for consultations and laboratory testing are ensured to make sure women and children receive confidential treatment as per needs. Both female and male health care providers are trained on using gender-sensitive protocol for counselling and on avoiding discrimination based on sex, age, etc. Data collection and use are made gender-sensitive by ensuring confidentiality of hospital and patient record as well as using sex-disaggregated data for health facility planning.
