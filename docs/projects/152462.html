Needs

According to the 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview, some 1,697,000 women, men and children will not have adequate shelter and NFIs in 2019. In addition, some 300,000 refugees seeking shelter in South Sudan will face emergency shelter and NFI challenges during the year. The number of people in need represents a 6 per cent from the previous year. Amongst the IDP population, women and children will remain especially vulnerable both due to their specific vulnerabilities and to the protection risks inherent in being without shelter or social support networks. Furthermore, a number of host community members do not have sufficient ES/NFIs as they have used their already limited resources to provide for the displaced. People’s ES/NFI needs are primarily linked to conflict-induced displacement, but at times also to food insecurity, economic deterioration and disease outbreaks. This is particularly the case for IDPs who have been subjected to multiple displacement. Any new outbreaks of violence are expected to discourage or prevent people from moving out of camp-like settings in large numbers. One noticeable trend since the conflict broke out in 2013 is how multi-displaced people tend to move along distinguishable routes, along which they have grown accustomed to the environment and receiving humanitarian services at certain points along the way. By understanding where host communities have provided support and where other services are available, the need for NFIs may decrease. At the same time, the number of returnees from neighboring countries is expected to increase in 2019, based on 2018 trends. Returnees’ needs include training in utilizing natural resources and participating in the local economy. Additionally, returnees need be supported in understanding their housing, land, and property rights in cases their land has been given away or reused. Therefore, NFI and Shelter needs must carefully be considered so as to respond to the varying needs, vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups of the society, considering differences in age, gender and disability, as well as the global do no harm. Constant displacement and insecurity prevents people from having consistent access to key NFIs. Emergency distributions of these items to the most vulnerable are key elements in emergency responses. In Raja County, displacement and difficult road conditions made the NFI assistance complicated. SI is planning to conduct further assessments in the northern and western axes of the county to assess the needs related to each sector.

Activities

Emergency S/NFI intervention:• SI will conduct assessments to identify the needs and design the shelter/NFI intervention. SI will assess the most remote areas of Raja County which have not received assistance (Northern and Western axes – Timsah and Boro Medina) and Fashoda, Baliet and Mvolo counties in order to provide emergency shelter and life-saving NFIs. • In Raja and Aburoc, where SI has a static presence, and thanks to the multi-sectoral assessments, SI will ensure the synergy between the emergency shelter and NFI activities with its WaSH and FSL interventions already in place. This multi sectoral targeting will ensure the complementarity between projects, especially in newly assessed areas in Raja County.• The selection criteria will be based on the recommendations of the sector, and discussed and validated with the community representatives to ensure the participation of the community in the design of the response. The list of beneficiaries will then be double checked by SI through household surveys to make sure that only the most vulnerable are selected by the project. Specific vulnerabilities will be taken into account, and dedicated support will be provided to the most vulnerable beneficiaries (dedicated line of distribution, hiring of carpenters to help in the setup of the shelter). Specific trainings will be provided to the beneficiaries to ensure that they are notably able to build their shelter, and to sensitize them to make sure that the Do No Harm principle is taken into account.• The NFI and shelter distributions will be done through in kind or in cash. The choice of modality will be done in synergy with the other invention in the area, and based on SI’s modality of intervention matrix. • SI will engage the community in a way that stimulates active participation through a full integration from the design of the project to the exit. Community leadership in the implementation of the project will allow communities to play an active role in the success of the project and will allow to ease a potential exit strategy at the end of the intervention, as SI will be able to handover the management to the communities. • SI will empower communities on monitoring, as Project Management Committees (PMC) will be formed to follow-up the good implementation and comprehension of the project. These PMCs will be trained by SI. SI will also conduct a post-distribution monitoring and KAP surveys to measure the level of satisfaction at different times of the project and adjust its intervention according to the feedback received.• SI will ensure the mainstreaming of GBV throughout all stages of the S/NFI response in order to prevent gender related risks. SI will consult women through women focus group discussions which will enable SI to adapt its intervention to women concerns. In every focus group discussions and committees formed under the project, SI will ensure that women will be empowered in the decision making and leadership.• SI will mainstream HLP and conflict sensitive approaches throughout all stages of S/NFI response.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

SI will liaise with community leaders and elders to get information about the location of beneficiaries to reach and inform about the assistance provided. Furthermore, consultations will be held through focus group discussions with women, men, girls and boys from the community itself to ensure that they are aware of the upcoming intervention and to ensure full integration of all groups without any abuse of power from local chiefs. The focus is to understand their needs and to let them know about all the components or the projects and their expected involvement. Finally, SI will publish the list of beneficiaries and will set up a complaint mechanism.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

SI provides accessible and timely information to affected populations on organizational procedures, structures and processes that affect them to ensure that they can make informed decisions and choices, and facilitate a dialogue between the organization and affected populations over information provision. SI will always include the most vulnerable in its messages (i.e. women, children, elderlies and disabled). For instance, on trainings on shelter construction, SI will work with local population and form committees to ensure that the messages are properly transmitted in the local dialect.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

SI is determined to prevent the sexual exploitation and abuse of beneficiaries by humanitarian workers and reaffirms its goal of achieving full implementation of the six core principles adopted in 2002 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee Task Force on Prevention and Response to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). The present PSEA Policy complements SI’s Code of Conduct and Protection Policy which both state that it is strictly forbidden for any employee to use its position to obtain any favor, including sexual favors, and to have sexual intercourse with minors. A Whistleblowing Policy and a feedback and complaints response mechanisms framework are also key pillars of SI’s policies package which allows any SI worker to report any misconduct at headquarters level. More concretely, the gender segregated focus group discussions conducted at the design phase of the project will allow SI to have a good understanding of the concerns of women and what they perceive as a threat in order to implement a contextualized do no harm intervention.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

SI will allow affected populations to play an active role in the decision-making processes that affect them through the establishment of clear guidelines and practices to engage them appropriately and ensure that the most marginalized and affected are represented and have influence. SI ensures consultations are widely done while appreciating diversity among different population. For example, consultations are done with people with disabilities, women, girls, boys, men and religious groups when necessary. Moreover, SI will conduct gender segregated focus groups discussions and set up a complaint mechanism.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Along with its policy on accountability, SI will seek the views of affected populations to improve policies and practices in programming, therefore a feedback and complaints response mechanism will be streamlined, appropriate and robust enough to deal with (communicate, receive, process, respond to and learn from) complaints about breaches in policy and stakeholder dissatisfaction. As an example, SI will set up a complaint desk during distributions.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Based on its broad monitoring and evaluation framework, SI ensures that monitoring and evaluation of the goals and objectives of programs involves the affected populations. This enables SI to improve its humanitarian assistance deliveries to be of quality and address the needs of the affected populations. For example, following the involvement of the communities on the orientation of the projects, SI will form Project Management Committees so that the communities are also part of the implementation process of the project in order to ensure that communities themselves will follow-up the progress on activities. Furthermore, focus group discussions including vulnerable people will be conducted in order to evaluate the project and its inclusion on gender and age groups.

Centrality of Protection in humanitarian action
Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In order to mainstream protection, SI strives to respect the Do No Harm principle. To that extent, SI initiates a protection risk analysis before and during the implementation of the project. The aim is to have a participatory methodology which ensures that the aid recipients (especially the most vulnerable i.e. disabled people, elderlies, women and children) are at the heart of the consultation process, and can voice their protection needs and risks. SI identifies the issues, their nature, their actors and their motivations in order to implement the project in the best way possible.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Assistance will be provided through meaningful access to impartial services to beneficiaries, while paying attention to the context and sociocultural norms. For example, consultations will be done with people with disabilities, elderlies, women and all these vulnerable groups will be represented in the distributions committees. These consultations will limit the risks created or heightened by aid. SI will work to prevent and minimize any unintended negative effects of the intervention (the Do No Harm principle), and work towards equality, impartiality, accountability, participation and empowerment. This will promote safety and dignity for the beneficiaries.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

In its program design and activity selection, SI sought to minimize the protection risks during the implementation stage. To that extent, during the initial assessments, SI ensures protection aspects are assessed to make sure that its interventions will not endanger lives of the beneficiaries. Furthermore, the Complaints Response Mechanism that will be put in place during the implementation will help SI to readjust its practices during the intervention and functions as a real-time monitoring tool. When choosing beneficiaries, the vulnerability to threats of different population sub-groups such as people with disabilities, elderlies, women and youth – exposed to different types of threats – is considered, together with their needs. Finally, every NFI distribution will include protection messaging.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

In order to ensure the access of beneficiaries, SI will make sure that they are protected during the distributions from their specific vulnerabilities. During its monitoring, SI seek to monitor the number or percentage of population declaring having challenges in accessing the humanitarian assistance. In addition, SI, in its monitoring ensures to understand the type of access challenges or barriers beneficiaries might be facing while trying to reach assistance. These data inform the modifications or strategies that SI can put in place in consultation with the beneficiaries to address the access challenges. Post distribution monitoring’s will measure the level of satisfaction on access related matters at different times of the project.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

SI ensures that its intervention will not put beneficiaries at risk. This is done by conducting risk analysis on shelter infrastructures. During NFI distributions, SI will consider the distance from the accommodation to the distribution site and ensure that beneficiaries reach their home before dark. Complaint desks on site will also allow SI to adjust its activities according to the beneficiaries’ feedback. Finally, post-distribution monitoring will measure the level of satisfaction on safety related matters at different times of the project.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

As SI always strives to respect the Do No Harm principle, this project will respect the dignity of beneficiaries. In order to do that, SI will always make sure that all negative effects of the intervention such as sexual assault or the distance of distribution points from the accommodation of beneficiaries. Knowledge, aptitudes and practices surveys and satisfaction surveys will be conducted to help SI measuring the satisfaction of beneficiaries.
