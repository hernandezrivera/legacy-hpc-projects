Project Summary

The Community Engagement Strengthening Project (CESP) is a response to the growing number of community-based organizations (CBOs) and local non-governmental organizations (LNGOs) across Syria, which have recently emerged in response to the Syrian crisis. In line with the global humanitarian community’s efforts around meaningful localization, CBOs and LNGOs are taking on prominent roles in humanitarian and early-recovery programming through design, targeting, leading consultation, and overseeing implementation. These community actors play a significant role in restoring confidence, sustaining the momentum of recovery and transformation and bringing divided communities together around activities that represent a mutual benefit.
CESP aims to strengthen community platforms that are inclusive, accountable, and responsive to community needs and aspirations. CESP builds on AKF’s early successful pilots through a three-step results-based implementation process of ‘Form, Build, and Action’. CESP will:
• Form/reform (8) new CBOs, 2 in Damascus, 1 in Rural Damascus and 5 in Hama governorate.
• Build the capacity of 12 newly formed CBOs and existing LNGOs.
• And through a qualified process, provide (20) community small grants (CSGs) to CBOs and LNGOs (10) in Damascus and Rural Damascus and 10 in Hama governorate to identify, design, implement and monitor community-based humanitarian and early recovery projects which meet immediate community needs.
AKF, through its Do No Harm approach, has cultivated a deeper understanding of prevailing authorities, power structures, and possible risks to beneficiaries and project staff. AKF will actively mitigate the unintended consequences of targeting strategies, sectarian fragilities, promote transparency and ensure broad community participation. CESP will ensure a gender-inclusive approach to these activities by ensuring:
• CBOs formed with adequate representation of women and adolescent girls.
• Organizational capacity assessments of CBOs/LNGOs designed to assess the understanding, knowledge and skills that members have on gender equality and women’s empowerment
• And community-based consulting groups for the award of CSGs have adequate representation of women and adolescent girls. CESP incorporates evidence-based design and implementation and continual beneficiary feedback loops. The project utilizes comprehensive evidence-gathering mechanisms which prioritize primary evidence gathering to determine relevance, impact, safety and efficiency of project design and implementation.

4 - Needs

Early Recovery and Livelihood (ERL) needs remain large in scale, severity, and complexity across the country, with an estimated 11.6 million people (51% Female) needing dignifying ERL support (HNO 2021). Before the pandemic, 41.9 % of households could cover their expenses. Currently, 9.7 % can do so (UN Inter-Agency 2020 Covid-19 Socio-Economic Impact Assessment). This is worsened by lack of chances to attain employable skills and working capital. About two-thirds of households lost at least one income sources, and about half lost 75 % to 100 % of their income.This contributed to eroding socioeconomic foundations of the Syrian people, affecting their ability to sustain adequate living standards, seek decent livelihood opportunities, and access social services and essential infrastructure.This has led to more strains on social cohesion and community security, as well as stresses affecting the social capital of Syrian communities.Due to multiple challenges placing pressure on local communities, there are gaps in social services uniquely municipal work such as solid waste removal.Salamieh District has a population of 318,163 people of whom (54%) female, 36% are PwD, 9% are IDPs and 0.40% are returnees. As per AKF 2020 Rapid Need Assessment in Salamieh District, a total of 37,261 households/162,995 individuals (50,35% females) are vulnerable.Today, the humanitarian landscape in Syria is more diverse with LNGOs working with the UN system and INGOs across the country to address challenges. These LNGOs are often connected in a sub-contracting relationship and unable to fully mature since donors aim to partner with INGOs. Meaningful localization in a protracted crisis requires building up community platforms within Syria.

5 - Implementation of activities

CESP adopts a three-step results-based implementation process of ‘Form, Build, and Action’:FORM – Increased formation of inclusive community-based organizations (CBOs) which involves community context analysis, stakeholder consultations, community consultation group establishment, general awareness building and selection of a CBO leadership body, with emphasis on gender and age. 8 CBOs will be formed with an inclusive gender and age-sensitive leadership body of 13 individuals (30% F) per CBO; including IDPs, residents, women, youth, elderly and PwDs.BUILD – CESP will support 8 new CBOs and 4 LNGOs through an organizational capacity building program reaching 144 members (at least 44F; 100M). Training topics cover planning and implementing humanitarian and early recovery programming; participatory rural appraisal and rapid assessments; mobilizing and empowering communities; communications and conflict management/resolution; gender equality and women’s empowerment, organizational management; and inclusive governance. Organizational Capacity Assessments of CBOs and LNGOs, including gender equality, will be conducted to determine training needs.ACTION – CBOs/LNGOs will mobilize communities to identify, design, implement and monitor community-based humanitarian and early recovery projects to meet community needs. 20 CSGs will be provided with 20 Community Small Grants (CSOs) through an open call for proposals. Community-based consulting groups will be established select projects to receive CSG support. CSGs include the establishment of community parks, awareness campaigns around GBV prevention, community-wide arts and culture activities, increasing street safety by solar lighting, and community psychosocial support events.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

AKF staff and civil society organizations may face pressure or threats from community members to get benefits and/or control over the project.likelihood: medium, impact: medium.AKF will establish a strong linkage with local leaders who influence community members and closely monitor project implementation to identify potential threats and take immediate response if staff or the local community are exposed to harm. CBO/LNGO members may be accused of bias in selecting beneficiaries or defining community needs.Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium.AKF will oversee the selection process and ensure a precise implementation based on the selection criteria. Regular and confidential monitoring and feedback/compliant reporting system will help identify cases of exploitations and take actions against them. AKF and the consultation groups will assess the proposals to ensure relevance to community needs and ensure neutrality in selecting beneficiaries.AKF staff might be accused of bias in selecting projects to receive CSGs.Likelihood: medium, Impact: low. AKF will ensure the inclusion of all stakeholders (women, men, youth, local leaders) in developing the selection criteria.Gender-related cultural and traditional norms and roles may discriminate/limit women’s participation in the CBO/LNGO or the small grants/community initiatives.Likelihood: Medium, Impact: Medium. AKF will explain and ensure women representation in CBO/LNGOs and promote their active involvement during the formation and implementation phase (at least 30 % females). Women will be asked preference of specific work/task to ensure their comfort. AKF will facilitate gender equality awareness-raising to facilitate the positive interaction of female participants.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: The deterioration of the security situation limiting movement of staff and beneficiaries.Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors the regional and local security situation using a variety of primary and secondary sources. If the mobility of staff and beneficiaries in rural areas within the Salamieh District is deemed high risk, AKF will delay the implementation of activities until the situation can be reassessed and determined safe for travel and implementation. Risk 2: High pressure from local communities to respond to their increasing needs with requests to expand small grants reach might lead to communities’ dissatisfaction. Likelihood: medium, impact: medium. Mitigation: AKF will openly communicate key messages on available funding to cultivate trust-building between AKF, CBOs/LNGOs and local communities.Risk 3: Inflation and exchange rate fluctuations may affect the level of funds available at the field level. Likelihood: High, Impact: low. Mitigation: AKF regularly monitors exchange rate gains and losses to consider the impact of fluctuations on the project and revise as needed. Inflation risk is mitigated by managing the amount of project liquid cash residing in the country. AKF manages reserve amounts by taking into consideration future cash flow needs to ensure cash on hand in the field is minimally exposed to inflation.Risk4: Lack of adherence on the part of MOSAL to deliver required permits results in either delayed or protracted approval processes for the legalization of CBO/LNGO projects. Mitigation: AKF will cooperate with CBOs/LNGOs to adjust implementation timelines till approvals are secured otherwise will have to allocate the fund for other priority projects of CSGs.
