Project Summary

LWFs Shelter and NFI project is targeting protracted conflict and displacement affected most vulnerable IDPs, and Returnees who have been displaced due to a protracted crisis over the past one year or more years , compounded by inter-clan, inter-tribal violence and cattle raiding. LWF planned to reach 1700 HHs ( about 10,200 people) from IDPs, and Returnees communities in Duk and Uror counties through provision of emergency Shelter and NFI kit for life saving.
The beneficiaries’ selection can be done through community participation and lead of community based Cash Committees (CDCS). The process involves identification, registration and verification of beneficiaries. To ensure complains are responded timely and properly there will be complain response committees at each payam and county level.
The project will address the shelter building through conditional cash modality to build Tukuls and restricted cash transfer modality for fulfilling HHs Emergency NFI Needs. The shelter component will be addressed through conditional cash transfers to beneficiary HHs in one round, the cash transfer can be made after single HHs prepared construction site ( clearing the ground and laying foundation ) and the continuous monitoring will be done on building of Tukul. The NFI component can be addressed through vouchers, by having agreement with local venders to supply identified items (restricted cash) and the beneficiaries can collected the items from venders.
After each cash distribution, there will be Post Cash Distribution Monitoring (PDM) that can inform future action and help to take corrective measures on ongoing program too.

Needs

The South has been faced with countless challenges, some of which arise from historical marginalisation, poverty and under-development, while others are driven by more recent events, including the protracted conflict and conflict which began in December 2013 between SPLM_IG and SPLM-IO. Peace Agreement on the Resolution of the Conflict in the Republic of South Sudan was signed in August 2015 and Transitional Government formed in April 2016; however the clash between SPLA-IG and SPLA-IO in Juba in July 2016 has resulted in various consequences throughout the country. Due to the compounding effects of widespread violence and instability, agricultural production reduced tremendously, the macro‐economy is deteriorated, hyperinflation realized (According to South Sudan Bureau of Statistic, the cereal price increase more that 600% since 2017 and still the inflation is not managed) and the shortage of foreign currency has eroded people’s purchasing power, especially that of the most vulnerable, curtailing their ability to access food for consumption through the markets. Jonglei is hosting the second highest IDP numbers, estimated at 378,821 people. Furthermore, the September 2018 IPC classified indicate that 62.5% of the population in Jonglei are in emergency, crisis and catastrophe level. For instance 535,000 people are in IPC 2 - stress, 810,000 are in IPC 3 - crisis, 275,000 are IPC 4 -emergency, 5000 are in IPC 5 catastrophe. Twic East, Bor South and Duk counties felt crisis (IPC 3); Twic East fall will fall under emergency IPC 4 by March 2019. There are an estimated 53,033 IDPs in Twic East and Duk alone in addition to those under the UN Protection of Civilian (POC) in Bor town. Many households in Bor South (Jalle), Duk and Twic East depend on gathering wild foods, borrowing and catching fish as their main source of survival including dependence on humanitarian assistance for their survivalThere are an estimated 54833 IDPs in Uror and Duk alone. The ongoing violence associated with the current crisis exacerbates competition between the state’s over 1.7 million pre-war inhabitants and new IDPs for access to common pasturelands, especially during years of drought. The 2018 figure from Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC), indicates that in Duk County; a total of 26033 IDPs individuals mainly women and children (boys and girls), and the elderly i.e 4,339 households. Most of the IDPs were from Uror, and Ayod Counties of Jonglei State, and from within the deserted Payams of Duk County i.e the Payuel payam which was attacked by trial raider in November 2018, were over 40 lives were lost. According to the assessment report by interagency in 2017, the influx of the IDPs was majorly due to a clash between the government and opposition forces in Uror County. Other reasons were cattle raiding and hunger. Similarly Due clash between government and Opposition forces in 2017 , Uror county (which was under opposition control from December 2013 to February 2017), divide into two , the western part that included three payams ( Yuai, Padeik, and Wiekol) fall under government and the Eastern parts includes 3 Payams (Pathai, Peri and Motot) are under opposition. Due to the clash the people moved to Duk and Akobo areas. The displaced and returnees HHS have limited access to basic shelter and other households’ materials. Women and children are more exposed to risks of rain, heat and mosquitoes which could escalate to more vulnerability. The project is to ensure households improve their shelter to meet the basic requirement using local materials like grass and poles and grasses and Basic NFIS. Therefore this project proposed to provide Shelter for new arrived Returnees, IDPS and NFI support to most vulnerable IDPS, Returnees, Elderly, disabled, child headed, Women head HHS etc. The process will lead by community level screening and NFI/shelter material and Cash distribution committee. Activities

• Conduct a joint Rapid Need Assessment to identify and register beneficiaries with other stakeholders including Government: The assessment will be made with cluster partners and stakeholders in the project areas to validate existing information and to have update date information for upcoming responses and prioritization with in intervention counties. • Conduct Rapid Market Assessment (RAM) for Shelter and NFI: The RAM is to be conducted by LWF technical team and support of external expert to validate existing RAM report, it is update of the market information based on past reports• Form and Train CDC (Cash Distribution Committees): In each payam on intervention Cash Committees comprising of nine members (including women, youth representative, chiefs representative, among others) will be formed and trained on beneficiaries selection , and registration , cash program and cash disbursement methods (direct cash, and vouchers) and their roles. In addition to Cash committee complain Response committee will be formed and trained to handle complains. • lifesaving NFIs assistant to most vulnerable 1500 HHS from IDPs and Returnees through conditional cash provision (Restricted cash): The assistant focus to provide basic NFI kits through cash based modalities to selected and most vulnerable households. • Lifesaving Shelter assistance to most vulnerable 1500 HHS from IDPs and Returnees to build Shelter (Tukuls) through conditional cash provision (Restricted cash): The assistance will focus to provide the most vulnerable female, child and elderly headed IDP, and returnee a in Duk and Uror, who lack access to safe shelters, with one round cash payment for accessing the materials to meet their unique shelter needs. • Training of 3600 HHS holds on life skills and shelter building: Beneficiaries and community stakeholders will participate in six training sessions to discuss optimal shelter design, identify active vendors with sufficient shelter materials, identify available labour and set fair prices for materials. All shelters will be built in accordance with SPHERE standards and include protection mechanisms such as doors with locks. In addition, 1000 IDP and returnee households will receive unrestricted cash for the purchase of the most urgently needed NFIs from selected vendors• Monitoring of construction of Tukuls: This is step by step follow up an advisors support with CDC and stakeholders to enforce the beneficiary build the tukuls and finalize. The Cash disbursement will start when the beneficiary identified the building site and lay foundation (clearing and measuring), then the construction of the shelter at individual beneficiary level will be monitored and reported. • Post Cash Distribution Monitoring and Reporting: This monitoring will be conducted from 2weeks of cash disbursement. Relevant questionnaires developed for shelter and NFI response and used. The date analysed and document to see the lesson, challenges and success in cash programming. Cash-Based Programming Approach:Over the past 5 years LWF South Sudan has effectively utilized cash-based programming to provide humanitarian assistance to over 200,000 people in opposition held and difficult to access counties, such as Ayod and Uror. The main focus of the cash modality was increasing food consumption, support of urgent basic needs, Shelter (Tukul building by returnees, IDPS and disaster affected Host community ) and more recently Sanitation and hygiene services. Cash-based programming has been selected as the primary modality for this proposed project as it enables individuals to play a key role in determining how to fulfil their most urgent needs and is more feasible in areas where the movement of physical goods is challenging. As a result of its long-standing engagement in the proposed counties,LWF South Sudan has a solid understanding of local market capacity in the target areas, and a proven local vendor selection process which is based on stakeholders

Briefly describe how
