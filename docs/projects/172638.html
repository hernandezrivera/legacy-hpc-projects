Project Summary

The proposed actions have been designed as a part of an overall Integrated Program aiming to respond to WASH, Health and Protection needs for the Southern and Western of Libya, targeting Sebha, Murzuq and, Tripoli municipalities.
- The WASH intervention will be focusing on providing the most vulnerable groups in the areas such as IDPs, Refugees, Migrants, women and children by ensuring a clean environment with regular disinfection in four schools and health facilities; regular provision of PPE to medical staff and by conducting awareness raising campaign on handwashing, Covid-19 and IPC, as main topics. In addition, this project is targeting urban communities, schools and health facilities with safe and dignified access to water and sanitation by rehabilitating the WASH facilities and infrastructures. Heath facilities will receive shredders and sterilizers to ensure safe management of COVID-19 infectious waste, while children in schools will benefit from hygiene kits designed using participatory approach. At the same time, to improve the capacity of Government and Municipalities to response to acute needs, IPC capacity building trainings will be organized and WASH spare parts will be provided to ensure operation, maintenance and durability of the proposed water and waste-water infrastructures.
- The health components (separated HRP proposal submitted to the Health Sector) will be focusing mainly on the rehabilitation of primary and secondary public health facilities by upgrading their basic services through the supply of the needed equipment, commodities and/or refurbishment and strengthening the capacity of the health care facility staff.
- The protection components (separated HRP proposal submitted to the Protection Sector) integrated into above responses will enhance protection through safe identification and referral of people in need of specialized protection and multi-sectoral services, based on outreach to individuals, households and community levels focusing on vulnerable groups such as children, women, PWSN.

1 - Needs

84,726 people in need including 22955 migrants; 34957 IDPs; 5752 Returnees; 13924 Non displaced and 7138 of Refugees, living in Tripoli, Sebha and Murzuq municipalities, areas that are facing critical challenges in WASH services. The following needs have been highlighted: poor capacity building of local service providers in delivery efficient services, including lack of a solid waste management system; deficiency of WASH facilities; limited access to safe water and sanitation facilities and basic hygiene items. As regards schools and health care facilities, poor availability of PPE, insufficiency of cleaning materials and shortage of technical supplies for operation & maintenance have been registered, as well as the need to train local and national authorities on IPC procedures. WASH facilities are important pillars for educational and health care facilities: if inadequate, diseases can spread among children, most of whom do not have access to safe and proper facilities, as per minimum SPHERE standard. Moreover, child friendly/girl friendly or universal inclusive design is not often taken into account. This has major consequences in terms of school frequency attendance for girls and children with disabilities. In general, lack of lighting, locks, privacy and sex-segregation increases the risk of GBV against girls. Regarding health care facilities, in the 2020 HNO it has been reported that around 24% of Libyans and 80% of migrants and refugees are facing challenges in accessing health services. Most of the HCFs in Libya do not reach the minimum SPHERE standards in terms of WASH requirements. Infectious waste is not being handled in safely, due to the absence of pedal-operated waste collection bins for waste segregation (infectious waste, sharp and normal), putting the cleaning workers, the municipality and also the community as a whole at high risk of COVID-19 infections. Power cuts are frequent in Southern Libya due to fuel shortages. This phenomenon often leads to the shut-down of schools and HCFs for several days. Few of these structures have photovoltaic panels as a secondary source of electricity.Many section of the wastewater network are regularly over-flooding, exposing the community at a high risk of public health threats. The over flooded areas can easily cause major diseases outbreaks like Malaria or Typhus. Moreover, it has been reported that 77% of children around Libya are suffering from diarrhea or other water borne diseases. Most of the inspection rooms of the Libyan wastewater system are clogged and in need of cleaning and rehabilitation, while pumps present in the lifting chambers need maintenance to ensure good functioning of the system. Furthermore, many leakages are reported in the Libyan WASH public infrastructure, resulting in the malfunctioning of the public handwashing points and public toilets. To limit the spread of COVID-19 cases it is important that the community always has safe and guaranteed access to handwashing points.Targeted municipalities do not usually have the spare parts required for rehabilitation, operation and maintenance of water and sewage network.Among the targeted municipalities, identified actions will be prioritized according to the results of a multi-sectorial assessment conducted by WW-GVC at the beginning of the project. Indeed, GVC has developed a Community Protection Approach (CPA), that is a community engagement and empowerment instrument used to design Integrated Protection Programs (IPP).

2 - Activities

SO1: Provision of PPE to ensure the safety of health workers for C19 risk. Cleaning materials for disinfection/environmental cleaning will be distributed in schools and HCFs. Trainings on the correct use of PPE and cleaning materials, handwashing, and C19/IPC will be provided following WHO and UNICEF guidelines. SO2: Rehabilitation of the WASH public infrastructure in urban areas with a focus on handwashing points. For the sewage network, in coordination with the targeted municipalities, clogged inspection rooms and pumps for the lifting stations will be rehabilitated.In HCFs, pedal-operated waste collection bins will be distributed with color-coded waste segregation according to the 3-bin system. Shredders and sterilizers designed to minimize the volume of the produced garbage and to sterilize will be installed.Rehabilitation of the WASH facilities in schools and HCFs according to SPHERE standards to limit the spread of C19 (installation of tanks, rehabilitation/construction of toilets, handwashing stations etc.) will be undertaken, considering the following criteria: sex-segregated and adapted to people with special needs where needed; child and girl friendly. Lighting and locks to ensure safe access will be provided. Handwashing stations installed in critical key locations. Photovoltaic panels alimenting the pumps and the shredders will be installed in schools and HCFs.Hygiene kits, the content of which will be identified with a participatory approach, will be distributed, targeting mostly children in schools.SO3: Capacity building trainings focusing on IPC, safe reopening protocols and disinfection will be conducted to strengthen the competences of municipalities members and other local stakeholders.Targeted municipalities will be provided with spare parts needed for rehabilitation, O&M of water and sewage networks. A radio connection will be established between the muhalla leaders and the Municipality to facilitate the contact for any maintenance needed.

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

The project will be accountable to the affected population at each phase of the cycle project thanks to GVC’s Community Protection Approach (CPA; https://cpainitiative.org/). Population engagement is a process of empowerment, following the Ladder of Citizen Participation. Different AGD groups will be identified based on a sampling approach and classified according to a scale of prioritization. Attention will be paid to ensure that underrepresented groups are considered. A selection committee will be composed of the heads of targeted facilities, community representatives and GVC and its implementing partner Migrace staff to design the project. They will also be included in the design of the M&E tools. The beneficiaries will be included in the implementation of the activities (ToT of Community mobilizers; Personal from targeted facilities; FGDs during the implementation for potential readjustments etc.). A complaint mechanism will be put in place: 1) Complaint boxes made available in each targeted structure/community; 2) On the GVC project management platform, an online complaints form, in English and Arabic will be accessible. This platform has been developed in in compliance with the GDPR and the principles of PIM; 3) During the monitoring visits of the M&E officer, s/he will may fill complaints with beneficiaries (i.e. including PwD, people who are illiterate and marginalized groups). A mechanism for identification, assessment and referral of families and individuals ensures meaningful access to assistance and services. A final evaluation will measure the impact of the intervention. An exit strategy will assure the durability of the project and the re-appropriation by the beneficiaries and the stakeholders. At last, GVC Libya will pay attention to apply GVC Policy such as the Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy, PSEAH. A reporting mechanism for cases of violence and/or abuse will be set up.GVC also aims at using the Common Feedback Mechanism.
