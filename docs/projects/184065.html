Project Summary

Provision of financial and administrative support to the affected women, men, girls and boys, as well as disabled people in South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan, Gedaref and Kassala states. Main activities include Cash programing for food assistance and livestock’s; Cash vouchers for seeds/agricultural inputs, initial capital transfer in the form of in kind assistance to selected women and men; rehabilitation of market infrastructures strengthening; promoting community base management of natural resources and public services such as health facilities, education, and community centers; PSEA and GBV awareness sessions and dignity kits distribution.

1 - Needs

Last IPC data shows that an estimated 7.3 million people in Sudan (16% of the population analyzed) were in high levels of acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or above) between April and May 2021, and required urgent action. Of these, around 5.5 million people were classified in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) while around 1.8 million were critically food insecure classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). The most affected localities of Red Sea State (Halaib and Jubayt-el-maaadin) were classified in Emergency (IPC Phase 4). An increase in localized conflicts triggered population displacement, which, combined with the deterioration of the economy, led to higher than usual levels of acute food insecurity. As a result, the highest prevalence of population in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse were observed in North Darfur (25%), followed by West Darfur (22%), North Kordofan (20%), South Kordofan (20%), Gedarif (19%) and Central, East and South Darfur states, ranging from 17-18%. During the first projection period (June–September 2021), which coincided with the peak lean season, it was estimated that around 9.7 million people (21% of the population analyzed) will likely be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse. Compared to the same period of the June 2020 IPC analysis, there is a 1 percentage point increase in the prevalence of the population in Emergency and a 1 percentage point reduction in those in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis). Although the total population in Crisis and Emergency has remained similar (21%), the severity has increased. There is also a noticeable deterioration (5% of the population analyzed) to worse IPC Phase classifications, mainly attributable to the impacts of the lean season, tribal conflicts, diminished labor opportunities causing low purchasing power, high food prices as well as inflation. In the projection period of October 2021 to February 2022, corresponding with the harvest season, 1.3 million people will be in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), with 4.6 million people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse and over 15 million people in Stressed (IPC Phase 2). This marks a decrease of 34.5% (from 7.1 to 4.6 million) of people in IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) and 50.9% (from 2.7 to 1.3 million) of people in IPC Phase 4 (Emergency), compared to the just ended analysis period (June – September 2021). The localities of Geneina of West Darfur, Hala’ib and Jabait-el-ma’aadin of Red Sea state and Al Buram and Haiban of South Kordofan state are expected to move from Emergency (IPC Phase 4) in the first projection to IPC Phase 3 (Crisis) in the October 2021-February 2022 projection. However, 37 localities will be in Phase 3 in the October 2021-February 2022 projection. The main harvest and post-harvest seasons are expected to significantly improve access to food and income from own production, in-kind payments for agricultural labour, and in-kind support from relatives compared to the lean season (June to September). Likewise, income from agricultural labour and cash crops’ sale will support market purchases of food when staple food prices seasonally decrease. Intercommunal conflicts, high prices, low purchasing power and seasonal floods will remain the key drivers of food insecurity during the October 2021-February 2022 projection period. Conflict is likely to persist though both the harvest and post-harvest seasons in conflict-prone areas of Sudan. The inter-tribal conflict/tension likely to increase during harvest as livestock herders will start seasonal movement and likely to increase clashes along the animal routes. The rate of displacement is expected to decline but the same number of IDPs, as many IDPs will try to join the harvest season in their rural areas while some areas are likely to experience new displacements but with a medium-low rate.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Provision of financial and administrative support to the affected women, men, girls and boys, as well as disabled people in South Darfur, East Darfur, South Kordofan, Gedaref and Kassala states. Main activities include Cash programing for livelihoods activities and livestock’s; Cash vouchers for seeds/agricultural inputs, training on business skills development and IGAs; initial capital transfer in the form of in kind assistance to selected women and men; rehabilitation of market infrastructures strengthening; promoting community base management of natural resources and public services such as health facilities, education, and community centers; PSEA and GBV awareness sessions and dignity kits distribution.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

CARE promotes environmental friendly and risk avert mechanisms in its livelihoods diversification interventions as they do have impact on the health of the public, environment and natural resources. The project provides training to its implementing staff, and community leaders as the project implementation plans and progresses for environmental sensitive actions. Mainly, income generating activities, pasture land rehabilitation, livestock vaccination, and market initiation activities are discussed and agreed with each sector ministry experts.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

CARE uses different strategies in relation to the activities planned to be implemented: as women engage on income generating activities, their domestic work burden, mobility related risks, and access and control over resources are well considered. Engaging men and boys, creating awareness on PSEA and others are among mitigation measures planned. Protection at individual, family and community levels are what will help to address the risks associated to the project activities planned. A number of dialogue and discussion sessions are planned by the project on PSEA, SGBV, women in business and leadership.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

CARE has established a number of channels to receive feedback and complaints from women, men, boys, girls and others with special needs. Phone number, complaint box, complaint day or help desk, face-to-face focus group discussion, and home visit, as appropriate. All community groups will be oriented on the use and procedures on annual basis. Two community accountability focal persons will be selected from each target community (a woman and man) and trained to facilitate the process.The response will continue to strengthen the already existing systems for preventing and responding to acts of sexual exploitation and abuse. Protection, including protection from sexual harassment, exploitation, and abuse (PSHEA), will be mainstreamed in the response. Key implementing staff from CARE, national NGOs, and seconded government staff will receive protection and PSHEA training and develop action plans to mainstream throughout the response period.
