Project Summary

DRC proposes a 6-month project in camps 2W and 6, where 1 687 HHs will receive TSA 1, 3 671 HHs will receive Care and Maintenance packages, 254 will receive household-level site improvement and 15 200 pieces of bamboo will be treated. Moreover, 20 HHs will be engaged in participatory design of alternative shelter strengthening techniques, 3 750 HHs reached through communications for monsoon preparedness, and targeted camp blocks will be involved in validation of monitoring results. DRC's AAP approach centers community decision-making at each stage of the project cycle so that activities are tailored to HH needs. All activities will be coordinated with SMS, the Shelter focal point and camp authorities, including response to CFRM referrals. In addition to training staff in PSEA, DRC will maintain a PSEA focal point in each camp.
DRC will develop prioritization criteria for distribution of TSA I and Care and Maintenance materials in consultation with Protection community groups of different ages, genders and abilities. Porter support and/or construction labour assistance will be provided to households with special shelter needs based on assessments of the household's capacity to carry shelter materials from the distribution point to their shelter and use the materials as intended. DRC uses a points-based system that allows households to choose the quantity and type of non-essential TSA I and Care and Maintenance materials that they want. Households will be provided gender-segregated training about the purpose and proper use of items prior to receiving TSA I materials and being loaned DRC tools. DRC will offer technical support and daily monitoring to ensure appropriate use of items.
The TSA I package will align with Sector standards and include treated borak produced by DRC's treatment plant, which is located in a host community adjacent to the camps. The plant will benefit the host community through cash-for-work (CfW) opportunities as well as technical training. CfW selection will prioritize vulnerable individuals identified by DRC Protection, and accommodations will be made so that assigned tasks are accessible (e.g. working from home for women, supervision roles for the elderly).
Shelters sited in areas that are prone to flooding or waterlogging, or that have household members with physical disabilities will be provided household-level site improvement tailored to individual needs. Households will be included in design and implementation decisions including construction modality to encourage community ownership and increase technical knowledge.
Selected households living in mud shelters will be engaged in participatory development of alternative wall strengthening techniques, focusing on available local materials and vernacular construction knowledge. Community perception of and willingness to adopt techniques will be a central consideration in the development process. Results will be used to advocate for a pilot.
DRC Protection community groups of different ages, genders and abilities will be engaged in the development of tie-down kit communication strategies catering to the needs of each group. Using participatory mapping techniques, groups will identify communication channels, potential barriers to understanding or ability to implement, and behavioural triggers that can facilitate proper installation of tie-down kits. Groups will then roll out their communication strategies to their communities.
DRC will conduct post-distribution and post-construction monitoring of shelter activities completed in Phase 1 and Phase 2 at the beginning and ending of this project. Results will be shared through gender-segregated open dialogues with the community to validate findings and collect recommendations for project improvement.

1 - Identified Needs

Shelter needs are considerable in the Cox's Bazar camps given restrictions on use of durable materials, extreme weather conditions, and the predominance of self-built shelters. Lack of livelihoods in the camps limits households' financial capacity to repair damaged shelters themselves: according to the J-MSNA (October 2021), 36% of households with shelter needs remain unable to solve them. Shelter plots are precarious and prone to landslides, soil erosion and flooding due to steep slopes and loss of vegetative ground covering. Cox's Bazar experiences two cyclone seasons, a monsoon season, and a winter season that is prone to fires in the camps due to dense living conditions and lack of adequate space for cooking. Emergency shelter needs due to extreme weather events or fires were exceptionally high in 2021 due to COVID-19 restrictions that reduced monsoon preparedness activities. 84% of shelters in Camp 6 and 69% of shelters in Camp 2W received emergency shelter assistance in 2021 during the monsoon season with the majority of damages being due to high winds and weakened materials succumbing to the rain. Shelters in these two camps are particularly vulnerable to extreme weather since only 22% of shelters in Camp 2W and less than 50% of shelters in Camp 6 will have received TSA 1 assistance by the end of 2021. DRC conducts household assessments prior to shelter distributions to verify needs and allocate points for non-essential TSA 1 items and Care and Maintenance packages accordingly. Only shelters that have not previously received TSA 1 distributions will be targeted for TSA 1 in 2022. Care and Maintenance packages will be provided to shelters that have previously received TSA 1. The impact of damaged shelters falls disproportionately on women, children and people with disabilities who spend most of their time inside their shelters due to mobility challenges, security concerns, and cultural restrictions. 41% of people with disabilities reported not leaving their shelters in the week prior to a disability survey, with almost twice as many women reporting being home-bound (54%) compared to men (26%) (REACH, May 2021). The majority of shelters are constructed out of untreated bamboo, which is prone to insect infestation and decay due to the humid climate particularly during the monsoon season. Demand for treated bamboo exceeds supply since there are only three humanitarian actors in Cox's Bazar who operate bamboo treatment plants. According to the Sector's Shelter Standard Assessment (September 2021), only 9% of surveyed shelters have installed tie-down kits properly, only 8% have adequate and functioning drainage, and 41% of shelters are located on unsafe sites. A large number of shelters in Camp 2W have mud walls and do not benefit from shelter repair packages because the packages are designed for bamboo and tarpaulin structures. Due to lack of appropriate materials and lack of technical support, mud walls collapse during the monsoon season and interviews that DRC conducted in 2021 show that households widely perceive mud walls as unsafe. There is need for mud wall strengthening techniques and materials so that households can maintain their shelters and live in safe structures during the monsoon season.
