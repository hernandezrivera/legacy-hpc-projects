Project Summary

The project will be implemented in six regions in Somalia namely- Awdal,Bakool ,Banadir, Lower Shabelle Nugaal and Sool regions. This will be achieved through the provision of curative and preventive nutrition services through decentralized treatment centres. Treatment of uncomplicated severe acute malnutrition (SAM) and moderate acute malnutrition (MAM) among children 6-59 will be done through Outpatient therapeutic programmes and targeted supplementary feeding programs whereas management of complicated SAM will be provided in six stabilization centers in line with national IMAM guidelines. To achieve this, Action against Hunger will provide the services in thirteen health facilities and six mobile clinics. This will be supported by a network of community health workers who will be actively engaged in screening and referral of cases from the community as well as provide health and nutrition education and sensitization sessions to support positive behaviour change in child care practices. To improve early detection and referral of acute malnutrition, caregivers of boys and girls 6-59 months will be trained on MUAC screening and provided with colour coded MUAC tapes.
The response will prioritize displaced persons and host communities who have been affected by crises and have limited access to these basic services. To ensure a coordinated response is achieved, the programme will link with health, WASH, food security interventions to address risk factors of malnutrition as well as actively engage external stake holders such as local authorities, Ministry of health, sector Clusters and other implementing agencies. In terms of data collection and reporting, Action Against Hunger will ensure that data is disaggregated by sex , age and disability at all levels of project implementation. On the service delivery side, the project will ensure equity in gender representation in the selection of participants for capacity building activities, awareness meetings and in the selection of community volunteers and other community activities.
Action against Hunger will conduct routine monitoring and evaluation of achievement versus the set targets by routinely monitoring programe indicators as well as conducting periodic surveys.
The project will continuously engage beneficiaries and provide feedback through well-established systems.

Mitigation Measures

Risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance:ACF have strong global policies in place to prevent Fraud, Conflict of Interest, and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), along with a strong Whistleblowing policy to receive serious complaints. ACF already maintains an active Complaints Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project areas, including contact numbers. Each complaint or request for assistance is responded to in a timely manner, and serious complaints are raised to ACF regional office to ensure accountable management. Risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities: ACF will include marginalized groups at the design , implementation and monitoring of the projects. To ensure that assistance and services are reaching the most vulnerable. ACF will identify and prioritize the most vulnerable groups in the community and ensure they are also targeted. ACF will identify central locations for delivery of nutrition services to ensure beneficiaries do not have to traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access them. Risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneficiaries thus negatively affecting social cohesion and sparking intercommunity conflict over access to resources: ACF will engage District local authorities, Health facility committees and Village structures including opinion leaders at the kick planning stage and during implementation to allow them part part of project implementation decisions.Risk of explosive hazard contamination in project target area: ACF will avoid placing health facilities or mobile clinics near military installations. Weapon bearers will not be allowed inside nutrition distribution points. Risk of land disputes and tenure insecurity in targeted project area: ACF will offer nutrition services in centres that are neutral in collaboration with MoH to allow access by all beneficiaries regardless of clan or origin. ACF will also work with local organizations who understands deep community land dynamics and are able to provide services in undisputed areas.

Please provide further explanation

ACF designs its nutrition interventions to ensure that local capacity and government structures such as ministry of health are engaged and incorporated in the programme design. Through community engagement communities are allowed to identify their potential and needs though community driven actions plans. Subsequently ACF will partner with government in the delivery of services and offer capacity building to ensure that joint program implementation strategies are developed and linked to community actions. This will ensure that displaced people are integrated into the interventions and are able to access the services just like the host communities.
