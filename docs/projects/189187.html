1 - Needs

As South Sudan enters final year of the transitional period, major provisions of peace agreement have not completed such as reunification of the forces, establishment of the transitional justices mechanisms but above all the conduct of the population that is a prerequisite for conduct of elections. All the hopes of things becoming better during the transitional period has finally turned out to be false after the transitional period witnessed even more humanitarian crises exacerbated by flooding, intercommunal violence in Tambura and other parts of Bahr el Ghazel and Upper Nile regions and violence between state and non-state actors majorly in Central Equatoria and Western Equatoria state. The fighting among the SPLM IO in Magenis area in Upper Nile. The resultant effects of these natural and man-made disaster have been huge displacements with catastrophic humanitarian impacts on the country. 2021 witnessed continuation OF flooding which displaced people , destroyed houses and property, triggered host community- IDPs conflicts over land in places like Mangalla in Central Equatoria State where thousand displaced persons are currently settled. The country is also witnessing voluntary returns of both IDPs and refugees and this has brought in a lot of issues such as need for shelter support, conflict with secondary occupants and demand for reintegration package amid intercommunal violence. The national government is constructing a sixty-meter wide Juba-Yambio-Wau highway with similar branch Mundri-Mvolo-Rumbeki-Tonj highway. While this national development is a highly welcomed project, it has serious implications for people’s housing, land and property rights. The highway is destroying buildings, planted forests, homes and business places. It has been reported that the construction companies will decide on payment package for anything destroyed by the construction. This has taken away the bargain power from the communities. The policy has also stated that the companies will only pay for the estimated value of the structures but not the land on which such structures are constructed. Landlords of Mundri Town have introduced an exorbitant land fees that threaten vulnerable groups from acquiring land deeds in the town where most people take their children to access education. The new landlord’s policy demands that one pays about 400$ to the landlords before proceeding to county authorities to pay for the land deeds. The chances are that people who live on unregistered land will forcefully be evicted.In Lui Payam of Mundri East county, recent months have seen tense relations between different clans regarding the question who owns the land on which Lui town established. Some clans with backing of their men in uniform have threatened use of arms to settle the issue. This calls for urgent establishment of committees to handle this. Mvolo county experiences violence between farmers and cattle keepers in dry season every year. In all the three counties where this plan will be implemented, bad cultures limit women’s access to, ownership and use of land and property. Women and girls can still not inherit houses, land and property left behind by their deceased parents and spouse. Majority of population still do not understand their HLP rights and do not know where to seek redress in case of violation. Most vulnerable households cannot afford to take cases of land to court or even pay for a services of a legal counsel. AYA will establish a legal clinic to provide legal counselling and assistance to vulnerable families, hire a Law Firm to represent the beneficiaries before the court of law, create awareness on HLP rights, organize monthly Women HLP Forum, Coordinate Women Authority Dialogue on women access to land, provide cash shelter support to IDPs to easily reintegrate in their communities as part of durable solutions and use of media particularly Amadi FM in Mundri and EveryVillage FM in Mvolo and drama to create awarenes

2 - Project activities

1.1. Establishment of one Legal Clinic in each County provide legal counselling and assistance to the beneciaries. This clinic will be a centre where those in need of legal counselling will get help. This clinic will be run by two experienced Lawyers. After receiving legal counselling, the most vulnerable beneficiaries who can not afford the cost associated with going to court will be referred to Law Farms hired by AYA to represent them in the courts. The clinic may conduct outreach to distant payams. The Lawyers working in this clinic will be responsible to forwarding cases to court and monitoring court proceedings. 1.2. Provision of free legal counselling and assistance to the vulnerable individuals and groups. Counselling will be provided by two experienced Lawyers who will study each case independently and advice the clients according. The Lawyers may carryout eld visits to document facts and make recommendation for actions including forwarding them cases to be considered for litigation support and or cash assistance 1.3. This project plan aims to hire Law Firms to represent the clients in courts in Mundri and Yambio. The most vulnerable beneficiaries after receiving free legal counselling will be linked to Law farms contracted by AYA to provide legal representation to vulnerable beneficiaries who may not have the resource or knowledge to present their cases in courts of law. This is important because land cases take long time in courts sometimes even after end of the project. Secondly AYA staff may not be the direct target of any threat from land-grabbing individuals or institutions. 1.4. Organize community level awareness sessions with COVID 19 prevention protocols observed. This activity is aimed at educating communities on their HLP rights, means of redress when such rights are violated. This activity will be conducted by Community Outreach Officers. 1.5. Establishment of Community Committee to advocate for fair treatment of the vulnerable, provide protective accompaniment to the vulnerable groups while processing their documentation and to launch complain with local authorities with view to stop malpractices. 1.6. Establishment of Monthly Women Land Forum at level of three counties to increase awareness of women and girls’ land rights, discuss challenges affecting women’s access to, ownership of and use of land. This activity will draw women from faith based groups, civil society, government and communities. The recommendations drawn here will be presented and discussed in Women- authority Dialogue Sessions. 1.7. Organize and Facilitate Women-Authority Dialogue. This activity will be a follow up to women land forum and will discuss issues raised there. This activity is expected to be organized quarterly.1.8. Establish a greater Mundri HLP Coordination forum that will meet monthly and bring together partners and decision makers to deliberate and strategize on HLP issues

5 - Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)
5.1. Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA)?

Yes

5.1.1. What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

Cash programming is very necessary in this project because the beneficiaries of HLP programming for example those vulnerable women, children and elderly who are forcefully evicted from their plots as result of government projects like the highway construction may need to purchase locally available materials such as grass, poles and even labour. This can best be provided in cash. Cash also allows the beneficiaries to make make decisions about themselves

5.1.2. Explain how the project design considered market functionality

This project design takes into account the functionality of markets. In all the counties targeted by this project plan. there are perfectly functioning markets. Mundri East county borders Juba county and engages in mutually beneficial trade. All the counties fall along the Juba-Mundri- Maridi - Yambio -Tambura- Wau highway which the biggest trans-south Sudan trade route. All the counties are connected by mobile network telephone lines. These counties also use mobile money services such as M-Gurush and Nile Pay

8.11. Mitigation Measures.

In HLP programming, a lot of protection risks exist. Most perpetrators of the HLP rights are people in power, military, businesses or armed groups. These perpetrators are known to be violent. For example armed groups that continue occupy civilian houses in Mundri East are unwilling to hear anything that affects their enjoyment of such property, For people with disability, who lie low in the power hierarchy face even greater risks such as been physically assaulted, land and houses being sold by the most powerful. Kidnapping, threat and even illegal evictions. The powerful may even target AYA staff. There may be risk of sexual exploitation from AYA staff and partners particularly against the vulnerable.AYA will work with hired law firms in order to reduce the risk associated with litigation of cases. AYA will work with police and other security groups to ensure rights of all ( beneficiaries especially the disables, staff and partners) involved in HLP services are protected Other ways will include proper planning, consultation of every group of beneficiaries, disseminating PSEA messages, making every staff sign Code of Conduct before starting any engagement with the community, strengthening community complaint mechanism, dissemination of humanitarian principles to the key stakeholders, training staffs on key GBV guiding principles and conducting assessments prior to the distribution of items. AYA will orient all of its volunteers on six principles of PSEA. This will include all the staff either part time or full time volunteers. Community awareness shall be done to communities on issues pertaining sexual exploitation and sexual abuse. The contacts for the focal points shall be given to the community members to enable reports of any act constitute SEA. Therefore, the proper feedback mechanism to the community shall be established to generate and disseminate some feedback to the communities. All these will help to mitigate the risks that are anticipated.On explosive ordinance of war, basic information will be integrated and where necessary Mine Action actors will be invited to give further guidance. This plan will from time to time carry out context analysis to understand issues related to land and property ownership
