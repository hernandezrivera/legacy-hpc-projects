Project Summary

The 2025 initiative in Fangak, Canal/Pigi, Twic East, Ayod, Leer, and Mayendit counties aims to address the ongoing humanitarian challenges faced by at-risk populations in these flood- and conflict-affected regions of South Sudan. The plan focuses on increasing livelihood assistance by providing farmers with seeds and agricultural tools to restore agricultural output to surplus levels. This approach is intended to reduce reliance on food imports and enhance self-sufficiency.
Farmers will also receive training in climate-smart agricultural practices to help them adapt to climate-related environmental changes. Additionally, the distribution of climate-resilient crop varieties resistant to floods and droughts will support this effort. The initiative's primary goal in Fangak, Canal/Pigi, Ayod, Twic East, Leer, and Mayendit counties is to address urgent food insecurity needs and bolster the recovery and resilience of vulnerable communities.
This effort aims to improve food access, revive livelihoods, and strengthen community self-reliance in areas heavily impacted by ongoing communal conflicts and recurrent flooding. The main objectives include the immediate restoration of agricultural and livestock activities while fostering long-term resilience through diverse livelihood options and improved farming practices.
The project targets explicitly at-risk groups, including internally displaced persons (IDPs), host communities, returnees, women, children, and older people, all of whom are significantly affected by food insecurity, displacement, and the collapse of local economies. By enhancing local capabilities for food production, income generation, and market access, the initiative seeks to improve the food security, nutritional health, and economic status of impacted households.
The food security and livelihood program in these flood- and conflict-affected areas includes activities to meet the urgent requirements of at-risk populations. These activities involve supplying farmers with seeds and tools to boost agricultural production, particularly in regions where flooding has harmed crops. The program will also offer training and support for climate-resilient farming practices, such as cultivating flood-resistant crops and implementing soil management techniques.
Furthermore, it promotes non-agricultural income-generating opportunities, like small-scale livestock farming, fishing, or starting small businesses. The program provides starter kits for poultry farming, vegetable gardening, and handicrafts. It also addresses community water needs by delivering clean water through truck deliveries or rehabilitating boreholes, which helps reduce the risk of waterborne illnesses that can worsen food insecurity. Hygiene kits will be distributed to improve sanitation practices, enhancing overall health and livelihood stability.
Animal vaccination and veterinary services will be provided to support livestock owners affected by floods or conflict. The initiative aims to strengthen community capacity for livestock management and protection, ensuring long-term livelihoods. Community-level training and awareness campaigns focused on disaster preparedness and resilience strategies will also be organised to help communities recover from potential future floods or conflicts.
Additionally, saving and loan programs will promote financial stability and assist households in their recovery efforts. Lastly, facilitating access to local markets or establishing temporary markets will enable flood-affected communities to sell agricultural products and purchase necessary supplies, thus aiding economic recovery in the region. Overall, the project is committed to improving the livelihood recovery of families affected by flooding, conflict, and displacement through small-scale income-generating efforts in targeted counties.

1. Needs

During the harvest and post-harvest season, from October 2024 to March 2025, the acute malnutrition situation in South Sudan is expected to remain relatively unchanged across all counties. In Canal/Pigi, the situation is classified as IPC AMN Phase 1 (Serious), while other counties are classified as IPC AMN Phase 4 (Critical). Bor County may see a slight improvement, but other areas, including Ayod, Canal/Pigi, Duk, Nyirol, and Twic East, will likely experience either marginal deterioration or no change, all within the same IPC AMN phase classifications. From December 2024 to March 2025, an estimated 51.3 per cent of the population is expected to face a Crisis (IPC AFI Phase 3) or worse levels of acute food insecurity. During the lean season from April to June 2025, the situation may worsen slightly in nine out of eleven counties. However, there will be no changes in the IPC AMN phase classifications compared to the earlier analysis period.Additionally, from April to July 2025, 62.4 per cent of the population may experience a Crisis (IPC AFI Phase 3) or acute food insecurity. Many communities face significant challenges, including conflict, flooding, and economic instability. Ongoing communal disputes in some areas have disrupted farming activities, displaced populations, and hindered access to fields for planting and harvesting. In conflict zones, farmers are often forced to flee, leaving their fields untended and crops destroyed, which leads to reduced harvest yields. Issues such as livestock theft and damage to infrastructure further complicate food production efforts. Flooding has significantly impacted crop yields in Unity State and parts of Jonglei. In 2024, substantial flooding occurred in Fangak, Canal/Pigi, and Leer, submerging fields and preventing harvests. Flooding also affects land availability for farming and hampers the storage and drying of harvested crops, causing further losses. Displacement caused by both flooding and conflict often prevents communities from returning to their farming areas, resulting in decreased agricultural production. In regions like Mayendit, Ayod, and Twic East, many people have been displaced, leading many to rely on humanitarian aid rather than agriculture for survival. Between September and November 2024, 6.3 million people—representing 47 per cent of the analyzed population—were classified in IPC Phase 3 or worse (Crisis or above). Among this group, 1.71 million people faced critical acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 4 - Emergency), and approximately 41,000 faced catastrophic levels (IPC Phase 5 - Catastrophe). The most food-insecure populations in South Sudan have been severely affected by a combination of climate-related shocks (such as flooding and dry spells), an economic crisis (marked by currency depreciation and high food prices), ongoing conflict and insecurity, low agricultural production, and the impacts of the conflict in Sudan. During the same period, 11 counties across the country reported an estimated 126,000 returnees in IPC Phase 4, while 62 counties were classified as IPC Phase 3, and 6 counties were in IPC Phase 2 (Stressed). Most households are struggling to access food from local markets due to the continuing economic crisis in the country. 2. Project activities

General Food Assistance (GFA), provides emergency food aid to vulnerable households who may be experiencing acute food insecurity due to conflict, displacement, or crop failure. Cash Transfers/Voucher Programs, Providing cash or vouchers for families to buy food in local markets, which supports both the population and local economies. Livestock vaccination and agriculture Support, This could include distributing seeds, tools, and farming inputs to improve agricultural production. In some cases, the focus might be on promoting flood-resistant crops or introducing better farming techniques. Fishing Support, for areas around rivers and lakes, providing support to local fishing communities, including nets, boats, or training in sustainable fishing practices. Nutrition Awareness and Education, Providing training on proper nutrition, especially for women and children, to combat malnutrition. Targeted Supplementary Feeding Programs (TSFP), Offering ready-to-use therapeutic foods (RUTF) or fortified foods for children, pregnant women, and lactating mothers who are at risk of malnutrition. Community-based Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM), implementing community outreach programs to treat acute malnutrition in young children, improving their health and survival rates. Safe Water Supply, in areas like Fangak, Mayendit, Twic East, or Leer, where access to clean water may be limited, FSL projects often include building or rehabilitating boreholes, hand pumps, or other water supply systems. Sanitation & Hygiene Education, Promoting hygiene practices, such as hand washing and water purification, through community education programs to reduce the spread of waterborne diseases. Emergency WASH Kits, Distribution of hygiene kits, including soap, water purification tablets, and other essential items. providing training in income-generating skills, such as small-scale business management or tailored, to help communities recover economically. offering training on sustainable farming practices, pest management, and irrigation techniques to increase agricultural productivity. Establishing community-level savings groups to help households, access credit and save for future needs. Protection of Vulnerable Groups, this project will focus on protecting women, children, and displaced persons by providing safe spaces, access to legal support, and preventing gender-based violence (GBV). Providing mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) to those affected by conflict or displacement to help rebuild resilience. Integrating Health, WASH, Protection, and GBV mainstreaming ensures a comprehensive and coordinated response to prevent and address GBV. This holistic approach provides survivors with the necessary services to recover, while also working to prevent further violence. It also enhances the resilience of communities by addressing the interconnectedness of health, safety, dignity, and human rights. Health is a fundamental pillar of well-being, and in the context of GBV, it is essential to ensure that survivors have access to adequate physical and mental health care. They are providing immediate treatment for physical injuries resulting from violence, as well as services for sexual and reproductive health (SRH), including emergency contraception and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) for HIV. GBV survivors often face severe psychological trauma, and integrating mental health services ensures that they receive the counseling and therapeutic care needed to recover. GBV can result in unintended pregnancies, sexually transmitted infections (STIs), and other reproductive health issues. Health services must integrate family planning, maternal health care, and STI treatment into the GBV response. Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene is integral to preventing disease and providing safety, especially in crisis or displacement settings. For GBV survivors, WASH services play a critical role in both health and hygiene facilities. Safe spaces, such as private in HFs.
