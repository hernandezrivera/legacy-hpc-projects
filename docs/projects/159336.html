Project Summary

The last two decades have seen a large number of interventions carried out in Somalia for the purpose of increasing water supply in both rural and urban areas of the country. However, access to water still remains low, with 49% of the population lacking access to a regular and stable improved water sources for drinking. During drought periods, affected communities either walk for long distances in search for water, and in some cases the population is displaced. Interventions by the Government and humanitarian agencies are scaled up to reach the affected communities, mainly through water trucking. Prior information on the location of the strategic water sources and the status of the water quality and quantity is necessary for planning and implementation of such interventions.
FAO, through the Somalia Water and Land Information Management (SWALIM) project keeps an inventory of water sources across the country. The inventory helps to quickly identify the strategic water sources, current operational status, quantity and quality of water for planning and timely intervention especially during drought periods. The key challenge has however been to keep the data up to date, considering the highly dynamic water sector in Somalia. Regular monitoring and updating of the water sources is crucial for the database to remain relevant. A comprehensive countrywide survey of the strategic water sources is planned for 2020, building on the currently ongoing survey and existing data at SWALIM. Key selected boreholes will be also installed with groundwater monitoring equipment for daily recording of water levels and basic water quality parameters.
Along the Juba and Shabelle River Basins, SWALIM has planned in 2020 to develop and integrate within the existing Flood Risk and Response Information Management System (FRRIMS) a tool to identify flooded areas in near real-time based on remote sensing analysis of satellite radar images. The tool will serve as an information channel between FAO-SWALIM, development partners and government authorities, to improve communication and collaboration, and as a web-based source of information for the public.

Project Activities

1. Field survey across all regions of Somalia to update the water sources database (May – August 2020). 2. Detailed laboratory analysis of water samples from strategic water sources across the country (May – August 2020).3. Setup of groundwater monitoring stations at key groundwater aquifers across the country, and continuous monitoring of water levels and basic water quality parameters (January – December 2020).4. Analysis of water sources data and production of regular bulletins on the status (January – December 2020).5. Regular monitoring and mapping of floods along Juba and Shabelle in near real time using radar images (March – May / October – November 2020).6. Training/capacity building of Government staff at Federal level, Federal Member State level and Somaliland in monitoring and mapping of water sources and floods; data analysis and reporting.

Project Indicators

- At least 1,500 strategic water sources surveyed and data updated.- At least 250 water samples from strategic water sources across the country analyzed for heavy metals and common compounds.- Fifteen (15) groundwater monitoring stations procured and installed in key selected aquifers across the country, and made operational.- Publication and dissemination of at least 4 water sources status bulletins (quarterly) to WASH partners and Government Water Authorities.- Publication and dissemination of flooded area maps on weekly basis during the Deyr and Gu Rains.- At least 30 Government staff trained on water sources and monitoring of groundwater.

Mitigation Measures

Lens 1: 1a. Exposure to landmines or explosive remnants of war Humanitarian access to some areas within Somalia is limited to conflict/security threats including presence of landmines or explosive remnants of war. The use of high resolution satellite images will be emphasized in areas with limited access for ground truthing. FAO has also invested in local staff who knows the security issues/risks of their respective areas. The staff also have very close relationships with the local communities. FAO will monitor closely the security of local staff and partner organizations with guidance from the United Nations Department of Safety and Security (UNDSS) in Somalia. Lens 2: 2b. Women, girls and boys are not included in consultations and decision-making processesIn times of floods and droughts women and children are most affected in the community. The early warning information generated during this project will target the most vulnerable members of the community who are women and children. The participation of women in workshops targeted to promote early warning information will be encouraged. Lens 3: 3a. Protection concerns stemming from displacementThe ultimate beneficiary of the SWALIM early warning information generated through this project include vulnerable communities. In times of high floods there is a high risk of the vulnerable communities being excluded from getting early warning information due to displacement. This project aims at scaling up efforts to reduce disaster and climate risk and strengthening the resilience and adaptive capacity of people at risk of displacement by investing in early warning for early action information systems and strengthening institutional and indigenous capacities to mitigate flood risks along the Juba and Shabelle Rivers. Further, the project will establishing capacity for preparedness and community-based early warning systems in order to prepare for safe and timely evacuations if displacement becomes unavoidable, thus ensuring the protection of all displaced persons.
