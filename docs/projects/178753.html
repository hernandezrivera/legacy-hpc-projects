Project Summary

The project aims at supporting war affected households by rehabilitating partially damaged houses and distributing NFI kits to displaced people
in Idleb and in Al Bab in Aleppo. In total, 1,900 HHs will benefit from the activities of this project (4,892 females – 4,608 males). The rehabilitation will target 400 houses that are partially damaged houses that people are living in or have returned to. NFI kits will be distributed to 800 HHs that have been displaced from different areas into Idleb and Aleppo (400 kits to the same beneficiaries). In camps, roads of five camps will be levelled, in addition to ground insulation of tents in these camps.

4 - Needs

As the war enters its tenth year in Syria, the humanitarian situation is becoming worse. 2020 witnessed several forced displacements from north Hama and south of Idleb into Idleb and Aleppo. As of 21 December 2020, some 2.7 million people remain displaced in northwest Syria. According to the CCCM Cluster, there were 29,785 displacements only in November 2020, with departures principally occurring from Ariha, and Ehsem. The main locations where displaced people arrived to include Dana, and Maaret Tamsrin sub-districts. The Cluster closely monitors 1,379 IDP sites that host 1,673,680 IDPs or 318,577 families. Of these, 80% are women and children, and 23,660 IDPs are reported to be persons with specific needs. (Feb 2021). "Eighty-two per cent of assessed households in Syria report a significant deterioration in their ability to meet basic needs since August 2019, due mainly to price increases and loss of income. This is particularly the case for over 1.9 million IDPs sheltering in informal settlements, planned camps and collective shelters, which offer inadequate protection against the elements and increase the risk of epidemic-prone diseases among this population. At the same time millions of people across Syria continue to live in damaged housing, particularly along former frontlines, with those paying rent now struggling more than before", HNO 2021. HNAP Shelter conditions report also highlights the issue of unfinished buildings in North Syria " Overall, 7 percent of IDP households live in unfinished homes or apartments. Rates are lowest in CSS (3 percent) - where the majority live in finished homes or apartments - and are highest in NS (14 percent)".

5 - Implementation of activities

This project will compromise Rehabilitation of 200 partially damaged houses (Activity 2.1.1), distributing 1000 core NFI kits ( Activity 1.1.1) distributing 1500 vouches for winterization (1.2.1), in addition to improving the infrastructure in camps (road levelling and gravelling and water culverts) (1.3.3). The activities of the project will benefit 16,500 beneficiaries (3,795 boys under 18 , 3,630 girls under 18, 4035 men, 4620 women). Upgrading of 200 unfinished houses: 200 HHS will benefit from this activity. HIHFAD will receive applications from beneficiaries who are in need of house upgrade, the application will include the ID of the applicants in addition to property ownership documents that will be verified by the community. Two members of the staff (one male and one female) who are trained on HLP will be responsible for any HLP issues confronted during implementation of the project. A technical needs assessment of houses will be conducted before starting the implementation of the rehabilitation works. The house should be unfinished, and the cost of all rehabilitation works will not exceed $900 for each house. The selection of beneficiaries for the Shelter rehabilitation activities will be based on the following criteria: Families who have returned to their damaged houses. Female headed households; Families with chronically ill members or with disabilities (both from host communities and IDPs) - Affected people will be consulted about what they need to be rehabilitated as a priority. PWD will be taken into consideration in all activities: in distribution, door to door distribution will be used for PWD, during upgrade, a ramp, and other assistive equipment in Washing facilities will be installed for PWD.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment ) Mitigation: 1 - Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2 - Train staff on code of conduct – 3 - Include code of conduct in all staff contracts about SEA complaints. Risk: Some beneficiaries (most vulnerable) might be at risks such as harassment during the distribution of NFI kits. Mitigation: 1 - allocate one day for distributing to female headed households. 2 - Separate between men and women during the distribution 3 - Give priority to vulnerable people in addition to door to door distribution to people with disabilities. Risk: Eviction from shelter due to lack of approval from property owner and/or exposing beneficiaries at risk due to lack of HLP documentation. Mitigation: 1. Abide by the HLP guidance document by the SNFI Cluster 2. Do not proceed with the Shelter rehabilitation unless with the consensus of the owner, proof of registered HLP ownership, verified ownership claims. If this won’t be available, feasible, refer to the HLP DD. 3. Include HLP caveat into the MoUs or agreements signed Risk: Tension between host communities, IDPs and returnees. mitigation: 1. Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs only. 2. Selection criteria are transparent and groups are fully informed. Distribution partners engage with the communities to sensitize the population. -Risk of physical harm at distribution points (security, safety)Distribution points will be organized to deal with Covid19 risk, clear marks with physical distance of 1.5 m, hand sanitizers will be available, and all staff will wear masks and gloves, crowd will be organized through timing of distribution, maximum of 10 people at the distribution point.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Persons with disabilities are at higher risks of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers. Women and girls with disabilities are two times at higher risk of neglect and exclusion from the families and the service providers, to compare with their peers without disabilities all of this makes the persons with disabilities in higher risks of SEA . Females with disabilities remain most invisible groups in the Syrian society, they may not leave their houses, and not participating social events in the communities hugely isolated. Thus, the project will ensure outreaching house-bounded females by ensuring appropriate gender balance within the team, so ensure our access to this group. HIHFAD provides all the hired staff an induction training on the protection principles, PSEA and GBV so they become oriented enough to provide the services in a dignified and safe way for the beneficiaries. A focal point for PSEA and GBV would be appointed per area. HIHFAD has a pre-established feedback system and safeguarding reporting channel that supports the ability of the targeted populations to submit their complaints, comments, and suggestions in a beneficiary-friendly and easy-to-access way.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Access restriction due to local fighting between different arm source groups in the targeted location which may restrict the access. The local fighting might delay the implementation of the activities. Mitigation: HIHFAD Security team will monitor the situation on the ground and send reports when necessary. The works will be suspended during the fighting and resumed after assessment of the situation. Assumption: the security situation remains relatively stable which allows people to continue living in the area and the activities of the project to take place. Risk: Closure of the border for commercial shipments or changing some regulation related to allowed items to be exported to Syria may have a very heavy negative impact on the cross-border activities. Mitigation: 1. Explore alternative supply lines through local procurement 2. Communicate regularly with logistic cluster to have valid information about new or changing regulations. Assumption: The crossing borders are open for humanitarian and commercial shipments. Risk: Risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment of women and girls) Mitigation: 1 - Establish a confidential complaint mechanism 2 - Train staff on code of conduct – 3 - Include code of conduct in all staff contracts that address any SEA complaints.
