Project Summary

This project seeks to address the nutrition sector priorities for the HRP which are mentioned in programme results framework.
SRD will address the nutrition sector priorities as listed in the HRP through essential preventative service package that can be provided at the community level by community workers in 5 PHCs and 40 CHWs. The service package will include:
1- Counselling on IYCF-E through community workers who are hired and trained by SRD.
2- Nutrition education group sessions that include all members of the households (male and female).
3- MUAC screening and referrals for boys and girls 6-59 months.
4- Distribution of micronutrient powders to boys and girls aged 6 to 59 months.
5- MUAC screening for pregnant and lactating women.
6- Distribution of micronutrient tablets or iron and folic acid to pregnant and lactating women.
7- Provide referral for all identified malnutrition cases to existing CMAM clinics to seek treatment. Either for boys and girls 6-59 months.
SRD will target the following villages throughout Aleppo/ North Aleppo, including, Efrin center, Jenderes,, Akhtarin, Bza’a, and Sandi in Efrin, Al-bab and A’zaz Districts, not only because of its severity category of 4 and 5, but also as an emergency response to the increase number of IDPs moving to these areas.
In these areas there is:
• High concentration of IDPs
• People in need in these locations are estimated as the following: Approximately 24,548 boys and girls aged 6- 59 months and 12,836 PLWs in needs (these numbers are inclining because of the increase number of IDPs form eastern Ghouta).

Needs

According to HNO 2019 , around 91,811 girls and boys aged 6-59 months are acutely undernourished and if malnutrition is not prevented additional 146,898 children under the age of five years will become acutely malnourished during 2019. Nutrition survey data shows that across Syria there are 19,263 severely malnourished children who are eleven times more likely to die than well-nourished children. If not treated appropriately half of the severely malnourished children could die due to complications related to severe acute malnutrition. ( Hama, Aleppo and Idleb governorates) . 4.7 million girls and boys under five years of age and pregnant and lactating women are at risk of under-nutrition and in need of preventive and curative nutrition services in 2019. 3.13 million girls and boys under 5 years of age require appropriate nourishment for adequate nutrition status while 1.59 million pregnant and lactating women require nutrition preventive nutrition services in the Hama, Idleb and Aleppo governorates). ( HNO 2019). In Aleppo/ North Aleppo, the nutrition situation is classified as severe on the severity scale for 2018 Although the prevalence of stunting and wasting among children are in an acceptable range, the prevalence of wasting among women is poor as per Humanitarian Response plan for 2019, and as per the surveillance conducted on November 2019 in three governorates in Syria ( Aleppo, Raqa and Idleb), it is found that the total number of detected malnourished cases is 692out of29, 136measured children (60% of themeasured children are under 2 years old), the cases classified as follow: 529MAM (1.82%) and 163 SAM (0.56%), GAM prevalence is (2.38%), 3 bilateral pitting edema cases were diagnosed because of malnutrition in Azaz, and Harim. While 11935 PLW were screened (5679 pregnant and 6256 lactating women), 364 of them were classified as malnourished. The IYCF practices are very poor and the prevalence of anaemia among children and women are moderate. Other aggravating factors such as diarrhoea are high in the context of relatively low service coverage (HNO 2019) The nutrition sector severity ranking for 2018 considers 66 of 270 sub-districts to be facing critical problems and in need of immediate humanitarian assistance and some of Aleppo sub-district are included especially in Al-Bab district (Sandi and Beza’a sub-districts), A’zaz district (Aghtrin sub-district) and in Efrin district (Efrin center, Jenderes sub-districts. All geographic locations were selected according to the sector severity ranking with focusing on category 4 and 5. In addition other indicators related to access and intensity of conflict were also taken into consideration. Moreover, these areas are considered IDPs reception areas especially in the recent situation of Eastern Ghouta displacement. Where nutrition cluster has tracked more than 59,000 arrivals from eastern Ghouta since 13 March moving into North-West Syria. Of these, 8,824 have arrived to CCCM Member Agency Reception Centres in Mezanaz, Maaret Elekhwanm Albab and Azaz.

Protection Risk Analysis

Gender Based Violence GBV: Health services are often the first, and in many occasions the only point of contact for GBV survivors seeking assistance, or coming for health care for GBV related emotional or physical conditions, including injuries, without necessarily disclose the abuse due to shame or fear. In order to facilitate the required care, survivors must have safe access to health facilities, and it is critical that health providers are equipped to offer survivor-centered, right-based, non-discriminatory, quality health services. Mitigating measures: - Prevention and management of sexual violence and Intimate Partner Violence is considered to be a life-saving intervention. Accordingly, affected population should have access to the reproductive health services of the Minimum Initial Service Package (MISP) as part of the essential health package. - Specialized health staff should be trained on providing first-line support, clinical management of sexual assault and rape, identification of GBV cases, and safe referrals. - Identification of safe referral pathways to ensure comprehensive management of GBV cases connecting health services and clinical management with social services, mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS), in a manner guarantees safety, confidentiality, and privacy of the survivor. - Presence of female staff in health facilities increase access of women and girls to health services. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: The health sector and healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels. Mitigating measures: - A PSEA policy describes standard of behavior for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. - Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. - Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness-raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. - Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff. - Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programs it is implementing and what they intend to deliver. - Design and implement appropriate programs based on an impartial assessment of needs and risks, and an understanding of the vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting and feedback mechanisms.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Security in Idleb and western Aleppo could affect access in implementing area, in case of bombing by Syrian armed forces and its alliances or by internal conflict of opposition’s armed groups. In case of skirmishes and limited clashes, the possibility of occurrence is moderate and the impact is low then temporary and limited activities suspension is expected. In case of bombing and airstrikes, the possibility of occurrence is low and the impact is low, and in this regard temporary and limited activities suspension is expected. That will be at low risk in Afrin district SRD is able to response immediately.
