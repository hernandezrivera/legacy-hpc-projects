Project Summary

The overall goal of the project is to alleviate suffering and increase the safety and security of conflict-affected and vulnerable IDPs, returnees and host communities. Specifically, WADA-SS proposes to conduct a project to respond to the protection needs of approximately 5100 IDPs, returnees, host community and spontaneous refugee returnees in Aweil East and West Counties, affected by cross boarder conflicts between nomadic ethnic groups from Sudan and Dinka Malual from South Sudan. Recurrent fighting in Raga and food crisis declared in South Sudan led to displacement of host community as some became IDPs and others moved to safe places in neighbouring countries like Sudan. Many of the IDPs have settled in areas close to the boarder in Aweil East and Aweil North, others integrated into the community while others moved further south in other states. This has created a burden to the two counties. Affected populations are settled in various IDP camps or “hotspots” along the border.
In 17 April 2023 up to now, UNCHR / IOM and WADA-SS conducted a spontaneous refugee’s returnee’s protection monitoring to Northern Bahr el Ghazal along the border points with Sudan. A total of 2,527 spontaneous refugee above / returnees had come back to South Sudan through the various border points/hotspots among others; Kiir-adem, Majok-yiuthiou, Jach and Mayom-angok. This increased demand for already inadequate humanitarian services in the border routs.
Aweil East has an estimated population of 66, 300 individuals and 87, 400 people who are in need of humanitarian assistance. According to Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) 2023, Aweil East has an estimated to have a population of 54,200 individuals and 250,000 people in need, this is . The target population (5100) only represents 5% of the total population in the two counties. The service gaps in protection and other humanitarian services are still immense as the two counties have been neglected. There is limited documented information on the current situation of the affected population and there are few humanitarian organizations operating in the county, mainly providing NFIs and health services. To address this information gap, WADA proposes to initiate this project with a rapid needs assessment to establish the actual status of the affected population, protection issues/concerns, mapping of the various hotspot including high return areas and partners responding as well as gaps in service provision.

1. Needs

According to the HNO, Northern Bahr el Ghazal has approximately 576,700 above people in need of humanitarian services in 2023/2024. The same report notes that Northern Bahr el Ghazal is one of the states with catastrophic levels of severity of needs and low access to humanitarian services in protection and Livelihoods services. In Aweil North and East where this project will be located, protection services are lacking and there are only 6 humanitarian organizations spread across the county mainly in nutrition and health actors. Northern Bahr El Ghazal State have most active crisis will be the intervention location for this project. Subsequently; women and children are the most vulnerable populations in these Counties have been heavily affected by major high rates of malnutrition and are at risk of emergency food insecurity, health and water, sanitation and hygiene related diseases. Also, Northern Bahr el Ghazal is one of the states heavily affected by the impacts of seasonal floods. Abnormally heavy seasonal flooding devastated large areas of South Sudan since July 2023/2024, with an estimated 908,000 people affected. The rains continued until December 2019 and put more people at risk. The Government of South Sudan declared a state of emergency in the flooded areas on 27 October 2019, according to the HNO. Some of the protection concerns identified include ; • The floods exacerbated the vulnerability of many communities and individuals already significantly negatively impacted by conflict. A lack of access to basic services and both the act of and conditions during displacement, even if only over a relatively short distance, exposed individuals to additional protection risks beyond those already being experienced as a result of the armed conflict. .• Key protection risks potentially include, but are not limited to, psychosocial distress as a consequence of the shock and disruption in daily routines; breakdown of social fabric and cohesion; separation of children from families or caregivers; increased risk of sexual and gender-based violence; loss of dignity from loss of key clothing items with the potential to lead to restricted mobility for key groups such as women; criminality and violence among or between community members relating to availability of resources and other issues; loss of or damage to civil documentation; unfamiliarity with the terrain creating risks for physical safety and security including from explosive hazards; and flooding creating the potential for exposure of explosive hazards. • Loss or continued lack of livelihoods as a consequence of the flooding can lead to negative coping strategies such as the worst forms of child labour; discrimination in food allocation within the family; and child marriage, among others.

2. Project activities

The overall goal of the project is to alleviate suffering and increase the safety and security of conflict-affected and vulnerable IDPs, refugees, returnees and host communities. Specifically, the project will focus on implementing the following key activities; (1) lead protection cluster coordination with key actors (2) Protection monitoring and assessments (3) Provide community structures with small Quick Impact Projects (QIPs) to promote peaceful co-existence (4) Core Relief Items/ NFIs distributions targeting IDPS and vulnerable individuals and female headed households including PSNs. These activities will provide targeted protection assistance to IDPs returnees and host community affected by flooding and others deasesters within the thematic area of general protection. To achieve the intended overall goal, WADA will establish its presence in Aweil North County with support from Aweil West Coordination office and Juba Country office. Competent local staff will be recruited to support the day to day protection monitoring in Aweil North and Aweil East. The Project staff will include; (i) A roving Senior Protection Officer who will provide leadership and technical oversight on the day to day implementation of the project in Aweil west and Aweil East. A Roving Protection Officer will lead in protection monitoring and assessments and all related activities and any other responsibility as delegated by the Senior Protection Officer. He will work on roving basis between Aweil North and Aweil East. (iii) Protection Monitors (4) will be recruited locally in each County; 2 in Aweil west and 2 in Aweil East to support smooth implementation of the project and support in protection monitoring and gathering timely information. (iv) Community Based Protection Networks (4) These are volunteers and will comprise of key informants and influential members of the community who will support to the project at the local level including gathering of emerging protection issues within the community. Each County will have 2 CBPNS. Each CBPN will receive a monthly incentive of $50. This project will improve access to protection services by strengthening coordination with stakeholders, provision of targeted material assistance to PoC, identification of PSNs and coordinating referrals for other services among others and ensuring multi-sectoral approach in humanitarian response by ensuring that protection remains central while delivering aid.
