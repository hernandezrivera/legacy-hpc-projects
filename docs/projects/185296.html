Project Summary

The humanitarian situation in Mali in general but also looking at the Region of Gao remains worrisome with growing conflicts, multifaceted insecurity, and climatic hazards (floods and droughts). This is compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic, which continues to increase pre-existing vulnerabilities with adverse short-, medium-, and long-term socio-economic consequences, exacerbating humanitarian risks and needs, including protection needs. The IDPs and the resident population have been severely affected by macro-challenges with the most vulnerable groups including all IDPs, women, children under five years, the elderly, people living with disabilities, female headed households and child headed households bearing the worst brunt of the challenges. Against this backdrop, WHH is proposing to support protracted IDPs and vulnerable resident population in multiple localities through Cash-Based Transfer (CBT) and In-kind Transfer modality from January-December 2022. Just like the IDPs, the rural vulnerable residents have also been severely affected by food insecurity and malnutrition as a result of the above stated situation. Therefore, WHH will provide food assistance to both IDPs and the vulnerable resident population.
Coupled with the need to give the target group flexibility to choose the kind of food varieties that they prefer in order to meet their diversified nutritional needs, a CBT modality is justified in some of the project target locations (villages access to market) and the target group will receive their food assistance through CBT modality and linked activites in the sectorsof food security, nutrition and WASH.

1 - Contexte

The humanitarian context in Mali for the year 2021 continues to be marked by pre-existing shocks (conflicts/security insecurities; droughts, floods, and epidemics) that threaten life and threaten the lives and livelihoods of affected households. The number of people in need in Gao has been estimated up to 700,000 people. Comparing the total population of about 710.000 people to the almost 700.000 people in need, the urgency of assistance is alarming.The overall security situation in the Gao region is deteriorating compared to last years, particularly in the 2021, the Ansongo circle and the areas around the Gao-Ansongo and Gao-Douentza road axes, where Gao-Douentza have become a target area to crime, clashes, incursion into villages, sabotage of telecommunication antennas, vehicle robberies, and IED attacks constraining the humanitarian access. The growing insecurity and military operations continue to affect the living conditions of the populations already weakened by the lack of access to basic social services. The region is home to nearly 70,000 internally displaced persons and more than 134,000 people who are considered to be food insecure (OCHA: 03/2021). Comparing the growth of the food insecurity, Gao has been showing a concerningly increasing number of affected populations rising only from 2020, 3 times the number in 2021 (134.000 people). The stated OCHA report highlights the importance to improved funding levels for humanitarian and development interventions in order to increase the response to the affected population in the region.
