Project Summary

In relation to the HRP 2022, activities linked to the NFI sectoral objectives, where 247,787 have been identified to be in need of NFI in the country, and 246,803 to be targeted.
The key focus province will be Kirundo.
The Burundi government since 2017 has been focusing on the voluntary repatriation of the refugees. By November 2021, 183,000 refugees had been assisted to voluntarily return to the country as per JRRRP .
Social integration between the host and returnees remains a major concern as identified in the national plan of JRRRP and HRP.
While 94% of returnees depend on agriculture as their main source of income, only 37% of returnee women have access to land. And Even those who have it have low production.
These has been workshops, for sensitization and trainings to enhance a peaceful co-existence between returnees and host communities, which has involved so far 5,500 beneficiaries, hence there need to emphasise and increase the beneficiaries and ensure a durable and sustainable reintegration
There will be a total of 3,000 direct beneficiaries from the delivery of quick impact projects through cash for work activities. Livelihood and resilience activities will target 500 households. Selection criteria will be developed in consultation with a community reference group (including representatives from across different groups and vulnerabilities: e.g. female headed households, PWD, PLW), to ensure that activities target the most vulnerable.
Tearfund will work with local Gender and Peace champions to lead social norms change and improve social cohesion within their communities. Specific emphasis will be put on working with young people (ensuring at least 20% of champions are youth, aged 18-35), in order to galvanise cross generational change and address problems of youth mobilisation for political and military aims which in the past have contributed towards the conflict. Youth will also make up at least 30 % of the participants in livelihood activities.
Target groups will respect 60% women and 40 % men.
The activities will be subdivided into the following main groups:
Community Conflict Transformation Dialogue(CCTD) Approach for Improving social cohesion: this aims at sustainable social cohesion and co-existence between the returnees, IDPs and their communities. It will also enable community based healing of memories and trauma. Tearfund will train a cohort of local peacebuilding champions who will hold a series of CCTDs with the returnees, IDPs and host communities. In conducting the CCTD sessions, this will create spaces for returnees, IDPs and host/remainee communities to engage with each other. At the end of the CCTD sessions, participants will be encouraged and supported to implement joint and shared community peacebuilding initiatives (e.g. sports events, theatre, community peace days).
SGBV will be addressed during these sessions as well as the Women Leadership.
Skilling through TVET/AVET for Livelihoods Recovery and Economic Resilience: participants will be organised to ensure sustainability of livelihood support for the project.Together with beneficiaries, we will identify their training needs to support their planned income generating activities (IGAs). Tearfund and HCB will provide a series of vocations training in 2 streams, Technical (e.g. sewing, cooking, mechanics, hairdressing) and Agricultural. A special focus will be on smart agriculture, as 94% of the population live off agriculture activities. Across both streams, syllabus will include basic business and economic skills including for example business plan development, basic accounting. Where necessary, participants who successfully complete the training will receive start up kits appropriate to their vocation. Tearfund will also establish ongoing mentoring opportunities for participants as they seek to grow their businesses.
