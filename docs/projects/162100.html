Project Summary

In order to build an adequate and harmonized response across all camps, this project aims to continue to strengthen and improve a wide range of Camp Management components including site management structures, access to services, community participation, site improvements and settlement planning that address vulnerability to natural hazards and natural resource management.

Needs

In the past two years Camp Management functions were put in place based on a crucial partnership between RRRC, UNHCR and Camp Management Support (CMS) agencies. In 2019, UNHCR has focused on consolidating these functions in camps to the extent possible given political limitations, enhance refugee participation and representation at camp level as well as strengthen the RRRC capacity both at Cox’s and camp level. For 2020, albeit there has been very significant progress since 2018 there is a need to inter alia continue to reinforce RRRC and CiCs capacities, strengthen the camp level coordination, increase refugee community representation(developed guidelines guarantee 50% women participation) and continue to ensure strong DRR response. The Camp Management interventions shall be designed to i) Ensure that every refugee including women, men, boys, girls and older person have access to services; ii) regular service monitoring is conducted iii) protection mainstreaming; iv) care and maintenance (small camp improvements especially taking into account DRR needs) ; v) community participation and representation; vi) capacity building through the CSI; vii) emergency preparedness and response; viii) information management and camp coordination. All strategies and activities will be coordinated with the Site Management and Site Development Sector(SMSD), other relevant stakeholders including the refugee communities. The sector leadership should reflect the coordination review.There is a need to continue to produce number of information products including camp profiling and facility mapping, barcoding of facilities in addition to sectoral assessments which help humanitarian agencies to reinforce the coordination system established in camps and make informed decisions both for regular and emergency programming. Using analytical studies provided by the Natural Hazard Task Force and specialized studies in 2019 there is a need to improve the conditions of camps, reduce the risk of landslide and flooding and coordinate the site improvement interventions and disaster response in congested camp settings. There is also a need to continue mobilizing the SUVs and CPP volunteers as part of the minimum support and response mechanism for life saving activities during the emergency. This shall increase resilience, reduce exposure, improve preparedness and create early warning systems for adverse events. UNHCR has invested in making communities (in particular women and youth) aware of risks and to help mitigate disasters. Regular site development works shall continue to be required to address community wide issues - improved access, channel clearing and strengthening, slope stabilization and larger scale community works. UNHCR shall continue to comply with the multi-sector site planning standards endorsed by ISCG for all site development actors and this shall be further reframed in the context of potential disaster risk infrastructure plan. Environmental impact assessment by UN agencies reveal that pertinent actions are required to improve the environmental condition that includes massive reforestation, abatement of air and water pollution, engaging both refugee and host community. Asian Elephant Experts suggests holistic intervention is necessary to save the Asian Elephant and other wildlife that are threatened by direct and indirect impact of the refugee influx. Elephant Response Team have continually and successfully prevented Human to Elephant conflict and conservation advocacy has been intensified. The camp greening strategy continues under the coordination of the EETWG and compliments the commitment UN agencies made to tackle the environmental effects caused by camp infrastructure and habitation. Greening of public services has started with Health Clinics being electrified using solar and generator hybrids.

Activities or outputs

In 2020 the revised coordination structure shall strengthen joint planning, implementation, and coordinated response on disaster preparedness and long-term camp development through the sector. In close coordination with partners UNHCR will work together to support the authorities to maintain and enhance a robust system of civilian administration in refugee settlements. UNHCR will support CiCs and SMS partners to reinforce channels of community participation and establish a representation system based on legitimacy, accountability, participation and transparency. Under the Capacity sharing project (CSI) a joint initiative of all agencies, multi-sector trainings for the CIC staff is being rolled out alongside coaching and mentorship and compilation of a camp guidebook to enable uniform application of guidelines and policies across all camps. For 2020 expansion of this activity shall include CIC, partner staff and stakeholders. UNHCR shall work with its multi-functional team and SMS and community-based protection partners in order to mobilize refugees to participate in leadership and decision-making structures. The mitigation of risks and hazards of GBV will be considered in all activities including in the maintenance/improvements of sites and site development engaging with community.UNHCR will ensure information management activities for Camp Coordination and Camp Management for evidence-based planning, monitoring and reporting . These activities include but are not limited to needs assessments, camp profiling, services mapping and monitoring, facility mapping, contact directories, training to partners and relevant stakeholders, risk and vulnerability analysis, monsoon incident reporting and response tracking, etc. In addition, UNHCR will collaborate with relevant sector and partners on harmonization of data, systems and process. UNHCR and partners will work to improve access and living conditions in refugee sites and adjacent host communities taking a holistic and integrated approach. These activities will enable intensive maintenance work to maintain access and immediately address infrastructure failure due the rain wind, landslides and flooding. To the extent possible labour-based community works shall be implemented across all areas of operation to enhance resilience and dignity, as well as enable more community driven improvements. These activities shall be managed by UNHCR and partners through the Global distribution Tool (GDT). UNHCR will continue to focus on three primary areas of intervention: drainage, access, slope stabilisation to reduce vulnerability to natural hazards. Collaboration with joint UN projects like SMEP shall continue for larger scale works. In 2020 UNHCR shall continue to support the socialisation of Ukhiya camps Profile with key donors in the context of UNDAF and district development plan initiatives and support studies to develop a strategic area-based development model encompassing both refugees and surrounding Bangladeshi communities. Community-based, robust Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) plans that enhance resilience and reduce the effects of the monsoon seasons and cyclone shall continue by capacitating and mobilizing SUVs and CPP volunteers in camps and host communities. Community based environmental and biodiversity awareness will continue across the camp to address deforestation and elephant conflict. UNHCR plans to work closely with the Forest Department to strengthen soil stabilization and environmental restoration. Stream rehabilitation has covered 7 km of water ways which shall be continued to abate water pollution. Conservation activities shall be further widened to include “Wild life human contact” with rescue and relief programmes. UNHCR is further working with IOM and other relevant agency’s on solar street lighting and technology recycling. A protection focus on all lighting, fuel and environmental activities shall continue to be the centre of planning.
