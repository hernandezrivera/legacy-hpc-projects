Project Summary

Building on previous combined actions, this multi-sectoral Project contributes to the protection of 40,175 vulnerable Palestinians in the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, from forcible transfer (FT) through an integrated, protection-centred approach to humanitarian responses. Specifically, the Project aims at: (1) preventing and responding to protection incidents (demolitions, settler-related, military operations etc.) with emergency assistance (cash and in-kind) and protective measures (RESPONSIVE ACTIVITIES); (2) mitigating protection risks for highly vulnerable communities (150) through synergizing community-based protection, the provision of basic and social infrastructure, legal aid and stakeholder mobilization (REMEDIAL ACTIVITIES); and (3) promoting increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations through coalition advocacy undertaken with Third States, International Organizations, Palestinian authorities and media outlets (ENVIRONMENT-BUILDING ACTIVITIES).
The Project is predicated upon empowerment and sustainability goals. Context-adapted responses and accountability to affected populations (including gender-age sensitivity) are ensured thanks to active beneficiary engagement throughout all stages of the project cycle, starting with participatory protection assessments and response planning. National ownership is further promoted through community capacity-building and proactive coordination with Palestinian authorities, at both central and sub-central levels. Wherever possible, the Project also champions a ‘nexus’ approach in order to link the target communities with development cooperation measures, aiming to reduce their dependency on humanitarian assistance and to ensure they are not left behind in pursuing national development goals and SDGs.
Within the framework of the HCT, the Project supports the implementation of the Area C Policy Framework and coordinates with other actors meeting the humanitarian and protection needs of communities at risk of FT. The Project provides a mechanism to respond to OCHA triggers for demolitions and settler-related incidents. Shelter, WASH, Education, Health, FSL, legal aid and emergency responses and advocacy will be coordinated through the relevant Clusters/WGs. The Project is thus envisaged to contribute to the realization of the following HRP objectives: 1. Increased respect for International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and International Human Rights Law (IHRL) and accountability for violations; 2. Protection response mechanisms are in place to prevent and mitigate the effects of the occupation and conflict related violence.
Note that implementing partners also plan to submit a number of sectoral projects, as single agencies, which will include some of the same activities (e.g.: legal aid services; shelter upgrades etc.). Some of these projects will complement this Project’s assistance, but will respond to a logic of ‘contiguity’ rather than ‘continuity’, since they are informed by different rationales and approaches. For this reason, financial resources will be kept separated. Our planning, monitoring and reporting systems prevent any duplication.

Needs

The situation in the WB remains a protracted protection crisis with humanitarian consequences driven by a lack of compliance with IHL and IHRL on the part of Israel as Occupying Power. Routine rights violations are a driver of both severe protection threats and chronic humanitarian vulnerabilities. In Area C, H2 and East Jerusalem, specifically, the Occupying Power has established a ‘coercive environment’ to push Palestinians out of the path of settlements through: a discriminatory permit regime for construction, lack of access to basic services, forced evictions and planned relocations, confiscation and destruction of civilian property, obstruction of humanitarian relief, land expropriation, settlement expansion, construction of the Wall, movement and access restrictions, declarations of live fire zones and closed areas, settler violence and military operations. Those most at risk of forcible transfer include 46 Bedouin and herder communities slated for relocation; those vulnerable to evictions and destruction of property in Area C; those affected by demolitions or the revocation of their residency rights in East Jerusalem; and over 350,000 Palestinians in 67 communities affected by settler violence and harassment (HNO 2018). Forcible transfer has serious immediate and long-term physical, socio-economic and emotional impact. It deprives Palestinians of their home and results in disruption to livelihoods, a reduced standard of living and limited access to elementary services. The progression of relocation plans for Bedouin communities remains a grave concern. The Project’s response to these challenges is guided by threats/vulnerability/capacity analyses (‘protection equation’) conducted with the target communities. The Project’s community protection approach is specifically designed to support adequate beneficiary participation in the design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation of interventions and to involve groups traditionally excluded from decision-making (women, children, PWDs) through dedicated focus groups and transect interviews. Likewise, assessments conducted as part of emergency and legal responses include the entire household to ensure vulnerable and marginalized individuals have a voice. Priority is systematically given to the most vulnerable, including female-headed households, the elderly, ill or disabled. Quantitative assessments conducted as part of this approach based on a Protection Vulnerability Index (PVI) continue to show a high level of protection vulnerability overall in the 163 communities targeted by the Project in 2018. 69% fall under the high vulnerability level (>50 in PVI); 31% record medium vulnerability (between 30 and 49 PVI); and none can be classified as low vulnerability (<30 PVI). Complementary qualitative assessments based on focus groups indicate that occupation-related practices affect men, boys, women and girls differently. Young Palestinian men are particularly affected by physical violence exerted by the military or settlers. Meanwhile women and girls are particularly affected by sexual harassment from settlers and gender-based violence within their own communities and households. Military operations and demolitions also disproportionally impact women and children. Trends analyses indicate that the Projects’ integrated approach is, overall, effective at mitigating protection risks. 68% of the communities targeted to date recorded improvement in protection from forcible transfer, reflected by a decrease in PVI score, while 3% remain stable and 29% regressed. The worsening ratio (29%) was due to a significant deterioration in the protection environment of these communities, generally due to a spike in settler-related incidents. Of note, 20% of the communities are “moving ahead” (vulnerability was reduced and their vulnerability level is below 50) indicating their readiness for transition, a gradual phasing over to development actors.

Activities

Result 1. Protection incidents (demolitions, settler-related, military operations etc.) are prevented – to the extent possible – and their worst effects are alleviated: 1.1 Systematic emergency response to demolitions with in-kind and/or cash assistance in order to cover damages to shelter, livelihoods and WASH assets; and 1.2 Provision of emergency assistance (cash/in-kind), livelihoods support and protective assets to households and communities affected by settler violence. Protective assets are identified and prioritized through a community-based approach, including focus groups with men, women and children. Result 2. The vulnerability of 150 communities subjected to a coercive environment is reduced through: 2.1 A community protection approach to promote greater agency, autonomy and self-protection capacities. The community-based protection approach involves multiple forms of community engagement to ensure the needs, priorities and concerns of men, women and children are captured. 2.2 The provision and rehabilitation of basic and social infrastructure – like water networks and reservoirs, schools, access roads, solar panels and clinics – to improve access to services and restore more dignified living conditions; 2.3 Legal aid to individuals, communities, Local Government Units and Ministries in the areas of housing, land and property rights, residency, freedom of movement and settler violence in order to prevent violations of IHL and IHRL, mitigate risks of aid obstruction and challenge discriminatory policies; 2.4 Engagement with humanitarian and development actors to address critical governance, humanitarian and development challenges in the target communities and support transition efforts, wherever possible. Result 3. Increased respect for IHL and IHRL and accountability for violations are promoted through: 3.1 Rapid response advocacy to mobilize Third States, IOs and Palestinian authorities towards the prevention of demolitions, seizures and evictions; 3.2 Targeted research, policy and legal advice and humanitarian advocacy with Third States and International Organizations to leverage their obligation to condemn violations of international law and to take measures to induce compliance with IHL, with a focus on destruction of property, obstruction of relief, deprivation of services, land confiscation, impunity for settler violence and other practices that generate a coercive environment leading to forcible transfer; 3.3 Policy development support and technical assistance to Palestinian Authorities at both central and sub-central levels to support their role in protecting and extending basic services to at risk Palestinian communities, particularly in Area C; 3.4 Strategic outreach to international and local media in order to raise awareness about the humanitarian crisis and build public support for more robust measures to suppress violations among key constituencies. The Project involves a continuous process of self-assessment and analysis of vulnerabilities and needs, used as an entry point for the development of local protection strategies. These provide a joint-programming framework for needs-based responses by the Project as well as other stakeholders. Beneficiaries are involved as implementers to reduce potential security risks and supplement their income. Accountability is further promoted through complaints handling and feedback mechanisms. Gender/age markers are taken into account during assessments, determination of priorities and M&E processes. Gender-responsive relief and protection activities are undertaken to meet women's specific material and safety needs. The Project aims to reduce the dependency of beneficiaries on humanitarian aid through a phased approach to implementation over 3-4 years, graduating communities from relief once the minimum standards required to enhance protection have been met and linking them with subsequent development cooperation measures, local processes and programs.
