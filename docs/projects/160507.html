Project Summary

The project “Hygiene, Nutrition and Winterization as Cash as well as Psychosocial Support for Conflict-Affected People in need along the Contact Line, Eastern Ukraine” (14 June 2019-13 June 2021) aims to improve food security, hygiene conditions, preparedness to winter and psychosocial well-being of conflict-affected populations in the 5-km zone along the 'contact line' in Eastern Ukraine (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts).
Whereas the project’s food, hygiene and winterization components are implemented by ASB Country Office Ukraine, the psychosocial support (PSS) is provided by ASB’s local partner, Samaritan Union of Ukraine (SSU).
Within the framework of the food security component of the project, vulnerable beneficiaries receive cash for food.
The project is funded by the Federal Foreign Office of the Federal Republic of Germany.

1 - Needs

The conflict in Eastern Ukraine continues to have a negative impact on the socioeconomic situation in Ukraine, particularly in the areas adjacent to the ‘contact line’. Thus, out of 3,5 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in Ukraine, 2,7 million people reside within the 20-km-zone on both sides of the ‘contact line’ (2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview). The current humanitarian crisis in Eastern Ukraine particularly affects older people, with 30% of the affected population being over the age of 60. According to the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster (FSLC), 1,1 million people in Ukraine are food insecure and/or require livelihood assistance. It is estimated that 13,000 people in GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts are still severely food insecure. The highest proportion of moderately or severely food insecure population (15%) is in the 5-km zone near the ‘contact line,’ compared with 10% within 5-20-km zone and 9% beyond the 20-km zone further away from the ‘contact line’ (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2018). As demonstrated by the WASH Cluster Study 2019, the demographic profile in the 20-km zone along the ‘contact line’ (GCA) is characterized by a higher number of older people (42% of the population and members in 67% of all HHs) when compared to the rest of GCA. The 20-km zone had also a significantly higher proportion of HHs in the poorest category: whereas 22% of HHs within the 20-km zone report they cannot always afford food, this is true for 10% of HHs in the GCA as a whole (WASH Cluster Study 2019). For the 20-km zone, food is the main expenditure (39% of monthly expenses for rural HHs and 42% for urban HHs (REACH Economic Security Assessment. March 2019). Household vulnerability increases in winter, when high heating costs negatively affect food security of particularly vulnerable HHs (FSLC’s Guidance Note on Winterisation2017-2018). Thus, in GCA, the share of HHs with poor and borderline FCS increased from 17.8% in June 2017 to 20.9% in February 2018 (Food Security and Socio-Economic Trend Analysis in Eastern Ukraine, March 2018). Food insecurity is more often experienced by vulnerable groups, for example, women, older people, persons with disabilities and chronic illnesses. As demonstrated by REACH Economic Security Assessment (March 2019), income per capita in female-headed HHs is 23% less than in male-headed HHs. In turn, older people tend to spend more on health (55.8%) than on food (26.2%) (2018 HelpAge Baseline report), which negatively affects their food security. Consequently, within the framework of the current ASB project, the profile of the beneficiaries of cash assistance demonstrates significant vulnerabilities in terms of gender and age. Thus, the caseload of beneficiaries selected based on Food Consumption Score, reduced Coping Strategy Index and general vulnerabilities, includes 66% women and 59% older people (60+). Within the 20-km zone (GCA of Luhanska and Donetska oblasts), 74% of households reported having at least one vulnerability, with the proportion being even higher (76%) for the 5-km zone (REACH Economic Security Assessment, March 2019). REACH also identified the possible need for additional assistance for the households with overlapping vulnerabilities, for example, older people with disabilities and/or chronic illnesses (REACH Protection Assessment of Isolated Settlements in Government-Controlled Areas Along the Contact Line, February 2019). Given difficult socioeconomic situation in the conflict area, food security interventions are of utmost importance, especially in the areas close to the ‘contact line’. To illustrate, the FSL Cluster identified food assistance as funding priority for HRP in the second half of 2019. Therefore, ASB will continue to support vulnerable populations in the communities within the 5-km zone along the ‘contact line’ with cash for food.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Purpose: To meet food needs of vulnerable conflict-affected populations in Eastern Ukraine.Output: vulnerable beneficiaries along the 'contact line' received food assistance.Outcome: target beneficiaries improve their food security situation.Activity 1.1. Beneficiary identification Given the multisectoral nature of the project (food, hygiene, winterization), the beneficiary selection methodology is based specifically but not entirely on food security profile of the households. Since food insecure households usually prioritize their food needs over other expenses, the beneficiaries identified for food aid will automatically receive WASH-related assistance. The beneficiary identification for the first caseload of 3000 beneficiaries took place in summer 2019. A new 8-month distribution cycle will start in March 2019. Consequently, there will be two rounds of assessment for the food and hygiene component in 2020:1) January-February 2020; 3) August-October 2020.The beneficiary assessment will take place in Avdiivka, Toretsk, Bakhmutskyi and Yasynuvatskyj raions in Donetska oblast as well as in Novoaidarskyj, Popasnianskyi and Stanychno-Luhanskyi raions in Luhanska oblast. Beneficiary selection will be undertaken in coordination with the FSL Cluster, local authorities and humanitarian agencies operating in the area. ASB will ensure that beneficiaries meet vulnerability criteria identified by HRP 2019-2020 (i.e. older persons, people with disabilities, female-headed households, IDPs).A hotline feedback mechanism will be available to ask questions, request assistance, provide feedback and lodge complaints. All complaints will be recorded in the Complaints Registration Database for follow-up. At the end of each distribution cycle (8 months of assistance), ASB will conduct the post-distribution monitoring (PDM). Households who were the most vulnerable during the initial stage of beneficiary selection, will be re-assessed and, if needed, included into the next caseload. This may eventually reduce the overall number of unique beneficiaries. All assessments, including PDM, are designed to allow age, gender and disability disaggregation.Activity 1.2. Distribution of cash to cover basic food needs There will be 3 distribution cycles of cash assistance, each lasting for 8 months. In each caseload, up to 3000 beneficiaries will receive cash assistance to cover their food and hygiene needs:• Caseload 1: July 2019-February 2020 • Caseload 2: March 2020-October 2020• Caseload 3: November 2020-June 2021Cash transfers to 3000 beneficiaries will take place once in 2-3 months, covering food needs for the respective period. The proposed cash intervention will follow the FSLC’s September 2018 recommendation (UAH 825 per person per month), covering 75% of minimum daily food needs (1,575 kcal). However, due to exchange rate fluctuations, ASB may reduce number of beneficiaries and/or revise the cash value. Within the framework of the previous project “Emergency Assistance to Conflict Affected Populations in Eastern Ukraine” (2018-2019), 98.2% of food security beneficiaries reported satisfaction with the cash modality of assistance. For the population in the 5-km zone near the line of contact (GCA of Donetska and Luhanska oblasts), the most preferred modes of delivery for cash assistance are bank account (48%) and post office (32%) (REACH Humanitarian Trend Analysis 2018). Thus, the modes of cash disbursement used by ASB (Oshchadbank and UkrPoshta) are in line with the preferences of the affected populations. Due to the home delivery of cash by Ukrposhta, older people and persons with disabilities would not need to travel to bank to withdraw their cash grants. If security situation deteriorates drastically, ASB will consider changing the target area or the target group to respond to new humanitarian needs. If local markets are disrupted, ASB will consider switching partially or fully from cash to in-kind modality.

3 - Indicators & targets

The main indicators and targets are the following:• 85% of beneficiaries are satisfied with the aid provided;• 85% of beneficiaries report that the assistance was easily accessible;• 70% of beneficiaries report that they are aware of the complaint mechanisms.• 3000 vulnerable beneficiaries per caseload received cash for food assistance;• at least 75% of beneficiaries have improved food consumption score;• 60% of program beneficiaries are female;• 30% of beneficiaries are older people;• 10% of beneficiaries are persons with disabilities and/or homebound.
