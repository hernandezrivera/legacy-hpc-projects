Project Summary

In 2018 an initiative by a group of deminers created a national non-governmental (non-profit) veterans-orientated organization Ukrainian Deminers Association (UDA). The main purpose of this organization is to solve state problems in the field of mine action (MA).
For this purpose, the UDA is coordinating on all levels: local, national and international. The UDA is a permanent member of MA activities (meetings, round tables, conferences, etc.) under international organizations (UNDP, OCHA, PCU OSCE, etc.) and (MoD, MoI, Ministry for the reintegration of TOT, etc.). The UDA has close cooperation with local Administrations of the Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts.
In response to Ukraine's challenges and threats related to the explosive remnants of the war (see “Needs” section), UDA's experts have developed a plan aimed at increasing the efficiency of the humanitarian demining (HD) process in East Ukraine through the implementation of national capacity building for explosive ordnance risk education (EORE), non-technical survey (NTS), technical survey (TS), and explosive remnants of war (ERW) clearance.
For these purposes, the UDA will deploy 3 HD teams and 2 EORE teams. Moreover, the main equipment of deminers will be provided by UDA’s American partner as a temporary importation of property which in turn will significantly reduce the cost of HD in East Ukraine and allows UDA’s quick deployment.
UDA has teamed with US-based company Tetra Tech and will implement activities based on a MoU between organizations. Tetra Tech currently holds the US State Department global MA contract and offers a wide range of international development services including capacity building support to local national organizations in a wide range of specialities. The primary aim of the Tetra Tech personnel managing this project is to optimize efficiency, effectiveness, and safety, skills transferal and ensuring good stewardship of donor funding. A key aspect of this is conducting training and mentoring of UDA personnel to enable them to manage operations independently in compliance with their own SOP’s, national standards, IMAS and donor regulations.
The M&E process during the implementation of this project will be provided by U.S. company Spilna Mova. UDA signed an MoU with Spilna Mova earlier this year and is already jointly implementing a US Government funded mine action project.
Cross-Cutting Issues:
While developing organizational capacity, UDA will train Ukrainian veterans to perform as a humanitarian demining operator. UDA is looking after the interests of representatives of a large group in the modern Ukrainian society, approximately 400,000 veterans. In addition to physical and mental healthcare issues, a major problem for Ukrainian veterans is finding employment for and enabling their successful reintegration into society.
UDA will also include a strong gender component in 2021 development plan. Inclusiveness and gender will be one of the main points during the implementation. These indicators will be tracked and reported throughout the program.
One of the most energizing capacity development components will be the participation of American counterparts in the training and implementation as consultants and advisors on the best practices on humanitarian demining, organizational and project management, M&E, strategic communications and gender.
UDA understands that effective mine action will contribute to Ukraine’s economic development and, ultimately, to the country’s stabilization. This project focuses on three objectives to support this goal : (1) build a sustainable Ukrainian mine action capacity through well-trained teams operating in compliance with IMAS; (2) increase access to safe land by prioritizing clearance areas for maximum socio-economic impact through proactive and effective coordination with stakeholders; and (3) reduce mine/ERW accidents through robust community liaison (CL) and effective EORE.

1 - Needs

The analysis of the latest report (Landmine Monitor 2019, released in November 2019) by the Geneva International Centre for Humanitarian Demining indicates that Ukraine is among the five most affected countries in the world and needs an immediate humanitarian demining response. Since 2015, there is an active work on humanitarian demining at Ukraine’s East. At the same time, more than 5 years of humanitarian demining have not yielded significant results. People continue to die, and the number of incidents related to the ERW remains high. According to international NGOs (The Halo Trust and DRC-DDG), the number of mine victims in eastern Ukraine reached 2,000. Moreover, the amount of contaminated territory in eastern Ukraine has not decreased significantly. According to the MoD of Ukraine, the contaminated area for 2020 remains almost the same (about 7 million square kilometres).• Since 2014 OCHA estimates 1,077 civilians have been killed or injured by landmines and ERW; more than 200 of these casualties are children• Globally, Ukraine ranks fourth for landmines and ERW casualties, and second for anti-vehicle mine casualties (2015-2018)• In 2019 landmines and ERW accounted for 35 percent of conflict-related civilian deaths.These figures will undoubtedly increase if a political solution to the conflict triggers the return of currently displaced persons to their home communities. The UN Office for Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) 2020 Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) issued in January contains multiple references to how the presence of landmines and ERW negatively impact the current situation and the humanitarian response to it. In particular, the area controlled by the Government of Ukraine (GOU) 5-20 km from the “Contact Line” with 2,000,000 inhabitants “remains heavily contaminated with landmines and ERW” but is home to the majority of basic services and facilities serving the conflict-affected regions. The HRP’s second strategic objective is to respond to the protection needs of conflict-affected Ukrainians. This is in turn supported by five thematic-specific objectives, including 2.4: “Prevent and mitigate physical harm and risks of ERW.” Likewise, the OSCE December 2019 report on the impact of mines and ERW in eastern Ukraine also notes significant contamination around civilian infrastructure “further threatening civilian access to water, gas, electricity and telecommunications.” The Chief OSCE Monitor for Ukraine also called for the signatories of the Minsk Agreements to implement their commitments in relation to landmine use and clearance, and stressed, “more needs to be done” in relation to mine action support to impacted civilian communities.GOU adopted a new MA law regulating humanitarian demining in Ukraine. The law divides responsibilities among state institutions and envisages the creation of a MA Centre. Ultimately, full implementation of the law will create a civilian-led mine action system that is transparent, accountable, and better equipped to set clearance priorities while overseeing the quality of services delivered. The cluster system has been operational in Ukraine since the start of the conflict in 2014 and subsequent humanitarian emergency. The mine action sub-cluster is active and vocal and coordinates activities of various implementing agencies while advocating for additional resources and required modifications to the legal instruments in Ukraine that govern mine action.It should be noted, that UDA will consider OHCHR research concerning casualties as a result of EO. According to GAM the most vulnerable category is male (31-46 ages) that is around 47%, boys (12-17 ages) – 17%, and female (30-39 ages) – 14%. Moreover, the statistics show us that many women are victims of improper handling of male EO.
