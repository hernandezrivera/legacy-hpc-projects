Project Summary

1. Summary
RAGJ shall promote and protect the rights of women, girls and Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) experience diverse forms of violence and living within Boyo, Menchum and Momo divisions in Northwest Cameroon. RGAJ plans to establish a referral system in each division to connect girls, women, and others at risk groups to appropriate psychosocial, legal and other services through networking with key public and private institutions. Hence, prevent and respond to Gender-Based Violence (GBV) occurring as a result of the ongoing armed conflict in northwest and southwest Cameroon.
RAGJ will facilitate access to relevant services and provide support to victims of GBV through the following activities:
1) Establish a Referral System for victims of GBV in three divisions, with three thematic focus – trauma/healing, legal support and reinsertion programs. This will create a safe space for women, girls and IDP survivors of GBV to discuss their concerns;
2) Radio sensitization program ‘My Rights, My Responsibilities: GBV and the Law’ to raise awareness on common forms of violence faced by IDPs, women and girls, how to prevent them, and where to access support in case of violation;
3) Carry out a multisector training on GBV and Law with focus on the four pillars of the UNSCR1325 (i) participation and representation of women in decision-making processes, and (ii) prevention of violence against women through the promotion of women’s rights, accountability and law enforcement, (iii) protection of women from sexual and GBV, and (iv) integration of gender concerns in emergency management mechanisms and processes.
4) Produce a Community Handbook on “GBV and the Law: Grassroots Localisation of UNSCR1325” with information on the legal framework for GBV, legal provisions and advice on specific GBV situations, and its legal and governance implications of UNSCR1325 for women, girls, communities and institutions at grassroots levels.
5) Provide specialized training and support for income generation activities;
6) Publish a quarterly Journal on GBV and the Law, with focus on the protection of the rights of women, girls and IDPs using case studies, best practices, the rule of law, etc.;
7) Carryout a blended He-For-She and She-For-He Campaign that addresses gender inequality with regards to both women and men, and girls and boys.
2. Purpose
Provide protection and remedy to victims, survivors and persons at risks of GBV through sensistisation, training, legal empowerment, economic empowerment and governance programing in Boyo, Menchum and Momo divisions.
3. Target:
• Direct beneficiaries shall include:
o 500 women, girls, IDPs and other at-risk persons of GBV who shall benefit from referral services like psychosocial support, pro bono legal services, health care services, and income-generating skills through the production of marketable produce and emergency kits; and
o 150 women and men, including representatives of local council, relevant government services, justice and security officers, and community/faith-based/development associations will benefit from the training on GBV and the law, which will improve their operational capacities and strengthen multi-sectoral collaboration with regards to the protections of women, girls and persons at risk of GBV violation.
• Indirect beneficiaries shall include:
o Approximately 100,000 people across the northwest region shall benefit from the radio education and sensitization programs on gender, peace and social cohesion; and
o An extended audience of about 2,000,000 people shall be the reached using the online platforms (of RAGJ and project beneficiaries) and traditional media cross Cameroon.

Implementing Partners

 Community and constituent leaders across villages in all divisions in Boyo, Menchum and Momo divisions shall facilitate access into communities and identification and mobilization of project participants, partners and collaborators.
 United Nations High Commission for Refugees (UNHCR) Protection and GBV clusters (and the Access to Justice and Rule of Law working group) shall facilitate coordination of collaborative action around protection monitoring and related activities with other partners.
 The Ministry of Social Affairs (MINAS) shall provide psychosocial therapy for trauma management of GBV survivors and other persons at risk.
 The Ministry of Women’s Empowerment and the Family (MINPROFF) together with partner ministries like Social Affairs, Justice and other Human Rights partners like law firms, CSOs shall assist in handling GBV referral cases and psychosocial and legal counselling.
 The Ministry of Justice (MINJUSTICE) through the Legal Department shall receive referral cases from partner ministries like Ministry of Women Empowerment and the Family, Social Affairs, Law Firms and CSO partners, RAGJ, and from concerned citizens for further counselling and eventual trial where applicable.
 The Ministry of Health shall work alongside other partners in the field of health to handle both GBV cases in communities and provide treatment where necessary.
 MEDIA; local and regional, private radio stations and traditional communication channels (like the use of the village crier or local market announcers) shall be used to call attention on the public to pay more attention to the COVID preventive and curative measures and the concerns of women and girls, especially with the prevailing violence occurring as a result of the ongoing conflicts.

1 - Besoins / Needs

1. Activities • Elaboration of the Implementation Strategy with stakeholders: An initial meeting shall hold to definite role and commitment of constituent leaders across Boyo, Menchum and Momo divisions. A strategy shall be devised to deal with security issues and security clearance into war zones will be obtained. • Establishment of Referral System on GBV: Project team shall liaise with psychosocial and legal services in both public and private institutions to establish the procedure for referral, counselling and support. • Radio Sensitization “My Rights, My Responsibilities: GBV and the Law”: Based on the realities and security state in each community, six thematic radio programs will run on focusing on human rights, gender laws and policies, preventive and curative measures for GBV victims in communities, peace, UNSCR1325, etc. • Production of Community Handbook “GBV and the Law: Grassroots Localisation of UNSCR1325”: 1000 copies will be shared with workshop trainees and project participants whom by default will become Community Facilitators in their respective groupings. • Training Workshops on GBV and the Law in each division: RAGJ shall train 150 (50 per workshop) men, women and youths from faith-based, cultural and development associations, government services officers, local council officers/staff, community leaders and justice and security officers. • Counselling and Establishment of Referral System: Project team shall liaise with psychosocial and legal services, and public and private institutions to establish the procedure for referral, counselling and support. This system shall provide appropriate, timely, safe and easily accessible services to survivors of GBV. • Income-Generation Training and Grant (of USD100 each) to 500 women, girls and IDPs. Training in Small-scale business management; Emergency kits (face masks, detergents, soaps, sanitizer and disinfectant); and Marketable produce (reusable sanitary pads, body lotion, medical ointment, cane baskets).• Focused Group Discussions: Six thematic “Peace Circle Discussions” will be organized in a safe space to enable girls and women GBV survivors to share their thoughts, experiences, challenges and signs of progress in solidarity. • Production of a Journal on GBV and the Law: A compilation of project outcomes shall be capitalized through the production of a quarterly (hard and soft copy) Journal with good practices, testimonies, stories, descriptions, drawings (graphic designs) on GBV as experienced by the women and girls benefiting from the project. • Follow-up on all project activities: Following the initial performance measurement indicators and the pre-implementation assessment of the situation of women and girls (including that of men and boys), monthly project reports will be produced. 2. Expected results • A functional referral system will be established to reporting, support-systems, and resilience-mechanisms to protect and promote women’s rights in crisis and conflict situations;• 500 women, girls, IDPs and at-risk groups will access our support and referral services for psychosocial, legal and other aid on GBV and human rights violation;• An increased number of GBV cases shall be reported, documented and treated;• 500 GBV survivors will gain skills in economic activities.• 500 families will benefit from more confident, emotionally stable and industrious women, girls and IDPs who will increase their family wellbeing, income, pride and motivate others to become self-reliant, especially in times of crisis; • 150 community leaders, government service heads, and justice and security officers will be trained GBV and Law within the context of the implementation of UNSCR1325;• Public and private institutions in northwest Cameroon will adopt more gender-inclusive work approaches as they track and speed-up the handling of GBV cases on and human/women’s rights violations;• Reduced level of stigmatization of GBV survivors in communities.
