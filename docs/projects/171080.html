Project Summary

This proposal is in line with the objectives and the framework of the OPTI country 2021 HRP strategy under objective 2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under IHL. Furthermore, this project is also aligned with the shelter and NFI cluster and SO2: The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources.
The overall project’s objective is to contribute to enhancing the coping capacity and meeting the basic needs of vulnerable households (HHs) with protection concerns due to their substandard housing conditions through shelter rehabilitation/adaptation support, while the specific objective of the project: substandard shelters of the most vulnerable households with protection concerns are supported to meet basic needs and enhance their coping capacity affected by the ongoing Covid-19 crisis including women, men, girls and boys in fragile and critically under-served communities. The objectives are in line with the needs identified in the humanitarian need overview (HNO) and Oxfam’s recent assessments.
Oxfam in partnership with the Youth Empowerment Center (YEC) plan to target 215 HHs with substandard shelters through:
(1) The adaptation and rehabilitation work of substandard shelters for 55 HHs (314 individuals) with persons with disabilities (PWDs) and HHs with people injured during the GMR to meet shelter basic needs and minimum standards.
(2) The rehabilitation works of 160 most vulnerable HHs (912 individuals) with substandard shelters to meet shelter basic needs and minimum standards.
A community engagement approach will be adopted during the implementation of the intervention to ensure a balanced representation including the specific needs, views, and feedback of women and PWDs. Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be activated in coordination with YEC and MoSD to ensure their direct engagement with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the action, such as, the selection process of beneficiaries and collecting their feedback and level of satisfaction during and after implementation, etc.
Oxfam’s Local partner, YEC has extensive experience demonstrated by its team’s capacities in implementing Shelter and WASH projects such as repairing/ rehabilitating 310 HHs main structures facilities (WASH and Shelter) and upgrading two designated emergency shelters. YEC has also gained the trust of communities and local authorities and is therefore able to effectively monitor and implement activities. YEC will sustain coordination with the Shelter Custer Coordinator, shelter cluster members and relevant stakeholders to avoid duplication and joint efforts, and will continue sharing information regarding the progress of implementation through the shelter cluster
The proposed action is highly relevant and can be achieved during the current spread out of the Covid-19 pandemic in the Gaza Strip considering COVID-19 safety mitigation measures which will be monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL and Public Health officers, effective coordination with targeted BNFs, local authorities and relevant stakeholders to overcome the challenges of the lockdown and movement restrictions. Moreover, the action will overcome barriers to accessing COVID-19 information, appropriate procedures to disseminate COVID-19 awareness information, education and communication (IEC) materials (remotely through Facebook; joint mobile calls and WhatsApp groups including Braille for blind people). The proposed action will therefore contribute to alleviating protection, security and public health risks experienced by PWDs and vulnerable HHs with substandard shelters taking into consideration the needs of women and girls.

1 - Needs

The action is informed by Oxfam’s Covid-19 Response Plan (April-September 2020), Shelter cluster Mid-year dashboard (July 2020), the Ministry of Public Works and Housing (MoPWH) assessment (Jan.2020), 5 Ways shelter and settlement programme help mitigation of COVID-19 (April 2020), minimum standards of shelter repair and upgrade (July 2018), inter-agency standing committee’s COVID-19 guidelines on inclusion of PwDs in humanitarian action (inter-agency standing committee in June 2020) and ensuring disability inclusion in COVID-19 response assessment (EducAid in Aug 2020). MoPWH assessment illustrated that 21,500 substandard shelters were identified and found in need for upgrading or rehabilitation works in the Gaza strip. The MOPWH’s recently updated assessment identified top priorities for targeting vulnerable groups including families headed households (FHH), PWDs, and people highly impacted by the deteriorated economic situation and COVID-19. MoSD confirmed that out of the 21,500 substandard shelters, there are more than 500 hardship-households in urgent need for Shelter support in Gaza North and Gaza Governorates. The COVID 19 pandemic also showed the inability of poor families living in substandard shelters to find suitable isolation areas within their homes, particularly the people living in overcrowded conditions. They are in need to improve the covered areas, provision of the hygienic and disinfectant materials and separating the shared facilities like toilets and kitchens. Furthermore, according to (National Society for Rehabilitation) NSR, more than 200 PWDs in the Gaza strip are suffering from different types of disabilities such as amputation, longitudinal paralysis, lower paralysis, quadriplegia, muscle weakness, etc. Accordingly, Gaza North and Gaza governorates include the highest number of COVID-19 cases including most vulnerable HHs, FHHs, PwDs and people highly impacted by the deteriorated economic situation. Thus, the aforementioned governorates were prioritized to be targeted under the proposed action in coordination with the shelter Cluster, MoSD, MOPWH, MA’AN, GVC and NRC. Targeted HHs selection will consider the nominated NSR list of PWDs verified by MoSD. and the nominated of list the vulnerable HHs with substandard shelters provided by the MoSD in coordination with MoPWH. Based on aforementioned and the needs highlighted in the updated HNO 2021, To identify the specific needs of targeted beneficiaries, Oxfam and YEC carried out a rapid need assessment (RNA) in coordination with the MoSD and NSR during the 3rd week of September 2020. Four remote FGDs (2 women and 2 Men) were conducted with 28 (11 women and 17 men) PWDs and vulnerable HHs with substandard shelters living in Gaza and Gaza North governorates. FGDs were carried out remotely through group mobile conversations complying with WHO’s and the Ministry of Health (MoH) COVID-19 mitigation measures. The RNA illustrated that PWDs are the most vulnerable groups at higher risk of getting infected by the COVID-19 virus, with existing major gaps in core shelter-items (doors, walls, roofs, windows, shower/toilet, kitchen, electrical connection and access to WASH facilities) to provide minimum level of physical and weather protection, safety, privacy, dignity, health and wellbeing of male and female family members of all ages, especially family members with physical disabilities. Moreover, participants requested to access adequate COVID-19 information to mitigate the transmission of Covid-19 and protect beneficiaries and others from infection risks. Furthermore, the need to promote women’s participation throughout the project implementation was highlighted to ensure that men are more receptive to women’s representation and participation.

2 - Activities

This project will respond to needs of targeted male and female beneficiaries in the targeted areas including children, elderly and PwDs. Oxfam, in close coordination with the MoSD and NSR will provide shelter adaptation and rehabilitation to most vulnerable HHs with PWDs including long-term or permanent disabilities. Works will be carried out according to SPHERE and (IASC) minimum shelter standards. This will provide safe and dignified shelter to targeted HHs and will contribute to alleviating the burden on women and children and mitigate the transmission of COVID-19 outbreak. The BNFs will be selected considering a combination of protection, economic and shelter technical vulnerability criteria. Factors reflecting protection vulnerability include age, disability, families with high numbers of dependents, women-headed households and families suffering from harsh living conditions including protection related problems while economic factors will include main income, source of income, depts, and the status of household assets. Factors reflecting shelter vulnerability include gaps in core shelter items within the 12 set categories based on the Shelter Cluster agreed Minimum Standards for housing in Gaza.Oxfam and YEC will monitor the works of the contracted company. The company will implement COVID-19 mitigation and safety measures during rehabilitation works. The adaptations include expanding areas for toilets, installing railings and ramps, expanding doors to be suitable for wheelchair access, installing entrance ramps to housing units or WASH facilities, tiling and other specific relevant works, etc.Considering IASC COVID-19 Key messages IEC materials will be disseminated remotely through Facebook, joint mobile calls and WhatsApp groups. Also, 100 blind people will be targeted using Braille. the IEC material will focus on COVID-19 mitigation measures at HH and community levels, best hygiene practices and increasing the sense of ownership to mitigate the transmission of COVID-19. Oxfam will implement the action in coordination with YEC and in collaboration with the relevant stakeholders to ensure access to systems, procedures and standards are in place to achieve effective, high quality delivery. Oxfam will bring together stakeholders, local authorities, end users through active participation in the Shelter clusters and engagement with relevant stakeholders. Oxfam will ensure prober delivery of the action as per the project action plan and budget in compliance with the donor programmatic, visibility, reporting, financial and procurement guidelines. Oxfam will provide advice and support on gender and protection sensitive project activities and gender mainstreaming. YEC will lead the community mobilization actions, the beneficiary's selection process, the engineering technical assessment and announcing the tenders in local newspapers in compliance with the donor, Oxfam and their organization’s procurement regulations and guidelines. Furthermore, Oxfam will ensure that information on reporting fraud and PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) is available to the project and the partner, and they are in line with PSEA network recommendations and according to IASC principles and guidance. 3 - Indicators

Based on Oxfam’s Common global Approach to MEAL and Social Accountability (CAMSA) the project will integrate a comprehensive Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) framework. CAMSA is an approach based on minimum requirements for MEL and Social Accountability aligned to Oxfam’s program quality efforts and provides guidance, tools and methodologies including on gender analysis and assessment of gender outcomes. Oxfam and its partners are committed to the EU data protection rules represented by the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and will enforce Oxfam and partners staff members to strictly consider its rules in all data processing and beneficiaries' database including accountability mechanisms. Oxfam is also committed to participatory MEAL in which partners, service providers, project stakeholders, and beneficiaries have active and significant involvement in monitoring and evaluation actions through continuous and regular consultation in the entire project cycle. All targeted groups, project beneficiaries and stakeholders are considered as influencers to the design, implementation, and results of all monitoring actions including needs assessments FGDs, baseline/end line surveys, project reviews, and evaluations.Monitoring of project actions will be conducted at two levels: Leadership Monitoring: Oxfam’s MEAL staff will lead the design of the project intervention logical framework, theory of change, MEAL planning, MEAL actions tracking, project internal and external evaluations, and learning. Technical Monitoring: Oxfam WASH technical team and partners will ensure the quality of implementation in line with technical standards, action plan and budget as well as baseline and end-line data collection. Oxfam and partners will conduct direct implementation of the project actions and day to day follow up by their field teams and experts.The MEAL framework will promote gender sensitive collaborative and effective, results-focused and accountable MEAL and social accountability practices throughout project implementation. Learning and feedback will also be key practices that will be an integral part of the MEAL framework and will be documented by Oxfam in close coordination with implementing partners. n case of emergency, Oxfam’s emergency MEAL minimum standards as well as minimum gender mainstreaming standards will be applied.. Gender-relevant needs, actions, and issues will be monitored during the project implementation ensuring gender sensitive project reviews and evaluations. Monitoring tools, data gathering analysis, and reporting will encompass qualitative and quantitative gender and age disaggregation and analysis. This will help in tracking performance against indicators, implementation plan. Monitoring tools such as baseline and end-line surveys using comprehensive and complementary tools including key Informative Interviews (KII), Stories of change, focus group discussions (FGDs), satisfaction sessions, and others will be included as relevant. The end-line survey will measure post intervention status of planned indicators and impact on people’s lives in addition to people’s satisfaction on quality of implemented actions and performance of Oxfam and it is partners. Both quantitative and qualitative methodologies will be applied in data collection and its analysis to capture both positive and negative impacts and to what extent the project was the key changing factor. Project partners will be requested to provide monthly, quarterly and final progress reports, which will measure progress against agreed upon benchmarks and milestones.Oxfam’s Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) provides wider access to diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback, and minor or major complaints at project implementation level. This system uses Information Communication Technology (ICT) for receiving feedback either through an online link, verbal feedback or recording complains received through other channels; such as the free helpline, text messages, Missed calls, and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, GBV, misconduct, or fraud, will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and Oxfam global safeguarding team and will be treated with confidentiality to ensure privacy. Moreover, Media and communication tools will be utilized during public awareness sessions to raise targeted population awareness towards safe and responsive feedback tools. However, all received feedback and complaints will be reported and managed by utilizing the ICT tool. Furthermore, minor or major issues will be followed by programmatic action and respond to complaints providers. 9 - Protection mainstreaming
9.1 - Do No Harm

Protection principles are considered in the project as part of the humanitarian principles governing Oxfam’s humanitarian interventions. Oxfam premised its project design mainly on the ‘do no harm approach’ ensuring that project activities or any resulting consequences do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to the essential WASH needs which would have put them at risk, had it not been available, and by conducting ongoing monitoring and collecting beneficiaries’ feedback to be aware of any potential harm and work on prevention measures. . It also takes into consideration the principles of active participation and involvement of served communities, accountability and empowerment. The action responds to Oxfam’s RNA, which confirmed that targeted PWDs and vulnerable HHs with substandard shelters have limited access to basic shelter needs and minimum standards, including improved living spaces to include adequate isolation spaces for people at higher risk of Covid-19. Vulnerability mapping and technical assessment at \HH level will be conducted taking into consideration the safety, dignity and confidentiality of the served population, while also ensuring that the protection of data and humanitarian actors is uncompromised. The technical assessment will also consider other aspects in need of maintenance, installations or improvements in the house which will contribute to enhancing privacy and protection of household members compromised by such deficiencies. The shelter adaptation and rehabilitation works will be carried out while carefully considering the time and methods to be safely accessible for both women and men including PWDs. Consulting people in a participatory manner will help realize these considerations. Maintaining Oxfam’s standards in data protection to reduce the social stigma associated with COVID-19 confirmed and suspected cases, which especially affects women, elderly and PWDs. Accessing COVID-19 information will be enhanced through appropriate procedures (remotely through Facebook; joint mobile calls and WhatsApp groups consequently in addition to using Braille for blind people) targeting project beneficiaries including women, children and PWDs considering gender and age-related needs. Oxfam’s Gender and Protection officer based in Gaza is part of the project team. The officer will be consulted on the planned actions ensuring alignment with Oxfam’s protection guidelines. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, to cope in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will reflect positively on HH members and will enhance their roles, as care givers, which will result in improved family wellbeing, especially children Local community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be activated in the targeted red zones in coordination with YEC and MoSD to ensure their direct engagement with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the action, thus, enhancing community engagement and reducing potential protection risks. Oxfam also ensures that information on reporting fraud, sexual harassment (SH) and sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA) is available to partners’ field staff.

9.2 - Equal and impartial access to aid

As part of the main principles of protection mainstreaming in all of Oxfam’s humanitarian work, special attention is paid to achieve equal and impartial access to aid. This will be done throughout the different stages of the project; the selection of targeted communities is premised on concrete and updated needs assessments confirming the vulnerability and the need of the intervention. Similarly, selecting the beneficiaries follows a clear process with selection criteria and presents equal opportunity for all eligible to benefit. Gender and age are also taken into consideration meaning that women, men, youth, elderly people, people with disabilities and children will be able to benefit from the intervention as fit for their needs without any discrimination based on their gender, age or status. Using a combination of community outreach approach, using virtual platforms for information sharing and actual work in the field will also work to address any obstacles related to the spread of COVID 19 that could hinder access to aid. Having a feedback mechanism available and accessible for all the targeted community will facilitate knowing people’s concerns and comments that could highlight obstacles of access whether physical or related to the processes of implementing the intervention and would inform response to ensure equality and impartiality.
