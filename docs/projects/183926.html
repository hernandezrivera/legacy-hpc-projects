Project Summary

NPO will target IDPs and host communities in Al Fasher & Um Kadada localitys by provision of basic curative and preventive primary health care, and increased access to nutrition assistance for affected children under 5 and pregnant and lactating women (PLW). Integrating Health and Nutrition activities will contribute to saving lives by reducing wasting and stunting levels caused by Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM) and Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM). It will also improve access to quality health care through the rehabilitation and maintenance of health facilities, immunization and health campaigns. The project will work with the community structures to increase community ownership in management and cost sharing. The response will be coordinated with local governance and humanitarian structures, and all activities will meet the needs of the most vulnerable populations, especially women, girls, the disabled, and elderly.
According to the OCHA 2021 report, the people in need of health and nutrition services in North Darfur, 57% are female and 43 % are male , 55% of them are children.
The protracted displacement of IDPs in camp settings and in host communities, coupled with economic hardship continues to makemany families rely on limited basic public services, this resulting in high risks to food security, acute malnutrition and outbreaks of infectious diseases increase in non-communicable diseases. Food insecurity and acute malnutrition continue to negatively impact nutrition outcomes in ND.
the proposed project, will provide an integrated health and nutrition package to support 10 OTP facilities in Al Fasher & Um Kadada localitys. The project will integrates community engagement activities to ensure communities have the knowledge and the skills to prevent or mitigate adverse health and nutrition related health problems.
NPO will address the high GAM rates through nutrition services at the 10 existing health facilities although the SMoH pipeline has always been insufficient to meet the large coverage area. The gap is still exists in staffing, community sensitization on community based management of acute malnutrition and IYCF that justify continued funding by UNICEF. It is imperative for NPO to continue to deliver the integrated services of health and nutrition. for the nutrition services the project will provid with MUAC tapes inludng the mother support groups to ensure all eligible children and PLWs are screened, acute malnourished children under five years of age (CU5) and PLWs (SAM) services are integrated within the health system referred to TSFP nutrition program and Children/PLWs needing consultation for other medical conditions are seen on the spot by medical staff working within the HFs (MAM) cases referred to SC.
NPO will receives all nutrition supplements in-kind from SMOH/UNICEF and will ensure all supported facilities have zero stock out of Ready-Use Therapeutic food (RUTF) The treatment services will target 1840 children (6-59 months) affected by severe acute malnutrition (SAM).This action propose to support routine screening and mass MUAC screening campaigns for active case finding, through various community mobilization techniques (Integrated Community volunteers, community owned resource person, care taker SAM mothers. The screening will be done at community and health facility level. Staff at each health facility will aim to conduct passive screening to ensure all eligible cases of CU5 and PLWs are admitted in the program or referral to SC .

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Mainstream protection throughout the planning and delivery of assistance to returnees and host communities. Through community-based protection approaches that put returnees and host at the centre of decision-making partners will promote community engagement throughout the Refugee Response. The annual Participatory Assessment process promotes participation through structured dialogue on the specific protection risks and assistance issues that different groups face. Gender inequalities are persistent across Sudan. The protracted nature of the conflict, particularly in Darfur states continues to reinforce pre-existing gender inequalities, with women having lesser opportunities. The exits records worrying rates of gendered violence especially sexual violence. The efforts to prioritize gender across the humanitarian response are in line with the government commitment to gender equality, visible through the advancement of laws such as the Criminal Act and the National Civil Service Act. These laws are related to the criminalization of the FGM and the right of abortion in the case of rape. The recent amendment is indicative of the possibility of a Sudan with more gender-equal laws and a better future for girls and women in the country. Taking lessons from the 2020 response, in 2021 will make efforts to address the needs of women, girls, men and boys through strengthening capacity on preparedness and making investments on systems and institutions which support gender inclusion. In 2021 humanitarian operational will continue efforts to ensure that the standards and commitments of the IASC Gender Policy are reflected in the response. Gender will be part of regular advocacy and key messages.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

The project will ensure an effective response to refugees in Sudan the response plans will follow an Age, Gender and Diversity approach by using participatory methodologies to promote the role of women, men, girls, and boys of all ages and backgrounds as agents of change in their families and communities. Specialized mechanisms need to be strengthened in 2021 to address Protection from of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) to handle sensitive complaints. The plan will advance a collective in-country prevention and response approach to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA) in addition to supporting individual entities evaluate and enhance their PSEA programmes. The PSEA Network will establish clear linkages with key sectors and actors (including affected populations) to implement the Joint Framework of Action for PSEA to include: • Ongoing capacity building and development of PSEA network members and focal points on specific measures and resources to enhance awareness amongst staff/personnel on PSEA; create and maintain an environment which prevents SEA;foster a common understanding on reporting. • Advocacy for stronger management and coordination of PSEA within agencies including through: (a) Incorporating PSEA focal point roles and responsibilities into work plans and performance evaluations; (b) Establishing codes of conduct where they do not already exist; (c) PSEA trainings for staff/personnel on PSEA. • Work closely with the GBV and CP sub-sector/actors to ensure that support for victims of SEA is integrated into GBV response services and fully accessible to them. Protocols and guidance will be developed to outline how agencies respond to victims of SEA and referral system.
