Project Summary

Green Hill's WASH project will improve the lives of Rohingya refugees community in Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, by providing clean water, upgraded sanitation facilities, and promoting good hygiene practices. The project will focus on camps 1W, 4, and 17.
The project will follow the WASH Sector's Joint Response Plan-2024, which emphasizes cost-efficient operation and maintenance of existing WASH services, ensuring the quality provision of water and sanitation facilities, and addressing solid waste management through infrastructure support and community engagement. The project will actively promote adequate hygiene behavior and distribute hygiene items using a multi-sectoral approach. In host communities, the project will work to support equitable access to quality WASH services that align with national development plans.
The project will pay particular attention to the empowerment of women and girls by actively involving them in decision-making processes and addressing their specific needs, such as providing essential hygiene supplies.
The project will ensure that all actions and interventions align with Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) guidelines, with a strong emphasis on preparedness and response for natural disasters and public health risks, including disease outbreaks. The project will be accountable to the individuals it aims to assist and will give consideration to age, gender, and disability inclusion. Careful management of the environment will also be a priority.
The project will aim to provide quality water supply, improve sanitation facilities, and promote sound hygiene practices for approximately 22,339 individuals and 4658 HH in four blocks within camp 1W, camp 4, camp 17.
The primary focus will be on achieving high standards of quality and resilience building through active community engagement. The project will also be prepared to provide emergency support as needed, responding to crises such as Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD), scabies, fires, floods, monsoons, and cyclones.
In essence, Green Hill's project will strive to enhance the living conditions and WASH services for Rohingya refugees, tackling significant challenges and working towards the long-term sustainability of these vital services while adhering to established sector standards and guidelines. The goal will be to make a meaningful impact and empower these communities to thrive.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Given the substantial number of refugees, it will remain imperative for WASH actors to persist in their efforts to enhance the quality, adequacy, equity, and dignified access to WASH services for all individuals, encompassing women, men, girls, boys, and individuals of all age groups, including those with disabilities. Water: According to the assessment conducted by the WASH Sector, a striking 86% of the Rohingya population in FDMN camps have reported insufficient water to meet their family's needs. Furthermore, data from the WASH sector monitoring report reveals that 85% of water sources are reported as functional. Nevertheless, only 90% of households have access to biologically safe drinking water. Over time, the declining underground water table has rendered many tube-wells non-functional, particularly during the dry season. Hand pumps remain a primary source for drinking water, bathing, and domestic use. Taking into account these challenges, this project will prioritize the provision of quality water at the household level, recognizing it as a critical component of the safe water chain. Sanitation: As per the WASH Sector recent report, a commendable 97% of sanitation facilities are reported as functional, indicating a satisfactory situation. However, it is essential to maintain the functionality of these facilities through regular repair and maintenance. The fact that one functional latrine serves 22 FDMN indicates a shortage of functional latrines. Solid waste management presents another noteworthy challenge, with only 75 grams of waste being collected per person per day from households. Sixty-seven percent of households report segregating their waste at the household level. Moreover, effective waste and fecal sludge management remains a concern, particularly during the monsoon season. Hygiene Promotion and Community Engagement: According to the WASH Sector Monitoring Report from March 2023, 93% of the community has access to soap for handwashing. As per the WASH Sector Strategy for 2024, 80% of women and girls have access to menstrual hygiene materials; however, the practice level remains unsatisfactory. Access to Menstrual Hygiene Management (MHM) facilities remains a challenge for many females due to stigma, taboos, and conservative practices. In some cases, it has been identified that Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) materials related to MHM are not contextualized. The prevalence of communicable diseases, such as Acute Watery Diarrhea (AWD) and Dengue, is also on the rise at the camp level. Taking into consideration all of these critical factors and identified gaps, GH will continue its existing WASH program in three camps and host communities within camp boundaries. This will be made possible with the technical and financial support from Community Partners International (CPI). GH's efforts will contribute to the operation and maintenance systems required to expand and ensure the sustainability of WASH infrastructure. This will be achieved through active community participation and engagement, utilizing a multi-level approach that aims to identify and address issues at the individual, household, community, and environmental levels in a complementary and holistic manner.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The primary objective of the WASH project is to enhance public health, disease prevention, and protection by providing improved water, sanitation, and hygiene support to the FDMN community. The project strategy will involve adaptive and integrated approaches. Coordination is maintained with various sectors, including WASH, health, protection, education, site management, and food security, ensuring effective and inclusive WASH interventions. The project employs a well-structured camp-level coordination mechanism, collaborating with the health sector for disease control and ensuring efficient site selection. It caters to both the camp and host community populations and emphasizes government stakeholder participation. The CiC offices play an active role in need assessments, approval processes, site selection, beneficiary selection, quality monitoring, community feedback collection, and project completion. Commenced in 2020, the project is set to continue, focusing on continuity and stability. Its distinctive feature is ensuring sustainable WASH interventions through community capacity building and engagement. Regular meetings with diverse community groups ensure accountability. Strong relationships with government stakeholders, WASH Focal Points, SMS, and other actors address service gaps and avoid overlap. The project aims to improve collaboration with government agencies and partners through timely reporting, adherence to regulations, and following government guidelines.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

All four principles of protection will be maintained in this project. This project will cause no harm to the affected community. Community people’s safety and dignity will be the first consideration for any kind of WASH facility construction. Impartiality and equity will be addressed in any services delivery. All the community people will have equal access to the WASH services without discrimination. Non of the population will be left behind in water, sanitation and hygiene services and access. Special consideration will be given to older people. Periodical meeting with different community groups will take place in phases of the project management cycle. Strengthen community people’s capacity through capacity building training will be ensured to empower themselves. Moreover, All staffs and community WASH volunteers will have training and orientation on PSEA and GVB.

3.3 - Age & Disability

This project will be dedicated to safeguarding all individuals, regardless of their age or abilities, and ensuring equal access to WASH services. It will take into account various age groups and their specific needs. A significant portion of the community will comprise children, and the project will place a strong emphasis on involving them at every stage. The expectations and feedback of children regarding WASH facility design will be integral to this project. Child-safe WASH facilities will be designed, accounting for toilet size and implementing protective measures such as walls and fences around open water sources, tanks, or wells. The project will equally consider the elderly and individuals with disabilities. WASH facilities will be designed to be elderly-friendly, with features like road access to the facility, railings for support, handy solar lighting, and convenient proximity for older individuals. In line with an age and disability-inclusive perspective, this project will strive to provide an environment that caters to the diverse needs of the entire community.

3.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

This project is fully accountable to the affected people. This project will ensure all affected people have quality and equitable access to WASH facilities. Affected people’s participation and perception will be included in every step. Discussions with the community in identifying needs, perspectives, and priorities will take place. GH has established a complaint feedback mechanism to ensure community voices are heard. Moreover, community-based monitoring mechanism will be ensured for effective monitoring. Open discussion platform with affected people like majhi meeting, community consultation meeting will be conducted for better learning and effective intervention.

Yes

3.5.1 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

Green Hill (GH) is unwavering in its commitment to ensuring protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA) through a comprehensive mainstreaming approach. This will include extensive PSEA training for project staff and volunteers to raise awareness of the risks and importance of prevention. A strict code of conduct will be established and enforced, applicable to all involved parties, with violations resulting in appropriate disciplinary actions. GH will set up a confidential and accessible complaints mechanism, encouraging beneficiaries and community members to report PSEA concerns while ensuring sensitivity, confidentiality, and a survivor-centered response. GH will actively involve the community in PSEA awareness and reporting mechanisms, empowering them to safeguard their own protection. Collaboration with other humanitarian actors and the government will harmonize PSEA

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

Yes, this project has DRR/environmental elements as disaster preparedness course of action. This project has emergency preparedness activities to reduce the WASH facility vulnerabilities. Site protection for the WASH facility, include disaster resilience feature, disinfection of the water point, tie down etc. Moreover, Green Hill is equipped with contingency stocks (jerry cans, aqua tabs, desludging machine, bamboo, bleaching powder etc.) to provide immediate support during and after the disaster. This project will not make any negative impact on the environment rather proper safety system for the toilet pits, a positive impact will be created. Green Hill will consider average flood level during the construction WASH facilities to mitigate the upcoming disaster risks. Transparency, accountability, and prompt action on PSEA efforts will be a priority, and perpetrators will be held accountable. GH will consider the specific needs and vulnerabilities of different genders and age groups within the community, tailoring protection measures accordingly.
