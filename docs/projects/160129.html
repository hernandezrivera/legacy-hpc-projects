Project Summary

Integrating the perspectives of affected people into response monitoring, through regular perception surveys, analysis and humanitarian integration of findings. The objective of this project is to obtain up to date, actionable information from affected people about how implementation of the ongoing response Somalia is experienced by aid recipients.

Project Activities

Ground Truth’s approach includes the following: i) systematically collecting the views of affected people on key aspects of a humanitarian programme; ii) analysing what they say; iii) helping agencies act on the findings; and iv) communicating the results back to affected communities. This approach should be considered cyclical, offering responding agencies and coordinators the chance to demonstrate course correction and adaption. GTS surveys aid recipients based upon a robust sample adapted from data used for HRP planning. Our survey is not a needs assessment but a performance monitoring tool called Constituent Voice, the only statistically-sound tool currently available for capturing community perceptions on response performance indicators. For Somalia, this is done via phone interviews with a local data collection partner, ensuring that people in hard-to-access areas. For 2020, our approach will be adapted to survey <1,000 aid recipients across disaster-affected areas, using a 95% confidence interval and 5% margin of error, and will be supported by 2 rounds of focus group discussions. Once analysed, key findings will be explored further by partners Africa’s Voices Foundation and triangulated with data from REACH and used to inform programmatic changes within the clusters and coordination system. With chronic needs, insufficient humanitarian resources and an increasingly complex shift toward durable solutions, it is critical for humanitarian actors to know what people think about the response and the relationship between the aid they receive and their future. GTS has support from OCHA, the HCT and the donor group to provide analysed community feedback data to inform planning on course-shifts in the response, reallocation of resources or deeper levels of community engagement. AAP is an identified priority area for the HCT for 2020. In addition to the activities already mentioned, GTS has led a planning process for 2020 to systematise AAP across the response, with multiple partners. The data produced by GTS will be used by media partners to address information gaps, dialogue further using different channels, inform needs assessment approaches on AAP by REACH and supported by the food security cluster to plan specific AAP activities with the ICCG. Without this, AAP across the response will be weaker and not systematised.

Mitigation Measures

This project both provides a feedback mechanism, and supports the humanitarian community to identify gaps in community ability to report concerns across a response through feedback mechanisms. It provides a check and balance on agency level community consultations, taking a ‘temperature check’ on community perspectives of the response.
