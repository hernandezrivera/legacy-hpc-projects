Project Summary

These project interventions aim at the first emergency response to provide protective, inclusive, safe and continued quality education for Covid-19 and conflict affected children in NE Nigeria. The project will achieve improved access for girls/boys to equitable inclusive education and learning opportunities in critical time of interruption of schooling due to crisis and ensure key messages delivered to target beneficiaries on how to protect from Covid-19 and continue quality and relevance of formal and non-formal education through awareness, key advocacy messages, alternative education approaches and back to learning.
As of 30 April 2020, 1728 confirmed cases with 51 deaths in Nigeria specifically in Borno there are 59 cases with five deaths, Adamawa reported 2 confirmed cases and Yobe reported one confirmed case. The Global Humanitarian Response Plan for COVID-19 lists conflict-affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe as high risk, vulnerable populations, due to food insecurity and IDP presence. Currently there are national school closures affecting 46,213,949 leaners (Pre-Primary: 7,159,262 Primary: 27,889,387 Secondary: 11,165,300) About 600,000 IDP children attending some form of learning in the camps and host communities will be affected by the stoppage of learning activities In addition to closing schools the Federal government has closed all non-essential services– including child services which indicates a strong need for integrated education and protection serviced to support the most vulnerable and marginalized children. These constraints are often compounded by critical gender-based barriers to information, services, resources and decision making, and can thus lead to the widening of gender inequalities. Data and needs are emerging each day, however the HRP articulates community communication and engagement as a response gap.
Schools closure put at risk several children who suddenly become out of school. In the IDPs camps, schools (TLS) are at the same time structures for child protection, thus, not only would children no longer have access to education, but also to protection.
The case of girls is more serious, for schools’ closure shall expose them within families to incidents of abuses reducing their chances in accessing the education response that shall be organized. As of now, there is no alternative measure in ensuring continuity of education during this period of school’s closure.
Depending on the duration of school’s closure, children may lose the learning skills, or the entire school year and find themselves into other tasks helping the family.
The following factors may contribute to increased spread: weaken health system, high population concentration, lack of access to potable water, insufficient sanitation infrastructure, and inadequate awareness of prevention measures. This is exacerbated in conflict-affected states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe.
Plan International has developed an Emergency Plan for Covid-19 response, where Education is critical element to respond to this emergency to convey key messages to communities, provide alternative education for children whose education is interrupted due to closure of schools. Plan International supported to EiE WG a response and contingency plan which outlines 3 main objectives:
• Prevent the spread and transmission of coronavirus through and among learners, teachers, parents and SBMC.
• Mitigate/Minimize the impact of school closure due to COVID19 on learning and wellbeing of learners, teachers, parents and SBMC through alternatives solution
• Ensure effective, inclusive and safe return to quality learning for learners, teachers, and SBMC

2 - Activities

Prevent spread and transmission of coronavirus through and among learners, teachers, parents and SBMCs • Development, printing and distribution of IEC and BCC materials (i.e. posters, leaflets, comics) on coronavirus prevention in local language • Development and dissemination of prevention key messages through pre-recorded radio messaging and lessons from ECD to Senior Secondary level • Procurement and distribution of radios- transistor radios and USB connected radios • Development and dissemination of prevention messages through SMS/text messaging • Mobilization and sensitization of Parents, SBMCs and community/tribal leaders (i.e. Principals, teachers, students, parents, PTAs) on infection prevention and control (IPC) using megaphones/posters, banners, pamphlets etc. Mitigate/Minimize the impact of school closure due to COVID19 on learning and wellbeing of learners, teachers, parents and SBMC through alternatives solution • Development, pre-positioning, and distribution of learning materials for learning outside of school/at home (i.e. through Family/community based reading circles) • Provision of psychosocial support (i.e. counselling, group activities, awareness) incl. recreational activities in family • Develop and provide support package to regular teachers (i.e. Incentive, IGA, Hygiene kits, etc.) • Develop and provide support package to volunteer teachers (i.e. Incentive, IGA, Hygiene kits, etc.) • Provide hygiene kits to learners and schools for use of teachers/SBMC members Ensure effective, inclusive and safe return to quality learning for learners, teachers, and SBMC • Rehabilitate/reconstruct 60 schools gender segregated latrines (2 blocks of latrines), latrine cleaning kits and Hand Washing Facilities• Improvement of ventilation in 88 classroom (i.e. through provision of fans or huge windows)• Implementation of Back to School Campaign • Development and implementation of accelerated learning programme/remedial/catch-up programme to make up for lost learning/teaching • Establishment and activation of school emergency plans Networking with State and LGA level Government Officials on COVID 19 Response • Baseline and needs assessment survey in sample communities and end line evaluation • Awareness raising sessions with government officials at State, LGAs and teachers on COVID 19 response, PSS and project orientation • Conducting a lesson learnt workshop with school administration, SBMC members, LGEA officials and State level stakeholders
