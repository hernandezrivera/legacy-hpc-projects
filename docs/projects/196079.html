Project Summary

As part of its shelter response, in line with SO1 Provide lifesaving and life sustaining shelter & NFI support & SO2 Reinforce an enabling protection environment and community cohesion by improving housing and related community/public infrastructure, PUI aims through this project to provide vulnerable, conflict-affected populations with access to improved, safe, healthy living accommodations.
Furthermore, PUI’s FOCOS strategy programming continues to be undertaken in a multi-year strategic way, with 2023 representing the ninth year of implementation of PUI’s integrated-area based approach (IABA) in Syria. Through this approach, PUI makes sure to create synergies between the different interventions implemented in areas in need in order to respond to the needs of the Population of Syria as a whole and to help them to go back to their pre-crisis standards of living, to enhance self-sufficiency and resilience in the long run and to promote social cohesion.
Therefore, the plan for 2023 includes implementing the following interventions:
- Rehabilitation of 400 partially damaged apartments and installation of 400 solar power systems, benefiting a total of 1900 returnees and 300 host community members in several high-need areas in 5 governorates as follows: 100 PDAs in Rural Damascus (500 beneficiaries), 150 PDAs in Dar’a (900 beneficiaries), 50 PDAs in Deir Ez-Zor (300 beneficiaries), 50 PDAs in Homs (250 beneficiaries) and 50 PDAs in Lattakia (250 beneficiaries).
- 200 shelter packages to improve housing conditions of 1200 beneficiaries; 100 packages for 700 beneficiaries in Aleppo and 100 packages for 500 beneficiaries in Idleb.
- As response to any new displacement waves in effects to frontline shifts, PUI will rehabilitate one collective shelters in Rural Damascus based on the identified need, benefitting approximately 200 households (1000 IDPs).

3 - Needs

As a result of an 11-year long crisis, Syria remains a complex humanitarian emergency context with widespread destruction and the largest number of internally displaced people in the world. According to HNO 2023, 5.7 million people in Syria will require shelter support in 2023. In accordance with the MSNA 2022 findings, 34 percent of the overall population live in ‘sub-standard’ shelter, of which 19 percent reside in finished residential buildings classified as damaged and/ or displaying ‘adequacy issues’. Lack of access to electricity/lighting, insulation from heat and cold, notable increase in damages to windows/doors, in addition to lack of privacy, are among the top shelter adequacy issues identified. Throughout 2022, PUI managed to rehabilitate 105 partially damaged apartments in Dar’a and Lattakia. Regarding the collective shelters, PUI have not conducted any new assessments recently, but it is expected that collective shelters will be opened in Rural Damascus

4 - Implementation of activities

In line with the sector’s guidance and with full cooperation with MOLAE and local stakeholders, PUI will rehabilitate a collective shelter to provide adequate living accommodations for residing IDPs based on Sphere Minimum Standards & shelter sector guidelines. The scope of work includes Civil, Sanitary, and Electrical works, sensitization of bathrooms for PwDs or elderlies; and Safety/protection works for the common spaces. After completion of works, PUI will hand over the shelter to local stakeholdersAlso, aiming to improve the living conditions for returnees, PUI will provide shelter packages and rehabilitate partially damaged houses with no structural damages and ownership documents are available, where the selection of locations will be in line with the Sector acknowledgment that location is cleared of ERW. Most vulnerable groups will be prioritized. The scope of work will include debris removal; closing holes; replacing/installing doors and windows; repairing floors, kitchens and bathrooms; and fixing/installing electric systems. PUI will also provide a household solar system to supply a minimum standard level of electricity, sufficient to ensure sufficient lighting inside the shelter and to charge devices.After the completion of works, apartments will be handed over to owners. PUI teams will follow up on the implementation to ensure satisfaction of affected population, and that delivered support is as per agreed standards and guidelines.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender segregated sanitation facilities can lead to increase in GBV and sexual exploitation. (Likelihood: High, impact: High).Mitigation measures: 1. advocate for provision of alternative accommodation/expansion of existing sites. 2. provide gender segregated sanitation facilities sufficiently close to dwellings. 3. provide lighting devices around shelter and WASH facilities. 4. work closely with protection and other sectors to understand the issues and take necessary actions together to address them. 5. conduct consultations such as on siting of facilities with all groups of population.Risk: Safety of beneficiaries/communities are at risk due to structural integrity of buildings and/or the close presence of ERWs to potential work sites. (Likelihood: High, impact: High) Mitigation measures: 1. conduct structural assessment of buildings by qualified personnel. 2. Repair/rehabilitate only structurally safe buildings 3. work closely with local stakeholders involved in ERW clearance to ensure that the building/area is free from ERW. 4. orient contractors/workers on risks of ERW and ask them to suspend the works and report immediately if a suspicious object is found. Risk: Lack of documents to prove property/occupancy rights including civil documentation. (Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium) Mitigation measures: 1. coordinate with MoLAE to ensure that alternative documents to ownership are recognized by all governorates.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk: beneficiaries exposed to SEA harm (Likelihood: low. Impact: High) Mitigation measures: PUI will raise the awareness on existing service providers where victims can seek help; Enforce policies with/through all partners to prevent beneficiaries’ exposure to harm; Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; Train staff on code of conduct; address any SEA complaints. PUI has a zero-tolerance policy towards sexual exploitation and abuse, and is committed to implementing the six principles adopted in 2012 by the Inter Agency Standing Committee Task Force on preventing and responding to sexual exploitation and abuse. Therefore, PUI has put an alert mechanism in place to allow anyone to come forward to report any incident or abuse. Furthermore, and in line with this policy, a specific complaint reporting mechanism for beneficiaries is put in place by the MEAL team.
