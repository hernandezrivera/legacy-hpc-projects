Project Summary

The 2022 REACH (IMPACT initiative) MSNA and information management capacity-building trainings to humanitarian actors (including local actors and INGOs) aim to contribute towards an evidence-driven humanitarian response strategy. Through REACH’s products, actors will be able to prioritize objectives and outcomes based upon empirical research and rigorous analysis products, while the capacity-building trainings will enable humanitarian actors to develop better in house skills for data management and analysis which will improve overall humanitarian targeting, coordination and planning. In light of the rapidly evolving humanitarian situation in Niger, the changing needs of vulnerable groups, and persisting information gaps, REACH proposes to continue supporting the identification of needs on a country-wide scale through completing a country-wide MSNA in 2022. The MSNA gathers information on the needs of different population groups, with the results statistically representative at the regional and department level, to inform the Inter sector coordination groups’ (ISCG) and the Humanitarian Country Team’s (HCT) 2023 strategies. 2022 will also be the third consecutive year of REACH MSNAs in Niger, enabling REACH to strengthen its trends analysis of the evolving humanitarian situation therein. REACH will work under the umbrella of the Information Management Working Group as lead of a joint Task Force co-lead by REACH and OCHA to build engagement and buy-in from various HCT members, sector leads agencies as well as national actors and authorities. The research design will be carried out within the framework of this task force, including the formulation of the methodology, needs indicators, areas as well as target groups. REACH will engage with relevant stakeholders to redefine indicators where needed, in line with evolved sector priorities. As part of the MSNA process, the indicators will be endorsed by the different sectors and sector leads working in Niger based on information gaps and sector strategies. REACH will incorporate stakeholder feedback on indicators by adapting the assessment tool before the start of data collection. Data collected nationwide will be compiled, analyzed and presented in a Multisector Needs Assessment report, several factsheets, maps and an anonymized database to inform the Humanitarian Needs Overview / Humanitarian Response Plan for 2023 and the entire humanitarian community. In addition, REACH proposes to provide humanitarian actors with technical trainings and support on data management to make use of existing information tools and products within the humanitarian response in Niger, as well as manage their own information production more effectively. Humanitarian coordination, whether at the national or regional level, cannot be achieved without coherent and strengthened information management activities that aim to identify the most urgent needs and existing gaps in order to provide relevant and effective responses.

1 - Besoins

In recent years, the Republic of Niger faced shocks that keep the Nigerien population in a precarious situation in terms of livelihood, protection and overall vulnerability. Diffa and Tillabéri regions have been particularly affected by regional crises, in the Three-Border Area and Lake Chad Basin. In terms of Information Management needs, there is a large number of humanitarian organizations responding to crises in the Tri-Border and Lake Chad areas, in a context that is increasingly volatile, and it is therefore critical to collect, analyze and disseminate needs assessments on vulnerabilities to support evidence-based response planning. Addressing the lack of updated data and information management capacity requires both proper analyses of needs, and standard methodologies for assessments such as multi-sectoral needs assessments. Therefore, REACH proposed response is designed to support better information management (IM) among humanitarian actors intervening in Niger where actors face serious challenges in collecting data in a systematic and comprehensive way, often resulting in significant gaps in the information required to design, plan, and evaluate aid.
