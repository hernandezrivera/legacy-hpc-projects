Project Summary

Through community-based and individually targeted protection interventions delivered at the community centres, DRC will seek to address the protection needs and improve the well-being, self-protection, and resilience of vulnerable women, girls, men, and boys.
DRC will target 4500 beneficiaries throughout interventions in the targeted locations. DRC will provide psychosocial support (PSS) to children, youth, and adult caregivers through structured sustained, and unstructured PSS for children, parenting sessions for caregiver activities at the community centres.
DRC’s PSS programs are tailored to different age and gender-related needs and experiences of beneficiaries and aim for equal participation of girls and boys. DRC will conduct structured PSS following DRC’s Under the Rainbow and Youth Program courses with vulnerable children and adolescents to develop their ability to process emotions and express themselves, increase feelings of self-worth and self-confidence, and positively interact with other children, parents, and wider social networks. DRC’s PSS programs are tailored to different needs and experiences of children (6-12 years) and adolescents (13-18 years) and aim for equal participation of girls and boys. In parallel, PSS sessions will be conducted with adult caregivers on stress management and positive and nurturing parenting. Structured PSS will be complemented by unstructured activities such as recreational activities, workshops, and social events around specific themes which will be chosen based on the needs and feedback expressed by the children, youth, and adults who visit the CC. In addition, young people who are interested and committed to coming together and forming a youth committee will be supported to implement community-based initiatives which will be identified by the youth themselves upon reflection on the protection risks that are most prevalent in their communities. The ultimate goal of the CBI would be to contribute to reducing exposure of children and youth to certain protection risks. Unstructured PSS and CBI will also contribute to enhancing the community's social fabric and social cohesion through interaction and dialogue; these activities will also facilitate community engagement and empower the youth to become active and responsible community members.
DRC will provide protection case management services, including individual protection assistance and referral to relevant services, to persons with specific needs (including older women and men, and people with disability) in order to address, and RE. Beneficiaries provided with case management support will likely be at high risk of multiple intersecting protection concerns, due to experience of trauma. Case management and structured PSS – provided at community centres are designed to respond to a different but equally urgent set of individual needs.
DRC will implement Explosive Ordnance Risk Education activities addressing the threat posed by the presence of EO for 7000 individuals, which is a designated priority for Humanitarian Mine Action interventions. DRC RE awareness-raising sessions will be participatory and educational sessions lasting between 40-90 minutes for groups of 5-25 participants, with the help of audio and visual aid to improve recognition of EO identification of risk indicators and factors and safe behaviors. Sessions will be provided to both adults and children, using age and gender-sensitive methodologies. DRC will also provide contractors engaged in construction works with RE sessions to ensure they have the knowledge and tools to recognize an explosive threat and act safely in hazardous situations.

3 - Needs

The conflict in Syria is now entering its second decade, and the number of people relying on humanitarian aid for survival continues to grow, a decade of conflict in Syria has displaced a total of 6,845,888 individuals, averaging around 1.4 million displaced per year inside and outside of the country, People in Need is 14 million. Multiple and compounded protection needs and exposure to, COVID 19 Pandemic, protection risks due to rights violations, and increased reliance on negative coping mechanisms continue to be reported in locations identified for this intervention.The majority of the targeted sub-districts are classified at level 4 of the severity of needs for protection (HNO 2022). Major protection concerns identified through the MSNA 2021 and confirmed by key informants interviewed by DRC in the proposed target areas include lack of civil documentation, family separation, psychosocial distress, child marriage, and child labor. Existing compounded vulnerabilities and the projected deterioration of economic conditions are likely to lead to an increase in negative coping mechanisms and psychological distress (HNO 2022). This is corroborated by the results of the MSNA 2021 (household survey) which shows that according to the majority of households interviewed. Men and boys, in particular, are more exposed to the explosive threat as they are more involved in activities such as farming, herding, moving/traveling, and rubble removal are more affected by contamination.

4 - Implementation of activities

DRC will provide quality and integrated protection services, with a focus on community-based approaches, including awareness-raising, psychosocial assistance, for children, such as under the Rainbow and Youth Program courses with vulnerable, mainly out-of-school children and adolescents with equal participation of girls and boys and parenting programming. DRC will support persons with specific protection needs and other community initiatives, through community centers.DRC will implement child protection case management in the Community center for children at high risk of multiple intersecting protection concerns such as child marriage, risk of violence, and having profound psychosocial support needs due to experience of trauma. DRC will specifically target children and adolescents, boys and girls, including host communities, IDPs, returnees, and refugees. DRC will ensure services are accessible for children with disabilities, victims of explosive devices, unaccompanied and separated children, and marginalized groups. DRC will implement Explosive Ordnance Risk Education activities addressing the threat posed by the presence of EO that is a designated priority for HMA interventions RE awareness-raising sessions will be participatory and educational sessions for groups of 5-25 participants, with the help of audio and visual aid to improve recognition of EO and identification of risk indicators and factors, and safe behaviors.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

DRC has a zero-tolerance policy against all forms of harm, abuse, neglect and exploitation by its staff and associated personnel. DRC is committed to ensuring that staff have appropriate knowledge and organizational support to conduct themselves and their work in a safe and appropriate way. To prevent sexual exploitation and abuse and ensure a high standard of conduct for staff, DRC ensures that all staff are trained on the code of conduct and on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEA). Staff who come in contact with children during their work (e.g. PSS activities and CM) and staff working at the CC will also be trained on the child safeguarding policy. Regular refresher sessions will be conducted to continuously remind staff of the expected behaviour and unacceptable behaviour. In 2021 DRC completed the PSEA partner self-assessment with a final score of 7/8. DRC will continue to prioritize PSEA through the 3 pillars of prevention, reporting and response. A PSEA action plan for DRC Syria will be developed which will focus on training all DRC staff (programme and support staff in all locations) on PSEA; creating awareness among DRC beneficiaries on what behaviours are not tolerated and how to report; reviewing and strengthening the channels of DRC feedback and complaints response mechanism in each field location to ensure the mechanism is known by beneficiaries and that it can be accessed safely and confidentially and without any barriers.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Targeting of most vulnerable not possible due to externally imposed restrictions (e.g. access) Mitigation: Adherence to clear vulnerability criteria; geographical targeting of areas of high vulnerability (e.g. active hostilities, returnee communities, overburdened host communities); dialogue with authorities on adherence to humanitarian needs-based targeting. Assumption: Qualified, well-trained staff are available in areas of intervention Mitigation: Remuneration and non-cash benefits (e.g training) sufficient to facilitate the recruitment of staff in new areas; continual training and supervision ensured to retain and maintain quality. Risk: Humanitarian space is further squeezed with increased restrictions on access. Mitigation: Dialogue with authorities on the importance of humanitarian access and protection space; coordinated advocacy with DINGOs, Risk: significant changes in lines of control create an influx of IDPs into DRC areas of intervention, exceeding the capacity to respond. Mitigation: Endorsed Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan in place; an emergency roster of capacitated staff available.
