Project Summary

Syrian children are now affected by a triple crisis: conflict, economic shock and COVID-19. An estimated 2 million children are out-of-school and face elevated protection risks related to, among others, child marriage and child labour. Ninety per cent of Syrians live poverty. In many governorates poverty is now the biggest reason for children dropping out of school. In addition, the pandemic has had an impact on the continuity of education in classrooms.
Only 54 per cent of the 17,958 pre-crisis schools were functional as of February 2020, causing overcrowding. Some schools operate in double/triple shifts to accommodate displaced children. In 2021, there were 25 reported attacks on schools (mostly in NWS), further contributing to the deterioration of access to education.
Many students are behind in their learning, finding it difficult to return to school or at risk of dropping out. Younger children, adolescents and children with disabilities face further challenges accessing learning services due to limited availability of ECE centres, secondary education services and vocational training opportunities or inclusive education for the estimated 15 per cent of children with disabilities. Key reasons children are not in school include schools not being in their area, schooling not being affordable and children working to support the household, the use of schools for non-educational purposes and other safety concerns—particularly in NWS.
UNICEF proposes to provide education services for preprimary, primary and secondary school-aged girls and boys to enable them to develop foundational numeracy and literacy skills, as well as life and citizenship skills in schools that are safe and welcoming, in order to strengthen child, family and community resilience.
UNICEF will contribute to:
A. Access to inclusive learning
B. Quality learning in safe and inclusive schools
C. Resilient Systems
Access will be promoted through Back-to-Learning Campaigns, Vouchers-for-Education, school textbooks and school supplies, support for national exams, rehabilitation of schools (formal and nonformal) and the provision of temporary classrooms. Schools will be furnished, equipped with gender-sensitive WASH facilities, and established in particularly vulnerable areas (Severity Scale 3-5). Rehabilitation is also needed in lower severity areas, where displaced children and returnees as well as large numbers of host community children reside, to contribute to resilience and social cohesion. UNICEF will ensure “safe access” through the clearing of UXO and COVID-19 free physical access will be guaranteed through sanitization of school buildings.
To improve the quality of education services, UNICEF will ensure schools are child-friendly, with teachers trained in child-centered pedagogy, and where children develop academic, cognitive, life and psychosocial skills that empower them to engage, relate, cope and contribute to community and family life. Due to the large number of children at different learning levels within grades, children will be provided with remedial learning support. Corporal punishment and violence in school will be tackled. Nonformal education will give adolescents the chance to find employment or re-integrate into public schools.
UNICEF will strengthen the resilience of grassroots education systems, facilitating bridges between schools and communities through the establishment of Parent Teacher Associations that cooperate with Headmasters, and by training education staff in monitoring schools, staff performance and children’s learning.
A continued failure to invest in education infrastructure, services and quality will result in squandered opportunities to help children learn and thrive. A substantial number of children will stay out of school, and abandon intentions to ever return.

4 - Needs

Protracted conflict, economic distress and COVID-19 continue to impact families, communities and education services. There were 25 verified attacks on schools in the first nine months of 2021 (HNO 2022). Displacement, damaged schools, overcrowded classrooms lack of affordable transportation and COVID-19 contribute to 2 million children remaining out-of-school and 1.6 million children at risk of dropping out (OOSC Survey, 2021). Children with disabilities are particularly affected. 54 per cent of school-age children in NWS are out of school (Draft 2022 JENA). Only 55% of the 17,958 schools registered pre-crisis were functional by February 2020 , with few functional schools in NES and NWS. The 2022 HNO national self-reported attendance is 82%, similar for boys and girls, but worse for older children than younger ones. Economic factors, including children working, drive non-attendance. A quarter of households indicate their children are not in school because there is no local school. There are only 196 schools for 1,017 camp sites in NWS. Many students learn in unconducive and/or unsafe learning environments, with only one classroom for every 54 school-aged children. A third of schools operate in shifts. Children also face risks when traveling to school. Less than half of caregivers feel their children are sufficiently learning. 60% of households with school age children cite a need to enhance teacher capacity.

5 - Implementation of activities

For 2022 and 2023 UNICEF will target 2,911,050 children a year, as well as teachers and caregivers. For light rehabilitation and prefabs (inc. furniture), UNICEF will work with MOE, providing technical assistance, monitoring, supplies and contracting (as needed), and ensuring targeting in line with the severity scale. UNICEF will provide supplies (inc. textbooks) and run the SLP through:• CLCs during or after hours. • School premises after hours • Ministry of Culture Integrated Learning Centres. • A ‘room in a neighbourhood house’ SLM are distributed in areas outside of government control, with government consent. The materials are being updated, aligned with the National Curriculum and digitalized. Teacher trainings, with MOE, focus on pedagogy, classroom management and PSS, or Life Skills, or learning assessments. For National Exams, UNICEF will contribute to Sector efforts to support crossline and cross-border students. PTAs will link parents and communities with government schools. For vouchers-for-education see Section 12. To mitigate the risk of COVID-19 in schools and NFE centres, UNICEF will ensure:• Gender-segregated hygiene facilities• Handwashing with soap • Disinfection of facilities• Sanitation supplies In Syria, including NES, UNICEF monitors through partners, TPMs and feedback mechanisms. In NWS, TPMs provide on-site verification and beneficiary interviews. UNICEF uses a WOS IMS to consolidate and analyze data.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UNICEF as co-lead agency for the inter-agency network (ICN) on PSEA, will continue to support and work closely with other agencies aiming at enhancing the portfolio pf protection from sexual exploitation and abuse. The main goal of the ICN is to enhance the networking for the benefit of people at risk, and ensure access to complaint mechanisms and specialized services for victims. Therefore, continuous capacity building for humanitarian workers on zero tolerance for SEA and awareness raising for the community on rights to humanitarian aid free of any exchange in dignified way is integrated in project activities. Consequently, as per-condition for contracting implementing partners, PSEA assurance and capacity building plan are guaranteed.
