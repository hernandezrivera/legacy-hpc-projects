Project Summary

Save the Children (SC) aims to ensure the safety and dignity of the refugees through delivering transitional shelter assistance or repair and maintenance (TSAII or new intervention with the support from IOM as pipeline partner in camp 8W) and shelter construction for EVI’s to 150 households out of 500 in camp 8W and 17. SC shelter team is well versed with PSEA training. As a refresher, SC will again arrange training session for the team. SC will seek support from ISCG to conduct an effective PSEA training for its staff.
Community session on sustainable safe shelter and basic child safeguarding sessions will be provided for both male and female groups around camp 8W and 17 within 500 HH. For male members, masonry and carpentry training will be given to 50 people (15 from 8W, 35 from 17 among the 500HHs). For female members, they will be provided with basic DRR session and basic shelter maintenance session among 500 HH in both camp 8W and 17 to create awareness and ownership towards their own shelter. A skilled female labor pool of approximately 15 female members (5 from camp 8W and 10 from camp 17) will be created for shelter construction with necessary training and technical guidance. SC expects these female group can contribute with shelter works in future as well. Child centered DRR (CCDRR) activity will be provided to adolescent children around both (involving children in consultations, developing awareness materials with pictures and child-friendly language, engaging them in activity-based learning and enabling them to act as passive first responders) camps among approximately 1180 children within them 600 are boys and 580 girls. Gender and inclusion through community awareness session and participations will be conducted with 1564 individuals (both adult and child) to establish an inclusive approach and understanding among them. SC will implement small scale SD work among both camp 8w and camp 17 (includes HH level drainage, access way, site protection) within the 500 HH to ensure safe access where PwSN will be addressed.
SC will monitor the distributed materials (under IOM pipeline agreement), by the community mobilizers, in the distributed areas (camp 8W) to ensure that distributed materials are safe and not sold to the local markets. SC will consult with respective community (8W) to identify need and shelter materials, SC will engage community for the quality assurance of materials and the storage of the materials (with security) as well in case of providing Shelter (reconstruction/new construction at camp 8W and 17) support. SC will ensure protection, gender equality and inclusion and accountability to affected populations are central and cross-cutting in the proposed actions and implementation. Through adopting accountability mechanisms community meetings, hotlines, helpdesk, children’s consultation, and post distribution monitoring) which are accessible to even older persons and people with disabilities to raise their complaints in a safe and dignified manner, SC will collect information from the affected populations regarding their views on the services. SC will address those feedbacks through proper initiatives.

1 - Identified Needs

Despite previous shelter interventions, the settlement area still needs significant shelter repair/upgradation. Issues with shelter remained a common concern for the majority of households, with almost one third of households reporting having bought shelter materials to make repairs. More than a quarter of households reported not being able to make improvements despite reporting issues, largely due to limited access to materials. All shelters are needed to be safe, repaired/upgraded with materials that are resistant to short term deterioration and can withstand during adverse weather, as 72% of households reported having faced issues with their shelter in the 4 months prior to data collection (J-MSNA, Refugees, 2021). Provision of support for EVIs during the repairs/upgradation of shelters is required. Assessing needs from different gender and age groups to incorporate those needs in the shelter intervention process is highly required. Treated bamboo for the longevity of the structures: To enhance the better performance of these shelters, treated bamboo is needed to enhance the duration of a robust structural approach. Shelter requires a thorough maintenance to sustain its quality for a certain period of time. Otherwise, the quality and strength of the shelter deteriorates. The communities are needed to be aware of the use of construction tools and materials, and the knowledge of maintaining safe shelter. As such, there is great need of rolling out training and orientations of safe shelter maintenance. The beneficiaries need to be aware about DRR, environment and sustainable living. Hence the beneficiaries also needs to have sessions for them to introduce and aware them about DRR, environmental issues and sustainable living. Due to the geographic difficulties, camps are always at risk of natural disasters, community awareness sessions or disaster preparedness are required to improve living conditions of the refugees with protection and safety.
