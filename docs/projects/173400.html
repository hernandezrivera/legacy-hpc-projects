Project Summary

The project will contribute to creating an environment that prevents, responds to and protects children and adolescents from various child protection violations, forms of gender-based violence and sexual violence in Rohingya camps and its associated host communities.
Interventions will be focused on strengthening existing of community-based child protection mechanisms to work effectively to prevent and respond to child protection and related SGBV affecting children and adolescents, will building capacity of adolescent groups and their caregivers to be able to able to prevent and respond to child protection issues and access to quality case management services.
The project aims at enhancing understanding of child protection issues among children, adolescents, parents and caregivers, will create an efficient community-based child protection mechanism and identify and refer children at risk to the appropriate child protection service providers. The project will integrate DRR in the capacity building packages for target groups. Do no harm principle will be assured in engaging children and adolescent in the project interventions.
EDUCO believes, the creation of equitable gender relations and gender equality must involve boys, girls, men and women. The project involves boys and girls, children with different abilities, men and women; empower them in both formal and informal leadership positions to support overcoming protection barriers.
Intervention will be implemented in camp 8E, camp 15 and camp 18 in collaboration with local partners ACLAB, FIVDB and relevant government agencies.
Build on the learning from previous projects, that EDUCO has been implementing in the Rohingya community, this project targets 14,000 children and adolescents (50% girls), who are deprived from access to protection services and 9200 of their adult care giver and community members. Focused on Child rights, specially, right to Education and protection, EDUCO has been working in Bangladesh since 1999 and involved in Rohingya response in 2018.

1 - Identified Needs

Since October 2017, total of 862,277 Rohingya refugees has been living in 34 camps in Cox´s Bazar, of them 52% are children (Joint GoB - UNHCR Population Factsheet). In the overpopulated camp settings, children and adolescent specially girls are highly exposed to neglect, abuse, exploitation, trafficking, including sexual and gender-based violence (GbV). Forced displacement and current living condition in the extremely congested refugee camps exacerbates protection risks for the children and adolescent specially for the adolescent girls and women and made them vulnerable to abuse, exploitation, violence, including sexual violence and neglect. Children have reported feeling unsafe and vulnerable to violence, including sexual violence. Girls, who represent a larger proportion of this vulnerable group, are particularly at risk of psychosocial distress, neglect, and abuse, separation from caregivers, sexual violence, child marriage and labour. Rohingya household consider child marriage a normal practice and some cases a copping strategy to protect girls or reduce burden on the family. 16% of HH reported an increase in child labour in their community in last six month during COVID 19 epidemic. There is increased trend of child marriage reported in JMSNA (9%), while 16% household reporting an increase in child missing cases, which may have lined with trafficking. 5% household reported overall increase of violence and children. Gender-Based Violence continues to be a constant threat, especially to women and girls in the camps. The reported GBV incidents include rape, physical assault, sexual violence, forced marriage, denial of resources, and psychological abuse. GBV is generally under-reported, the recorded cases are likely to represent only a small fraction of the overall number. Adolescent girls, who are more vulnerable to child/forced marriage, trafficking, and sexual violence face heightened obstacles to access services. Several studies also found that Rohingya men and boys are subjected to different forms of sexual violence and the multifaceted impact of violence. The security environment in and around the camps impacted negatively on the well-being of both Rohingya refugees and host communities, particularly for women and girls, during 2019, limiting access to public spaces and basic services.13% of household reported any security concerns since the COVID 19 outbreak. More importantly, there is lack of safe and non-violent home environment for children and adolescent and the level of domestic violence against children is high in the Rohingya camps. Adolescents have inadequate access to information about legal rights, services, and complaint and feedback mechanisms, which makes them less confident to come forward and seek justice. Structured community-based protection responses is required to ensure access to basic services for children with specific needs as one third of the population exhibiting vulnerabilities. Side by side, prolonged crisis has been equally impacted the host community specially the most vulnerable groups and forced them to cope with harmful practices including involvement in child labour and child marriage and restoring to smuggling trafficking networks. Child protection and gender-based issues have been identified in host communities. To address the target people in need on child protection knowledge, skill, understanding, awareness and access to services both in Rohingya camp and host community Educo will support a community-based approach, leading awareness raising on key child protection issues with a strong gender focus and strengthening community networks by building on communities' own capacities and processes, targeting adolescents to build their resilience and empower them to lead community awareness sessions, empowering community leaders and sensitizing families on issues related to child protection.

2 - Activities or outputs

Activity 1: Community based child protection mapping exercise to identify to identify sub-vulnerable groups and Action planning workshop with the community level Community Based Child Protection Mapping assessment is an effective tools to identify sub-vulnerable groups of children, girls and other, as well as identifying local practices, capacities and processes when dealing with child protection issues within the targeted community, the project will undertake such assessment in the Rohingya community living in Refugee camps and host community.Activity 2: Develop/update life skill-based module on child development, child protection and IEC materials development to support awareness session and training: Curricula and module will be developed and printed for adult (CBCPC members, parents and religious leader) on Child Protection, Gender Based Violence, Sexual Violence, Domestic Violence, Child Development, Positive Parenting, Child Rights, Women's Rights Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Prevention and Response to Covid-19 as about gender and positive change in behavior.Activity 3: Formation and provide support CBCPC with capacity building and training:The project will identify community leaders, religious leaders, parents, and caregivers and mobilse them into the Community Based Child Protection Committee (CBCPC) with 20 members (50% female) in each subblock of the targeted Rohingya camps. In camp total 90 CBCPC will be formed with active and voluntary participation of 1800 (50% female) community leaders, parents, and caregivers in targeted Rohingya camps and total 20 CBCPC of 400 Community members (50% female) in two unions of Host community. Project will undertake capacity building program (training curricula, content and IEC materials) for training CBCPC on their roles and responsibilities, child/adolescent protection issues and SGBV, (for example, early marriage, child/adolescent labor, domestic violence), existing referral mechanisms and the case management actors and services.Activity 4: Support mass awareness raising on child protection issues and GBV against Children through Community Radio Naf 99.2 FM and Support children, adolescent and youth lead Campaign on Ending violence against children (EVAC).Activity 5: Establish Child help desk with Government Social Welfare office at Ukhiya and ensure a feedback mechanism will in in place in every sub-blocks in the targeted camps to ensure children and community can share their opinion, concerns and reactions which will mitigated in consultation with them by the project. Activity 6: Adolescent group formation, capacity building on-life skill, resilience activities etc.-The project will mobilize adolescent ages from 12 to 17 preliminarily and form 110 adolescent groups (90 in camp and 20 in host) with 20 members (10 boys and 10 girls) in each. Activity 7: Establish/ renovate and operate multipurpose center (MPC): For adolescent, 3 adolescent and child friendly facilities with learning and recreation materials will be set up in camp and host community. 750 Adolescent girls and boys will be supported with life skills, prevocational skills, resilience activities in MPCs. Activity 8: Support caregivers’ skills enhancement to allow children to become more supportive to child protection needs and create a safe and non-violent home environment . Activity 9: Capacity training on Child protection and GBV against children with different stakeholders/CP actors.Activity 10: Coordinate and support existing case management and referral system - Affected and at-risk girls and boys specially adolescents girls will have access to quality age, diversity and gender sensitive child protection services.
