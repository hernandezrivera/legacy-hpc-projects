Project Summary

IMAAR proposes a 12-month project that uses a flexible programming approach that includes upgrading IDP sites/camps.
IMAAR will contribute to one objective of the Shelter/NFI sector which is:
SO1) Provide life-saving and life-sustaining shelter and NFI support.
Implementation of HH level improvements (Tent graveling) and Implementation of site-level improvements/ infrastructure (Infrastructure in IDP sites).
The project formulation has been built based on the HNO 2022, needs identified by the SNFI cluster, and supported with Rapid Need Assessments conducted by IMAAR between Jan-2022 and firsthand consultations with camps management and local authorities/stakeholders.
The proposed project aims to address the emergency and more sustainable shelter needs of most vulnerable IDPs with acute needs, living in Dana sub-district (Idleb Governorate) in NW Syria.
IMAAR’s shelter program recognizes the different needs and vulnerabilities of boys, girls, men, and women of different ages and strives to address the specific needs of each group across the range of shelter interventions.
Considering their vulnerability, IDPs living in IDP sites/camps, female-headed HHs, PWDs, and elderly persons at risk will form the priority groups of IMAAR’s shelter program, particularly those among the high numbers of most affected by winter severe weather to provide core Shelter assistance. IMAAR considers that the above-mentioned groups are the most vulnerable as they do not have the same access to livelihood opportunities. Therefore, their capacity to access Shelter assistance is lower, forcing females to resort to negative coping mechanisms, therefore this program will target at least 25% female/child-headed HHs & 30% HHs with PWDs/elderly members from the total number of the targeted HHs.
IMAAR’s IDP sites/camps improvement response aims to provide critical services to 2,400 IDP HHs/12,000 individuals living in 18 prioritized IDP sites/camps affected by poor weather conditions in the sub-district of Dana in Idleb Governorate to ensure safe and dignified living conditions for the most vulnerable people in NW Syria, with a focus on appropriate preventive measures to alleviate the effects of flooding and prepare for winter through tent and roads gravelling, where needed.
Equally, the basic shelter repairs will enhance sustainable, recovery-oriented shelter solutions will contribute to the rehabilitation of all flood-affected IDP sites, IMAAR’s shelter program aims to address the shelter needs of 1,000 vulnerable HHs/5,000 individuals through basic shelter repairs through tent gravelling activities.
This component will be guided by the shelter rehabilitation guidance published by the SNFI cluster in 2018 and ensure that HLP documentations are well carried out in parallel with field coordination with the relevant local councils.
The program will seek feedback from both male & female beneficiaries and fully interact with both genders to ensure equitable access & equal opportunities.
The overall target number of beneficiaries is 12,000 individuals (2,730men, 3,102women, 3,010 boys, and 3,158 girls). 12,000 individuals supported through IDP sites/camps improvement through gravelling of all camps paths and roads – there are 5,000 individuals of the total target beneficiaries that will be assisted by basic shelter repairs (tent levelling and gravelling).

4 - Needs

According to the SNFI HNO 2022, IDP sites are designed to function as a temporary option of last resort, providing only a minimum level of services. However, in northwest Syria, close to 1.7 million people live in 1,389 IDP sites with inadequate access to shelter, safe water, food, health and psycho-social support) in northeast Syria, there are nearly 290,800 individuals residing in last-resort sites with many have been displaced for up to 4 years. Access to basic services remains a challenge and there is a need to improve living conditions to uphold humanitarian standards. In northwest Syria, 83 per cent of IDPs are living in sites with a critical level of population density. In northeast Syria, there has been an increase of 26,600 IDPs in camps and sites between January and November 2021. IDP sites are particularly vulnerable to incidents, like floods, fires and high-speed winds.Infrastructure upgrades to tented settlements particularly in NWS remain underfunded. Upgrades such as roads gravelling, ensuring accessible are critical to prevent flooding during the winter season.Moreover, according to the RNA conducted by IMAAR in Jan 2022 on 25 camps affected by floods during the current winter in Dana sub-district, Idleb: -IMAAR found that most of the roads inside the camps are in a very poor condition, not graveled and most of them have agricultural soil, not straight and the water accumulates in it.

5 - Implementation of activities

IDP sites/camps improvement: This will include community engagement, topographic surveys, finalize BOQs, HLP due diligence, Design of infrastructure rehabilitation plan, The present activity will be guided by the good practice guide for infrastructure rehabilitation shared by relevant cluster and the Basic Guiding Requirements guidance to Reduce Flood Incidents in IDP Sites in NWS shared relevant clusters in April 2021 and consists of designing a detailed, technical rehabilitation plan, developing the detailed bill of quantities, technical specifications, and scope of works through conducting technical assessment and topographic survey of the infrastructure works needed via specialized engineering and design services team.procurement process, implementation of rehabilitation of 18 IDP camps affected by poor weather conditions through tents and roads graveling & infrastructure improvements, where needed via qualified contractors, supervision & monitoring by IMAAR. Handover of the rehabilitated works to the camp management. Provision of maintenance services by the contractor as per defects-liability clause of the contract. Forming maintenance committees for the rehabilitated works in IDP sites.The activity of Implementation of site level improvements/ infrastructure will target 12,000 IDPs living in 18 IDP sites by internal road and paths gravelling, the tents gravelling activity will cover 1,000 IDP HHS- 5,000 IDP individuals, based on the needs assessed in targeted sites.

10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Risk1: Sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. MS: Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, Train staff on code of conduct, Code of conduct in all staff contracts& Address any SEA complaints.Risk2: The risks of GBV can be compounded by overcrowding and lack of privacy. In multi-household dwellings, lack of doors and partitions for sleeping and changing clothes can increase exposure to sexual harassment and assault. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. MS: Advocate for appropriate and adequate assistance to be provided. We will include women, girls, and other at-risk groups to determine such things as if systems/criteria are in place to determine how shelters are being allocated; if they are built for safety and privacy; and/or if shelter materials and shelter-related NFIs are being distributed in areas that are safe, Consultations with women, girls, boy, and men should be organized & community safety audits.Risk3: Crowded living conditions and lack of lighting and gender-segregated sanitation facilities can lead to an increase in GBV and sexual exploitation as well as communicable diseases. Likelihood: High. Impact: High.MS: provision of gender-segregated sanitation and hygiene facilities sufficiently close to dwellings, provision of lighting around shelter and WASH facilities, and work closely with protection and other sectors.
