Project Summary

The proposed intervention is in line with, and contributes to 2023 HRP Strategic Objectives to provide lifesaving NFI/shelter assistance to crisis-affected population and to enable these crisis-affected populations to access adequate shelter solutions that provides protection, safety, security and space to live in a dignified manner. Project operations in West and South Kordofan will support vulnerable individuals and respective households affected by both natural disasters and man-made crises. Targets shall include vulnerable communities in need of adequate shelter solutions and life-saving Non-Food Items. The yearly rainy season has brought and will bring grave negative effects on the population in the above mentioned states (with specific regard to West Kordofan). Providing environmentally friendly, durable and locally sourced shelter materials and solutions will constitute the main focus of the proposed plan, working closely with key partners (i.e., INGOs, NNGOs, government entities, federal and state HAC, localities, ES/NF Icluster coordinator and partners at both federal and state levels), particularly for returnees and/or IDPs. Sustainability of the interventions will be fostered with the aim to enable shift from emergency settings and operational modalities, once most urgent needs are addressed. Wherever feasible and cost-effective, the plan shall explore possible options of cash provisions and/or using a voucher system to facilitate access to local shelter materials In West Darfur, the project will continue supporting long-term IDPs which have been affected by most recent and longstanding violence outbreaks due to tribal conflicts. In West and South Kordofan, the focus will be on providing shelter and NFI support to flood victims, as well as supporting NFI/sheltering needs of host communities made vulnerable by the overall contextual insecurity. The poor shelter conditions in temporary convergence points/locations will be targeted with emergency shelter rehabilitation kits, to be complemented by NFI support to ensure safety/security and restore dignity of beneficiaries/programme participants. The provision of shelter and NFI materials will help vulnerable population restore their dignity and reduce their exposure to protection and health hazards. Highly vulnerable population with specific regard to (PLW) women (as well as single woman lead HHs), girls/boys, elderly and people living with disabilities constitute the primary focus of the plan. Protection will be mainstreamed across all interventions to ensure that rights of vulnerable populations are understood and secured at all levels (e.g., CRM, PDM, segregated spaces, disability/femininity friendly spaces, dignity kits among NFIs, etc.). Concern will procure and store shelter and NFI materials as much as practically possible in the basis close or within the intervention areas. Minimum standards for Shelter and NFI kit packages will meet Sphere standards and will be harmonized with the ES/NFI cluster at both federal and state levels as per previous interventions. Synergizing activities with sectors WASH, FSL, and H&N is standard in Concern operations.

1 - Needs

Sudan has been struggling with political and economic hardship that resulted an increase of people in need of humanitarian assistance to 14.3 million people according to (UNHRP 2022). According to IPC, a large part of Sudan and our programme areas -West Darfur, West and South Kordofan will face crisis level food insecurity (Level 3) between October 2022 and January 2023 with a high probability of escalating in 2023. UNDP HDI rates Sudan among the ‘low human development countries’ placing at 170 on the 189 country-scale that creates urging needs for life-saving support for million. The country has been facing massive displacement with post the war which started on 15th April. More than 1 million people have been internally displaced and also more than 300,000 people being refugees in the neighboring countries of South Sudan, Chad & Egypt. Apart from the economic and political turmoil, ethnic conflicts over limited natural resources particular in West Darfur state is immersing with a displacement of 600,000 people in our operation area. Protracted refugee settlements in both West and South Kordofan states continue pressing limited natural resources on host community population, and climate driven challenges e.g. flooding will continue affecting the limited services. Projections for 2023 will regrettably align with 2022 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), which identified 3.7 million people in need of shelter and non-food item (NFI) assistance throughout the year countrywide. This includes IDPs, refugees and asylum seekers, vulnerable residents, and returnees. By the end of September 2023, 211,133 were internally displaced by inter-communal conflict while 48,406 new refugees and asylum seekers arrived to Sudan. Furthermore, 24,859 families lost their homes due to flooding while as much as 348,724 people were affected. In 2023, due to continuous deteriorating political situation, continued internal displacement and refugee influxes, it is likely that shelter and NFI needs will continue to increase, while funding will keep on shrinking. While partners are working to the last mile to provide assistance, gaps still exist in terms of funding (the Shelter NFI sector was 4 per cent funded), stock availability for responding to new and sudden displacements, limited partner presence in some areas, and overall volatile security and economic instability. Challenges for the next year will relate to these gaps, and will be exacerbated by recurrent access constraints, as well as climate change and needed adaptation/mitigation as local materials used for emergency shelter construction are not available throughout the year and are bulky to store. Hence, there is a challenge to provide climate-suitable shelter materials, especially during the rainy season. Furthermore, the use of local materials such as wooden poles poses a risk to already stressed environments in some states. These are trends which Concern is observing in all operational areas, and which are affecting displaced individuals needing dignified sheltering solutions and non-food items replenishment.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

The assessment, planning and implementation of shelter and NFI activities will factor the environmental impact of the intervention, and will aim at mitigating unintended arms by ensuring synergies with forestation activities and selecting durable/locally available shelter/NFI materials with low environmental footprint. The materials and construction methodology will be defined, selected and adapted to prevent over-exploitation, pollution and degradation of environmental conditions. The shelter materials are generally locally available, are easy to use for self construction, could be dismantled/disposed easily over time, and will not cause any environmental hazards.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by Code of Conduct and associated policies. Concern’s Code of Conduct sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. Concern also updated its Child Safeguarding and Child Protection Policy that will be applied for this intervention. To ensure appropriate protection mainstreaming, Concern will apply international and internal program quality monitoring standard procedures to target most vulnerable persons of concern with a focus on women, children, elderly, and persons living with disabilities. Protection principles such as CRM tools which will be complemented by international guidelines, Sphere standards and Camp Management Tool kit to ensure accountability to, and participation of, affected population. Principles of protection mainstreaming will be exercised, including: meaningful access through consultation and involvement of communities about the best way to deliver services and consideration for vulnerable groups, and accountability mechanisms. Protection / (S)GBV risks faced by women and girls will be core focus of capacity building for staff and volunteers to ensure they are able to detect cases of abuse, recognize and refer to protection-mandated entities. NFI kits will contain dignity items for women and girls; shelters will be segregated and well lit for protection, with women-lead households receiving assistance for construction. Post-Distribution Monitoring will be conducted, and timely reports submitted to UNHCR in order to reconcile distributed kits and evaluate the effectiveness/efficiency of the intervention, with specific regard to community feedback through complaint and response mechanisms (CRM).

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Concern’s protection strategy is guided by two central documents, notably the organisation’s Programme Participant Protection Policy and Code of Conduct; and the2010 Humanitarian Accountability Partnership (HAP) Standard in Accountability and Quality Management. Concern’s Programme Participant Protection Policy sets out all steps that are reasonably possible to protect programme beneficiaries – children, women and men. Concern’s approach to protection is based on the key concept of analysis and management of risks: i) the risks faced by the communities we assist; ii) the specific risks of the most vulnerable groups within these communities, iii) the risks our programme may create; and reducing vulnerabilities as a consequence. As certified member of HAP, Concern is committed to implementing the HAP Standard related to different aspects of beneficiaries’ accountability. Three of these aspects are particularly relevant to conflict sensitivity and protection: beneficiaries’ access to information; beneficiaries’ participation in all the stages of the programme; and establishing a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM). As of August 2022, Concern Sudan’s Complaint and Response Mechanism (CRM) was updated and aligned with overall staff and communities’ expectations as well as ground realities through a consultative exercise undertaken with the aim to adjust Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). Concern will incorporate beneficiaries’ feedback through two ways. Every 6 months Accountability team will conduct FGDs with programme participants, as well as interviews with other stakeholders related to effectiveness of CRM system. At the time of conclusion of a complaint, each complainant will be asked for their feedback using a Satisfaction and Consultation form. Based on data received from these two sources, Concern will regularly evaluate and re-design the CRM system to improve its accessibility, responsiveness, efficiency and anonymity. Accountability towards affected populations as well as awareness raising is ensured through information materials as brochures, posters, business cards in Arabic, as well as through an informative video developed for the process. All posters, brochures, and business cards will be distributed to programme participants and will be posted/available wherever Concern Sudan work to ensure transparent information sharing.
