Project Summary

Since October 2018, LEARN Consortium has supported equitable and safe access to essential basic services, including WASH, Shelter and Health, MPCA and Protection, for the most vulnerable populations affected by conflict and displacement in Northeast Syria (NES). LEARN is comprised of Solidarités International (SI), Un Ponte Per (UPP), Relief International (RI) and People in Need (PIN).
The Program Vision is to increase the resilience of vulnerable, conflict-affected populations, through the provision of equitable and safe, integrated, multi-sectoral services and agile emergency response. While the Program Goal is to reduce morbidity and mortality and improve living conditions, resilience and dignity of affected populations in North East Syria, through coordinated, timely emergency Health, Protection, WASH, Shelter, MPCA, HCIMA, and ERMS services, which meet the most acute humanitarian needs.
In Phase IV of the project, LEARN will continue to build on and prioritize integration between partners where it is feasible and desirable. LEARN will continue to respond to the emergency needs of the most vulnerable populations in Northeast Syria (NES) through a needs-based, people centered, and integrated, multi-sectoral approach. These approaches will simultaneously build resilience, reduce dependence on aid and restore livelihoods and markets.
Objective Health: Reduce morbidity and mortality among vulnerable, conflict-affected populations through improved access to quality health services.
LEARN will continue to provide life-saving health services to the affected population in order to reduce morbidity, mortality and injury through sustained support to health facilities and community outreach. UPP continues to provide both full and partial support to facilities with an extensive exit strategy for each.
UPP aims to improve clinical quality of health and strengthen the local health system in all its’ components in accordance UPP’s 2021 Health Strategy towards the development of a comprehensive model of primary health care. This will be achieved by applying a participatory approach, to engage and empower individuals and communities in achieving equal access to health, and through an Area-Based Approach (ABA) which entails a geographic focus to strengthen collaboration among partners and ensure a collaborative multi-sectoral response. UPP will implement a person-centered approach combined with an integrated approach based on medical quality, preventative measures, and community engagement.
Objective Early Recovery and Market Assessment (ERMS): Support vulnerable households to restore, protect and develop livelihoods.
In Phase IV, LEARN will address the growing and disparate needs in the Livelihoods Restoration and New Livelihoods Development sectors by combining economic empowerment of the most vulnerable with employment opportunities and investment in existing value chains, focused on the thematic areas of women empowerment. This will be achieved through support to women cooperatives to restore their livelihoods affected by the crisis, and through support to vocational training graduates for them to start their own businesses and create new livelihoods. The proposed activities will be designed with a view to maximum integration within the livelihoods sector and complementary to other sectoral areas of programming under the LEARN where possible. A “circular” logic to activities is seen as the most effective way to ensure the largest impact possible.
Protection will be mainstreamed through all activities, and complemented by a robust referral mechanism.
Through a flexible and timely LEARN RRM, which incorporates learning from previous Phases, LEARN will continue to respond to acute shocks across the whole of NES.

3 - Needs

Health: (HNO 2021)In Aleppo Gov., 403,000 people are in need of health assistance. According to the 2020 HeRAMS, this area is least served by health facilities in the whole of Syria, and Menbij has the lowest availability of hospitals and functional PHCCs. In Al-Hasakeh Gov., 670,000 people are in need of health assistance. According to the 2020 HeRAMS report, there are no fully functional health services. Only 54% of services are even partially functioning, and many of these lack access to safe water, medical equipment, generators and skilled staff.In Ar-Raqqa Gov., 432,000 people are in need of health assistance; up to 75% of the population in rural areas. As of November 2020, Ar-Raqqa subdistrict hosted the majority of IDPs living in informal settlements and collective centers in NES (>80,000 individuals).ERMS:Throughout Syria, the main barriers to employment are the lack of employment opportunities, the lack of financing and the lack of skills (HNAP Comparative Overview: Socio-Economic and Poverty Comparison January 2020 and 2021).Women’s employment or integration in the market chain is often overcome through women cooperatives, notably in Al-Hasakeh and Ar-Raqqa. Cooperatives surveyed by UPP are showing a potential for diversification of activities and recruitment of new members, but they lack structure, business planning and capacity to invest.

4 - Implementation of activities

Building on Phase I, II and III implementation, UPP will provide comprehensive support to 4 CPHCC, 1 HP, 2 ambulance centres, and a network of 8 ambulances to address emergency health needs and improve the quality healthcare for vulnerable communities. UPP will cover the running costs for the HFs and salaries for medical and non-medical staff. UPP will also provide training and capacity building to HF staff.UPP will deliver a comprehensive package of healthcare including primary health care of communicable and non-communicable diseases, reproductive health (including antenatal care (ANC) and postnatal care (PNC)), community health, child care and nutrition.Besides, UPP will continue provision of pharmaceuticals, medical supplies, medical and non-medical equipment. ERMS: In Phase IV LEARN project will combine activities to restore and improve livelihood opportunities with others aiming at the creation of new livelihood opportunities. UPP will support 7 women cooperatives in restoring and improving their businesses, offering small grants as well as business training and coaching. On the other side, UPP will offer start-up grants to TVET graduates and business coaching in order to establish new businesses. The TVET graduates will be previously trained by another consortium partner. The livelihood restoration activities will be implemented in Al-Hasake and Ar-Raqqa Governorates, while the new livelihood development activities will be implemented in Ar-Raqqa.

8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

UPP PSEA efforts continue as part of its core capacity building pillar, as well as coordination and best practice and advocacy through the Co-Chairship of the PSEA Network coordinated by IOM. According to a WoS PSEA Network/ HNAP report (Oct '21), there is a low level of SEA awareness amongst beneficiaries in NES. UPP and IPs work to raise awareness on protection topics incl. SEA awareness. From a localization approach UPP PSEA and Protection technical team at HQ and field work with local IPs to support in development/enhancement of local IPs integrity package policies and practices (recruitment, CoCs, assessments, etc). Further, as a mitigation measure, the PSEA policy is shared with the local partners and all the staff members abide by it. Moreover, regular trainings on PSEA are foreseen as part of the capacity building plan for health staff in the supported Health Facility as well. Finally, a complaint box for FCRM including SEA allegations is available for the population to be able to report if needed. UPP will further promote the use of common tools developed through PSEA Network and GBV/CP sWGs to prevent/mitigate SEA risks.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

A context of stable security is key to allow the supported health facilities to remain operational 24/7 for the population to access to life-saving and basic primary health care services. It's essential that staff, especially implementing partner staff, maintain access to HFs to ensure constant presence and monitoring of the activities. In the current scenario, where a global pandemic intersects with a fragile geopolitical context, the main risks are linked to uncontrolled pandemic spread and suspension of activities in case of contraction of COVID-19 by health staff, as well as a shift of frontlines and reactivation of military operations between SDF, GoT and GoS taking back control over areas of intervention, which may require a shift of assistance to areas not affected by the shift in control. A further risk entails targeted attacks to humanitarian actors, such as staff being targeted by terrorist groups. Partners follow limited and low profile interactions with local/political authorities, with no direct interactions with SDF, always affirming neutrality and humanitarian principles, thereby avoiding interactions/relationships that may lead to perceived affiliation with military/political entities.Risk or fraud related to payments or commodities being diverted/stolen by staff, local authorities, criminals or armed groups depicts another important risk. Rigorous application of security and finance SOPs, including acceptance and anti-fraud measures are being followed.
