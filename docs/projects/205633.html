Project Summary

The project aims to improve the overall health and nutrition of children and Pregnant and lactating mother through the direct provision of health and nutrition services. The Goals of the project is to improve health and nutrition for vulnerable children and mothers, including children with disabilities and increase the number of children who are well-nourished and protected from infection and disease. The project will support Usratuna PHCC and Usratuna Rehabilitation Center, Way Station and Mangateen PHCU providing drugs, prevention and treatment of moderate malnutrition (MAM) for children 0-59 months, measles and pentavalent vaccinations, capacity building for Health and Community Based works and a good referral pathway. The project will provide rehabilitation services (including physiotherapist, speech therapy and occupational therapy). The beneficiaries of the services will be children, children with Disabilities, mothers, including returnees and refugees in IDPs Camps, Host communities in Juba County

1. Needs

The 2023 South Sudan Humanitarian Needs Overview estimates that 8.9 million people will be in need of humanitarian assistance inThe inter-sectoral analysis conducted using cluster people in need revealed that there are needs in every county of the country. Among them, there are 2.2 million women and 4.9 million children, including 2.4 million girls and 2.5 million boys. More people are projected to experience severe food insecurity inSouth Sudan`s health system is unable to cope with, respond to and provide essential life-saving health services in humanitarian emergencies, as it relies heavily on international humanitarian assistance and face many challenges as lick of funding, staff, medicines and equipment. Women, children, the elderly and PLW are particularly vulnerable to the limited access to health care. As for lack of health services, the people in South Sudan suffer the lack of nutrition services. An estimated 2.2 million people, including 1.5 million host community, 315500 returnees and 390000 IDPs, will suffer from acute malnutrition.In Central Equatorial there is a severe deficiency with regards to basic health services the HNO2023 identified the needs to cover the health services for 717.9K in the state. Among the IDP camps, the settlements not recognized as IDP camps are the most severely affected by the lack of humanitarian assistance related to health and nutrition services. Within Juba, Mangateen PHCU and Way Station are positioned close to IDP camp not recognised. In 2021 conducted 17213 and 12778 visits to persons in need. The two facilities are under the CES/MoH and provide free health services to IDPs. The facilities are not actually supported by the MoH, indeed are supported through humanitarian assistance. It is crucial to keep supporting the facilities, which are currently the only health services accessible for the population of Mangateen and Way Station IDP camps. . For people with disabilities, stigma can be a major barrier to participation, they are the most vulnerable people, excluded from key opportunities and services. For this reason capacity building about GBV and inclusion is one of the priorities for the health workers and community workers.Usratuna PHCC and rehabilitation centres run by OVCI carry out unique services in South Sudan related to prevention, rehabilitation and disability care. With approximately 12,000 visits per year and 24,000 laboratory tests at the PHCC Ovci cares for over 4000 women per year (4 visits during the pregnancy) in its ANC service. Each woman is followed during and after pregnancy with check-ups, ultrasounds and support with micronutrients (folic acid, iron and milk). The services are tailor-made and the women are followed individually, both within Usratuna PHCC and the two supported dispensaries: Mangateen and Way Station. Support for the population (especially women and children) comes through the distribution of milk in Ovci-supported dispensaries, which is an incentive for PLWs to go to health centres and thus make the necessary visits for themselves and their children. Through our nutrition services we are able to support around 5000 people (children under 5 and PLWs) each year suffering from MAM and refer people in SAM to specialised centres in Juba. Our referral system has been strengthened over the past year for increased support of the population in Juba. This allows us to take care of about 1,000 disabled children each year in our rehabilitation centre and to train staff from different realities (NGOs, PHCC, etc.) on disability prevention systems through nutrition or early diagnosis.
