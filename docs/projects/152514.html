Project Summary

LWFs Food Security and Livelihoods (FSL) project is targeting protracted conflict and displacement affected most vulnerable IDPs, and Returnees who have been displaced due to a protracted crisis over the past one year or more years , compounded by inter-clan, inter-tribal violence and cattle raiding. LWF planned to reach 8000 HHs(of which 79% is female) from IDPs, and Returnees communities in Duk, Uror and Twic East counties through increasing food consumption (using cash modalities) and enhancing agricultural production capacities. The agricultural capacities of targets beneficiaries will be enhanced through building skill on farm practices (on farm and off farm management) and Supporting by agricultural inputs.
The beneficiaries’ selection can be done through community participation and lead of community based Cash Committees (CDCS). The process involves identification, registration and verification of beneficiaries. To ensure complains are responded timely and properly there will be complain response committees at each payam and county level.
The project will address the lifesaving food consumption through unconditional cash modality. In addition the farmers supporting through farm field schools, seeds fairs and direct seeds and tools delivery. The farmers will get intensive season long training on agricultural practices and skills to ensure that the targets will be resilient and bounce back future shocks (absorb the shocks).
LWF will integrate all its projects (WASH, Food security, alternatives livelihoods, shelter/NFI, Education) in the operation to maximize the lifesaving, to minimize further deterioration of livelihoods and building resilience.
The project will be implemented in coordination with stakeholders and partners working in the operation areas in food security and livelihoods, Education, WASH, Shelter, NFI and health). The coordination can help to find solution for common challenges, learn from each other, and avoid duplication efforts and maximise outcomes and impacts.

Needs

With protracted instability and ever deteriorating political, economic and security situations in South Sudan, the situation is not good, more than 2.4 Million South Sudanese have sought refuge elsewhere in the neighbouring countries – Uganda, Ethiopia, Kenya and Sudan. In general, the country is characterized by ethnic violence, high levels of poverty and exclusion, low literacy levels, gender inequality, limited access to basic services, poor infrastructure, and weak institutions. According IPC September 2018 Alert, in Jonglei State 275,000 people will be under IPC 4 (Emergency), 810,000 People under IPC 3(Crisis) and 535,000 IPC 2(stressed). Among eleven counties of Jonglei state Uror, Twic East and Duk are fall under Crisis (IPC 3). In this state worsening food insecurity is primarily driven by continued conflict, and protracted displacement, tribal attacks which has contributed to reduced crop production disrupted livelihoods and high inflation of commodities in the market. As a result there is high food consumption gap (4-5 months food gap in a year) in the Uror, Duk and Twic East counties. According to the local Relief and Rehabilitation Commission (RRC) offices, there are an estimated 54,833 IDPs in Uror and Duk alone. Duk hosts 26,033 IDPs (some 4,339 households) and Uror hosts the, 28,800 IDPs (about 4,800 households) -mainly women and children (boys and girls), and the elderly. The effect of the conflict, tribal fighting and the deepening economic crisis compounded by devaluation is also hard hitting market dependent poor. LWF has been implementing project on agriculture and livelihoods from 2018-2018 , the project focuses of recovery of agriculture and livelihoods in Twic East and Duk counties , the midterm evaluation of the project conducted in February 2018 indicated that efforts have been made by focusing on supply of agricultural inputs and providing basic practical training to farmers. This has led to some improvement by targeted beneficiaries in terms of increase in size of farm land, increasing production from agriculture, fish production and market and increasing of income from income generation activities. However the change is concentrated on targeted beneficiaries and there is need to address additional vulnerable beneficiaries to support build their resilience while saving lives. LWF has learnt that to transform the very little subsistence food production in the areas to better self-feeding by households, it is highly important to intervene in proper agricultural extension, mainly to develop farming skills and practices. As the community is largely agro pastoral and their inclination to farming is low, the past effort showed the potential with the groups and need of scaling up the agricultural knowledge and skill development in a way than sustainably solve and transform the communities’ production capacities to build resilience.Women tend to concentrate their labour on crop production on small hand-cultivated plots. In crop farming, men are involved in preparing the ground for sowing. Due to insecurity, farmers tend to stick to tending plots close to their homes. Decision-making on how to dispose of assets is mainly the responsibility of men although in some families women are consulted. This indicates that there are few alternative livelihoods readily available for IDPs and that restarting cultivation is the only way to stop reliance on humanitarian assistance.The IDPS and Returnees has been in dare condition, there is high need of life saving action for the returnees , who has come back to place of origin without any resource at and for the IDPS who has moved out of their villages and settled in communities , who has been also in crises situation. In addition there is need to have strong link between lifesaving protecting livelihoods from deterioration, through building production capacities to build resilience among vulnerable.
