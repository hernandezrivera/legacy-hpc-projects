Project Summary

o CRS plans to implement Nutrition interventions in Jonglei State under the Resilience and Food Security Program and in Awerial through WFP partnership. In Jonglei, the project promotes nutrition awareness and behavior change in MIYCN and provides conflict-affected HHs with either unconditional or conditional food support as determined by need assessments and the situation on the ground. It also to uses evidence-based social behavior change communication to promote socially appropriate maternal and child feeding practices and assist in the development of target community self-efficacy. In Awerial, the project will implement blanket supplementary feeding program (BSFP) and a targeted supplementary feeding program (TSFP) for pregnant and lactating mothers and children under 5. In Awerial and Yirol, the project will implement conditional food distribution to save lives, reduce severe food insecurity and stabilize the nutritional status of high-risk groups including IDPs, refugees, returnees, women and children. The CGFD will ensure adequate household food consumption from April to August 2019. The project will also provide school meals to alleviate hunger in malnourished schoolchildren and improve cognition; the school meals will also help address micronutrient deficiencies and engage parents.

Needs

Acute malnutrition remains a major public health emergency in several parts of South Sudan, and is of particular concern for pregnant and lactating women and children under five because of their increased nutritional needs. In Yirol West and Yirol East in Lakes State, 2018 surveys have revealed a prevalence of 13% Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) and 22% Global Acute Malnutrition rates, respectively. The risk of acute malnutrition continues to be high among the IDPs, especially among children and pregnant and lactating women. Most of the IDPs do not have land or inputs to prepare household gardens or other food crops, and thus depend entirely on humanitarian assistance. In Jonglei State, the 2017 SMART survey showed global acute malnutrition weight-for-height (GAM/WHZ) prevalence above the WHO emergency threshold of 15%, with a peak of 26.1% in parts of Jonglei, close to the Extremely Critical classification. Alarming proxy GAM (mid-upper arm circumference) prevalence above 20% was observed in other parts of Jonglei in the first quarter of 2017. Widespread fighting, displacement and poor access to services, disease outbreaks, extremely poor diet (in terms of both quality and quantity), low coverage of sanitation facilities and poor hygiene practices are the key drivers of the high levels of acute malnutrition across South Sudan. Levels of acute malnutrition deteriorated even further in the peak lean season of July, with some parts of Jonglei declared to be at extreme critical nutrition level.

Activities

In Jonglei, the project's principle aim is to support community based social and behavior change communication (SBCC) activities on nutrition. Activities include:• Disseminate information, education and communication (IEC) material and tools for nutrition promotion • Conduct SBCC (breastfeeding campaign, social mobilization and advocacy) events• Conduct community meetings on improved nutrition practice• Support improved household maternal, infant and young children nutrition (MIYCN) practices. • Conduct training of trainers (ToT) on MIYCN for RFSP staff and partners• Conduct MIYCN and SBCC training for community volunteers, lead mothers and male community leaders• Promote good nutrition practices at community level through counseling and messaging • Provide facilitation support to mother-to-mother support groups (MTMSG)• Establish new and strengthen existing peer support and action oriented groups• Conduct social marketing research on barriers to adoption of optimal ANC practices • Print and distribute maternal health and ANC IEC materials In Lakes, the project provides conditional food distribution in 3 counties (Yirol East, Yirol West and Awerial) and provides blanket and targeted supplementary feeding services in Lakes State seeks to save lives, reduce severe food insecurity and stabilize the nutritional status of high-risk groups including IDPs, refugees, returnees, women and children. After discussions with UNICEF and coordination with the Nutrition Cluster, the project does not have an OTP component as this is handled by another agency. Rather, referrals are made as needed and close coordination is done with the OTP service providers in the area.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

CRS is committed to mainstreaming protection in all its programming, to ensure the safety, the meaningful access and the dignity of all beneficiaries, in line with the Global Protection Cluster guidance. For this project, CRS will implement a gender equality plan to increase engagement of, and participation by, women and youth, guided by the CRS Integral Human Development approach. The plan will strengthen the capacity of communities to use the community dialogue that engages local government, traditional leadership, men, women and youth in gender transformation to collectively define how gender inequality negatively affects their communities and come up with solutions to increase protection, social cohesion and food security. The project directly targets women’s participation, as they are typically the primary caregivers for the children, but men and boys will also be involved and sensitized on various ways in which they can create a positive environment at the household and community level and minimize household (and particularly women and children’s) vulnerabilities. There is active and inclusive community engagement in all stages of the program cycle that builds on and strengthens existing community and state structures, resources and capacities. Staff also coordinate closely and advocate with relevant actors to enhance the protective environment, avoid duplication and prevent, mitigate and respond to protection risks.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

CRS works with County level government, such as the RRA, local authority, County Commissioner, and Payam Administrator; members of the Project Management Committees who are selected for their diverse representation across community groups; parents; and CNV committee members. We consult with them on the best ways and times for community mobilization, and community members’ specific needs and concerns about the project. CNV community members specifically are consulted to assist with identifying beneficiaries, support community development plans, support implementation of agreed interventions and projects, support food distribution and monitoring, and provide feedback.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

The project conducts post-distribution and end-use monitoring interviews and implements accountability and feedback mechanisms in food distributions and nutrition activities to assess beneficiary satisfaction. These activities help the program assess its efficiency and improve programming. The project uses pre-distribution addresses and project implementation teams to provide feedback on issues raised by the communities. The program uses a complaints log sheet to track those issues. The project conducts beneficiary-based biannual surveys with program beneficiaries across different interventions in the implementation area. These surveys assess beneficiary access to program services, track progress towards achieving outcome and impact targets, highlight areas for improvement, and track progress towards achieving resilience or recovery by program participants.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project integrates gender equity from the beginning, starting with an analysis-based design to help implement activities to enable men and women and boys and girls to have equal access to participation and priority setting in the program. In December 2017, the Jonglei project conducted a thorough review of its program strategies and DIP with stakeholders, staff and participants to identify gaps and barriers to women’s participation in the program. The review identified social and cultural barriers, including traditional roles and the non-participation of girls in schooling. That, in turn, excludes women from decision-making at the household and community levels. The review made several recommendations for action which are being carried out this year, including increasing women’s representation and creating women-only meetings at community level to ensure that women can participate in decisions about program priorities.
