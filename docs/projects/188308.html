Project Summary

According to OCHA's HNO 2022 draft, 13.4 million people in Syria require humanitarian assistance - a 21% increase compared to 2021 - as needs are increasingly exacerbated by the deteriorating economy.
Statistics indicate increased exposure to ongoing hostilities, particularly in the Northwest. limited access to the Services; Displacement, which often separates children from family members, including primary caregivers, is disrupting the education of 2.45 million children aged 5-17 years out of school, increasing protection risks reported by parties to the conflict. Children and primary caregivers also suffer Vulnerabilities associated with direct risks of the worst forms of child labour, disability, physical/psychological injury, severe neglect, family separation (including anticipated separation due to COVID19), child recruitment, and child marriage. The project aims to reduce the above-mentioned protection risks for displaced children and the host community in northwestern Syria in Jisr al-Shughur in the al-Janudiyah area within the Child Protection Center of the Al-Haramain Foundation, in line with the minimum standards of child protection, as the center will provide multiple services in the field of child protection from through it:
1. Awareness campaigns through awareness teams targeting women, men and children for child protection issues
2- Implement case management services that address the vulnerabilities of children and primary caregivers associated with direct risks of worst forms of child labour, children with disabilities, physical and psychological injury, severe neglect, family separation (including expected separation due to COVID19), child recruitment and child marriage. These services will be provided through referrals from the community protection committees and through the monitoring team, according to joint procedures between the authorities.
3. Provide Individual Protection Assistance (ECF) to cover protection gaps for children if the service is not available within the communities
4. Capacity building of child protection workers.
5. Implementation of mental health and psychosocial support activities within the Al-Harameen Center for war-traumatized children.
6. Provide parenting sessions for primary care providers
The project will focus on mitigating protection risks for children with disabilities
The center will serve the most vulnerable children. All activities are aligned with the 2022 Humanitarian Response Plan as well as the Strategic Objectives of the Protection Cluster. Services will be provided for one year.
. The project aims to accommodate children with disabilities, and all activities will be implemented according to
Recommendations of the Protection Cluster and the World Health Organization to deal with Covid-19 and the necessary preventive measures to protect beneficiaries and humanitarian workers.
The project will ensure the accountability of the affected population and stakeholders through coordination, participation, transparent accountability, complaints mechanism, feedback mechanism and worker competency monitoring.
Reports will be prepared and coordinated with the Child Protection Sub-Cluster (4Ws, Case Management Reporting Tool) to report on project activities
The monitoring plan will ensure that:
1. Monitor the quality of the implemented activities and compare the action plan using case management monitoring and evaluation tools and report any issue Case management monitoring (recording - evaluation - intervention plan - closing the case and ensuring access to the service) while maintaining confidentiality and privacy of the case and that the names are encrypted
2. Track and collect project indicators to measure project progress at the output level
The project is designed to reduce protection from sexual exploitation and abuse,
The project is designed to strengthen GQ to include women and girls as an integral part of the project's activities at all stages during its tenure.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

The risk of tension within or within the community due to service delivery sites or the types of services provided if one community is provided and no service is provided to a neighboring community.1- Ensure comprehensive planning of services to mitigate risks and communicate with communities in a proactive and informed manner.2. Ensure that neighboring communities are consulted regarding site services and remediation. Including access barriers for those with injuries/disabilities and communication mechanisms for people with visual/auditory impairments.3. Linking the protection intervention to existing services within the target communities to reinforce the system's approach.4. Forming community committees to enhance community protection mechanisms.Girls, boys, women, and men are at risk as a result of violence related to the civilian population/places targeted by armed groups; Failuretoconsider the safety and security of accessing/receiving services; Failure to take into account contextual information (such as aerial bombardment) and to target civilian infrastructure en route to, during, and directly after service provision.1. Consider hostilities patterns and contextual analysis as part of decisions to establish a fixed site or provide a mobile service.2. Ensure that a safety audit is conducted to ensure safe access (road and location) for people with mobility impairment (due to physical, visual/auditory defects, and psychological issues).


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Low level of obligations related to sexual exploitation and abuse, women disproportionately exposure to misconduct by humanitarian workers at risk of sexual exploitation and abuse, and low level of safe reporting of complaints of SEA.Mitigation:1 - Impose mandatory training on protection from PSEAon all employees, including management, field staff, day laborers, volunteers, contractors, and vendors, in each area where there are active processes to ensure that employees are aware of their obligations related to protection from sexual exploitation and abuse and to equip employees with the knowledge and skills to prevent Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Response to both. 2 - Create mechanisms for reporting protection from SEA at the community level, including establishing contact points, according to the location and settings for reporting and posting.3 - Engage with communities and key stakeholders in strengthening community complaints mechanisms, including determining the provision of victim assistance services in all targeted locations.Conduct regular awareness-raising activities on protection from sexual exploitation and abuse, policies, standards, and codes of conduct related to protection from sexual exploitation.4 - Provide specific awareness sessions to target groups such as women, girls, boys, and men in reception centers, camps, sites for displaced people, host communities, and societyAll project personnel sign the Code of Conduct for PSEA


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: COVID-19 pandemic and its impact on awareness activities, case management, and training probability: high Mitigation: - Maintaining social distance between beneficiaries and the project staff - Use methods of protection, including masks, gloves, and sterilizers -Avoiding meetings that contain many beneficiaries -Transfer project personnel in batches within transportation means to reduce congestion.Risk 2: CPactivities are suspended due to the conflict. probability: medium. Mitigation: in the event of attacks or invasion of the region, the service center will be moved to the JisrAl-Shughour area in coordination with the local authorities to ensure the safety ofproject personnel and beneficiaries and after acquiring the required approval Risk 3:Council local intervention and impeding the implementation of the project interference by armed groups to prevent the implementation of child protection activities that are related to early marriage and Child protection issues situation probability: Medium Mitigation: signing a memorandum of understanding with the local council to avoid obstacles, interference or external dictated by armed groups -Raising awareness of the community and primary caregivers to cooperate mitigating the risks of protection. - Establishing community protection networks that are made up of community leaders and influencers to support unhindered child protection activities.
