Project Summary

This integrated intervention is to respond to the needs of the most vulnerable among conflict- affected IDP and host community girls, boys, women and men in NWS by providing a comprehensive package of water and sanitation services that include the provision of drinking water, the implementation of garbage collection activities, provision of sanitation services and the distribution of hygiene items. The project aims to improve health through access to sanitation and a healthy and dignified environment and increase access to sustainable water, sanitation, environmental hygiene solutions and provision of appropriate, core hygiene items, and good personal hygiene practice for IDPs, also, host community populations in the targeted locations. Moreover, Silk Road aims to improve the overall condition of most affected people with a special focus on the newly IDPs and the already existing IDPs in the targeted communities through implementing planned activities aimed at filling the gaps and reducing the needs between the displaced and the new to reducing the risk of disease through access to essential hygiene items, improved awareness and knowledge of health risks. Sustainability will be aimed through the re- establishment of a cost recovery system. The quantity of water that will be provided to each person in the targeted communities is 35 L per person a day. The proposed activities include; Providing pumps, generators, pipes and electrical panelboards, rehabilitation of water supply system, equipping wells with galvanized pipes, submersible pumps, rehabilitation of concrete bases and housing for the pumps and generators and providing solar energy systems for some water stations. After rehabilitation wors are done, the systems will be provided with operations and maintenance support to ensure the sustainability of the provided assistance. The assistance will cover the costs of fuel to operate the standby generators, disinfection chemicals (Chlorine), stipends for operators/technicians and tools/spare parts for 12 months. During this period of support, a cost recovery (Tariff) system will be established to ensure continuous cash flow required for running the systems, this will involve a study on a suitable tariff rate that takes into account the dwindling financial capacity of the benefiting population and will be based on focus group discussions with a representative group of the community including members of local councils of targeted communities. In addition to the development of the water unit in the civil councils to ensure that they have adequate operations and maintenance teams. These teams will be responsible for ensuring sustainable water services by activating a tariff system. In addition to hygiene promotion activities and hygiene kits distribution to improve hygiene knowledge and access to hygiene materials,

which coincide with the water activities. Proposed activities will be the best solution for the current situation and in this context that can allow newly IDPs and the already existing IDPs to have access to safe water through restored water systems to reduce vulnerability to preventable illnesses and improve resilience and IDPs and other vulnerable groups (disabled, elderly) have access to life-saving assistance to reduce the risk of WASHrelated morbidity and mortality. Furthermore, the proposed WASH interventions in this proposal will ensure that they do not create additional risks for the affected population (especially IDPs women and children and the newly IDPs) but will provide the best solutions to the suffering population in the targeted areas, also Silk Road will ensure that the health impact in this emergency WASH intervention will reduced disease risk and reduced transmission risk and it adhering to internationally recognized standards and best practices, ensuring that services are provided in line with the Do No Harm, WASH cluster standards, and humanitarian principles.

3 - Needs

This needs assessment was conducted in 12 communities in October 2022 using several questionnaires (Household level interviews & Key informant interviews) to address the objectives of this assessment. The data collection strategy included Key informant interviews with local actors, members of the local council and engineers and technicians, as well as 130 Household-level interviews and our team Observation as well. Key findings: In most villages, people rely on water Trucking or other ways to secure their water needs. Also, not all people can afford the costs of purchasing water by trucks (Most of those interviewed confirmed this fact (Above 90 %), whether residents or other groups interviewed), which reflects the economic challenges facing the population to secure safe water in sufficient quantities. The lack of water besides limited access to essential hygiene products for any reason especially the limited economic capacity contributes to limited hygiene practices (More than 50 % of the people).Regarding the negative coping strategies when water is not available, the highest percentage of people reduce water consumption in addition to modifying hygiene practices such as bathing less. Most of the population (More than 66 % of the people) change personal hygiene practices due to the lack of water where most of the people use bath less than once or twice a week, lower percentage less than once every two weeks.

4 - Implementation of activities

Silk Road will Provide the needed rehabilitation of the water systems and water stations in the targeted communities (11 water systems). Provision of the needed materials and works to establish 4 solar-powered operating systems. Supply needed diesel fuel to operate7 water stations and systems in the targeted communities for 8 months. Supply engine oil and spare parts for7 water stations for8 months (filters, oil, and air filters) for periodic maintenance of the generators in the targeted stations. Support 7 water stations and water systems in the targeted communities with needed Sodium Hypochlorite for disinfecting water for 8 months.Conducting regular water quality testing at the household level in all targeted locations (11 water systems). Regular maintenance for the whole water system for 11 water systems. Establishment of the cost-recovery mechanisms and water safety plans. The first three months is full support, and the rest of the months will be in a way of partial support. Distribution of Hygiene items one-off during the project. Delivering hygiene promotion activities through a door to door visits. Support the municipality and waste management system in the targeted locations for 8 months. Provide the needed rehabilitation of the sewage systems in 5 communities. Engagement with PWDs in project activities Continuous monitoring / MEAL activities. Post-service monitoring
