Project Summary

The recent escalation resulted in additional 520,000 people forced to leave their homes, since 1st December 2019. (WHO, 2020) reported that as of 31 January, at least 53 health facilities had suspended services due to insecurity, threats of attacks or the simple fact that entire areas have been deserted by civilians seeking refuge from violence and daily bombardments. An estimated 2.9 million people in Syria’s northwest are in need of health care (WHO, 2020), with women and children estimated to comprise 76% of this population (OCHA, 2020).
Gender-based violence (GBV) continues to affect the lives of women and girls, with adolescent girls, women-headed households, especially those divorced and widowed (HNO Syria, 2019). Children, including those unaccompanied, separated or living with older/disabled caregivers, remain particularly vulnerable to exploitation and GBV.
Human Appeal (HA) is an active partner with the protection cluster and was running a specialized healthcare facility for women and children, Al-Iman Hospital, in the rural Aleppo since 2015. An airstrike on Al-Iman Hospital in August 2019 damaged the building severely and considering the fragile security situation, HA shifted its operations to Sarmada town in the Idleb governorate and took charge of former Sarmada Hospital. HA has renamed this hospital to “Al-Iman Hospital” and continuously providing specialized healthcare and nutrition services for IDPs, with a focus on women and children. Since the start date November 2019, HA has integrated protection services in the new location and started reporting to the protection cluster in December. The protection team got training in January 2020 to extend their skills and learn more about case management, GBV, CP and referral pathways.
The project aims to support Al-Iman Hospital to ensure continuity of its protection services with 5.20 % integrated with nutrition with 6.50 % and health care services with 88.30% to the conflict-affected communities. This support will ensure the provision of protection services for a total of 2,000 individuals during the course of 7 months. Human Appeal has provided 27 individuals with protection services over 1.5 months and we are going to increase the target beneficiaries in the coming days after the training our team got in January 2020.
Aligned with HRP objectives the main protection sector project interventions will include:
1. Promotion of community-based child protection for girls and boys in the health facility
2. Delivery of specialized child protection services (case management) for girls and boys in the health facility
3. Advocacy with concerned people and key stakeholders to promote safe environments for children.
4. Provide activities empowering women and girls and GBV prevention activities
5. Having safe spaces and for women and children in the health facility
6. Provide livelihood opportunities to empower women and girls and strengthen their resilience through HA FSL interventions.
7. Increase the capacity of GBV actors to respond and prevent GBV through strategic and structured approaches
8. Increase the capacity of CP actors to respond and prevent protection concerns through strategic and structured approaches
9. Awareness-raising sessions on early marriages and its effects on children and prevention of anaemia through RH services in the health facility.
The project will ensure outreach and inclusion of vulnerable communities from IDP camps and remote locations with the provision of a Mobile Health Clinic and offering protection and GBV services and disseminating of relevant IEC/BCC messages.

4 - Needs

Civilians in northwest Syria have endured over eight years of war and hardship, with many civilians displaced multiple times during this period. Of the estimated 4 million people living in northwest Syria, at least 2.8 million people are estimated to require humanitarian assistance. The current escalation displaced around 520,000 people; around 76% of them are women and children (OCHA, 2019). Most of the refugees are moving towards areas close to the Turkish borders. Sarmada is a town in Idleb’s most populated Harim district housing about 35% of the total governorate’s population. Diverse and interlinked protection needs continue to be widespread throughout Syria, and severe in some areas. They are driven by a range of situations including the impacts of active hostilities, new displacements, cumulative long- term consequences of the crisis and dynamics linked to return increasing the stress on already overburdened communities. According to UN-led needs assessment initiatives, the occurrence of multiple protection issues in the same community is not uncommon. This environment continues to require a sustained response by humanitarian actors, capable of working with communities, to provide specialized services to respond to the most urgent needs, to mitigate risks and to contribute to building a favourable protection environment, with particular attention to groups with specific needs and/or who are at risk*(*2020 Syria HRP - Annex F - Sector Template_Protection DRAFT-V1_15122019()

5 - Implementation of activities

HA has been running “Al-Iman Specialized Hospital for Women and Children” since 2015 in the rural Aleppo as the only specialized HF for women and children. However, in August 2019, an airstrike on the HF severely damaged the infrastructure resulting in serious risks to staff and beneficiaries. Consequently, HA shifted its operations to Sarmada in the Idleb governorate. The shifting was made after consultation with the Idleb Health Directorate and an MOU exists between both parties.Sarmada is part of sub-district Dana and is on the route of existing movement of displaced population from Idleb. Total population of Dana sub-district is over 560,000 whereby 50% are children between 0-17. The Al-Iman Hospital is located at the entrance of Sarmada and serves as the first major healthcare facility for the nearby rural populations, as well as a host of IDP camps. Its catchment area has a population of over 50,000. It is the only HF with 24/7, free-of-cost CEmOC and pediatric services along with nutrition and protection services. Despite challenges in maintaining a free-of-cost and 24/7 service in the conflict-affected area, HA is undeterred and committed to continue with the provision of integrated protection, health and nutrition and services, especially for women and children.Al-Iman Hospital currently provides: 1. Child protection services in the health facility level2. Women protection services in the health facility level3. GBV in the health facility level4. Nutrition services5. Healthcare services

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks 1: Safety and security is the major and high risk across Syria. Likelihood: Medium,Impact: High Mitigation measures: a. The project has selected Sarmada as this location has remained safe from airstrikes due to its proximity with the Turkish border. Also, HA has signed an MOU with Idleb Health Directorate that provides security from local influences. Furthermore, the project will manage a warehouse outside the hospital in addition to the one based in the hospital to store life-saving drugs. Risk 2: Supplies of essentials, particularly drugs, are dependent on supply from Turkey and the access tocross border aid might be limited. Similarly, the continuous availability of professional staff on a long-term basis is low risk. Likelihood: Low, Impact: Medium Mitigationmeasures: A. HA has two warehouses to store life-saving medicines in sufficient quantities. It also engages with other actors like MSF, Americares and WHO for the supply of medicines and other consumables on need-basis. Risk 3: The hospital needs continuous access to stable electricity, fuel connections. Such interruptions can cause problems in the incubation section as well as to interrupt surgical procedures. Likelihood: low, Impact: High Mitigation measure: Currently, electricity is covered through generators.However, there is always a risk of interruptions either due to fuel supply or generators going out of order. HA has therefore proposed solar energy solutions for the hospital to use as an alternate and reduce its reliance on fuel-based energy generation. Risk4: Syria being a patriarchal society where men have dominant roles at the household and community levels. Women require cooperation and permissions from men to participate in the community therefore the project may not effectively deliver the intended services.The project will ensure the provision of all services in a culturally sensitive and traditionally the acceptable manner through female staff and female CHW's.
