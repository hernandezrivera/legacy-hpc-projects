Project Summary

IOMs’ health interventions aim to provide life-saving assistance and improve the resilience of affected populations, with special attention to gender and age considerations and vulnerable groups.
This will be achieved through contributing to
i) Increased safe, dignified and meaningful access to and utilization of quality comprehensive health services for all crisis-affected populations, with special focus on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS); and non-communicable diseases (NCD);
ii) Improved health-seeking behaviour, encouragement of healthy living and promotion of the utilization of essential service packages and community engagement, with special attention to vulnerable groups
iii) Measures to prevent, control and (timely) respond to health emergencies, during disasters (including monsoon and cyclone) and outbreaks of communicable diseases, including COVID-19 and acute watery diarrhoea (AWD).
IOM also aims to contribute to the improvement of quality of services through performance monitoring, evaluation, coordination and capacity building.
IOM will directly implement these interventions through eight health posts (HP), 5 primary health care centres (PHCC) and two severe acute respiratory infections treatment centres SARI ITC), as well as work with five implementing partners to support six HP, five PHCC, one comprehensive maternal centre and 383 community health workers (CHW) for Risk Communication and Community Engagement (RCCE). In addition, IOM will support government facilities in host communities through human resource and staff transport as well as supplies and maintenance of infrastructure and equipment, overall supporting two upazila health complexes, four union-level health and family welfare facilities as well as three community clinics.

1 - Identified Needs

The Government of Bangladesh (GoB) and humanitarian partners continue to coordinate to provide life-saving assistance to more than 900,000 Rohingya refugees in Cox’s Bazar district, who have increased vulnerabilities due to risk of communicable diseases, limited access to comprehensive health services and poor living conditions. Furthermore, the impact of the COVID-19 outbreak on health-seeking behaviour may overall negatively affect health outcomes (Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment, May 2021). While the surge in COVID-19 cases mid-2021 resulted in a scale up of functional beds at camp-level SARI ITCs, the coinciding movement restrictions decreased utilization of health facilities (Health Sector Bulletin #15). IOM facilities, for instance, witnessed a decrease in consultations of more than 6% in the first nine months of 2021 compared to the previous year, affecting use of sexual reproductive health (SRH) and immunization services, and possible negative health outcomes for women and children. Other diseases with outbreak potential are also still concerning. Confirmed cholera cases until August 2021 were already significantly higher than in the whole year of 2020 (Epi-Highlights, Week 39, 2021); and 2021 witnessed increases in (vaccine-preventable) diphtheria cases and a recent surge in dengue fever. This demonstrates the need for improved health-seeking behaviour through effective community engagement and risk communication, including CHWs for health promotion/education, awareness raising, household visits, courtyard sessions and dissemination of Information, Education and Communication (IEC) materials, with consideration for gender, age and vulnerable groups. There is also a need for increased access to primary and secondary health services through support for facilities offering the essential health package (with attention to women, children, and vulnerable groups); secondary care (including ambulances, referral costs, availability of specialists); comprehensive SRH services including: antenatal care (ANC), postnatal care (PNC), assisted deliveries in facilities, basic emergency obstetric and new-born care (BeMOnc) and comprehensive emergency obstetric and new-born care (CEmONC), modern family planning methods and GBV services; and integrated holistic MHPSS services in healthcare facilities. The IOM MHPSS needs assessment in 2021 highlighted experiences of depression, stress and anxiety, as well as related coping mechanisms, resilience and protective factors among Rohingya and host communities. These findings show the need for continuation of promoting positive coping strategies, providing comprehensive MHPSS community services and outreach, as well as cultural and creative activities (e.g. Rohingya Cultural Memory Centre). Movement restrictions in 2021 also limited activities for camp-level disaster risk reduction and preparedness. Heavy rains damaged shelters and health facilities and the need for increased training and improved inter-sectoral coordination was identified during the response (JRP Implementation Update, Aug-Sep 2021). Continued support for systems and institutions for early diagnosis, isolation and management of diseases with outbreak potential is needed to strengthen emergency preparedness and response, including prepositioning of supplies, support for mobile medical teams and medical hubs, continued monitoring and surveillance, reporting, rapid investigation and contact tracing teams, sentinel sites for sample collection, laboratory systems, functional beds/isolation centres for management of diseases with outbreak potential, as well as coordination of the Dispatch and Referral Unit (DRU) for referrals in emergency settings and COVID-19 cases.

2 - Activities or outputs

1- Access to and utilization of quality and gender sensitive primary health services, especially for women, children, and other vulnerable groups.2- Strengthened delivery of specialized and emergency care in secondary and tertiary health facilities through deployment of specialists in various disciplines (including obstetricians and gynaecologist), provision of emergency referral (with dedicated 24/7 ambulance and payment mechanism) and support for community referrals.3- Provision of comprehensive and life-saving sexual reproductive health services, including ANC, PNC, assisted delivery services, modern family planning, Menstrual Regulation (MR) & Post Abortion Care (PAC), integrated GBV/SRH services and referrals.4- Support information management (stationery, equipment, communication cost), supply of visibility items and other printed materials (e.g. registers, guidelines), and establish an electronic medical record system.5- Coordination, preparedness and response measures for health emergencies; disaster operations of emergency mobile medical teams, ambulance dispatch and referral during health emergencies, and support of repositioning of medical supplies and other logistics.6- Establish and maintain institutions for early diagnosis, isolation and management of diseases with outbreak potential, including SARI ITCs with maternity wards, and support the health sector through the dispatch and referral unit (DRU).7- Monitoring and surveillance, including reporting through DHIS II, Early Warning, Alert and Response Systems (EWARS), support for rapid investigation and contact tracing teams, sample collection from sentinel sites, and establishment of laboratory systems (with quality assurance mechanism) for diagnosis of diseases and complications.8- Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) protocols, procedures and systems are established and monitored at all facilities (including provision of PPE and waste management systems), and interventions for the prevention of transmission (such as mass vaccination campaigns) are supported.9- Strengthen the capacity of all staff (health workers and CHWS) to provide quality services through training on SOPs and treatment guidelines on relevant topics (IPC, NCDs, communicable diseases, COVID-19, MHPSS etc.), workshops, on-the job training, and supportive supervision.10- Ensure medical logistics and supply chain management for the provision of quality medicines, medical supplies and equipment.11- Continued maintenance and repair of all facilities for improvement of quality of services, disaster preparedness, IPC compliance, and installation of MHPSS spaces with maintenance and repairs of equipment.12- Effective community engagement and health risk communication approaches through health promotion and education, awareness raising, household visits, courtyard session and dissemination of IEC materials, with consideration of gender, age and vulnerable groups.13- Provision of holistic mental health and psychosocial support (MHPSS) services as integral component in health care facilities.14- Conduct comprehensive community support MHPSS services, increasing outreach, socio-relational, cultural (such as Rohingya Cultural Memory Centre), art and creative activities promoting positive coping strategies.15- Conduct facility assessments and program audits/reviews.
