Project Summary

Cash for food transfers provide flexibility for beneficiaries to choose priorities among food needs, Syria Relief will implement the revised Standard operating Principles (SoPs) which were updated according to the current changes in the Syrian crisis context, so this project will be a continuation of the cash support projects in the most severity ranked (severe and major) districts in Idleb and Aleppo, Grants will be provided to the same beneficiaries on a monthly-basis through a money transfer agent contracted and vetted by Syria Relief following the procedures agreed in the SoPs. The value of cash transfers will be US$60 per household, based on the standard food basket agreed by the Food Security Cluster and Cash Working Group for Syria. The cash transfer will be delivered over eight months in 2019 to (4,000) food insecure households and their children in Idleb and Aleppo to meet their needs and fill the gaps in the food security poor response in these locations. Syria Relief will be communicating with CBR-TWG to ensure proper coordination and be updated about cash transfer challenges. The selection will be targeting families who have met one or more of the following vulnerability criteria since these will affect directly/in-directly the food security (priority will be given to those that meet multiple criteria):
a) Families living in skeleton buildings, unfinished buildings, tents, makeshift shelters etc.
b) Families sharing collective space in their shelter with other families
c) More than one family in household or households with more than 10 members
d) Elderly (60+) without family support
e) Single-female headed household (children under 15) without extended family support
f) Single-male headed household (children under 15) without extended family support
g) Pregnant or Lactating Female in household without extended family support.
h) Disability of head of household
i) Disability of dependent
j) Demonstrated severe medical condition of head of household
k) Demonstrated severe medical condition of dependent

Needs

According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview 2019 (HNO), almost eight years of conflict in Syria has resulted in an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Because of the hostilities, large-scale displacement of people within Syria continues; and as part of local agreements, which largely occurred in areas that were previously classified by the UN as either besieged or hard-to-reach; resulting in 1,470,523 IDPs in the targeted districts classified to be in major and severe conditions of food insecurity, including those living in last resort sites and collective centers, in addition to overburdened host communities. Although markets are functioning in many areas, the conflict is highly unstable, particularly in Idleb where the security and humanitarian situation has rapidly deteriorated. If people are displaced to areas without functioning markets, or existing markets in some areas deteriorate, then in-kind assistance remains the only feasible means to ensure people can receive food. The catchment population of these districts is ( 4,242,849), while the total number of FSL sector People in need PiN in these districts is more than 2 million people, out of those 1,542,000 people are food insecure and the rest 558,000 people are at risk of food insecurity. Part of those food insecure people around 97,336 are living in Afrin district, who are likely to be at a higher degree of needs than the prevalence in the district as these locations were reached with humanitarian assistance less than 3 times between January and October 2018 according to the gap analysis of the FS cluster in NW Syria. These districts include a huge number of returnees to their origins in 2018, this number roughly is around 177,500 people, 29%out of them (51,500) are adopting emergency coping strategy and at greater level of vulnerability (WoS (FSA and FSLA) – 2018). With an 83 - 89 percent people living below poverty lines (3.6 million people) of the population in these districts are estimated to be living in extreme poverty and food prices have increased dramatically. As a result, the proportion of income spent on food has soared whilst incomes and household food production have decreased. Before the crisis, about 25% of households spent over half their annual income on food. In 2018, an estimated 40 percent of assessed households in these districts spend more than 65 percent of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65 percent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency negative coping strategies as around 50%of households have reduced the number of meals they consume each day, and more than 30 per cent restrict the food consumption of adults to allow children to eat.Upon to the needs assessment carried out in 2018 by SR staff in Idleb and Aleppo, 77% mentioned that many people are facing shortages. Persons with disability was the most vulnerable groups/individuals in the recently displaced population according to 19% of KIIs, followed by pregnant/lactating women as the second vulnerable group.As per the Gender livelihoods assessment conducted by SR in December 2018, during crisis, women resorted to having an effective role in securing family income, in particular that they lost their husbands, lost their source of income (agricultural land, employment, productive assets) or injured head of their households then became more responsible for their families and children.Reliance on food assistance for a significant proportion of Syria’s population is expected to continue throughout the coming year. Long term displaced increasing vulnerability and suffered from increased prices in commodities. For the critical Sector Objective 1 of “ Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance ”, coverage is typically only between 0-25%

Implementation of activities

• Communicating with the FSL cluster to get the data of the food insecure people with the gaps in response. Then a coordination with the other FSL respective partners in the field and Turkey hub to avoid the duplication in the response. Later on, the identification of the exact locations to be reported to the cluster. • Tow trainings will be provided to the field teams in two topics, Protection Mainstreaming and Cash Transfer Programming, to enhance and promote a mutual understanding of cash programming and implementation.• Signing the Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with each local council in the identified communities. This is required to maintain the community engagement and participation in the implementation. Establishing the vulnerability review committees. Committee members were independently selected from the community and consists of but not limited to three – four well-known people from the community like taking care to include key demographics from various age and gender groups. The selection criteria will be clearly explained to committee members. They provide SR team with the data to be verified afterwards.The selection criteria as mentioned in the summary. • Commencing the procurement of the Money transfer delivery/distribution agencies. Then contracting with the awarded money transfer agencies (MTAs) for the distribution of cash. Later on, SR will orient the agency to the developed cash SoP. • Printing the paper vouchers and deliver them into Syria to be distributed to the beneficiaries prior to the cash transfer. The vouchers will include some security features to avoid the counterfeited samples. • Distribution of paper vouchers to the beneficiaries. The distribution points will be selected after high coordination with the local councils to get the security clearance and easy access to the vulnerable groups. * SR Field Team will communicate with the beneficiaries through local councils to announce distribution dates and schedules. • Distribution of the cash by the agents. At the distribution points, dedicated female staff will be maintained to ensure female access and protection. At the distribution of vouchers, dedicated advice and consultations served by female staff will be maintained to ensure female access and protection.• Carrying out the distribution monitoring by the M&E team during the distribution. Accompanied with the distribution process either for paper vouchers or for cash, MEAL team will be existed to observe the process and raised the beneficiary awareness to the complaint mechanism.Syria Relief aim at consulting 20% of the beneficiaries, • Post-distribution monitoring will be conducted after each round of cash distribution. SR monitoring team in the field will carry out the post-distribution monitoring survey after two weeks from the redemption accomplishment in each round, the survey will be conducted to 20% of the beneficiaries to assess the quantity, quality, MT agents and SR staff behaviors. perceptions on the targeting process (and knowledge of criteria), and the use of the cash provided (e.g. used, kept, shared.. etc.) as well as to keep an eye on the market an price inflation to adapt with better timeline and mechanism of intervention.• Payment of Agents will take place after the reconciliation of the supporting documents in each round. At the end of each distribution period, Syria Relief will collect the redeemed vouchers and reconcile all of the supporting documents and invoices. Following verification by the relevant units of SR operational staff (finance, procurement and MEAL) according to the developed SOP. The total number and value of vouchers received will be tabulated. Upon confirmation, both the SR field team member and the vendor will sign the financial sales receipt. Accordingly, vendors will receive their payment.• SR will report the cash distribution activities to the FSL cluster and CBR-TWG on monthly basis using the 4Ws matrix formats

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumption – The activities can be completed without delay.Risk: As the activities will be implemented during the rainy season, Syria Relief might face difficulties accessing the area. Mitigation strategy: SR has anticipated such situation could happen and as a consequence, included the risk of not being able to access the communities and market areas due to heavy raining or flooding by designing the work plan of the activities.Risk: Escalation of conflict exacerbates insecurity in target areas affecting the SR's ability to operateMitigation strategy: 1. Ensure the operational context is secure enough to ensure access 2. Hiring of staff and volunteers from the local community. 3. Staff trained on remote management. 4. Re-direction of programming into other accessible governorates.Risk: Voucher programming mismanaged / Corruption/Price manipulationMitigation: 1. Be open with the community about the project 2. Use clearly verifiable beneficiary targeting criteria and check beneficiary list 4. Segregation of duties 5. Strong data management 6. Have a plan B ready (pre-identify back-up vendors)Risk: Forging / Creation of Black MarketMitigation: 1. Print vouchers with watermarks 2. Change colour of vouchers every month 3. Print progressive numbers on the vouchers 4. Awareness raising campaignsRisk: No official approval to start activities and/or limited staff access to monitor progress/activities in the fieldMitigation: 1. Advocacy efforts 2. Effective use of remote monitoring systems 3. Continued negotiationsRisk: there is a risk markets are not functioning properlyMitigation strategy: The situation of the market will be monitored on regular basis. In case the situation would change, SR would consider switching the modality Assumption – The money transfer agent is following all SR policies, instructions and methodology.Risk: Diversion of cash when using money transfer agent/Hawala Mitigation: The agent needs the beneficiaries’ signatures and beneficiaries’ original vouchers alongside the invoice requesting payment, otherwise the agent will not be reimbursed. The signed lists should be co-signed by SR finance. Verification of payment with a random sample of beneficiaries A complaints and feedback mechanism will be in place managed by the SR for reporting of any possible issues with the transfer agent. SR will use some tools for cash risk mitigation: Regular price monitoring exercises will be conducted to ensure that the project does not have an adverse effect on prices and market functionality. Feedback mechanisms will be available to allow non-beneficiaries to request being considered for inclusion in the project, as well as for people to raise concerns about inclusion errors.
