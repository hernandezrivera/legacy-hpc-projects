Project Summary

The purpose of the project is improving the lifesaving assistance and equitable access to essential protection services for IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities in South Darfur and East Darfur. Vulnerable women, girls, people with disabilities, UASC and elderly will be prioritized among the PSNs identified. ALIGHT strategy intends to increase and strengthen the community-based structures (CBPNs, Women and Youth Safe Spaces) in ED and SD to have a leading role in the protection monitoring, prevention and response. 4 Women Centers (2 in SD and 2 in ED) and 1 Child Safe Space in SD As Salam will be constructed and child protection activities will focus in this locality. Awareness and capacity building on protection issues will reinforce the intervention of staff, community-based structures, local justice system and health workers. PSNs and in particular survivors of GBV and child abuse, children with disabilities and unaccompanied minors will be sustained with case management, PSS, legal aid, recreational activities and cash support based on their needs. Children will be targeted with a campaign for civil documentation and birth registration to ensure they can claim their rights and access the different services. Vulnerable women will receive dignity kits and will be supported to develop income generating activities to increase their living conditions. Community-protection projects will be developed to mitigate protection risks for example connected to access resources. Indeed, the complexity of protection’s causes and consequences requires collaboration and coordination among sectors. Therefore, ALIGHT will be present with protection, wash, shelter/NFI, health and nutrition interventions in the target localities. The synergic vision consents ALIGHT to look beyond the emergency approach to build pathways of behavioral and value change with IDPs, returnees and host communities to enable a protective environment. Due to the conflict started the 15th of April, the protection needs increased and therefore Nyala, Khartoum and Gezira State are considered as hotposts localities for specific life-saving interventions, in addition to the previous localities in ED and SD. Alight will set up mobile GBV and PSS teams to provide mobile protection services to outpost locations where static services do not exist. The mobile team will consist of case workers and community mobilizers for GBV sector and health, and nutrition staff for health and nutrition sector deployed on a weekly or bi-weekly basis as the situation may demand. Once mobile coordination mechanisms are established and security clearance obtained, mobile teams travel to target communities on a set interval. The number and composition of mobile team members and necessities needed will depend on displacement density, context and population per site, the distance between sites with static services and assessed needs for direct program support. In case staff deployment is not possible, Alight will provide remote support for case management and PSS, in collaboration with local partners. At the same time, Alight will build the capacity of existing service providers in the community, which will be able to identify and appropriate support survivors (the capacity building will include PSEAH). Capacity building and awareness raising will ultimately increase direct access to services for the affected population, whether they are offered by Alight, other partners, government, or local authorities. Alight will also distribute dignity kits and provide FTR services or alternative accommodation for UASC in collaboration with the Clusters and partners.

1 - Needs

In September 2022 OCHA indicated more than 2.3 million people in need of humanitarian assistance in East Darfur and South Darfur. South Darfur hosts the largest protracted IDP population in the country and East Darfur is hosting the largest group of South Sudanese refugees in all of Darfur (UNHCR, 31 July 2022). The rule of law and access to justice remain low in Darfur as police forces have limited presence and are not equipped to deal with protection incidents in the region (Global Protection Cluster 10/02/2022). Therefore, IDPs are often subject to attacks, including incidents of gender-based violence and kidnapping. Since the coup in October 2021, Darfur has witnessed an increase in sexual violence incidents against women and girls, including rape and sexual assault and many such incidents go unpunished. An average of 56,5% of girls is married before 18 years old in both states. (OCHA, September 2022). The Protection cluster’s 2023 HNO Persons in Need (PiN) is 3.2 million, of whom 56% are children, and 22% are women. Inter-tribal conflict, protracted displacement, the economic crisis, food insecurity, poor basic social services, flash flooding, and the refugee influx are the main drivers of humanitarian needs. Intercommunal violence is also likely to increase during the harvest season due to the competition over land and resources. Identified needs includes: physical protection of IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities (both women/girls and men/boys), including at water and firewood collection points and during cultivation and harvest seasons; prevention and timely response to protection risks for PSNs (including women, children, the elderly, persons with disabilities, the chronically ill and marginalized), who are most at risk of all forms of violence; need of legal services, documentation and birth registration, whose lack also affects freedom of movement and access to basic services. Unable to meet their basic needs, many vulnerable individuals adopt negative coping strategies, such as unsafe or exploitative labor, begging, and committing crimes, child labor, child marriage and survival sex. So, there is urgent need to increase access to livelihoods and provision of health and PSS. Due to the conflict started the 15th of April, the protection needs increased and therefore Nyala and Khartoum are considered as hotposts localities for specific life-saving interventions. Heavy fighting with indiscriminate bombing and shelling coming into civilian houses forced thousands of people to flee Khartoum and Nyala North and to be displaced in Gezira State or in Nyala South or surrounding areas. No law enforcement body is present on the ground. Families are forced to move and sometimes their members split; in addition to the looting and occupation of civilian houses by armed forces, all these factors increased protection risks, especially for women and children, with consequent reporting of GBV incidents and identified UASC and high risk of sexual exploitation due to critical economic and security situation. Conflict-related trauma affects the entire population, and the lack of adequate response and coping skills also increases protection risks, especially for children and women who rely on male adults in the family for livelihood and are exposed to domestic violence due to the high stress, as reported by the GBV Sub Cluster. The conflict also negatively impacts the community-supporting networks that were active before the crisis, due to deaths, displacement, widespread violence, connectivity breakdown, and fear. SCI reported the increase of recruitment of children in armed groups, especially in Darfur; addressing this critical child protection incident will also contribute to the de-mobilization of paramilitary groups. GBV survivors are facing further risks because of the disruption of the services and referral pathways (due to insecurity, looting and bombing and lack of qualified staff).

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

ALIGHT uses the AGD approach throughout the project design and implementation, especially during beneficiary selection and data monitoring. The project focus on strengthening community-based protection structures and collaboration with other sectors like Health and Nutrition, WASH, Shelter and NFI to ensure the Do No harm principle. ALIGHT follows the IASC Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action, the IASC Guidelines for Inclusion of Persons with Disability in Humanitarian Action, the Child Protection Minimum Standards in Humanitarian Action and the Sphere Handbook. ALIGHT commits to ensure accessibility across the age categories and vulnerability scenarios, with particular regard for pregnant women/new mothers and infants, older and severely ill and people with disability. ALIGHT also participates in the Sudan Protection Cluster and GBV and CP sub-clusters and local working group to ensure coordination, networking, sharing and learning of best practices, advocacy and protection monitoring.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

ALIGHT follows the AAP approach, supporting community participation, feedback and ensuring transparency about the interventions. This is to ensure the project decisions and actions are responsive to the expressed priorities, needs, capacities and views of all persons of concern irrespective of Age, Gender, Diversity and Disability backgrounds. ALIGHT will ensure a balanced boys, girls, women and men in all project activities with relative 60% female and 40% male. Data for beneficiaries will be disaggregated by gender, age groups and location, as the project activities are tailored for different groups in line with developmental stages, needs and vulnerabilities and basing on relevant parameters appropriate in reverence to heightened protection risks of the persons of concern. Activities like awareness raising and dialogues will be conducted in women and child/youth safe spaces and in the communities.ALIGHT participates in the PSEA Network in Sudan, attending meetings and supporting assessments and coordination with other UN agencies and other relevant international INGOs. ALIGHT has a PSEAH Policy, Guidelines for Sudan, Child Protection Policy and Code of Conduct and it is undergoing a revision of the PSEAH Implementation structure, ensuring accessibility to Feedback and Complaint Response Mechanism to all the beneficiaries and capacity building for staff, partners and suppliers or contractors about SEAH standards. The PSEAH Implementation structure in ALIGHT follows the Six IASC Core Principles and the principles of anti-discrimination, accountability, safety security and protection and survivor-centered approach, ensuring the respect of survivor’s will and confidentiality in every step of the reporting and responding process. Different communication channels are available for the beneficiaries (hot line, front desk, suggestion boxes, email...) and confidential data of SEAH incidents will be shared to other agencies or donors based on pre-existing agreements. Under the project, ALIGHT will conduct Safeguarding risk assessments to mitigate any potential or identified risks of SEA. ALIGHT will ensure Zero Tolerance for SEAH. Jointly with the PSEA task force, ALIGHT will develop relevant IEC materials and distribute it in communities, raise awareness on SEAH and ensure appropriate measures are undertaken for investigations.
