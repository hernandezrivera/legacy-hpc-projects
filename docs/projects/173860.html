Project Summary

This project takes into consideration the gender dimensions of the impact of COVID-19 due to the increased burden of care, hygiene and sanitation needs this outbreak poses on women and girls. The project aims to scale up YIPDI’s provision of lifesaving GBV assistance to the most vulnerable conflict affected populations (women, men, girls and boys) in Borno State whilst building the capacity of Community Base structures to ensure timely access to critical multisectoral GBV services through the use of toll free telephone lines to provide case management psychosocial support and effective referrals.
The main objective of this intervention is to enhance timely access and build Community Base capacity to provide critical GBV services for vulnerable individuals and support referrals to lifesaving health services, remote mobile one to one, and video counselling, follow-up sessions and mass dissemination of key GBV prevention messages integrated into COVID-19 prevention, which includes safe hand hygiene practices and social distancing, delivered through targeted sensitizations, with specific sessions targeting boys and men in 4 LGAs of Borno State (Dikwa, Jere, Gwoza and Monguno)..

1 - Needs

Northeast Nigeria, especially Borno State, is one of the largest continuing humanitarian crises in the world. Widespread displacement has taken place and continues due to ongoing conflict between Non state armed Group and the Nigerian Armed Forces. The conflict appears to be intensifying and needs are massive. According to OCHA Situation Report published on 26th Nov 2020, the number of people in need of urgent assistance in north-east Nigeria rose from 7.9 million in May to 10. 6 Million 2020. Most vulnerable groups include children, the elderly, adolescent female-headed households and people with disabilities.The GBV sectors estimate that In Borno state Nigeria, up to 1,510,277 individuals are in need of GBV services, among these 546,618 are IDPs, 325,368 are returnees, 429,486 are host communities GBV 2021 HRP/HNO. Abduction, sexual violence and rape, is a defining characteristic of the ongoing conflict, with 6 out of 10 women in the northeast having experienced one or more forms of gender-based violence (UNFPA).Like other health emergencies, it is expected that vulnerabilities of women and girls will increase with the outbreak of COVID-19 as restrictions are put in place by the government leaving women with reduced access to services, reduced ability to continue with livelihood activities and increased disease burden due to social norms creating increased health and safety risks. Additionally, as service providers become increasingly focused on providing care to COVID-19 cases,it is likely to impact other essential services such as reproductive health services and the clinical management of rape. In 2019 88% of perpetrators of GBV cases reported to case management agencies were husbands. Many of these women are trapped in their homes with their abusers which heightens GBV risks and prevent access to critical services and social network. Service provision schedule has been disrupted greatly and service providers have had to withdraw frontline personnel from deep field locations in order to curb the spread of the virus, leaving affected women and girls with limited or no option of accessing services. In addition, pre-existing gender inequalities and harmful norms, coupled with increased exposure to abusers at home and within camps, creates the right environment for violence to thrive. In almost all the countries affected by the pandemic, there are unsettling amounts of information on the surge of GBV . In Nigeria, Women’s rights organizations and service providers across the country have reported increase in GBV incidents reported to them since the COVID-19 outbreak. It is clear, however, that most cases of GBV will remain unreported due to the lack of available, safe, ethical, quality response services, as well as fears of stigmatization, reprisal, and lack of access to appropriate information on seeking help (GBV Sub Sector, 2020). As clearly illustrated in the policy brief published by the UN in April 2020, women and girls are disproportionately impacted by the COVID-19. In the Northern Nigerian context women and girls have limited access to information due to restrictive gender norms that inhibit the participation of women in public spaces.Furthermore, the economic fallout from livelihood loss since the invent of Covid 19 have led to increased household stress which can increase the risk for intimate partner violence (IPV) and also increased risky coping strategies including survival sex, early/forced marriage and and risk of SEA (IASC GBV guidelines, 2020) . Women have limited resources to purchase infection prevention products such as soap, hand sanitizers and face masks. Menstrual health and hygiene needs of women and girls often go unmet. With the pandemic, these are further exacerbated. As such, it is critical that YIPDI sustains its efforts to protect vulnerable communities during the COVID-19 pandemic and ensure timely access to lifesaving GBV services.

2 - Activities

Overall project objective: To improve access to critical GBV services and strengthen community resilience and structures that prevent and respond to Gender-Based Violence. -The intervention will scale up the continuation of timely access to critical multisectoral GBV services for vulnerable individuals through capacity building of the locals for the provision of GBV case management and psychosocial support services. Reproductive and sexual health education will be provided to adolescent girls and boys via the hot mini-groups sessions by YIPDI staff, held at the LGAs were YIPDI has their operations, taking into account the need for social distancing. the remote service delivery will use YIPDI’s existing hotline access number, scaled up to support state-wide access to lifesaving services. The call centre workers will provide remote mobile video counselling, referrals, follow-up sessions, mobile adolescent sexual and reproductive health information and referral services throughout Borno state. Working in close collaboration with the Ministry of Women Affairs (MWA) and YIPDI will establish a state-level comprehensive hotline infrastructure using YIPDI’s toll-free line as a base which will be expanded to a national reach. To ensure the continuation of quality services for these vulnerable women and girls, referral pathways that links to one-stop centres and other essential services will be updated regularly in coordination with service providers and widely disseminated to facilitate timely referrals. Confidential survivor information will be managed through the GBVIMS and Primero platforms. In a bid to minimize the challenges of movement restrictions and social distancing guidelines that potentially hinders the provision of essential GBV services to women and girls. YIPDI has partitioned areas within safe spaces in field locations where they operate, that are converted and sectioned into telephone booths for case management to enhance access to telephones for those vulnerable women and girls who may not have access to mobile phones. The booths will be equipped with internet facilities, including the installation of solar power to keep the infrastructure operational at all times for prompt access to a confidential space. Additionally, the spaces will be manned by volunteers living within the communities who will check temperatures and supervise adherence to social distancing, hand hygiene and crowd control. YIPDI’s WASH hygiene promoters will work in collaboration with the GBV volunteers to make sure hand-washing stations have adequate supply of liquid soap, clean water and alcohol-based wipes to disinfect handsets after each use. Different modes of communication which include key GBV and COVID-19 prevention messages in different local languages will be developed to publicize the toll-free line and its use. For a wider reach, especially for areas that are not accessible, radio jingles will be used. In the 4 LGAs where YIPDI operates and has ongoing GBV service provision, trained volunteers selected from the community will carry out mini-group sensitizations on GBV and awareness-raising on the remote case management incorporated with COVID-19 prevention messaging. YIPDI’s WASH team will provide handwashing stations and access to soap and clean water for hand washing in the call centre and ensure the centres’ sanitation facilities meets the minimum WASH sphere standards. This will also be replicated in YIPDI operated safe spaces. Soap used for hand washing and in sanitary facilities as well as Vaseline will be produced by women who were trained by YIPDI’s GBV team through a life skills acquisition project.
