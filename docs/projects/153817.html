Project Summary

BAORD SEDO seeks to a cash-for-work program in the Iraqi governorate of Diyala Al- Adheim - Jalawla Al Saadia district for a total of 1250 beneficiaries 14 working days. This program will aim to facilitate the return process to Target areas and improve food security in the area through two means: 1) providing cash income to returnee, and other vulnerable families in order to increase economic access to food, and 2) increasing food security though projects that improve agricultural and market conditions.
Cash-based interventions, such as cash-for-work, have various advantages. Direct cash transfers provide a flexible resource to affected households and empower individuals to spend money according to their needs and priorities. Distribution costs are usually lower than distributions of food or non-food items and CFW can be implemented quickly because it is not reliant on delay-prone shipping. CFW stimulates recovery of the local economy by creating short-term work, reintroducing income flow, supporting local businesses through the purchase of construction materials, and encouraging households to purchase food and non-food items locally. CFW programs also fund the rehabilitation of damaged community assets while maintaining the dignity of the affected population through meaningful community engagement in the selection and implementation of projects. CFW activities provide short-term employment for large numbers of the population and can be designed to encourage the integration and participation of women and other marginalized groups in the area .
Cash-for-work projects provide men and women to earn an income, and can increase their status both within their household and in the community. The projects also have the potential to lead to greater decision making authority over household spending and other related decisions. Sensitivity to gender dynamics will be a central component of all programs,( BAORD ) projects are targeting women headed households, which have increased drastically over past years. Helping these women gain access to financial resources not only helps them to improve their livelihoods, but also promotes positive attitudes in the community toward women in terms of their capacity and their contribution to economic and social development. The approach would be to engage communities, through village committees, and participation of both women and men throughout the project cycle to ensure effectiveness.

Needs

Years of conflict in Iraq has created a protracted and complex humanitarian crisis and emergency that has eroded livelihoods and increased the vulnerability to food insecurity of the majority of the population. The current conflict has displaced families and destroyed food production systems. During 2018, as many as 11 million Iraqis will require some form of humanitarian assistance. Both availability of and access to food and agricultural inputs has been negatively affected by the conflict, as have agricultural livelihoods. There are 2.9 million people who are food insecure including displaced families, host communities, returnees and remainees that require food assistance. Seventy seven percent of those who are food insecure are women, children, elderly and female headed households. Out of those displaced, 1.5 million people are expected to return to recently retaken locations of origin during 2019. The cumulative adverse effects of displacements caused by conflict has resulted in loss of life and earning, population displacement, depletion of productive assets and damage to social and physical infrastructure. Under cash for work activities, agriculture related damaged infrastructure will be rehabilitated and repaired, which will improve agricultural productivity in host communities and facilitate the process of returnees. Scaling up cash for work activities/opportunities will help increase income diversification and stabilize access for poor households to meet life saving necessities. Setting up a cash for work project in the targeted governorate will also help to develop/create productive communal assets and rehabilitate/repair damaged agriculture infrastructure while supporting livelihoods, reducing people's dependence on food assistance, promoting community recovery and building resilience. Mitigation Measures

BAORD have experience in successfully delivering various kinds of project despite ongoing conflict. Additionally, we have designed security plans for the team and participants according to different scenarios. Our primary priority is the safety and security of the beneficiaries; therefore, we will conduct safety and security assessments prior to implementation and update the safety plans accordingly. And there will be coordination with local authorities in the community Clear selection criteria and vulnerability criteria standards will be explained to communities and beneficiaries. And accessible accountability mechanism will be used in the project, known communication lines as well. Awareness session will be provided to the beneficiaries, Do no harm Standards will be applied at all stages of implementation and the project life cycle. In order to do that, BAORD will conduct baseline assessments to ensure that none of the project activities will harm the community in any way, such as by disrespecting the local culture, ethics, customs, and traditions. For example, we will organize the training workshops with particular sensitivity in order to accommodate the needs of both men and women, and to make sure both groups are comfortable. Additionally, BAORD will conduct security risk assessments and safety and security reports in order to ensure the safety and security of project beneficiaries. We will not risk the safety of the people. When there is a dangerous risk in the community or when we are particularly concerned about the well-being of beneficiaries, activities will be postponed, modified, or canceled accordingly. Community Empowerment and Mobilization Program equipped both organizations with capacity, knowledge, and procedure to mobilize and advocate for projects in and based on community, so the communities adopt to the projects. Furthermore, BAORD will open offices in the area of project implementation and the offices will be open to the community, and our field staff will follow-up with project participants on a weekly basis in order to mentor them and provide career counseling and coaching, as well as listen to any concerns they might have. And facilitator will be provided during the training
