Project Summary

This program is a continuous intervention from SDH and its partners to contribute to the reduction of morbidity and mortality, improve overall health services for conflict-affected Syrians, and provide equitable access to healthcare services in order to support the healthcare system in the targeted areas, as well as to provide life-saving and life-sustaining humanitarian assistance.
The intervention will focus mainly on the provision and improvement of primary healthcare services and other specialized services.
The program will provide essential healthcare services of many sub-districts targeted beneficiaries through the supported health facilities in the north of Syria.
this program consists of these projects:
1- support primary healthcare centers (PHC):
- Afrin PHC B0203081.
- Bab El-Hawa camps PHC center G0703148.
- Muhambal PHC G0705035.
- AlSahara PHC in Atareb.
(the plan is to fully support 5 PHCs so far in line with the essential health package, these centers are distributed in Aleppo, Idlib, targeting: 96,000 BNFs/ Men: 12,400, Female: 33,600, Boys: 22,800, Girls: 27,200).
2- support Field hospitals FH:
-Afamia FH G0703170 in Harim (with the ability to relocate the location to cover the Needs in other location's gap).
( the plan is to fully support the hospital with the equitable operational cost to be able to provide the essential healthcare package, targeting: 60,000 BNFs/ Men: 11,100, Female: 23,700, Boys: 7,300, Girls: 17,900).
3- support Physiotherapy centers:
- Batbu Physical rehab center B0200206.
- shaheed Mustafa Abed Physical rehab Centre B0200207.
- Youth center B0200112.
( the plan is to fully support the PC with the equitable operational cost to be able to provide the essential healthcare package, targeting: 12,000 BNFs/ Men: 9,500, Female: 1,700, Boys: 1,000, Girls: 800).
4- support SDH's CCTCs.
- Al-Seeraj Center in Atareb CCTC_3.
- Al-Seeraj Center in Hazano CCTC_5.
- Al-Seeraj Center in Daret Azza CCTC_7.
- Al-Seeraj Center in Kelly CCTC_20.
( the plan is to continue fully support the CCTCs with the equitable operational cost to be able to provide the required IPC level & treatment procedures for COVID-19 cases, targeting: 6,000 BNFs of suspected+confirmed+probable cases/ Men: 2,800, Female: 1,900, Boys: 800, Girls: 500).
the program within its all projects will provide health services for targeted areas' populations including the host community and the IDPs.
some projects of this program are already secured funded, and we are reaching more partners & donors for more cooperation opportunities.

4 - Needs

According to the March 2021 Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 13.4 M people inside Syria are in need of humanitarian assistance (including 2.4 million in acute need). While the disrupted health system impacts the entire population, the health sector utilizes its severity scale to focus its support on sub-districts with the level of Areas of highest severity that are generally found in the North of Syria. Among the 12.4 M people in need of health services, 5.6 million children under age 17, 6.4 million women of reproductive age, 0.5 M elderly, and 3.1 M with disability. (Compared to 2020, the health sector PiN has increased 3%)Th COVID-19 pandemic has exacerbated weaknesses in the health system, till 4 of April 21,486 COVID-19 cases and over 600 deaths reported in the NWS.Relatedly, areas of active hostilities – such as north-west and north-east Syria, IDP camps, and informal settlements, and locations without reliable healthcare systems and shelter remain highly vulnerable to epidemic-prone disease. Recent assessments indicate the need for an improved supply of medicines and services within PHCs, – particularly for non-communicable diseases, where NCDs and chronic conditions particularly affect vulnerable groups such as IDP women and girls, elderly people and PwD (an estimated 28% of people over 12 years of age live with disabilities in target locations according to HNAP 2021) and requirecontinuous care by PHC providers. CDs remain a priority.Need for increased COVID-19 testing and vaccination. where 22,143 confirmed cases were reported with 655 total death. The pandemic is occurring within the context of already dire health conditions, infrastructure and high-risk factors for the transmission of COVID-19.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: possibility: high effect: high mitigation measures: - Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities. - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare through the Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA), - Providing preparedness training for the facilities and staff-care in case of incidents. 2- Risk-based violence, gender, and sexual exploitation as a result of overcrowding and shared toilets as well as the exploitation of staff to patients in exchange for service delivery: possibility: high, effect: high mitigation measures: Establishing separated toilets for men and women in the health facility - Ensuring that the waiting areas are sufficient for all patients – Allocating someone from the guards to regulate the flow of patients – Establishing an easy-to-reach complaints mechanism( complaints box, WhatsApp number, exit interviews) - Training all employees on the policy of preventing harassment in the workplace and including this policy in their contracts. 3- neglection of Healthcare for Vulnerable Groups and Equitable Access to Health Services: possibility: moderate, effect: high mitigation measures: - Analyze and disaggregate all data by sex, age, and disability. Analyze the needs of vulnerable and minority groups (such as adults and children with disabilities), throughout the program cycle (assessment, analysis, design, implementation, and monitoring) with identification of risk factors and rights violations impacting service provision for beneficiaries. 4- Discrimination in service delivery: possibility: moderate, effect: high mitigation measures: - Selecting accessible and safe locations of health facilities.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

•Assumptions: SDH will coordinate closely with the health clusters, as well as local authorities (including health directorate) and local NGOs operating inside Syria throughout the proposed intervention, sending feedback to the cluster when interventions are needed to be addressed.Risk: an emergency status occur (increase the #COVID-19 cases "the second wave").Mitigation: SDH will use its coordination channels for developing a coordinated response plan with the partners. Risk: The close of borders which prevent the shipments of supplies and medications to access to facilities. mitigation: Develop a strong sustainable strategy for accessing and deliver goods through local actors. •Assumptions: health facilities remains accessible for the SDH team and for the beneficiaries. Risk: The conflict prevents beneficiaries and team access to facilities. mitigation: SDH through the S&S team has developed a strong risk management strategy for emergency situations. •Assumptions: currency exchange rates remain within an acceptable range that does not affect planned activities. Risk: high volatility of exchange currency. mitigation: fixing the operational cost inside Syria in USD.•Assumptions: the medical supplies & medicines & PEE are provided to the supported HFs. Risk: UNSCR wasn't renewed, So, interruption of WHO supply line as well as the supply chain from Turkey to Syria. mitigation: SDH has a strong supply chain based on Turkish & Syrian companies that move the commodities & medical supplies for commercial purposes which will not be affected by the closure of UNSCR to ensure the continued provision of Medical supplies to NWS.
