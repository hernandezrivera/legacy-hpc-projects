Project Summary

This project proposed by Sustainable Children Aid (SCA) will target 7,500 vulnerable children, directly and indirectly to include IDPs, Returnees and Host communities in Kajokeji County of Central Equatoria state with significant impact aiming at supporting Children and young people affected by armed conflict and will further contribute to Protection Cluster objectives 1 and 2 in the HRP 2020. This project will also build on the previous Child Protection and GBV interventions done by SCA implemented through funding from RR-IOM and UNICEF in 2017 IDPs populations (Children and Women) in Logo, Kerwa and Ajio IDP camps, Liwolo payam. In 2020, SCA proposes to improve protection of conflict affected males and females under the age of 18 years and internally displaced children, especially separated and unaccompanied children.

1 - Needs

In 2019, Kejo keji and its surrounding areas, just like other Counties in South Sudan, witnessed an increased humanitarian needs poised by presence of the internally displaced persons, returnees and host communities. Emergency household items, food security and livelihoods support, protection, education and health services were among people’s priorities. Health services are poor, with some health Centres not functional while others lack medical supplies, especially anti-malarial medicine, and there were no health referral outlets in many of the places, many schools are not fully operational due to lack of teachers, who allegedly flew to neighboring Uganda due to the conflict. The overall situation of Children has continued to deteriorate in 2019 and expected to worsen in 2020, with continued incidents of recruitment, abuse, exploitation and other grave violations being reported(OCHA: Fact finding mission to Kajokeji,August,2019). (IRNA- Korijo IDP Camp 17-10-2018 Report), out of the 40, 294 IDPs, 69% are Children i.e. 27,718 (Boys-13823.Girls-13895), needing urgent, robust and coordinated humanitarian assistance. The recent development of events in South Sudan, including the planned return of SPLA/ IO opposition leader, Dr. Riek Machar and the anticipated formation of a unity Government, is expected to attract returnees, IDPs, to return to Kejo Keji and South Sudan and this is expected to further put pressure on the already strained and inadequate humanitarian assistance in the area, causing further suffering, distress, violation and abuse of rights of the affected people especially children and women who are the most vulnerable. In 2020, Sustainable Children Aid (SCA), proposes to build on its long years of local presence and expertise in implementing Child Protection programs in Kejo keji, this includes, Child Protection activities implemented, through funding from RRF-IOM, UNICEF and IsraAID since 2012, to provide the much needed protection services through; conducting awareness raising to communities on Child Protection (Child rights, Land mine awareness, etc), conducting additional Identification, Documentation Registration of UASC for FTR process (this will include the 59 caseload SCA already has in the CPIMS) , Conduct follow up and support to vulnerable Children through case management and referrals to specialized services (Health, Legal, etc.) providers operating in Kajo-Keji, Provide training to social workers on PSS and PFA in the respective communities on Child Protection in Emergencies and case management, continuing Family tracing and reunification (FTR)of UASC, Support some identified vulnerable children and place some of the UASC in alternative care, Identify additional Community based Child Protection focal Points and multi sectoral focal points on CPiE, Provide parental skills training to caregivers and community members, Provide structured community-based psycho-social support activities to the children using local Play materials through the existing community structures, awareness raising on prevention and response to Child Protection issues through campaigns.

2 - Activities

This Child Protection project will target 7,500 vulnerable children, directly and indirectly to include IDPs, Returnees and Host communities in Kajokeji County of Central Equatoria state with significant impact aiming at supporting Children and young people affected by armed conflict and will further contribute to Protection Cluster objectives 1 and 2 in the HRP 2020. This project will also build on the previous Child Protection and GBV interventions, SCA implemented through funding from RR-IOM and UNICEF in 2017 IDPs populations (Children and Women) in Logo, Kerwa and Ajio IDP camps, Liwolo payam. In 2020, SCA proposes to improve protection of conflict affected males and females under the age of 18 years and internally displaced children, especially separated and unaccompanied children who will be better protected through the following activities: 1. Comprehensive case management. 2. Strengthening the established community-based child protection mechanisms. 3. Improvement of the established referral pathway for GBV and CP. 4. Provision of alternative (Poster) care for vulnerable Children. 5. Conduct Family Tracing and Reunification.6. Mainstreaming/Integration of CP activities into Education, WASH (Partner, ARC to support with Hygiene and Sanitation services/Materials), Health (ARC and TRI-SS to support with Medical) SE/NFI (Titi Foundation and SPEDO to Support with Shelter and Non Food Items and (Titi Foundation, SCA and Diocese of Kajokeji) to support with FSL. 7. Conduct awareness raising to prevent and respond to child protection issues.8. Train care givers on good parental skills to poster parents9. Train community based child protection and multi-sectoral focal points /service providers.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

To ensure programming is gender and age responsive, SCA will adopt the following 3 strategies: • Ensure planning and programming are relevant and responding to the needs of affected population equally as per target groups, Gender and vulnerabilities. This will include considering gender and age related issues during assessment, design, monitoring and evaluation and reporting phase of the CP project. • Ensure affected people irrespective of their gender or age are actively engaged in influencing humanitarian planning process more than just being passive recipient of aid. This will include supporting participation of affected people with specific gender and age groups to provide thoughts, ideas and suggestions on how they should benefit from humanitarian assistance. • Ensure process and programming are gender and age responsive and ensuring proper monitoring, accountability and reporting including use of IASC Global Gender commitments and framework. This will include using monitoring and global tools that captures age and gender issues in all phases of project implementation and reporting.
