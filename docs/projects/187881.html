Project Summary

IOM CCCM activities plan to contribute to two of the CCCM Sector objectives for 2022 -2023 and to improve the coordination of humanitarian lifesaving, multi-sectoral assistance.
The project aims to address Objective 1 through:
Managing and coordinating multi-sectoral assistance in 11 planned camps (PCs).
• Continuing the implementation of small-scale care and maintenance activities in 11 planned camps and in 50 self-settled sites.
Strengthening emergency preparedness and (fire) response in 11 planned camps and in 50 self-settled sites.
Continuing services monitoring, referral and AAP mechanism in 11 planned camps.
Identifying and collecting information about IDP sites – including the ones managed- in Idleb and Rural Aleppo, including multi-sectoral gap analysis and referral to IOM Cross-Border Unit for multi-sectoral response and assistance.
The project plans to meet Objective 2 through:
• Enhancing camp management capacity of camp management in informal settlements in Idleb and Rural Aleppo under the Site Management Support (SMS) project; and
• Enhancing camp management capacity of NGOs through the provision of ad hoc- trainings for humanitarian workers based in Syria.
Strengthening participatory structure, including implementation of activities to promote women and other vulnerable groups’ participation and management of committees, in 11 planned camps.
IOM intends to implement the above activities until 2023.

4 - Needs

In November 2021, the CCCM Cluster tracked 288,175 displacements in NWS. The needs of those who are displaced are rapidly increasing and critical basic services such as health, shelter, food, WASH, and protection persist for hundreds of people. The devaluation of the Syrian pound compounded by the COVID-19 pandemic has made life more difficult for an already strained displaced population. The last assessment conducted in the 11 IOM PCs showed that men and women are depending significantly on humanitarian aid due to the inability to access livelihood opportunities and to sustain their families with shelter and other services. Economic hardship, insecurity and threat of continued displacement leaves more than 2.7 million people in NWS vulnerable and in need of essential, multi-sectoral assistance in formal and informal sites, in addition to mitigation measures for fires and floods. In line with the Cluster objectives, IOM will reinforce camp management systems in 11 PCs providing basic care and maintenance of camp infrastructures and coordinate the provision of services. The Monitoring Assessment Team (MAT), IOM will continue identifying and assessing the needs of most vulnerable informal sites and refer them to the IOM Cross-Border Unit for targeted assistance. IOM will provide camp management support to strengthen site representation and management in most vulnerable, self-settled sites and conduct mitigation of COVID-19 and fires targeting 60,000 IDPs.

5 - Implementation of activities

IOM will continue assessing the needs of the affected people in 11 IOM-managed camps through a participatory and inclusive approach. Based on the feedback received, IOM will base programs and assistance to better address the needs identified of the affected communities living in the 11 PCs for the coming 2 years. IOM will continue to strengthen CE to enable camp populations to participate in decision-making processes and take an active role in CM, focusing on age and gender vulnerability criteria. IOM will coordinate multi-sectoral assistance and provide care and maintenance in camps. IOM will implement the Area Based Approach to target the most vulnerable and underserved self-settled sites through MAT to identify, assess, and refer for a sectoral response within IOM Cross-Border. IOM will train site representatives, provide small-scale care and maintenance, training to mitigate risks, and implement community participation activities. MAT team will be responsible for conducting site-based assessments and reporting on various incidents with data collection following IASC standards and will regularly be reported to Clusters. IOM will continue capacity-building targeting IPs’ staff and site committees to improve AAP and protection in CCCM. IOM will work with IOM Protection and Access Units to target community leaders in NWS with capacity building on International Humanitarian Law (IHL), IASC standards, humanitarian principles, humanitarian access, protection, CCCM, and PSEA.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risks: 1. Continued operations of PCs and informal sites will create negative pull and push factors for IDPs. 2. Needs assessments will raise unrealistic expectations among IDPs 3.COVID-19 in NWS spreads among the population without adequate health infrastructure 4.Management structures could provide legitimacy to actors who are not representative of the IDP communities, increasing inter-community tension. Mitigation:1.Maintain CCCM guidance on camp expansion and establishment ensuring that camps are a last resort. 2. Ensure assessments are brief 3. COVID-19 mitigation measures are applied during the project period). 4.Ensure management structure is representative of the community with selection criteria. 5. Provide gender balanced awareness-raising with IDPs and field workers; ensure IPs work with humanitarian principles and apply IOM AAP frameworks. Risk 2: IDPs are evicted by local actors or security forces. IDPs living in PCs and self-settled sites are evicted by landowners, local actors and security forces without prior notification. Mitigation: 1. Establish communication between camp management and local actors. 2. Develop guidance materials to support IPs communication with local authorities. 3. Maintain referral and incidents mechanism. 4. Ensure camp closure and exit planning involve the affected community and local authorities. 5. Develop guidance for exit plans and camp closure. 6. Ensure IDPs are voluntarily leaving and other shelter options are identified.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

For all activities, the risk of SEA by IPs and their sub-contractors exists during thephysical implementation phase. For this, key mitigation measures include havingall IPs ensure that their staff have read and signed their Code of Conduct, havetaken part in PSEA training, have a dedicated complaint mechanism in place, andthat TPM teams monitor to ensure activities are carried out in a principled manner.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access restrictions: active fighting, sporadic shifting of conflict lines and potential closure of the border might prevent delivery of humanitarian assistance. IPs are advised to report security and access-related issues and develop evacuation/contingency plans. Assumption that COVID-19 cases are promptly isolated to avoid risk of widespread outbreak. COVID-19 risk mitigations are applied based on Cluster COVID-19 guidance. Assumption local stakeholders respect humanitarian facilities in line with land agreement signed with the related Local Councils which guarantees utilization and access. Risk local stakeholders disregard commitment of non-interference in CCCM activities and/or take control of the site. IOM through the IP will engage with the local council to mitigate this risk. Access issues will be shared in coordination with OCHA Access WG. Contingency areas will be pre-identified with the cluster during project design. ‘Duty of care’ strategy in place for local partners. IPs are to report incidents interference and security incidents. IOM utilizes third-party monitoring to conduct warehouse verification, distribution, and post-monitoring activities to mitigate the risk. HLP Due Diligence Guidelines will be utilized.UN Cross-Border agreement allowing the delivery of critical humanitarian aid expires July 2022 with possibility the resolution will not be renewed. Mitigation adequate and experienced IPs are trained on CCCM to ensure coordination of services continue.
