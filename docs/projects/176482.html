Project Summary

Through this intervention, VSF Germany intends to support the IDPs, host subsistence farmers and fishermen in Tonj North county to increase their livelihood self-sufficiency. VSF Germany is implementing a 3 year European Union Funded project title FEDIS in Tonj North County. As per VSFG’s FEDIS project impact assessment report-2020, only 47% of the community in Tonj North is food secure based on the parameter of having enough harvest/yield to last to the next growing season. A crop yield assessment conducted by VSF G in November 2020 revealed that the average yield per HH was at 270 kg/HH which is far below the national threshold of 1,100kg/HH. This was due to the poor harvest stirred with the prolonged dry season and later torrential rains that lead to extensive floods. Covid'19 pandemic remain a great threat to the community espcially to the elder persons that compose a substantial number of the population. Further exasperating the situation is the persistent inter clan conflict that has led to displacement of over 5000 HH. Other factors like rapid deterioration of the value of the South Sudanese pound which directly affects the purchasing power of the community as well as high market prices continue to ripple the local economy. A disproportionately large proportion of income (57%) is spent on food purchase from markets but which only contribute 35% of the food consumed. The high inflation rates, high market prices and often times inaccessibility to the main market centre increases the vulnerability of the community. It is critical to support the most affected households to begin to restore lost crop agriculture or fishing livelihoods, thus enabling them to have increased food availability through personal production, as well as a small income for increased food access. The project targets 5,000 HHs of Tonj North County (2000 M, 2000 F, 500 Boys, 500 Girls) in Warrap State. Of these, 2,500HHs (1,500 M, 1000 F) from the payams closers to water points (Manalor and Awul Payams) will be supported with fishing kits which avails immediate benefits by providing white meat for the HHs(fish) as well as providing an alternative source of income through the sale of surplus fish. This income will be used to supplement the HHs food requirements as well as address their urgent needs. To ensure maximum realization of benefits as well as ensure sustainability, HHs will be trained on improved fish production practices. The selected households will be further disintegrated into vulnerability cadres to include people with disability, lactating and expectant mothers and elderly people. The selected fisher folks shall be strengthened on better fish harvesting and handling techniques. They shall be trained on environment friendly fish catching methods (correct net sizes and hooks), fish hygiene, preservation, packaging and marketing. The households will be supported to increase their immediate food stock and have their knowledge of basic food production enhanced to boost their food preserve and stores to sustain them through the lean period. Beneficiaries will be encouraged to reserve seeds for the following planting season using both known traditional and conventional methods. A further 2500 HHs (1500 F, 1000 M) will be supported with vegetable kits to address their nutritional requirement in the short-term (early 3-weeks). Income from the sale of surplus produce will be used to purchase alternative food requirements such as Dura (sorghum) as well as supplement the HH dietary needs. Farmers will receive basic training on improved agronomic practices with regards to micro-irrigation of small-scale vegetable production and Covid'19 awareness lessons. All 5000 HHs will be supported with crop kits and farming tools for the main cropping season thereby increasing the farmers’ access to quality seeds and tools.

1 - Needs

About 80% of the population of War rap State practice subsistence crop farming with 69% of the community keeping livestock. However, between 31- 49% of the population suffers recurrent food insecurity as a result of low levels of agricultural and livestock productivity which contributes only 54% of the food consumed. Tonj North County in Warrap State has experienced long standing recurrent conflict characterized by inter and intra-communal violent cattle raiding attacks that invariably results in loss of life, loss of livestock assets, displacement of populations, destruction of property and disruption of livelihoods. Other triggers of violence have invariably been attributed to competition over resources (water, pastures and migratory routes access), socio-cultural factors, political tensions and fear for spread of livestock diseases by migrating herds. The annually recurring conflict between the warring clans of Atoc and Awan as well as Lier and Noi clans. Escalated in November 2020, resulting in the displacement of 5,139 HH, loss of over 2,200 heads of livestock and disruption of the main cropping season activities in Tonj North County. Some communities in Manlor, Awul, Akop and Alebeek payams in Tonj North missed the cropping season due to displacement negatively impacting on the realization of sustainable development in the county. The September 2020 IPC denotes the area to be under IPC level 4-emergency stage and this is envisaged to further deteriorate during the hunger gap (lean season). With the increasing cases of Covid'19 pandemic, it is notable that the livelihood of the community is likely to affected with the strict regulatory measures being put in place to restrict movement and limit essential livelihood activities. Through the project, livelihood assistance by provision of agricultural inputs (seeds, tools and fishing kits) aims to support the selected households displaced by conflict to begin restoring their lost agriculture or fishing livelihoods, thereby enabling them to have increased food availability through personal production, as well as a small income for increased food access. The households will not only increase their immediate food stock but will have their capacities enhanced to build on existing local strategies and preserve thereby store food to sustain them during the dry seasons.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

VSF Germany has a several policies in respect of the working environment in South Sudan and includes chapters on Child Protection and Protection of Women that recognizes the vulnerability and susceptibility to common vices . Affirmative action on equal opportunities and access to basic rights is active. Frequent analysis of beneficiaries dimensions especially on agro-pastoral livelihoods are carried out by project managers to inform interventions designs. Such programming measures include ensuring inclusion of all beneficiaries in Community Dialogue meetings and workshops where important decisions are made, and also feedback forums set to recieve any cases reported on PSEA; program design that ensures equitable allocation of resources to women and children hence possess some have decision rights – like vegetables sales hence some aspects of protection; Through established Community Dialogue procedures, VSF Germany staff and supported extension workers undertake consultations with community leaders and representatives from boma level to county levels shall provide awareness information on consequences of any kind of abuse reported and promote social protection mechanisms, safety and security considerations, feedback and complaints mechanisms, and also management of services for vulnerable members which safeguards the the vulnerable from exploitation and abuses.

7.4.4. How will your project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable members (e.g. individuals living with disabilities, individuals who are not literate, women and girls)?

Community Dialogue procedures have incorporated beneficial customs of treatment of aged people which gives them maximum respect and preferential treatment. The case of people with disability is championed during the same sessions and equitable treatment procedures are ensured. Such actions include physical support with animals during vaccination and treatment, modified cultivation implements to suit their postures . Agricultural and animal health services are highly sought in the pastoral communities of South Sudan, much so in the present context where survival and sustenance are significantly dependent on crop farming and risk of losses of livestock from disease are high. Through established Community Dialogue procedures, accountability is progressively established at the state level, then county level and further to the villages through popular participation and all round representation.

7.4.1. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about the project, feedback mechanism and/or complaint mechanism?

Community leaders and the oversight committees established specifically provide awareness to the community and ensure the project objectives reach the majority of the beneficiaries. Through established Community Dialogue procedures, accountability is progressively established at the state level, then county level and further to the villages through popular participation and all round representation. The project will employ participatory techniques to refine program feedback and reporting procedures that will enable beneficiaries and the community to determine potential and actual negative effects of the project and refer the same for action. The mechanisms will ensure transparency, responsibility and accountability by all parties especially project staff and local leadership. A more responsive and effective traditional feedback and complaints mechanism will be adopted. However, parallel mechanisms are in place to validate and interrogate the system, especially in respect of responsibility and accountability of personnel of partners and the organisation

7.4.3. How will your project use the beneficiaries’ feedback to adapt project assistance?

The beneficiaries feed back and complaints received through the established Community institutions shall be kept confidential and be held with the mass they hold. The project through the project manager shall analyse the contents of the complaints and feedback to determine their gravity and how they affect the intervention. With his team through dialogue, the committees shall be informed of the final result of the analysis and the outcomes incorporated in to the program. Feedback and complaints mechanism remain key and other parallel mechanisms in place to validate and interrogate the existing system, especially in respect of responsibility and accountability

8.11. Mitigation Measures

In the current context of conflict and insecurity, access to water for domestic use, and firewood by women are significantly constrained. This is further compounded by increased absence of male heads of households. The program will limit the amount of time taken from women’s busy schedule through fore-planning with women, and advocating for prioritization of intervention process for female and old persons headed households to limit the possible extra time that women and girls may need to spend outside the home as a result of the program interventions. One of the major limiting barriers to the minority groups in this project location is male dominance and females exclusivity to community issues due to their busy house chore schedules. The project will limit the amount of time taken from these women’s busy schedule through prior-planning with women, and advocating for prioritization of intervention process for female and old persons headed households in this context access to water for domestic use, firewood and market for animal products by women are significantly constrained and highly exposes them to risks of exploitation.The programs intends to enhance equal and safe participation of women through training in basic community livelihood skills that will encourage them to part of the community resources caretakers. The project will be monitoring for discrimination, abuse and exploitation with particular attention to women, children, older people, people with disabilities and minorities. The project monitoring will focus on knowledge, attitude and practices of beneficiaries in regard to potential harm agricultural activities. The project will find out about discrimination, exploitation and abuses on beneficiaries by service provider and people in power within the program value chain. There will be evaluation of safety, dignity and security measures employed by project.

12.1.2. Decreased proportion of populations facing IPC 3,4 and 5

The high levels of acute food insecurity continue to be driven by the cumulative effects of the cyclic inter-communal conflict that has disrupted livelihoods and impacted on households’ access to other food sources, such as wild foods, fish, and livestock products. The conflict has continued to cause enormous destruction to communal productive assets, loss of human life, and caused enormous local population displacement. The action will promote activities that will protect the displaced population from fall further in vulnerability by building on their resilience and ensuring food stability. The most important shocks after the prolonged conflict and floods are related to food insecurity or access to food. Access to food is either through own production or purchase form the market. In this regard, the project will strengthen capacity for increased food production and also on-farm income generation to counter high food prices. The other type of resilience that the action will stimulate is protection from climatic shocks – this type of shock has seriously affected food production due to changed cycles of drought, dry spells, irregular rainfall as well as accompanying crop pests and diseases. The project will support and promote adoption of appropriate climate smart production techniques and technologies that shall strengthen productivity and spring them to food stability.
