Project Summary

The overall objective of this project is to protect the livelihoods, improve the resilience and productive capacity of communities in middle and southern eastern slopes.
The objective is drawn from the Country Strategic Objective:
SO3: “The ability of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with the protracted crisis, including environmental threats, is supported, while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued”.
This is also in line with FSS guidance for Immediate assistance and livelihood support for farmers in Area C of the West Bank.
This is also in line with FSS objective ob1: “Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced”, and the activity: “Livelihoods of vulnerable households are protected, and their resilience, food security and productive capacity restored/enhanced”.

Needs

The context of Palestine has remained largely unchanged from the past year, with the main challenges originating from the protracted conflict/crises, economic stagnation, high unemployment, gender inequalities and high rates of poverty and food insecurity. In the WB, the prolonged period of slow economic growth, restrictions on movement, trade, investment and access to land and water resources, together with higher food prices, continue to erode the fragile livelihoods of poor Palestinians. Poverty (13.9%) and food insecurity (12.7%) are still entrenched and more localized to pockets of land, particularly in the Southern Governorates. More than 60% of the Bedouin and herding communities in Area C of the WB are food-insecure (WFP, 2018). The lack of progress towards peace and reconciliation creates an unsustainable economic situation in WB. Additionally, tensions further increased in WB following the announcement of the move of the United States Embassy to Jerusalem at the end of December 2017. The overall guiding principle for CARE’s theory of change is adopting an impact-driven approach, acknowledging that our impact groups (women, girls, boys & youth) require emergency and early recovery supports, however, CARE will utilize interventions that address both immediate needs, as well as the underlying drivers of those needs. Based on CARE various reflections & learning from the gender transformative programming in the opt, female negative experiences of the protracted fragile context are compounded by social norms which inhibit their access to food, inputs, finance and livelihoods’ services, as well as lower control over income and household decision making (CARE, validated 2018). Palestinian women own less than 8% of agricultural holdings, but they contribute about 60% to labor requirements in agriculture (PCBS 2017). This problem can be further exacerbated if the interventions under this project fail to take into account unintended negative impact (e.g. overburdening women due to the extra required workload). The design of this project has been guided by the assessments of the needs of target communities, based on a number of key sources, including comprehensive assessments conducted for similar ongoing project implemented by CARE /partner funded by Government of Canada (AYADI Project), various and comprehensive need assessments conducted by CARE & PARC in Southern, Middle and Northern WB (2017 & 2018), data & priority matrix provided by FSS cluster’s FSS (Sep, 2018), CARE/ PARC internal needs analysis, coordination meetings with MoA, Gender Transformative Insights (CARE WBG 2018), Vegetables & Sheep/Goat dairy assessment (CARE 2018), gender in agriculture researches (2017/2018 in WB). During Sep-Oct 2018, a series of consultation workshops/ Focus groups were held by CARE/PARC with representatives of farmers/ herders, women's CBOs & local councils in targeted locations to update/revalidate their needs and to guide the design of this action; with taking into consideration the gender needs and age groups. All of the activities below reflect the needs realized at the led level, and which also are in line with what has been identified from HNO. Most of the interviewed framers/herders indicated high constraints in accessing to irrigation water due to damaged/ deteriorated carriers, as well high needs to rehabilitate greenhouses, water cisterns & pools, agricultural roads, rehabilitating Livestock infrastructure, livestock inputs support & capacity building. The lack of possibilities to rebuild these agricultural/ livestock infrastructures leaves households unable to provide neither for their own consumption nor for income generation. On the other hand, Training and extension services are overlapping, not coordinated, outdated, unspecialized and not linked to consumers’ preferences. Thus, Farmers use weak and harmful production practices affecting the quality and quantity of their products.

Activities

The project was designed in coordination with other actors and with cooperation between CARE/ PARC & MoA, FSS, and other stakeholders in order to avoid duplication, maximize the number of beneficiaries those in need. The activities will be implemented in partnership with PARC, who will be responsible for the technical implementation at field level. CARE will be responsible for the overall quality management and will act as the main contact for donors & stakeholders. The project will target female and male small-scale farming /herding HHS/communities who are living in middle and southeastern slopes of WB Area C. It directly responds to the special needs of female/male small-scale farming & herding /Bedouin families and communities through providing initial production inputs; rehabilitating their individual and/or community-wide livelihood assets & infrastructure (Water networks, agricultural road, etc.); providing them with necessary supplies/tools, and with existing basic knowledge needed to preserve & sustain their farming/herding activities. Prior to any project implementation, the organization will strictly coordinate the final definition and selection of localities & beneficiaries with line ministries offices, & with any other organization working in the same area and sector of activities. The project team will finalize & validate the selection criteria per activity to ensure transparency. The criteria will look into socioeconomic status/ vulnerability, access, small-scale farmers, women-headed households (farmers), availability of water, availability of agricultural services, previous bene t from previous projects, etc. Activities for O1: Small-scale male/female livestock herders in Area C ( eastern slopes (ES) of the WB- Ramallah, Jerusalem and Bethlehem) to protect their livelihood & improve their resilience: • Provide required inputs for protecting, sustaining & increasing livestock holders’ resilience (climate tolerant seeds- (ES) Ramallah, Jerusalem, Bethlehem), farm management inputs (dividers, rehabilitating shelters/barns/farms, etc in (ES) of Jerusalem and Bethlehem)), in addition to inputs for to improve quality (e.g. inputs/ tools for better hygiene of milking & dairy processing). • Improve access to required water & road infrastructure by providing and/or rehabilitating water cisterns, water harvesting systems, irrigation networks in (Jerusalem, Jericho, Hebron ); and rehabilitating agricultural roads (Bethlehem, Hebron). • Awareness raising/extension visits on best practices & techniques (hygiene, recording systems, diseases prevention, etc.) to be conducted by the project staff and in full coordination with MoA before & during supplying inputs and trainings. Farmers expect training & extension services to guide their farm/stock management and production practices. Activities for Outcome 2: Small-scale plant production farmers in Area C improved their resilience by restoring/increasing the quality & quantity of their production for self and market consumption • Protection/rehabilitation of agriculture productive assets & essential infrastructure for farmers, including rehabilitating greenhouses in (Jericho) ,The targeted beneficiaries will be the smallest scale farmers (women/men), most vulnerable, poorest & lowest income; whose greenhouses is suffering from partial-massive destruction resulted in degradation in the quantity & quality lands’ production), community-wide or individual water networks (Jericho and Hebron), cisterns and harvesting systems, Ponds (earth, concrete, metallic) in (Jericho, Hebron); agricultural roads (Bethlehem, Hebron). • basic tools and equipment, etc. • Provide awareness/extension visits on best agricultural practices & techniques (increase production, quality, and marketability of farmers’ produce) to be conducted by the project staff & in full coordination with MoA before & during supplying inputs/awareness.

Indicators

Indicators Indicators for O1: • 150 barns rehabilitated serving at least 4,500 sheep/goats in favor of 150 HHs (HH headed by women represent 10%) • 30 cisterns with 3600 cubic meter water storage capacities constructed/rehabilitated; serving 150 dunums in favor of 120 HHs (women represent 10%). • 8 Km of of irrigation networks and 2 water distribution points constructed/ rehabilitated in Hebron and Jericho ( 4 Km and 2 distribution point ensuring access of herders who own at least 3,200 heads of sheep/goats, & irrigating 320 dunums of lands in favor of 80 HHs (women represent 5%) and 4 km of irrigation networks constructed/ rehabilitated to serve & irrigate 320 dunums of open fields (Vegetables) in favor of 80 HHs (women represent at least 5%). • 25 Km agricultural roads rehabilitated/opened (7.5 km; serving 188 herders (7,500 sheep/goats) & enabling their access to 1,500 dunums (women represent at least 5%) and 17.5 Km enabling their access to 3,500 dunums (open fields vegetables) in favor of 437 HHs (women represent at least 5%). • 600 women headed HHS (small-scale female breeders) provided with necessary hygiene milking collection containers; for better quality & shelf life of milk (women represent 100%). • 150 women headed HHs (small-scale breeders) provided by necessary hygiene small HH dairy processing kits & infrastructure support in order to make their processing units more convenient & hygienic for women, for processing dairy products with high quality & to get rid of the dairy wastes without affecting the environment (women represent 100%). • 250 small-scale breeders are provided with high yield/drought tolerant seeds (37.5 tons) for fodder production covering 2,500 dunums, (women represent at least 15%) • 1,698 herders (approx 49.7% female) produce higher quantity & quality dairy products and apply better linked to existing marketing channels. • 3,125 temporary working days created in favor of 283 male skilled/unskilled laborers. Indicators for O2: • 6 pools (earth, concrete, metallic) with total storage capacity of 3,600 cubic meters are constructed/ rehabilitated serving 300 dunums of agricultural lands in favor of 30 HHs (women represent at least 10%) • 30 dunums greenhouses provided with a cover sheet, internal irrigation networks, and organic fertilizers in favor of 30 HHs (women represent at least 16%). •. Productivity & accessibility of 4,150 dunums of marginal agricultural land improved, in favor of 577 HHs (women represent at least 7%). • 5,145 temporary working days created in favor of 454 male skilled/unskilled laborers. CARE/PARC will finalize the selection criteria, complaint and feedback mechanisms (documenting, processing and responding to complaints to ensure transparency and accountability to affected population), and will conduct a baseline survey using participatory approaches engaging CBOs and local committees. Monitoring data will be collected from the field and reported on a monthly basis. CARE/PARC will also conduct field visits and regularly meet with communities’ representatives/ beneficiaries/etc. to ensure effective implementation, ownership, vulnerability etc. over project results and progress. An end line evaluation will also be conducted. In addition, protection will be mainstreamed in the project delivery (including respect for principles of non-discrimination, do no harm, etc.); and cross-cutting issues, including gender, age, environment and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR). Furthermore, the project will be incorporated into CARE’s broader Impact, Learning and Accountability System (ILAS), which has overarching quality control standards and process for monitoring, promoting reflection and capturing learning, evidence, outcomes, and impact. Finally, there is a potential adoption of indicators under the guidance of the FSS, upon the availability of resources and consolidation of good practices.
