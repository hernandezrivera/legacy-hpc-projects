Project Summary

Cameroon is continuing to face a severe and complex humanitarian crisis in its Far North region caused by recurrent shocks, including insecurity with the increasing presence and influence of Non-State Armed
Groups (NSAGs) and armed conflicts, increasing effects of climate change with droughts and floods, and
epidemics, that can occur simultaneously, in an already underserved region with a disrupted local economy. The impact on the most vulnerable populations is severe as it exposes them to important protection risks, while the continuous violence, including recurring incursions by NSAGs in the targeted areas, but also clashes between communities, has continued to lead to conflict-driven displacements. In this context, the IRC is proposing to address humanitarian needs through a two-year intervention aiming at providing an integrated response for crisis-affected populations in the Far North region of Cameroon. The IRC has designed the proposed program to combine both lifesaving and early recovery services for affected populations so that communities can increase their resilience and capacities to better face future shocks. The IRC will implement a tailored, beneficiary-centered and gradual and integrated package combining complementary interventions in Protection, Multipurpose Cash, Economic Recovery and WASH. The IRC will involve local actors, authorities, community leaders and the targeted communities in the implementation of the program, to ensure it is addressing their most pressing needs, as well as to foster local ownership of the activities.

1- BESOINS / NEEDS
1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

The Far North region of Cameroon is facing a severe, complex, and protracted humanitarian crisis, combining chronic insecurity due to the increasing presence and influence of Non-State Armed Groups (NSAGs) and their in-depth incursions in the Cameroonian territory, weak public services in a historically underserved region, disrupted local economy, and increasing effects of climate change. This situation is directly impacting the most vulnerable populations by exposing them to protection risks, at home and during NSAGs attacks on villages, along with the destruction or loss of their homes, livelihoods, and economic assets, which is decreasing their capacities to cope (e.g., limiting access to basic services such as healthcare, education or functioning markets and difficulties with meeting their basic needs) and increasing their vulnerabilities. In this context, violence and insecurity have forced 544,885 conflict-affected populations to leave their homes, among which 357,631 are internally displaced people (IDPs), 135,257 are returnees and are 51,997 out-of-camp refugees , most of them located in Logone-et-Chari, Mayo-Sava and Mayo-Tsanaga divisions which account for 90.2% of the population of concerns. Among them, 56.5% are women and girls, 62% are children and 5% are elderly . The continuous violence, leading to displacement, includes recurring incursions by NSAGs, but also clashes between communities. Indeed, recent intercommunity conflicts in the Logone-Birni division at the beginning of December 2021 have forced approximately new 60,000 persons to flee to Chad and 40,000 persons to internally flee to other areas , due to clashes over diminishing water resources. These conflicts are likely to increase in a context of food insecurity and competition over more and more scarce resources due to climatic hazards such as floods, heavy rains, and droughts. Among the displaced persons surveyed , 95.7% have fled due to intercommunity conflicts and 56.5% have no intention of returning to their areas of origins as long as the security, the economic situation, access to shelters and lands have not been improved and infrastructures have not been rehabilitated.
