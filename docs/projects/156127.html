Project Summary

The project is aimed to ensure access of all children under five including for the children with special needs, and women to the comprehensive quality CMAM services, disseminate and support C-IYCF practices and promote health and nutrition seeking and basic hygienic behaviors within the population in need.
Community Health Workers (CHWs) will cover 12 communities around Kafar jnna and Basena PHCs in Aleppo and Idleb governorates.

Number of beneficiaries / groups in Sharan and Afrin subdistrict (Kafar Janna PHC):
- 1108 Children under 5 (498 boys and 610 girls) and 493 PLWs.

Needs

According to the updated 2019 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 11.7 million people will need urgent humanitarian assistance in Syria this year. 13.2 million People require health assistance. The latest Health Resources and Services Availability Mapping System report for the Whole of Syria (WoS) shows that access to public HFs has deteriorated with only 46% fully functioning. Spesifically, Watan is aiming to cover with the basic health and nutrition services 16000 population in Basena and 6158 in Kafar Jnna. The Basena community belongs to Haram district in the north of Idlib, 6 km away from Harem, equidistant between Oraiba and Kfr Hom. The people of the mentioned communities do not have access to the basic health and nutrition services. The PHC in Basena community was destroyed. Now there are also 7 camps with a total population of 9,000 IDPs (1,500 families) where the PHC (health and nutrition services) has to be set up. According to Watan, there will be more IDP influx to the Basena surrounding communities due to the current emergency situation in Syria. This will cause an increased need for a response to mass displacements and immediate needs of host communities and IDPs, especially emergency health services. For these reasons above, WATAN Foundation is planning to support Basena PHC centre through the provision of the essential package of primary health care (level 3) and nutrition. Basena PHC centre will offer an essential package of PHC services, including: Integrated management of Newborn and Childhood Illnesses (IMNCI), Reproductive Health, Nutrition and Non-Communicable diseases (NCD). Those requiring critical medical care will be served through an effective referral pathway linking the PHC with secondary health care facilities in Harem by ambulance. These interventions will be integrated by Community Health Workers (CHWs) at community level. WATAN is supporting Kafar Jnna PHC in Afrin district which has been suffering of lack of basic health and nutrition life-saving services. IDPs from Eastern Ghouta have also taken shelter there and it stretched already constraint health and nutrition services. WATAN is planning to integrate the provided health services with nutrition services at health facility and community levels. CHWs will cover 11 communities around Kafar jnna PHC.

Implementation of activities

The first activity in the project will be training of health workers then implementation of nutrition activities in health facilities level and community level by doing:Health facility- and community-based screening for acute malnutrition to children under five and to pregnant and lactating women. Treatment of acutely malnourished children and PLW in health facility. Counselling on WHO recommended Infant and Young Child Feeding practices maternal nutrition in HFs and Community levels.PLW being reached with IYCF education in health facilities and community sessions like mother-to mother support group. Provide Micronutrient supplementation to women (PLW) and children (U5) for prevention of micronutrient deficiencies.

Protection Risk Analysis

Violence against Health Facilities and Healthcare Providers: Data on attacks against healthcare collected through Surveillance System for Attacks on Health Care (SSA)1 during the period of January - September 2018 documented 134 attacks across Syria, exceeding the incidents reported throughout the year 2017 (122 incidents). Although the rate of attacks against healthcare declined significantly over the course of 2018, violence is still a main issue facing healthcare providers and affecting their ability to respond in contested areas, with a long term effect hampering the scale up effort in newly accessible areas that witnessed recent change of control. Probability: Moderate. Effect: High - Applying the designated de-confliction mechanism for health facilities, and increasing advocacy efforts by the health sector with all parties to the conflict to engage them on health protection topics and respect of international humanitarian law (IHL). - Continued reporting on attacks against healthcare.Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse PSEA: PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) is a term used by the UN and NGO community to refer to measures taken to protect vulnerable people from sexual exploitation and abuse by staff and associated personnel. During the course of their work, aid providers come into contact with vulnerable people. Humanitarian workers have access to goods and services that put them in a position of power over the community. Unfortunately, some humanitarian workers have used this imbalance of power to exploit and abuse vulnerable members of the community. The health sector and healthcare providers should strive and commit to preventing sexual exploitation and abuse both on organizational and programmatic levels. Probability: Moderate. Effect: HighMitigating measures: - A PSEA policy describes standard of behaviour for organization’s staff and representatives, specifically forbidding sexual exploitation and abuse, and inclusive to accountability measures. - Effective community based complaints mechanisms, including victim assistance. - Effective and comprehensive mechanisms should be established to ensure awareness raising on SEA amongst personnel, with continuous training on the topic. - Effective recruitment and performance management of all humanitarian staff. - Provide information to communities and people affected by crisis about the organization, the principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the programmes it is implementing and what they intend to deliver. - Design and implement appropriate programmes based on an impartial assessment of needs and risks, and an understanding of the vulnerabilities and capacities of different groups, clear reporting and feedback mechanisms.Provision of Healthcare for Vulnerable Groups and Equitable Access to Health Services: The health sector recognizes that seven years of ongoing crisis in Syria has impacted access to health of all the population on different levels. Nevertheless, among the 13 million people in need for health assistance during 2019, five groups have been identified as the most vulnerable due to their inherent status that can be exacerbated by the crisis, 1) children under U5 (11%), 2) women of reproductive age (26%), 3) people living with disability PWD (15%), 4) over 50 years of age (12%), 5) internally displaced personnel IDPs and returnees ( 36%). Probability: Moderate. Effect: HighMitigating measures: - Inclusion of vulnerable and minority groups needs throughout the program cycle (analysis, design, implementation, and monitoring) through gender, age, and disability disaggregated analysis of beneficiaries with identification of risk factors impacting service provision. - Regular revision and updating of regular, contingency, and emergency plans with emphasis on Do No Harm principles, specifically in scenarios involving sudden mass movements of IDPs and significant returns activities.
