Project Summary

In recognition that conflict-affected primary school-aged children in Nigeria, Niger, and Cameroon face major challenges to accessing quality education and protection services, IRC Deutschland proposes to partner with ECHO under the Pilot Programmatic Partnership to deliver the 3-year PEACE project. PEACE takes an integrated and adaptive approach to education and child protection, to support 171,065 school-aged boys and girls, their carers and educators, most affected by crisis across target countries to access and learn in safe educational environments. The PEACE evidence-based approach towards improving educational access, quality, and safety builds on previous IRC programming and includes:
1. Four core models to expand access to formal and non-formal education that is conflict-sensitive and designed to support children who are most in need;
2. Support to caregivers and communities so that they may more fully ensure girls’ and boys’ safety, access, and learning; and
3. Capacity building of teachers and facilitators in order to improve the quality of instruction and provide safe learning environments for children.
Child protection measures are integrated throughout PEACE, from individual case management support to individual and group psycho-social support activities for caregivers and children.
In light of the COVID 19 pandemic, PEACE will adapt programming to ensure the continuity of child protection and learning support in safe environments, as well as COVID-19-specific messaging related to health and or WASH.

1 - Besoins / Needs

The already-weak state of education and protection services in the Lake Chad Basin has been exacerbated by years of conflict, displacement, and direct targeting of schools, putting the education of over 3 million children at risk and exposing them to further violence, with over 1,000 schools closed or non-functional due to violence or unrest in northeast Nigeria, Chad, Cameroon and Niger. AccessIn Cameroon, IRC found that targeted attacks caused 144 primary schools in Mayo Sava and Logone et Chari to close in the 2016-2017 school year, with other schools having been taken over by the military or used to shelter displaced population. As of December 2019, over 40% of villages in the Far North region did not have a primary, secondary or Quranic school, with 50% of existing schools located at more than one hour’s walk. The 2020 HRP for Cameroon also note inadequate infrastructures, including buildings unsafe for children and limited capacity of schools to welcome displaced boys and girls. Low levels of teacher attendance in functioning formal schools is another barrier to educational access, with one common factor of teacher absences being poor salary.Caregivers and communities The financial costs of schooling has been identified as a salient concern in Cameroon, especially for girls, refugees and IDPs. Many caregivers are reluctant to send their children to school due to safety concerns and the lack of safety and early warning systems to prevent and mitigate recurring risks. Girls are less likely than boys to be sent to school for safety reasons, as well as domestic chores, and cultural norms that prioritize boys schooling. The ability of adults living in times of conflict and displacement to provide nurturing care for their children may be hampered by their own experiences of trauma, compounded by economic hardship and social isolation. This can result in an increased incidence of children’s exposure to interfamilial conflict, violence or emotional neglect. Supporting parents and school personnel to cope and be a source of psychosocial support (PSS) for their children is therefore key to reducing the risks of violence to children in their homes and communities. Teacher and facilitators Many teachers lack qualifications, experience, and basic skills in PEACE target areas, with low teacher capacity being a significant barrier to achieving quality education and learning outcomes for children.Beyond teaching literacy and numeracy, there is a salient need to better equip teachers to identify children in need of case management and support the social and emotion learning (SEL) of all children. A lack of basic teaching and learning materials is also a challenge to the quality of instruction, and the continued influx of displaced children is a factor to the extremely overstretched classrooms and school conditions.Child ProtectionAbuse and exploitation of children is widespread in the West Africa region, with the majority of children experiencing violent discipline and more than 80% of children having experienced at least one deprivation of their basic rights. Protection concerns are compounded in conflict affected areas of the Lake Chad Region due to the presence of non-state armed actors and on-going violence.Displacement exposes girls and boys to protection risks including separation from their families and exposure to violence in their homes and communities, heightening levels of distress. Displaced children are less likely to be enrolled in school and highly implicated in household livelihoods through potentially risky activities, such as collecting firewood. Girls face a particularly high risk of sexual violence and early marriage. Boys face heightened risks of recruitment into armed groups and different forms of banditry, leading them into a cycle of violence that inhibits their access to learning opportunities and community life.
