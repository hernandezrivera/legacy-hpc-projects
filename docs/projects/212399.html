Project Summary

PUI targets children and youth in vulnerable and underserved communities to enhance their access to quality education in a protective and suitable learning environment. The educational intervention of PUI provides non-formal education programs to help children and youth in Syria integrate/return to formal education and continue their academic attainment. This proposed intervention aims at encouraging out-of-school children and dropped-out students to resume their education through tailored Back to Learning Campaigns, in addition to empowering those children with Self-Learning Program to enable them to catch-up and reintegrate in the formal education system. PUI will also assist children by providing them with kits, education supplies and Remedial Classes to cover for any gaps and ensure every child’s educational needs are met. Quality of education will further be enhanced by providing capacity building activities to teachers and school staff, training them on Active Learning, psycho-Social Support, and Life Skills. Moreover, PUI will ensure that children have access to safe and protective learning environment by rehabilitating damaged schools in the targeted locations and by initiating Parenting Awareness Campaign for parents and caregivers. The light rehabilitation of the schools will provide pupils with education facilities, promoting the safety and well-being of students, teachers and administration, while the Awareness campaign will contribute to a safer and more inclusive environment for students. The comprehensive strategy developed by PUI will have a direct impact on both access and quality of education, contributing to enable children’s full potential. More specifically, access will be increased by rehabilitating schools and by targeting OOSC and CwD while quality will be improved by teacher trainings and remedial classes. PUI formulates its intervention at the local level, in close collaboration with local authorities such as the Directorate of Education, as well as the local communities.
PUI policies and operational guidelines align with IASC's Commitments to Accountability to Affected Populations. PUI ensures accountability by placing affected populations at the center of all decisions and actions that affect their lives. Thus, targeted populations will be involved throughout all phases of the project to feed into the design of the interventions and guide the identification of both activities and beneficiaries based on PUI’s selection criteria. PUI’s field team will inform the community and beneficiaries about the Beneficiary Feedback Mechanism (BFM) to address any issues related to PUI’s intervention. PUI’s MEAL department will ensure timely management of all feedback received and that an accurate response of what has been done by PUI is provided to the people who shared them within a suitable time frame. In addition, PUI’s MEAL department will independently monitor all activities and will conduct post-implementation/distribution monitoring when needed. Finally, PUI aims to build a program to meet the needs of girls, boys, women and men living in targeted locations, taking into consideration vulnerabilities, to enhance their access to basic services, foster their self-sufficiency, but also promote social cohesion while ensuring that the program would have no negative side effects and that the risk for girls, boys, women and men to be exposed to any kind of risk when accessing assistance would be mitigated. All PUI staff are regularly trained on the organization’s strategy for Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Child Protection Policy, and Code of Conduct. Awareness sessions will be conducted for the community (including parents, teachers and children) to educate them about PSEA and Child Protection. Gender and Protection will be mainstreamed within this intervention, with gender, disability and age-sensitive school rehabilitations and a focus on equal access to education.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

In alignment with SO1, SO2, and SO3, PUI aims to achieve two main objectives across most of the Syrian governorates (Aleppo, Dar’a, Deir ez-Zor, Hama, Homs, Idleb, Lattakia, Tartous, and Rural Damascus), namely: 1. Providing children with early childhood care and education (ECCE), targeting a total of 14,038 beneficiaries, and 2. Providing children and youth with non-formal education programs, including remedial classes (RC) and Self-Learning Program (SLP), targeting a total of 155,253 beneficiaries. The first objective will be achieved by implementing the following activities:- Providing professional development to teaching staff and train them on the key competencies.- Providing teachers and education personnel with monetary and non-monetary incentives and teaching resources.- Providing children and teachers with school-based psychosocial support programs.- Providing children with recreational materials/Activities.- Providing schools with school furniture and teaching materials and resources.While for the second, the following activities will be implemented:- Conducting Back-to-Learning (BTL) campaigns.- Assisting children with cross-line exams- Providing teachers and education personnel with teaching resources, kits and guides in non-formal settings.- Providing children with life skills and citizenship education programs in non-formal settings.- Providing children with NGO-developed, non-formal curriculum for remedial/catch-up/SLP/ALP/home-based education, etc.)/printing the curriculum.- Providing children with supplementary printed and online materials, educational games, health awareness and MHPSS materials etc. in non-formal settings.- Providing children with recreational materials in non-formal settings/activities- Providing children school supplies in non-formal settings.- Providing teachers with teaching materials and resources such as chalk, rulers, markers, etc. for teaching.- Providing children and youth with non-formal education programs - remedial classes- Providing children and youth with non-formal education programs - Self-Learning Program (SLP)In addition to the above strategy, PUI plans to conduct light rehabilitation in51 schools, divided as follows: 4 schools in Aleppo, 8 schools in Dar’a, 5 schools in Deir ez-Zor, 3 schools in Hama, 3 schools in Homs, 4 schools in Idleb, 4 schools in Lattakia, 11 schools in Rural Damascus, and 9 schools in Tartous.Rehabilitation works will be based on detailed needs assessment carried out at the beginning of the implementation phase, and will include Repair/replacement of doors, windows and stairs with metal protections, repair/ provision of tables and whiteboards, electricity works, civil works, rehabilitation and maintenance of WASH facilities.Gender and Protection will be mainstreamed within this intervention, with gender, disability and age-sensitive school rehabilitations and a focus on equal access to education.
