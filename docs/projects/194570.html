Project Summary

UNICEF in collaboration with relevant stakeholders is planning to provide lifesaving integrated and multispectral (Child protection, Education, WASH, Health and Nutrition) humanitarian assistance targeting South Sudanese and Ethiopian refugees in White Nile, Kassala, Gedaref, South Kordofan, West Kordofan, North Kordofan, Khartoum, Blue Nile, North Darfur, South Darfur, East Darfur and Central Darfur states. Integrated health and nutrition services under this project will focus on securing essential supplies, and scaling-up the provision of preventative and curative interventions. UNICEF will work with relevant humanitarian actors on the ground to maintain and strengthen the delivery of comprehensive package of primary health care services, community-based management of sever acute malnutrition (CMAM), infant and young child feeding (IYCF) counselling and micro-nutrient supplementation for mothers and children under-five years of age. We aim to reach total of 193,300 children and pregnant lasting women in 33 localities. The WASH interventions aim to reach the most vulnerable refugees’ communities with immediate, life-saving water supply, including water trucking and rehabilitation of existing water facilities, construction of new emergency water facilities, as well as operation and maintenance services. The project will reach approximately 500,000 people. The interventions are based on national WASH strategies with a focus on maximizing inter-agency, multi-sectoral, area-based opportunities, and addressing specific needs, socio-political dynamics and humanitarian presence. As majority of the refugees reside in temporary gathering sites, they are highly vulnerable and face various challenges including trauma, physically abuse, injuries; or loss of family members and personal belongings. Through this project, child protection services will target 50,630 people with focus on supporting case management, referral services for children and family tracing.

1 - Needs

Sudan continued to face protracted, complex and overlapping humanitarian challenges. The economic situation continued to deteriorate during the reporting period with shortages of electricity, fuel, food, and other commodities severely impacting livelihoods. The protracted political crisis and widespread insecurity are likely to continue to constrain economic activity and deter investors, while high inflation and unemployment will subdue consumption. According to the 2023 Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), 15.8 million people are need to various humanitarian assistances which is reflected as 21 per cent increase compared to the previous year. This includes 2.5 million IDPs, 0.9 million refugees, 0.9 million returnees and 11.4 million vulnerable residents. Meanwhile, all people require life-sustaining support to meet minimum living standards.In September 2022, the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) estimated that Sudan hosts about 1.14 million refugees, of whom 43 per cent are children, 52 per cent are adults and five per cent elderly. Due to an ongoing verification process, UNHCR estimates that this number will be reduced to 926,000 refugees in 2023. The largest group of refugees is from South Sudan, with an estimated 811,000 South Sudanese refugees of whom 52 per cent are female. Ethiopian refugees also continued to arrive, but to a lesser extent in eastern Sudan and Blue Nile State. It is estimated that total of 58,000 Ethiopian refugees have crossed into Sudan since the start of the crisis in northern Ethiopia in November 2020. Approximately 38 per cent of refugees in Sudan live in camps, while about 50 per cent of the refugees live in Khartoum and White Nile states. Taking in consideration that voluntary return is not an option for most refugees due to the situation in their countries of origin, and third-country resettlement options remain limited. This situation disproportionately affects women, girls, and other vulnerable individuals who bear the brunt of adverse coping strategies.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

The project is designed to contribute significantly to gender equality. As women are traditionally tasked to take care of their children and family members, they are more affected by economic instability, outbreak of diseases and malnutrition of children in the affected areas. The differentiated needs of women and young girls with respect to Education,Health, Nutrition and WASH services including security/safety and gender-sensitive facilities will be systematically taken into account in project implementation. For example, construction/rehabilitation of water systems will enable people to have safe access to improved water sources closer to their houses and to collect water without spending a long-time queuing especially for the women and young girls, thereby reducing the risk of violence and abuse.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

Affected populations had been consulted at the project design and planning phase, for example to determine the locations of to be constructed water sources. UNICEF’s implementing partners shall conduct initial assessment of target refugees’ settlement including community consultation at the start of the project. UNICEF and its partners will not only engage with refugees’ community leaders, but also a wider group of stakeholders, including women and youth groups, to ensure their views and needs are included in the project design, implementation and evaluation. For women’s group, shall organize a separate meeting as women may not be comfortable to voice their opinion in front of male community members. During project implementation, refugees’ community members will be encouraged to express their views during dialogue opportunities to avoid misunderstanding and to ensure that all community members equally benefit from the project. UNICEF’s implementing partners shall be flexible to modify the engagement modality if initially planned activities are causing potential conflict (Do No Harm). Monitoring of the project cannot be done properly without cooperation from the affected population. Thus, UNICEF team shall seek for active feedback and complaints from the targeted beneficiaries regarding the project impact and progress throughout the cycle. Monitoring of the interventions cannot be done properly without cooperation from the affected population. Our team shall seek for active feedback and complaints from the local communities regarding the project impact and progress. The establishment of two-way communication channels between the service providers and the targeted beneficiaries is encouraged and highlighted as a priority within UNICEF supported interventions (taking into consideration that there are variations among the humanitarian actors in terms of the practice and use of different complaint and feedback mechanism). PSEA is integral to UNICEF Sudan’s work. Each implementing partners’ organizational risk is assessed, and partner staff are trained to prevent and respond to PSEA. UNICEF is actively engaging with other humanitarian actors and partners to ensure coordinated, appropriate and effective responses to incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse. Focal points for PSEA will be assigned across the project sites to provide the needed assistants and ensure the implementation of the UNICEF-supported interventions including capacity-building and support risk mitigation and awareness raising as well as victim assistance. All UNICEF service delivery points will offer safe and child- and gender-sensitive channels for refugees’ communities to make complaints on sexual exploitation and abuse, and referrals for survivor-centered services
