Project Summary

In September 2017, SOS CVN, with the support of our International Office, we conducted an assessment to understand the extent of the humanitarian needs. The assessment report indicated that Child headed and Female headed households, Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC), Disabled persons, and elders are among the naturally vulnerable people and most affected by the crisis. Women were left with children and with no resources to take care of them, in a situation of fear of further violence and extreme uncertainty about the future. Food remains the first priority, followed by psychosocial support and protection, education for their children, and health services.
SOS CVN will join more than 30 INGOs operational in the area dealing with lifesaving and livelihood, protection to start an emergency response programme. SOS CVN will intervene to address the food needs; the project will target 500 households and provide food vouchers to Child headed and female headed household. The project will also cater for 4000 vulnerable children through PSS and Case Management for UASC and survivor of SGBV under 18 and will identify Malnourished Children and refer them to other partners. Children that have lost parental care and with no traceable of family of origin will be admitted into Children’s Villages in Jos or Abuja. The project will organize child protection committees, so that the committees will strongly advocate on the issue and address the problem together with responsible government and other relevant partners. To address the issue of poor sanitation, SOS CVN will set up two toilets to serve the Child Friendly Center that will established in the community. The interventions will be implemented in collaboration with other stakeholders such as Child Protection Sub-sector working group, Maiduguri and Abuja level, Food security and Livelihood Sector, National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA), State Emergency Management Agency SEMA, Presidential Committee on the North East Initiative and the International NGO safety Organization (INSO).

Needs

According to the report of the assessment carried out in September 2017, people within the communities, including returnees and IDPs, were affected by insecurity that creates an impasse and makes it difficult to move from emergency to recovery. IDPs, Child headed & Female headed households, Unaccompanied and Separated Children (UASC) physically challenged and elders are among the naturally vulnerable people and most affected by the crisis. Women are left with the young children and with no resources to take care of them, The needs identified during the assessment were: 1. Food is the first priority for most of the communities. During the assessment mission carried out by SOS CVN, women who attended the meeting were carrying their small children who in most cases looked malnourished. Insecurity limits possibilities of fully resuming farming activities and local production is insufficient to cover needs. Additional food has to be imported, which results in substantial increase of market prices. World Food Program (WFP) is distributing food among some of the communities but not regularly and not to all families. 2. From the assessment report, there are 3000 UASC in Jere who have lost their family, the children looked traumatized and majority of them are out of school, there were reported case of violence and abuse. Over 4000 children are at risk due to lack of established child protection system as highlighted in the assessment report.3. Sanitation: This remains a big challenge, particularly in the urban areas of Gwoidamgari Community. Sediments, garbage and dirty water, including sewage water running into the street and along the roads, blocked all drainages. This constitutes a high risk and can quickly spread diseases such as cholera. 4. Livelihood recovery: generally, the livelihoods of the people have deteriorated precariously and far from recovery. Women seem to be quite busy to ensure the survival of their children. Some are engaged in small business for survival. This area must be strengthened, as this is a base for survival. 5. During the assessment conducted by SOS Children’s Villages in 2017, it was noted that Water is available, though drinking water is often purchased from private vendors. There are boreholes in the area, which in some cases do not work. It is not clear if it is due to poor maintenance and/or lack of spare parts or lack of skilled people.

Activities

1. FOOD NEEDS: In order to address the food needs, the project will target 500 households and provide food vouchers at NGN15000 per household per month. The project will use a system of providing E-voucher cards to beneficiaries who will get food from a vendor identified by SOS CVN. This system will prevent fraud, prevent duplication where beneficiaries can get food twice in a quarter and the voucher system will facilitate easy reporting. 2. CHILD PROTECTION: In order to intervene and address the highlighted problem the project plans to reach 4,000 children and 500 care givers with the setup a child friendly space, where the UASC can play and learn. The project staff will conduct PSS and Case Management for UASC, Survivor of SGBV under 18, Children at risk, identify Malnourished Children and refer to other partners. Children that have lost parental care and with no traceable family of origin will be admitted into Children’s Village in Jos or Abuja. Five Hundred (500) care givers will be trained on positive parenting skills this is to reduce the rate of child abandonment. The project will organize/or facilitate the setting up of child protection committees, and the committees will protect children from potential abuse and strongly advocate on the issue and address the problem together with responsible government and other relevant partners. 3. Sanitation: To address the issue of poor sanitation, SOS CVN will set up two toilets to serve the Child Friendly Centers established in the community. 500 young girls will be supported with dignity kits per quarter to improve their sanitary condition. 4. Livelihood recovery: SOS CVN will continue advocacy for improved livelihood for the target groups through participation in different thematic working groups in the area. In case of availability of funds in future SOS CVN will develop programmes to improve livelihood situation of the target group in Jere LGA. The response will directly reach 4,000 children and 500 Households with severe food, Hygiene, protection and other basic needs as expressed above and for this 349,983 EURO is required for one year. This will be a pilot and will be implemented in Gwoidamgari Community of Jere Local Government Area, Borno State The project will address the issue of child protection during project implementation by conducting briefings on child protection and safeguarding during different meetings in the community. The issue will also be addressed during the identification of beneficiaries and during every distribution exercise; we will discuss issues relating to sexual exploitation. Messages will be produced on posters and brochures highlighting the problem of sexual exploitation and abuse. Child protection committee member will be actively involved to support in the awareness raising. Each volunteer will sign a code of conduct and all reported cases to be referred to law enforcers.
