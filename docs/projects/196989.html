Project Summary

According to the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs OCHA report in February 2022
Among the 57,000 returnees:
• 5.1 thousand people live in sub-areas categorized as risk level 5 ("catastrophic") according to the JAF methodology sectors.
• 23.6 thousand people live in sub-areas classified as risk level 4 (“very severe”) according to the CAF methodology
between sectors.
• 28,000 live in sub-areas classified as risk level 3 ("severe") according to the JAF methodology sectors.
One in five families indicate that women
- Girls feel insecure in their own places, and violence based on the social level is widespread, as most families suffer from the psychological burden resulting from the displacement of a person from their home, or witnessing destruction, loss, or separation from family members and assets. More than half of the IDP population reported safety and security concerns related to displacement, and problems with habitat and resettlement.
Safety and security concerns remain a gendered issue as women and girls continue to be disproportionately affected by various forms of violence. Significantly increased risks of exploitation and abuse (including gender-based violence) were reported as a concern for female-headed households and households headed by a person with a disability; This points to the increased protection risks they face compared to other affected populations, and the lack of functioning institutions that guarantee the rights and safety of women and girls undermines their freedom of choice and capacities.
In the project, we primarily aim for vulnerable women and girls, including GBV survivors and persons with disabilities, to have access to timely, high-quality, safe and confidential GBV services for displaced and returnee women and girls who have experienced any impacts. to violence or those most vulnerable to it (field surveys and reports) in the city of Khan Shaykhoun and its countryside, and the city of Maarat al-Numan and its countryside, where 1,000 women and girls will be targeted equally between the areas of Khan Shaykhoun and Maarat areas through two fixed community centers and a mobile team (if necessary) through:
• Support the provision of CMR and specialized GBV case management in project sites.
• Mobilizing PSS actors to provide individual and group counseling services.
• Support the purchase and distribution of personal hygiene and dignity kits for vulnerable women and girls.

3 - Needs

Syria remains a complex humanitarian and protection emergency characterized by more than 10 years of ongoing hostilities and its long-term consequences including widespread destruction of civilian infrastructure, economic collapse, explosive ordnance contamination, and the largest number of internally displaced persons in the world.Recently, accelerated economic decline and COVID-19 have become additional major drivers of needs, exacerbating vulnerabilities.Access to basic services, including water, electricity and health care, is becoming increasingly limited, as well as widespread destruction of civilian and agricultural infrastructure, including homes, schools, health facilities, water supply and irrigation systems, reducing the resilience of the population.The levels of protection decreased, and consequently, cases of violence exacerbated, and cases of early and forced marriage increased, in addition to the registration of new cases of gender-based violence.Our primary project objective is to enable vulnerable women and girls, including survivors of gender-based violence and persons with disabilities, to access high-quality, safe, timely and confidential GBV services for women and girls who are internally displaced and returnees to Khan Shaykhoun city and its countryside. And the city of Maarat al-Numan and its countryside.

4 - Implementation of activities

The project's activities and services will target women and girls who are survivors or most vulnerable to gender-based violence through: • Support the provision of CMR and specialized case management using the health facilities and GBV Comprehensive Centers in the project sites.• Mobilizing PSS actors to provide individual and group counseling services.• Support the purchase and distribution of personal hygiene and dignity kits for vulnerable women and girls. - All interventions are monitored and evaluated by a specialized team that prepares periodic monthly reports for the project management and the Commission, through which it measures quantitative and qualitative output indicators such as (measuring the number of cases of exposure to violence). The work team is keen to be careful in dealing with females to ensure their dignity and respect, by paying attention to the following points:Patience: Recipients often feel stressed and frustrated.Attention and listening: to the observations and complaints submitted by women and girls.- Use positive language: when dealing with survivors and focus on humanitarian goals, taking care not to discriminate on the basis of social classes, and respecting privacy.
