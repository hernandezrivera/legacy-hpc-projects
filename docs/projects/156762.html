Project Summary

RI will focus this project on providing short-term emergency livelihood opportunities to protect 400 vulnerable households through community paid work selected from the IDPs, host communities and returnees in Kesra district of Deir ez Zor governorate of Syria. Given the level of need of the host community and the increasing social tensions, the project will target at least 40% of beneficiaries from the host community based on the belief that strong local communities are in a better position to provide a safe and human environment for vulnerable displaced populations. The proposed activities will help the host population absorb the shocks of the IDP influx and at the same time will promote self-reliance, resilience, and empowerment amongst the IDP community while contributing to the local economy. Given the particular vulnerabilities of women within the Syria context, this project will seek to prioritize and protect vulnerable women such as widows, divorces, GBV survivors, and women headed households as much as possible. RI will also target at least 30% women and young adults for participation in CFW activities. This will be implemented through a selection of culturally appropriate activities whereby women are safe and comfortable to work and participate. RI will also strive to recruit female staff that would be responsible for recruiting beneficiaries and community mobilization activities.
Beneficiary selection will be done in coordination with other NGOs as well as local authorities. For the selection of the Cash for Work projects sites, RI will use the following criteria that all projects should meet:

•Be communal in nature, participatory and benefiting the targeted community
•Require low technological inputs, labor skills, and physical inputs;
•Can be completed in a short implementation timeframe;
•Adhere to the Do No Harm principles and avoid adverse impacts on the environment;
•Assets that will be used in the medium to long term and can be quickly established and maintained;
•Gender sensitivity with work that is safe, culturally appropriate and accessible for men and women.

Needs

Syrian Crisis, now in its eighth year has grown into an overwhelming complex humanitarian crisis due to its severity, scale and ever growing needs on ground. As north-east Syria emerges from military operations against the Islamic State, the region has seen a noted increase in self-organised returns. In the first half of 2018, close to 155,000 people were reported to have returned to their area of origin in Raqqa and Hassakeh governorates. According to Humanitarian Situation Overview in Syria (HSOS) reports an estimated 235,500 residents (pre-conflict population and IDPs), the highest reported population figure throughout assessed communities in NES. Most of the IDPs were internal movements within Deir-ez- Zor District as a likely result of the offensive against the ISIL in south-eastern Dier ez Zor. Hundreds of individuals are without jobs, or proper source of income due to the ongoing conflict and security situation. Newly displaced people, for example, are highly vulnerable, requiring emergency assistance as they seek safety for their families and struggle to find means for survival. All displaced families are depleting their savings and selling their assets. Host communities are becoming equally destitute, striving to share their assets with the IDPs and returnees. A recent study by REACH reports that an estimated 6.5 million Syrians are food insecure and an additional 2.5 million people are at risk of food insecurity. From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65%of their expenditure on food, 57.2 percent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65% of food insecure people are adopting negative coping strategies to survive. Many conflict affected communities in Kesra District, Deir ez Zor Governorate are turning to short-term negative coping strategies with long-term consequences such as selling existing assets or diversion of expenditures from health and basic services to meet daily food and shelter needs. Growing crops was the most commonly reported source of income across Deir-ez- Zor governorate Common agricultural activities were growing wheat, cotton and vegetables as well as rearing sheep, cattle and chickens. Farmers have been facing serious challenges in sustaining their activities due to the increase in prices and availability of inputs as well as irrigation issues which are found to be particularly common in these areas.

Implementation of activities

RI will use a participatory approach to mobilization and sensitization key stakeholders in Kesrah for targeting the most vulnerable individuals of the communities. RI will ensure a close follow-up by allocating a dedicated team which will include an expat project manager, project officer and project assistants who will be directly involved in the project implementation. Identified beneficiaries for the intervention will be verified and entered into a beneficiary database capturing all their household demographics. Selection of CFW sites will be done in consultation with local communities. Each beneficiary will be given protective clothing such as gloves, safety boots and work suites for safety before taking part in CFW activities. RI will also form CFW implementation committees in each identified site to supervise and monitor CFW activities on a daily basis. All beneficiaries will sign attendance registers before and after completion of CFW activities each day. Selected beneficiaries will work for 4hrs per day for a 20-day working cycle. Each beneficiary will be paid and the equivalent of USD200 at the end of the 20-day working cycle. The project will be monitored throughout implementation using RI Monitoring and Evaluation guidelines. Collected data will be disaggregated by gender and age. RI employs M&E Officers who monitor the project activities on a daily basis and conduct PDM in order to analyze the project impact and receive beneficiaries feedback. A beneficiary database will be created for this specific project. A compliant mechanism for beneficiaries will be put in place.

Protection Risk Analysis

Throughout the project cycle, RI will aim to ensure gender equality is sufficiently assessed and monitored. Gender considerations and protection components will be mainstreamed into all project assessments and evaluations, and data will be gender disaggregated to allow for effective measurement. Through the RI NESRI NES program compliant mechanism beneficiaries can register complaints for immediate follow up by an independent Accountability Officer. The Mechanism includes complaints boxes, email account and a telephone hotline. RI has fully functional offices in Kesra, Deir ez Zor where security personnel are monitoring the security situation on a daily basis in Kesra and surrounding areas. RI will minimize risks through regular risk analysis, adequate monitoring and good management. RI will coordinate with local authorities in project implementation. The implementation team will communicate closely with RI’s network of locally based partners and field staff to keep informed about security situation and foreseen risk trends. RI will use humanitarian and conflict data analysis to inform strategies by context. Due to the particular vulnerabilities of women within the Syria context, this project will seek a minimum of 30% women and youths participation in CFW activities. This will be implemented through the selection of culturally appropriate activities whereby women are safe and comfortable to work and participate. RI will also strive to recruit female staff who would be responsible for recruiting beneficiaries and community mobilization activities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Cash for work sites can be effectively identified by the community and require relatively low skilled labor for support. Given the current strain on public infrastructures and services in the targeted district, this is not foreseen as a major problem however will be mitigated early on through discussions with local stakeholders and through the signing of MoUs once the community sites for cash for work are identified. All CFW participants will be provided with safety materials such as gloves.RI also assumes the areas of operation will be maintained with relative level of safety allowing for regular access and monitoring of project activities. Should the level of access or safety change RI would quickly review alternative options in similar locations with the possibility of shifting activities in case the unlikely situation in which Kesra becomes less accessible. RI will coordinate with local authorities in project implementation. The implementation team will communicate closely with RI’s network of locally based partners and field staff to keep informed about security situation and foreseen risk trends.RI will use humanitarian and conflict data analysis to inform strategies by context.
