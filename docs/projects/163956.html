Project Summary

This project intend to improve on the shelter situation of 2000 households affected by the crisis in NW/SW regions by December 2020
Provide basic household items to 1300 households affected by the crisis in the NW/SW regions by December 2020.
The project will focus on more vulnerable HH among IDPs and host. Especially HH headed by Children, women or disable men.
Beneficiaries will be supported to build their HHs and sensitized on the good use of items received. In addition, they will benefit of specific sensitization around child protection, prevention of GBV and other cross-cutting issues like gender and inclusion.

Besoins / Needs

This project will be implemented in the North West and South West Regions of Cameroon. These regions have been in crisis for about 4 years. This crisis has led to high political instability and has negatively affected more than four million persons. There has been open hostilities especially between the security forces and the Non State Armed Groups (NSAGs) that has resulted in a lot of man slaughter on both sides. Also a lot of destruction has been done in the North West and South West Regions. Many homes have been burnt and many others destroyed and property looted. This has made so many people to flee from their communities for safety areas many in the major towns such as Bamenda and Buea or to the nearby bushes. Persistent armed clashes have resulted in increasing movement of people and displacement over the last three years. According to OCHA report of August 2019, 1.3 million people are in need due to the crisis in the North West and South West regions including 346,000 host families. A total of 530,000 people are internally displaced while 70% of IDPs interviewed in both regions are in need of assistance. Women lost their income generating activities and found themselves in difficult economic conditions. The arrival of IDPs has led to overcrowding in houses and a lack of sleeping materials. According to the MIRA (October 2019), some IDPs are reportedly sleeping in huts in farms and some others are living in other precarious housing situations. Referencing the latest OCHA sitrep, the Shelter and NFI general outlook for SW and NW regions is worrying, with a high percentage of the displaced population self-settled in the bushes in rural areas and suffering from strong shelter needs as well as protection challenges. The Shelter Cluster’s assessment report (December 2018) highlighted that almost all assessment informants were in need of shelter assistance, with 44% reporting complete destruction of shelters and 30% reporting partial destruction. Many of the displaced populations are in rural, non-village settings, without access to markets, living in very sub-standard, inadequate and makeshift shelters. Some of the displaced populations informed their houses has been partially and severely damaged, many of them completely burnt. According to the protection cluster strategy (August 2019), these incidents resulted in large scale displacement and burning down of private property by warring parties have led to the destruction of civil status documents, including birth and marriage certificates. Furthermore, hundreds of female IDPs have given birth in the bushes and forests and their children have not had the opportunity to be registered. This has challenges when IDPs come across checkpoints (which are increasing in number) and are asked to present their documents. Additionally, this has implications for children enrolled in school as they need to present ID cards/birth certificates prior to registering for official exams. Most of the IDPs are asked by the authorities to go back to their places of origin to get court judgments (for the birth certificates) and certificates of nationality (for the identification) in order to reconstitute these documents. The key areas of response identified in the Emergency Response Plan (ERP) remain valid and are Shelter and NFIs, Food security, Health, Protection, Water, Sanitation and Hygiene, including management of menstrual hygiene.
