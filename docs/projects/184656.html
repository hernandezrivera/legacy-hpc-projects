Project Summary

According to the 2022 HNO A total of 7.7 million Somali women, men and children are estimated to require humanitarian assistance in 2022. With 2.9 million people estimated to be internally displaced throughout the country, of these, 2.2 million require urgent humanitarian assistance. IDPs are chronically more food insecure and vulnerable than host communities. Somalia continues to grapple with multiple shocks that exacerbate existing acute food insecurity and acute malnutrition. The failure of the 2021 GU rains put the pastoral and the agro-pastoral livelihoods in one of the worst crisis. The COVID-19 pandemic and associated socioeconomic impacts also contributed to the food insecurity situation in Somalia. As a result, the FNSAU post GU assessment projected 3.5 million Somalis would be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) through at least December 2021, in the absence of humanitarian assistance. That includes 1.1 million internally displaced persons who will face moderate to large food consumption gaps through late 2021.
Lower juba is one of the worst affected region by the protracted drought. The ministry of Humanitarian and disaster management of Juballand estimated over 60% of the animals in Juballand have perished due to lack of water and pasture. With no alternative livelihood or coping strategy left, over 11 cases of suicide were also reported in Jubaland. Besides, Kismayo alone host an estimated 90,000 new and protracted IDP’s in 144 sites (CCCM Cluster), displaced by multifaceted conflicts, floods, drought and government evictions. This intervention thus targets IDPs, urban poor and poor agro-pastoral households that have been hit by the successive droughts, conﬂict, Desert locust, COVID-19 and displacements and are in dire need of immediate food assistance and livelihood support.
SFH will address the immediate household food needs in the target locations through direct unconditional cash transfer activity targeting 500 most vulnerable IDPs and pastoral dropout households including women and children headed households for a period of 5 months at the rate of 75 USD per beneficiary in Lower Juba. Besides, to build resilience and prevent sudden shocks, this intervention will contribute to household food security through income generating activities while restoring productive communal assets such as Communal water pan and canals. 400 unskilled, but most vulnerable, able-bodied men and women who will work for 80 dollars per months and 8 supervisors/Forman’s @120 for 3 months with benefit from the conditional cash grant.
The proposed action also targets to provide seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs and tools such as: - nutritionally dense Crop and Vegetable seeds to 500 riverine farmers in Gobweyn, Wirkoy, qamqam, yontoy and Sanguuni locations, this will reduce the dependency syndrome on food aid while contributing to increased agricultural production at the community level.
To protect key livelihoods assets of IDPs and to preserve future means of livelihoods another 500 vulnerable pastorals from pastoral communities within the district will be supported with livestock treatment drugs and fodder vouchers for their lactating animals to replenish their physical body and enhance their market value. In general, the following will be achieved through this response:
1. Provide unconditional cash grant to 500 most vulnerable households in Kismayo @75 dollar for 5 round;
2. Provide seasonally appropriate livelihood inputs (Crop and vegetable) to 500 riverine and small-scale poor farmers along river juba in Kismayo district.
3.Provide assorted agricultural hand tools to 500 poor farmers to enhance their farming and production ability.
4. 500 pastoral households will be provided with livestock treatment and fodder vouchers
5. 200HH supported through conditional cash transfer for restoration of communal assets
6. Conduct Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), DRR and Early warnings.

1. - Projects activities

The objective of the food security and livelihoods project is to improve the food security status of the targeted population through the protection of key productive assets. The project proposes to provide vulnerable households with unconditional cash grants in order to support them to purchase their food and non-food basic items from the local markets and productive assets (seeds and tools; and livestock treatment or folder). The intervention aims to provide unconditional cash grant to 500 most vulnerable households in Kismayo @75 dollar for 5 round. Households with malnourished child, elderly, pregnant and lactating women or sick member will be prioritized for the Unconditional grant. PDM will be conducted after 1st and 3rd round of cash transfers to monitor the usage of cash and assess the impact on the market.Monthly cash payments, meeting the Minimum Expenditure Basket as determined by FSL CLUSTER will be distributed to each targeted household in Lower juba. These cash grants will improve the food security of 500 vulnerable households and will be implemented at a critical point of the year—during the hunger gap. The cash grants will enable households to have a more nutritionally balanced food basket, by both increasing the quantity and quality of food. In addition, the cash voucher will provide households with the opportunity to purchase other household goods and assets. Post distribution monitoring of our previous cash grant activities conducted by SFH indicate that the cash grants are an effective means to quickly respond to households’ insufficient income and meet their basic food needs and 75% of the cash grant will be for food. SFH will apply standardized methodology for beneficiary selection, cash delivery, results monitoring framework and risk management.Cash grants are essential for the households who are most in need of immediate food security. However, rebuilding communal assets is also critical to deter further erosion of livelihoods and restore coping capacities to future shocks. SFH will support additional 1000 households with the protection of productive assets, including seeds, tools and livestock treatment. Households with livestock will receive treatment supplied for their animals and thus an estimated 4000 animals receive treatment: De-worming and exto-parasite treatment. These livestock services will mitigate the effects of desert locust and drought risks and prevent them from deteriorating into emergency phase. In line with the FSL strategy, this proposed intervention will give priority to households with DISABLE persons, Minority groups, women and child headed households. MEN, WOMEN, BOYS AND GIRLS are all given appropriate priority. Rehabilitation of communal water pans and primary canal through CFW will also be considered to restore productive communal assets. Households in riverine and agro-pastoralist communities will receive seeds and tools; Types and varieties of seeds and tools to be distributed will be determined by the benefiting communities. When possible, agriculture and livestock inputs will be procured locally and distributed via a voucher system; if availability of quality inputs is insufficient, inputs will be imported and distributed in kind. All beneficiaries will receive a technical training in improved livestock and/or agriculture techniques and practices. SFH will Conduct 4 sessions of 25 participants each (Cognizant of COVID-19) on Good Agricultural practices (GAP) for the 500 livelihood input beneficiaries to enhance their capacity of good farming techniques and to improve food production. Due to the lack of established formal financial institutions in Somalia, SFH has established a contract with a “money vendor” (Hormud). The proposed cash grants will be distributed through this local financial institution.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

Market analysis is an essential component of situation analysis, it informs responses and delivery modalities. It is also a critical element of contingency planning and preparedness. To successfully enroll this program SFH will monitor market situation and conduct market based participatory assessment whose objectives are: To limit the risk of interventions causing a negative effect on local markets; To increase the effectiveness and efficiency of programme responses by promoting dignity and boosting the local economy; and To strengthen interventions by identifying new avenues to support existing market systems and structures. The selection of critical markets is driven by the identification of household needs and vulnerabilitiesUsing standardized tools to collect and analyzed key market information, appropriate stakeholders will be involved: Crisis affected populations that includes: Access (physical, financial and social) to markets, including potential barriers pre-and post-crisis. Distance from markets (this will provide a geographical limit to the area of analysis) pre- and post-crisis. Seasonal differences. Prices of key commodities pre- and post-crisis. Quality of the key commodities available in local markets pre- and post- crisis. Pre- and post-crisis cash transfer mechanisms (e.g. banks, phones). Labor wages pre- and post-crisis and Sources of livelihood pre- and post-crisisTraders putting into consideration: Volume of trade pre- and post-crisis. Seasonal trade variation, ideally over the last five years. Availability of commodities pre- and post-crisis. Supply chains and the effect or potential effect of the crisis. Prices of key commodities (both when bought and when sold). Power relations between market actors and potential trader cartels. Existence of trader groups (positive/negative). Social, ethnic and political issues among traders. Market storage capacity and access to credit and other financial services.Financial institutions and service providers: Functioning of money transfer systems. Capacity for increasing the use of such systems. Current client base, volume of business and Geographical coverage.Producers: Ability to provide products and increase production if needed. Prices. Seasonality and access to buyer markets. Additionally, appropriate indicators will be used to measure the market functionality that will inform the design of the intervention.Cash grant and voucher: Price of the commodities used in deciding the grant/value voucher amount.Cash for Work/Food for Work: Rate of beneficiaries leaving lower paying but more sustainable work for inclusion in the CFW/FFW. Availability of workers for daily labor employers and the daily rate of unskilled labor.

4.6 - Mitigation Measures

4.1 - Minority clans/ethnic groups are not included in consultations and/or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance. Mitigation measure:- a) AAP committee member and local authorities including IDPs existing governance structures will be involved in the sensitization, registration and distribution process this will strengthened feedback mechanisms and ensure transparency. b) Prior sensitization on the targeting criteria, individual entitlements, distribution day and date will enhance transparency and accountability. c) Gender inclusive and Needs-based targeting regardless of displacement status will be prioritized. d) SFH will establish accessible and functional response and feedback mechanism include real-time free hot lines e) Establish information desks at the distribution sites to receive timely complaints or compliments from the beneficiaries f) Prior sensitization of the target beneficiaries on how the feedback and response mechanism work. g) Targeting will be guided by needs, access and vulnerability and the vulnerable groups include women headed household, child headed households. h) Inclusive representation of the committees will be prioritized to ensure persons with disability or special needs are represented and their data made available. i) SFH will make special arrangements for persons who have difficulty accessing the distribution point or unable to carry the kits. they will be assisted and their safe arrivals ensured. 4.4 - Risk of exposure of violence as a result of distance to and location of humanitarian assistance/services or indiscriminate attacks from armed actors. Mitigation measures:- a) SFH will Identify central locations to ensure beneficiaries do not traverse long distances or pass through conflict zones to access distribution points. b) Distributions should take place in daylight hours giving beneficiaries sufficient time to travel home before nightfall. c) KIT will be distributed in an appropriate sacks/bag that can be carried to allow beneficiaries to be able to carry home with no additional physical or financial burden and without being dependent on others to carry it for them
