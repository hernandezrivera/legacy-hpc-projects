4 - Needs

The crisis in North East Syria (NES) is characterized by severe protection, economic, livelihoods and food security (FS) concerns, with around 2.2 million people (out of a total population of 3.1 million people in NES) in need of humanitarian assistance, of which around 2 million are categorized as food insecure. According to MSNA 2020, a total of 4.5 million people were reported to be in need of food assistance in ten governorates across NES and NWS, with Aleppo, Idleb and Al-Hassakeh reporting the highest number of people in moderate and acute need. These three governorates host the highest number of Internally Displaced Persons, with up to a third of the current total population being IDPs. The primary constraint in accessing food across Syria is economic as opposed to availability of food. With decreased income generating opportunities and increasing prices, households are experiencing increasing difficulties in meeting basic food requirements (Cash for Food will provide a short-term solution to this). Negative coping strategies identified include selling assets, taking high-risk/illegal jobs, and child labour. Rural areas reported a higher frequency of using wild food, hunting, harvesting immature crops or collecting food from wild plants compared with urban areas (MSNA 2020). According to DCAs assessments, 52% of assessed households in Menbij and Kobane reported borrowing money from relatives, friends and neighbours, 39% reported begging and relying on charity or donations. According to the HNAP, in Ar- Raqqa, 83% of the assessed households reported borrowing or buying on credit, 37% reported utilizing their savings, 26% reported relying on remittances, and 25% reported selling their goods, in order to buy food.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will: *Provide vulnerable HHs with regular food assistance in the form of Cash for Food grants. In coordination with the FSL WG, DCA will apply the targeting criteria for FS partners in NES, to which DCA has contributed. This looks at socioeconomic status, family size, family head, family status (IDP, host or returnee) and FS indicators. First, beneficiaries are identified by local committees, community leaders/ Kumins who will support the development of a tentative list of HHs to be assessed. As mentioned, HHs will be able to request their HH be assessed; later, DCA will conduct a follow-up assessment of all HHs within target areas. Beneficiary HHs will receive the monthly cash for food grants for a total of 8 months before they are reassessed again. Should vulnerable HHs still fit within the vulnerability criteria after 8 months of receiving grants, they may qualify for another cycle of assistance. The value of the monthly grants will be agreed with the FS WG and aim to provide 75% coverage of the previous month's SMEB food component and will be calculated per number of HH members. PDM will be conducted regularly throughout the project. In order to support income generating activities specifically targeting vulnerable people, the project will: *Provide Cash for Work opportunities. Assessments at the start of the project will include FGDs to ensure the facilitated schemes are gender and culturally appropriate, as well as coordination with HAO. 120 HHs will be engaged in Cash for Work (CfW) activities with a two-fold objective of meeting immediate needs of labour capable HHs and the rehabilitation of communal assets. DCA will conduct PDMs regularly. All beneficiaries will have access to DCAs CFM.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: Increased risk of inter-community tension due to varied assistance packages across members. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High Mitigation measures: Agree to minimum assistance package across sector. Assess and validate any changes before they occur. Protection Risk: Increased risk of tension within households due to deprivation from cash assistance. Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium Mitigation measures: DCA will continuously sensitize beneficiaries about the assistance package and the rights of everyone in the household in regard to the cash assistance and emphasize that the cash is mainly to cover basic needs. PDM survey will include questions on this to measure the impact of household level relationships. Protection Risk: Tensions due to jealousies between IDPs and Host communities Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Ensure that distribution is conducted based on needs and not based on status. Selection criteria are transparent, and groups are fully informed. Teams engage with the communities to sensitize the population to beneficiary selection criteria.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

Protection Risk: Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members or partner staff members. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigation measures: Project staff will communicate regularly and clearly with the community about what services are available and who services are for. Regular training will be provided to project staff on the Code of Conduct, Child Safeguarding and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA). A complaints and feedback mechanism will be established to allow staff, partners, or community members to raise complaints confidentially. These will be addressed in a timely and appropriate manner by project or management staff depending on the nature of the complaint.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk: Safety and security risks in project locations impact project implementation, causing delays and changes in operational areas, and also threaten the safety and security of project staff and beneficiaries. Mitigation: DCA has a designated security focal point who actively monitors the security situation in project locations, sets up security SOPs and procedures, and conducts regular security-related training and briefings for staff. Project management remains flexible and changes project locations and modalities as and when required. Hibernation, relocation and evacuation plans are in place. First aid kits are present in all project locations. Duty of Care policy and procedures are in place for national staff. The project may shift temporarily to a remote management modality, should this be necessary. Risk: Local authority-imposed COVID-19 curfews in NES prevent project staff from moving and/or working, and/or local authority-imposed curfews in NES/KRI prevent hawala agents from transferring money for distribution and salaries.Assumption: Project locations will remain accessible and DCA will continue to have permission to operate (through modified activities to protect beneficiaries and staff), and Hawalas will be able to continue transferring money. Mitigation measures: DCA staff will follow local authority-imposed curfews and restrictions related to COVID-19. DCA will regularly communicate with hawala agents to ensure supply chains are operational. DCA will communicate with other NGOs and NES Forum should selected hawala agent not be able to transfer funds.
