Project Summary

Conflict and displacement continue to affect North East Syria (NES), while needs are heightened due to the COVID-19outbreak, impacting healthcare service provision, especially in underserved areas and IDPs/refugees camps in Hassakeh,Deir-ez Zor, Raqqa and Aleppo Gov.Through this Action, in continuation with previous ECHO-funded phases (Jun.’17-Apr.'22), UPP and health and protection local IPs , aim to ameliorate living conditions and save lives of war-affected persons in NES by improving access to emergency&primaryhealth care, life-saving obstetric and reproductive health (RH) services, COVID-19 preparedness&response, and to protection and prevention services. Continued support will be provided toPHCCs in Raqqa city and Areesha, Al Hol, Abu Khashab, Mahmoudli camps, in addition to new support to Serekanyecamp PHCC; to essential RH services at secondary care level in Maternity Deps. in Tabqa and Manbij and in RaqqaMaternity and Pediatric Hospital, and to the COVID-19 Treatment Facility in Manbij. Continued support will also beensured to the Emergency Management and Coordination Center (EMCC) for both emergency and COVID-19 referrals.The protection intervention (described and budgeted in module entry HSYR22-HEA-188482-1) will ensure continuity of critical CP and GBV life-saving services for women, girls (WGs), and boys, as well as caregivers, in Safe spaces in Raqqa. Self-protection capacities of pop. will be strengthened throughprotection mainstreaming, prevention outreach/campaigns, and capacity building of local actors. UPP and IPs expects to reach at least 165.786 beneficiaries (IDPs, returnees, refugees, local pop., TCN, other personson the move) with health and protection services over 13 months. Through a flexible structure and a wide geographiccoverage, the Action provides the capacity to quickly assess and respond to emerging health and protection needs indifferent locations in NES.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project aims at providing safe and equal access to life-saving health assistance, CPHC including community health, RH at primary and secondary level, referral system, and specialised care for COVID-19. Identified groups include: IDPs, refugees, HCs, girls&women of reproductive age, children&newborns, persons with complex medical conditions, PwDs. Targeted locations were selected based on needs analysis identifying health needs in IDPs camps and settlements, under-served urban and remote areas. Ben. will have free access to the services, designed according to specific needs of the targeted groups incl. gender&age considerations: *CPHC and HP in CPHCCs in Al Hol and Areesha camps, and in Raqqa city; *BCPHC and HP in BPHCCs in Mahmoudli, Serekaniye and Abu Khashab camps; *Basic RH care through MDUs in HTR areas in DeZ and Tabqa *Life-saving obstetrics and comprehensive RH in MDs in Tabqa and Manbij and in RMPH (Raqqa) *Life-saving specialised treatment for COVID-19 cases in Manbij CTF; *Ambulances’ referral and COVID-19 Hotlines through the EMCC. Resilience of war-affected populations will be enhanced through capacity building, monitoring and technical advice. Support to C-19 response measures in HFs in line with the strategic plan by WHO and NES-Forum will be provided. Protection will be mainstreamed to ensure assistance is provided in a safe, dignified, accountable&participatory manner, incl 1-1/group prev. protection outreach and community campaigns, referrals and FCRM.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: PoC are exposed to higher level of community/HH tension due to messages provided during outreach and prevention activities. Mitigation: participatory approach to identify key messages tailored to each location. Contingency planning. Evaluation of messages. Mitigation: Train staff on Code of Conduct and Child Safeguarding Policy. FCRM in place. PSEA awareness raising. Risk: Persons with reduced mobility are excluded from the service due to reduced access. Mitigation: Adaptation of HFs infrastructure to ensure equal access. Household visits to spread messages. ProM assessments.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Protection will be mainstreamed into all delivered services to ensure assistance is provided in a safe, dignified, accountable&participatory manner; to increase access to protection services, and to ensure local staff’s understanding of integrity/protection principles, PSEA, protection threats and needs. UPP is co-Chair of the NES PSEA Network and will streamline PSEA efforts to prevent the risk of SEA. Capacity building of UPP and partners staff on ProM, Protection core concepts, PSEA/Safeguarding. The project ensures PSEA & Child Safeguarding with training, sensitization & PSEA mainstreaming in FCRM, in coord. with the UPP co-chaired NES PSEA Network. The FCRM ensures any feedback or complaint can be raised and addressed, each PSEA complaint being addressed per UPP guidelines.


11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risks of further displacement affect NES due to protracted tensions along the Peace Spring Op.(PSO). In Al Hol camp, risks for NGOs and ben. accessing NGOs services increased in 2021 due to deteriorating security conditions and targeted attacks, and UPP security posture in PHCCs was heightened. Overall, scarce and unmonitored HW disposal areas in NES exacerbates environmental risks linked to the provision of health care. Protraction of crisis and depletion of socio-economic conditions are likely to worsen protection needs esp. for women and FHHs, boys and girls, and exacerbate neg. coping mechanisms- whilst the foreseen humanitarian aid funding cuts risk discontinuation or closure of life-saving services. UPP has designed a detailed country and project Risks analysis, scenario, and contingency plan identifying each risk (economic, geopolitical, political, environmental, programmatical, financial) that the project can encounter and defined mitigation measures, contingency planning, and scenarios to answer those risks. The contingency plan is to be updated and revisited throughout the project.
