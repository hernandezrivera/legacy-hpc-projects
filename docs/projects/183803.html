Project Summary

Medair aims to provide multi-sectoral response to crisis affected communities in Blue Nile State, including health, nutrition, and WASH services. The WASH response plan includes support to health facilities to ensure provision of safe, reliable water supply; adequate sanitation facilities for patients, caregivers and healthcare workers; appropriate waste management systems; and facility and community based hygiene promotion.

1 - Needs

Targeted localities and health facilities within Blue Nile State lack basic WASH services, thereby increasing risk to patient, caregivers and healthcare workers in disease transmission. Lack of water supply is often cited as top priority need in order to be able to implement appropriate infection prevention and control (IPC) measures to reduce disease transmission and provide a sanitary service environment. In addition, few health facilities have appropriate waste management facilities to properly store and dispose of biological, sharps and general waste. These materials either accumulate at the facility or are stored/disposed of unsafely introducing pathogens and hazards to the surrounding area. Community WASH needs (particularly access to safe water) are also commonly present. Alongside poor household hygiene behaviours, the lack of WASH services at the community level is a significant contributing factor to morbidity and mortality. The 2020 MSNA REACH Data indicates that 57%, 49% and 32% of the locality populations (Geisan, Bau and El Kurmuk, respectively) lack access to safe water. Blue Nile State is also vulnerable to flood and drought cyclic events. Floods often damage existing infrastructure and increase risks of water-related disease transmission including AWD, cholera, Hepatitis E and malarial breeding grounds. In addition, droughts often lead to reduced water quantity available so that people may rely on less safe water sources. The lack of water also directly impacts hygiene behaviours such as handwashing, reducing the critical disease preventative benefits of this behaviour. Recurrent disease outbreaks such as cholera exacerbate the ongoing risks of COVID-19 that continues to have a heavy impact on morbidity and mortality. As many households have experienced multiple shocks which reduces their economic means, access to soap, safe water handling containers and/or water treatment supplies may be affected and increase risks during disease outbreaks.

2 - Activities/Outputs

The project plans to implement the following: • Rehabilitation and/or construction of water supply systems for targeted health facilities. These may include well rehabilitation or drilling, manual pump upgrade to solar-powered mechanical, water storage tank(s) depending on demand and well capacity, water distribution network to multiple access points within the health facilities; • Water treatment system (in-line chlorination when feasible) including staff training and establishment of FRC monitoring routine; • Repair of community water supply where appropriate to increase access to safe water for population and address preventative WASH services; • Construction/rehabilitation of clinical waste management systems for targeted health facilities, including placenta pits, garbage pits, ash pits, sharp pits and incinerators; • Staff training on waste segregation and establishment of monitoring and maintenance plans; • Construction/rehabilitation of health facility latrines to ensure gender-segregated, accessible facilities accommodating mobility challenges related to patients and/or elderly caregivers; • Installation of handwashing stations with soap and water supply at latrines and key locations throughout health facilities; • Hygiene behaviour change communication (BCC) at the household level using peer-support groups to encourage sustained uptake of improved hygiene behaviours, particularly handwashing, safe water handling, safe sanitation and personal hygiene; • Integration of health facility based messaging with community hygiene BCC; • Targeted distribution of WASH NFI in response to disease outbreak (e.g. AWD/cholera) to support critical preventative hygiene behaviours; NFI to be confirmed based on specifics of outbreak but likely to include soap, handwashing basin, water collection and storage containers and water treatment chemicals (if necessary).

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

All WASH infrastructure will evaluate potential environmental risks before construction. Medair will implement the NEAT+ Humanitarian environmental assessment tool to identify potential risks and consider mitigation options. New water points will be assessed for potential impact on water availability based on hydrogeologic conditions, nearby users and aquifer productivity. Wells will be deepened if necessary to account for future fluctuations in water table related to drought conditions. WASH infrastructure construction will not specifically rely on collection of local materials in order to avoid contributing to deforestation; the scale of infrastructure construction is also anticipated to be low relative to natural resources in the area. Infrastructure will be designed to ensure adequate protection from flood risks where present.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Medair seeks to ensure safe and equitable access to WASH services. Particular attention is given to women and children, as well as vulnerable groups such as the elderly and disabled, in the design and siting of infrastructure and in ensuring that they have equal opportunity to provide feedback and participate in the management of community facilities. Specific examples include: evaluating potential water points to ensure safe access during the day and night with consideration for potential seasonal access constraints; community-wide engagement on site selection to allow for input of women and other vulnerable groups such as the elderly or physically disabled; encouraging the involvement of women in management, such as through Water User Committees; establishing BCC groups to promote hygiene behavior change through women and provide a safe space to discuss challenges they face; conducting qualitative research such as focus group discussions to understand barriers and challenges in behavior change. All staff members are trained on gender sensitive service delivery and share information on reporting mechanisms for beneficiary feedback. Efforts are made to ensure beneficiary feedback is available in gender and culturally appropriate methods, particularly to encourage women in the communities to feel comfortable providing their input.
