Project Summary

The Republic of South Sudan is a landlocked country in East-Central Africa. Since its independence, the country has been rocked by fragility, economic stagnation, recurrent natural disasters, and political instability. The World Bank estimates that 80 percent of South Sudan’s estimated 12.4 million population lives on 1 dollar a day and 5.9 million people need humanitarian assistance. South Sudan Humanitarian Response Plan 2024 estimates that at least 9 million people, including returnees and refugees in South Sudan, are expected to experience critical needs in 2024. Six (6) million of these people were targeted with some form of humanitarian support, depending on the resources available. According to the IPC Acute Food Insecurity and Malnutrition Analysis September 2024 - July 2025 published on 18th November, 2024, an estimated 6.3 million people (47 percent of the population analyzed) are classified in IPC Phase 3 or above (Crisis or worse). Of this total, 1.71 million people are facing critical levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) – and a further 41,000 people are facing catastrophic levels of acute food insecurity – classified as IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe). The overall objective of this action will be to provide enhanced resilience through provision of emergency livelihood support to the vulnerable communities (Host, IDPs and returnees) of Nasir Country in Upper Nile State. The project will provide livelihoods support to households facing severe acute food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 or worse) to safeguard their livelihoods, with a particular focus on households engaged in agriculture to maintain or resume their food production. This project will also strengthen resilience to and adaptive capacity for climate change by supporting livelihood diversification and climate change mitigation practices. Through food production promoting interventions the action will target 42,000 individuals (7980 men, 9660 women, 11760 boys and 12600 girls) with assorted agricultural and fishing inputs then further support them through customized training on good agricultural and aquaculture practices and climate resilience. From the total targeted beneficiaries, 2,100 are persons living with disabilities will be supported. The action will not only focus on services provision but also focuses on integrated and multi sector interventions to contribute to the reduction of malnutrition. The target beneficiaries’ number and selection criteria will be discussed in meetings with the committees prior to beneficiary identification and registration. Part of the vulnerability criteria will focus on the women headed households, Elderly, Child headed and households with high number of children under 5 years but of households who have access to land and fishing grounds in which they can make use of their kits. Farmers will receive basic trainings on improved agronomic practices with regards to micro-irrigation of small-scale commercial vegetable production. Cross-cutting issues such as gender and GBV, nutrition, HIV/AIDS, group dynamics and management will also be a key component of each training. The Project will be implemented by VSF Germany under its Food Security and Livelihood Program and build on the results of the previous and on-going Livelihoods Projects in Upper Nile State. Within the Greater Upper Nile State, VSF-G has extensive experience in implementation of food security interventions related to Agriculture production including direct distribution of livelihood kits, dry season vegetable and fishing production, animal health and general value chain addition leveraging on the strong and long-standing good working relationships with the local development partners, CBOs, local and State. VSF Germany will engage volunteers from local community to communicate with the host community and explain why the IDPs/returnees are the primary beneficiaries for these commodities and the selection criteria used.

1. Needs

Recent data indicates that up to November 2024, a total of 182,000 (58%) people in Luakpiny/Nasir’s county experienced crisis IPC3 food insecurity situation or worse. It is projected that in the period leading to July 2025 this figure will rise to 212,000 (70%), 76,000 of them (25%) drifting into emergency phase. The county is home to extremely vulnerable groups, including women, children, the elderly, displaced people, returnees and persons with disability. These groups face heightened risks of malnutrition, morbidity due physical and cultural barriers. It is estimated that up to 40% of Nasir’s population falls into this category.The county heavily relies on livestock and fishing as primary livelihoods. These sectors are crucial for food security, income generation, and social cohesion. However, challenges like climate change, conflict, disease outbreaks, and market access hinder their potential. Livestock rearing, particularly cattle, goats, and sheep, faces threats from droughts, floods, diseases, and insecurity. Similarly, the fishing sector, primarily along the Sobat River, is impacted by overfishing, pollution, and climate change. To address these issues, interventions are needed to improve livestock health, increased crop production, enhance pasture and water management, support market access, promote sustainable fishing practices, and adapt to climate change. By addressing these challenges, Nasir County can strengthen its food security, improve livelihoods, and build resilience to future shocks.The high levels of food insecurity anticipated to be driven by the depletion of cereal stocks by majority of the households; a rise in staple food prices due to low local supply to markets and inaccessibility to markets during rainy season; limited income-earning opportunities; the persistent macro-economic challenges in the country. The deterioration in food security is compounded by the impact of the ongoing crisis in Sudan and its effects on supplies to markets and influxes of returnees in Upper Nile from both Sudan and Ethiopia. Upper Nile state is considered the main entry for majority of returnees from Sudan and Ethiopia and this is likely to put more burden on the local communities’ resources. The deterioration in the food security situation is likely to be mitigated by the availability of some wild foods, fish, and increased access to livestock products such as milk because the animals will be near the homesteads.Nasir County needs significant capacity building and training to address its challenges. Prioritizing livestock, fisheries, and agriculture development, coupled with training in veterinary services, disease control, and modern farming techniques, can enhance productivity. Additionally, capacity building in disaster risk reduction, climate change adaptation, and conflict resolution is crucial. To improve livelihoods, establishing market facilities, promoting value addition to agricultural products, and implementing anticipatory action through early warning systems, contingency planning, and strategic stockpiling can help mitigate the impact of shocks and stressesSporadic displacement of people due to floods and insecurity has contributed to inconsistency in agro-production. There is need to equip the communities with transformative agricultural practices to withstand climate change. Training of cultivators will include climate smart agricultural practices to strengthen their resilience.There are eight (8) Payams, namely: Dingkar, Jikmir, Kiech Kuon, Kuerenge-Ke, Mading, Maker, Nasir / Luakpiny and Roam inside Nasir county with a total of 36 Bomas. According to South Sudan Census of 2008, Nassir had an estimated population of 201,000. Intervention will target all gender with special attention to female, most vulnerable, internally displaces, returnees, people with disabilities (PWDs) and host communities. VSFG proposes to cover caseload as outlined by the Project call.

2. Project activities

1. Consultation with authorities and sensitization of communities at Payam level: VSFG intends to sensitize and mobilize the communities to support and appreciate the project. This shall be done from the State level, County Level and Boma levels through dialogue and meetings. 2. Formation and Training of Accountability to Affected Population Committee (AAPC): As characteristic with most Humanitarian Projects, VSFG shall endeavor to ensure that AAP Committees exist and work effectively with the implementing Team. VSFG is aware that in some cases, the AAP Committees may have been established through other interventions while in other cases, they may not be active or present. 3. Beneficiary Identification and registration: VSFG shall target the most vulnerable within the identified Payams focus on credible selection approaches while doing no harm to individuals. Registration process shall therefore follow after narrowing on the most vulnerable within the agreed locations. 4. Collection and transportation of livelihood kits from the agreed pipeline collection point. VSF-Germany shall collect inputs as per the Release Order(s) from pre-agreed pipeline agency (FAO) warehouse and transport them to a temporary distribution storage in Mandeng for onward distribution to payams. Truck(s)/ Boats will be hired to further preposition the kits at payam collection points based on the distribution schedule. 5. Distribution of Emergency Livelihood inputs: In line with pre-agreed distribution guidelines, VSF-G shall work with the AAP committees to distribute kits (assorted crops, vegetables seeds and fishing kits) directly to targeted households 6. Post-distribution monitoring (PDM): VSF-G shall conduct a rapid post-distribution monitoring (PDM) immediately after the distribution exercise. PDM will gather rapid feedback from beneficiaries regarding timeliness of distribution, quantity and quality of inputs provided, fairness of the process etc. 7. Fish Production Training: Selected fisher-folk will undergo training at payam level in their established groups. The training will be practical and the curricula will be customized to involve making and mending fishnets where necessary, protection of young fish in the river, hygiene measures in handling the catch and sanitation. 8. Fish Post-Harvest handling & preservation Training: Fish post-harvest and preservation training sessions will be conducted in payams. The fisher folks will be taken through hygienic preservation methods: smoking, salting, drying as well as proven techniques for moisture-minimizing bulk storage. 9. Establishment of vegetable Demonstration Gardens : VSF-G will establish demonstration gardens in the targeted Payams. Before the distribution of livelihood kits, VSF-G will engage with community leaders and legitimate land owners to obtain land for establishing the units. The demo farms will be located near Sobat River to showcase the advantage of micro-irrigation and other elements of good agricultural practices. 10. Vegetable Production Training: Selected beneficiaries will be trained in the demo farms established. Member selection criteria will ensure participation of vulnerable groups (widows, youth, PLWD and the elderly). Training will be practical and participatory using community own resource persons., 11. Vegetable Post-Harvest & Preservation Training: Selected beneficiaries will participate in training sessions facilitated by VSFG which will focus on post-harvest and preservation techniques using mainly sun drying techniques. 12. Monitoring and evaluation shall form part of the Project implementation for all activities. VSFG has an establish a monitoring and evaluation (M&E) framework and tools which shall be applied to ensure the Project meet the pre-set goals 13. Vaccination and treatment of 45,000 Animals through CAHWs14. Production of Visibility Materials
