Project Summary

The nutrition project proposal aims to providing treatment for 2400 severely and 3800 moderately acute malnutrition (boys, girls) and 1016 MAM PLWs through OTP and provision TSFP supplies, the project will contributed to sector outcome 2:Displaced populations, returnees and host communities meet their basic needs and/or access basic services while increasing their self-reliance.
The treatment of SAM & MAM is based on CMAM approach, comprising of treatment of SAM and MAM in 11 OTPs with participation of the community and 110 outreach Volunteer who will conduct continuous awareness rising sessions on CMAM and IYCF messages and active case finding for defaulters and new SAM and MAM cases.
In the treatment phase, it is expected Outpatient Therapeutic program (OTP) for the treatment of 2400 children 6-59 months with Severely Acute Malnutrition(SAM)without medical complications in coordination with UNICEF provide treatment supplies that may include Ready-to-Use Therapeutic food, deworming drugs, and facility based vaccination, while provision of cash voucher for treatment and transportation of Severely Acute Malnutrition with medical complication to be referred by the medical doctor to Stabilization Center (SC) inpatient medical care.

Secondly; Targeted Supplementary Feeding Program (TSFP) for the treatment of 3800 children 6-59 months and 1016 PLWs with Moderately Acute Malnutrition (MAM) in coordination with WFP to provide supplies Ready-to-Use Supplementary food.
ASSIST will conduct facility based and Periodic MUAC screening of children 6-59 months and PLWs every 3 months using MUAC strip based on the following admission criterial:
• OTP: Weight-for-height ≤–3 Z-score, mid-upper-arm circumference <115 mm,
• TSFP: MUAC ≥ 115 and < 125 mm, No Odema, WHZ ≥-3 z-score and < -2 z-score weight for height, All children discharged from OTP. For PLWs 2nd or 3rd trimester of pregnancy and MUAC < 210 mm, Infant < 6 months old and MUAC < 210 mm.
IYCF activities will be undertaken through staff, community mobilization and Mother groups leaders, will focus on community support, including mother groups, community-based health promotion, implementation of ten Steps for mothers to successful breastfeeding and proper food for Mothers and their children. Printing of IEC materials to be disseminated during IYCF sessions.
Provision of NIP circles target families of all children discharged from OTP, for PLWs 2nd or 3rd trimester of pregnancy and MUAC < 210 mm, Infant < 6 months old and MUAC < 210 mm, to help them to address the problems of malnutrition by empowering mothers and fathers to share their knowledge and skills on how to improve the nutrition of family members using locally available resources, behavior change (improve awareness), improve the food / food security), education meal (improve dietary practices).
The observation in 11 TSFP centers in south kordufan and OTPs in Darfur was managed by ASSIST showed that the level of malnourished for U5 year’s in girls is higher than boys because traditionally, women and girl have less access to nutrition food source because the priorities to food is given to men and boy. In addition women and girl are responsible from child care, food preparation, housekeeping, bring water from long distance and major farming operation while men have strong decision making on productive resources, also parents prefer having boys than girls.
The project focused on do not Harm principle to avoid any harm. The accountability to effected population will carried out, gender will be mainstreamed and will ensure participation of all groups including disabilities and minorities. To ensure equal gender participation and access to all groups in the project, gender analysis has been carried out to identify the different needs of the women, girls, boys and men. ASSIST will insure the resilience and sustainability of the project through the community capacity building of the MoH staff, community outreach.

Needs

Based on the Mass MUAC screening in South Kordufan March-October/2017,VAM reports May-October - 2018 for Kereinki in West Darfur and Gaissan in Blue Nile and Abbassiya Inter-agency assessment February8/2018 findings indicates; Kadugli 14% GAM, 0.3% SAM, and 12% MAM, Abu-Jubaiha GAM 25%, SAM 3.2% and MAM 22% and Al Abbassiya 27291 U5 were screened, 95 children SAM, 31 Oedema and 1313 MAM. Based on the December 17/2018 SMART Survey conducted in Abugebaiha, Abbassiya in SK and January 10/2019 in Giassan in Blue Nile the results shows; Prevalence of GAM 16.3% and 17.2%, MAM 14.7% and 13.1%, SAM 3.2% and 4.2% and stunting 9.3% and 8.2% respectively, also indicates critical to medium access to routine vaccination, ANC for pregnant and folic acids, diarrhea infection 2 weeks before the survey, and increasing food insecurity due decline in the service delivery and remoteness of the villages. According to FEWSNET report June 2018-May 2019, the devaluation of local currency, high inflation with an average rate 68.9%, the scarcity & high prices of fuel have led to increase in prices of food by 150-200% in 2019 for agriculture and Animal livestock producers. The Limited purchasing power further expected to affect ability to access food from the market, reduced food availability & diet diversity could negatively affect the nutrition status of Children 6-59 months and PLWs. 65% have food stock enough only for 3 months, 25% have for 5 months, and 10% having for one year. 95% take two meals per day & will reduce to one/day as their harvest depleted. People displaced and returnees in recent months receive limited aid assistance.WFP monitoring market updates December 2018 indicate, the sorghum prices increased by 160 to 283%, goats' prices increased by 56 to 259% respectively compared to 5- years average. 20% of population have poor food consumption score due to depleted of food stock, rising food prices, poor purchasing power, limited livelihood options, low income level, 13% of vulnerable households are food insecure with average GAM rate of 10.5% in South Kordufan and 24.5% in Blue Nile with the situation expected to be worse in SPLM-N-controlled areas.Integrated food security phase classification for SK have 44% in Phase 1, 41% Phase 2: stressed, 13% in Phase 3 crisis: and 2% in phase 4 emergency and Blue Nile State shows 55% in Phase 1, 28% Phase 2: 10% Phase 3: and 7% in phase 4: and West Darfur 33% Phase 1, 41% Phase 2: 17% Phase 3: and 4% in phase 4.Accountability to affected population will be considered within project cycle with 30% women participation to voice grievances, rise complain and give feedback which will be part of the decision making during Monitoring and evaluation. Protection will be mainstreamed in WASH facilities in OTP centers, Mobile OTP/TSFP services delivered in remote prevent expose to violence and risks and lastly, Volunteer and staff training will include Family tracing and reunification, Referral and psychosocial support.

Impact on environment/natural resources

Environmental considerations will take into account during project cycle, will integrating environmental issues into nutrition program to create multiple benefits, through NIP circles ASSIST will support tree planting as an efficient way to combat desertification and can support communities with sustainable access to nutritional food, it can also provide an alternative source of income for a women’s community group as they can sell crops on local market. Also will support planting of fruit trees in TSFP and OTPs centers combine with reuse of grey water from hand-washing facilities for extra water needs to create a natural fencing around the centers.Include environmental messaging to beneficiaries, to raise awareness about personal hygiene, hygiene practice, combine with environmental activities such as clean-up campaign and environmental rehabilitation.Include fuel efficient cooking techniques, e.g. soaking beans in mother groups and NIPP circles training, and promote Fuel Efficient Stoves to reduce wood use for cooking. During NIP activities promote crops with high nutritional value and reduced cooking time.Implement a system for solid waste management in OTPs and TSFP Centers and training staff on it, also the staff will collect empty therapeutic meal bags to be dispose in safety way. Also can reduce the use of plastic bags, by replacing with for example textile bags in nutrition food distributions activities. Reduce wood consumption by using environmentally friendly material e.g zink in construction of waiting areas, replace the use of fired bricks with Stabilized Soil Blocks (SSB) or Cement Blocks (CSB) for construction and rehabilitation of OTPs and TSFP centers.
