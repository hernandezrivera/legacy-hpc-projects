Project Summary

Needs:
The severity of humanitarian needs has continued to increase, with an estimated 14.9M people in need of health assistance. The health sector severity scale revealed catastrophic need in areas like Aleppo, Ar-Raqqa and Rural Damascus in 2024. In 2023, these governorates faced outbreaks of AWDs, measles and meningitis. In Aleppo, Hama and Lattakia, health services have been severely impacted by the Feb 2023 earthquake. HFs have insufficient consumables and equipment. Lack of essential medicines and inadequate HWs were key barriers to health care access, particularly for NCDs, which account for 49.8% of mortality. Patients are facing reduced access due to sharp increases in medication prices. According to HNO 2024, 65% of HFs do not provide services to NCDs and mental health.
Intervention, Implementation of activities:
Medair integrates activities to ensure delivery of quality services and supports rehabilitation works, medical equipment and furniture for PHCs to restore functionality. Medair works closely with DoH and provides oversight to engineering firms to ensure that rehabilitation work complies with standards. HWs will receive training on NCDs, IPC, SGBV, RH & PFA. EWARS notifiable cases will be identified and reported. CHWs will share health messages and identify PLWMI for AD. Through CVA, Medair will support referral for obstetric cases, SAM with complications and acute events to nearest SHC services and provide a voucher for the vulnerable NCD patients to access medications at pre-identified pharmacies. Protection Risk Analysis:
Medair’s approach to protection mainstreaming and integration seeks to provide the most vulnerable clients with access to tailored services that adhere to the principle of Do No Harm. Medair conducts regular context analysis, monitoring, and communication with local authorities, and participation of affected populations.
GBV related risks: mitigation measures to reduce this include training HWs on how to deal with survivors of GBV, confidentiality, survivor-center approach, and referrals.
Service provision: low quality services might expose beneficiaries to further harm and deterioration of their health conditions. Mitigation measures include regular monitoring of service provision, capacity building, effective feedback and complaints mechanisms.
Protection monitoring allows Medair to track whether clients are able to access services, as well as their degree of satisfaction or any concerns associated with the services received.
PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation & Abuse) strategy:
Abuse and exploitation both towards staff and beneficiaries seeking services, either in exchange for services/assistance or not. All acts falling under the definition of SEA are prohibited and considered a gross violation of Medair Code of Conduct, which all Medair employees must sign and commit to adhere to. Medair makes available immediate and confidential mechanisms to report SEA for Medair staff members or any other person who may have been a victim of SEA or may have witnessed or otherwise been informed of a case of SEA involving Medair.
Risks, Assumptions & Mitigating measures:
Risks: the existence of many checkpoints may limit access to the project area. Medair will coordinate with local government. All required documentation will accompany shipments of goods along with staff who are familiar with the situation.
Risk: HWs may leave workstations due to deteriorating social and economic conditions. Medair considers covering their night shift compensation and transportation costs & will collaborate with DoH to have adequately trained staff.
Risks: Delays in procurement. Mitigation: a procurement plan will be completed at the start of the project to ensure sufficient lead time.
Risk: Planned activities are not approved by relevant Ministries or SARC or approved with but with significant delays or adjusted procedures. Maintain engagement with each Line Ministry to facilitate approvals.
