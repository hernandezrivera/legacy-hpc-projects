Project Summary

Through collaboration and coordination with key education stakeholders, the project will identify and address leading barriers to accessing education for children and communities affected by displacement; IDPs, returnees and vulnerable host communities to enable enrolment and retention.

1 - Needs

Influx of displaced people in some locations in the country has increased pressure of education system, facilities in those areas, affecting both the ability to access and provide quality education to children as school infrastructure is insufficient in the face of the increased demand caused by the influx. The current economic crisis has grossly affected peoples' livelihood sources, skyrocketing inflation amist unprecedented currency devaluation further crippling families' pottential to afford basic school requirements for their children. Schools struggle with insufficient staffing - many relying on non trained volunteer teachers from the communities to fill the staffing gaps; majority of teacher have not received capacity building in key areas like pedagogy, COVID-19 prevention in a long time. School infrastructure is damaged, overcrowded and lacks basic furtniture for teachers and students to use. Many schools do not have latrines, while those that have them they are in poor state, not gender disagregated and too few for the school population they are meant to serve.

2 - Activities/Outputs

Capacity Building: Key stakeholders in the school system such as teachers in the targeted schools will be trained in various relevant packages such as Teacher Preparedness and Training Package (TPTP), Teachers in Crisis Contexts (TiCC), Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA), Child Safeguarding, Psychosocial support (PSS) among others as will be guided by the Education Sector leadership and MoE. A keen focus will be put on increasing awareness about COVID-19 prevention and general child protection perspectives in education. PTAs will also be supported to develop and implement School Improvement Plans (SIPs) for their schools, to increase their active participation and ownership in the schools in their communitiesRehabilitation/ construction of school infrastructure: NRC engineers will jointly work with MoE technical teams to carry out technical assessment of school buildings (including WASH facilities) to ascertain their structural integrity, sufficiency and agree on the upgrades/works needed to either restore or ensure sufficiency. In some cases, additional classrooms or temporary learning spaces (TLSs) will be established to provide more classroom space, if deemed necessary. Gender segregated latrines will be a key focus in this activity to address the protection concerns (GBV) as well as provide the culturally required privacy for students per gender. Implementation of the works will be through the School Improvement Plan (SIP) modality led by the PTAs while MoE and NRC technical teams supervise for quality assurance and compliance to agreed upon standards. School supplies: School level assessments will be carried out to find out the student and teacher population of each targeted school, the specific school supplies needed for students, teachers and the school in general for better performance. The materials will include scholastic materials, school uniforms, furniture, hygiene and COVID-19 prevention materials (PPE), dignity kits for teenage girls (menstrual health management) as well as recreational materials. NRC will procure the materials and deliver them directly to the beneficiaries, with MoE’s participation.Teacher incentive payment: to address the issues of teacher scarcity, abnormally high teacher-student ratios in some schools with high populations, additional qualified teachers will be jointly selected with MoE from the community and engaged to support in teaching the additional classrooms established. A monthly incentive will be paid to these teachers to facilitate them in their work. The rate will be guided by the Education Sector approved standards for the same

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

Design of school infrastructure upgrades will include adaptations with climate conditions in the specific locations e.g high temperatures, flooding, etc. Tree planting in schools will be a key component of our work with PTAs, to provide both wind breakers and shade/cooling during the different seasons.

6 - Protection mainstreaming
6.1 - Does the project incorporate protection principles and promote meaningful access, safety and dignity for the beneficiaries?

4=Fully in place

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Child protection, child safeguaging, prevention of sexual exploitation ans abuse and COVID-19 prevention will be key components of the teacher training activities. Provision of gender segregated latrines, is meant to address the protection concerns that stem from sharing latrines for both male and female students and teachers in school. Having women/girls only consultation sessions ensures free expression and considering gender-related concerns and addressing them throught the project
