Project Summary

Cabaret is in livelihood zone HT05 according to the classification of the CNSA and characterized by important levels of plantain production. According to the latest IPC report, 26,433 individuals are at a crisis level 4 (extreme poverty with less than US $ 1.23 / day) and 105,730 in emergency phase (40% of the population IPC 3). Mercy Corps is proposing an unconditional cash-based assistance to support 2,000 households in IPC 4 (approx. 20% of actual HHs in that category) to meet their immediate food needs, as well as agriculture inputs interventions to support 500 farmers in IPC 4 and 3 to recover agricultural livelihoods and build resilience throughout the next season.
Mercy Corps will conduct unconditional cash transfers to 2,000 households for three months, with a value of $100 to cover 100% of the cost of food basket set by CNSA ($90) plus an additional $10 to cover transport costs. This cash will support HHs in crisis to be able to pay for food during the lean season, as prices have increased as a consequence of the drought by +9%. The transfers will be conducted from April to June 2019 in order to rapidly stabilize the food security situation for those most vulnerable households. Mercy Corps will work through local organizations (2 women’s associations and 2 mixed farmers associations), and in coordination with the local CASEC/ASEC and mayors’ offices to define community criteria to target vulnerable households for the cash-based assistance to support food security. Beneficiaries will be selected according to IPC ratings, specifically the Household Group Classification. This food security response will target households with individuals in IPC4 category, with a particular focus on households with children under five, pregnant or lactating women, elderly, or disabled members. Households experiencing IPC4 levels of vulnerability will be identified by at least one of the following criteria: (1) households experiencing large food consumption gaps causing symptoms of acute malnutrition in young children, and/or (2) households experiencing extreme loss of livelihood assets that contribute to immediate food consumption gaps. Mercy Corps will make these determinations based on measurements of households’ food consumption (Food Consumption Score), livelihood change (Coping Strategies Index), and nutritional status (acute malnutrition among children under 5).
In parallel, as a way to improve food security projections for the next season and improve local capacity to produce food at affordable prices, Mercy Corps will facilitate local associations to distribute vouchers for agricultural inputs (February - March 2019) to 500 drought-affected farmers (IPC 3 & 4) that will reduce risk and build resilience for the 2019 hurricane season and agricultural cycle. They will benefit from climate-smart agricultural training and extension services offered by associations through a Training of trainers approach to apply garden risk mitigation practices to better prevent future losses. Mercy Corps will engage the community to identify climatic risks to which they are vulnerable and design local risk mitigation strategies for which the community can be accountable.

Needs

Haiti is the poorest country in the Americas with a growth rate estimated at 2.7% in 2014, and is particularly exposed to hurricanes, tropical storms and droughts. The country’s ability to adapt and cope to those climate hazards is currently very limited and the effects are exacerbated by the country’s inadequate infrastructure and fragility of already overexploited natural resources; especially ecosystems (forests) that help to limit the floods caused during extreme rainfall events and minimize surface evapotranspiration during extreme variations in average temperature. The country is facing critical and chronic food and nutritional insecurity, with more than 30% of the Haitian population acutely food insecure and 131,000 children malnourished. For 2018, The El Niño phenomenon caused drought stress in many parts of the country which led to low production of cereals and beans. This, combined with rising commodity prices (+ 9% / yr) and year-on-year inflation of 14.1%, affected households' ability to purchase food. According to the latest IPC evaluation conducted in October 2018 by the CNSA, five departments in the country have been affected by drought (North, Northwest, South, Grand’Anse and West), from which 145,078 people (5% of population in these areas) are in IPC phase 4 (emergency) and 629,082 people (25%) are in IPC phase 3 (crisis), representing approximately 30% of the population facing food insecurity. Regarding the nutrition situation, according to the WHO, global acute malnutrition rates are higher (5-10%) than the alert threshold in all zones. This alarming situation is not likely to improve without efficient humanitarian interventions and assistance, because weather forecasts for March to May 2019 indicate a strong probability of El Niño to increase (50-55% chance during the autumn and 65-70% during the winter of 2018-19). Additionally, persistent inflation and depreciation of the gourde, as well as the lean season are factors that will reinforce the deterioration of food security. Consequently, the population size in crisis and emergency phases could potentially increase to 39% (982,651 people) (2018 CNSA IPC report). Mercy Corps’ vision in Haiti is to build resilience through the use of community-driven, community-led, and market-based approaches. In such a disaster prone country with a long, complex relationship with aid, this is critical to achieving sustainable impact. In response to the current food security crisis, we propose activities that will build the capacity of local associations to increase community and household resilience to climate hazards, while building their capacity to play a lead role in future responses. Mercy Corps will train these associations to help farmers adapt climate smart and nutritionally diverse practices. An adaptation of our Credit for Conservation methodology will link subsidies (vouchers instead of credit) for agriculture inputs to household garden risk mitigation and climate-smart agriculture. The seed and/or tools packages will be adapted to mitigate drought and landslides, and ensure food security and nutritional diversity in household gardens. Recipients will donate a portion of their seeds after the harvest to the association to begin building a seed bank. This approach was designed by our local partners after Hurricane Matthew in 2016 and has been highly effective with a near 100% donation rate. Direct Cash transfer will support most vulnerable households meet their immediate food needs while efforts will be concentrated to build community resilience to future climate hazards and shocks.

Activities or outputs

Objective 1: Basic food needs of vulnerable families affected by drought are covered. Activity 1: Mercy Corps (MC) will conduct a rapid market & food security assessment in order to determine food needs, and verify that the conditions (existing financial services providers, market status, security) are favorable for cash transfers, and potential impact on the local market. A participatory process will identify stakeholders (local partners) by assessing their organizational capacity, and providing a baseline measure of capacity per organization. Activity 2: MC will target 2,000 of the most vulnerable households for cash assistance using the comparative frequency listing approach (recommended as an effective targeting strategy by CNSA) to request samples of households from selected community leaders. These lists will be cross-referenced to identify the 2,000 most vulnerable HHs. MC will follow a “Do No Harm” approach, analyzing community dynamics and tensions, stakeholders’ interests and influences to better build community engagement, and not aggravate the vulnerabilities, especially pertaining to women and marginalized groups. MC will carry out community outreach through local partners to share information about beneficiary registration, and will assist in educating recipients about transfer amounts, beneficiary rights, and Mercy Corps' accountability systems (including complaints committees and hotlines). Activity 3: Mercy Corps will partner with a local Financial Service Provider to conduct cash transfers for registered beneficiaries. Beneficiaries will receive cash transfers of $100 USD once a month for 3 months during the lean season (April through June 2019). The amount was set according to the CNSA food basket and Minimum Expenditure Basket calculations for a family of 5, and will allow for beneficiaries to cover 100% of their food basket as well as have an extra 10% “protective margin” to ensure that transport needs are covered related to food purchases. The amount may be adjusted based on HH size. Objective 2: 500 drought-affected farmers have reduced their vulnerability to future climatic shocks. Activity 1: The organizational analysis will result in a climate-smart agricultural capacity building plan for each partner MC will then conduct training and intentional “learning-by-doing” activities such as cross-visits and exchanges to train on the practices. As farmer associations often exclude women from leadership and decision-making roles, MC will focus particularly on strengthening women's participation in intervention design and decision-making around implementation of the drought mitigation activities. CBOs will also receive training on gender, disability analysis and mainstreaming, to ensure that program results are equitable across genders and within marginalized groups. Activity 2: MC will provide partners with a grant for the equivalent of $150 per HH to be used toward the purchase of seeds and tools for 500 drought-affected farmers in IPC 3 and 4. They will elaborate seed lists and negotiate with local vendors. MC will ensure that the quality of the goods and services being offered met MARNDR standards and provide training on procurement best practices. MC will provide silos to the farmer associations to enable them to develop small community seed banks. At the end of the harvest, HHs which had received a seed package will contribute a small portion of seeds resulting from the harvest to the community seed bank and storing them for the next planting season. As designed by our local partners in nearby Arcahaie and Montrouis after Hurricane Matthew, this approach builds resilience in the community to mitigate against heavy losses of crops and seeds and allows the associations to procure low-cost seeds without having to purchase the bulk of their seeds on the open market.
