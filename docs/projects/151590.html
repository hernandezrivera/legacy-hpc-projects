Project Summary

The proposed project will increase access to education for the most vulnerable children and adolescents (female and male), including IDPs and other emergency-affected, while also supporting those already enrolled to continue learning in a safe and protective space. The lack of educational opportunities coupled with an uncertain environment due to displacement leave many children in psychosocial distress and vulnerable to further violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation. UNICEF will deliver a comprehensive Education in Emergencies package of evidenced effective interventions, inclusive of social mobilisation, enrolment campaigns, psychosocial support, gender-sensitive WASH, lifesaving and lifeskills development, essential education supplies, and teacher training aimed to achieve the highest positive education results for girls and boys, and for women and men to better improve emergency education service delivery. Without child-friendly education, protection, safe water, and psychosocial support, these children and adolescents are at risk of losing hope, of accepting violence as the norm, and replicating it - undermining their own future, the future of Somalia, and the stability of the region.

Needs

In Somalia, of the 4.4 million school-aged children, only 1.5 million children areenrolled in school. Somalia has lowest enrollment rate in the world. Only 17% of childrenliving IDPs settlements are enrolled in primary school. Gender disparities are extremelyhigh and not in favor of girls, while only 15% of the teachers are female. Lack ofschool facilities, inability of families to pay the education cost, household chores forgirls, insufficient number of qualified teachers, and ineffective community engagement inschool governance are the main barriers to education. Without adequate learning andprotective environment that school provides, Somali girls and boys are at risk of childlabor. While boys are vulnerable to recruitment into armed groups, girls are mostvulnerable to other risks such as GBV and early marriages. Currently, a total of 1.83 millionchildren are in need of assistance to access education opportunities. Those alreadyenrolled require assistance to continue schooling so that they do not risk dropping outwithout having gained basic literacy and numeracy. In addition, there remains a high needto enroll more out-of-school children in order to ensure they are do not fall prey to thecountless vulnerabilities, which may either cause them immediate harm as well proventing them from reachingtheir full development potential. The most vulnerable children and adolescents includethose from IDP communities, children affected by conflict and other emergencies, girls,nomadic/pastoralist children and those from rural communities. Together, these childrenand adolescent girls and boys constitute the most marginalized and are at risk ofexclusion.

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

The proposed interventions will enhance the resilience at individual, community and system level for durable solution. Infrastructural interventions through rehabilitation/construction of latrine, drinking water facilities and classroom serves not only the immediate need but is a long-term investment and will improve the safety and security of children for improved learning environment. The community can contribute toward school governance in any absent of government support. As the capacity of MoE improves the government may later take over school operation.The provision of incentive for teachers, prioritizing the selection of female, and building their capacity for continuous professional development has a longer impact on the education system as support to enhance the education workforce, which currently lacks trained and qualified teachers. Again, with strengthened MoE capacity, the government will have a cadre of some trained teachers whose skills can be further developed. The trained teachers will receive incentive to provide EiE services and as a result more incentivized to educate children. Ultimately, both the teachers and the students will gain the necessary knowledge and skills to contribute toward a more developed, peaceful and prosperous Somalia. The training of CEC members on areas and issues such as the importance of education, gender, conflict sensitivity, Safe School Declaration, and psychosocial support will ensure the active engagement of the community in the provision of quality child-centred education. CECs will also assist to sustain these efforts at the local level for school improvement. CECs will be supported to actively plan and implement enrollment drives and encourage parents and community leaders and influencers to advocate for children’s education, particularly for girls, thus positively leaving more durable impact.The distribution of recreation materials will not only improve children’s physical health through active play but also have great impact on their mental health. This leads toward improved children’s social skills in sharing, team spirit, cooperation, social harmony, tolerance and respect, thus reducing conflict and improved peacebuilding.UNICEF will closely work with MoE at all level to develop their capacity in coordination, monitoring, management, and supervision thus contributing to a more effective and efficient education system that is responsive to the learning needs of children – whether in emergency or as part of the longer-term development context. It is also envisioned that MoE will uptake their responsibility as duty bearers and are convinced to maximise the allocation of funds to education, particularly as part of the humanitarian response to minimize negative impact on children during times of stress.Education is a long-term investment and has a great impact for the future generation to live in peace and harmony, contribute to a more just, peaceful and prosperous society.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

In many IDPs settlement, the lack of active community participation in school governance and prioritization of needs by the affected community is a weak area, particularly meagre women’s representation in community committees & their voices in decision-making & feedback mechanism.Careful consideration will be taken to have strong representation of IDPs, particularly women in Community Education Committees (CEC). The role of the community to support education cannot be underestimated. Community members will be sensitized on the importance of education, particularly during emergencies. Community members will be rallied to send children to school. Support will be provided to strengthen the capacity of CEC members to understand and perform their role and responsibilities to voice their concerns, participatory planning and development of school improvement plans and overall take active decision in support of children’s education. This effort would help to enhance the communities’ resilience for durable solutions to assist children to enroll & remain in school during emergencies.In many IDPs settlement, parents are busy in livelihood seeking and producing opportunities, and girls often are saddled with the responsibility for caring of young siblings, which hamper their participation in education. This project will encourage more female teachers who can also serve as good role models for girls and parents are more confident to send their daughter to school. Children of female-headed households and unaccompanied children are more likely to not enroll in school due to household and livelihood responsibilities. School costs often deter parents from sending their children to school. The provision of school supplies will address the out-of-pocket expense issue, & motivate parents to send their children to school. Additionally, where available,school feeding also serves not only an incentive to school enrolment but can also improve the nutrition status for vulnerable children

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

The lack of gender-sensitive latrine, hand-washing facilities & clean water may increase school absenteeism & dropout of adolescent girls due particularly to challenges around menstrual hygiene management. This also increases their higher risk to be out of school, decreased coping mechanisms,& entering forced and/or early children marriage.The likelihood of this risk is high in IDP settlement where the school infrastructure is poor & lacking in gender-sensitiveWASH facilities.The provision of gender-sensitive latrine & hand-washing facilities will improve children’s health and hygiene, particularly for pubescent girls. The availability of these facilities, coupled with health & hygiene education & other lifeskills will contribute to improved positive health and hygiene practices & increase the chances of girls to sustain their education. Supporting girl’s education & addressing the social barriers for girl’s education will be key to this project.There is a strong likelihood of young poor boys to be engaged in child labor or recruitment to armed group if they remain out of school due to poverty and inability of family to pay the cost associated to school participation. Age-appropriate educational opportunities, inclusive of lifeskills, for boys will reduce the chance of child labor & their recruitment by armed groups. Moreover, the provision of teaching learning material will also address the issue of out-of-pocket expenses associated with schooling.This will also encourage the affected communities to enroll their children into school. CwDhave a higher chance to remain out of school as the infrastructure is not disability friendly. Teachers&students are not sensitive towards the needs of differently abled children.In this connection, one separate latrine will be constructed for children with disability where we construct new latrine.A ram will be developed for classroom during the repair and maintaince.Teachers&CEC members will be trained to support the needs ofCwD.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

In armed conflict, access to education services are greatly affected due to the attack on school or school occupied by armed group. In this connection schools are not safe for children, particularly girls while children with disability suffer even more. In times of emergencies where schools are not available in IDP camps and other settlements, Temporary Learning Spaces(TLS) provides the solution to provide children with a safe and protective learning environment, where children may feel secured, gain a sense of normalcy and routine, while also be supported to alleviate their fear, apprehension and stress.When children feel safe and protected at school parents have more time to address other issues which hamper their daily and social life. Supporting the psychosocial needs of children will improve children’s resilience such that they are better able to deal with the trauma and stress affecting them.Teachers and CEC members will also be trained on the psychosocial needs of children and how to provide them with the care and support they need. Teachers and CEC members will also be trained on the Safe School Declaration, advocacy and negotiation skills whereby they may act to mitigate risks associated with forces threatening to attack or occupy schools. Likewise, with other groups who may want to use schools for other purposes than education.Through education in emergency intervention, Safe School Declaration, code of conduct for teachers Child friendly spaces, recreation activities, community engagement will enhance the safety and security of children in addition to the provision of educational services in child friendly environment.All these steps will mitigate the impact of conflict and violence, particularly on children but also community members.
