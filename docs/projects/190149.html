Project Summary

This project spans for six months and focuses on an explosive ordnance (EO) threat assessment aiming to analyse the pattern of conflict, the nature of weapons used and where EO may be located, and the delivery of explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) messages to humanitarian personnel and populations at risk.
This project is in line with the EHF 2nd Reserve Allocation Strategy, and centres on two pillars of Humanitarian Mine Action: with mobilisation and threat assessment as the first steps towards land release, and Risk Education. Proposed interventions in support of these components are outlined below.
1) Mobilization and Threat Assessment
An international UNMAS Surge Operations Officer based in Addis Ababa will be supported by three national technical experts, deployed to Addis Ababa and Tigray, and further supported by Chief Mine Action Program, Information Management Officer and Programme Officer based in the capital, Addis Ababa. The team will travel throughout Afar, Amhara, and Tigray to conduct analysis and determine the pattern of conflict, the nature of weapons used and where EO may be located. The team will triangulate this information with the operational plans of humanitarian partners, to generate a list of priority areas that must urgently undergo Non-Technical Survey to identify and detail present risks and to plan how to minimize their impact on the effective delivery of aid programmes. An Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA) platform will be developed to enable both UNMAS/UNOPS and mine action partners from the Mine Action Area of Responsibility (MA AoR) to plan, monitor and visualize critical variables, get an overview of the EO contamination, produce customized reports and perform geospatial analysis to inform future clearance activities. The IMSMA database will receive inputs from all mine action partners and be able to analyse and display various types of data ranging from locations of explosive hazards, number of EORE beneficiaries to humanitarian response plans and movements of internally displaced persons. The national IMSMA officer will be trained in the setup and use of the system, enabling the mine action office in Ethiopia to sustainably produce IMSMA outputs, such as maps, graphic presentations and data for planning and monitoring, which will be shared by the UN community, humanitarian aid organizations and other interested parties.
2) Explosive Risk Reduction
The UNMAS team will work with local partners operating in Afar, Amhara, and Tigray to deliver EORE to communities, using training materials that are gender-responsive and contextually relevant. EORE equips its beneficiaries with the knowledge and information to recognize and avoid EO and to report their concerns to the mine action team, thus informing the team’s assessment. EO affects women, men, girls and boys differently; each group will receive targeted messaging, and the imagery used during the training will be inclusive.
While risk education training and activities are ongoing, information will be continuously shared with aid agencies operating in the area through the inter-cluster coordination group, humanitarian country team and other fora. UNMAS will oversee the implementation of EORE grants, support the delivery of training and other types of support to humanitarian partners to mitigate explosive risk, including taking part in humanitarian assessments.
To amplify EORE messaging, UNMAS will identify focal points within humanitarian organizations to train as trainers, so that EORE can be incorporated and replicated into their communications with communities.
The project will encourage presence of international mine action NGOs in the country and establish synergies with them, if possible, already at the stage of the baseline survey, whereas the NGOs could contribute with resources for the non-technical and technical surveys, to better maximize use of resources.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

UNMAS will work with national implementing partners who will have strong knowledge of the intervention areas and of the affected population’s cultural norms, languages and habits. For both explosive threat assessment and EORE activities, community liaison will be a key component as well as information gathering and sharing with CSOs, grassroots organizations and community leaders in the intervention areas. In addition, as EO affects women, men, girls and boys differently, each group will receive targeted messaging and the imagery used during EORE sessions will be inclusive. The EORE materials will be adapted to the local languages, field-tested to ensure they are easily understandable by the local community, and adjusted as necessary. Finally, affected populations will be able to report concerns and provide feedback to the EORE teams, and the AAP/PSEA reporting channels of the wider humanitarian system will be systematically shared.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

Affected populations will be able to report concerns about explosive ordnance to the EORE teams. The teams will use a variety of awareness raising formats including community sessions, focus-group discussions (with groups of women, youth and the elderly for instance), child-friendly sessions at schools, door-to-door outreach to ensure people with mobility impairment or household chores are not left behind, and leaflet distribution and/or radio broadcast in harder-to-reach areas. The teams will also seek to collect feedback on EORE materials as well as on the content and methodology of the EORE sessions, to ensure that they are adapted to the affected populations’ needs. As women in affected communities often feel more comfortable to report their concerns to other women, implementing partners will be encouraged to recruit gender-balanced teams. Finally, the teams will systematically share the AAP/PSEA reporting channels of the wider humanitarian system with affected populations.
