Project Summary

Based on the August- September 2019 IPC analysis, it is expected that 6.5 million people in South Sudan (54% of the total population) face Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse acute food insecurity. In the post-harvest period of January to April 2020, the food security situation will deteriorate as households’ food stocks start depleting and an estimated 5.5 million are likely to face Crisis phase or worse acute food insecurity Unity state where the project falls has 71.3% or 214,000 people in emergency or catastrophic phases of the IPC classification and a further 395,000 in phase 3. This project is therefore developed to address the food insecurity issues.
Through this project, Mercy Corps is planning to target the most vulnerable men, women, boys and girls in Rubkona and Panyijiar for IGAs through distribution of livelihood inputs to support beneficiaries during the dry season hunger gap, which will be from February – April 2020 (three months earlier than normal). The business start-up grants will be complemented by low input vegetable gardening and staple crop production (from March to May) as a means to promote household self-reliance and immediate food provision through own food production for both home consumption and sale of surplus. The project will also contribute to sustainable, equitable production, income and employment for the vulnerable through market-led production.
Mercy Corps appreciates that emergencies affects people differently. In South Sudan women, children and elderly are the worst affected with the conflict and economic shocks. As such in terms of targeting or selection, women, child headed households, elderly, PWD and the chronically ill will be highly preferred during the process. All activities including targeting, post distribution monitoring, valuations will engage women separately to ensure adequate contribution to issues affecting them. All reports will be presented with information dis aggregated by gender. The project will target a total of 10,830 households that comprises approximately 65,000 individual beneficiaries.
The proposed project will contribute to the Food Security and Livelihoods Cluster Objectives 2 and 3 namely:
- Cluster Objective 2: Enhance emergency food production through complementary vegetable and crop seeds and fishing and livestock support.
- Cluster Objective 3: Reduce dependency on food and agricultural inputs to support and strengthen household’s ability to absorb shocks.
Cash for work grants to most vulnerable households with labor ability will avail temporary income for vulnerable households to meet their immediate food requirement while community common resource properties like dykes are rehabilitated to combat flood events.
Cash programming will be complemented by vegetable farming and staple crops inputs as a means to diversify their diets. Livelihood promotion will include the following activities:
- Support vulnerable households towards short-cycle vegetable food production systems for home consumption and sale of surplus by distributing livelihoods assets e.g. vegetable seeds and tools, pro-resilience livelihood business start-up grants aimed at diversifying livelihood options.
- Where possible, livelihood activities are supported to improve household income aimed at immediate improved household purchasing power of diversified food of choice and working capital of other livelihood options.
- Communities are supported in preferred livelihood supply chain management and livelihood value chain development aimed at strengthening their resilience to the causes and effects of the common disasters.

1 - Needs

Over the years, conflict has devastated the livelihoods of millions of South Sudanese. According to the August to September 2019 South Sudan IPC projection, the food security situation remains a concern in the four Counties of Mayendit, Guit and Panyijiar where Emergency (IPC Phase 4) outcomes have persisted due to insecurity, cattle raiding, high food prices, flood-related destruction of crops and disruptions to market access. In Leer, Rubkona, Guit and Panyijiar Counties, delivery of significant amounts of funded Humanitarian Food Assistance will likely mitigate high food consumption gaps and maintain Crisis (IPC 3) levels of acute food insecurity. Between January and April 2020, Crisis (IPC Phase 3) acute food insecurity is expected to persist in Leer, Mayendit, Koch, Rukona, Pariang, Mayom, and Abienmhom given availability of some harvest, fish, livestock products, wild foods, and continued access to markets. In Guit and Panyijiar, food security situation is expected to deteriorate due to limited access to livestock products, early cereals depletion resulting from flood-related damages to crops and Fall Army Worms infestations. Furthermore, expected insecurity and cattle-raiding is likely to disrupt regular access to assistance and markets. Overall, an estimated 685,000 people, representing 64.9% of the State population are expected to be in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) and Emergency (IPC Phase 4) during the January to April 2020 projection period. Worsening food insecurity is primarily driven by protracted conflict and displacements in Unity State, which have contributed to insufficient crop production, disruptions to livelihoods and persistent macroeconomic deterioration. Livelihoods have been further eroded by climatic shocks, such as prolonged dry spells and flooding, and pest infestations (e.g. Fall Armyworm). Of extreme concern is the anticipated worst-case scenario of large-scale Catastrophe leading to Famine (IPC Phase 5) which is reported likely in protracted absence of humanitarian assistance and conflict-related restrictions to population movement, and according to the IPC September 2019 outlook, counties of greatest concern include Panyijiar in Unity State, South Sudan. Therefore, delivering humanitarian assistance linking relief and early recovery livelihood activities is needed urgently to save lives in the proposed geographical target of Unity State. However, even with the levels of humanitarian assistance in May-July 2018, Panyijiar County remained in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) acute food insecurity and will require large-scale assistance to prevent extreme food security outcomes and loss of lives. In a context of chronic conflict, economic shocks, poverty, climate extreme shocks of frequent flash floods within the flood plain of River Nile are further undermining food security and livelihood viability in Unity State of South Sudan. The South Sudan Situation Report for September 2019 , has reported that the living conditions of South Sudan people in many parts of Mayom, Mayendit and Panyijiar in Unity State have worsened due to floods caused by torrential rains. Floods have destroyed crops and productive assets and damaged essential service infrastructure and have increased the risk of diseases like malaria and respiratory infections. Authorities and aid organizations have reported that an estimated 37,000 people in Unity State have been affected by the floods.The Famine Early Warning report for September 2019, FEWSNET , has reported that some households mainly in Unity, Jonglei and Upper Nile States anticipate that harvests in 2019 will be lower than last year due to pests and diseases and flood-related destruction of crops from July-September. Critical levels of malnutrition have been projected in Unity State for the period of October-December 2019.The South Sudan Port and Monitoring report for June 2019 has reported 384 displaced individuals (98HH) arriving in Panyijiar between 5-26 June 2019.

2 - Activities

Activity 1.1.I Mobilize and introduce community to Mercy Corps programming and solicitation of information on preferred crop varieties, prioritized community assets, as well as gathering information to refine curriculum for cash grants and farmer field school and demonstrations. Activity 1.1.2 Disseminate information on program to community to begin community-based targeting and registration process. Activity 1.1.3 Conduct risk and resilience assessment, market analysis and baseline survey that establishes food security and nutritional status of the targeted population and evaluates trader and market capacity (market research). Activity 1.1.4 Provision of fishing kits to 4,000 households in Rubkona and Panyijiar Counties of Unity State. Activity 1.1.5 Provision of cash for work grants to 800 most vulnerable households with labour ability (of which at least 50% are female headed) in Unity State. Activity 1.1.6 Conducting of Post Distribution Monitoring to assess the impact of cash provision for beneficiary households. Activity 1.1.7 Strengthen grassroots informal extension service agents through targeting and training 40 Extension agents (30% females) for extension service delivery in intensive vegetable production and market linkages. Activity 1.1.8 Organize 1 ,200 HH members into 30 vegetable producer groups (50% with women leadership) for collective effort and risk sharing in vegetable production. Farmers pull scare resources and work together for individual profit. Activity 1.1.9 Train 4000 farmers (60% women) in intensive and sustainable vegetable production. Farmers will be trained on the agronomic practices of vegetable production. The training will include land preparation, nursery management, seed spacing and planting, inter cropping, crop rotations; soil and water conservation methods, common diseases and pest control, use of cultural and biological means to control diseases and pests, harvest and post-harvest handling techniques, market linkages and seed selection and preservation of viable (OPVs) seeds. Activity 2. 1.1 Distribute high value and assorted vegetable seeds (OPVs) such as Kalez, okra, Jews melon, spinach, pumpkin, amaranths, Eggplant, cabbage, onions, cowpeas and tomatoes) and tools (hoes or maloda and a watering can) to 1,200 most vulnerable targeted households Activity 2.1. 2 Provision of practical based training and education of farmers on improved agronomic practices (targeting 50% women through engagement of women´s associations). Activity 2.1. 3 Provision of staple crop inputs to 4000 households. Activity 2.1.4 Distribution of small business start-up grants to 600 hh (child headed HH, female headed vulnerable households, youth – male & female from vulnerable households) Activity 2.I.4 Conducting of Post Distribution Monitoring to assess the impact of the input provision for beneficiary households. Activity 2. Extension service delivery to farmer groups for low cost and market-led production Activity 2.1.1 Community sensitization and mobilization for Income Generating activity skills training Activity 2.1.2 Select and register 600 beneficiaries for business start-up grants. Activity 2.1.3 Organize 600 trainees into classes (depending on final enrolment) for IGA trainings. Activity 2.2.1 Conduct end-line survey.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Given the complex nature of displacement and conflict in South Sudan in addition to already existing high level needs in our areas of operation, Mercy Corps also engages with and targets host communities. This helps to support conflict sensitive programming and social cohesion from the start of the project. Not only are host communities benefiting from our project, but we also work to support host and displaced communities working together to manage potential sources of conflict (e.g. water sources) by supporting both communities to communicate proactively and reactively to conflict through different community based structures. The current crisis in South Sudan has caused significant protection issues for women, men, girls, and boys. All programming will therefore be based on a thorough protection analysis, in order to ensure that activities are not causing risks to civilians in targeted areas. Mercy Corps interventions on fishery and vegetable production support, in addition to promoting trade and dietary diversity would also enable IDP’s and host communities to access food varieties close to home.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes it is stable and safe but highly fluid. Panyijar County, in Southern Unity State, is in an opposition-controlled region and home to an almost exclusively Nuer population. The region has remained relatively stable throughout South Sudan’s civil war, in large part because of its geographic isolation and the natural barrier provided by the Sudd, a vast swamp that encompasses the area. As a result, the county hosts a large number of internally displaced people estimated at 74,888 individuals in August 2018. Most internally displaced people in Panyijar are thought to be displaced from neighboring Leer and Mayendit counties, which have been home to some of the conflict’s most intense fighting. The vast majority of internally displaced people in Panyijar report having strong social connections in the County that predate their arrival and have informed their decisions to relocate to the region. In Rubkona there are Protection Of Civilians camps where displaced people and returnees can seek protection and safety.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

The project will work wit agricultural extension workers who will continue with extension after the life of the project. VSLA agents will continue monitoring savings groups and link participants to traders and fishermen. Trainings will be integrated to allow all social groups to attend and even those who might migrate, they can move along with the attained skills.Fishermen will be trained on how to manage their fishing gears so that they last long and will be encouraged to form fishing VSLA saving groups to raise money to buy their own fishing kits including canoes. Seed banking trainings will be conducted for the communities to have stock of seeds and be prepared for planting seasons. A market systems development approach will ensure traders have the capacity to supply agricultural inputs locally for the benefit of farmers. Sustainable use of trees will be managed through natural resource management plans to encourage use of energy saving mud stoves that save the use of firewood for the benefit of the present and future generations. . Beneficiary trainings on conservation farming and fishery promote and ensure environmental sustainability. Market-led production which is linked to improved housed income can provide working capital for continuous operation cost of project activities at household level of food production. By the time the project ends, it is expected that the County Agriculture and Fishery Department will be covering recurrent costs for agricultural extension services including the salary of the extension workers seconded by government, so that the extension service delivery is fully functional. The proposed activities under this project are largely towards capacity-building and facilitating resilience building and food security for vulnerable people. This action builds capacity and alliances, and strengthens farmer institutions using a market-based approach. This approach will increase production and income of the target groups bringing stability.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

Protection mapping will be conducted to identify threats, vulnerabilities and assess community coping mechanisms to mitigate impacts on the safety and dignity of people. Protection risk analysis will be based on the findings of the Protection Mapping and proposes measures to mitigate the negative impacts the organisation may have on the protection environment. This will be followed by a Protection Mainstreaming Plan that includes the mitigation measures identified in the Protection Risk Analysis. It details mainstreaming activities and identifies costs and helps the organisation to track the implementation of mainstreaming activities. Mercy Corps will conduct an evaluation to assess the impact of the Mainstreaming Plan.Mercy Corps is committed to ensuring safe programming and mainstreaming protection at all stages of this project. Mainstreaming protection in the project will significantly mitigate the impact of exclusion of marginalized groups, particularly women from decision-making processes and in ensuring that we are better able to meet the needs expressly identified by our female beneficiaries, as well as those people with specific needs. Mercy Corps will take pro-active steps to ensure beneficiaries’ meaningful access to impartial assistance and facilities in proportion to need and without any barriers/discrimination. Mercy Corps has a strong community based approach throughout project implementation. Throughout programmes, it has succeeded in finding opportunities for women to participate in activities not traditionally targeted at them. The integration of protection staff in the project team will help ensure the implementation of key activities beyond basic safe programming and Do No Harm approaches. Special attention will be given to individuals and groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance and facilities.Complaints mechanisms will also be closely linked with protection committees and local GBV partners.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Women, men, girls, and boys within target communities will be directly involved at all stages of the program cycle in order to encourage ownership and participation in program activities. Community structures, networks, and support groups are fundamental to building resilience and are at the heart of the Mercy Corp’s programme strategy. Mercy Corps recognizes that communities are not homogenous, and the gender analysis will enable us to jointly design an appropriate intervention, with the community, looking at the needs of all sectors. Mercy Corps is fundamentally committed to ensuring accountability to communities and installing a rigorous feedback mechanism which is part of the program. Community structures, networks, and support groups are fundamental to building resilience and are at the heart of Mercy Corps programme strategy. The organisation recognizes that communities are not homogenous, and the gender analysis will enable us to jointly design an appropriate intervention, with the community, looking at the needs of all sectors. Where possible, community members will be given the opportunity to evaluate the program on an ongoing basis. Humanitarian accountability mechanisms will be set in place through designated staff members and MEAL staffs. In line with Humanitarian Standard on Quality and Accountability (CHS), MC will ensure that the affected population and other actors have access to timely, relevant and clear information about the project, organization, their rights, entitlements, and when and where to access the services offered. This information will be communicated to the targeted population, authorities, etc. at the project start and will continuously be shared during implementation. Depending on the audience, the information will be shared in the appropriate way (e.g. local language, in pictures). Complaint and feedback mechanisms will be set up to monitor programme implementation and ensure the voices of the beneficiaries are heard.
