Project Summary

The project aims at enhancing access to agricultural livelihood opportunities for vulnerable H.Hs by supporting wheat value chain marketing to improve long-term food security and local market resilience by purchasing wheat directly from farmers. 606 tons of wheat crops (around 75 % soft and 25 % durum) will be purchased from the most vulnerable wheat farmers (after testing crop samples using SOP before purchases) according to the market prices in Teftanaz sub-district PC(SY070004), Mhambal sub-district PC(SY070502) and Maaret Tamasrin sub-district PC (SY070005) with selection criteria based on need and vulnerability with prioritizing women, disabled, elderly, and internally displaced farmers who cultivated lands or properties with wheat crops and meet the following criteria.
- Farmers who didn’t benefit from any livelihood project for 2021-2022.
- Farmers who grew high-quality wheat crops.
- Smallholder farmers who own or rent land between 5 to 10 dunums
- Farmers primary source of income is agriculture.
- Farmers reside in the targeted area.
beneficiaries’ names and vulnerabilities will be identified through registration and verification procedures in coordination with Local councils and Farmer’s Associations. M&E team will verify the data on the ground. Up to 220 farmers will be selected, each farmer can provide between 1 to 3 tons of wheat. Each farmer will receive support fees (25 $ per ton-WPTF suggestion).
The purchased wheat will be milled with 75 % extraction rate flour, and 25% bran (Which will be used to cover milling costs) to produce 454.5 MT of local flour, at the same time 778.5 tons of imported Turkish flour will be provided by IHH as a free contribution to the project. The total quantity of flour which is 1233 tons will be transported to the bakery for baking and producing 2,361,195 good quality bread bundles weight 600 g to be distributed to 4200 H.Hs of the IDPs in 31 camps in Maaret Tamasrin subdistrict in Haranbush and Kafr - Kafrehmul communities for 25 days a month up to 10 months. where one day at the beginning of each month will be allocated for replacing beneficiaries' bread cards after verifying their ID documents.
Each ton of flour will produce 1915 bread bundles. The modality of bread distribution will be blanket modality that will cover all the H.Hs in the targeted camps based on the following distribution criteria:
-HH with 1-3 individuals will receive one bread bundle, HH with 4-6 individuals will receive two bread bundles, HH with 7-9 individuals will receive three bread bundles,HH with 10 individuals and more will receive four bread bundles.
IHH will be the direct implementing partner of the project while IC will be the sub-IP. IHH will be responsible for wheat purchasing, selecting farmers, signing MOU with LC&FA and farmers, storing wheat and flour in the warehouses, contracting mill and bakery, and providing 778.5 tons of flour as a free contribution to the project. While IC will be responsible for selecting beneficiaries of the IDPs in the camps, signing MOUs with camps managers, distributing bread to IDPs in the camps for up to 10 months and updating beneficiary lists on a monthly basis, and preparing a waiting list for filling gaps when beneficiaries move out of the area covered.
IHH&IC MEAL team will conduct field visits to check the condition of the warehouse, bakeries, the milling process, the delivery of bread, bread quality, and adherence to FSL guidelines and code of ethics during the implementation of activities. Project activities will be coordinated closely with local authorities, the FSL cluster, and other humanitarian actors in addition to the local councils, and Farmers' Associations. This is to avoid any duplication and to engage stakeholders and beneficiaries in designing, implementing, and evaluating project activities.IHH&IC have a strong programmatic structure with a strong presence on the ground and excellent track records in delivering similar projects.

0 - Project Summary (extended)

This project aims to enhance access to agricultural livelihood opportunities for vulnerable households (H.Hs) in Northwest Syria by supporting the wheat value chain. Through the direct purchase of wheat from farmers, milling, and distribution of flour and bread, the project seeks to improve long-term food security and local market resilience, prioritizing the most vulnerable farmers and internally displaced persons (IDPs). Overview:The project focuses on purchasing 606 tons of wheat crops directly from vulnerable wheat farmers in three sub-districts. It involves selecting farmers based on need and vulnerability criteria, milling the purchased wheat, and distributing flour and bread to IDP households in 31 camps within the targeted area. Key Components: Wheat Purchase and Selection: Vulnerable farmers meeting specific criteria, including prioritizing women, disabled, elderly, and internally displaced farmers, will be selected for wheat purchase based on need and crop quality. Flour Production: Purchased wheat will be milled to produce flour with a 75% extraction rate, ensuring local milling costs are covered through bran production. Additionally, imported Turkish flour will supplement the project's flour stock. Bread Distribution: Flour will be used to produce good quality bread bundles, distributed to IDP households in camps according to household size, with up to 25 days of distribution per month for 10 months. Monitoring and Evaluation: A robust monitoring and evaluation framework will be implemented, involving field visits to assess warehouse conditions, milling processes, bread quality, and adherence to food security and livelihood guidelines. Expected Outcomes: Livelihood Support: Vulnerable farmers will receive support through direct wheat purchases, contributing to their economic resilience and livelihood sustainability. Food Security: The project will enhance food security for both farmers and IDP households by ensuring access to staple food items such as flour and bread. Local Market Resilience: By strengthening the wheat value chain, the project aims to improve the resilience of local markets, fostering economic stability and growth in the targeted communities.
