Project Summary

The proposed project swiftly addresses food insecurity of 10,000 households (50,000 individuals) through three rounds of food distribution, in addition to distribute a bread on daily basis to 7000 HHs (35000 individuals) whom living in the camps in North of Syria for 10 months, where each family will recive 1 K.G of bread for 6 days a week for 10 months.
According to the multiple reports from the Clusters and multi-sector needs assessment at the end of 2018, about 6.5 million individuals are food insecure and 2.5 millions are at risk of food insecure.
After informing details of food distribution 3-7 days prior to the event, 10,000 households will receive food basket for 3 months through small and multiple distribution points at each village to avoid a high number of beneficiaries at the same place.
Ensuring the beneficiaries feedbacks and recommendations to inform the project implementation, a leaflet on feedback mechanism will be circulated during distribution.
Through setting a selection criteria based on vulnerability analysis, the most vulnerable groups among the IDP will be supported and they can reduce their likeliness of engaging negative coping mechanism, and their life-risks related to food shortage to be mitigated which will be verified through post distribution monitoring activities.

Needs

In 2018, a nation-wide, WoS coordinated needs assessment was conducted to complement sector-specificassessments. While sector assessments – where feasible- form the basis of the sector specific needsidentification process, by ensuring needs are identified at a more granular level, the multi-sector needsassessment (MSNA) provides an overall representation of the situation and helps ensure inter-sectoralcoherence.The MSNA is a multi-hub and multi-partner assessment coordinated by OCHA to identify humanitarian needs at community level, and according to the results of the assessments we can present the following key messages:• From the assessed households, an estimated 40 percent of Syrians spend more than 65 per cent of theirexpenditure on food, 57.2 per cent of food insecure households reported to have outstanding debt and 65per cent of food insecure people are adopting crisis or emergency harmful coping strategies.• Eight years into the crisis, the main drivers to food insecurity are prolonged displacements, loss and lackof livelihoods and reduced production capacity which in turn resulted in limited physical and financialaccess to food, high prices and inflation contributing to reduced purchasing power and continuouslivelihood depletion of the most vulnerable populations.• Almost 40 per cent of the surveyed households report to be IDPs or returnees, 74.5 per cent of whichreported to be displaced for more than 12 months. Among them 478,282 displaced people living in lastresort camps are of particular concern as they have limited access to a diversified diet or incomeopportunities.• With an 83 - 89 per cent of the population living below poverty lines, and with a cumulative GDP loss of US$226 billion between 2011 and 2016 out of which US$16 billion in losses and damages attributable tothe agriculture sector alone 119, the relationship between poverty and food security is becominginseparable. A monthly food ration with staple items costs at least 80 per cent of an unskilled labourer’smonthly salary and 50-80 percent of a public service employee’s monthly salary, thus demonstrating theexistence of “working poor” in Syria.• The worst drought in 30 years affected cereal production during the 2017/2018 winter season in Syria.This has come as an additional burden on the prolonged crisis with a shortfall of 1.2 million metric tonnesof wheat to meet domestic requirements (food use) of 3.4 million metric tonnes.• Unprecedented livestock asset losses estimated to about half of the pre-crisis situation affecting livelihoods has led to irreversible coping strategies.• There is an urgent need for multiple actions to provide food and livelihoods assistanceAs to cover the needs mentioned above, this emergency response project designed to help IDPs and most vulnerable groups to reduce the negative in the effects of purchasing food and reducing number of meals by providing them with food baskets.

Implementation of activities

This project will be implemented directly by HIH staff and will provide 10,000 HHs from the IDPs and Vulnerable Returnees and locals in the pre-mentioned villages with food baskets for 3 months, the distribution modality will be distribution points on the villages and to avoid having a high number of beneficiaries at the same place, we will conduct the distribution through many small distribution points at each village.also HIH team will distribute wheat flour bread to 7000 HHs from the IDPs whom living in the camps on daily basis for 6 days a week along of 10 months of distribution.the project implementation will be through 5 main activities,1. HIH field team will start to collect the updated new IDPs beneficiaries lists from the local councils of the targeted villages to ensure that the collected data is still valid.2. HIH logistic team will announce for 2 public tenders in turkey in addition to one inside Syria the 1st one in Turkey to select a supplier which will be able to provide the 30,000 food baskets with the best quality in lowest price and the shortest time of delivering of these food baskets to the field HIH warehouse inside Syria, insisting that 10,000 food at minimum out of 30,000 food baskets will be delivered to the field warehouse by the vendor within a month after supplier selection, while the 2nd tender in Turkey will be to select a supplier which will be able to provide the 1680 ton of wheat flour with the best quality in lowest price and the shortest time of delivering of these wheat flour to the field HIH warehouse inside Syria divided on 5 shipments and the one in Syria will be aimed to select a vendor to receive the wheat flour and bake it in field bakeries and deliver the agreed quantities of bread to the agreed camps on daily basis with agreed quality.3. while the head office in Turkey is working on the procurement of the food baskets and wheat flour the HIH field team will conduct home visits randomly to the beneficiaries' houses which will achieve the 10% of the total beneficiaries listed number which we already received from the local councils, these visits will be verify that all of the provided information by the local councils are match the real situation.4. HIH field team will conduct kick-off workshops with all of the local councils and local authorities and camps managements in all of the targeted villages to sign a MOUs which will explain all of the project activities and objectives,the field team also will print out a distribution cards for all of the agreed beneficiaries in the target area and distribute those cards to the beneficiaries which will make them able receive the food baskets or the wheat flour from the distribution points, before the distribution time will inform all of the beneficiaries at least from 3 to 7 days about the distribution date, time and place.the distribution points will be small to avoid the risks as we mentioned.we will conduct 3 round of food baskets distribution to the same beneficiaries, in addition to daily distribution of bread to the same beneficiaries for 10 months.5. HIH filed MEAL team will conduct the PDM after each round of food baskets distribution and on monthly basis for the bread's one to measure the beneficiaries satisfaction regarding the quality of the distributed food baskets and the quality of the bread, the field team behaviors and the timing of the response.

Protection Risk Analysis

Protection risk 1: There is a risk of discrimination or perceptions of discrimination for some groups from the beneficiaries lists:Likelihood 1: Medium. Impact 1: HighMitigating measures 1: 1. Ensure consultation with communities related to locations of distribution to understand relevant community perspectives; 2. Conduct verification visits on the household level to ensure that the provided lists inline with the agreed criteria; 3. open many channels for feedback and complains from the beginning of the project.Protection risk 2: risk of sexual exploitation by humanitarian workers (risk of harassment) of women and girls.Likelihood 2: High. Impact 1: HighMitigating measures 2: 1. Establish a confidential complaint mechanism; 2. Train staff on code of conduct; 3. Code of conduct in all staff contracts Address any SEA complaints; 4. ensure existence of Female staff in each distribution point "from the distribution teams or MEAL team".

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Access to specific Sub-districts remains open.The Food baskets arrived to our warehouses on time.No drastic changes on the Bab-Alhawa border regulations. Risks:Risk 1: escalation the airstrikes in targeted areas.Mitigation 1: Conducting the distribution via many micro distribution points meaning don't have any high number of collected people in the same place in addition to cancel the day of distribution when mentioning any fighter jet hovering in the sky.Risk 2: Turkish Government decided to close the Bab-Alhawa border for urgent issue.Mitigation 2: 2.1. put this issue on the contract with the supplier and put a condition that after signing the contract if Bab-Alhawa closed for any issue, the supplier will provide HIH with the same quantities of agreed items in Side Syria with the same specification from local resources.2.2. Immediate contracting with multi suppliers inside Syria to provide the same food items with the same quantities.Risk 3: Delay from the supplier on delivering the Food Baskets to HIH warehouse.Mitigation 3: Put a high penalty for the delay in delivering on the supplier in the signed contract, so the supplier will be more focus on his commitment with the delivery date.
