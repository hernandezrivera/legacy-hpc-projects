Project Summary

This program aims at supporting the resilience of the conflict affected people in northwest Syria, particularly in Idleb and Aleppo governorate. Shafak's proposed is designed to contribute to supporting the transition to the early recovery phase, as it supports the targeted communities with long-term assistance activities, programs that focus on building/restoring the communities' infrastructure, local economic empowerment, and initiatives that ensure engagement and participation of the community in recovering from the protracted crisis. This program will achieve its objectives through the following:
a) Providing short-term employment opportunities through Cash-for-Work activities to 2000 HHs in two rounds.
b) Offering rural micro-financing opportunities to 250 HHs, each beneficiary will receive 750 USD.
c) Providing vocational and skills training to 1500 youth, men and women applicants and then support successful trainees (Expecting 800 trainees) with small-scale cash grants to enable them restart/initiate their small enterprises, each grant total will be 600 USD per beneficiary.
d) Rehabilitating the local economic, public and social infrastructure (7 Projects) through linking with the Cash-for-Work activity or through service providers. Each project cost will be about 15000 USD
e) Supporting local community and social cohesion initiatives.

4 - Needs

The crisis in Syria continues to strain the socio-economic foundations of Syrian people, thereby affecting their ability to sustain adequate standards of living, seek decent livelihood opportunities, and access essential infrastructure and services. These factors have deepened poverty and weakened communities’ resilience to cope with internal and external shocks. The World Bank reclassified Syria as a Low-Income Country (LIC) in 2018 implying that the Gross National Income per capital has fallen below US$ 996. A key determinant of living standards is the availability of electricity. 1 in 2 people has less than 12 hours of electricity supply on a daily basis . Lack of access to sufficient electricity has constrained, for instance, the supply of clean drinking water, management of essential health services and a sense of safety in private and public spaces after sunset, particularly for females. Geographically, 72 sub-districts show a severe shortage of electricity. Likewise, the assessment indicates that up to 25 per cent of basic social infrastructure remains destroyed, negatively impacting provision and availability of and sustained access to essential services such as physical access, water supply, sewerage, energy, and solid waste managementزThe opportunities and abilities for the affected population to find decent sources of income are highly constrained. According to estimates in 2015, over 83 per cent of Syrians lived below the poverty line and recent indication suggests that poverty has worsened since 2015 . Out of around 11 million potential members of the workforce (between 18 and 65 years of age), more than half (3.4 million males and 3.1 million females) are unable to find sustained, reliable and decent work. The assessment also indicates that 55 per cent of total males and 75 per cent of total females report less income from all sources than they need to maintain a moderate living standard.

5 - Implementation of activities

Shafak has its implementation approach with regards to the early recovery projects, Shafak starts its programs with community mobilization activities, and conduct orientation sessions about the project activities, approaches and assistance modalities. For the cash for work activity, this proposed action will work with communities, to identify priority projects in the community that would help restore or protect assets, particularly related to livelihoods, water, and sanitation such as: waste management, restoration of public infrastructure. Cash-for-Work opportunities will be provided to households from the 'very poor' and 'poor' wealth groups who were formerly reliant on unskilled labor. Unskilled laborers will be employed for a duration of 20 days to two months, during which time they will receive wages on rates agreed as per the market surveys and Cluster's guidance. Regarding small business activities, Shafak aims at supporting a group of selected applicants with vocational training, receive their business plans, based on the evaluation of which a narrower group will receive the cash small grant. This activity will offer 1500 applicants an extensive training on various occupations; in addition, the action will provide structured support to 800 small businesses and enterprises in the target locations, enabling them to re-start, increase trade and become more profitable. Both CFW and small business cash support will be in the voucher-based approach. Designing and printing the voucher will be done in Turkey to ensure the availability of the security features on the vouchers. As for rehabilitation of infrastructure shafak will identify 7 top community projects to be implemented through service provider. micro-finance shafak will identify 250 beneficiary, cluster them into groups and support them with fund then this fund will be received back on monthly patches then to be distributed to other members groups and so on.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

- limitations on movement and physical security risk due to conflict activity. - Community and family stigmatization for participating in programming, family resistance to girl’s mobility. - Low participation of girls because of family and community resistance. Shafak will mitigate the above risks through the community orientation sessions, in which the Shafak staff ensure that equality concepts are conveyed to the community. The project team in coordination with MEAL and protection technical teams will identify and address protection risks caused/perpetuated by the intervention, design activities in-line with protection principles, monitor whether protection mainstreaming is taking place, evaluate the impact of protection mainstreaming actions on affected populations. By taking into account the following elements throughout the project life cycle:1. Prioritize Safety and Dignity and Avoid Causing Harm: Prevent and minimize as much as possible any unintended negative effects of the intervention on affected people. This also includes ensuring data protection during registration, referrals, case management, IPA etc. As such, the project team will be following the Media Guidelines developed by the protection cluster which ensure data protection and confidentiality at all stages, information sharing protocols and acquiring informed consent from beneficiaries.2. Meaningful Access: Arrange for individuals and particularly children to access services in proportion to need and without barriers, while paying special attention to groups who may be particularly vulnerable or have difficulty accessing assistance.3. Accountability: Set-up appropriate mechanisms, through which affected populations can measure the adequacy of interventions, and address concerns an and complaints.4. Participation and Empowerment: support the development of communities and individual capacities and assist beneficiaries to advocate and claim their rights.
