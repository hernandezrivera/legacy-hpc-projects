Project Summary

The last consecutive rains of Borena was not normal. According to the information provided by the zonal DRM office on March. 14, 2022 and confirmed by our own assessment on February 28 to March 3, 2022, the last Gana rain was very minimum in amount with erratic and uneven distribution throughout the season and the Furmata rain was absent. In addition, the beginning of the current Hagaya rainfall was delayed by four weeks and even totally disappeared in most parts of the zone.
The failure of Gana and Furmata rains and the delay of Hagaya has caused a tremendous impact on the lives and livelihoods of Borana pastoralists and agro-pastoralists. Total 138 water schemes are non-functional (28.3%) that need urgent solution to addressee 83,795 number of population. From 581 total ponds only 150 (25%) have water and can serve only for the coming two weeks. Similarly, from 857 total traditional elas about 440 are dry up and the existing functional is also not well-off to use it because the water table is drown. As a result, over half a million livestock have died and the remaining condition is very critical. The service rendering capacity of public institutions such as schools and health centres are imperiled because the water shortage (Borena zone Emergency Taskforce report, January 30, 2021).
As per the zone DRM assessment February 22, 2022, out of 556,859 people living in13 woredas facing water shortages only 177,553 have access to water distributed using water trucks by the government and a few humanitarian agencies including CISP. About 166,130 people need immediate food assistance while 682,804 people living in 130 kebeles are highly at risk.
The project targets three most affected and less supported woredas, namely Dire, Teltele and Elwaya. There woredas constituted 21% of the total affected population in the zone and less emergency interventions compare to the others. A few water trucking and other WASH activities are carried out in all the targeted woredas. As a result, people are either travelling long distance, about 35 km round trips to find water or drinking unsafe surface water, which may trigger water borne diseases. Consequently, the water consumption level of the community has dropped much below the stand to 1.5 to 2 lit/day/person & in the course of traveling long distance to fetch water, women & girls could exposed to GBV & girls are giving away their education.
To rescue the life and livelihood of these communities CISP under this project is planning to rehabilitate three water supply systems, water trucking until the water systems rehabilitated and/or next rain starts, promoting hygiene and sanitation practices, and local partner capacity building activities. Totally this project will address 58313 people (29156 male and 29157 female) including 5248 people with disabilities through durable solutions.

1. - Projects activities

1. Water System Rehabilitations • Rehabilitation of Dibe Gaya WSS, Taltale Woreda: It is the only water source for the Dibe Gaya &surrounding kebeles for Human &animal consumption. The system has been serving the community during this drought season &because of overutilization of the 18 kw submersible pump is burned &the system ceased operation. Hence, the Dibe Gaya Community is facing critical water shortage problem. Thus, it is plan to replace the damaged pump &serve about 7500 (3600 m &3900 f) peoples of the kebele &its environs. • Rehabilitation of Masgerdo-Brindar WSS, Elawaye Woreda: the system was previously constructed by Oromia Water bureau &the expansion work is done by GPDI. The system was providing service for Masgerdo &Brindar kebele communities extending up to 12 kms. The system ceased the provision of service for the 8450(4056 m &4394 f) people because of the total damage of Generator. The community is currently facing acute water shortage. The project will install the 90 kvs generator &associated activities.• Rehabilitation of Megado WSS, Dire Woreda: it is situated in Megado village of Dire Woreda which is the source of water for pastoralists from Ethiopia &Kenya as is closer to the Ethio-Kenya border. The Megado community has no alternative water sources like ponds as the area is known for its very hot climate. The community uses the system throughout the year. The existing generator is most of the time damaged because of floods coming from highland areas of Dilo woreda which needs relocation of Generator house for durable solution. 18.5 kw submersible pump was also burnt because of overutilization as the system serves higher amount of cattle population. Hence, the community is currently having acute water shortage which needs urgent response. The project will reactivate the system by fixing the problems &benefit 8,263(4,214 F &4049 M) people.2. Provision of capacity building trainings: • Support community based water management system: this activity aimed at ensuring sustainable community management of water system by strengthening or establishing water management committees, cost recovery system &supporting scheme operators/care takers.• Training for Woreda &Zonal Water Technicians: CISP’s own assessment shows that the woreda &zonal technicians have big technical skill gaps in the maintenance of water supply systems including switchboards, generators &other electromechanical equipment. Hence, it is imperative to give on the job practical training for woreda &zonal technicians by trained professional trainers. Hence, the project planned to train 2 persons per woreda from 14 woredas &3 technicians from zonal level for 10 days. Besides, the project will equip the woreda water offices with maintenance tools like mechanical tools box which will help them during maintenances. Considering resource limitation, the tools box will be delivered to woredas of project intervention only. • Maintenance of non-functional generators &switchboards which is not providing services because of small failure of parts: the main focus of many project was replacement of existing equipment with new ones. Maintenance of existing generators, pumps &switchboards was overlooked by government &NGOs. CISP has the observation that at many water sites we can have upto 3 generators in a row all not providing proper services. Majority of them are not functional due to minor technical defects which can be maintained with minor cost as compared to purchase of new items. Hence, the project planned to rehabilitate existing generators by deploying technicians &supplying spare parts. 3. Hygiene &sanitation promotions: To promote community awareness on the personal hygiene &environmental sanitation, awareness raising campaign will be conducted using multi methods at sites of the proposed motorized schemes. In the campaigns majority of the population living in the projects areas, which are estimated to be 34,100 (>60 women) will participate.

3.2 - How will your project work with relevant local & national actors (e.g. CSOs, advocacy groups) in support of effective design, implementation and monitoring of the response, as well as ensure their meaningful participation in supporting this response

The project will be managed by CISP, and activities will be implemented in collaboration with community and Woreda and Zone Water, Energy and Mineral offices. The regional bureau will provide continuous coordination and monitoring throughout the project implementation period. In addition, Borena Zone Water, Energy and Mineral Office will mobilize its experts and service rig for the installation of the pumps and jointly facilitate trainings of community operators. The Women and Children Affairs and Social and Labour Affairs will monitor the participation of women, children and people living with disabilities. It will also make sure that the systems are children, women, and disabilities friendly. CISP will be also responsible for direct implementation of the project, supply project inputs, provision of logistic and overall coordination. The organization will provide all staff necessary for the implementation of envisaged activities. CISP works in the area in partnership with local and International Humanitarian Organizations implementing emergency response projects and during the implementation will be applied the usual principles of the organization- coordination, complementarily, synergy and harmonization with other development actors operating in the area. Intervention kebeles of this project have been systematically selected in such a way that there will not be duplication of resources/efforts rather complementing with their interventions, for example the rehabilitation of the water systems will shorten the distance of water source not only for this project but also other partners (AAH and the government) working water trucking. GOAL and UNICEF are working on nutrition and water trucking in the operational areas (Teltele and Dire, respectively) for host communities and IDPs. Hence, our intervention complements the already existing interventions, and no activities will overlap activities of other partners. Particularly, the project will integrate with nutrition projects supplying water to health centres. In Ethiopia in general and in the targeted region in particular, emergency interventions are coordinating by different sectoral clusters. And these clusters using as coordination, collaboration, experience sharing and an advocacy platform among active CSOs and UN agencies in their respective sectors. All the emergency interventions in the zone are coordinated by the zonal emergency task force led by zone administration while WASH related activities will be coordinated by the zone water office. CISP is an active member of the zone emergency taskforce as well as of the federal and regional WASH clusters.

3.6 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members using age and gender appropriate, safe and confidential channels?

The project will consider the Feedback and Compliance Mechanism (FCM) as accountability for the affected populations (AAP) and the donor to deliver the project objectives to the best satisfaction of end beneficiaries/clients through client response programming approach (CRP). The CRP will systematically listen to the perspectives of our clients and use their feedback to make decisions and communicate and explain to our clients how their feedback has informed our actions. The mechanism involves people at every stage and has been developed in a participatory way with representatives from all stakeholder groups including the most vulnerable and excluded. This was done through involving the community in the process of assessments, program design, monitoring and evaluation and then set up complaints and response mechanisms including listening exercises; perceptions studies; community score cards; story-telling, mobile phone calls or text messaging (SMS), SOP and others. In case that a project receives complaints or feedback that fall outside our remit, we will have a system in place to refer the feedback onwards to someone who is in a position to deal with the issue (known as referral pathways). The mechanism will handle sensitive and non-sensitive complaints with the proper procedures to handle both types. The mechanism for handling complaints will also take into account specific contexts and safety to recognize potential dangers and risks to all stakeholders involved in each process, including staff, complainants, witnesses and others. The mechanisms will include ways to prevent harassment, injury or harm to those wishing to raise an issue or complaint. The system will ensure the confidentiality of program participants when managing their complaints. This will assure that any information given is restricted to a limited number of people on a need-to-know basis and is not disseminated more widely. Feedback and complaints will be handled objectively and with fairness. All users will be made aware of the procedures; so that they understand its purpose, have sufficient information on how to access it and understand how it works. It will also be accessible for all groups within the community, including the most vulnerable and those with disabilities; this will be done through multiple channels to ensure everyone can be reached. Staff will be trained to ensure that standards are met in receiving, processing, reviewing, ensure everyone can be reached. Staff will be trained to ensure that standards are met in receiving, processing, reviewing, responding and resolving complaints. The system will be guided by policy and procedures that set out the key characteristics of the mechanism, such as who is responsible for receiving and handling complaints, how feedback will be recorded, reviewed and actions communicated, who has access to what information. Response time. The policy or guidelines will also clearly articulate, or signpost the process for managing sensitive complaints. The FCM will also be complemented by post-distribution monitoring (PDM) to measure the clients' feedback and satisfaction on items distributed by the project.
