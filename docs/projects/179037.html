Project Summary

The project aims to improve the quality of the lives of children and adolescents affected by the current crisis in Syria, specifically in Idleb governorate (Idleb, Ariha, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Binnish districts). The project is built to be linked to HRP objectives by improving access to education services, the quality of the services provided and the systems that support these services. With a commitment to sustainability the response aims to complement existing systems rather than creating parallel ones. The project will contribute to providing the needed education requirement through the following activities:
•Rehabilitating non-functioning learning spaces;
•Supplying students and staff with student and teaching kits - an essential component in the effective teaching and learning process;
•Providing learning spaces with safety and security materials/equipment in order to protect students from dangers they may face in attending school therefore improving access to education;
•Enhancing professional development to teaching staff (active learning, self-learning, life skills).
•Providing incentives to education staff as well as they are an imperative component in the education process.
•Enhancing the knowledge and life skills of communities hence facilitating behavior change in the importance of education and increasing the school enrollment rate.
The response prioritizes the most disadvantaged population groups, in particular those in areas with high severity need and children that are disproportionately underserved. In particular, the focuses will be on out of school children (OOSC) in Idleb, Ariha, Jisr-Ash-Shugur, Binnish districts), to ensure that students continue to learn and improve the quality of the provided education.
This project will allow Nasaem to provide a complete program supporting education, as well as improving services provided and ensuring the longevity of the facilities. Child protection, child participation and gender issues will be mainstreamed in all project activities to increase the reach and enhance the impact of the project.
The recent global pandemic of coronavirus 2019 (Covid-19) has put additional stress on humanitarian response in general; the project will ensure the implementation of Covid-19 preventive measures (distribute soap, sanitizer and masks, as well as maintain a safe distance during project activities).

4 - Needs

Based on the HNO 2021, an estimated 6.9 million people (97 per cent children) need humanitarian education assistance. The sector is not able to provide a 2021 estimate of out of school children, however, in 2020, an estimated 2.45 million children were out of school and 1.6 million children were at risk of dropping out. Due to the cumulative effect of the conflict, the closure of learning facilities, and/or reduced learning hours due to COVID-19, worsening economic conditions, increased population numbers and displacement, the numbers of children out of school, and children who are at risk of dropping out, is almost certain to increase. As consequences of COVID 19 formal education will continue to have a limited absorption and retention capacity and be carried out in largely inadequate and at times unsafe, learning environments.In Idleb governorate, the devastating humanitarian situation and the absence of adequate support have impacted the education sector and loss of components of the educational process. This lack of support for the education sector has negative effects in the future and causes generations of children to lose their right to education, which may make them vulnerable to ignorance and delinquency or perhaps to be involved in the armed conflict in Syria for males. According to field surveys conducted by Nasaem Khair team, in coordination with Education directorate in Idleb, it is figured out the main problems with sending and keeping children (17-6 years) in school. The schools in the project locations are reaching their limit in terms of student capacity and their capability of providing adequate, well equipped, safe learning spaces.

5 - Implementation of activities

Output 1:Children and adolescents have access to protective learning spaces and learning materials, and recreational kits to facilitate an effective learning environment.Activities:1.1 Rehabilitate schools with gender-sensitive operating equipment and furniture.1.2 Provision of recreational kits for affected girls and boys.1.3 Provision of learning kits for boys and girls.1.4 Provision of teaching kits for boys and girls.1.5. Facilitate school transportation to and from hard-to-reach, insecure and distant areas.Output2:Teachers and school administration staff (women and men) trained and strengthened to effectively teach school age children (boys and girls).Activities:2.1 Provide trainings on teaching methodology, psycho social support, school management, EiE, INEE standards, and hygiene promotion.2.2 Support teachers and school administration staff (women and men) with basic monthly incentives.Output3:Host community persons on IDPs teachers, parents, children and communities (women, men, girls and boys) have stronger awareness of the importance of education and received life skill messages.Activities:3.1 Conduct emergency life-skills session for affected boys and girls, including life-saving hygiene and health messages (including drink clean water, use latrines and wash hands with soap in accordance with INEE standards. Hygiene messages will be tailored to the different needs of boys and girls.3.2 Deliver emergency life skills and importance of education (Not to lose generation) messages to IDPs and host community members through awareness-raising sessions, establishment of PTAs and Back-To-School.For the project, a Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) Framework will be developed.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: A breach of ethics. - Mitigation measure: Nasaem will train the staff on the code of conduct, child safeguarding, anti-harassment and prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policies and ask them to sign them. - Risk: Staff may find sensitive protection cases. Mitigation measure: Nasaem’s field staff will receive training on the referral of cases of children, women, the elderly and the disabled to the available service providers in the areas based on the updated service map.The project will priorities the most vulnerable persons (males and females including IDPs), considering the children situations and difficulties within these HHs (priorities to HHs including children with disabilities or chronic diseases), to receive services during this project.The project is designed to enable equal and impartial access through community consultation and participation across age, gender and diversity in how to reach the most vulnerable groups and not to exclude any groups and provide the identified services with dignity and impartiality.Awareness sessions will be conducted about strengthening self-protection mechanisms, rights' assisting, GBV services, increasing knowledge about woman and her main roles and rights.In addition, males will attend sessions about GBV risks to increase knowledge and awareness and to reduce following bad or negative coping strategies. Starting with the project's first needs assessments and during the whole project period, NKO will focus on using the "Do No Harm" approach to integrate conflict sensitivity wherever.The feedback (complaint) mechanisms will be in place and all beneficiaries must be aware of them.
