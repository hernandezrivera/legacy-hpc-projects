Project Summary

This project seeks to address malnutrition in a holistic manner by ensuring that the multiple deprivations that contribute to malnutrition are jointly addressed through integrated programming to ensure a more sustainable intervention. To achieve this, the program will implement both nutrition specific and nutrition sensitive interventions. The nutrition specific interventions that align with sector objective 2 will include; treatment of Severe Acute Malnutrition with and without medical complications in children under five and children 6-59 months respectively, Moderate Acute Malnutrition among children 6-59 months and Supplementary Feeding Program for PLW and other vulnerable persons as well as IYCF in Emergencies. In line with sector objective 1 on prevention, the program interventions will include a broader social behavior change communication encompassing maternal, infant and young child nutrition, promotion of optimal feeding and care practices through cooking demonstrations and a campaign-based Vitamin A and deworming supplementation in addition to provision of zinc tablets and iron folates as may be required. The WASH, Livelihood support, Health and Protection program interventions will be mainstreamed to ensure the other contributing factors to malnutrition such as sanitation, morbidity, food insecurity and protection elements that limit access and utilization of the services in the program are addressed. In summery, this project will target 11,610 caretakers of children 0-23 months with MIYCN counseling, 24,670 (12,335 boys, 12,335 girls) with Vitamin A supplementation, 20, 317 (10,158 boys, 10, 159 girls) with deworming interventions, SAM treatment for children below 5 years reaching 4660 (2330 boys, 2330 girls), MAM treatment for children under 5 reaching 10,590 (5,290 boys, 5,290 girls) and treat 6,960 pregnant and lactating women with acute malnutrition. All these will be done in 20 OTP/TSFP facilities and 1 SC in Nasir county.

1. Needs

The rates of Malnutrition in South Sudan, upper Nile and Nasir county in specific continue to erode the desired foundation needed for optimal maternal and child nutrition outcomes and health. Nasir is one of the counties in the 'malnutrition hotspot' greater Upper Nile where GAM rates remain above the 15% emergency threshold according to the WHO standards. The FSNMS survey conducted by FAO/WFP/GOSS and UNICEF between July and September 2023 indicated a GAM prevalence of 21.10% in Nasir. Previously in 2022, a SMART survey indicated a GAM rate of 15.9%. This shows potential deterioration of the nutrition situation in the county. The contributing factors to acute malnutrition in upper Nile remain high and with high impact on the resulting nutritional status for women and children. In order to achieve the desired nutrition outcomes in children such as improved survival, physical growth, cognitive development, good health, school readiness and performance; and improved nutrition outcomes in women such as good health and improved productivity, more must be done to tackle the immediate and underlying determinants of acute malnutrition. FSNMS round 29 shows that in Nasir, the food consumption indicators such as MDD, MMF and the composite indicator for frequency and diversity MAD are very poor; 21%, 34.6% and 16.1% respectively. Morbidity is a major contributing factor as well with Malaria prevalence at 39.4% and acute respiratory infections at 19.9%. The sanitation situation in the county is also very poor with access to improved sanitation facilities standing at only 4.90% and only 21.5% being able to access drinking water from an improved water source; consequently, diarrhea is at 21.7%. Food insecurity as a result of poor harvests and inadequate support to produce due to insecurity and issues around clan revenge and child abduction threats have ensured many households have little food reserves. The county is highly reliant on imported goods and services that come in through the Ethiopian boarder; however, these products are beyond reach of most households as market prices continue to soar even as the value of the South Sudanese Pound continue to plummet. Currently, the locals in Nasir can only trade in Ethiopian Birr. The operational environment has made it difficult to tackle the determinants of malnutrition in the county effectively. The county is vast and difficult to access with only 8 out of the current 20 nutrition facilities being accessible through the river, a task that also remains ardous. Movement from the base in Mandeng to Kier which is one of the 8 facilities along the river can take upwards of 7 hrs on a speedboat. Supervision in the 12 inland areas are equally daunting as they involve walking around for long hours. While all these challenges are being tackled, inadequate funding has not enabled the project team to conduct as many outreach services as should be the case because the staffing is also lean. Proximity of the county to Ethiopia has seen it register many returnees from Ethiopia and some from Sudan. In terms of social protections services, Nasir does not have any Cash Assistance services, BSFP is minimal but GFD is intermittently provided. The care and feeding practices are also not optimal since Exclusive breastfeeding rate is only approximately 39.3% according to the recent FSNMS round 29; this is way below the World Health Assembly Target of at least 50% by 2025. The nutrition sensitive interventions such as heath, WASH and Food Security and Livelihood are fragile and need further support. Through an integrated approach, it will be possible to tackle the immediate and underlying causes/determinants of malnutrition while also coordinating with other stakeholders and strengthening the leadership for health and nutrition intervention at the county health department. The project will seek to address inequalities by supporting boys and girls, women, IDPs, Returnees and host population.
