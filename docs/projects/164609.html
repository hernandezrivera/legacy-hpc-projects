Project Summary

This project will support 1500 farmer households with agricultural inputs and technical support for wheat crop planting. This support will improve bread production (the staple food for Syrian people) which has been negatively affected by the lack of wheat and high prices of production supplies.
The project will strength local production capacity and local markets, therefore, the self-reliance of the community to be stable against external conflicts. Also, the project will contribute in enhancing the quality of production as we will support farmers by high-quality seeds and all needed agricultural inputs (fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides, irrigation fuel), in addition to technical support through training and monitoring visits to farmers’ fields and providing guidance advices in order to have highest and best production.
This project will also support 3000 vulnerable households including female-headed and child-headed households living in peri-urban and rural areas to produce their own nutritious food through distributing vaccinated chicken and chicken feed to 2000 families, and distributing sheep and fodder to 1000 families, in addition to supporting them with the required knowledge and basic advices to achieve the best possible production of eggs, milk and meat.
This will strengthen and re-establish productive small backyard chicken and sheep flocks to support local food production and to improve household nutrition, especially for children and pregnant women, and to open up additional sources of income for rural and peri-urban women through small-scale marketing of eggs, milk, and meat.

4 - Needs

HNO 2020 states that 7.9 million people in Syria are food insecure and an additional 1.9 million people are at risk of food insecurity. Under the sector’s objective related to agriculture and livelihoods, the sector aims to reach 1.5 million households (7.5 million people) out of the total number of people in need (9.8 million). Appropriate agricultural inputs (cereal as well as vegetable production) will be provided along with technical capacity-building targeting 1.2 million households to ensure that the most vulnerable households receive timely inputs as per the seasonal calendar to be able to produce and contribute to domestic production requirements. Out of this, an estimated 300,000 of the most vulnerable farming households will receive protection food rations so that they are able to protect their assets up to harvesting thus contributing to the sector’s integration strategy. The sector will also target 500,000 households with Support to small-scale food production (horticulture, poultry-egg laying hens, market gardens). A further 1 million households will be targeted for asset-building and asset-protection, specifically for livestock keepers. Out of this 600,000 households will be targeted with emergency livestock treatment and training for veterinary services (including community animal health workers). Another 300,000 households will be targeted with livelihood support programmes including vocational trainings and income generation related to small businesses and enterprises. Local procurement will be encouraged to create demand, where markets have sufficient capacity. With 1.9 million individuals at risk of food insecurity, it is vital to restore and create livelihood opportunities for households to reduce use of negative coping mechanism in order to meet their daily needs. The sector’s second objective aims to restore and strengthen productive assets and create livelihood opportunities for the targeted populations, thus contributing to the overall food

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

- Protection risk: The risks of GBV can happen because of overcrowding in distribution points, or may happen after distributing while returning home, which may lead to harm some women and may cause domestic violence. - Likelihood: Medium. - Impact: High. - Mitigating measures: 1- We will not distribute in a late time of the day. 2- We will not choose distribution points far from the residential complex, also we will distribute in each village separately. 3- During distribution, we will divide the location into two sides, one side for women and the other for men. 4- Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, with two phone numbers: one for men and other for women. - Protection risk: The risks of exploitation of beneficiaries by the field workers. - Likelihood: weak. - Impact: High. - Mitigating measures: 1- We will choose the field team carefully, who have high behavioral qualities. 2- Establish a confidential complaint mechanism, with two phone numbers: one for men and other for women
