Project Summary

The World Relief Sudan (WRS) integrated project is planned to be implemented in five localities of Central Darfur (Azum, Golo, Nertiti, Rokero, Umdukun) & four localities of West Darfur (Geneina, Kerenik, Kulbus, Sirba). The project mainly focuses on providing lifesaving health & nutrition, food security & access to sustainable WASH services to reduce the morbidity & mortality among, IDPs and conflict-affected people.
WRS has been operational in Sudan, mainly in West Darfur, since 2004, responding to the severe food insecurity and humanitarian crisis. WRS is currently providing interventions in health and nutrition, FSL, peacebuilding and WASH activities in West Darfur. In 2015, WRS expanded its integrated humanitarian emergency response, recovery and development programs to Central Darfur State in the localities of Azum, Nertiti, Golo & Rokoro and is operating health, nutrition, WASH, & FSL in those areas. Recently the organization further expanded its operation to Umdukun to implement WASH activities through approach of “locality wide wash intervention.”
Food Security and Livelihoods activities are designed to enhance the nutrition & food security of households through increased access to food through agriculture or enhanced purchasing power. In the medium and long-term, they are designed to enhance the resilience of households to external shocks and jumpstart livelihoods activities and increase HH self-sufficiency.
To increase access to lifesaving primary health care services and reduce morbidity and mortality from treatable ailments, WRS plans to support 17 health facilities. Services include providing outpatient services, consultations & treatments, EPI with routine immunization against all national target diseases, cold chain support, & antenatal & post-natal care with SMoH to provide overall operation & management of the health facilities.
To address high levels of malnutrition and increase community access to nutrition services, WRS plans to rehabilitate, equip & provide nutrition supplies to 35 nutrition centers (OTP/TSFP). Services include providing treatment to SAM & MAM cases, nutritional assessment, growth monitoring, community outreach with IYCF messages for mothers and other caretakers.
RRR sector mainly focuses in high returnee areas employing integrated sectoral approach to address their basic needs related protection, food, health & water supply. The project targets Rokero, Golo, & Sirba localities. This sector mainly focuses on durable solutions that help conflict affected people resume their livelihoods in a sustainable manner.
WASH objectives focus on three priority areas. The first priority area is enhancing community access to potable water sources through construction of new water sources, upgrading existing water systems & rehabilitating existing water points. The second focus area is the promotion of sanitation through a community-based approach. The third focus area is ensuring the sustainability of WASH infrastructure.
All interventions will be done in partnership with line ministries, community-based committees, local authorities, community leaders & beneficiaries themselves. As described below, interventions are designed to meet immediate emergency needs of the beneficiaries mainly women, girls & children while employing approaches that increase community ownership & thus sustainability.
WR cash based component focuses on CFW program. This will be implemented in all our proposed localities mainly targeting returnees & vulnerable communities. The number of working days & months vary depending on the type of infrastructure work that we will be doing in the particular area. The CFW mainly done during the months of December to February where the local food price is relatively available & cheaper. Cash will be directly paid to the beneficiaries on monthly basis based on person days completed by the beneficiaries & agreed work norms. Market survey will be conducted to set wage rate.

Needs

In preparing this project, WRS conducted rapid assessments in all areas and KAP and SMART surveys, assessed data from existing project sites, & consulted secondary source reports. FSL: WRS’s baseline survey in July 2018 in 6 IDP camps in West Darfur shows that 71% of respondents earn a monthly income of 700 SDG or below. It also revealed negative coping mechanisms including borrowing from relatives and friends, taking children out of school & reducing expenditures on food by especially during the hunger gap months. Challenges related to agriculture in both areas include irregular rainfall, lack of quality seeds, and inability to access farmlands due to conflict. Few other livelihoods are available, and linkages to markets remain weak. Youth without viable economic options are at higher risk of conscription violence. Health: Data from WRS’s health facilities in Central Darfur shows that ARIs (33.4%), followed by malaria (28.2%) and diarrhea diseases (20.6%) are leading causes of morbidity. Data from West Darfur mimics these trends. Both areas witness frequent epidemics of communicable diseases such as meningitis, measles, whooping cough, and yellow fever & were impacted by the outbreak of AWD in 2017-2018. Reproductive healthcare is also of major concern in both states. In Central Darfur, only about 65% of women have access to skilled care during childbirth. Due to low access to health facilities in some places women and children are victims of GBV while traveling longer distance. In this regard mobile clinics will be established to areas with hard to access to ensure protection of beneficiaries. Nutrition: A SMART survey conducted by WRS in Rokero in April 2018 revealed a GAM rate of 20.2% which includes a SAM rate of 4.6% and a MAM rate of 15.6%, above WHO emergency thresholds. A household survey conducted by WRS in Jebel Marra areas revealed that only 13.6% of the surveyed mothers practice EBF, and only 29% of the lactating mothers’ breastfeed the infants for 1 year. RRR: This sector mainly focus on integrated sectors to address the needs of returnees in Golo, Rokero and Sirba localities. WASH: In, Central Darfur rapid assessments revealed a daily water supply is less than two L/P/D. More than 60% of hand pumps are not functioning, and less than 5% of households treat water taken from unprotected sources. Although 97% in one locality reported washing their hands after defecation, only 57% reported using soap. In West Darfur, problems include animals sharing water points with humans, decrease of borehole yields due to climate variability, and poor operation and maintenance of water facilities all causing overcrowding of existing water points. Only 20% of the population have traditional latrines, while 80% of the HHs practice open defecation. In both areas, poor access to water and sanitation facilities increased the risk of GBV for women and girls.

Activities

Activities are designed to address the most critical needs presented above to save lives & enhance resilience. Gender & protection are mainstreamed throughout, and activities are implemented in accordance with national guidelines and in collaboration with relevant authorities and line ministries. FSL: Support farming HH with seeds, tools & training; Support pastoralist households with access to animals and animal vaccination, treatment and access to water; CFW activities to repair roads and boost social service infrastructure; Establishment of village saving & loan associations; Vocational & business skills training for women and youth; Support tree nurseries. Health: Support staff, running costs, and drug supply management at 17 health facilities; Mobilize and train CHPs; Enhance reproductive healthcare services; Provide curative consultations; Conduct communicable disease surveillance, work with UN agencies, MoH, local & international NGO. In this project emphasis will be given to ensure that vulnerable including children & women are protected and served with dignity. Nutrition: TSFP & OTP services in 35 sites including screening, admission, treatment, and follow-up for children under five & PLWs; IYCF services including mobilization of CNVs, Mothers’ Support Groups, & promotion of local foods. RRR: Focuses on durable solutions, including distribute agri inputs, capacity building in agriculture, livestock treatment and vaccination, procurement of veterinary vaccines & drugs, rehabilitate & construct hand dug well, provide vocational skill training, construct local shelters, distribute NFIs, construct water supplies, & etc. WASH: Rehabilitation and upgrading of water points; Support to WMCs; Water quality monitoring and treatment; WASH in schools; Hygiene and handwashing promotion; Latrine construction; Community clean-up campaigns and vector control activities; integration of water tariff systems; integrated water resource management activities.

Impact on environment/natural resources

The environmental marker guidelines were reviewed to ensure that the project is designed to minimize any potential negative environmental impact, & mitigation measures will be implemented throughout. To ensure environmental compliance &adhere to no-do-harm principles, WRS create awareness on environmental factors in each sector &integrate environmental messages in outreach &community campaigns. In the FSL sector, WRS is supporting tree nurseries, &the seedlings produced will be planted in the project operational areas including at health &nutrition facilities. Beneficiaries of agriculture activities are trained on improved farming techniques with emphasis on conservation agriculture, irrigation management, & organic pest management. Health personnel will be trained on management &disposal of bio-hazard materials &WRS will also work with stakeholders on the proper use of the existing incinerators for safe disposal of biomedical to minimize environmental impacts. Nutrition beneficiaries will be encouraged to bring used rations packets back to facilities for monitoring &proper disposal. RRR As part of WASH activities, trained community members will further create awareness to the beneficiary communities on efficient use of water resources while Water Management Committees will monitor ground water &take joint measures to enhance the recharge of ground water. Where possible, WRS installs solar energy at water points, which is more environmentally friendly, cost effective &efficient than diesel energy. Additional inputs for health Coordination with other stakeholders: WRS will work closely together with MoH, WHO, UNICEF &national or INGOs health stakeholders at all levels during the course of implementation. Protection mainstreaming: will be mainstreamed across this project, locations of health centers are managed as safe spaces that are centrally located &accessible to large numbers of the population; hence to prevent long distances which are risky to women &girls for GBV. All project staff will be sensitized on the risks facing many of the women accessing services, as well as children, such as gender-based violence or child abuse. Environment Protection: The project intends to mitigate environmental effects from medical waste through routine collection &proper disposal of health facilities’ waste using existing &newly constructed incinerators. The project will also train health care workers on safe handling of biohazard material &disposal of biomedical waste. Gender mainstreaming: Key to inclusive project implementation is that gender has been considered during staff recruitment &all efforts are in place to ensure equity during recruitment &capacity development. Home visits conducted by CHPs also will ensure that women &girls, who are generally more confined to the home due to greater responsibilities of household chores can access messaging &services. Both female &male beneficiaries will engaged on gender sensitivity training.
