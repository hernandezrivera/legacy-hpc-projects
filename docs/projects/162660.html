Project Summary

The project will aim at building livelihood opportunities, improving economic self-reliance, reducing food insecurity and vulnerability in a sustainable living environment in West and Central Darfur.
Regarding agricultural inputs, this project aims at improving access to good quality and improved seeds, tools, water access for irrigation as well as trainings to strengthen the skills of community members in order to empower the targeted populations. Moreover, vulnerable HH unable to cultivate land (either because of specific vulnerabilities or because of a lack of access to land) will benefit from home gardening activities. During the winter season, vegetable seeds will be distributed to women farmers. This activity, in addition to training, will provide an alternative and complementary source of food for families as well as a source of income to improve household food security. Besides the vegetable production, it will improve vegetables’ consumption at household level, especially towards children. In addition, this support will extend the production season and increase household access to diversified food sources
The other long-term impacts of the project are linked to the capacity building of community. Trainings on best agricultural practices will be delivered by TGH to the targeted beneficiaries.
In terms of livelihood, TGH will introduce new and durable opportunities, mainly focusing on strengthening communities’ livelihoods. The development of income generating activities (IGAs) aims at creating new and sustainable sources of income. TGH will support IGAs from existing or new initiatives, promoted by vulnerable individuals and women, through material support (equipment required for the IGA) as well as through the conduct of technical and vocational training. The identification of women and/ or vulnerable people will be made during focus group discussions, including community leaders, on the basis of vulnerability criteria, but also on the basis of proposed activities and capacities to implement IGAs. Moreover, the development of saving groups among communities will enhance the sustainability of those IGAs. Environmental sensitization will be integrated in the project’s activities through capacity building and trainings. These actions will allow beneficiaries to provide their households with food and, on the longer-term, to provide them with an income that will help reducing the dependency on humanitarian assistance.
Moreover, in order to protect the environment and resolve conflicts between farmers and breeders, TGH will implement reforestation activities along pasture roads, while sensitizing communities on environmental issues.
In Central Darfur, the project will target 2.050 beneficiaries in Mukjar locality, 12.500 in Golo locality, 500 beneficiaries in Bindizi locality and 2.000 in Um Dukhun locality. Regarding West Darfur, the project will target 2.000 beneficiaries in Geneina locality and 4.000 beneficiaries in Kreinik locality.

1 - Needs

Despite average rainfalls in most areas of Sudan in 2018, the economic crisis negatively impacted the food security (reduced crop production due to high fuel and input prices, lack of cash limiting the possibility to use daily workers for farm works) and market availability of staples remained low. In August 2019, there were an estimated 5.8 million individuals experiencing Crisis or worse level of food insecurity (IPC Phase 3 and above). The rapid deteriorating economic situation, conflict and natural shocks in the country had an overall impact on the agricultural season and the food security of all. The economic crisis has indeed driven more people into poverty (see figures after) putting at stake their food security. Out of the 6.2 million people in need, 4.7 million will be targeted under the FSL sector in 2020, an increase from 4.4 million compared to 2019 (HRP FSL Sector 2020). In conflict and displacement areas, most vulnerable households, including newly displaced people, continue to face critical food gaps. In March 2019, WFP estimated that more than 58 percent of the country’s population may not be able to afford the price of the local food basket and was facing continued food access challenges. The highest rates are reported in the Darfur States, which means that households’ have financial difficulties in accessing food, therefore likely resulting in poor food consumption. To cope with the lack of food or money to buy food, approximately 40 percent of IDPs and refugees resort to livelihoods related coping strategies. The economic crisis that is currently hitting Sudan as well as the removal of wheat subsidies in the 2018 government budget are the main reasons that led to a huge increase in agricultural prices. According to FAO’s Global Information and Early Warning System (GIEWS), in May 2019 retail prices of sorghum were at near record levels in most markets, and between 55 and 85 percent above the already high levels of last year. In July 2019, prices of millet grains were 15 to 25 percent higher than in 2018. This situation also led to a significant increase in the price of local food basket, approximately 58 percent of households cannot afford a local food basket due to limited purchasing power resulting from inflation and high food prices. The most important rise was observed in Um Dukhun and Mukjar (CD), where prices were 147 percent higher in May 2018 relative to the same time last year. Rapidly increasing prices were an important factor underlying the observed increase in the level of food insecurity as the HH were unable to afford the local basket food. In Um Dukhun, and Mukjar (main areas of TGH intervention), 59 percent of households (from WFP Monitoring sample) were found to be food insecure, which represents a 13 percent rise from May 2017. West Darfur is also an area particularly prone to such interventions, and more specifically the localities of Geneina, Kreinik and Beida, which are characterized by an overwhelming presence of protracted IDPs, most often settled for more than 10 years now, in addition to what recent influx of returnees from Chad have been observed. According to the IPC, IDPs are considered the most affected by food insecurity while also exerting pressure on host communities’ livelihoods. IDPs are characterized by a large proportion of women, children and elderly, since many men were involved in fighting and have been victims of the conflict. In Darfur, the women are in charge of the agricultural work, labor that represents their major source of income. They are also in charge of cooking for the whole family. Cultivation areas are often far from housings. In spite of security constraints, some individuals nevertheless continue to walk long distances on a daily basis to cultivate, including women, young girls and boys. This puts them at risk of GBV Concerning the environment concern, its protection has not been a priority for populations of WD and CD.

5.1 - Impact on environment/natural resources

In the communities targeted by this project, most of the population is entirely dependent on agro-pastoral activities. Also, the deterioration of the environment (reduction of water, forest cover, grazing land) contributes to the fragility and degradation of the livelihoods of households in the area as well as to increased tensions between farmers and herders. The environmental issues identified are related to the reduction of the majority of forest cover and grazing lands near and moderately close to villages. Over the course of successive conflicts, these resources have been overused by the population due to a lack of alternatives (fuelwood, shelter construction, animal feed, urban construction). Also, the available grazing land for ruminants has been drastically reduced as a result of the separation between Sudan and South-Sudan, resulting in the loss of winter grazing land now owned by South-Sudan. This reduction has contributed to the accumulation of animals on small areas and thus to overgrazing. Finally, climate change and the increase in desert areas have contributed to a reduction in the presence of trees and shrubs. The objective of this project will therefore be to contribute to reforestation. This project will also improve knowledge on how to disseminate varieties of sorghum and millet adapted to the constraints of the environment in rural areas: a variety with a short cycle that is more resistant to drought, a variety that develops an important plant cover incompatible with weed emergence, a variety that develops plant outgrowths at the heart of the ears of cobs, and which slows down the damage caused by birds at seed ripening stages.

6 - Protection mainstreaming

3=Yes, room for improvement

6.1 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Through the organization of fairs, instead of direct distribution, TGH ensures that the beneficiaries can select the items they really need, without deciding for them. This allows the beneficiaries to restore their dignity and enhance their resilience. Throughout the project, TGH will pay attention to protection mainstreaming regarding youth and women. Indeed, some activities, such as the pasture restoration one, present some risks for women. Therefore, they will not be targeted to take part to this activity. On the opposite, young adults face high rate of unemployment in Darfur States. They will be targeted on priority to participate to the cash-for-work activity for the reforestation activity. Furthermore, in the hereby presented project, TGH will include people with disabilities in the activities' implementation. Indeed, people with disabilities will be identified to receive agricultural inputs and trainings.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

TGH puts in place mechanisms to enable beneficiaries and affected populations to provide feedback and complaints. At the beginning of the project implementation, TGH will organize a meeting with leaders and other relevant stakeholders to present the activities and explain that community complaints and advices are necessary to the action’s improvement. TGH will present to the beneficiaries the different ways to communicate their issues (special complains during distribution, direct contact with TGH staff, visit to TGH office, etc.). When a complaint is addressed to TGH, it is acknowledged by the sector managers who provide the necessary information to the affected population to ensure their comprehension of TGH operations, to address answers to beneficiaries concerns and complaints prioritization criteria and operational or budget limitations. According to the relevance of the concerns addressed by the community to the Base Managers and Program team in the field, these can also be analyzed in further details and considered in activities planning. While explaining the complaint process, TGH team will mention GBV and SEA and highlight the importance to raise any concerns regarding these topics. Transparency is ensured through adequate information sharing and communication to beneficiaries and affected populations have already been mentioned above and include meetings and specific complaint mechanisms and responses with affected populations, Sheikhs or other community leaders and more importantly, availability of TGH staff.
