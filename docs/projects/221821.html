Project Summary

Since 2027, Northern Mozambique has been affected by a protracted armed conflict, characterized by violence and insecurity, destruction of civilian infrastructure, disruption of local economies, loss of livelihoods, violations of human rights and abuses to the civilian population, resulting in killings, large scale displacement, family separations, forced recruitments and use of children in hostilities, abductions and conflict-related sexual violence. The conflict is complex and multi-dimensional, with several endogenous and exogenous factors and dynamics contributing to its persistence.
In Cabo Delgado Province, the armed conflict has disrupted the lives and livelihoods of up to 1.28 million Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), many of whom have been displaced multiple times, forcing them to abandon their belongings, assets, livelihoods and means of survival at each displacement. Despite continued fighting, according to the DTM , there are a total of 716,878 IDPs still living in resettlement sites, displacement camps or with host communities, while more than 610,00 have returned to their areas of origin.
The conflict has also enhanced food insecurity and malnutrition, as agricultural activities have been abandoned, contributing to nearly 875,000 people in IPC phase 3 and above. Northern Mozambique is also prone to recurrent climatic shocks (cyclone, floods) which further affect already vulnerable households, and the forecasted La Niña climatic phenomenon may further affect vulnerable communities’ ability to recover their livelihood and access social services.
Both IDPs and returnees are faced with severely disrupted livelihoods, limited public essential services and infrastructure, and require tailored support to meet their basic needs, restore their livelihoods for self-reliance, ultimately improving their food security.
Currently, FAO supports IDPs, host communities and returnees with emergency agriculture, livestock and fisheries inputs, contributing to restore their lost assets, and allow vulnerable households to produce food for themselves and to sell to others, ultimately reducing their dependence from aid interventions, increasing food availability and restoring their livelihoods. FAO also provides technical assistance to farmers, herders and fisherfolks, helping them to improve their nutrition, production and productivity through training programs, increasing access to extension services, and animal vaccinations, bringing together different stakeholders (local government technicians, producers and private sector) to build synergies for sustainable interventions.
Withing the Humanitarian Response Plan 2025, FAO aims at supporting 343,196 IDPs, host community members and returnees in the 17 target districts of Cabo Delgado, to rebuild and restore agriculture-based livelihoods (crops, livestock, and fisheries) and improve households' food and nutrition security.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Withing the Mozambique Humanitarian Response Plan 2025, FAO aims at targeting, in line with the Food Security Cluster strategy, 343,196 people in need - IDPs, host community members and returnees - to rebuild their agriculture-based livelihoods (crops, livestock and fisheries) and to improve households' food security and nutrition.Planned agriculture interventions will consist in the distribution of different emergency agriculture kits and technical support packages. The agricultural production kit will be composed of key staple and cash crops for the main agriculture season (mainly, but not limited to, maize, sesame, cowpeas) and tools , while the fresh season kit will focus on the production of vegetables through distribution of an assorted list of horticulture seeds and tools. FAO will also continue to support the reactivation of local seed systems through local seed multiplication of adapted varieties and promotion and support to existing seed banks as a strategy to create local capacity to respond to localized incidents caused by floods or by droughts, reduce dependency on the external seeds, and to rebuild the local seed market system in the province. Livestock interventions will consist in distribution of backyard poultry and small ruminants’ kits, provision of essential vaccinations (such as for poultry Newcastle disease) essential treatments such as deworming, alongside trainings and tools to support community veterinary extension services.Fisheries interventions consist in the distribution of different fishing gears tailored to the specific coastal areas (line fish gears, octopus fishing kits, household level cold chain tools, etc.), as well as strengthening of the CCP (Fishers Community Council) and technical assistance to fish processing and conservation.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Provision of agriculture inputs to plant at least 0.5 ha of land, to restore IDPs, host communities and returnee households’ crop and horticulture production. Livestock interventions will consist in the distribution of 5 poultry per household (1 male, 4 females), support for vaccination campaigns to prevent diseases on chicken and cattle and rabies and improved feeding.Fisheries interventions will consist of distribution of fishing gears, provision of equipment for fish conservation and marketing to reduce post-fishing losses, restoration of fisheries management system - electronic registration of fisherfolks, vessels and gears; revitalization of co-management system, with focus on the restructuring of the Fisheries Community Councils (CCP).The implementation of the plan will be supported by an complementary training program targeting the technical staff and the beneficiaries in the areas of (i) agriculture - post-harvest techniques to reduce and prevent post-harvest losses and for value addition; local seed multiplication to increases the availability of good quality seeds, Integrated Pest Management (IPM) to prevent losses and production of safe food, Good Agricultural Practices (GAP), Climate-Smart Agriculture (CSA) Practices, water conservation and management; (ii) livestock - livestock basic care, animal husbandry, training of community vaccinators, preparation of supplementary feeding for cattle: (iii) fisheries - on fish handling and food safety, fish processing and marketing, gear assembling and trainings to respond to different needs of the Fisheries Community Councils (CCP); and (iv) preparedness – training of government officials and community-based organizations on early warning systems, anticipatory actions, preparedness to respond to climate induced hazards aiming to empower communities to prevent and act in a timely and appropriate manner for effective adaptation and response.To maximize results, a monitoring end evaluation system and knowledge sharing system will be implemented. This system will be composed by the local communities that will be involved in the dissemination of the project activities, selection of the beneficiaries, post-distribution monitoring, organization of field days for knowledge sharing, monitoring of project results at farmers level and the participatory evaluation of the project to the effectiveness and outcome of the interventions, as well as the beneficiaries’ level of satisfaction results.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Through coordination with other key stakeholders in the agriculture and fisheries sectors, the dissemination of key information and by taking a leadership role in agriculture/fisheries livelihoods response, FAO's flexible programming will be applied to ensure interventions are flexible and agile, implemented with a participatory and inclusive approach, within the Do No Harm approach and through a conflict sensitive programming lens, aiming at leaving no one behind. A conflict-sensitive approach that considers the community as a whole, mitigating any potential tensions between recipients of humanitarian assistance and other community members, will be implemented throughout the project cycle. A results-based Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) system will be established and will be used as a continuous, important management tool, producing evidence to inform partners/donor, for learning and advocacy, as well as recommendations to timely reach the objectives and re-orient the project strategy, activities and targetsIn line with the Humanitarian Country Team Protection Strategy of June 2023, FAO’s interventions under the Humanitarian Response Plan will contribute to the achievement of the strategic objectives toward the Centrality of Protection, including promoting awareness of the community on risks associated with explosive hazards, and disseminating information on the referral pathways for access to protection services.Sensitization will be given during the process of targeting about Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) mechanisms: i) Linha Verde 1458, a toll free inter-agency/multisectoral hotline accessible 7 days a week for any type of issue (request of information, complaint, etc.); ii) the dissemination of posters and pamphlets and sensitization about Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) every time communities are gathered for any type of activities; iii) support given to community committees, to enhance communities' awareness about protection in general, PSEA in particular, and to be a platform for eventual complaints and feedback mechanism. FAO will make all stakeholders and project partners aware of and commit to FAO policies and guidelines on PSEA. These principles will guide their engagement in project implementation. FAO will also employ conflict-sensitive and prevention-oriented approaches though the project cycle.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The rationale of the planned intervention is to target affected populations who have lost their agriculture, livestock and fisheries assets and livelihoods, and consequently lost their capacity of food production because of displacement and as an important constraint when they return to their areas of origin. The intervention will be people-centered and context-specific for a high level of ownership, human resources’ strengthening and sustainability. Gender integration is a fundamental part of FAO programmes. Gender-related inequalities will be addressed by specifically targeting women in the activities where they excel, identifying their specific needs and how to address them. In line with the Government of Mozambique’s Gender Strategy and Action Plan for the Agrarian Sector, this project will reinforce the direct link between agricultural development and the role of women as key actors in the agricultural (including fisheries) sector. The project will emphasize and enhance women’s access to productive assets to increase their economic power/self-reliance and will ensure women’s active participation in decision making processes (from beneficiaries selection, to inputs distribution, access to training, to project’s assessment). For example, while women are not directly involved in boat fishing, they are numerous in octopus capture and seashell collection, and are particularly engaged in their processing and marketing. In Mozambique, women bear the bulk of the responsibility in providing for the food and nutrition needs of the family including care and feeding of the children. Therefore, increased access to vegetable seeds and fisheries inputs will specifically target and prioritize women wherever possible. The project will adopt a gender transformative approach, with a gender analysis conducted during inception (focus groups) to identify specific needs, to assess specific outcomes and impact, while gender disaggregated data will be continuously collected/monitored during the implementation of the project. Overall, the intervention aims at strengthening women’s role in production and income generation, without creating tension within the household.FAO will ensure that the targeted communities, including vulnerable population groups, are provided with appropriate, accessible and timely information on the type of assistance proposed by the project, and will have the opportunity to contribute to the activities design and review. All eligibility criteria will be presented, discussed, and eventually reviewed before being validated and applied by all stakeholders.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

While the project does not primarily target People with Disabilities (PwD), they will be prioritized during the beneficiary selection process, including ensuring their participation in the decision-making process and post implementation monitoring phase.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The opening of new production blocks (machambas) in IDPs settlements and relocation areas in host districts for crops production can contribute to deforestation and erosion. FAO has expertise on Agroforestry and will work on the integration of agroforestry activities with emergency agriculture interventions. Moreover, FAO will continue to promote the use of biological pesticides, integrated pest management, and organic fertilizers (composting) to reduce post-harvest losses, impact of pests on yields, and reduce the environmental footprint.
