Project Summary

Silk Road proposes 10 months integrated and direct WASH response to continue and expand its humanitarian services to the vulnerable population in different communities in NWS. The project interventions will target 210990 individuals (59% are IDPs) for WASH. The project aims to promote principled access to basic WASH services to all people including vulnerable groups at risk, accountability to affected people ensuring that services are delivered in a gender-sensitive manner and will consider the needs of different gender and age groups, people living with disabilities, etc.
WASH activities will include Rehabilitation and operation of both drinking water and sanitation/sewer systems at the community level which will provide comprehensive and sustainable solutions to the unfortunate reality they are suffering from. Silk Road will ensure providing adequate drinking water via water operation of the rehabilitated water systems (35 up to 40 litres per capita per day) while constantly assuring the quality of the water through chlorination and tests. Also, Silk Road aims to implement waste management activities (Solid waste removal and rubbish bins distribution and support municipality system) daily beside plus providing hygiene promotion educational sessions and use of IEC materials on handwashing with soap or ash at critical times, domestic hygiene, safe excreta/solid disposal, water protection and waterborne diseases, risks and impacts of Covid 19 and ways to limit its spread and change the wrong and unhealthy hygienic practices through a door to door visits, and provision of the needed personal items that will protect people and enhance their personal hygiene (hygiene kits). During the period of the operation of the water systems, a cost recovery system will be established and tested in the last two months direct supervision of Silk Road to ensure continuous cash flow required for running the systems during and after the project (Only the last two months of the project will be supported 70 % while the remaining cost will be secured by local councils), Silk Road in coordination with local councils and communities will study the suitable tariff rate that takes into account the dwindling financial capacity of the benefiting population and will be based on focus group discussions with a representative group of the community including members of local councils of targeted communities.
The proposed rehabilitation of infrastructure in this proposal will provide the best solutions to the suffering population ( host and IDPs together) because this activity will ensure that the provision of water through the rehabilitated network to everyone will be fair and there will be no discrimination against the bene􀂦ciaries. Also, we will ensure that they do not create additional risks for the affected population, also Silk Road will ensure that the health impact in this emergency WASH intervention will reduce disease risk and reduced transmission risk and it adhering to internationally recognized standards and best practices, ensuring that services are provided in line with the Do No Harm framework, Sphere standards, and humanitarian principles and that services are of the highest quality.
By implementing these main WASH activities, will be the best solution for the current situation and in this context that can allow existed IDPs and hosts to have access to safe water through restored water systems to reduce vulnerability to preventable illnesses and improve resilience and IDPs and other vulnerable groups (disabled, elderly) have access to life-saving assistance to reduce the risk of WASH-related morbidity and mortality. Within the current situation of the targeted people in the communities and the scope of the intervention, the treatment of the pumped water will be the most effective method to secure access to safe water in sufficient quantity and quality and can ensure equitable and unrestricted access to all members of the community.

4 - Needs

In north-west Syria, the need for fuel is highest regionally, as is the need for safe water (26 %). Safe water is particularly important among very vulnerable households (36 per cent) and female-headed households (33 %). According to the multi need assessment conducted by Silk Road, the Key Findings of WASH: The primary source of water drinking is mainly closed well (37%) and water trucking (53%), others receive through water trucking, (10%) Network one time each week for 3 hours through a private well related to the local council. 73% of the interviewed KIIs mentioned that women and men can’t access clean and safe water; the most challenging of accessing for both women and men are the high price of water and the lack of household water storage capacity.On the community level, 58% of people suffer from a lack of a sufficient amount of water and 42% of them have enough water for their various uses. KIs indicated 73% of IDPs don’t have a sufficient amount of water to cover their daily consumption.Hygiene and Sanitation gap, we found two negative coping strategies:1- People reduce water consumption, or they spending money on other things to supply drinking water,2- People modify hygiene practices such as bathing less and washing clothes.Regarding garbage collection system, there is a critical need in most communities to support the municipalities and waste management systems through rehabilitation and maintenance of the tractors, equipment and vehicles for waste collection, provide needed diesel fuel and support the daily wages of workers beside distribution of waste metal bins to reduce the prevalence of epidemics and diseases due to the suspension of the garbage removing in many villages and neighbourhoods.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1- Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV (Likelihood high- Impact high). Mitigating Measure:Promote Establishment of the adequate management plan; Promote involvement /engagement with communities in distribution planning and feedback from different groups on the package for improvement; training of staff and enforcement of code of PSEA code of conduct among partner staff.2- Distribution of expired unhealthy items (Likelihood medium - Impact high).Mitigating Measure:Establish and monitor expiry date of supplies prepositioned in the warehouse, ensure relevant storage provision and carry out timely disposal of damaged/expired items. Strengthen end-user monitoring.3-Quality and/or quantity not adequate as per humanitarian standards (Likelihood high- Impact high).Mitigating Measure:Ensure provision of safe water as per country Water Quality standards where possible and establish regular water quality monitoring including water source, water trucks and end-user samples; Collect water from safe sources certified by water authorities or local councils.Ensure and comply with agreed context /SPHERE standards (minimum humanitarian standard at 15 litres/person/day) and establish water quantity tracking & monitoring system involving beneficiaries;4- Health risk associated with garbage removal (Likelihood high- Impact low).Ensure staff/partners/contractors involved in SWM activities wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff/partners/contractors.
