Project Summary

This project aims to enhance the protective environment for 8,526 children, their families and communities in Kutupalong, Teknaf camp, and hos community in Cox’s Bazar district, through the provision of Child Protection services. Agrajattra seeks to strengthen resiliency and protection of Rohingya vulnerable girls, boys, adolescents and their caregivers through age- and gender- appropriate community-based Child Protection (CP) and case management prevention and response services.
A holistic project that can address various CP needs such as CFS activities, Case Management, Government liaision rather than providing one specific service on some specific groups limits the potential of benefits of the project.
The focus will remain on four main areas to provide a safe secure environment for the FDMN Rohingya population building a favorable protection environment in the Community Based Protection Mechanism, strengthening Government leadership at the operational level, improving services and building social cohesion with host communities. Community-based protection programmers’ that enhance the ability of refugees to manage their own lives are crucial. Achieving progress in these areas will also address the Government’s concern about the security implications of having hundreds of thousands of uneducated and idle young people within the country’s borders.
Strengthening Community Based Protection in Camp and Host areas as per Child Protection Act 2013 through regular meeting with Camp Authorities and Host will ensure that the values and protection mechanism are upheld, any challenges faced can be addressed for a durable solution. The meeting will also emphasis the need for child protection in community.
Awareness raising session in the host & Rohingya community on child rights, child marriage, COVID-19, DRR, monsoon, drowning, Vaccination, fire safety, PSEA, safeguarding, SRH, MHM and other social norms will put emphasis on behavioral changes on issue. An all round issued are often selected as our experience in outreach have shown that people respond more when overall social topics are address in a session rather than specific topics.
Workshop with Government Officials in CiC, UNO level is to build capacity of duty bearers with the necessity of Child Protection, challenges and social cohesion in both camp and host community to ensure community based protection mechanism.
Providing life skill, resilience activities, structural PSS activities to child and adolescent ensures that the beneficiaries have skills to take back when they will be repatriated to Myanmar, as well as coping with stigma, mental trauma, stress , learning social skills. The activities will be module based where the beneficiaries can choose on the design and delivery of services.
Some session those are sensitive such as SRH,MHM sessions etc would be gender specific.
Specialized case management and referral services, Family tracing will ensure continue support of CPMS. Training of staff & other actors in the camp & host area on PSEA, cross cutting issues to uphold CPMS. Capacity building of staff to provide best services . A feedback mechanism will be established to listen to any complaints or feedback from beneficiaries.
Coordination with CPSS will continue through 5W,sitrep,active participation in meeting and inter-sector linkage activities as needed.

1 - Identified Needs

With the Rohingya Refugee Crisis entering its fourth year, reliance on negative coping mechanisms within the refugee community is on the rise, disproportionately affecting vulnerable children. The lack of durable solutions and a constricted humanitarian space continues to erode the protective environment of children. A report on “Findings from the Child Protection Assessment in Rohingya Refugee Camps in Cox’s Bazar” in October 2021 states that - the most reported child protection concerns perceived by respondents as existing currently in the camps were Child Labour (64%), neglect (59%), Child Marriage (57%) and risks of trafficking (32%). These were followed closely by depression (27%), separation of children (25%) and physical abuse of the child (23%). While the Government of Bangladesh has taken commendable measures to lead the response, including taking over site management in 2021, certain government policies and practices continue to limit access to rights for the Rohingya population. As a result, negative coping strategies such as early marriage, child labor and violence against children are expected to continue to rise and existing risks to persist. The project aims to address these issues with awareness session, structured PSS and life skill sessions. Child Labor–According to the October 2021 CPSS report child labor increased to 16%. Recent CPIMS+ trend shows that of those child labor case are found 88% of them are boys and 12% are girls. Cultural acceptance of the practice and the reliance on low-cost labor has enhanced the vulnerability of children, particularly adolescent boys, who are being employed outside the camps in hotels, construction work, as tom-tom/rickshaw drivers or as daily workers in the camp.. Child marriage - 3% of girls aged 10-17, and 1% of boys aged 10-17 were reported as having been married as reported in JMSNA 2021. The key causes of child marriage that emerged from the assessment included economic reasons (49%) followed by family pressure/force (39%) and cultural acceptability (28%). This indicates a need for a holistic response which eases both economic tensions in families as well as behavioural change and norms in communities. Child trafficking and exploitation – While limited information is available on this issue, according to the CPIMS all partner’s data cases of suspected or known trafficking have been on the increase since 2018. Perpetrators are often community members, known to the family or child. Children most at risk are adolescent girls (between 14-17-years old). Adolescent girls are exposed to trafficking as sexual exploitation or as domestic workers, while adolescent boys are forced into labor and drug smuggling. Recent CPIMS+ analysis suggest that among all physical violence faced 55% are girls and 45% are boys. Among these 31% faces emotional or psychological abuse and 23% faces sexual abuse. 14% are child distress and 14% from child trafficking.Dangers and injuries – In addition to standard risks related to natural disasters (floods, landslides, fires, etc.), children in the camps (especially those under 10 years old) are exposed to hazards inherent to the poor infrastructure and congestion in the camp. These include drowning in WASH pits and canals, injuries during construction of camp structures and water borne diseases. Another major risk is children getting lost and thus, vulnerable to traffickers. Accountability to Affected Population – As per IASC’s All accountability procedures the project would include complaints and feedback mechanisms, post-distribution monitoring, refugee protection procedures, and referral pathways would be child-friendly and accessible to children.
