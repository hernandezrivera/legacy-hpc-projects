Project Summary

The Equitable Access to Integrated Water and Sanitation services project will seek to reduce the Water and Sanitation (WASH) morbidity and mortality through provision of resilient WASH in Melut, Baliet and Nasir. This project seeks to increase equitable access to portable water, appropriate sanitation, and promote safe hygiene practices by IDP populations, returnees, and host community in Melut, Baliet and Nasir counties. The project will contribute to the 3 WASH Cluster sector objectives which are, to Contribute to reducing water and sanitation related morbidity and mortality; Provide safe, equitable access to water and sanitation basic services; and Support recovery and resilience building to achieve durable solutions
The project will achieve the following 3 outcomes:
1. Increased access to adequate portable water by 27,000 people (14,000 women and13,000) Men, returnees and host community members in Melut, Baliet and Nasir
2. Increased access to basic sanitation facilities for 16,000 people (Women-8500 and Men-7500) and 700 Under 5 children and their caretakers, Pregnant and Lactating mothers in Melut, Baliet and Nasir
3. Improved access to hygiene promotion and Covid-19 awareness for 90,000 community members in Melut, Baliet and Nasir
Relief International will leverage on its long-term contextual understanding and analysis of WASH needs in Melut, Baliet and Nasir in designing and implementing the project activities. To improve equitable access to portable water, RI will facilitate the rehabilitation of 30 non-functional hand pumps in the two counties. 6 of the water points, will be installed with solar pumps to increase availability of drinking water at IDP and returnee sites. Water quality tests will be conducted before and after borehole rehabilitation, with shock chlorination performed based on the water quality test results. To ensure sustainability, pump minders and water point user committees will receive training on the maintenance and management of the water points, respectively.
To improve sanitation outcomes, the project will facilitate the construction of 800 flood resilient latrines at household level (200 in Baliet, 300 in Melut and 300 in Nasir). Community will be encouraged to fix their own handwashing stations for promotion of safe hygiene practices. 10 flood resilient latrines with hand washing facilities will be constructed at nutrition facilities. Gender and protection measures for children and women will be incorporated in the design, siting and construction of the latrines.
Similarly, Hygiene sessions will be conducted in both the community and in nutrition facilities. An estimated 6,000 hygiene promotion and Covid-19 awareness sessions targeting 90,000 people will be conducted during the project period. 4,500 most vulnerable households and caretakers of Children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) will receive NFI kits sourced from the WASH cluster to promote hygiene practices in the community.
In this context, RI will specifically consider the impact of activities on gender dynamics, actively ensuring that measures are in place to promote safe and equitable access to WASH services by women and girls. RI will highlight gender mainstreaming and integrate gender-related considerations into all planned activities to ensure that men, women, boys, and girls have equal access to project services. Some specific considerations will include: RI will consider the water points are easily and safely accessible; designs of WASH infrastructure is gender and protection sensitive; ensure security of latrines (with locking mechanisms inside and outside); construct separate latrines for males and females; NFI kits are sensitive to the needs of girls, women, children and other vulnerable groups; and women will be equally represented in water user committees and other project community monitoring structures. Also, to ensure equitable access, the needs of people with disabilities, and older people will be considered.

1. Needs

Interagency Rapid Needs Assessments (IRNA) conducted in August 2023 in Nasir indicate a dire water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) situation in the areas. According to the assessments, the main water sources for drinking, bathing, washing, cooking, and other household activities are nearby rivers, swamps, and flooded hand pumps. Recurrent flooding regularly damages existing water supply infrastructure.The assessment reports reveal that the majority of households do not have water collection buckets for fetching and storing water at home, affecting the average daily water consumption of individuals as per the Sphere standard, which is 15 litres. Existing hand pumps are overused due to the population increase and are in need of repair/maintenance. The report observes that there is a shortage of safe drinking water at schools, health, and nutrition facilities, and people are obtaining drinking water from open and unprotected/contaminated water sources. Unprotected water sources used for human consumption are also the same sources for the drinking water of domestic animals, causing a high risk of contamination and easily contributing to the spread of communicable diseases. On the sanitation aspect, for example, in Nasir, 85% of the host community, IDPs, and returnees are practicing open defecation. There are no designated areas (defined/managed defecation areas) for open defecation in all locations/villages visited during IRNA for all age groups (men, women, children, and elderly/disabled). Deteriorated poor sanitation/environmental conditions were observed during IRNA at most residential villages assessed, especially at highland areas where new displaced and returnees' populations have gathered.The general hygiene conditions of the population at assessment locations are very poor. Utensils were observed to be very dirty, and the drying of utensils is done on dirty floors where domestic animals like dogs would access them. Health records indicate a high incidence of diarrheal disease in the four counties, mostly affecting children and the elderly. Soap usage is low as it is not easily available in local markets, and it is expensive for the local community, costing $2 for 800 grams of laundry soap.Similarly, the multi-county assessment that was done from November 2022 to May 2023 by Nonviolent Peaceforce (NP) and Solidarites International (SI) in Malakal, Baliet and Melut indicates that 60% of respondents attested that the population relies on unprotected water source, and half of the respondents does not treat water for drinking. In parallel, access to proper sanitation services is very limited (only 14% of the population has access to functional latrines), and widespread open defecation has been identified. Hygiene promotion activities with a strong focus on hand washing, water usage, disinfection techniques and water safety plans to generate positive hygiene behaviours change and prevent prevalence of WASH related diseases is needed. Due to this project, children and families in the targeted counties will have access to clean water for human consumption, use safely managed sanitation facilities, and abandon open defecation. Hygiene promotion will be key, focusing on behaviour change, targeting both children and adults in the community.

2. Project activities

The project key project activities will include the following: 1.Increased access to adequate portable water for 27,000 people (14,000 women and: 13,000 Men :) in Melut, Baliet and Nasir counties. RI will facilitate the rehabilitation of 30 non-functional hand pumps and Six existing boreholes will be rehabilitated and upgraded to solar-powered water yard systems. Water quality tests will be conducted before and after the installation of solar-powered water yard systems and borehole rehabilitation, with shock chlorination performed based on the water quality test results. To ensure sustainability, pump minders and water point user committees will receive training on the maintenance and management of the water points, respectively.2. Increased access to basic sanitation facilities for 16,000 people and 700 Under 5 children (Women-8500 and Men-7500) in the four the two counties. To achieve this, 800 improved climate-resilient household latrines will be constructed in IDP camps and crowded areas where the returnees and hosts are residing. 10 latrines will be constructed at 10 nutrition facilities to cater for the needs of the community and school children. Gender and protection measures for children and women will be incorporated into the activity. Two hundred twenty-four handwashing stations will be constructed in the latrine blocks to promote hygiene practices.3. Improved access to hygiene promotion and Covid-19 awareness for school children and community members in Melut, Baliet and Nasir counties. Hygiene sessions will be conducted in both the community nutrition centers based on the F-Diagram and other baseline study recommendations. An estimated 6000 hygiene promotion and Covid-19 awareness sessions targeting 90,000 people will be conducted during the project period. 20 Community hygiene campaigns will be held to increase the dissemination of hygiene messaging. 4500 Hygiene kits will be distributed to selected individual households and caretakers of malnourished children. Non-Food Items (NFIs) from the WASH cluster will be distributed in the four target counties according to assessments.4. Training: RI will establish and train water user committees and local pump mechanics to oversee the management and maintenance of water supply systems. The organization will sensitize and empower Water User Committee (WUC) members to assume water management responsibilities beyond the project's duration.1. Water User Committee Training: RI will conduct training for 36 Water User Committees focusing on the management of installed and rehabilitated water supply systems. The training will be divided into 7 sessions, each with 20 participants. WASH staff will regularly evaluate the committee's performance using a template developed by RI's WASH sector.2. Local Pump Mechanics Training: RI will train 36 local pump mechanics in the proper operation and management of water supply schemes and latrines. The training will emphasize 75% practical and 25% theoretical components, providing confidence for maintaining these systems. Sessions will be divided into 4, with 10 local pump mechanics per session. Additionally, experienced WASH technicians will provide on-the-job training during borehole rehabilitation and solar-powered water supply system installation.3. Home Health Promoters Training: RI will train 180 home health promoters (HHPs) for conducting hygiene promotion activities at the household and community levels. Training will focus on village mapping, identifying poor hygiene practices, conducting hygiene promotion sessions at various levels, reporting using beneficiary tracking sheets, and conveying key hygiene messages.
