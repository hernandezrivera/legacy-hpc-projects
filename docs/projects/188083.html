Project Summary

INTERSOS Health program follows an integrated approach which is aimed at providing life-saving and life-sustaining community and primary healthcare assistance. Moreover, INTERSOS, in cooperation with the Syrian Arab Red Crescent (SARC) and Ministry of Health, is committed to build resilience and capacities of communities affected by the crisis in Syria. INTERSOS will implement multi sectoral actions where health will be integrated with components of Protection (including child protection and GBV) and Education. Health projects aim to achieve the following main results: strengthening skills and capacities of health personnel on PHC and diseases outbreaks (including COVID-19 related topics - IPC, Triage), enhancing access to essential health services in remote areas, raising awareness on good health behavior and practices in the Governorates of Hama, Rural Damascus, Idleb, Dar’a. The action applies a combined mobile (mainly the 2022) and static (in 2023) approach whereby static health facilities are enhanced and equipped alongside with the deployment of mobile health teams in hard-to-reach areas with no health clinic available. As a final result, access to quality PHC services for vulnerable communities in hard-to-reach areas will be increased and expanded. The mobile units will provide primary health care (including ANC/PNC and pediatric consultations), nutrition-integrated services (screening and IYCF), support to immunization, risk communication and active prevention awareness (for communicable and non-communicable diseases) A comprehensive capacity building program will contribute to strengthen Health Workers and first-line responders’ skills to decrease health risks, protect and assist vulnerable individuals with high-severity needs. Specific, tailored, technical trainings and capacity building sessions will be provided to SARC Health staff and volunteers as well INTERSOS community mobilizers on relevant COVID 19, health topics, PHC, common childhood illnesses, basic emergency obstetric and new-born care and health education and promotion. INTERSOS will facilitate and monitor the delivery of health services to beneficiaries through SARC health workers and INTERSOS medical staff to ensure continuity and availability of PHC services through diagnosis and treatment of common illnesses among children, PLW, adolescents, PwDs, elderly, adults according to the national protocols.

4 - Needs

In 2022 it is expected to have 12.2 million Syrians in need of health assistance, and in 2023 it is estimated to reach 12.4 million people. The ongoing and protracted crisis, the effects of the C-19 pandemic and the deterioration of the economic system in the country led to have chronic barriers to access healthcare services. Only 54% of the health facilities are operative, with limited human resources, medical equipment and pharmaceuticals, the lack of reproductive healthcare services in rural areas, the increase of the morbidity and mortality related to non-communicable diseases, and the reduced immunization coverage (including the one against C-19) has an impact on the increase of communicable diseases. The lack of water provision and drainage systems have an impact on the registered water-borne diseases and may affect the malnutrition status in some areas. INTERSOS is focusing its program, integrated between Health, Education, Protection and Livelihood, in 21 sub-districts (all in severity 3 and counting 1.5 million PiN) in Hama, Idlib, Rural Damascus and Dar’a. es. Findings from the data collected in the past months of projects implementations, in coordination with SARC, helped to improve the quality of provided services and health assistance, and to design an evidence-based response to effectively address identified needs within the Syrian healthcare system, and in integration with the other sectors implemented in the same areas.

5 - Implementation of activities

Based on in-country lessons learned and main needs assessed, for 2022-2023 INTERSOS will focus on the provision of PHC through mobile approach targeting rural–hard-to-reach areas expanding to the support of Health facilities in 2023. For 2022 INTERSOS will provide a total of 40,200 OPCs, including CDs/ NCDs/ RH and pediatric consultations. MIKA will be provided to PwDs and CwDs. A total of 8 mobile teams (3 MHUs & 5 MMTs) will be equipped and deployed in the 4 targeted governorates. 200 HWs, operating in the mobile teams along with HWs from SARC Health Facilities, will be trained on IPC, COVID-19 awareness, IYCF, malnutrition surveillance, IMCI, RH, MH Gap, Medical Assessment of PwDs assistive devices needs, Health Awareness messages. Community Health positive behaviours will be sought through capacitated Community Health Mobilisers. CHMs will be first trained on IPC, COVID-19 awareness and facilitation skills and will be then conducting RCCE across the targeted communities, providing nutrition awareness with a focus on NCDs prevention. Following an integrated approach HWs will receive Safe Identification & Referral training, Child Protection and GBV. IPC and Health & Hygiene trainings will be provided by INTERSOS to school personnel and health supervisors and awareness will be raised among the schools’ population, and caregivers. OPCs are monitored through INTERSOS Health database/quality checklist and the capacities of the HWs evaluated through pre and post-test

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: Risk of discrimination, perceptions of discrimination/exclusion of vulnerable groups, lack of communication and engagement of target groups. Mitigation: Ensure that consultations with communities, in a way to tailor and adapt the intervention according to needs; Continuous involvement of community throughout the project, in line with the principles of accountability to affected populations (AAP); Adopt and enforce a lean and effective Complaint Response and Feedback Mechanisms, Production and dissemination of key messages, inclusive and accessible to wide audiences (e.g. RCCE campaigns). Risk: GBV victims and survivors lack safe, dignified and stigma-free access to PHC and support. Mitigation: Ensure Health Workers are trained on GBV principles and response strategies and are enabled to identify GBV cases and provide safe referrals; include PSS and GBV prevention and response activities in health interventions; Secure protection mainstreaming in all health programs. Risk: SEA. Mitigation: strict PSEA policy and SOPs are in place; Provide inception and refresher training to INTERSOS and SARC staff; ensure a proper reporting system, data protection and referral pathways are in place. Risk: Increase of tensions between IDPs, Returnees and HCs due to unequal provision of assistance. Mitigation: Secure that a community mapping and conflict-sensitivity analysis are performed at the onset of the project; consultations and transparent communications to foster social cohesion.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

R: INTERSOS/implementing partners (IP) staff, suppliers (etc.) have prior conviction for SEA related offenses; IP, staff member, suppliers, do not comply/accept PSEA policy; IP does not have a strong PSEA system in place; SEA perpetrated by staff/service providers; PSEA allegations are not timely/effectively addressed; survivors have limited access to safe assistance/remedies; lack of PSEA reporting mechanisms; communities not aware of PSEA risks/reporting channels. M: INTERSOS has a Global PSEA/CP Policy that applies to all personnel in addition to a country level Strategy to ensure SEA risks/mitigation strategies/procedures are tailored to the context. INTERSOS staff must sign a PSEA/CP Policy Declaration, attend a PSEA/CP Policy induction and one refresher training per year. One PSEA Referent and two PSEA Focal Points are appointed to receive complaints/reports about alleged acts of SEA and follow up in compliance with relevant guiding principles, ensuring safety of survivors. Complaints are referred to the in-country PSEA Referent and are subject to investigation. An assessment about IP commitment and capacity to prevent/respond to SEA is conducted; efforts are made with IP to harmonize PSEA strategies/reporting procedures to the best extent possible. Training is offered to partners to strengthen the identification of SEA risks/adoption of related SEA mitigation measures; when possible awareness raising on PSEA risks/reporting channels is conducted at community level.

INTERSOS relies on security assessment andprotocols, combined with SARC guidance, supervision and networks in the field; Revisionsof target communities, workplan and scheduled activities might be envisioned ifnecessary. GBV and PSEA: risk of reinforcing gender stereotypes by excluding womenfrom participation; risk of PSEA. PSEA strategy enforced; Financial Risk and Accountability:exchange rates fluctuations affect achievement of targets; lack of transparency; diversionor misuse of project financial resources. INTERSOS has an internal system and SOPs tosecure compliance and accountability: careful, systematic and rigorous monitoring bothof project activities and project spending is carried out; financial controlling rules andprocesses are in place; regular communication with the donor in case of economic shockand re-alignment of targets.
