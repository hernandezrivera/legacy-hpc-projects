Project Summary

Needs

Nigeria has a complex coordination structure, with federal level mechanisms managing the national interface with the humanitarian community on all strategic issues. At operational level, the humanitarian actors interact with autonomous state structures with devolved functions whose mechanisms and governance structures does not necessarily mirror the national level mechanisms. However, the mechanisms are closely inter- linked and constant engagement with government officials at both federal and state levels is crucial for the success of humanitarian response. OCHA plays a strategic role within the overall humanitarian architecture in discharging its core functions (coordination, advocacy, information management and resource mobilization) in support of the Humanitarian Coordinator (HC) and the Deputy Humanitarian Coordinator. OCHA services are required to support coordination of over 80 humanitarian partners currently operating in the northeast. OCHA will continue to consolidate its expanded presence in the north east, especially given the number of humanitarian workers deployed to the area which has more than doubled since end of 2016. OCHA will continued to supported both the Operational HCT and the ISWG to become more effective in the BAY (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) states. For better coordination at the sub state level, OCHA will continue providing the necessary support to coordination at Local Government Area (LGA) level. Access and CMCOORD activities have been scaled up including the development and endorsement by the HCT of the CMCOORD Guidance and Access strategy. Implementation is envisaged to continue in 2019. As of October 2018, OCHA has three operational sub offices providing services in the BAY states. The office in Maiduguri supports the sub offices in Yola and Damaturu and consolidates operations as one north east response. The Deputy Head of Office based in Maiduguri oversees all three offices and acts as the head of north east OCHA operations. OCHA’s presence in Abuja under the leadership of the Head of office continues to support field operations—but it’s emphasis is also on supporting the Humanitarian Coordinator, UN agencies, donors and NGO partners located in the capital. This includes the HCT and other inter- agency coordination mechanisms at Abuja level. The OCHA Abuja office is further responsible for ensuring strategic engagement with relevant federal government ministries and key interlocutors, including the Ministry of Budget and Planning, the Nigeria Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) and the Presidential Committee on the Northeast Initiative (PCNI). The OCHA Humanitarian Financing Unit (HFU), established in May 2017 and based in Maiduguri manages the Nigerian Humanitarian Fund (NHF) while a Humanitarian Financing Advisor based in Abuja supports on resource mobilization for the NHF and the HRP through strengthening donor relations and building private sector partnerships.

Activities

1. Advice and support to the HC and DHC on principled, timely and effective humanitarian action, thereby helping them to ensure strong and decisive leadership2. Regular HC/DHC and HCT interaction with the relevant Ministries and Government humanitarian coordination mechanisms at national and state levels on operational issues3. Produce HNO and HRP and revise as needed.4. Establish and maintain high level quality of information and analysis from OCHA sub office on needs and response, recognizing timeliness, quality and relevance.4. Dissemination of CMCOORD Guidance to the Nigerian military and humanitarian partners
