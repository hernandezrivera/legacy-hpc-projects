Project Summary

The Renee Moawad Foundation (RMF) is a Lebanese non-profit organization that operates all over Lebanon under 5 core areas: (1) Education, (2) Agricultural Development, (3) Economic Development, (4) Health and Social Care, and (5) Local Authority and Decentralization programming over 90M USD since its inception.
Most recently, following the catastrophic Beirut blast that left approximately 300,000 people displaced, over 6,000 injured and approximately 180 dead, RMF formed a Beirut Emergency Response Unit to:
• Clean up homes and streets from debris and rubble on a daily basis through groups of RMF staff and volunteers
• Distribute over 600 food parcels
• Implement a cash-for-works program whereby a number of impacted people from the community have been working on rehabilitation and cleaning debris in their areas in exchange for USD $20 / day (fresh money)
• Coordinate with hospitals and clinics to receive critically needed medicine and trauma supplies (supplied to RMF by Project HOPE)
• Rehabilitate a number of homes so affected people can have access to them before
winter (in partnership with Dar Al Handasa, MAN Enterprise and other local NGOs).
Through the proposed intervention, RMF will endeavor to expand its emergency response and health sector intervention reach through its partnership with Project HOPE, a U.S. based Non-Profit Organization with more than 60 years of experience in Global Health and Emergency Response around the Globe. Project HOPE brings a wealth of experience in Global Supply Chain Management having delivered over 2 billion USD in medical commodities including Pharmaceuticals, medical supplies and consumables around the world since its founding.
In response to the urgent health sector needs resulting post-port explosions and the surge in COVID-19 cases in the greater Beirut area, Project HOPE has already actioned a supply chain pipeline of IEHK's, medical supplies, PPE and other medical commodities to help respond to the most urgent immediate health needs of vulnerable cohorts affected by the explosion via RMF's Supply Chain Platform. These commodities constitute approximately 150,000 USD cost share to the proposed program for distribution to RMF-supported health facilities and MMU that fall outside of health facilities identified in Flash Appeal.
As Beirut continues to cope with the aftermath of the port explosions as a COVID-19 outbreak complicates the humanitarian response effort. 3,241 new cases have been confirmed in the last week, representing almost one-third of all confirmed cases in Lebanon since the start of the pandemic. The surge in COVID-19 cases is straining an already overburdened health system and health workers at functioning health facilities face overcrowding, medicine and supply shortages and a surge in demand for health services, related to both the explosion and COVID-19.
RMF in partnership with Project HOPE will focus on a three-month intervention to support 10 PHCC's pre-identified through UNCT-led health cluster and Emergency Operation Cells and RMF needs assessments which align with the flash appeal health sector objectives and activities. RMF has been carrying out PHCC needs assessments within 3KMs blast radius and will continue with systematic assessment as needs evolve. RMF and Project HOPE will coordinate the response and support packages to PHCCs targeted with the MoPH PHC referral network.
-Enhance access to quality essential and critical health services including for emergency response by procuring urgent lifesaving trauma kits and essential medications.
--Procurement of needed essential medications and supplies (RH, acute, chronic, etc..), medical consumables, reproductive health supplies sufficient for at least 3 months.
-Strengthen infection prevention and control measures to prevent a sharp increase in COVID cases.
-- Procurement of PPE to 10 PHCC's pre-identified through health sector and RMF assessments serving vulnerable cohorts.

1 - Needs

RMF is acutely aware of the health sector needs identified through multi-sector/multi-agency emergency assessments over the last 2 weeks and needs identified across the health sector by the Ministry of Public Health's action plan for primary, secondary and tertiary level support. These assessments have revealed that:1. Six major hospitals and 20 Primary Health Care Clinics sustained partial or heavy structural damage.2. Out of 55 health facilities within a 15 km radius of the blast only half are fully operational. 40% of these sustained moderate to serious damage. 3. Access to health services for the most vulnerable including children under 5, women of reproductive health age, the elderly and those living with chronic diseases, displaced persons, refugees and migrants are all likely to decrease. RMF and Project HOPE have assessed the needs at 10 PHCCs and will coordinate the support package with the MoPH PHC referral network. The PHCC include Karageuzian, Boulghorjian, Karantina, St. John the Baptist Health Center, St. Antoine Akkawi, Directorate of Health - Tarik El-Jadidah - Hariri Foundation, Rahibat Al Mahabeh PHC Karm El Zeytoun, Saideh PHC, Youth Orthodox PHC, LAUMCRH PHC and RMF MMU. These specific PHCC's were targeted given that they form part of PHCC's defined as requiring support and which currently lack organizational and institutional support surrounding pharmaceuticals (essential medicines, RH and chronic disease medication, medical supplies and consumables as well as PPE to manage its their current caseloads are approximately 300 patients per day which include vulnerable cohorts (children U5, women of reproductive health age, the elderly, the disabled, internally displaced persons, refugees and migrants). RMF and Project HOPE are cognizant of the heightened risk to the affected population seeking care, including vulnerable cohorts given emergency health needs resulting from the blast coupled with the need to manage the surge in COVID-19 cases; while concomitantly ensuring access to health care, appropriate physician to patient care ratios, physical distancing and infection, prevention and control measures. As such, RMF and its implementing partner Project HOPE will focus primarily on supply chain functions; however, will coordinate closely with MoPH PHC referral network and with other cooperating agencies to ensure equitable access to health and protection services at facilities targeted. RMF will leverage its current supply chain platform and its partnership with Project HOPE to provide sufficient supply of pharmaceuticals, medical supplies and consumables as well as PPE to support targeted health institutions for 3 months.

2 - Activities

INITIALLY PLANNED ACTIVITIES: RMF and its implementing partner Project HOPE will maintain focus on enhancing supply chain management functions to ensure the availability of pharmaceuticals, medical supplies and consumables to 10 PHCC's-- Karageuzian, Boulghorjian, Karantina, St. John the Baptist Health Center, St. Antoine Akkawi, Saideh PHC, Directorate of Health - Tarik El-Jadidah - Hariri Foundation, Youth Orthodox PHC, Rahibat Al Mahabeh PHC Karm El Zeytoun, LAUMCRH PHC, as well as the RMF MMU. The MMU will decrease the burden on utilization at hospitals. RMF MMU will utilize a smart phone application to track patient progress and follow up. RMF along with Project HOPE monitor and evaluate impact of medical commodity supply chain on access to health services by the population and vulnerable cohorts’ health facilities targeted. Primary activities of the intervention will include: 1. Leveraging Project HOPE's international in-kind and procurement platform to provide a three month supply of essential medicines, supplies and consumables to 10 pre-identified PHCCs via IEHKs primary and supplemental modules. 2. Ensure timely distribution of medical commodities through the intervention period. 3. Ensure proper tracking and inventory systems are in place at health facilities targeted to manage medical commodity supply and consumption. 4. Train existing personnel at health facility level in medical commodity and inventory management. 5. Monitor and evaluate impact of medical commodity supply chain on access to health services by the population and vulnerable cohorts health facilities targeted. 6. Coordinate with health cluster partners to ensure equitable access to health services for vulnerable groups in post disaster settings--including referral mechanisms for MHPSS and GBV services for vulnerable women and girls. UPDATED PLAN NOVEMBER 2020: Sufficient funds have not been received to implement all activities during the project period. In-kind donations have of medicines have been received and thus staffing and support costs have been retained in order to implement that component of the project.
