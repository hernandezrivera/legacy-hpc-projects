Project Summary

Suicide is a pressing health concern worldwide, with over 800,000 people dying by suicide yearly. Since the Covid-19 pandemic, related uncertainties, fears, and health measures led to increase in suicide and associated mental disorders. In 2020, 150 people died by suicide in Lebanon, a low- to middle- income country facing social, political, and economic collapse since October 2019. Despite the impact of mental health activities launched via non- profits such as Embrace, a local non- profit that promotes mental health and suicide prevention, resources remain restricted while community needs exponentiate. Today, people in Lebanon are experiencing severe livelihood risks and absence of basic life necessities including medication, gasoline, and food. These burdens, in addition to the stressors of the pandemic, come as a detriment to citizens’ mental health and are especially magnified for those with predisposition or pre-existing mental illnesses. Additionally, hundreds of people are still grappling with the mental, emotional, and financial consequences of the Beirut explosion which continues to manifesting through trauma symptoms and loss of hope.
From this project Embrace expects to increase the capacity of both the National Lifeline and the Mental Health Clinic. The current national situation exacerbates the risk factors individuals live on a daily basis further increasing their risk of mental health symptoms and problems, which in return necessitates the need to strengthen community prevention services. The project intends to support the growth of the National Lifeline as a service that has been very well adopted by the community and is under high demand to become a 24 hour service as it currently operates for 21 hours. This service is operated by specialized volunteers trained on active listening and collaborative problem solving for intervention in suicide crises. The current situation has deteriorated the progress and/or treatment of individuals with mental illnesses. Mental health services are no longer affordable, resulting in an increased demand for funded mental health services, usually provided through non- profits, with long waiting lists and limited resources. Additionally, the purchasing power of individuals has dropped dramatically and rapidly, causing them to view mental health as a luxury while prioritizing basic living supplies and bills. The project intends to increase the mental health clinical capacity to respond to the growing community need and respectively sustain a strong impact, while playing a crucial role in the economic recovery of Lebanon, given the link between mental health and one’s ability to function productively in their community and society. Following Embrace’s mandate, this project intends to increase its inclusivity of communities with disabilities and their access to mental health services. 1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Embrace plans to expand activities under the National Lifeline and Mental Health Clinic to ensure access to lifesaving mental health services for the most vulnerable in context of the deterioration of Lebanon’s socio-economic conditions. The National Lifeline (1564) is a preventive community tool, launched in 2017 by Embrace in collaboration with the National Mental Health Program. It is a phone-based lifesaving service providing suicide risk assessment, emotional support, de-escalation of suicide crises and dispatching of the National Mental Health Emergency Mobile Team. It is the only emergency mental health hotline in Lebanon, signposted in all ministry national response plans. The Lifeline supports over 12,000 callers annually with zero lives lost to date. It runs for 21 hours daily and is under continuous demand for 24 hour operations. The clinic was launched in Beirut after the Beirut explosion in response to the growing need for specialized mental health services. The clinic offers free psychiatric and psychological consultations, covers medication and inpatient mental health hospitalization bills. Health inequities have been widened with the increase in need of hospitalization and decrease in capacity to afford it. Through the clinic, Embrace secures the finances to cover the average psychiatric stay and handles hospital control through clinic psychiatrists and nurse in coordination with hospital employees.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

As research shows vulnerable populations are at a higher risk of mental health and may present culturally or legally sensitive situations such as abuse, gender and sexual inclusion. Under its mandate, Embrace increases access to mental health services to all individuals without discrimination. Accordingly, these populations into are integrated into all services with standard operational procedures and holistic response to guide the safe delivery of services in potential risk situations. The operational procedures layout responsibilities, legal and protection guidelines with coordination with relevant ministerial entities. Holistic response involves social work to ensure referral to community resources to complete support as needed. Embrace services are currently offered in Arabic, English and French. The Lifeline is widening its integration by increasing the recruitment of operators from diverse nationalities such as Armenian and backgrounds such as migrants.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

The National Lifeline is equipped with a data-entry software to capture the number of calls, demographics, suicide risk assessment, mental health history and risk- protective factors of callers. Furthermore, the Lifeline trainings are monitored and evaluated to report on their impact, along with quality control surveys to collect beneficiary feedback on the Lifeline's service. The clinic is also equipped with an electronic medical health record system that tracks the number of beneficiaries, their demographics, the number of consultations, the diagnosis, treatment, progress and satisfaction of beneficiaries receiving the service. All data captured from Embrace activities is handled confidentially and analyzed on a monthly basis and published on our website to guide further research and interventions needed to improve mental health services for beneficiaries and for Lebanon. These reports can portray an increase in capacity in the Lifeline and clinic and the impact this increase has.
