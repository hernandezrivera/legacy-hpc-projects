Project Summary

According to the Somalia Food Security Cluster, an average of 1.85 million people received food assistance monthly from April to August. Approximately 400 000 people also benefitted from government-led rural and urban safety net programs. In April, the government enacted a 20-100 percent tax exemption on imported food commodities in order to mitigate the socio-economic impact of COVID-19 on the population. Therefore, various kinds of humanitarian interventions and government support have played and continue to play a critical role in preventing acute food insecurity consequences in Somalia. Based on results of assessments conducted in July and August 2020, approximately 1.3 million people are currently facing Crisis or worse (IPC Phase 3 or higher) outcomes through September 2020 in the presence of humanitarian food assistance. An additional 2.5 million people are Stressed (IPC Phase 2), bringing the total number of people experiencing acute food insecurity to 3.8 million.
Since the beginning of 2020, Farmers and poor communities in different catchment areas of Bosaso have witnessed innumerable desert locust invasions that have devastated and destroyed plantations which would have played an important role in ensuring steady food security. The purpose of this project is to extend food aid in form of direct cash transfers to communities, and farmers in Barokhle, Carmo, and Eldahir catchment areas of Bosaso, that are undergoing severe conditions of hunger which were exacerbated by a combination of COVID-19 consequences, hot summer, and most importantly desert locust invasions.
As a result, of the extended dry spell, and Desert Locusts that have led to significant reductions in household stocks and income from the 2020 Gu season cereal harvest, and agricultural employment, some poor agro-pastoral households will face moderate to large food consumption gaps through the end of 2020. As a result, a considerable proportion of poor households, and farmers in Barokhle, Carmo, and Eldahir catchment areas of Bosaso are facing moderate to large food consumption gaps and this may continue until the end of 2020 unless they receive the financial intervention.
Additionally, farmlands that have been devastated by desert locust invasion remain in dire need of assistance that would enable them to access seedlings and resume farming activities. This would be instrumental in addressing food insecurity concerns in the future.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The project will be using cash transfers because the food needs of the vulnerable communities are varied. Therefore cash transfers would enable the beneficiaries to access the food they need based on their individual preferences. Besides, transfers will reduce the risk of spreading COVID-19, as the beneficiaries will be receiving via their mobile lines. Additionally, farmers are in dire need of immediate support. Therefore cash transfer would be a quicker mechanism to reach out to them without compromising their farming schedule. Additionally, TSS, based on previous projects conducted, and consultations with the beneficiaries found UCT the most appropriate way to transfer aid to vulnerable HH because they allow people to spend the money on what they need most, rather than having other people decide. This is particularly important to help empower women who might not otherwise have access to money or be able to make spending decisions.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

Through providing cash-based transfers, the beneficiaries are expected to buy essential food supplies they need to meet their daily living demands without compromising health and safety. Markets in Bosaso and its catchment areas sell necessary food items which are essential for improving the lives of the most vulnerable. Regarding this approach, therefore, beneficiaries shall based on their food needs use the cash transferred to them without intervention from TSS. The money will often be spent in local markets, meaning that it can help protect local production, create jobs, and benefit other people in the area. There are robust systems in place to ensure payments reach the right people and are not being subject to fraud or corruption. In the same vein, the beneficiaries choose UCT as the modality they would like to be used in the project.

This measures out access to beneficiaries with the project. The project intends to have a community meeting in relation to the project activities with the community leaders to solicit feedback and further also intends to hold monthly meetings with women and girls who attend activities at the TSS to address their concerns on the activities enrolled and make changes on the activities The project has put measures to and reporting channels regarding any protection, the issue be it from our staff or the beneficiaries themselves to prevent violations of rights from occurring or recurring stop ongoing violations, provide remedies, through reparation and rehabilitation, if violations have occurred; and foster an environment conducive to respect for the rights of women, men, girls, boys and persons with disabilities in accordance with the law. As the project design is to benefit the host community, IDPs, returnees, and other affected populations, the project intends to ensure that the services provided don'tpose threats to the beneficiaries but rather address protection concerns across the population group targeted through carrying out joint activities and also further the project intends to engage the local authorities in the intended project locations.
