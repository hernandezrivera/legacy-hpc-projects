Project Summary

The project seeks to strengthen case management through direct service provision to survivors of gender-based violence (GBV) and unaccompanied and separated children (UASC), including children at risk of being recruited by armed forces or armed groups (CAAFAG). The interventions aim at direct services and referral services for where a holistic approach is necessary. This includes providing training and mentoring to GBV and CP staffs from INTERSOS and implementing partners (IPs). GBViE and CPiE aim to provide immediate lifesaving assistance to vulnerable populations. Beneficiaries will receive comprehensive case management and psycho-social support (PSS) - including emergency care, referral to INTERSOS medical teams trained on clinical management of rape (CMR). Additionally, access tailored material assistance, basic emotional support, and follow up on all services (direct and referrals). Capacity building of duty bearers including formal and informal authorities to strengthen and improve identification. Considering current approaches, the emphasis is on scaling up prevention and response services to survivors of GBV and children who have been separated from their families (by restoring the family links/family tracing efforts) who are the primary duty bearers. Commitment to participate in strengthening service mapping to facilitate appropriate referrals and through coordination and maintaining links necessary in service delivery. Comprehensive services to survivors as well as unaccompanied and separated children (UASC) at risk, in the long run, ensure the protection of children from recruitment by armed forces or armed groups (CAAFAG) and even use of girls in and by armed group/forces (gender-based violence (GBV)). The project will further strengthen protection monitoring in areas of settlement of returnees and IDPs through community-based protection monitors (or advocates) working with CSOs to ensure that quality evidence-based reports about protection issues and human rights violations in a systematic collection of protection-related data to inform better response and advocacy.

Project Activities

• Providing Survivor-centred multi-sectorial support (including provision or referral to Medical/legal/PSS services and but not limited to safety relocation) through comprehensive case management provided by trained caseworkers, relevant service providers as well as Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) according to beneficiaries needs.• Identified youth formerly associated with armed forces and groups and those at risk in conflict-affected areas are provided with inclusive community-based reintegration support (which included; enrolling back to school/formal education, Vocational and livelihood skills training, linking to psychosocial support structures including safe spaces, foster care and family tracing/reunification for UASC). CAAFAG who cannot readily be reunified with family members may also access to interim care center services, while Vocational Skills training graduates to be supported on business start-ups or with kits and equipped with business skills (per ILO -SYOB guidelines). • Support separated children, survivors of gender-based violence and children formerly associated with armed forces or groups receive quality comprehensive services that meet their specific needs and improve their protective environment/rights-claim. • Facilitate training with civil society members and authorities on protection principles and best interests practices for children and survivors centred approach. • Equip traditional duty bearers as well as medical personnel, and humanitarian staff with relevant information and approaches on child protection in emergencies including assessing and management of child-friendly environment or space.• Facilitate awareness-raising initiatives, direct advocacy and public outreach campaigns on GBV prevention, response and mitigation that support identification and referral of individuals with specific needs or who are at heightened risks.• Support health facilities equipped to provide clinical care of sexual assault survivors and referral systems to nutrition centres.• Provision of tailored material support to GBV survivors and unaccompanied/separated children and provision of dignity kits including solar lanterns to Survivors.• Strengthening community-based protection mechanisms by building the capacity of the community members through capacity building.• Support existing systems for prevention of separation of children, recruitment of children as well as gender-based violence at community, sub-national and national levels through mobilization and education of all key stakeholders.• Protection monitoring of displacement and protection violations

Mitigation Measures

The project will engage and consult all categories and layers of the affected population in identifying and responding to protection needs preferably by consulting women, men, boys, girls, youth, persons with disabilities, older persons and marginalized groups to understand their needs, coping strategies and preferences for protection services. Engage the community and committee representative to play an active role in identifying solutions and in the decision-making processes that affect the targeted population, so as to promote a sense of ownership, build their self-esteem and improve the relevance and sustainability of the response. Promote and support self-protection capacities and avoid persons resorting to negative coping strategies to mitigate risks and reinforce the capacity of the community and family to act as a protective layer for children against violations. INTERSOS has accessible, confidential and locally understood feedback mechanisms for suggestions and complaints with a view to improving programming, understanding community perceptions, promote beneficiary empowerment and assist in detecting misconduct e.g. Feedback box in centers and hotlines that beneficiaries call for appreciation, complaints and suggestions. A strong feedback mechanism will enhance the capacity of INTERSOS Staff to prevent challenges and address areas of the projected weakness thus improving risks management in relation to the delivery of services. The project will further ensure that targeting of humanitarian assistance is done in line with the humanitarian principles, identifying the most vulnerable and potentially invisible individuals and communities that are exposed to protection violations (especially girls, who when separated are used by local communities as domestic helps hence not seen to be alone/without nuclear or extended family). Staff will choose distribution locations that are easily reachable for all those affected, away from security hazards as well as accessible for different social groups The staff will design distribution locations so that all individuals can access them with safety and dignity, with specific arrangements for women and children, in line with cultural sensitivity We will ensure measures are in place to distribute supplies to vulnerable individuals (prioritize) and those who are unable to travel to distribution points, such as older people or persons with disabilities (where prioritization would explore most reliable forms for Cash and Voucher Assistance (CVA) if applicable). The management will ensure that fundamental CVA trainings are undertaken by all staffs who plan to engage and linking mobile notification of transfer to the feedback loop system. The project will Identity existing coping mechanisms and community-based protection mechanisms and ensure any intervention is built on and/or reinforces the existing mechanism in place.

Please provide further explanation

The early phase of project implementation, INTERSOS will work on developing clear program exit strategies that link participants to assess their protection situation of the communities to understand protection needs of the community and provide protection response to increase their resilience and minimize their negative coping strategies. Additionally, INTERSOS will incorporate transition for the protection components throughout the project. By working closely with local Community structures to directly strengthen their capacity to provide quality services on a long-term basis. We will focus on the technical quality of training and capacity building activities (for case management, psychosocial support, legal assistance, etc.) The provision of critical information to and referrals of the most vulnerable beneficiaries alongside the community empowerment is meant to address the most critical protection needs until the communities are empowered and capable to address the needs themselves. Sustainability of the project is essential. Therefore, INTERSOS will work towards transferring responsibilities to the established local authorities’/community-based organizations by building and strengthening their capacity to enable them to continue monitoring protection violations in the project areas even after the project ends. INTERSOS interventions are based on a bottom-up approach and consults communities at the grassroots level to inculcate community ownerships enhancing long term suitability. In the long term, INTERSOS is optimistic that as the government grows stronger; its institutions will be able to absorb increasing responsibility for the delivery of humanitarian services. To further enhance the sustainability and the ownership of the project it will include activities and support to ensure sustainable protection outcomes beyond the period of protection interventions (such as referrals, awareness sessions and enrollment in activities to support income generation based on the activities prioritized by the community). This will be achieved through coordination with other agencies and partners, engagement of affected population and local authorities as well as development an exit strategy together with the beneficiaries which will rely on structures already existing at the community level.
