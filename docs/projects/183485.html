Project Summary

The project aims to provide the most needed support to farmers, herders and fisherfolks
communities in Gaza and North Gaza Governorates. The project will also provide
rehabilitation services to small scale poultry farmers in Rafah Governorate. Target groups
suffer from increased challenges due to the deteriorating socio-economic conditions
resulting from over 14 years of blockade, recurring rounds of violence, natural shocks (i.e.
spikes of heatwaves and winter storms), and the COVID-19 crisis. The project will address
Country HRP Strategic Objective 3, “the capacity of vulnerable Palestinians to cope with,
and overcome, a protracted crisis, including from environmental threats, is supported,
while solutions to violations and other root causes of threats and shocks are pursued.
Oxfam will implement the project with two local partners, the Agricultural Development
Association (PARC) and the Economic and Social Development Centre (ESDC). Both
partners have solid experience implementing agricultural activities across the Gaza Strip
and a long track record in providing humanitarian assistance and livelihood support
interventions. The project aims to enhance the productive capacities of 700 farmers,
herders, poultry farmers and fisherfolks- and their HHs- to sustain their food security and
increase their livelihood resilience (total 3,850 individuals). Activities will include the
rehabilitation of land, greenhouses, rainwater harvesting ponds (linked to the
greenhouses), poultry farms, small ruminant sheds, and wells. This will be conducted by
providing maintenance services and installation of solar energy. The project will also
support fisherfolks with boat body rehabilitation and food vouchers. The project activities
were coordinated with the Food Security Sector (FSS) coordinator and the Ministry of
Agriculture (MoA). Areas of intervention were selected based on the HRP 2022 priority
matrix. Oxfam aligns its programmes with safe-programming strategies and guidance that continue to guide its project implementation in all activities in emergency and in non-emergency situations to ensure communities and beneficiaries are safeguarded against sexual exploitation and abuse.

1 - Needs

According to HNO 2022, more than 1.5 million people need assistance in the Occupied Palestinian Territory (OPT), with 73% in Gaza. The unemployment rate for the second quarter of 2021 stood at 44.7%, while the poverty rate was estimated to have reached 56.4%. Israel’s military occupation and its coercive environment in Gaza, with over 14 years of blockade and recurring rounds of violence, remain the root causes of food insecurity. According to World Bank (June 2021), the agricultural sector suffered direct damages estimated at USD 45 million resulting from the May 2021 hostilities. More than 26,000 dunums of orchards, vegetable crops and greenhouses, and more than 450 livestock and poultry farms were fully or partially destroyed. According to the Palestinian Ministry of Labor, it is estimated that more than 19 thousand workers were affected by direct damages to active facilities, 37 per cent of them working in the agriculture sector. Fisheries continue to suffer from access limitations to fishing zones and recurrent sea closures. Continuously changing and unpredictable fishing zone limits, coupled with restrictions on importing essential fishing equipment, continue to affect fishers’ livelihoods. Ageing boats, nets, engines and other equipment are in dire need of rehabilitation or replacement. Electricity cuts continue to impact farmers’ access to water to irrigate their land, causing additional unaffordable costs for many small-scale and vulnerable farmers. Herders in Gaza also continue to struggle with limited grazing areas and poor or non-existent animal shelters, causing re-occurring deaths and illnesses to small ruminants in particular. Poor poultry farming environments and access to services primarily affect small-scale poultry farmers -specifically chicken - already challenged by the market that large-scale farmers control. Oxfam, PARC and ESDC carried out focus group discussions (FGDs) and key informant interviews (KII) in targeted communities. The farmers' group (open field and greenhouses) identified that their main challenges are water-related issues for irrigation. For example, network damages, water salinity, shortages of water, and shortages of power supplies to extract water from wells; seen as the most significant barrier to farmers. The provision of agricultural inputs was classified as a chronic problem as well. Through its rapid damage assessment, PARC identified some 50 poultry farms needing immediate rehabilitation works, especially coping with extreme weather conditions. PARC also conducted a damage assessment to identify rainwater harvesting ponds and greenhouses affected during the May 2021 hostilities, which PARC established during the last few years. The assessment found that 30 ponds and greenhouses were partially affected. ESDC worked closely with the MoA fishery department to identify the urgent needs of fisherfolk in the Northern area. It was evident that around 30 engine boat bodies and 60 pedal boat bodies need urgent rehabilitation. Fisherfolks in the northern area face more difficulty sustaining their livelihood with the decrease of fishing area and repeated attacks by the Israeli forces. The reduction of the national safety net payment, significantly reduced during 2021, has affected most fisherfolk who depend on social safety net (SSN) regular payment as a source of income. Small ruminant herder communities in the Northern prioritized the rehabilitation of animal shades. Reducing grazing areas forces them to rely on animal shelters to protect their animals, especially in extreme weather conditions (heat and cold), increasing in frequency over the last few years. The project activities are aligned with the FSS priority matrix of activities identified per location agreed by FSS partners, MoA and MoSD during consultations conducted during the HNO discussions. Further assessments using FDGs and consultation interviews will be carried out at the project’s start to re-validate these specific needs

2 - Activities

Project activities will address complex vulnerable livelihoods caused by poor infrastructure, depletion of livelihood assets and resources of vulnerable livelihood groups and increasing unemployment across the population. Activities include support to open land and greenhouse agricultural activities, access to water resources and irrigation systems. Activities will include adapting to renewable energy (i.e., water wells and poultry farms) to respond to the power crisis. The rehabilitation of poultry and small ruminant farms/sheds and fishing boats will also be conducted. Fishing boats, land and greenhouse rehabilitation will be implemented through conditional cash. Moreover, improving the perception of female workers in the agriculture sector will be a key activity to raise the awareness of men and women (especially youth) working within the sector on the division of labor, women’s participation and agricultural rights.Output 1: Livelihoods of farmers and their families are protected through access to agricultural assetsActivity 1.1 Rehabilitation of 5 agricultural wells (2 in the North Governorate and 3 in Gaza Governorate) This activity will involve equipping 5 agricultural wells - affected during the May hostilities- with a photovoltaic system that will provide an alternative clean source of power to operate the wells during the daytime. This activity will be implemented in partnership with PARC and targeting 130 farmers HHActivity 1.2 Land rehabilitation for 80 open-land small scale farmers Rehabilitation services will be conducted in 160 dunums in North Gaza and Gaza Governorates belonging to 80 small-scale vegetable farmers and their HHs through conditional cash assistance/self-help approach. Benefiting farmers will be required to prepare, cultivate, extend irrigation networks, and purchase required agricultural fertilizers. The cash instalments will be released in two or three phases based on the work progress in the farms. This activity will be implemented in partnership with PARC and targeting 80 farmers HHActivity 1.3 Rehabilitation of 30 rainwater harvesting ponds and greenhouses (Greenhouses linked to the rain water harvesting ponds) in the Northern Governorate. This activity will support farmers in restoring their livelihoods and improving water storage capacity in their farms. Conditional cash assistance will be provided to 30 farmers whose greenhouses and agricultural rainwater harvesting ponds were damaged during the last hostilities. Farmers will be requested to rehabilitate greenhouses and rainwater harvesting ponds depending on the level and nature of damages. Cash assistance will be released in three instalments based on work progress. This activity will be implemented in partnership with PARCActivity 1.4 Rehabilitation of 50 poultry farms in Rafah governorate This activity will entail rehabilitating the structure of poultry farms and installing manual heating systems for 50 poultry farms in Rafah using olive waste. The rehabilitation works will include installing solar systems aligned with FAO guidelines (the average capacity per farm is 1 kW which can provide the necessary power for light and ventilation). This will support small-scale poultry farmers to cope with climate change impacts, including extreme weather conditions. This activity will be implemented in partnership with PARC and targeting 50 poultry farmers HHsActivity 1.5 Rehabilitation of 150 small ruminant animal shades in the Northern Governorate This activity will support targeted herders to deal with heatwaves and cold depressions occurring in more frequency and intensity during the past years due to climate change. This activity will be carried out by the ESDC and will include the rehabilitation of the entire structure of barns with a feeder, water tank and water drinkers.Activity 1.6 Rehabilitation of 100 boat bodies in the Northern Governorate The rehabilitation will be supported through the provision of conditional cash assistance to 100 fisherfolks. Cash instalments will be released in three phases based on the progress of rehabilitation works. This activity will be implemented in partnership with ESDCOutput 2 support the food security of fisherfolk and women-headed householdActivity 2.1 Support 160 fisherfolks’ HHs in the North governorate with 3 rounds of food vouchers Vulnerable fishing workers in the North Governorate, will be supported with basic food vouchers for 3 months. The support will be provided during the periods of repairing and rehabilitating boats and when marine fishing activity is less and the selection process for this activity will include using Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool (UVAT) . this activity will be implemented in partnership with ESDC. Oxfam will collect disaggregated data by sex, age, and also by disability to all project beneficiaries. Oxfam has provided training on safe programming to its partner staff working in its humanitarian programme particularly in the field and those responsible for cash and voucher assistance . It will ensure that risks related to access of women to benefit from cash are continuously analyzed and mitigated.

3 - Indicators

Oxfam is committed to Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL), including gender-sensitive M&E. Oxfam also has a Common Approach to Monitoring and Social Accountability (CAMSA), which aligns with good international practices in monitoring, evaluation, and learning, promoting collaborative and effective, results-focused, and accountable MEL practice and Social Accountability. CAMSA is an approach based on minimum requirements for MEL and Social Accountability aligned to Oxfam’s program quality efforts. It provides guidance, tools and methodologies, including gender analysis and the assessment of gender outcomes. Within this partnership, Oxfam will take the lead in monitoring and evaluating the project, ensuring that indicators are closely monitored, and gathering feedback from beneficiaries throughout the implementation period of the project, allowing Oxfam to amend activities where relevant to address emerging beneficiaries’ needs. M&E will be conducted and assessed based on a participatory approach and PARC and ESDC committees and CBOs' involvement in the targeted communities. Due to the COVID-19 crisis, Oxfam’s monitoring activities will be adapted by using safe measures to interact with communities. Therefore, monitoring activities will include operational monitoring of the progress of implementation against the work plan in compliance with project standards and procedures, satisfaction of beneficiaries post distribution, and project impact monitoring. This will be developed at the start with the project jointly by Oxfam and its two partners in consultation with project community committees to ensure their engagement. 9.1c - How will feedback be used?

Oxfam’s Accountability, Feedback, and Response Mechanism (Your Word Counts) is an accountability mechanism that provides broader access for diverse vulnerable groups to raise their thoughts, positive or negative feedback and minor or major complaints during project implementation. This system uses information communication technology (ICT) to receive feedback either online, through verbal feedback or by recording complaints received through other channels such as helplines and feedback boxes. The system automatically refers feedback received to relevant program staff and is monitored by Oxfam’s MEAL Officer. However, complaints related to safeguarding, misconduct, or fraud will be referred to Oxfam’s Country Director and safeguarding team in line with Oxfam’s confidentiality policies and reporting procedures, thus will not be recorded by the system. Oxfam has already started adapting its intervention accountability and monitoring mechanisms to best address the needs of the people served in a changing environment due to the COVID-19 outbreak. Oxfam’s helpline will be primarily used to report any misconduct, fraud, exploitation or abuse incidents, which will be automatically transferred to the designated teams. Beneficiaries will be provided with information on the available mechanisms and encouraged to use them whenever needed. This information will be made available in MoUs that beneficiaries will sign at the beginning of the project. Moreover, Oxfam will carry out training on PSEA and safe programming to partners field team to ensure the proper understanding of the use of these mechanisms

10. – Do No Harm
10.1 - Do No Harm

Oxfam premised its project design on the ‘do no harm approach’, ensuring that project activities do not present any harm to the targeted population by facilitating access to essential protection needs, which would have put them at risk had it not been available. Technical assessments will consider the safety, dignity and confidentiality of the served population while also protecting data and humanitarian actors uncompromised. Preserving dignity is essential to maintaining self-esteem and confidence, vital to coping in stressful and potentially overwhelming humanitarian situations. Supporting women’s self-esteem and confidence will reflect positively on them and will enhance the roles of each member of the household and will result in improved wellbeing of the family, especially children as care receivers. Before implementing activities, partners will be invited to attend a training session by Oxfam’s Gender and Protection Officer on the overall protection principles and how to detect GBV. This should equip them with knowledge and tools to conduct a risk analysis that identifies risks, risks level, likelihood and mitigation measures to be taken into consideration in advance. Most importantly, activities will be designed and implemented in close coordination and full engagement and consultation of targeted communities in liaison with relevant authorities whenever needed and appropriate. Specifically, the following steps will be implemented to integrate do no harm throughout the project: 1- Project risk assessment will be prepared at the project start to identify clear measures to mitigate any risks with beneficiaries, contractors, Oxfam, PARC and ESDC staff, community committees and the broader community by the project. 2-Active engagement with targeted communities, including their representatives, through the project community committees that will include farmer representatives (women CBOs and local committees in target localities). This approach will be the basis for all steps of the project cycle, ensuring meaningful representation of women and youth whenever possible to ensure that the project’s design is responsive to project beneficiaries' different needs and concerns. 3- Supporting women in accessing cash for work activities by providing safe and appropriate work opportunities in terms of location and time. All activities will follow a quick risk assessment which will identify any potential risks related to women’s participation in the activities and identify means to support them within the project period or beyond through a referral mechanism. 4- Cash for work activity will be aligned with the child protection policy prohibiting child labour. It will also promote women participation’s in agricultural typically un-accounted for, employing the equal access principle of protection and considering preserving the dignity of targeted women. This will be conducted by tailoring activities according to what is most suitable for them in terms of timing, form, location, payment method, and implications of HH and child care burdens. 5- Activities will be adapted to COVID19 related preventive measures. COVID-19 corresponding SoPs will be integrated into the project design. For example, Oxfam requires implementing projects under COVID-19 for project staff, suppliers, and volunteers to be trained in Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) and to sign a consent form agreeing to engage in any project activity. Oxfam offered this training to its partner staff involved in current and previous projects implemented during COVID-19.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

Project community committees (with equitable gender and age representation) will be established at the community level to ensure their direct engagement with targeted communities and beneficiaries throughout the intervention. This will enhance community engagement and reduce any bias in selecting, targeting beneficiaries and service distribution. Moreover, the project design has considered gender and protection principles by emphasizing the participation of women in the project’s committees. This will be integrated through - Orientation sessions about project activities with project community committees - Dissemination of designated feedback mechanisms designed under this project under partners’ and Oxfam’s responsibilities, - The use of accountability community engagement mechanisms: such as the dissemination of the beneficiaries selection criteria (that will focus on scoring the most vulnerable and affected groups such as small scale farmers, having livelihood activities as the sole source of income, HH protection concerns, etc.), clarity, orientation of the selection process, announcement to communities about activities and procedures to receive benefits, etc. - Developing further Oxfam's existing feedback mechanisms to improve accessibility and appropriateness of these mechanisms to the specific target groups -Carrying out safe programming training to partner field and project staff that ensures continuous identification of risks and acting upon these risks throughout the project cycle
