Project Summary

This project aims to Improve equitable access to quality lifesaving curative nutrition services through systematic identification, referral and treatment of acutely malnourished cases in nine districts in Somalia. The project responds to the gender and age specific needs such as through Nutrition intervention services to vulnerable drought and conflict affected communities in the IDPs of Daynile, Afgoye, Kismayo, Gurel, Baidoa, Galkacyo, Garowe, Bosaso and Hargeisa districts in CSS, Puntland and Somaliland. The project will run 9 district hospital stabilization centers covering both fixed and mobile OTP services in each district to strength the referral pathway in the specified districts from community to fixed health center and district hospitals as part of child and maternal mortality reduction activities and in overall health system strengthing specially the newly emerging stated in Somalia. The project is targeting 58,320 under five children, 11,664 under one children as part of routine immunization targets, 26,244 pregnant and lactating women, and Targeted/actual SAM(U5) caseload (60% coverage) is 2,729 in OTPs as SC annual target is 262.
this project will cover staff incentives for child health and nutrition services, nine ambulances to increase the referral from communities to health centers and hospital in the districts, and capacity building for frontline staff in the health facility to increase the quality of primary and secondary health service delivery in the nine districts.

Needs

Malnutrition (undernutrition) remains the major problem persisted over time in Somalia with high levels of Global Acute Malnutrition (GAM) well above emergency threshold of 15%. During 2016 and 2017, the humanitarian situation in Somalia continued to deteriorate because of the poor rains in Gu and Deyr seasons of 2016 and 2017, causing widespread food insecurity, deepening health, nutritional and water related issues and exacerbated by large-scale movement of people. In 2018, the overall food security situation in Somalia has improved when compared to a year ago, the latest food security and nutrition assessment by FAO’s FSNAU, has revealed. The results indicate that most pastoralist areas have seen an improvement in pasture, water availability and increasing herd sizes. However, despite the positive outlook and gains made, humanitarian assistance needs to be sustained to support the people in need. Most importantly these gains has not translated into improvement in the nutrition situation and categorically the nutrition situation remains of serious concern. Recent nutrition surveys across the country indicates slight improvement in few areas and stagnated nutrition situation in most parts of the country. The trends in GAM prevalence(WHZ) across remained at serious levels when compared to last six months back. As a result, nearly 954,007 children are expected to be acutely malnourished, including 173,594 who are expected to be severely malnourished during the next one year period (Sep 2018-Sep 2019). PAC conducted a survey in Sep 2018 and discovered that most of the basic needs of human missing specially health and nutrition services and mainly they do not have secondary health services and secondary health service is under funded.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

PAC acknowledges and bides by the humanitarian codes which require Accountability to target beneficiaries as pillar in delivering humanitarian aid to needy people. Community based Nutrition management committee (NMC) will be formed by the target community. The committee will be formed based on community based selection criteria which will be developed with the community. The formation process will ensure representation and active participation of women with target of at least 70% of its members being women. In addition, the committee will comprise of representatives from the internal displaced population (IDP) or village community i.e( IDP/village leader, Shiekh, teacher) and Health workers. These Community based Nutrition management committee (NMC) will be the center-pole in leading the program management (implementation, monitoring and evaluation process) so as to increase accountability and community participation. The role & responsibilities of the Nutrition management committee (NMC) will include; management of beneficiary complaints and provision of feedback mechanisms, conflict resolution to ensure the adherence to the principle of Do-No-Harm approach is used during the entire project cycle. the Nutrition committee will be trained so as to enable them participate awareness campaigns and support the planning of mobile outreach events to ensure that access to basic nutrition services are provided to all target areas. Based on this mitigating measures, PAC will ensure that no one is excluded form the nutrition services based on their displacement and both IDPs and host-communities will be equal to the service provided.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

This project has seriously taken a note that women and girls are at increased risk of violence due to the on-going civil unrest in this project area and may be unable to access assistance or make their needs known. On the other side the men's roles as protectors in this otherwise conflict affected area has placed them on greater responsibility for risk taking. PAC Somalia therefore has first taken account of the different needs, recognize the potential barriers faced and ensure women and men access nutrition services equally. All data will be disaggregated by sex and age and apply a gender analysis. PAC will involve from the outset women, girls, boys and men, including those that belong to the vulnerable groups, in nutrition assessments, priority setting, programme design, interventions and evaluation. All nutrition consultations, examination and care will be in privacy. Nutrition personnel (women and men) will be adequately represented in gender theme groups.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

The project location can be easily accessed by the local and international organizations and no incidents have been reported recently. PAC has been operating in the project from time to time, including now, thus, the relationship between PAC and the communities in those district is very strong, this has enabled PAC to implement many projects in nutrition, this shows that PAC has cordial relationship with the communities in the regions where it intervenes, PAC enjoys full access from all corners of the district including the government , civil societies and beneficiaries. we have main office in Mogadishu, Garowe, Hargeisa and Kismayo, which allows easily to access the targeted locations. all PAC projects will not create community conflicts including the current project.
