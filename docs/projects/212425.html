Project Summary

According to the latest HNO, there is a significant increase in the number of people requiring humanitarian assistance in Syria, with a total of 16.7 million individuals identified as in need.(8.4 million females) and (8.3 million males), marking a rise from 15.3 million in 2023. Among those in need, 5.5 million are displaced, with over 2 million residing in last resort sites. The vast majority is in the governorates of Aleppo 3,505,148 (22%) and Idleb 2,596,397 (16%)
Following 12 years of conflict and persistent economic crisis, Syria faced further adversity with a series of earthquakes striking northern Syria and Turkiye on February 6 and 20, 2023. These earthquakes displaced hundreds of thousands of families, resulting in nearly 5,900 deaths and over 12,800 injuries in Syria.
The food security situation in Syria is worsening, with approximately 15.5 million people (65% of the population) requiring food or livelihood support, including 28% who are persons with disabilities. Significant concentrations of food needs are observed in Idleb (73%) estimated in need of food assistance and Aleppo (58%).
In 2023, SARD conducted a Yearly Needs Assessment across various sectors. The analysis of priority needs revealed that limited access to food is a pressing concern, with 97.1% of households expressing this need. In Aleppo, 81.0% of households prefer cash for food purchases; similarly, in Idleb, 67.7% of respondents express the same preference.
SARD’s intervention aims to provide life-saving assistance and enhance resilience for the most vulnerable crisis-affected families through MPC distribution, addressing multiple household vulnerabilities that result from the twelve years of conflict and the earthquakes. SARD's target is to reach 35,000 individuals, providing short-term food assistance for three rounds and monthly food assistance for eight rounds.
Vulnerability is identified and prioritized through a selection and scoring mechanism. Assessment of the security, locations, tenders, and markets’ surveys will enable SARD to determine the modality of response.
Through a protection lens, SARD’s intervention is designed to ensure that vulnerable families have access to dignified and timely assistance through a robust response mechanism. To ensure that marginalized groups such as female-headed households, the elderly, and persons with disabilities (PwD) have safe and secure access to assistance, specialized and gender-sensitive activities have formed an integral part of SARD’s response strategy.
The proposed intervention is in line with the objectives and strategic direction of the various Northwest Syria Cluster Response Plans.
SARD has included two MPC components under this submission; the first is the one-off emergency MPC with full SMEB value and the second is the regular MPC to cover the basic needs.
The proposed activities outlined below will target approximately 7000 HHs/35000 vulnerable displaced and resident individuals in North West Syria (in Idleb and North Aleppo). 2,000 HHS will benefit from 3 rounds of emergency MPC ( 150 $) and 5000 HHs will benefit from 8 months of repetitive MPCA (100 $).
SARD MEAL team will conduct Distribution Monitoring and Post Distribution Monitoring activities for each round.
