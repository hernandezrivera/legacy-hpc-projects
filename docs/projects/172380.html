Project Summary

The humanitarian crisis in Zimbabwe is expected to have far-reaching implications for school readiness, attendance and participation. The Education Cluster estimates that, of the more than 3.4 million children of school going age (3 to 12 years) will need emergency and specialized education services. This includes more than half a million children in acute need, such as: children not enrolled in school; orphans and other vulnerable children, including children with disabilities and children living with HIV; and those in need of school feeding. The education system in Zimbabwe is already stretched as a result of multiple crises, including the impact of Cyclone Idai last year, the economic crisis coupled with hyperinflation and the ongoing drought and COVID-19 pandemic. Without a well-resourced response, the combined impact of these crises risks having a lasting negative children’s’ education and increasing drop outs. Without a conducive and disease-free school environment, COVID-19 poses a risk to children’s health and wellbeing.
Economic stress, further increased by the impact of COVID-, drought and Cyclone Idai has resulted in families living without access to basic services, increased stress in households, psychosocial distress and depression in children and caregivers, resulting in increased risk of sexual and other forms of violence and abuse against children and women.
The project is a humanitarian response to ensure continued access to safe and inclusive learning for children by minimizing the disruption of learning in 66 Wards of 3 Districts of Mashonaland Central province in Zimbabwe. The program aims to mitigate the immediate and long term effects of the current drought and effects of cyclone and COVID 19 in Zimbabwe in order to achieve overall child wellbeing by assisting 33 000 children in 3 targeted districts.

1 - Activities

Creating demand for Education through Campaigns and community dialoguesFOST will work through the School Development Councils for 66 schools to mobilse and sensitize communities on the importance of education as a right of children. The communities will be sensitized on the Education Amendment Act, advocating for increasing enrolment of girls and retaining them in school. Awareness raising will be done to retain pregnant girls and children living with disabilities in schools. The campaigns will also focus on prevention of SGBV for girls and women Provision of Access to Education for OVC through Block grantsFOST will reduce school drop outs for very vulnerable children by ensuring free access to education through payment of School Block Grants to 66 schools and provision of stationary to 3300 direct beneficiaries (50 children per school multiplied by the 66 targeted schools). Section of beneficiaries should ensure an equal number of boys and girls with particular attention to children with disability and those that had already droped out or at risk of droping out of school. On average, at least, 33 000 children will benefit (directly and indirectly) from the block grants. This is from the 66 targeted schools with an average enrolment of 500 children per each school. Provision PSS in emergencies through trainings for teachers The multiple crises experienced by families and communities have impacted on the Psychosocial wellbeing of children. FOST will train two teachers from each of the 66 schools (1 school in each of the 66 wards) reaching 132 teachers in total. Each school will have 1 Male and 1 female teacher trained on PSS and emergency sensitive education delivery to learners. Each of the 66 schools will be expected to establish either an in school or out of school club for children in the community that will act as a source of moral support and a place where children can access SRHR information. In these clubs children will also have space to support each other to do their homework from school School Feeding Committees Trainings for schoolsEach targeted school will have at least one nutritional garden with drought resistant vegetables for relish for the school feeding for learners. FOST will support these schools with the drought resilient vegetables and some gardening equipment. Each school will have a functional school feeding committee comprising of male and female school staff, school development committee members, health workers and village head representatives who will be trained on proper hygiene, food handling and storage for the school feeding program. Schools will be encouraged to use the block grant to buy materials such as feeding utensils and construction of a food preparation site

8 - Accountability to Affected People
8.1 - Describe how your project was designed in consultation with communities? How will you incorporate communities in project implementation and what feedback mechanisms do you have in place?

Community leaders will contribute to decisions about the location of the activities, selection of kid’s club leaders, and about systems and policies to ensure that children are safe, secure and well supported in their communities. The community and schools will select the children and teachers respectively to participate and benefit from the project. The Ministry of Education at district level will identify the schools to participate in the project basing on vulnerability levels FOST will apply participatory approaches in terms of its implementation strategies across all the objectives stated in this proposal. The participatory approaches will ensure among other things community ownership and internalization of the project implementation processes for long term sustainability. For years FOST volunteers have acted as interface of the community they choose to serve and their commitment to cascade skills and to provide services to the more vulnerable members of the community have been essential to child protection and the livelihoods of many poor families and their children. From teachers who offer psycho-social support to children and colleagues in their schools, to Health Workers who are still the first port of call for anybody who needs medical care in farms, to Youth Volunteers who lead Kid’s Clubs, one of the main strengths of FOST is to have been able to engage this invaluable human resource To ensure effective information sharing and communication channels, sharing information about FOST programmes will be done in a timely, accessible and inclusive way that put affected communities in a position to understand and shape decisions that impact their lives. Effective feedback channels as well as complaints and response mechanisms will be established, so that FOST and its partners will be aware of what impact programmes were having on participants and could incorporate feedback or address problems rapidly, including prevention of sexual abuse and exploitation.
