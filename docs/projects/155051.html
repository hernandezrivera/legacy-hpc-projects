Project Summary

PUI, jointly with Aquassistance, carried out a WaSH, Health and Nutrition assessment in July and August 2018. As a result, PUI developed an integrated approach in these three sectors that aimes at reducing public health risks. Thus, the intervention proposed is integrated in this approach, with the objective to support both Rohingya population and host communities through a better access to quality primary health care in Cox's Bazaar district. More specifically, PUI teams will built two PHCC to provide a comprehensive package of primary health care services, set-up a referral mechanism for emergencies and disseminate information and sensitization sessions. This project also include gender issues, aiming at improve access to quality health services for women. Furthermore, PUI will develop monitoring mechanisms and participate to coordination systems in order to ensure a relevant and coordinated response, based on the needs.

Needs

Through the 2019 Joint Response Plan, over 1.2 million people are identified in need of humanitarian assistance in Bangladesh from both Rohingya refugees and members of host communities. In July 2018, Première Urgence Internationale and Aquassistance carried out a WaSH, Health and Nutrition assessment in both Teknaf and Ukhia upazilas. The results showed that, 12 months after the beginning of the crisis, the acute emergency phase is moving to a more stable emergency phase but challenges are still substantial. In particular, WaSH, Nutrition and Health needs are major priorities. Whilst the target of health facilities per population is met, distribution, capacities and access are inequitable. Visits to facilities revealed that most have reached their maximum capacity and are now saturated. There is a lack of space, equipment, medicines and trained medical staff required for consultations needs. Furthermore, most of the facilities have difficult access, which constitutes an additional obstacle to access healthcare for the populations of the area. Thus, communicable diseases, especially vector borne diseases such as chikungunya, dengue malaria, are the most common causes of morbidity. Outbreaks of preventable diseases are also a recurring problem. Interrelated determinants of living conditions, access to health services, feeding practices, immunization rate, and nutrition status are substantial factors that contribute to high mortality rates among vulnerable groups. Basic Emergency Obstetric and Neonatal Cares (BEmONC) is also a critical gap. Finally, PUI’s Rapid Health Need Assessment (December 2017) and IOM’s May 2018 report both highlight the very high need for Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS) services. Sensitization and Information issues are critical, bad habits and weak knowledges having strong consequences on health. Access and gender issues are also critical. Rohingya women and girls have been disproportionately affected by the crisis. After the mass killings and displacement to Bangladesh, many households are female-headed. In these, Rohingya women and children are exposed to additional risks of further sexual exploitation, abuse and trafficking by both refugees and Bangladeshis. In addition, Health and WaSH facilities were constructed rapidly in the camps, sometimes without consideration for cultural and gender norms which leads to exclusion. For example, in 57% of households, women feel unsafe using latrines at night (REACH). Furthermore, the coverage of preventive maternal and child care and SRH services remains low with quality issues. Hygiene practices and knowledge about young child feeding are not widespread enough as well. The participation of women in camp governance and cultural activities is, in a similar way, extremely low due to conservative cultural barriers and limited empowerment opportunities. Health, Nutrition, and WaSH are interconnected needs and should be understood together. Indeed, insufficient quantity and poor quality of the water as well as inadequacy and insufficiency of WaSH facilities led to health risks and diseases. Health and Nutrition issues also have an impact on the hygiene: if most of the population receives hygiene kits regularly, many of the refugees sell the products to pay rent and buy food and medicine instead. Thus, a comprehensive approach on Health, Nutrition and WaSH helps to reduce public health risks and to act on the main drivers that reduce the morbidity and mortality of the most vulnerable people.

Activities or outputs

Output 1: Quality primary health care is provided for vulnerable populations 1.1. Install primary health care facilitiesTwo PHCC will be built, in line with sector minimum service package. All stakeholders working in the Health sector will be involved in identifying the location of the PHCC in order to avoid duplication of activities. 1.2. Provide a comprehensive package of primary health care servicesIn line with the sector mininum package, services provided will include curative and preventive care for women and children, basic treatment of non-communicable diseases, an EPI with routine vaccination, growth monitoring and psychosocial support counselling. A maternity ward will also be included. Care will be provided free of charge for PLW, children under 5, and those with the highest mortality and morbidity rates. 1.3. Set-up a referral and counter-referral mechanism for obstetrical and pediatric emergenciesPaediatric and obstetric emergencies that cannot be handled on site will be referred to a higher level health facility as well as the most serious cases of mental health. 1.4. Information and awareness of communities about Health, Nutrition and Hygiene good practicesA team of Health and Hygiene Promoters will be trained and equipped with IEC tools to raise awareness of good health practices. Hygiene promotion and water disease prevention sessions will be implemented. 1.5. Capacity building of medical staffIn order to guarantee the quality of the care provided, PUI staffs will benefit from training on the themes identified as priorities at the beginning of the project. Output 2: Access to quality health services is improved for women 2.1. Provide Sexual and Reproductive Health services SRHS will be offered, both curative and preventive. Pregnancies identified as high-risk will be referred to a higher-level structure. 2.2. Improve access to the health facilities for women and girlsPUI will promote and develop means to improve their safety and privacy. In coordination with the SMS agency and the camp strategy, equipment such as streetlights will be installed. In the PHCC, appropriately gendered measures will be taken, such as consultation areas exclusively reserved for women and supervised by female medical staff.2.3. Sensitization and information about women health and hygiene practicesA team of Health and Hygiene Promoters will be trained and equipped to sensitize women on good hygiene and health practices. PUI teams will also disseminate information on services available for women.2.4. Identification, care and management of psychosocial cases (gender-based)PUI will provide the minimum package of activities required of the psychosocial support. PUI will also undertake the clinical management of rape and sexual violence cases, whilst referring them to the appropriate Protection actor for further case management. Output 3: Health sector coordination and monitoring of the response and the services provided are strengthened 3.1. Setting-up a complaints and feedback mechanismCFM desks will be set-up in order to address complaints from community members regarding PUI health activities. Complaints will be addressed either on-site when possible or referred to the Site Management Agency or the appropriate partner. The complaints addressed will be follow-up to ensure the efficiency of the response. 3.2. Regular assessments and monitoring of the activitiesThe quality of the response and of the activities provided by PUI will be regularly assessed through individual field visits, satisfaction surveys or trainings. 3.3. Active participation to health coordination mechanisms PUI teams will participate actively to the sectoral coordination meetings and will share monitoring and evaluation data with the partners on a monthly basis or when relevant to improve collective knowledge and to ensure a coordinated response.
