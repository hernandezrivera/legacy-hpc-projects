Project Summary

UNHCR leads two Sectors – Emergency Shelter and Non-Food Items (ES/NFI) and Protection, in addition to partaking in coordination in the RRR Sector both at the National and Sub National Level. UNHCR interventions target all five Darfur States, South, West and North Kordofan states, in addition to Blue Nile State for IDP (new and protracted) – an estimated 430,000 individuals and 300,000 individuals’ returnees. UNHCR will improve the protection of IDPs and returnees through durable solutions profiling, strengthened monitoring, provision of community-based protection initiatives to enhance resilience, shelters/NFI and capacity of partners at the national and state level. UNHCR will target all new IDPs with protection and ES/NFI, and provide assistance to protracted IDPs and returnees based on needs assessment and verification. UNHCR will engage and lead needs assessments and verification of beneficiaries of ES/NFI to ensure priority assistance is given to people with specific needs (PSNs) and women (targeting an estimated 60%) in coordination with government counterparts (Ministry of Social Welfare, Humanitarian Aid Commissioner and Voluntary Return and Reintegration Commission), and partners. Thematic issues such as disaster risk reduction for fire and flood affected households, using environmental friendly shelter solutions, and engage with local communities to ensure their participation. Lastly, in line with Accountability to Affected Populations, UNHCR will prioritize continuous consultations before interventions, conduct monitoring on-site during interventions and also Post Distribution Monitoring.

Needs

According to the Government of the Sudan, the numbers of internally displaced people is some 1.9 million individuals across Sudan in 2019, with returnees estimated at 324,328 individuals. The vast majority of the IDPs in Sudan has been displaced for several years, with limited prospects for durable solutions (protracted displacement). While security has improved significantly in areas affected by internal displacement, IDPs continue to face risks to their physical safety and integrity. In areas close to ceasefire lines in Jabal Marra, and South Kordofan, the presence of military/militias deployed expose IDPs, returnees or local residents to grave protection risks. Women and girls are particularly exposed when moving to work on fields, collect water or firewood, or going to markets, while men and boys risk arbitrary arrest and beating. IDPs attempting to go back to their home areas and cultivate their land have also been exposed to threats and violence. Returnee numbers have increased since 2015 but continue to lack access to adequate basic services including water, health and nutrition services; water supply and dwell in temporary shelters for extended periods of time. Furthermore, the number of inter-communal violence has decreased, localized armed violence takes place, clashing over access to, use and management of resources, especially land and water. Returnees have also lost their assets and may have limited livelihood opportunities necessary for effective reintegration in their place of origin (especially affecting women and youth). Return areas are scattered within the locality which poses a further challenge for the provision of services. Critical needs in return areas require that basic services be available, in order to provide assistant such as shelter through building materials or funds to rehabilitate their destroyed or abandoned shelters. There also remains an urgent need for peace building and reconciliation related interventions to reduce tension among communities, particularly among returnees and nomadic communities.In 2018, UNHCR reported around 320,000 assisted Sudanese refugees living in 12 camps in Chad. With improvements in the security situation in most conflict areas in Darfur, there have been reports of spontaneous returns of recognized Sudanese refugees from Chad to their areas of origin in Central, West and North Darfur. As in other return areas in North and Central Darfur, the main reason for return of refugees from Chad include the relatively calm security situation in places of origin, reduction of humanitarian assistance in the camps, change of curriculum, and restriction of movement outside the camps. Some refugees and IDPs that returned to Darfur were unable to return to their places of origin. Reportedly, many are willing to return to their home villages, but remain concerned about safety and security in their places of origin.

Activities

All the five Darfur States, Kordofans will be will be targeted for this intervention – while planned response in areas in Blue Nile State. UNHCR prioritization for the IDP response is determined by the type of displaced population while exploring durable solutions. UNHCR will continue to respond to IDP emergency response, which includes new displaced population in additions to newly accessed IDPs. Given a large caseload of protracted population, UNHCR will continue to engage with interventions through its Sector responsibilities as coordinating agency in the Protection and ES-NFI Sector, and partaking in the RRR Sector. Key activities under include leading coordination of the Sectors, participation in inter-agency assessment missions; procurement and managing the NFI Core Pipeline, , conducting trainings and workshops for community leaders, service providers and local authorities on protection for protection priority areas; advocacy and policy through relevant forum on plans for durable solutions; and synchronized approach with refugee operation where specific locations host refugees. In 2019, UNHCR will continue to work on voluntary repatriation of Sudanese refugees, supporting the return of some 20,000 Sudanese refugees from camps in Chad to villages in North, West and Central Darfur. Additionally, 10,000 refugees that spontaneously returned to Darfur from Chad will benefit from reintegration assistance. Six reception centres will be maintained at key border crossings. Protection services including counselling, family tracing and reunification, awareness raising, as well as referral pathways for PSN will continue to be provided. UNHCR will also be working with local authorities to facilitate access to land and documentation in Sudan, and families will be provided with cash grants or provision of shelter materials to re-establish their homes in Darfur.All activities will consider the different protection needs of Women, men, children and People with Special Needs

Impact on environment/natural resources

There is expectation of some negative impact on the environment due to the fuel usage, the fossil fuel used for energy and lighting, however, UNHCR take mitigation measures in terms of reforestation, distribution of Ethanol instead of charcoal, awareness raising and discussion with the persons on concern on the negative impact. The SSAFE strategy is currently rolled-out in different parts of Sudan to ensure there is minimal effect on natural resources, while improving the balance by different environment-enhancing activities. Impact on environment/natural resources. UNHCR will engage with communities on orientation sessions on environmental conservation, promote more durable shelter solutions through community engagement in shelter constructions and tapping into traditional shelter building techniques.
