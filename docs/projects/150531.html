Project Summary

Arabbuna, Abdallah Younes and Barta'a supply and hygiene promotion project aims to improve the health situation of 11191 individuals in those three mentioned regions. The inhabitants of those communities are currently suffering from chronic health problems due to lack of access to clean and adequate water and hygiene education that in turn resulted in poor economic status. The project will address these key needs which form the basis of all development through participatory, inclusive and effective means to bring about the following life changing project purposes:
•Fulfillment of fundamental of human right to water by Ensure equitable, sufficient and sustainable access to basic quality WASH services, available when needed in accordance with safety and dignity of the unserved, undeserved and most vulnerable Palestinians under occupation in Araboneh by enhance access to water through installation of water networks and construction of water reservoir.
•Strengthening national WASH sector accountability, governance system, advocacy, evidence-based planning, and coordination mechanism including inter-sectorial capacity.
The project will have primary beneficiaries of 11191 people with access to safe and adequate water and hygiene education.
The Union of Agriculture Work Committee will implement the project with the Municipality Council and the target beneficiaries and CBOs. The project will be implemented by integrating water supply, facilities and mass hygiene education as a package, community participation, and Capacity development of CBOs.

Needs

Water is an essential condition for life, which is indispensable for development, ensuring nutrition, providing health and environmental services. A needs assessment conducted in May 2018 reveals that Arabbuna, Abdallah Younes and Barta'a communities located in Jenin are in dire need of improved access to water services. The needs assessment revealed that “there are no network water available, and the families take the water by water tanker from A number of unsafe and unreliable sources. The magnitude of the problem is illustrated by an increase in water demand in the summer, where families cannot access water more than five times per month. Social security of these communities require the accessibility to clean water sources and the necessity to secure water and sanitation. Due to low coverage of improved water supply and the utilization of polluted water sources, the inhabitants are at risk of increased mortality. The results of the survey carried out for a number of residents of the region and the council showed the following.1. High population growth.2. Diseases caused by the use of pool water and water tanks are considered the biggest pathogen among residents of the area because of polluted collection water and mobile tanks that bring water from unreliable sources and because of the erosion of these tanks, which results in increased spending on treatment.3. The per capita range water supply in Araboneh from 15 - 20 liters per day while the minimum recommended by the World Health Organization is 100 liters per day.4. The cost of a one Cubic meters of water increases up to NIS 15 at the peak period in summer.5. Lack of water in schools and public and health facilities in the village due to lack of water availability.

Activities

A considerable part of Arabbuna which is about one fourth of the community is located in Area C and consisting of 254 individuals who are not connected to a water network similarly to those living within the other part of the community within Areas A & B. Similarly, Abdallah Younis Village and Barta'a are located totally in Area C with a total number of 10167 individuals. Activities are designed to enhance accessibility to water sources and increase water storage in significance of better water consumption and reduced expenditure allocated to purchase water. Furthermore, the activities are planned to improve the sphere of water and sanitation and protect the communities that utilize unreliable water sources and require the availability of water networks. Therefore, the project outputs are assumed as follows: Output 1. The safe water supply coverage of the districts increased.Output 2. Elapsed time to fetch water for rural women and girls reduced. Output 3. The project district population with access to and use of sanitation facilities increased.The project activities are as follows: • Construction local network (Transmission line 4 Diameter 1400 m, 3 diameter 1770 m, 2 diameter 11500 m, 1 diameter 1000 m • Construction of 430 House Connection• Awareness raising sessions for both men and women will be organized separately to promote good hygiene practices (focusing on hand washing with soap, self-cleaning, etc), saving and recycling of water will be provided to women and men at household and community level.

Indicators

Monitoring and Evaluation: A baseline survey will be conducted at initiation of the project, which will be updated and compared to a final evaluation. Information collected through semi structured interviews, household visits and field observations. On-going monitoring will allow UAWC to evaluate methodologies and impact of the activities, to identify challenges and best practices; information will be gathered and analyzed on monthly basis. Particular attention will be paid to the methods of working with local communities and protections committees. UAWC will ensure proper mechanism of asset distribution, gender sensitivities, as well as the appropriateness of the intervention in the target area. Project activities will be monitored by a Project Management Team composed of key stakeholders responsible for monitoring and evaluation; the results communicated to humanitarian actors and project beneficiaries.Indicators include: Indicator 1. 40% decrease in the price of a cubic meter Indicator 2. Monthly household consumption increased from 10 CM to 20. Indicator 3. 30% of polluted water transformed to healthy and hygiene water. Indicator 4. 20% of males and females practice hygiene.
