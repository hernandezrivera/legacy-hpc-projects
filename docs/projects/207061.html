Project Summary

For more than seven years now, NW and SW regions have been the center of violence that has resulted in the displacement of over 630,000 individuals (UNICEF, July 2023). The conflict has aggravated the humanitarian situation with needs ranging from immediate food assistance, livelihoods enhancement, protection, shelter/NFI, water and sanitation as well as access to health and education. The majority of the displaced persons especially youths tend to engage in negative coping strategies such as survival sex, child marriage, street hawking and petty theft.
The prolonged crises often cause adverse impacts that accumulate by not allowing affected households to rebuild their livelihoods and recover. The accumulated negative impacts of protracted or recurring shocks on household livelihoods have long-term effects and reinforce intergenerational transmission of poverty and pushing poor households further into greater vulnerability. As much attention is given to saving lives, equal attention must be given to saving livelihoods in order to achieve household and community resilience. In the two affected regions, damage to livelihoods has resulted not only in already limited income sources becoming further diminished, but also in households losing one or more of their food sources.
This project aims to provide integrated food and nutritional assistance to crisis-affected populations (men, women, girls, boys, PLHIV and PWD) in Boyo and Momo divisions of the North West Region of Cameroon as a means to support their self-reliance and recovery needs. The project will implement Emergency General Food Assistance (in kind), strengthen and support livelihoods capacities as well provide Blanket Supplementary Feeding to vulnerable Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) and host communities in target areas. In this intervention, order to sustainable support food security for those assisted, COMINSUD plans to enhance capacities for at least 300 households (50% women headed, 4 % persons with a disability, 2%single mothers) in the area of livelihoods enhancement. To achieve this COMINSUD will identify from the targeted food recipient households critical cases that it will train on at least one out of 5 clusters of livelihoods activities (livestock – poultry, piggery, gardening, petty trade/retail trade, restuaration/food service) and provide a start-up cash support plus technical follow ups to render these supported households self-sufficient by the end of the year
This project alignments with Specific Outcome 1 (SO1) of WFP’s Country Strategic Plan (2022-2026): Crisis-affected populations including refugees, internally displaced persons (IDPs), returnees and host populations in Cameroon have safe access to adequate and nutritious food during and in the aftermath of crises. The project will contribute to vision 2030 (i.e., the world has eradicated food insecurity and malnutrition) and SDG 2 (i.e., Zero Hunger).
Specifically the project will ensure continued and regular access to adequate and nutritious food through general food assistance (In kind) to 10,000 crisis-affected women, men, girls, and boys in Boyo and Momo divisions (North-West region) by the end of December 2024 as well as enhance livelihoods for 300 households within the targeted beneficiaries while promoting inclusion of women, men, girls, boys, PWD, PLHIV in all aspects of the project and ensure everyone has equal opportunities, rights, and obligations to benefit.
The project will as well enhance improvement in nutrition-related practices through social and behavioral change communication (SBCC) to 80,000 people in the target area.

1.1 Décrivez le projet avec les activités et leur mise en oeuvre, le lien avec les besoins ainsi que les résultats attendus / Please describe the assessed needs the project will address through its activities.

In 2023, an estimate of 4.7 million people are in need humanitarian assistance across the country, indicating an increase of 21 percent from 2022. OCHA in their report between January 2023 to June 2023 indicated that out of the 4.7 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, only 2.7 million people were targeted, out of this number targeted, just 758K were reached with assistance, indicating a 28 % reached.In the South-West and North-West, severe acute malnutrition rates vary from 0 to 2% on areas where screening was done (partners monitoring data). Results from the March mass MUAC screening of over 58,000 children, in 17 health districts, identified 1,700 wasting cases (including up to 300 SAM cases). Some health districts of concern are Ako, Bafut, Fundong and Mbengwi in the North-West (Nutrition Cluster NUICEF June 2023) . It is estimated that in 2023, 291,862 children under 5 will suffer from wasting including 111,817 children with severe wasting, underscoring the need to continue support to the treatment programme. This can be true because 32000 out of 1000 boys and girls aged 6-23 months enrolled in the Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme (BSFP) and no boys and girls, 6 -59 months were admitted into the integrated management of severe acute malnutrition programme in the North-West and South-West regions.According to UNICEF, limited funding, and restricted access hindered the geographical coverage of nutritional response; especially SAM treatment. However, UNICEF partners in the NWSW nutrition cluster conducted a mass MUAC screening exercises in November 2022, over 55,500 children were screened in both regions with more SAM cases in NW (394) and SW (94). There is an urgent need to ensure continuity of care and consistency between the screening, referral, and treatment of children with SAM in 2024.Partners such as WFP and UNICEF have put in place a horizon to reach 78,196 children aged 6 to 59 months with severe acute malnutrition with treatment, and 452,000 primary caregivers of children aged 0 to 23 months to receive infant and young child feeding counselling. In 2023, it is estimated that up to 48,800 boys and girls under 5 years are likely to develop acute malnutrition in the NW and SW, an estimated 291,862 children under 5 will suffer from wasting. This includes 111,817 children underscoring the need to support the prevention programme.Households in the crisis affected regions are in need of regular access to adequate and nutritious food through general food assistanceAs much attention is given to saving lives, equal attention must be given to saving livelihoods in order to achieve household and community resilience. In the two affected regions, damage to livelihoods has resulted not only in already limited income sources becoming further diminished, but also in households losing one or more of their food sources.The number of persons displaced and or returning and a large number of host community receiving food assistance is an indication of the severity of livelihoods means that have been lost in the crisis. There is a strong need to enhance the livelihoods means of those receiving general food assistance so that in the long run they become self-sufficient in quality food at household level.
