Project Summary

ACTED, Terre des Hommes (Tdh) and IMPACT Initiatives will collaborate in partnership to deliver life-saving and multi-sectoral assistance to vulnerable and conflict affected populations in Northeast Nigeria, by providing assistance within a framework centred on delivering evidence-based, coordinated, and principled humanitarian relief. Catalyzing on existing partnership structures, complimentary technical expertise, and strong experience delivering critical relief to populations in Northeast Nigeria, the Consortium will ensure integral value add through the proposed continued partnership. The Action has been designed based on findings from the multi-sector needs assessment (MSNA), led by REACH Initiative in June/July 2019 (Annex 5), and which highlights critical gaps within the target sectors. Further, the proposed Action is underpinned by lessons learned from ongoing activities implemented by all Consortium partners, in order to ensure an iterative adaptation of ongoing life-saving services for contextualized and effective response.
Through the joint venture, critical CCCM, shelter, NFI, HLP, child protection, and evidence-based information management services will be provided, underpinned by cross-cutting protection thematics emphasizing protection mainstreaming and beneficiary participatory approaches. The delivery of multi-sectoral assistance, coupled with robust coordination mechanisms and participatory approaches, aim to enhance sustainability and catalyse synergies between humanitarian partners and stakeholders. The proposed Action thereby aims to improve rationalisation of resources for increased efficiacy of humanitarian interventions via strengthened information management and coordination mechanisms, and promote protection mainstreamed systemic responses. Further, the Action will provide evidence-based information management support services for closure of critical information gaps, benefitting all operational humanitarian actors in the Northeast.

1 - Needs

The proposed Action is designed to target critical needs facing conflict- and displacement-affected communities within the setting of the protracted conflict in the Northeast of Nigeria, via the delivery of multi-sectoral assistance. At the core of the programmatic strategy adopted under this Consortium approach, is the objective to increase safety, dignity and protection of the most vulnerable. Partners aim to achieve this objective through delivery of assistance packages that link within the greater humanitarian intervention strategies, target gaps, enhance protection mainstreaming in target sites, and deliver hard assistance packages to the most vulnerable. To contribute to improving equitable access to critical services in a timely manner, underpinning the dignity, safety, and overall protection of displaced communities, ACTED is proposing the continued delivery of CCCM services in Stadium Camp, MMC. In partnership, to ensure protection of the most vulnerable members of the community is safegaurded, Tdh will deliver child protection services targeting children and adolescents residing in the camp. The dual approach aims to provide a strong and cross-cutting protection mainstreaming strategy, facilitating not only dignified living conditions, equitable service access, and coordinated camp-level humanitarian systemic response, but also the delivery of specific and tailored assistance to children and adolescents with critical protection needs. Moreover, in Monguno Town, ACTED and Tdh have developed an approach for joint targeting of unmet needs of the most vulnerable IDPs through delivery of protection-centred response. Tdh’s child protection component aims to institute safeguarding mechanisms for children and adolescents living in camp settings, bolstered through the ability to provide shelter and non-food item assistance via internal referral pathways to ACTED. The continuation of existing internal referral pathways between the partners from current interventions will be catalysed to strengthen this approach, and also extended to other operational protection actors, facilitating direct access to shelter and NFI assistance for protection cases across Monguno Town. In both Stadium Camp and in Monguno Town, the Consortium partnership aims to address continued humanitarian needs, using an approach that directly addresses protection concerns faced by IDPs. CCCM activities proposed under the intervention are in line with ACTED’s 2020 CCCM Strategy, with the objective of achieving safety, dignity, and protection, also including legal stay, of IDPs (see Annex 15, 2020 CCCM Strategy). To enhance mechanisms for protection of the most vulnerable members of the beneficiary communities, Tdh will implement child protection (CP) activities to respond to the risks and consequences of violence and abuse for children, as well as caregivers, in Stadium Camp, Maiduguri, and Veterinary Camp, Monguno. Protection services will include psychosocial support in the child and youth-friendly spaces, providing critical rehabilitation and resilience building opportunities for the most vulnerable. The current project constitutes the CP component of Tdh’s ongoing ECHO-funded integrated CP and Education in Emergencies (EiE) program in Maiduguri, and alternative funding is being sought for the EiE component. Moreover, to address the vast unmet needs of displacement-affected populations living in informal sites, often facing critical service gaps due to low humanitarian engagement, ACTED is proposing a mobile component, capable of delivering mobile CCCM, Shelter, non-food item, and HLP assistance to vulnerable populations in informal sites. Finally, REACH implements a yearly, methodologically-sound and representative study of needs of all affected populations in all accessible areas and a more regular, more qualitative and de facto less representative study on needs of affected populations in hard-to-reach areas, as well as general information management.

2 - Activities

1. Camp Coordination and Information Management ACTED will conduct a safety audit of Stadium, assessing available services, equitable access to services, and camp development needs. Over the course of the Action, two safety audits will be conducted (Quarter 1, Quarter 3); safety audits will serve to inform the development of the Humanitarian Site Development Plan (HSDP), setting clear objectives for site development and service needs in Stadium Camp, as well as one multi-sector contingency plan. Partner and service mapping will be conducted, producing outputs on who is doing what, where, and when (4W), coupled with monthly site profiling in order to track camp demographics, movements, developments in context, and collect partner reports on activities.2. Participatory Camp Governance ACTED will continue to facilitate existing camp committees in Stadium Camp, in line with Sector Standards to ensure harmonization, currently consisting of the following 8 committees: Camp Management Committee, Security/Watch Committee, Site Maintenance Committee, Health Committee, Food Security Committee, POC Committees (Women, Youth, Elderly and PSN). ACTED CCCM will support the appointment and transition of camp committee members, stipulating a one-year membership, with a minimum of 10 members per committee, to ensure equal and fair representation across site resident communities. All camp committees will be provided with support to review existing, and develop new frameworks for, respective TORs. To ensure understanding, application, and enforcement of protection mainstreaming principles, as well as the ability to report any gaps, ACTED will provide a five-day training to the Youth, Women and Camp Management committees, covering the following modules: Introduction to Humanitarianism and CCCM, CCCM Roles and Responsibilities, General Protection Principles, PSEA, and Introduction to Partners. 3. Communication and community engagement ACTED CCCM Team will facilitate quarterly Town Hall meetings with the beneficiary community, outreach programs, including door to door campaigns and megaphone announcements, and will maintain information boards within the camp to ensure reliable access to information on available services and key messages. The CCCM Team will set up and maintain one complaints, enquires and information (CEI) desk three days a week, accessible to all camp residents. ACTED will encourage community documentary making, via the ‘Our Camp, The Series’ initiative, enabling community members to produce videos on life in the camp, and their own stories. ACTED will support development of 6 PSA videos, facilitate the identification and implementation of community initiatives, and construct community centre using upcycled plastic bottle waste.4. Site maintenance and site improvements ACTED will maintain all lighting installations provided in 2019, strengthen fire preparedness and response capacity within Stadium Camp, ensure the sustainability of solid waste management system, facilitate collection of plastic bottles for the pilot construction of the community centre 5. Emergency shelter assistance to vulnerable IDPs in MongunoShelter construction will be conducted by ACTED’s technical Shelter Team (Bama type)6. Emergency NFI assistance to vulnerable IDPs in MongunoACTED provides provide critical non-food item assistance in line with the Shelter/NFI Sector approved ‘Improved NFI Kit’ standard, while ensuring beneficiary households have the claim to decide which items within the kit, as well as quantities of items, best reflect their personal needs. 6. Post Distribution Monitoring To ensure accountability, transparency, and effective learning mechanisms via integration of beneficiary feedback, ACTED’s independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) will conduct quarterly post distribution monitoring exercises to assess Shelter and NFI interventions.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

- ACTED’s CCCM Strategy prioritizes protection mainstreaming, promoting a rights-based approach for site residents, through dual pillars of participatory governance and AAP. Fundamental to successful implementation of CCCM services based on this two-tiered approach, is the facilitation of platforms for active and community driven camp governance structures. ACTED’s camp governance activities will support existing camp committees in Stadium Camp, comprised of members that represent the full spectrum of camp residents, including women, youth, older persons, and people with specific needs (PSNs). Camp committee meetings and governance platforms aim to support site residents in realizing their rights, and have their voices heard and included in greater humanitarian system programming at the camp level, ensuring that concerns and needs of the most marginalized are proactively included and addressed via participatory governance structures. - To ensure understanding, application, and enforcement of protection mainstreaming principles, as well as the ability to report any gaps, ACTED will provide a five-day training to the Youth, Women and Camp Management committees, covering the following modules: Introduction to Humanitarianism and CCCM, CCCM Roles and Responsibilities, General Protection Principles, PSEA, and Introduction to Partners. These trainings aim to ensure that camp committee members understand basic principles of CCCM, protection principles, PSEA, who provides what services, and reporting mechanisms. These trainings, combined with consistent communication and community participation, serve to underpin the community’s ability to hold humanitarian partners accountable, and thereby reinforce AAP.- To ensure equitable access to services, and uphold protection mainstreaming as central to the wellbeing, safety and dignity of camp residents, ACTED will facilitate site maintenance and site improvement works in Stadium Camp throughout project duration. Information generated through participatory hazard mapping, the development of multi-sectoral contingency plans, and continuous site monitoring and profiling activites, ACTED CCCM Team will work to proactively identify priority site care, maintenance, and improvement needs. - Staff and workers from national and international NGOs (which includes the staff of consortium members) and government representatives who will be trained on general Child Protection, Child Protection Mainstreaming into their respective sectoral responses, safe identification, and safe referrals of vulnerable children- Trainings will thereby also include recording and reporting on needs and gaps faced by the community, prioritization of available resources, coordination with partners when available, and ensuring strong understanding of protection mainstreaming principles and how to apply them. ACTED’s Mobile Team will be strengthend by the inclusion of one Protection/HLP Officer, which will further support in providing the community with information on housing, land and property rights, and support community governance structures as well as local leaders in building knowledge of HLP and capacity to mitigiate HLP protection risks.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

- Under all activities, Under the proposed activity, ACTED will ensure that complaints and feedback response mechanisms (CRM) are available for all beneficiaries. All beneficiaries will receive information on how to lodge complaints with the relevant CCCM Agency in target camps, enabling referrals back to ACTED for further follow up and closure. In addition, ACTED will develop an electronic CRM form accessible on Kobo, in order to verify receipt of aid entitlements with the beneficiary at the time of aid delivery. In the event the beneficiary needs to make further inquires or complaints however, ACTED will continue to receive referred feedback from the CCCM Agent (INTERSOS and IOM in Monguno). - To facilitate identification of best practices, lessons learned, and to close the feedback loop between beneficiaries and ACTED Program Teams, ACTED will return to each site of intervention post-exit in order to conduct the post-exit survey, as well as to conduct post distribution monitoring as relevant in line with the shelter and/or non-food item assistance delivered at the location. The post exit survey will collect information on the sustainability of the mobile CCCM intervention, on continued functionality and operation of CMCs and SMCs, successful implementation of critical risk mitigation and response strategies utilizing resources and knowledge provided by the ACTED Team, as well as beneficiary perception on usefulness of the intervention. This survey will aim to assess ability of key local stakeholders in continuing to run day-to-day operations of the site, as well as to collect information on continuing needs in the target locations. In addition, this survey will collect data on increased capacity of CMCs and SMCs to engage with other operational partners, as relevant. The committees set up by ACTED will have received training on communication, including reporting to operational partners on critical gaps and needs.
