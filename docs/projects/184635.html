Project Summary

DKH and its implementing partners (IPs) in Somalia plan to continue targeting populations facing severe acute food insecurity and contribute to protecting the livelihoods of vulnerable populations experiencing multiple declines in food and income sources due to recurrent disasters, conflicts, and COVID-19 pandemic in Somalia. A total of 4,000 households (24,000 people; 13,680 F) vulnerable internally displaced persons (IDPs), destitute host communities, as well as food insecure and vulnerable host community households, particularly poor and marginalized women, men, and children, will be targeted by the project. The project strategies aim to protect the livelihoods, enhance local food production and adoption of resilient strategies to mitigate against recurrent shocks. Targeted beneficiaries for food access will receive support through both unconditional CVA, based on the regional MEB, and conditional cash for work/assets (CfW/CfA). The project will target the most vulnerable and marginalized riverine communities with conditional CfA and agriculture inputs to restore lost livelihoods and protect and support the building of key livelihood assets through rehabilitation of communities’ assets that were depleted by drought, floods, or conflicts. The daily cash rate for CfW/CfA will be determined below the local average daily pay for casual work. The beneficiaries will be identified by the communities, who will be sensitized to take ownership of the CfW projects. A community-driven approach will be applied to ensure that members of the community above 18 years of age, women, men, and people living with disabilities, are involved in the planning, selecting, implementation, and approaches of programmes, particularly in the selection of CfW schemes, taking into consideration the different obligations, challenges, time constraints, and priorities of women and men across the seasonal calendar. Most vulnerable IDP HHs with school-going boys and girls and people living with disabilities will be prioritized for support with food vouchers to facilitate their retention in school. Women will also be targeted for support to produce fodder for the livestock among rural populations facing the devastation of the desert locusts. The project will also promote governance on natural resources to not only be enhanced but also gain transformative capacity thereby reducing conflicts in the use and managing of resources. DKH and IPs will also seek to strengthen social capital among communities on the use of the rehabilitated water facilities for productive utilization. The project will prioritize community livelihood assets, mostly water sources will be rehabilitated and maintained as per community priority areas, which will be determined through a participatory approach to ensure that they are productive for the communities targeted. The IP will promote the use of improved agriculture technologies through the promotion of early-maturing, high yielding, drought-tolerant and disease tolerant crop varieties, small-scale irrigation, provide farming tools and seeds, train farmers, and conduct market assessments to inform interventions. Protection, gender, and human rights approaches will be mainstreamed in the program, ensuring that deserving cases are selected to benefit from the project, especially vulnerable women and those living with disabilities. DKH and IPs will use mobile money facilities for the convenience of access of the support by PLWD. To ensure accountability to people affected, DKH and IPs with promote consultations and information sharing on the project interventions, conduct Post Distribution Monitoring to gather information from sampled beneficiaries on the effectiveness of the project, promote use of community feedback mechanisms in place, ensuring that the beneficiaries contribute to the project implementation. To capture the details of the beneficiaries, the project will design tools disaggregating the data by age, sex and disability.

2.1.1 - What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

The use of cash enhances the decision-making and dignity of the beneficiary as they can make an independent choice, with the flexibility provided by cash, to purchase what they need. The project prefers the use of cash and voucher assistance as it provides vulnerable households with a source of income through CVA to enable them access to food during emergencies. While cash enhances the beneficiaries' income level, it also contributes to strengthening local market functionality with the injection of cash into the local markets. CVA are conditional to the building community assets that enhance recovery and reduce the effects of drought. Further, cash transfer is quicker and more cost-efficient than providing in-kind commodities, in addition to the items purchased by households being culturally appropriate and suit their specific needs.

2.1.2 - Explain how the project design considered market functionality?

The project has considered market functionality through conducting assessments, including market and seasonality analysis and post-distribution monitoring to assess effects on the market functionalities. DKH consider the use of EMMA tools to enable a detailed market analysis to support the market systems and to contribute to improving response analysis and programme implementation; and to better understand, accommodate, and make use of market systems. The use of these tools will therefore support consideration of market functions in the cash decision. The cash will be provided based on the minimum expenditure basket, ensuring that while the beneficiaries' purchasing power is increased they will be able to get access to livelihoods from the existing markets. DKH will also conduct PDM and market systems monitoring system which will also be used to monitor the effects of the cash distributed into the local market. The results of the seasonality analysis will inform the appropriate time of the year to implement the interventions, to ensure that the markets function as normal and beneficiaries are able to access goods and services from the markets. Besides, the amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market. The cash amounts distributed with be guided and based on the minimum expenditure basket (MEB) per region of interventions, as defined by the Somalia Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU). On the other hand, market functionality will also be considered for determining the amounts for multipurpose cash transfer, especially for the cash for assets aimed at rehabilitating livelihood assets for communities targeted with protection and restoration of lost livelihood sources, where market assessments will be conducted. During the implementation of the project, the team will liaise with other stakeholders to monitor the price behaviour and supplies for commodities to inform the effects the cash intervention is having on the local economy. Besides, the amount of cash being injected will be little and may not have a negative effect on the prices of goods in the local market. Decisions will be made appropriately to ensure the cash is used for the right purpose, with or little effects on the market functionality.
