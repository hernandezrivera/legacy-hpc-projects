Project Summary

Though the proposed intervention INTERSOS aims to provide emergency humanitarian assistance at Reception Centers and Camp settings in Ngala and Monguno LGAs. The rationale of the project is to address critical humanitarian needs which were not covered by the previous interventions as well as provide emergency humanitarian assistance for the newly displaced in the targeted locations. In the project focus areas, INTERSOS will scale up its intervention with provision of basic reception and hosting services to the new arrivals. INTERSOS will strive to fulfill the basic humanitarian needs of the most vulnerable communities through the provision of adequate temporary housing solutions and reception center facilities. With the assistance of other services providers, the project will enhance the different referral pathways and the referral mechanism. The project has been designed to meet the affected population’s most urgent needs in a sustainable way that empowers local communities and service providers by working through existing structures such as camp committees.

1 - Needs

Throughout the second part of 2019, the rainy season and an increase of security incidents significantly impacted the crisis-affected population in LGAs such as Ngala and Monguno. Camp settings in Monguno and Ngala experienced multiple incidents due to floods and fire outbreak contributing to suffering of the camp population. In August, a total of 1,656 HHs in Monguno and 317 in Ngala were affected by floods withrendered roads impassable, reduced accessibility and increased shelter/NFIs damages. The security incidents registered throughout the year triggered the displacement, particularly in Ngala where in July a total number of 1,992 individuals arrived due to increased violence and fear of being cut off from food assistance during the rainy season. In Ngala Reception Center a total of 3,306 new arrivals were registered from January to September 2019. From July to mid-October, due to the lack of available shelters in camp settings, the center overreached its capacity. Peak population was recorded in July with 501 HHs accommodated in the facility, 400% more than the planned capacity of 102 HHs. Ngala Arabic camp hosts 17,117 individuals. Through the daily monitor and dedicated shelter assessment, the need for renovation of shelters and re- design of the camp site plan emerged as a main need. As of October 2019, according to International Organization for Migration Displacement Tracking Matrix (IOM DTM), the town of Monguno is estimated to host 158,570 IDPs. Monguno Reception Center continued to receive an average of 100 HHs in the last 6 months. They were mostly displaced from Kukawa and Baga axis. INTERSOS is also currently managing 6 camps in Monguno (Kuya, Water board, GSSS, Ngurno, Fulatari, Stadium) where the population has reached19,636 individuals. The lack of space and land in the current camps remain a continuing challenge. While a new extension of Water Board camp has been identified and INTERSOS as CCCM agency is currently engaged in the finalization of the land demarcation and kick-off, a re-organization of space in the camps is required in order to continue to accommodate the new arrivals and to improve the living condition of camp population. Additionally, improvement of site plans will contribute to prevention of fire outbreaks and mitigate the risks of floods for the coming year.Since 2017, INTERSOS is managing reception centers and providing CCCM services in both locations. Following CCCM sector priorities and the dire needs of CCCM services, INTERSOS will continue to provide lifesaving and humanitarian assistance and to address the basic needs of 104,511 individuals still in need in Monguno and Ngala camps, by improving its camp coordination and management services, with particular attention at prevention and mitigation measures for floods and fire outbreaks risks and decongestion of the sites

2 - Activities

INTERSOS will improve site management through site mapping, site monitoring and management by providing Daily monitoring of the site and organizing coordination meetings such as CCCM meeting, CCCM LGA sector meetings to tackle upraising issues and allow for a platform for joint coordination of activities. The project will strengthen and build effective partnerships with all implementing partners through efficient representation and communication to enable identified enabling to identify needs and gaps in the camps. Additionally, in close collaboration with CCCM/SHELTER/NFI sector, INTERSOS will work to prevent and mitigate the risks of floods in the 7 camps.Site planning and upgrade of camp infrastructures:The site planning will outline demarcation of specific points such as burial grounds, waste management disposal sites and community engagement in site maintenance activities such as sanitation. Furthermore, through this activity provision of sand bags and back filling of pits, construction of water drainage channels, rehabilitation and drainage of exceeding water flow during the rainy season and decrease levels and areas of flooding within the camp, to avoid water stagnation will be granted.Training of community leaders and camp committees on CCCM issues: All the camp committees (10 per each camp) will receive refresh training on CCCM Roles and Responsibilities, including protection principles. In a second phase, workshops will be organized to improve the daily operation of these committees and strengthen their ownership of the camps. Community leaders will be also part of the training and workshops. Pre and post test will be filled out by the participants to assess the impact of the training. Session will be conducted in different local languages, according to the audience provenance INTERSOS will continue to provide day to day management of the facilities and maintenance of the structure throughout all the project implementation period. The Reception Center facilities were constructed in 2017 and since then continued to daily hosts hundreds of individuals. While INTERSOS staff ensures that the structures are properly used, the continuous influx requires that the facilities are properly maintained with small works. The Reception Center managers will identify the maintenance needs and address it, ensuring that the facilities are maintained up to standards.
