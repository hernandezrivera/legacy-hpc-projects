Project Summary

The overall objective of the project is to provide emergency and sustainable WASH services to the disaster affected IDPs and host communities in Puntland and Hieshabelle of Somalia. At the inception of the project, AADSOM will hold a workshop to the prominent community members including women and IDPs to familiarize them the modalities/objective and roles will be discussed such as project objectives, implementation strategies, scope, feedback and complaint mechanisms, beneficiary selection criteria, beneficiary entitlement and roles. Side meetings with girls, boys, men and women will also be convened to ensure their different needs are addressed. AADSOM will present the project documents to cluster members to avoid overlaps, following the cluster discussions. AADSOM will provide lifesaving safe water to 1000 IDPs in Bari (500HHs) and Hiran IDPs (500HHs), benefiting 6,000 men, women, girls and boys who have no access of safe water in two months of time. As an exit strategy, the project will rehabilitate the existing water sources (1 borehole, 6 berkeds and 15 shallow wells). In order to keep the sustainability of the water source and to increase the ownership of the community to the rehabilitated of the water sources, AADSOM will train WASH committees and well operators. As cross cutting, AADSOM will conduct community training on DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms. In order to reduce the risk of GBV and discourage open defecation, AADSOM will construct 100 gender sensitive IDP latrines, and 4 lockable, gender sensitive twin block latrines complete with hand washing basins in 2 schools and 2 health facilities with equal access of men, women, boys and girls. AADSOM will carryout hygiene promotion campaigns participating women, men, boys and girls and is planned to reach 35,000 people. The HP awareness campaigns will carry out by CHPs who are trained through this project. Finally, to encourage men, women and children to wash their hands with soap during the use of latrines and to fetch and store the water safely, AADSOM will distribute 1000 HP kits to 1000 IDP HHs (500 HH in Bari and 500 HH in Hiran), who are at risk of AWD and/or HHs with malnourished children and pregnant & lactating women. During the project implementation, AADSOM will encourage women to take part the decision making of the community decisions. AADSOM will make continuous monitoring during the project implementation and will handover the rehabilitated WASH services to the community leaders with the presence of the local authorities. The project is complementarity with the existing AADSOM food security and WASH projects in Bari and Hiran regions of Puntland and Hirshabelle states of Somalia.

Needs

Despite improvements, there are humanitarian needs in Somalia according to recent findings. The rainfall received in the last Gu’ season were spread across Somalia. The rains in April worsened conditions in overcrowded IDP settlements and displaced more people along riverine areas due to flooding. In Hiran, thousands of people have been affected after the floods inundated houses, livelihood assets, WASH infrastructures and crops. 87% of the WASH facilities have been inundated; Hygiene and sanitation are very poor as there is limited number of saved latrines, hand washing with soap at critical moment is low as access to soap and availability to hand washing point is limited. Because of lack of funding opportunity, repairing was limited, thus people were compiled to practice open defecation and drink unsafe water, which is suspected the outbreak of AWD/cholera diseases. A recent forecast indicates likelihood of normal to above normal 2018 Deyr rains across Somalia, which will deteriorate the already poor WASH services in Hiran. In the Northeast, heavy rainfall, flooding and cyclone were reported in parts of Alula, Iskushuban, and Qardho, resulting in livestock deaths, destruction of WASH facilities and the closure of roads. Floods/droughts/cyclones naturally contribute to trigger increases in epidemics such as AWD/Cholera and measles. The reported open defecation practice by 27% of the IDPs indicates a vulnerability to waterborne diseases, which may increase with the floods. Children and women who have to search limited quantities of water; raised protection issues around safe sanitation access for women and girls, adequate access for those with vulnerabilities such as, disabled persons, elderly, or school age children on their own and livestock loss with reduced income for WASH operation and maintenance. In this regard, AADSOM proposes access to safe clean water, sanitation, and hygiene for people in emergency through emergency and sustainable WASH services.

Activities

1) Conduct workshop with the community members to familiarize them the project objectives, activities, feedback and complaint mechanisms for accountability & transparence; 2) Distribute safe water to 1000 IDP HHs through water voucher (90l/day/HH) for a period of 60 days; 3) Rehabilitate 6 berkads: install 1 of berkads with solar, construct water kiosks, animal troughs and water tanks; 4) Rehabilitate: (1) 1 borehole with hybrid; (2) 15 flood proof shallow wells (18 with hand pumps with equal access of men, women and children; 5) Establish 22 WASH committees (88men, 66women) and 44 well operators to manage the WASH facilities. As cross cutting train 100 persons (50men and 50women) from the community leaders, elders, women groups and youths on DRR and conflict resolution mechanisms; 6) Conduct continuous water treatment, chlorination and routine water quality testing; 7) Construct 4 twin block flood proof and gender sensitive institutional latrines with lockable doors and hand washing points at 2 schools and 2 health facilities. 8) Construct 100 gender sensitive IDP latrines with lockable doors and hand washing points (50 in Bari and 50 in Hiran). All WASH facilities will be constructed in a way to protect from destruction during floods as durable solution.9) Conduct hygiene promotion activities to reach 35,000 women, men, boys and girls. 40 CHWs (20 women and 20 men) will carry out community sensitization and hygiene promotion practices including hand washing with soap. KAP survey will be done at the inception and completion of the project; 10) Conduct training to 40 CHWs (20women and 20men) on hygiene promotion practices using WASH cluster manuals. The CHWs will develop action plans to disseminate gender and age segregated audiences; 11) Distribute 1,000 HP kits to 1,000 IDPs who are at risk of AWD/cholera diseases and malnutrition. Each HP kit contains: 200 aqua tabs, 5 bars of 800mg soaps, 1 Jerry can (20lt) and 1 Bucket (20lt).

How is the project linked to durable solutions/resilience?

In order to keep the sustainability of the project and to increase durable solutions/resilience, All the WASH facilities will be done in a flood proof way of construction for flood mitigation and to keep the sustainability of the facilities after flooding. AADSOM will give training to the selected WASH committees (the committees will be shared equally men and women), to keep the maintenance of the rehabilitated WASH facilities and the cleanness of the Water source areas and the constructed latrines. AADSOM will also give training to the well operators on operation and maintenance. The well operators will also be trained the collection of revenues and bookkeeping mechanisms, to continue running of the water sources and its repairing after the project is faced out. The community will also be trained on DRR methodologies to mitigate disasters before it occurs.To increase the communities' ownership of the project, CHPs will be given training on how to mobilize the community on good hygiene promotion practices during the project implementation and after the project is faced out. After the completion of the project, AADSOM will handover the rehabilitated water sources and the constructed latrines to the WASH committees with the presence of the local authorities.

Protection risk analysis & Mitigating measures
Preventing risks of exclusion based on displacement

Conflict, drought and flooding are the root causes of IDPs in Somalia. Preventing displacement and IDPs’ protection within their country is mostly an obligation of the state but in circumstances where the national authorities need support or where state protection is not guaranteed, this role cascades to the international community. But protection gaps not only results from the reluctance of states to guarantee the displaced persons’ rights but also the insufficiency of state organ’s capacity to provide the necessary protection. And as such, protection gaps identification is a necessary for prioritization and development of measures to support protection capability. Protection can be seen as an objective which requires full respect for the right of all without discrimination and limitation to survival and physical security but covers the full range of rights, including civil and political rights, like freedom of movement and political participation, and economic, social and cultural rights, such as the rights to health, food security, shelter, WASH and education. Thus, in this project, AADSOM will provide WASH services inside their settlements to reduce the risk of GBV against IDP women/girls. IDPs will be included all the established WASH committees and CHPs to safeguard their interest. AADSOM will also include the IDPs all the decision-making meetings during the project implementation.

Preventing risks of exclusion based on gender and age

Women and girls are at risk of GBV as they walk long distance to water sources and their latrine needs. Girls end up having less time to study as they spend much time fetching water. Lack or limited access to latrines also affects women and girls most and they risk GBV when walking long distance to look for places to answer calls of nature or dispose menstrual towels. Women and girls require more privacy to answer calls of nature (urinate/defecate/manage menses). Bringing the water sources near the settlements significantly increases the security of women and girls fetching the water who otherwise needed to go outside their settlements. At the same time latrines will be lockable and the location of the new latrines is discussed with the community in order to increase security in regards to GBV. The hygiene committees will also exist of both sexes since this will optimize the messaging based or the roles of men and women in society.

Mitigating the impact of armed conflict and violence

In Puntland: The security is sufficiently safe and stable and AADSOM has no encountered any security problem during the implementation of the previous projects WASH and Food security to the project locations. Currently AADSOM is implementing food security project with FAO with the involvement of state level line ministry, the community elders, women groups and youths. In Hiran Region: The security is relatively stable as the government with the support of AMISOM contingent from Djibouti, although sometimes the insurgents penetrate some parts of the district and do interceptions the security of the district, where innocent people lose their lives. AADSOM has good relation with the clan elders in the district since it has been working through out its establishment. AADSOM also has good understanding of the security context in the area. A security checklist is provided on monthly basis by the field security focal point at AADSOM’s office in Belet Weyne. AADSOM anticipates that the project can be implemented smoothly without any security threats and challenges. In the event the insecurity prevents staff movements, it is anticipated that it would be for short periods only and coordination can continue by phone until the situation calm down and travel can be resumed. All other assumptions can be addressed by negotiations and discussion with local authorities that may cause delays, but are normally successful in dealing with challenges that arise.
