Project Summary

FARM STEW South Sudan (FSSS) will reduce hunger, disease, and poverty in 1,200 households in Tonj South County, where food security, malnutrition, and the threat of COVID-19 are at crisis level. The primary project focus will be hands-on farming, sanitation, and nutrition training and equipping of poor families and vulnerable people with inputs and skills to build resilience. Primary funding is sought from the HRP and in-kind contribution from FARM STEW International in the form of a land cruiser vehicle to run the project activities.
FSSS' proposed project aims to support IDPs, host and vulnerable communities in Tonj South County to increase their livelihood and self-sufficiency. FSSS’s needs assessment in Tonj identified a significant unmet need for both IDPs and host communities who have lost productive crop and agriculture assets due to ongoing conflict or displacement. As a result, they have lost food and livestock assets, reducing purchasing power and incomes that hinder food security. It is critical to support these households to restore lost crops and livelihoods, thus increasing food availability through personal production and a small income for increased food access.
FSSS plans to target 1200 households (HHs) for agriculture training on vegetable and staple crops to increase food availability. Microenterprise training and village savings and loan associations enable them to save and invest in building income-earning capacity to develop resilience and coping mechanisms against shocks by earning alternative incomes. We will also boost fishing activities by distributing fishing gear to complement their diets and sell the surplus fish to earn some revenue. FSSS will also promote COVID- 19 awareness and sanitation training to reduce disease prevalence in the community. The project will have a total population of 5460 females and 2940 males, including all family members as beneficiaries. FSS will implement the project in three administrative payams: Manyang-ngok, Thiet, and Tonj Centre payams. The project will also do post-planting monitoring and provide periodic technical advice and follow-up as necessary. During April and May, FSSS will distribute staple crop seeds, tools, fishing gear, and sanitation hand washing facilities (tippy taps) to all the farmers in the project area.
The project will conduct preventive nutrition training through the preparation of nutrient-dense meals. This willl be implemented through conducting cooking classes comprising of preparation of soya milk, soya eggs, soya meat, soya snacks, rainbow dish or golden pot among others.

2 - Project activities

The activities designed to be implemented under this project include ; 1. Identify farmers to receive staple crop seeds, vegetable seeds and other farm inputs, procurement and prepositioning of sufficient quantity and quality of improved vegetable staple crop seeds and tools to the project sites before distribution to the affected communities, distribution of vegetable seeds to 1200 households, train farmers on vegetable nursery bed preparation, seedling transplanting, and management to enhance their farming skills, Post distribution monitoring (PDM) by FSSS M & E officer during the first season planting, measure the progress of activities and ensure accountability to the affected population.2. Identify VSLA and Microenterprise members to be trained, Prepare VSLA and Microenterprise training material and booklets, Train VSLA field officers, VSLA agents, and the VSLA beneficiary participants, Conduct training on value addition and preparation of nutrient-dense meals like rainbow meals, soya milk, soya meat, soya mandazi, etc. Conduct microenterprise training for upcoming business women and men to empower them economically through earning extra income to access alternative goods, Conduct value addition activities; cooking classes to the beneficiary on the preparation of soya milk, rainbow meal, a vegetable golden meal which will reduce malnutrition condition in children, Conduct attitude change training to enable potential farmers to accept cross-cutting social transformation, e.g. GBV and Peace building, among others.3. COVID 19 and disease prevention and awareness activities: Distribution of washing facilities like tippy tap Jeri cans, soap, and bleaches, distribution of implements to groups for latrine construction, train beneficiaries on setting up washing facilities, distribution of face masks to beneficiaries households to prevent covid 19 spread and to wear during training sessions, conduct training on Covid 19 awareness to the beneficiary households, distribute sanitary pads to young girls. Train households on the construction of rubbish pits, dish drying rake, washing stations, compose pit, etc4. Fishing activities: Identify fishermen to receive fishing kits, distribute fishing kits to fishermen, train fisher folks on new fishing skills, proper use of nets, train fisher folks on fish preservation techniques, and Conduct post-distribution monitoring

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

All FSSS staff are trained on PSEA and are in position to create awareness and inform beneficiaries on risks related to PSEA. FSSS stew always distribute flyers, make posters and T-shirts carrying messages about PSEA. Beneficiaries and community leaders are availed phone numbers and people to approach in case an incident of PSEA occurs. FSSS will set up an anonymous complaint mechanism whereby any beneficiary aggrieved will be able to complain. A complaint will be channeled to a project person charged with that responsibility and trained on protection matters. The identity of the complainant will not be revealed to non-authorized persons in the project. Where practical, FSSS will use the mobile phone technology whereby an aggrieved person will instantly send a complaint through a short text message from where s/he will be. There will also be a complaint handling mechanism committees established at the village level. FSS has successfully used these mechanisms in Eastern Equatoria where it is implementing its integrated food security project. It has a confidential procedure of whistleblowing and reporting where reported information can be investigated and referred to relevant authorities if found to be true. FSSS will conduct protection sensitization sessions for female-headed households to train them about potential protection issues, available complaint mechanisms, and coping mechanisms to deal with problems should they occur. These sessions will help women understand what steps they should take in case of any protection issue and the types of support that FSSS can provide. The FSSS field-level Senior Management Team will be involved in the distribution and post-distribution monitoring to ensure that protection-related problems, mostly related to women-headed households, are addressed. FSSS will ensure that training and other project activities fit into female beneficiaries' day-to-day working schedules, including distributing tools linked to sites accessible to women and children within the community. FSSS will employ participatory assessments to identify, verify and profile targeted beneficiaries. These criteria will prioritize vulnerable people, including IDPs, host communities, and female-headed HHSFSSS will also perform periodic feedback sessions with community project beneficiaries to understand their protection concerns and design tailor-made interventions in collaboration with the UN and other agencies with protection programming

12.1.1. Decreased prevalence of global acute malnutrition among children under age 5 in severity levels 3 and 4

The World Food Program and others are addressing the malnutrition problem by providing fortified soy and corn blend for acutely malnourished people. This program is laudable for providing food for the hungry, but it does not solve the long-term problem of food shortage and malnutrition. While FS-SS will refer malnourished children to clinics where WFP rations and treatment is available, we also seek to address this problem with training to improve crop production, planting diverse crops, and introducing soy products to increase protein. Training in soy production and processing is based on training from the National Soy Research Laboratory at the University of Illinois in the USA. FSSS will train HH in extracting soy milk and creating value-added soy products. Soy milk and enriched porridge (using soy by-products) are crucial to infants in the first two years of life by giving them an extra source of nutrition. FARM STEW trainers and curriculum are clear that breast milk is the best and should be the exclusive form of nutrition for the first six months of life. The addition of soy products in the diet can improve the MUAC scores of malnourished children. In fact, soy is the key protein-rich ingredient in most supplemental foods used to treat moderate malnutrition by WFP and other agencies. Also, grinding soy fortified porridge and other products (such as soy-kawa) can be a means of value addition. FARM STEW's techniques have been modified for the context of rural South Sudan. FSSS will train mothers and caregivers on preparation of nutrient-dense meals using soya beans and kitchen garden vegetables. the project will train these beneficiaries on how to prepare vegetable rainbow dish, rainbow salads, golden dish from vegetables. Also women and men will be trained on how to prepare soya milk, soya eggs, soya meat , soya mandazi, soya cakes and snacks. This will drastically prevent and reduce acute multinutrittion. This model have been tried in Eastern Equatoria, greater Magwi county where Multinutrion in baseline study was at 53% and when measured at end 18 months, the mulnutrition rate droped to 3%. The said project was funded by Swiss agency for Developement and cooperation with matching fund from FARM STEW international
