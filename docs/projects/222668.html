Project Summary

The Literacy and Life Skills Project in Metuge Host Communities is a strategic initiative addressing the educational and developmental challenges faced by children and adolescents aged 13-17 who have been out of school for extended periods or have never enrolled in formal education. These adolescents, particularly in Cabo Delgado, are at heightened risk of remaining out of the education system due to the impacts of ongoing conflict, displacement, and infrastructure damage. The project provides alternative education opportunities focused on life skills, basic literacy, and numeracy, empowering youth to reintegrate into their communities and contribute to sustainable development.
The program focuses on providing learners with basic literacy and life skills competences as to reintegrating them into formal education systems or equipping them with essential life skills, literacy, and numeracy. Metuge, one of the third-priority districts identified in Cabo Delgado, serves as the program's operational area. While vulnerabilities persist in this district, they are less critical compared to the first- and second-priority areas.
Alongside education, the project promotes problem-solving and leadership through service-learning and adventure education, enabling youth to identify and address real community challenges. Participants work collaboratively to design and implement community service projects, building their capacity to analyze problems and develop creative, impactful solutions.
The project fosters personal growth and social capital among youth by creating environments that are emotionally and physically safe, instilling a sense of belonging and ownership, and encouraging self-discovery and skill development. It also nurtures high-quality relationships with peers and adults, promotes moral character development, and helps adolescents recognize and reconcile conflicting values.
By integrating bonding and bridging social capital, the project strengthens community cohesion and resilience. Bonding social capital is developed through the close relationships formed within youth clubs, while bridging social capital is built through collaborative projects that connect different community groups. These efforts aim to create a foundation for long-term social and economic development, ensuring inclusive opportunities for adolescents in Metuge.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

The Project Literacy and Life Skills for Host and Internal Displaced Communities in Metuge is an initiative designed to address the educational and developmental needs of children and adolescents aged 13-17 who have been out of school for extended periods or have never enrolled in formal education. These adolescents face significant risks of exclusion from the education system due to conflict, displacement, and lack of access to formal schooling. The project aims to provide alternative education opportunities, focusing on life skills, basic literacy, and numeracy, empowering them to reintegrate into their communities and contribute meaningfully to their development. The program also nurtures personal growth by providing emotionally and physically safe environments, fostering self-worth, and encouraging the discovery of individual strengths. Through bonding and bridging social capital, the project strengthens relationships within youth groups and across diverse community groups, promoting inclusivity and resilience.

2 - Describe the priority activities your project will undertake, please detail priority activities by geographical areas you’re targeting?

Activities planned under this project include Accelerated and Alternative Education for IDP and HC community children (6-12 Y) and adolescents (13-17Y). The implementation of these two activities under the HRP 2025 entails different sub-activities such as: • Community engagement and school awareness about the AEP (Alternative Education Program);• Enrolment and program initiation;• Distribution of kits for students, teachers, and facilitators;• Allocation of one (1) teacher and one (1) facilitator/animator per class;• Allocation of one (1) community mobilizer for each location;• Training for teachers and pedagogical coordinators, including the development of lesson plans;• Training for teachers on peace, tolerance, and human rights• Conducting final tests and sharing the lists with schools to reintegrate all children;• Monitoring process to ensure the quality of implementation;• Creation of Education Committees to ensure the absorption of students into formal education.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

The Project identifies potential risks and ensures that its interventions do not cause harm or negatively impact the communities it serves by integrating a comprehensive risk assessment and mitigation strategy into its design and implementation.To identify risks, the project actively engages with local stakeholders, including community leaders, educators, and families, to understand the unique challenges and vulnerabilities in the host communities. This collaborative approach ensures that the project aligns with the social, cultural, and economic dynamics of the area. Specific attention is given to identifying risks associated with marginalization, gender inequality, and resource allocation, particularly for adolescent girls and other vulnerable groups.The project addresses social risks, such as potential stigma or exclusion faced by adolescents who are out of school or participating in non-formal education programs, by promoting inclusive community awareness campaigns. These campaigns foster understanding and support for the program’s goals, reducing discrimination and encouraging broader community buy-in.To avoid negative environmental and logistical impacts, the project ensures that the distribution of learning supplies, hygiene kits, and school feeding programs is carefully planned to avoid inequitable resource distribution that could cause tensions among community members. Monitoring mechanisms are in place to track resource use and adjust strategies if any issues arise.The program mitigates psychological risks by creating emotionally safe spaces for adolescents, where they can build self-esteem and explore their potential without fear of judgment. Teachers and facilitators receive training in psychosocial support and inclusive education to ensure they are equipped to handle diverse needs sensitively and effectively.Finally, the project ensures sustainability by involving community members in planning and execution. This participatory approach not only empowers local stakeholders but also fosters long-term ownership, ensuring that interventions enhance, rather than disrupt, existing community structures and relationships. Through these measures, the project minimizes risks while maximizing positive impact.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The proposed project fosters meaningful access to assistance and promotes participation and empowerment, particularly for women and the elderly, through various strategic approaches. The provision of essential materials, such as hygiene kits, learning supplies, and menstrual hygiene management products, aims to eliminate barriers that often hinder girls and adolescent women from participating in education, thus supporting their school attendance and performance. Furthermore, by prioritizing marginalized groups, including girls and women, the project addresses structural barriers such as the lack of assistive devices and menstrual hygiene support, bolstering their self-confidence and autonomy while equipping them with life skills and literacy and numeracy competencies.Community mobilization also plays a key role by encouraging active participation of the elderly in awareness campaigns and local initiatives. This approach values their contribution as custodians of cultural knowledge and creates opportunities for them to share their experiences, strengthening their role as leaders and mentors. Such involvement fosters the development of resilient communities and ensures the sustainability of initiatives. The project also addresses the educational needs of displaced adolescents by providing flexible, age-appropriate opportunities for those who have dropped out of school, facilitating their reintegration into formal education systems or enabling them to acquire essential life skills.School feeding programs encourage attendance, improve nutrition, and support cognitive development, particularly in emergency settings, benefiting both students and their families. To create an inclusive educational environment, teachers receive training in conflict-sensitive teaching methods, inclusive strategies, and psychosocial support, ensuring classrooms are safe and welcoming spaces.Additionally, contingency planning and disaster risk reduction measures are integrated into the project to mitigate the impact of future crises on educational continuity. Local engagement mobilizes communities to support and sustain these initiatives, establishing a solid foundation for social and educational transformation. Through these efforts, the project significantly contributes to expanding access to education, promoting inclusion, and empowering vulnerable groups, including women, youth, and the elderly, while strengthening communities as a whole.

6 - Disability Inclusion
6.1 - Explain how the project will identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to assistance ?

The Literacy and Life Skills Project in Metuge Host and Integrated Communities integrates specific strategies to identify people with disabilities and remove barriers to their access to education, ensuring inclusivity and equity in its implementation.To identify people with disabilities, the project collaborates with community leaders, families, and local organizations to locate adolescents with disabilities who are out of school or have been excluded from educational opportunities. Community engagement and outreach campaigns raise awareness about the importance of inclusive education and encourage families to register children with disabilities. Additionally, assessments are conducted during the enrolment process to identify the specific needs of individuals, including physical, cognitive, and sensory disabilities. Data from local records and partnerships with health and social services further support this identification process.Once identified, the project takes active steps to advocates to remove barriers to education for adolescents with disabilities. Assistive devices such as hearing aids, mobility aids, and adapted learning materials are provided to ensure their full participation in learning activities. Learning environments are modified to include accessibility features, such as ramps and inclusive restroom facilities, making them physically accessible to all.Teachers and facilitators are trained in inclusive education methods to ensure that they can adapt their teaching strategies to accommodate the diverse needs of students with disabilities. This training includes fostering a supportive and empathetic learning atmosphere and incorporating teaching aids tailored to different abilities.To address societal and cultural barriers, the project conducts sensitization campaigns to combat stigma and discrimination against people with disabilities. These efforts promote acceptance and encourage communities to support inclusive practices.

7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

The Project has been designed to minimize its environmental footprint, but it recognizes the potential for localized environmental impacts from its activities. These impacts are mainly associated with the distribution of learning supplies, hygiene kits, and menstrual hygiene materials, as well as the construction or adaptation of learning spaces to accommodate educational needs. Resource consumption, waste generation, and increased use of local infrastructure are key areas of concern.The production and distribution of supplies may lead to waste if materials are not managed properly, especially in areas with limited waste disposal systems. Similarly, constructing or modifying educational spaces could result in the consumption of natural resources such as timber or other building materials, potentially contributing to environmental degradation if not managed sustainably. Increased use of local facilities may also lead to wear and tear, posing challenges to their long-term viability.To mitigate these impacts, the project emphasizes the use of sustainable and environmentally friendly practices. Construction and renovation efforts will prioritize energy-efficient and resource-conscious designs, reducing the strain on local ecosystems. Learning materials and hygiene kits will be sourced with sustainability in mind, using biodegradable or reusable items to limit waste. Waste management systems will be established in collaboration with local authorities to ensure proper disposal or recycling of materials, while educational components will promote environmental awareness and sustainable practices among participants and the community.The project also recommends conducting environmental assessments for any significant construction activities to ensure they comply with sustainability standards. Monitoring mechanisms will track the environmental impact of project activities, allowing for adjustments to minimize harm. Partnerships with local environmental organizations will enhance these efforts, bringing expertise and additional resources to the initiative.
