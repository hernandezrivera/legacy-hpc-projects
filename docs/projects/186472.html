Project Summary

The conflict in North Nigeria resulting from Boko Haram has had devastating consequences in the Northeast region bringing increasing demand on the already limited water resources, sanitation infrastructure, and hygiene practices etc. The humanitarian response plan for 2021 has also documented serious gaps in the WASH sector with reports of about 2.9 million people needing water, sanitation and hygiene intervention. So far, 2.5 million people are targeted for intervention
GHIV Africa will focus on the four main objectives highlighted by the WASH sector in 2021 to support affected people to enjoy the benefits of water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH). This project will target 5000 people in the two LGAs (2500 Konduga and 2500 Gwoza). The project will target mainly IDPs in informal settlements and host communities. GHIV Africa will implement WASH hardware and software interventions, addressing inadequate water supply, inadequate sanitation facilities, general environmental cleaning and poor hygiene practices contributing to the risk of disease.

1 - Needs

: Current poor WASH conditions in camps and host communities remain a priority concern for humanitarian assistance in northeast Nigeria and are a leading cause of morbidity and mortality. Diarrhea disease is the second most common health concern and can exacerbate conditions like severe acute malnutrition (SAM). The practice of open defecation remains a persistent factor, especially in overcrowded camps with a constant influx of new arrivals in konduga and Gwoza. In Konduga and Gwoza, the Sphere standard of 15 liters of water/person/day is not being met due to continued influx of new arrivals. Also due to the season, most of the boreholes experience a drop down thereby creating scarcity of water. During intervention, GHIV Africa WASH staff will consult with local community leaders, HPs, WASH partners in the respective LGAs, State Ministry of Water Resources (SMoWR) and Rural Water Supply & Sanitation Agency (RUWASA) to develop ongoing plans for water provision and ensure that the project complements those efforts. Under this modification, GHIV Africa will continue to implement a core set of emergency WASH interventions in Konduga and Gwoza focusing on formal IDP camps, informal IDP camps, host communities. GHIV Africa will implement WASH hardware and software interventions, addressing inadequate water supply, inadequate sanitation facilities, general environmental cleaning and poor hygiene practices contributing to the risk of disease. GHIV Africa therefore, seeks to implement WASH interventions to address these issues in IDP camps, informal settlements, and host communities

2 - Activities

GHIV Africa shall be implementing the following activities:1. Rehabilitate and upgrade water sources and distribution systems2.Improve water quality management: water testing and treatment training3.: Rehabilitate existing latrines in IDP and host communities4.: Improve maintenance and cleaning of latrines 5.: Identification and training of Hygiene Promoters to engage in hygiene promotion6. Formation and training of Child Health Clubs (CHCs) to engage in hygiene promotion in schools7. Collection and disposal of solid waste in IDP camps and host communities8. Procurement and distribution of hygiene kits. Community mapping at the onset of the project and community engagement will be used as a strategy to implement the above activities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

GHIV Africa will ensure that safety, dignity, accountability and do no harm procedures are implemented and respected during the entire period of implementation. GHIV Africa will constantly monitor the security situation of the area of intervention in order for the mobile teams to choose secured and authorized roads to access the vulnerable IDPs in need. The mobile teams will only organise their activities in places considered as safe, in areas agreed on with the local authorities to minimize the risks taken by beneficiaries of settling in areas of continuing conflict or areas that have not been cleared of landmines and unexploded ordnance.To support the recognition of protection concerns, GHIV Africa field staffs will receive an initial training on child protection and GBV mainstreaming before the implementation of the activities. There will also be an emphasis to ensure an equal number of project staff will be female to ease the communication and interaction with female beneficiaries. Mobile teams will allow a flexible access to various locations where IDPs will be established, even temporarily, protection services will thus be available during day time, ensure safety for children, and the teams are free to come back to any location if any urgent needs arise. In order to avoid the creation of tensions or added risk for the beneficiaries who will receive items through the distribution of NFIs and food distribution for in the project activities, GHIV Africa will ensure strong information sharing on selection criteria, the value of items and usage and distribution processes, and that beneficiaries’ specific needs and opinions are taken into account. GHIV Africa’s MEAL department will establish a feedback mechanism for beneficiaries.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GHIV Africa has established a Complaint and response mechanism (CRM) – a formalized process providing a safe confidential, accessible, transparent and effective channel for beneficiaries and other relevant stakeholders to raise concerns, register complaints, call for investigation and make suggestions. GHIV Africa will systematically investigate and follow up on complaints in a timely manner. The purpose of the CRM and relevant contact information (phone, email, physical address etc.) is communicated to beneficiaries through complaints boxes wherever relevant, project activities as well as on distributed materials and / or at distribution sites. Staff will explain the purpose to beneficiaries. Received complaints are categorized according to level of sensitivity and severity and responded to in accordance to GHIV Africa’s Standard Operating Procedures for Programme-related Complaints Response and Feedback Mechanism. GHIV Africa will inform beneficiaries and the broader community on the selection criteria as well as on the distribution process in order to avoid the creation of tension. GHIV Africa’s Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability and Learning (MEAL) mechanisms provide a two-way communication channel through which beneficiaries and affected populations can give feedback and complaints, which will enable GHIV Africa to adjust its intervention accordingly and address issues directly in a time-effective manner. GHIV Africa will also ensure that its staff will comply with its code of conduct and serious measures will be taken if staff violates it during the course of the project. Lastly, GHIV Africa's firm commitment to the humanitarian principles of humanity, impartiality, independence and neutrality strive to ensure the ‘Do No Harm’ approach by making sure that its activities are not inadvertently worsening tensions among different groups due to its mere presence and/or provision of assistance.
