Project Summary

"Due to COVID - 19 millions of children suffered huge loss in terms of academic year and their educational need. In 2021, along with regular activities, IRC will focus on alternative modalities like caregiver-led education, remote support, adaptation of different app and IVR support to ensure educational support and cover the learning loss.
The project designed to reach 9346 (Children 4365, parents 4981; Female – 5349, Male - 3997) beneficiaries from both Rohingya (5521) and host (3825) community through existing interventions in camps (2E, 8E and 22) and host community (Ukhiya). The activities will align with education sector’s 4 Objectives: Access; Quality; Community Engagement and System Strengthening and will reach its goal through establishing 2-storied learning centers (LC) and Home based Learning (HBL) facility to address the scarcity of available space in camps with adequate community.
The project will increase capacity of LCMC member/parents/caregivers through community outreach, awareness session including conflict sensitivity module on addressing local conflict drivers and social cohesions, equity and gender mainstreaming and inclusion of children with special needs with overall impact on children education.
The project will establish feedback mechanism (i.e client satisfaction survey, feedback box) and beneficiary level data collection through dashboard to ensure IRC’s global approach of Accountability. Integration with IRC’s Protection Department for specialized referrals for GVB and case management will be done. Autonomous Learning (AL) program will support the children with limited mobility (and girls) as it address travel issues in the camp.
Following the instructions of RRRC and CiC office, IRC will implement all activities in the camps aligning ISCG and education sector’s standard and guidelines and will share all required data/information agreed with sector. IRC will follow risk mapping and sector approved design while establishing learning facility, develop emergency preparedness plan and will orient relevant stakeholders on DRR.

1 - Identified Needs

"Since August 2017, an estimated 745,000 Rohingya refugees fled to Cox’s Bazar, increasing the total number of Rohingya refugees to more than 860,000 (ISCG situation report, June 2020). As different organization started to move their response strategy from initial response phase to sustained response, necessity of information on needs and vulnerabilities of the population increased to design and implement programs. According to the different need assessment in 2019, only 76% of assessed facilities offered services for the 3-5 age group, compared to the 99% of assessed facilities providing services for children aged 6-14. (REACH/ENA 2019). The Multi-sector Need Assessment 2019 stated that, 69 % of the households reported at least one 5-17 years old aged child not attending any learning opportunities. Pre-dominated socio-cultural barriers (23%), marriage (36%) and domestic support (20%) are the main factors preventing children and youth to fully access education opportunities (J-MSNA, 2019).Due to COVID 19, the needs of affected population has increased to a whole new level. According to the MSNA 2020 fact sheet for Rohingya community, “86% of children previously in education reportedly continued studying remotely as learning centers were closed. However, 14% of households with children previously in education reported planning not to send all children back to learning centers, in particular households with high dependency ratios.” While addressing the challenges, various issues came up like – insufficient learning materials support, having low level of guidance from teachers, household engagement and having no one in household to support in education. Among the issues, lack of guidance from teachers and no one having to support in household have been reported as nearly 44% (MSNA 2020 fact sheet, Rohingya response). In host community, according to the MSNA 2020 fact sheet, “One quarter of households reported that the COVID-19 outbreak disrupted education. At the same time, 31% of households reported that school-aged children were not attending any formal learning before the COVID-19 outbreak.” To ensure the educational support to both Rohingya and Host community, Education Sector regularly conduct meetings with DPEO and ADPEOs. This is also an opportunity where education sector get some idea of the need identified by DPEO and ADPEOs. Based on the regular official meeting on November 2020 with Education Sector, DPEO and ADPEOs have also focused some areas where educational need still exist. The major focus was to provide technology based educational support to the children as well as extend support to the parents to support their children with education at home. From the findings of another report (The Gendered Impact of COVID 19, October 2020), school closer is the most significant impact on children for their education. This has been reported by both women and men in Rohingya and host community and the % is over 80% in all cases. In addition, from the MSNA 2020, half of HH’s reported rarely or never being consulted about their needs and preferences with humanitarian aid. HH’s not speaking English or Bangla report being even less likely to be consulted. This highlights the need for enhanced community voice and participation in our programs. This is also a priority area for the JRP review-Feedback and Participation."

2 - Activities or outputs

1. Establish 2-storied Learning centers and home based learning facilities (HBL) addressing the sacrcity of space in camps to ensure the provision of Early Childhood Education, Basic Education and Life Skills Training.2. Conduct participatory needs assessment including relevant baseline and end line survey for identifying relevant age group, specific target group (Girls, person with disability) and educational activities.3. Equip learning facilities (measured as classrooms/learning spaces), including alternative learning modalities and accessibility for children with special need, for refugee settings.4. Distance learning support (phone calls, SMS, IVR), developed around specific vulnerabilities faced by children, such as inclusion of children with special needs and inclusion of females in learning activities, for children and parent/caregivers to provide guidance for continuing home based education.5. Create technology based (Learning apps in Tablets) learning opportunities for the children to support enrollment and retention in the MCP program and directly support ICT-enabled learning through leadership of the Cluster Task Team. 6. Conduct teacher competency assessment and develop teachers’ competencies through Cluster task team using the community of practice (CoP).7. In-service and foundation training for teacher trainers, teachers and learning facilitator as per the Teacher Professional Development framework, endorsed by the Education Sector.8. Provide level-specific teaching material (didactic kits & School Supply) and learning material (school kits, Recreation Kits) including textbooks per GIEP guidelines, education kits per Education Sector standards and according to appropriate age group to reduce noted materials gaps and financial barriers to learning.9. Develop and roll out learners’ assessment guideline and tools, such as ASER+ and conduct regular learning outcome assessment to inform teaching and learning practice and progression, aligned with Cluster Assessment Guidelines.10. Parenting education and awareness session on the importance of education/inclusive education, sensitization protection concerns/systems (inclusion of siblings) with a specific focus on ensuring gender participation and participation of children with special needs.11. Management committee (LCMC) formation, considering academia, diversity and gender; Monthly motivational session on humanitarian response (school based)12. Engaging parents and community stakeholders in ‘Participatory Monitoring’ linking with IRC's Protection Monitoring for Referral Pathways and refferals for specialized service to improve LC’s teaching-learning environment and create community of practice (CoP) among the parents to support their children.13. Experience sharing/Cultural event between host and Rohingya children to contribute to increased community social cohesion.14. Promote behavioral change among the parents for responsive caregiving and play based learning with clear linkages with the Health and Nutrition sectors.15. Build cross sectoral intervention with health and nutrition to ensure positive learning environment for children.
