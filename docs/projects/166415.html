Project Summary

WHO’s response strategy is aligned with The National Preparedness and Response Plan for COVID-19 and WHO COVID-19 Strategic Preparedness and Response Plan as articulated in the Health Sector COVID-19 Joint Response Plan 2020. The activities prioritized under this project have been selected based on the Health Sector needs, rapid assessments and stakeholders consultation. WHO's project activities follow the nine pillars of the health sector strategic response (coordination, planning and monitoring; risk communication and community engagement; surveillance; point of entry; district laboratories; infection prevention and control; case management; essential health services; operational support and logistics) to reduce and contain the spread of COIVD-19 as well as other diseases of outbreak potential, prevent disease transmission, lead the health sector response to the crises and to ensure continuity of essential healthcare services during the course of the pandemic as well as acute events of monsoon and cyclone. Considering the novelty of the COVID-19 disease, the project activities will be updated as the epidemiological situation changes and the international knowledge and experience in fighting the diseases evolves. WHO is the only actor in support of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare with the mandate and capacity to set priorities, minimum standards, and support quality health service delivery.

Objectives

Prevent transmission and amplification of COVID-19,by enhancing infection prevention and control in community and health care settings and effectively communicate critical risks (counter misinformation) to men, women, boys and girls.3. Provide technical leadership, coordination, collaboration and information management support to all partners responding to the COVID-19 pandemic.4. Ensure continuity of equitable access to and utilization of quality lifesaving and comprehensive primary and secondary health services for all crisis-affected populations with a special focus on sexual, reproductive, maternal, neonatal, child and adolescent health; mental health and psycho-social support; and non-communicable diseases while adapting services to the current context.

Needs

Cox’s Bazar is ranked 8/64 districts for composite risk of exposure to COVID-19. Confirmed cases in Cox’s Bazar district suggests transmission in Rohingya refugee camps is likely in the near future. Given the transmission dynamics of the virus & the population density, limited scope for social distancing, poor WASH situation & underlying vulnerabilities including chronic disease & malnutrition in the Rohingya camps, containment strategies such as laboratory testing & contact tracing may prove effective only in delaying the outbreak briefly. Epidemiological projections of 600,000 Rohingyas living in Kutupalong-Balukhali Expansion Site, reveal that introduction of COVID-19 cases in the site could lead to up to 590,000 cases within a year & over 2100 deaths. Globally about 20% of cases are severe or critical, with a crude clinical case fatality rate currently of over 3%, increasing in older age groups & those with certain underlying conditions. Over 30,000 Rohingyas in Cox's Bazar are over 60 years old & considerable numbers suffer from co-morbidities including chronic non-communicable disease & communicable disease. In all scenarios, the number of cases is expected to significantly exceed the resources in available health facilities. The current capacity of 168 isolation beds is insufficient to treat the expected severe cases of COVID-19. Healthcare facilities are not equipped with equipment including oxygen & PPE to treat COVID-19 patients. No facilities in the camps can treat those requiring critical care. The only tertiary facility in the district is 250-bedded & faces chronic shortages of human resources & struggles with Infection Prevention & Control (IPC) issues. Enhancing disease surveillance is vital to identify & isolate cases early, trace contacts & provide timely care. If cases are found to be higher in specific age group, sex & location tailored responses are required. Applying stringent IPC measures in healthcare facilities & communities & households is crucial to prevent transmission & amplification of cases. Given the global demand for PPEs local suppliers meeting required standards must be utilized. Risk communication in the camps is a challenge, due to restriction on communications & no written text in Rohingya language. Appropriate communication modalities need to be adopted to ensure the target population & vulnerable groups such as women, girls & elderly who have relatively less ease of access to information receives the correct information through accepted two-way communication channels. A coordinated approach to COVID-19 response is essential to minimize duplication, reduce gaps & ensure uniform standards and protocols based on global evidence. Health information products and disease guidelines will need to be timely updated based on emerging evidence, different transmission scenarios & resource settings & shared with all stakeholders in the response. Close coordination and collaboration with other sectors (Protection, WASH, Site Management, CWC Working group, etc.) is essential. Existing health systems issues like high staff turnover & supply chain matters are expected to be further exacerbated due to COVID-19. The Rohingyas remain at high risk for communicable diseases while monsoon & cyclone seasons aggravate risk to diseases pose & interruption in service delivery. The distancing measures put in place may limit access to vital SRH services for women & adolescents, including GBV as perpetrators of abuse may use restrictions due to COVID-19 to exercise power & control over their partners leading to an upward surge in intimate partner violence cases. With the lockdown measures, access to health services and psycho-social support will become even more challenging. SRH services including GBV informed by consultations with women, men, boys & girls to minimize potential negative impact of COVID-19 to the extent possible for those in lockdown, isolation or quarantine facilities will need to be prioritized.
