Project Summary

This action seeks to prevent and respond to GBV mainly faced by women and girls who are either internally displaced, refugee returnees, refugees and vulnerable host communities in Bakool, Gedo, Bay, Nugaal and Bari Regions. Protection crisis continues to prevail as a result of the multi-faceted armed conflict and inter-communal violence. This situation is compounded by natural disasters such as drought and floods which further weaken the resilience of the households and community at large. The economic strain faced by households is one of the key drivers of domestic violence and separation of families in search of greener pastures. The formal and informal GBV response system is equally weakened as a result of limited resources and inadequate technical support for survivors of GBV especially psycho-social support services and inadequate access to PEP kits in remote hard to reach locations of Waajid, Xudur, Luuq, Bardhere, Qansadhere and Buurhakaba. This affects access to targeted and timely response for cases such as rape which require support within 72 hours. Recent assessments conducted by WVI indicate that survivors of GBV especially women and girls continue to suffer from stigma and phenomena like rape and other forms of SGBV is a taboo subject. WVI existing project reports indicate that most of the GBV cases reported in the IDP camps. Women and girls from host communities who herd animals in far flung areas and fetch water from water points that are at a long distance usually fall prey to the perpetrators of GBV. It has also been reported that intimate partner violence tends to increase at household level as a result of cash based assistance since women are the ones usually registered as household heads.
It is against this background that WVI seeks to prioritize increasing the geographical reach for life saving services and improving the service provision. Population targeted (33,600) includes boys, girls, women and men mostly in IDP camps and remote locations. Women and girls at risk of GBV will be provided with livelihood support through training (TVET) and cash based interventions. In addition, the project will advocate for and create safe spaces for women and girls. Men and boys will equally be mobilized against GBV. The project will deliberately work with faith leaders to advocate against GBV and eventually partner with them to speak against the vice and reduce stigma. Clan elders and GBV committees will be targeted for capacity building on reporting and referral pathway to ensure services are accessed within the critical window period. GBV medical support at the existing WV supported health facilities will also be improved through capacity building and positioning of staff to offer comprehensive services to survivors.

Mitigation Measures

2a. Exclusion and marginalization: This will be done in line with the WVI existing accountability mechanisms which empower the community's to know their rights to lodge complaints in the event of any incidents of marginalization during beneficiary selection. WVI has a toll free telephone number displayed at all project sites/locations to facilitate the process of complaints and feedback sharing free from victimization.3b. External stakeholder interference in beneficiary selection: Using ‘Do no harm principles’, WV will seek to avoid physical or psycho-social risks that would be detrimental to project beneficiaries, and will serve all people irrespective of age, sex or clan. Given vulnerabilities associated with issues related to clan dynamics, WV has done a thorough analysis of the context and has conducted the Do-No-Harm Assessments for target districts. The project will be intentional in ensuring leaders from both IDP and host communities participate during setting of criteria for beneficiary selection - this will include representatives of people with disability groups. 4d. Protection mainstreaming: Protection lens will be applied in the project by ensuring that activities are not contributing to further harm of the affected populations. The project will be deliberate in training staff on protection mainstreaming to ensure things are done differently across other sectors as well. For the cash component, protection of the beneficiaries will take center stage in beneficiary registration through a transparent and consultative process. Competition over access to basic commodities and services are often one of the major causes of community conflict.
