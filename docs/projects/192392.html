Project Summary

The proposed intervention sets to alleviate the vulnerable status of Lebanese and migrants in Lebanon, due to the ongoing socio-economic crisis which has highly affected and impeded the health sector. Through this project, Caritas is set to provide contributions to patients share of hospitalizations fees for life-saving and non-COVID-19 related hospitalization for Lebanese and Migrants. The intervention will focus on persons with low and middle incomes. Older people, people with disabilities, and those with serious medical issues living in Akkar, Beirut, North and South Lebanon, Mount Lebanon, and Bekaa and regarded to be below the poverty line.
CL has been implementing health-related interventions and programming since 1972, providing primary and secondary healthcare services to vulnerable communities across the country. In 2020, CL delivered more than 500,000 services to 38,500 beneficiaries. Over 170 physicians from different specializations are operating through CL’s Primary Health Care (PHC), dispensaries and Medical Mobile Units (MMU) on a daily basis. Likewise, CL has existing contracts in place with over 30 hospitals in Lebanon detailing the means of engagement; and a majority of these hospitals are contracted by UNHCR. As such, through this intervention, Caritas will aim to tackle objective 1 and 4 of the ERP health chapter.
This program will be a direct continuation of an already existing program set to finish end of October and that has reached the maximum targets, and still have a great number of patients in need. As part of a visibility plan, Caritas had set out a social media campaign to advertise the service being provided and had coordinated with municipalities and other partners for referrals. A high number of cases were assessed but many were rejected due to the low unit cost set. As such, this project aims to increase the cost to cover Lebanese beneficiaries after NSSF coverage or after MoPH coverage and migrants.
After the case is assessed and is found eligible and authorized, the support will be provided either through reimbursement to the patient or direct payment to the hospital. The average value for Lebanese is 600 USD per case, with a ceiling of USD 1,000 for extraordinary instances. Moreover, during an emergency, the MoU will act as a safeguard, ensuring that the patient receives lifesaving assistance after confirmation from the CL focal point, even before any funds are transferred.
As a result, through this intervention, CL will target 150 vulnerable patients (105 Lebanese and 45 Migrants; 50% female and 50% male) and provide them inpatient hospital coverage. Vulnerable beneficiaries will be identified from those who approach the CL PHCCs on their own initiative after learning about CL’s services in the area. In addition, further, identification will be done through referrals to CL’s PHCCs internally by the social workers in the social centers and other departments within CL. In addition, CL will receive external referrals by other NGOs and CL’s stakeholders such as contracted hospitals, priests, and municipalities among others. An IP form should be filled out to track all referrals. For the Lebanese and migrant target, the medico-social workers will assess the case based on an assessment and they will conduct an HHV. During the HHV, through the use of the Social Assessment form, the medico-social workers collect information about the family members, the housing conditions, the economic situation.

1. - Brief description of the specific project activities under each sector objective

Objective 1 - Act. 1.1 – Advertise, Target and Outreach: Beneficiaries are referred to CL through different channels (social centers, CL database, hotline for SHC, other NGOs, hospitals etc.).Act 1.2 – Initial screening and social assessment: Following the social and medical assessment by the social worker and physician, the patient answering the criteria can then benefit from the assistance. Act 1.3 – Approve or rejection of case for payment: the patient will be told of the cause of rejection to ensure transparency. Act 1.4 – Payment or reimbursement of SHC. Act 1.5 – PDM: The patient share of beneficiaries will be covered under this project. A sample size of 55 beneficiaries will be interviewed based on confidentiality level of 95% and 5% of confidence interval.

2. - What is your protection risk mitigation plan?

CL will implement recommendations developed by the Inter-Agency Network to better understand the heighten risks as well as PSEA prevention and response. CL works to identify and mitigate negative effects of projects as quickly as possible through the use of a range of feedback mechanisms. Continuous monitoring and evaluation of the project will ensure negative consequences will be address by staff as soon as they emerge. CL takes an Age, Gender, and Diversity approach to ensure at all beneficiaries participate in decisions. Meaningful access is accounted throughout the project taking into considering the realities of elderly, individuals with serious medical conditions or disabilities, female-headed household, and other persons with specific needs. Social cohesion is applied by avoiding marginalization of vulnerable communities in the targeting and ensuring that all beneficiaries are supported either through this service or by referral to another similar service.

3. - How will the project ensure accountability for affected populations (AAP)?

Caritas Lebanon is a member of the CHS alliance and a strong advocate of Accountability to beneficiaries, in addition to donors. Caritas has a number of well-practiced quality assurance mechanisms and accountability strategies in place. Constant communication between the project coordination team and the medical staff and follow up with the hospitals and beneficiaries ensures good interaction between all stakeholders involved in the project. This allows feedback for rapid responses to changing dynamics. Additionally, this program has a hotline for SHC since last year and a separate hotline for feedback and complaints. The Do No Harm principles are embedded in the project design, and social stability factors are taken into account. The project builds on existing deep-rooted relationships developed with Lebanese and migrant communities. Services have been updated by engaging with different communities and lessons learned from last year’s intervention.
