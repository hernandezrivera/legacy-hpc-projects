Project Summary

With little or no initiatives on supporting engagement of crisis-affected people and other stakeholders in humanitarian activities, humanitarian work had never been going on smoothly in South Sudan. Many obstacles/challenges had been experienced by humanitarian aid workers at different levels when delivering humanitarian services. In some cases, young men from either host communities or IDPs who are in possession of guns do harass humanitarian aid workers, vandalize health facilities and school structures. Therefore, in light of this, there is need to empower community/crisis-affected people participation in humanitarian service delivery through establishment of key contacts/relationship building between community leaders/representatives of various interests, capacity building, reinforcement of community governance structures and strengthening of feedback systems to ensure the humanitarian aid agencies and other stakeholders are accountable to the beneficiaries in the project areas. By bringing these groups together, the crisis-affected people and other stakeholders will have an opportunity to voice their opinions, as well as to better appreciate and understand the impact humanitarian services are making in the communities, thereby promoting mutual respect between the community and humanitarian aid workers and helping to increase partners’ confidence and a safe environment for the provision of humanitarian services. UNKEA will install and reinforce community governance structures that will oversee activities at payam level and county level. The payam appointed leadership will oversee monthly meeting of community groups/working groups on Communicating with Communities (CwC), community engagement and accountability point where sensitization on humanitarian work from different clusters will be done, feedbacks will be obtained and awareness sessions will be conducted. The multi-sectoral feedback committees who will be responsible for coordination of activities in their respective clusters and directly report to UNKEA on monthly basis will be non segregatively constituted by sex or age and will include health, nutrition and WASH committee, community development, gender sensitivity and social protection committees, an advocacy committee for marginalized persons and a peace building and conflict resolution committee. UNKEA will perform capacity building training to empower the community groups and multi-sectoral feedback committees to enable them drive community participation and involvement in humanitarian work.

1 - Needs

Though the recently 2018 revitalized peace agreement gives hope to South Sudanese for peace and stability, the general situation remains dire in many parts of the Country. The cumulative effects of years of conflict, violence and destroyed livelihoods have left more than seven million people or about two thirds of the population in dire need of some form of humanitarian assistance and protection (WHO September, 2019). Therefore, as humanitarian aid agencies are delivering humanitarian assistance in term of food, education, WASH, health and nutrition service, to these crisis-affected populations who have very weakened community governance structures or none at all, with many civilians who are in possession of guns, humanitarian aid workers had never been able to delivery services freely. According to report on humanitarian access in South Sudan, 2019, it was reported that South Sudan is one of the most challenging operating environments in the world, due to security concerns and logistic challenge. Access to the crisis-affected population is particularly challenging in disputed areas. Of recent, it was reported that about 1.5 million people are still living in areas where armed hostilities, violence against humanitarian aid workers and assets, and other access impediments render humanitarian activities severely restricted, or in some cases impossible (UN-OCHA report, 2019). Many at times there are issues to do with revenge killings targeting national humanitarian aid workers, harassment of humanitarian aid workers, vandalization of boreholes, health facilities and school structures and looting of aid supplies by army youth civilians/fighting forces. This culture of vandalization of boreholes, schools and health structures by army youths or people who are or whose relatives are the main beneficiaries of such facilities has been making humanitarian aid agencies to incur un necessary costs. Similarly, the culture of revenge killings and harassment of humanitarian aid workers brought an adverse effect where the wave of these attacks have prompted the humanitarian aid workers to threaten pull out of the all the services if the revenge attacks and other forms of harassments continue. In light of this context, there is need to set up and reinforce community management/governing structures to relook at the issue of revenge killing, harassment of humanitarian aid workers and vandalization of boreholes, health and school structures from a critical perspective through empowerment of community participation and involvement in the work of humanitarian aid workers. Crisis-affected people and other stakeholders should be enlightened on recognition of humanitarian work, and commitment to upholding the safety and security of humanitarian aid workers, including by providing a feedback mechanism platform that is relevant, feasible and sustainable for both humanitarian aid actors and the local community.

2 - Activities

Through this project UNKEA will expand its scope and build from its previous project, Support Humanitarian Services by setting up/strengthening community management and governing structures and feedback mechanisms to empower engagement and participation of community/crisis-affected people and other stakeholders in humanitarian work in Nasir, Maiwut, Ulang and Tonj North County. UNKEA will set up different sectoral groups and representatives/working groups on Communicating with Communities (CwC), community engagement and accountability point of view, from community and other stakeholders in new locations and strengthen those already formed in areas where previous project implementation is ongoing. These groups and representatives will include; representatives from youth groups, representations of churches, women groups, other interest groups or organizations. All these groups and representatives will be trained on relevant capacity building topics which will enable them to play crucial coordinating role to enhance the work of humanitarian organizations which include; coordinating information gathering in hard to reach locations, conducting social mobilization and sensitizations of communities on importance of not practicing revenge killings more especially on humanitarian aid workers, enlightening the communities on importance of taking good care/management of public assets such as boreholes, schools and health facilities. There will be a well elaborated feedback and complaint mechanism in place, where suggestion boxes will be set up in public places and the regular feedback, complaints and other relevant information gathered in the community will be analyst, by UNKEA monitoring and evaluation team. The results of the analysis will be shared with UNKEA’s higher management and other humanitarian actors who are operating in these Counties. This was seen working very well in the ongoing CCS project UNKEA is implementing in Nasir, Maiwut and Ulang County therefore, in this project UNKEA plans to scale up to other areas and seek to maintain continuation of services in the above-mentioned Counties. The project will reach a total of 20,000 direct beneficiaries and 50, 000 indirect beneficiaries. The summary of the crisis-affected persons/beneficiaries as per county is as follows: Nasir has 7000 direct beneficiaries (3000 Male and 4000 Female), Maiwut has 6000 direct beneficiaries (3300 Male and 2700 Female), Ulang has 4000 direct beneficiaries (2600 Male and 1400 Female) and Tonj North has 3000 direct beneficiaries (1650 Male and 1350 Female) who are going to be enlighten on the importance of having full participation and involvement in the work of humanitarian agencies.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the project will set up and reinforce community management/governing structures which will be responsible for enlightening the community on importance of taking good care of public assets and creating conducive environment for humanitarian actors. The groups and representatives responsible for this will be drawn from various groups of interests, local authority and traditional leaders included. The rules or regulations adopted by this groups in governing the use/operation of this public assets will reinforce through government/community system.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the main aim of this project is to create a conducive environment for humanitarian actors, peaceful coexistence among communities/IDPs/returnees and good management of public assets. This is in agreement with the national objective of protection for all.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

The environment had never been sufficiently stable all the times to either crisis-affected people or humanitarian actors in these targeted counties. Armed conflict among communities or at the national level and revenge killings had been threatening the stability. However, with recently signed 2018 revitalized peace agreement the war between forces have reduced but incidents of revenge killings are still on. This project will try to create an environment which is conducive for both crisis-affected people and humanitarian actors, through capacity building training which is going to be done on different sectoral groups and representatives drawn crisis-affected people, humanitarian actors and other stakeholders.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the different sectoral groups and representatives will be trained on capacity build and they will ensure the continuation of services in their areas where humanitarian services are delivered even if this CCS project is not active. They will reach to the crisis-affected population and local authority through working groups on Communicating with Communities (CwC), community engagement and accountability point of view and the message they will pass on to the community on creating conducive environment for the humanitarian workers may be adopted for a longer period in the community.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

It is identified that leaving out some groups when enlightening the communities about their involvement in humanitarian work can be a serious one therefore, UNKEA will ensure that everyone in the community get the needed information. Youth groups will highly be involved in this project because not doing so can post a lot of risks on public assets and humanitarian actors on revenge killings issues. Forced taxation of humanitarian assistance is a risk which can threaten the work of humanitarian if not properly handle because the workers may not be willing to work in such area. This project will ensure there is a mutual understanding between humanitarian actors and local authorities/taxation authority. The beneficiaries not accessing/use complaint and feedback mechanisms has been identified as a risk and it will be dealt with through making use of established feedback and complain mechanism, to support in monitoring and evaluation of the services delivery by the humanitarian and community actors.

9.14 - How will you project ensure feedback from the most vulnerable community members?

Feedback and complaints mechanisms are based on community/group preferences and cultural norms to ensure that they are accessible, safe and easy to use. In this project UNKEA will form or establish a working group on Communicating with Communities (CwC) which will comprise of all group representatives from the community with women, girls and those people living with disabilities(PLWD) included and they will ensure that all beneficiaries’ feedback and complaints are captured and all the project activities are communicated to community. There will be feedback and complaints suggestion boxes put in places which can be easily access and safely use by all literate people including those living with disabilities. Women and girls who are members of working group on CwC will take lead in conducting face-to-face interview with illiterate beneficiaries (women, girls and PLWD) and record their feedback and complaints on audio which can be sent to the UNKEA senior management with a lot confidentiality.
