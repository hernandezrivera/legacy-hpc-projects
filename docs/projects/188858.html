Project Summary

Plan International Nigeria (PIN) is proposing through this action to implement a 1-year integrated education and child protection action in Borno state targeting Bama, Gwoza and Monguno reaching a total of 200,000 unique beneficiaries to support affected children including out of school, those at risk of dropping out and koranic school (children, teachers, parent, school infrastructure, etc.). Main focus will be made on creating and maintaining the protective learning environment for children and teachers, as well as strengthening their knowledge and skills so cope with threats and attacks; special focus will also be put on developing and expanding relevant non-formal and informal education opportunities for children and adolescents. Through this action’s support, parents will be at the centre of awareness sessions on the importance of education (specially girls’ enrollment/retention in school, inclusion of children with disabilities, etc…) which will also be applied to support parents and communities in enrolling and retaining their children in school and learning centers. This action will target school aged children, girls and boys affected by the protracted crisis in Northeast Nigeria, especially in Borno state (Bama, Gwoza and Monguno LGAs). Plan international has strong presence in these locations and also is expecting to start similar project with possibilities to create synergies during the implementation. This action will leverage on previous and existing interventions we have implemented across these locations. Also, this action is designed in line with Northeast EiE Working Group objectives to provide access to inclusive quality basic education to children and adolescents.
The specific objective is to provide quality educational opportunities for out of school children and adolescents, particularly internally displaced boys and girls, and mainstream approaches that enhance safe and inclusive learning environment for children. This action will also strengthen community-based child protection strategies for girls and boys, including adolescents. This action builds on PINs humanitarian experience in northeast Nigeria, lessons learned from PINs recent implemented actions in Nigeria, as well as a new Needs Assessment from Borno State which was undertaken in November 2021. The Needs Assessment shows that school attendance in general is low and that there is a steep drop off in attendance between primary and secondary school. It also shows that internally displaced boys and girls are significantly more disadvantaged in terms of accessing education compared to non-displaced boys and girls. In relation to protection risks, the needs assessment shows a high prevalence of unaccompanied and separated children, poor access to basic services, psychosocial distress and sexual and gender-based violence. To address these needs the expected results of this action are: An increased number of crisis-affected girls and boys have access to well-managed, safe, and inclusive non-formal and formal learning spaces and are provided with quality education for better learning outcomes to overcome barriers in accessing formal education.

1 - Needs

UNOCHA estimates that the humanitarian crisis in Nigeria’s north-eastern states of Borno, Adamawa and Yobe (the so-called BAY states) is expected to persist unabated with some 8.7 million people needing humanitarian assistance and 6.4 million targeted for support in 2021. Of this, 1.92 million people, majority of whom are women and girls, are internally displaced (new or continued) and facing the threat of violence. Despite Education being a basic human right enshrined in a wide range of internationally recognized conventions and declarations , the efforts being made by all actors on the ground to ensure improved access to safe, inclusive and equitable quality education and lifelong learning opportunities remain inadequate, especially for girls and young women leaving them more vulnerable to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse. The challenges affecting the education system in the Northeast Nigeria are: compromised safety of learning environment; inadequate numbers of teachers with professional teaching qualifications; inadequate number of female teachers; inadequate infrastructure (high pupil to classroom/latrine ratio); inadequate scholastic materials including dignity kits for adolescent girls and young women; low community (including parental) ownership and participation in education; and distance to school and frequent school attacks/abductions. Furthermore, economic needs at household level seems to far outweigh the importance of girl’s education with parents preferring to enrol boys to school as opposed to girls who are often seen as a source of income when they are married off at a young age. As a result, an estimated 60% of primary school-age children are not attending school , with girls disproportionally affected: 41.8% and 42.2% in Adamawa and Yobe states respectively, compared to 27.2% at national level. This inadequate access to inclusive and gender responsive quality education in emergencies in a safe and protective environment continues to exacerbate the exposure of girls and young women in the Northeast (Borno, Adamawa and Yobe) to Sexual Exploitation and Abuse leaving them susceptible to Gender Based Violence. According to UNOCHA, women and girls are under threat of violence, abduction, and rape. Gender-based violence (GBV) including sexual violence as well as forced and child marriages continue to be reported and are attributed to the conflict, insecurity and poor living conditions in IDP camps and informal settlements. Adolescent girls in conflict zones are 90% more likely to be out of school, and 70% of women in humanitarian settings are more likely to experience GBV . Additionally, there are barriers specific to girls: early marriage, the prioritization of educating the boy child, increase of domestic duties, all exacerbated by COVID-19 and deepening the serious risk of girls not returning to school. With all the needs and pressures at household level, desperation drives women and girls to negative coping strategies, such as survival sex—exchanging sex for food and other necessities which leads to early/unwanted pregnancies as well as leaving them vulnerable to the risk of possible conscription by armed elements to act as spies or agents of Person Borne Improvised Explosive devices. The conflict, explosive remnants of war, and insecurity have cut people off from their main means of livelihoods which has caused major food insecurity in Northeast Nigeria which in turn negatively impacts the education and GBV responses.Providing quality educational opportunities (both formal and non-formal) that meet the specific needs of girls in displacement contexts means that displaced girls and young women can benefit from the protection of an accountable social service in which they have the time, opportunity, and support to evolve into key actors in their own protection to prevent Gender Based Violence.

2 - Activities

This action is inspired by the learnings made on previous interventions implemented by Plan International Nigeria in Borno state with funding from various donors (EU, UNICEF, USAID, AECID, ect…). The action has been designed in line with the key strategies and priorities the multi year Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) 2019- 2021, the Nigeria Education Sector strategic objectives and Plan International’s education in emergencies strategy. Plan International is targeting the most vulnerable children and youth in need of emergency life-saving assistance in Borno state, focusing on its commitment education in emergencies interventions to respond to the critical needs of those affected by the insurgency. With an effort to further align the proposed action with the Education Sector objectives and priorities, the project is contributing to the following Education Sector response strategies: i) Provide teaching and learning kits to teachers and students; ii) Provide temporary incentives to support teachers in hard-to-reach areas. Through these response strategies, the action is aimed, particularly, at increasing innovative access to quality and inclusive formal, non-formal and early childhood development education in safe and protective environment. Plan International Nigeria will work in close coordination with relevant education stakeholders such state ministry of education (MoE), State Universal Basic Education Board (SUBEB), Local Government Education Authority, School Based Management Committees in order to successfully implement this action in public/government owned schools.Also, Plan International will work very closely with State Agency for Mass Education (SAME), Ministry for Religious Affairs (MoRA), Community Education Committees (CECs) to implement this action in non-formal ALP and IQE centres in order to facilitate the enrolment and retention of school children in school. Plan International Nigeria employ a community and right based approach during the implementation of this action to ensure its continuity.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

PIN will consult with boys, girls, women, and men in the community, including people with disability, to identify barriers, safety concerns and preferred channels of receiving and giving complaints and feedback. PIN will develop and produce IEC materials and conduct quarterly awareness raising as well as set up appropriate child-friendly feedback and reporting mechanisms based on the outcome of the consultations. PIN will collect complaints and feedback on a regular basis based on the agreed channels of feedback and frequency of receiving feedback and complaints. PIN will conduct orientation to community representatives, SBMCs and CBCPMs on safeguarding, PSEA, and child friendly feedback and reporting mechanisms. The project will ensure feedback on interventions are provided to women and men, boys, and girls, as well as involving teachers, CFS facilitators and other community members through the complaint and feedback mechanism identified and agreed upon by the community members (suggestion boxes, toll free line, satisfaction surveys, community feedback meeting). Their feedback will be used to adjust and improve the action and PIN will ensure the closing of the feedback loop by providing a response to the complaints and feedback received. PIN will use Plan International’s Child-Friendly Feedback Mechanism toolkit to conduct these activities.
