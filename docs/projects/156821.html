Project Summary

The proposed project aims to address emergency and more sustainable shelter and NFI needs of vulnerable households with acute needs (both IDPs and host community members) in Northeast Syria. The intervention will target a total of 50,058 individuals.

Needs

According to HNO 2019, 4.7 million people are in need of shelter support whereas 4.4 million people require NFI assistance in Syria. More specifically, in the proposed governorates of intervention, the estimated number of people in need according to HNO 2019 is as follows: 184,476 (shelter) and 207,008 (NFI) in Ar-Raqqa, 191,121 (shelter) and 179,231 (NFI) in Deir Ez-Zor, and 125,146 (shelter) and 193,140 (NFI) in Al Hasakeh. Financial Tracking Services data indicate that only 30% of shelter and NFI needs were covered within the scope of 2018 Syria Humanitarian Response plan. HNO 2019 identified clothing items as the highest reported NFI need in Syria (14.78% reporting frequency) and other items which ACTED plans to distribute (heating fuel, winter clothing, and winter heaters) are also frequently reported as crucial needs since all of these items were reported as unavailable and/or unaffordable in many communities. ACTED conducted a large-scale Shelter and NFI assessment in December 2018 in Deir Ez Zor governorate. According to the assessment, most IDPs were residing in collective centres. Households reported to stay on average one year or more in these centre, despite the fact that their level of safety was deemed very low (72 of the assesses households reported considering it barely safe). Moreover, these centres host particularly vulnerable residents: 54% are women and girls, 37% pregnant or lactating women, 23% of households have more than 6 members, and 26% households have a member with a disability. The 98% of residents expressed urgent needs of repairs aimed at improving the sense of security, privacy and dignity. ACTED also conducted a needs assessment in Ein Issa and Ar-Raqqa sub-districts in Ar-Raqqa governorate in August 2018. The results indicated that the destruction levels in Ar-Raqqa governorate are particularly high in the wake of the offensive conducted to retake it from ISIL. Multiple houses/apartments have endured significant damages and need to be repaired to provide for a safe and dignified shelter to their occupants. In kind distribution of shelter materials would not be suitable given the diversity of needs that require work repair tailored to the damages of each shelter, and the skills and capacity of each household to conduct repairs. Therefore, direct implementation of shelter rehabilitation is a crucial need in the area. Equally, needs for NFI items were reported to be extremely high for 98% of surveyed respondents. Water containers, heaters, stoves, clothing, mattresses, blankets and solar lightening were found to be the most urgent needed items for 2/3 of respondents, with 94.31% reporting lacking financial capacities to address these needs.

Implementation of activities

ACTED will implement a multi-faceted intervention to respond to IDP and host community needs in Northeast Syria. As part of the shelter assistance, ACTED will rehabilitate damaged houses and collective centres and distribute emergency shelter kits to ensure safe and dignified shelter solutions for people and prevent potential protection issues that would result from inadequate shelters. While rehabilitation of damaged shelters will be conducted in communities, emergency shelter kit distributions will prioritize the acute shelter needs of IDPs, particularly those residing in camps or informal settlements, but also those residing in host communities and living in collective centres, unfinished buildings, or those without shelter. ACTED will also provide cash grants for shelter rehabilitation. While resorting to contractor is preferable as it allows professional rehabilitation works, this may not be feasible in case of large scale project where contractors would not necessarily be able to conduct a large number of rehabilitation works in parallel. ACTED’s alternative scheme consists in the provision of a small grant to each household to conduct the repair by themselves. In all suggested modalities, ACTED will register the beneficiaries with a door-to-door registration and verification, during which the rehabilitation needs of a household will be evaluated with specific price estimation conducted for each household. ACTED will also address NFI needs of individuals living in communities, camps and informal settlements, through voucher and in-kind modalities. Under this component, ACTED will distribute a variety of in-kind kits including non-food items, heaters, and other winter items as well as restricted value vouchers for winter clothes, fuel, and other winter items. ACTED will focus on households in dire need of winter support due to a lack of key NFI items. Displaced families will be primarily targeted. The most vulnerable households of the host community may be included when and where relevant, namely when they demonstrate high risks threatening their safety and dignity.

Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Non-inclusion of special needs during project design/implementationLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: The proposed intervention will be implemented in accordance with ACTED’s gender approach, which follows the Gender Handbook for Humanitarian Action reviewed in 2017 by the Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC). ACTED incorporates gender analysis and considerations into the full project management cycle, from project design to implementation and monitoring. During the community sensitisation, ACTED encourages the identification of special needs of most vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly, or people living with disabilities and conducts FGDs and/or community meetings prior to implementation. ACTED addresses where possible gender and protection concerns. In areas out of the scope of programming or expertise, ACTED relies on referral procedures to specialized agencies. Risk 2: Gender-based violence and sexual exploitation and abuseLikelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: All ACTED staff are required to sign a Code of Conduct which directly refers to the Protection of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) and to Gender Based Violence (GBV). As part of on-going efforts to improve accountability, ACTED is committed to implement a beneficiary Complaints and Response Mechanisms (CRM) in all of its projects to allow beneficiaries to raise concerns, make complaints and give feedback on ACTED work, as well as the work of the contractors, and to relay any arising issue, including potential PSEA or GBV issue. When selecting beneficiary shelters’ households, priority will be given to address the needs of unaccompanied women and households with particularly vulnerable individuals such as young girls, pregnant or lactating women, and elderly and disabled persons will be favoured. Activities will be tailored to the specific needs of these groups by providing one-on-one in depth information session with each beneficiary household to mitigate any potential protection risks. During NFI distributions, special attention will be given to minimize safety concerns and to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse during beneficiaries’ selection and distribution. This will be achieved through ongoing monitoring of distribution, careful triangulation of information on beneficiaries and effective communication with communities on the modalities for the targeting of beneficiaries and vulnerability criteria. ACTED will distribute in small groups to reduce the risk to beneficiaries and limit queuing time to less than 20 minutes. Daily workers will be hired to support households to carry the kits, in particular for extremely vulnerable individuals. ACTED will ensure that there is a protected humanitarian space, and that no armed group is present during distributions, and no weapons will be allowed into the distribution. Risk 3: Inadequate intervention to meet the needs of the most vulnerable groups Likelihood: Low – Impact: HighMitigation measure: ACTED will ensure to tailor shelter rehabilitations to the needs of the most vulnerable groups such as PLWDs, women, and girls to maximize their safety, privacy, and dignity. In individual shelter rehabilitations, ACTED staff take beneficiary needs and requests into account in developing Bills of Quantity to address specific beneficiary needs as much as possible. For NFI distribution, ACTED will constantly monitor the process for protection and gender issues to ensure they are adequately identified and addressed.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The major risk for the proposed project is a limited or major escalation of security situation or a shift in the control over target areas which could prevent access to project area. To mitigate any potential security issue, ACTED identifies alternative locations wherein the intervention can take place in all targeted hubs and activities can be shifted to these locations if needed. Moreover, ACTED security focal points in all target areas assess the security context on a daily basis and take precautions when needed including suspension of field movements or activities. Another risk for the project could materialize if target populations becoming hostile towards INGOs due to a perception of compromised neutrality in the intervention. Even though this risk is considered unlikely, it would have severe consequences. To mitigate this risk, ACTED clearly communicates to its beneficiaries and local authorities on its humanitarian values and principle of neutrality. Furthermore, ACTED staff are systematically signing a code of conduct guaranteeing their neutrality. To ensure the host communities that ACTED’s neutrality is not compromised, ACTED works closely with local authorities and obtains all authorizations needed to conduct the activities. ACTED has an established complaints and response mechanism (CRM) inside Syria, particularly through WhatsApp, which is highly used as a method of communication between ACTED and external stakeholders. Through CRM, beneficiaries can safely provide feedback, report any project issues, and easily express concerns regarding the neutrality of ACTED. A change of government/policy attitude in Syria could lead to closure of border or imposition of other practices which could negatively impact humanitarian access to Syria. To prevent this risk from materializing, ACTED continuously builds and develops its relationship with the relevant authorities. If the general attitude towards cross-border humanitarian assistance should change, ACTED will adapt its modus operandi to new requirements that may arise to ensure a continued assistance. ACTED is present in Northeast Syria with staff based in the location, which facilitates access in the area.
