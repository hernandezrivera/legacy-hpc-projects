Project Summary

Many schools are being used as shelters and many others suffered damage to their infrastructures in the affected areas. These initial assessments have revealed 16% of schools (34)1 in Grenada, Petite Martinique and Carriacou have suffered some form of damage impacting a student population of over 6,000. Similarly 262 schools or 11% of the overall number of schools, including 14 ECD facilities have been damaged (approximately 50 children). While sex and age disaggregated information isn’t currently available for the population in shelters within Grenada, for St. Vincent and the Grenadines 170 boys and 139 girls occupy spaces in shelters.
Therefore minor repairs and timely rehabilitation will be necessary for safe and timely reopening of school before the next school year. Children who are in the shelters need psychosocial support as well as basic educational activities that help them to establish routine and sense of normalcy. Many of these children also lost valuable items during the storm. UNICEF already had pre-positioned recreational kits for students and teachers which are already distributed in the Shelters.
Together with the Ministry of Education, CDEMA and regional education partners the response within the Education sector will focus on:

· Provision of psychosocial support (PSS) in the schools that are used as shelters also extending this to frontline workers
· Training of teachers to receive and provide psychosocial support and protection from risks including violence prevention to affected children
· Technical support for a MoE-led coordination mechanism and response plan The coordination mechanism will also support identification of existing resources that can be redirected to support the emergency.
· Support assessments to enable continued education
· Support minor repair, refurbishment and rehabilitation of schools used as shelters and suffered damage to their buildings to ensure safe and timely reopening of schools at the beginning of the new school year by September 2024.
·
Additionally with the eminent destruction of houses and schools, families also face issues when sending their children to school. By linking cash with education there is a possibility to focus on
1. Ensuring Access to Education-
2. Improving Educational Outcomes - Families can use cash transfers to create a more conducive learning environment at home, such as purchasing study materials or ensuring a quiet study space.
3. Addressing Barriers to Education - Cash transfers can be particularly effective in encouraging the education of girls by offsetting direct and opportunity costs, thereby addressing gender disparities in education.
Project Objectives
• Girls and boys access early childhood, primary and secondary education in safe and protective learning spaces
• Children and adolescents receive psychosocial support and development life-saving and disaster preparedness skills to restore well-being and build resiliente.
• Provide educational and recreational materials to facilitate early childhood development, educational, and psychosocial supporting activities for the return to protective learning spaces

Project Activities
