Project Summary

Despite recent revitalized peace deal signing by the different factions, the country has continued to witness increasing ceasefire violations especially in the Equatorias and bureaucratic impediments, further undermining efforts to create the previous status quo. Subsequently, 5.7 million people will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2019 and at least 5.2 million people are estimated to be facing extreme levels of food insecurity in the beginning of 2019 with significant populations projected to face alarming food consumption gaps, asset depletion and malnutrition rates in the Equatorias, Jonglei among others regions (IPC September, 2018).
Proposed actions will target beneficiaries primarily made up 2,500 households (15,000 beneficiaries) in Jonglei, Eastern and Western Equatoria States. These will be a total of 5000 girls, 5000 boys, 2500 men and 2500 women - these include elderly and people with special needs amongst the most vulnerable emergency affected IDPs and host communities affected by the ongoing conflict. The action will respond to prioritized locations taking into account cost effective measures of prepositioning supplies through WASH Cluster/IOM pipeline.
Community Aid for Relief and Development (CARD) proposes to implement activities which are aimed at improving access to safe water supply, improving safe and gender appropriate sanitation facilities , hygiene promotion, support children admitted for SAM treatment discharged with WASH/Hygiene kit from functional OTP/SC , provision of access to safe drinking water, provision of functional handwashing facilities by soap and provision of WASH minimum package of activities to mitigate WASH-related diseases including Ebola Virus Disease hotspots in the Equatorias.
CARD will in addition regularly review and adapt ways of working with affected populations and increase levels of transparency and participation of affected boys, girls, women and men. CARD will also mainstream cross cutting measures as gender equality and women and youth empowerment,
protection, environmental conservation, HIV/AIDS, accountability to affected populations and youth involvement including taking into account Gender Based Violence and sexual exploitation and mitigating risks associated with crowding and long distances during implementation.

Needs

Despite recent revitalized peace deal signing by the different factions, the country has continued to witness increasing ceasefire violations especially in the Equatorias and bureaucratic impediments, further undermining efforts to create the previous status quo. Subsequently, 5.7 million people will not have access to adequate water, sanitation and hygiene in 2019 and at least 5.2 million people are estimated to be facing extreme levels of food insecurity in the beginning of 2019 with significant populations projected to face alarming food consumption gaps, asset depletion and malnutrition rates in the Equatorias, Jonglei among others regions (IPC September, 2018). Jonglei and Eastern Equatoria are among other regions where WASH needs are the highest, with increasing cases of gender based and sexual violence particularly among women and girls who continue to face increased risk of harassment and assault when collecting water and using communal latrines, and access to menstrual hygiene products, and appropriate and dignified washing locations remains their key need. Two thirds of the population are using unprotected water sources, with over 90% practising open defeacation, 87% lacking access to WASH NFIs. Children under 19, constitute nearly 50% of the total cholera cases in the recent outbreak. The probable risk factors fuelling transmission include: using untreated water from the River Nile and water tankers; lack of household chlorination of drinking water; eating food from unregulated roadside food vendors/makeshift markets; open defecation/poor latrine (South Sudan Situation Reports on Cholera 2017/18; FSMS 2018). Available sanitation facilities are limited and not gender disaggregated and do not take care of the needs of those with disabilities. Community Knowledge Attitudes and Practices related to water safety, cholera preparedness/control, hygiene and sanitation practices still has a long way to go, with open defecation, lack of hand washing during critical times, poor disposal of solid waste etc being widespread (WASH Cluster Updates, 2018). Proposed actions will target beneficiaries primarily made up 2,500 households (15,000 beneficiaries) in Jonglei, Eastern and Western Equatoria States. These will be a total of 5000 girls, 5000 boys, 2500 men and 2500 women - these include elderly and people with special needs amongst the most vulnerable emergency affected IDPs and host communities affected by the ongoing conflict. The action will respond to prioritized locations taking into account cost effective measures of prepositioning supplies through WASH Cluster/IOM pipeline. Community Aid for Relief and Development (CARD) has been working in the proposed locations for the past five years and proposes to implement activities which are aimed at improving access to safe water supply, improving safe and gender appropriate sanitation facilities , hygiene promotion, support children admitted for SAM treatment discharged with WASH/Hygiene kit from functional OTP/SC , provision of access to safe drinking water, provision of functional handwashing facilities by soap and provision of WASH minimum package of activities to mitigate WASH-related diseases including Ebola Virus Disease hotspots in the Equatorias. CARD will in addition regularly review and adapt ways of working with affected populations and increase levels of transparency and participation of affected boys, girls, women and men. CARD will also mainstream cross cutting measures as gender equality and women and youth empowerment, protection, environmental conservation, HIV/AIDS, accountability to affected populations and youth involvement including taking into account Gender Based Violence and sexual exploitation and mitigating risks associated with crowding and long distances during implementation.

Activities

• Ensure equitable access to safe and sufficient water to vulnerable and affected populations via assessment and rehabilitation of 100 dysfunctional boreholes in Jonglei, Eastern and Western Equatoria• Ensure 15,000 targeted beneficiaries practice safe, sanitary and hygienic living through secure, dignified and gender appropriate. sanitation services via establishment 50 latrines and rehabilitation of 100 latrine facilities. • Carry out gender appropriate response starting from a risk assessment, for secure access, location and design of water points, toilets and showers in Jonglei, Eastern and Western Equatoria• Request from WASH Cluster Pipeline and provide WASH/Hygiene kits for the admitted among 500 households SAM at OTP/SC till the household level • Mobilize and train 300 hygiene promoters/ Health/Nutrition workers/volunteers trained on the WASH minimum package of activities• Increase knowledge among 15,000 beneficiaries on appropriate hygiene practices to prevent and mitigate WASH-related diseases. • Set up of WASH services, training of 300 health workers and pupils as ambassadors for appropriate hygiene practices within handwashing with soap at critical times at their household level • Provide safe drinking water and nutritional centers (OTP/SC) from protected water point till the appetite test for SAM/MAM• Provide timely WASH minimum package of interventions targeting 2,500 HHs on an ad-hoc basis, adapted to the specificities of the related-outbreaks, at an institution level as well as for the wider community in Jonglei, Eastern and Western Equatoria

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

As with all the other projects implemented by the organization, CARD will adopt a consultative approach in the implementation of this project; by including ensuring a two-way communication and dialogue throughout the project cycle. Beneficiaries (both male and female) will be dis-aggregated and involved in equal representation - taking into account the different roles and needs of boys, girls, women and men); local leaders among other relevant authorities in decisionmaking as well and also ensuring their full participation in implementation, monitoring and especially evaluating the project. All project activities will be aimed at obtaining full respect for the rights of the beneficiaries and communities. Implementation committees will be selected among IDP/mobile communities with linkages with RRC and local leadership to ensure targets aremet. Their roles will involve but not limited to beneficiary identification, selectionand verification; including participation in trainings and cholera preparedness activities.
