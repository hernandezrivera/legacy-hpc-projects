Needs

The target population included in this proposal, women IDPs in formal camps in Kirkuk are highly vulnerable persons – especially female headed households – since they are dependent upon limited financial and humanitarian aid despite their many urgent needs. Protracted displacement in camps can also have highly complex implications for the socioeconomic fabric of IDPs. Kirkuk currently hosts 113,877 IDPs , of which 65,532 IDPs are displaced from within the governorate, 23,676 originate from Salah al Din and 14,346 from Ninewa – with small numbers from Diyala, Babylon and Anbar. Of this, 12,510 IDPs (52% women) are living inside four camps to the east of Kirkuk city, which are – Yahyawa (2,934), Nazrawa (1,742), Laylan IDP (6,158) and 2 (1,676). Sala al-Din currently hosts 151,920 IDPs, of which 128, 514 IDPs are displaced from within the governorate, 19,212 originate from Kirkuk, 1,848 from Ninewa, 1,506 from Diyala and 840 from Anbar. Of this, 12,315 (55% women) are living in seven camps in Tikrit and Shirqat, which are – Basateen Al Sheuokh (1,425), Al Sh’hamah (353), Al-Alam 1 (949), 2 (1045) & 3 (855), Al-Karama (1,898) and Al Qadiseya complex building (5,785). Many IDPs living in camps are experiencing protracted displacement due to a perception that they have an affiliation with extremist groups. In a survey by the CCCM Cluster partners (REACH) employment was selected as one of the top three priority needs by residents in five camps. Other priority needs included food, medical care and clothing. Medical care and employment were common among all five surveyed camps. Most people are presently living on humanitarian aid, gifts, wages from unskilled labour, and support from friends, for example; and due to having been living in displacement for more than three years, have exhausted many positive coping strategies; e.g. savings, lending, assets sale etc. Given their protracted stay, the lack of employment opportunities makes it harder for IDP families to survive; particularly for women headed households – which comprise 20% of all households in Salah al-Din IDP camps and 19% of all households in Kirkuk IDP camps. The few cases where humanitarian partners have previously supported employment creation for IDP women, remained focused narrowly upon sewing and cooking. This severely limits the scope of employment opportunities, which makes exacerbates the vulnerability of women headed households. Due to lack of diverse livelihoods opportunities, displaced women are also highly vulnerable to exploitative labour conditions and to negative coping options. There is an urgent need to restore livelihoods and provide income earning opportunities for these women IDPs who may remain living in-camp for an unforeseeable time. Needs assessments carried out by UNDP and its implementation partners in newly accessible areas have found that there is severe loss of livelihoods assets due to fighting and selling of assets as a means to purchase other necessities. The proposed, evidence-based emergency livelihoods intervention is designed to address the multiple challenges to increasing financial security, resilience, and independence. In this context, livelihoods assistance to restore access to basic needs, especially for women is an emergency means of support. The beneficiaries will receive assets and be trained in business management to facilitate skills transfer, and capacity building to manage their own businesses.

Activities

The objective of the project is to provide immediate access to income, to support highly vulnerable women headed households and women living in camps, in order to facilitate decent work for survival and strengthen resilience. UNDP will achieve this through the identification of potential national NGOs that have experience in and are eligible to receive small grants in order to provide emergency livelihood opportunities (asset replacement and small business creation). The project has three outputs; 1-Technical and business management skills of women IDPs improved; 2-women IDPs set-up their own small businesses in different sectors; 3-Committees of micro-entrepreneur women established to set-up revolving fund for recycling of micro-credits. To achieve the results against output 1, the Project will i) Select implementing partner/training institute, ii. Conduct market assessment to identify areas for new businesses; iii) Select interested beneficiaries and conduct training needs assessment, iv) Design and deliver the training, and v) Prepare business plan for each beneficiary (including selection of sector/business type, goods and services needed, location for business, target market/costumers). Each recipient will be required to sign an individual contract with UNDP and it’s implementing partner – with endorsement of the Ministry of Labour and Social Affair Iraq (MoLSA) – committing to the use of assets provided for the creation and maintenance of an enterprise. This will ensure that assets are not resold after project completion. Under output 2: the Project will; i) Support the beneficiaries to prepare the place (rental, repair, fixing etc), ii) Purchase required goods and materials, and iii) 2.3 Deliver the goods to the business site. For the results under output 3, the Project will: i) Prepare procedures and rules for committee management in consultation with women IDPs, ii) Establish the committees with membership and selected committee office bearers, and iii) Hold regular meetings of the committees and maintain records. The Emergency Livelihoods Cluster will be closely consulted in identification of beneficiaries and implementation of the Project activities. In order to deliver this project, UNDP will select a responsible party from amongst the network of its partner NGOs. It will also build partnerships with technical organizations with experience in Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs); e.g. ILO, UNIDO, Technical and Vocation Education a nd training (TVET)/UNESCO, GIZ and Swedish Academy for Training. These partnerships will be established for training and mentoring of beneficiaries on business management. UNDP will also explore partnership opportunities with Iraqi Company for Financing of SMEs (ICF-SMEs) in order to arrange credit from banks for additional financing to the Project beneficiaries from the Iraqi commercial and public banks.

Protection Risk Analysis

100% of recipients are women headed-households and women, creating an opportunity for families who have had assets lost or damaged due to on-going conflict, to reinstate their business for income generation. However, asset replacement carries several protection-based risks, namely: • Exposure: Recipients of assets may be identified by community members as a target for theft, violence, and other criminal activities due to cultural bias; particularly in environments where local protection services such as police or social assets such as family are non-existent or disbanded. This may also influence the likelihood of women to participate in business sectors typically targeted to men. • Habitual resale: Women are vulnerable to negative coping strategies, particularly when they represent the head of the household, or as a widow, as their access to income has often deteriorated during crisis. Such negative coping mechanisms may include the resale of received assets to generate an income out of fear or habit.

Mitigation Measures

UNDP currently employs a Gender Specialist and can refer to the Gender Focal Point within the Emergency Livelihoods Cluster; their technical assistance throughout the design and implementation are key. At the field level, partners will be required to introduce a complaints management system as a due process to ensure that there is a transparent feedback loop for beneficiaries, whilst also creating a hotline for immediate concerns. Protection focal points will be identified with support from the designated UNDP Gender Specialist, to ensure no abuse of power or non–compliance by community leaders (Mohktars) and/or other key actors. This is aimed at e ensuring safety and entitlement and providing a discrete mechanism for women to provide feedback and report any abuse or misconduct All UNDP Partners are educated on principles of PSEA and SGBV, including undergoing a thorough internal risk analysis before engagement. Do No Harm principles are included in project implementation training, whilst implementing partners are audited to ensure their compliance with recommendations, such as the signing of ST/SGB codes of conduct. UNDP fosters capacity building and behavioral change to address the root causes SEA and SGBV. UNDP will work with partners to ensure the identification and creation safe spaces, and anonymous reporting, to allow the identification of vulnerable at risk groups, and in turn supporting these individuals in livelihoods activities.

How is your project accountable to affected population

Accountability to affected people will be achieved through various methods. UNDP will look to ensure meaningful participation at all stages of the project management cycle (assessment, planning, design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation). To introduce themes of two-way communication with target communities, regular and transparent messaging will be delivered. All messages or project updates will be shared with Mukhtars, Anjumans, religious leaders and community entities (committees, elected leaders) in areas of engagement. The channel through which information is to be shared, will be assessed based on availability, access and level of participation such as in mosques, through sector meetings, community activities and during household visits. An imperative will be placed on enabling transparent feedback loops. Focus group discussions and other methods of engagement will be adopted using the Age, Gender, Diversity (AGD) approach to select target group for FGDs to discuss the key messages and to answer questions related project activities. Coordination at various stakeholder levels, from the macro (government) to micro (beneficiaries) will occur in project implementation. Private sector stakeholders at the meso-level are included in the dialogue, whilst aligning to government strategies through coordination with chambers of commerce, ministries of trade and industry. During planning, UNDP will seek for partners to hold separate consultations with women and men, including youth and older people, whilst during implementation offering tasks suitable for groups with specific needs, particularly older persons and people with disabilities, during monitoring and evaluation, lessons learned, will be fed back into the project.
