Project Summary

This project delivers a strategic emergency-based intervention to overcome the access-based obstruction to IDPs' sites in the severe risky sub-districts in Idleb Governate (Dana, Qourqeena, Maaret Tamsri, Armanaz and Salqin).
Its main objective is to ensure uninterrupted physical access to IDP sites during winter and as when emergency situations arise. Maram Foundation MF will undertake strategic planning with response functions to ensure the delivery of the humanitarian goods to those in need, at the right time, in the right place. In order to create scientific-based response and well-planned intervention during the disaster, MF will design communication plan and risk analysis in order to prognose the risks and engage the stakeholders in planned response. A contingency plan (CP) will be designed, which involves making decisions in advance about the management of crisis, coordination and communications procedures. This plan should lead to timely and effective disaster-relief operations. The CP will be designed for Idleb gov. and those that characterize higher probability that a disaster/emergency situation may occur. The CP will be including:
•Determine the risk that are most likely to occur
•Prioritize the identified risks and rank them based on the probability they will occur.
•Develop realistic scenarios for the highest ranked risks and their impact
•Create a timeline for when the scenarios might occur
•Specify a trigger point to put the CP in action
•Determine the proper action to be undertaken to mitigate the impact of the disaster and allow the humanitarian assistance to access.
In order to design this CP, MF will establish a strong coordination network of stakeholders targeting the sub-districts of Idleb governorate, especially those that are identified as the highest risky flooded/hazarded locations. The established committee will be consisting of the Local Council (LC) some managers in the related sub-districts and MF response team.
The committee members will convene coordination meetings and follow up on activities in the affected communities. The committee will assist in finalizing the CP, updating the climate severe conditions in their areas and call for the response on the trigger point.
Based on the outcomes of the committee, MF will be targeting the highest risky locations with awareness signs and signals to draw the attention of those access roads’ users to the possible risks and guide them for safer access/exit. MF Rapid Response Teams RRT will conduct site visits to the locations to have an accurate overview about the site and access roads and to identify whether there are humanitarian organizations acting on the ground for a better response and notification coordination.
Additionally, as per the outcome of this committee, MF will design a map-based dashboard to identify the risky locations and accordingly locate the procured service providers machineries nearby them in the call up time. MF is intending to procure service provider/s in each sub-district in order to cover the majority of Idleb governate principally in the risky location. Once the crisis occurs, MF will be informed by the focal points (members of the crisis committee). Urgent information and description of issue will be delivered. Accordingly, MF will assign its RRTs and the procured contractor/s to response within 24-48 hours. The response will be in terms of soft road graveling, excavations, blockage (large stones) furnishing, and others.
The project will have a positive impact on all beneficiaries in the selected areas , where the total number of the targeted persons approximately: 54,221 IDPs (12471 men, 13555 women, 17351 Boys, 10844 Girls) from different age groups (Young children, Children, Adolescents, Young, Middle-aged adults and elderly).
This includes about 2,530 Persons with Disabilities; 2,530 IDPs (624 men, 678 women, 686 Boys, 542 Girls)

4 - Needs

Per the 2021 Needs and Response Summary report (February 2021) there has been an “almost 20 % increase in the number of people in need compared to early 2020 with a disproportionate impact on women and children. As of January 2021, around 13.4 million people are estimated to require some form of humanitarian and protection assistance, due to a convergence of factors arising from a sharp reduction in purchasing power, the loss of essential livelihoods and income, mounting food insecurity, limited access to basic services such as health, WASH, education, nutrition and critical protection services, inadequate shelter conditions as well as the immediate and longer-term effects of the COVID-19 pandemic.As one of MF’s emergency responses to crisis, i.e. floods, it has conducted needs assessment on 14.01.2021. The reports stated that flooded camps were built on agricultural land that highly require ground isolation to avoid floods in winter. MF has also conducted a pure-technical assessment of roads accessing to IDP sites in Nov. 2020. in different camp clusters in Dana and Maaret Tmasreen sub-districts. The assessment highlighted:•The status-quo of the access-roads as well as inside camps and• The # of affected population , which reflects the high need for emergency during the crisis time.Watershed analysis and potential scenarios for flooding were analyzed based on technical software. MF has also conducted sites investigations in Atmeh and Al-Karameh camp clusters, located in Dana sub-district, which are being exposed yearly to floods and cuts in access roads. The site observation has shown the need of long/mid-term intervention as well as short-term intervention to mitigate the flood risks and consequences.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

There are several protection risks as follows: 1- Access to target location is not available to all 24/7; Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigating Measure: closely monitor these provisions/aspects during implementation. Ensure women and adolescent girls are consulted during the design, implementation and monitoring phase of projects. Accountability mechanisms are put in place and received feedback from users is followed up. Lessons from previous experience are shared and learned. 2- Potential exposure to explosive hazard (IED/UXO/ERW) during assessment/ rehabilitation of wastewater infrastructure (WWTP, drainage networks). Likelihood: Medium, Impact: High Mitigating Measure: Work closely with Mine Action actors in the area and conduct assessments/rehabilitation works only in areas declared as cleared; Conduct safety training with staff and contractors. 3- Health risks associated with construction works and exposure to raw sewage and gases. Likelihood: High, Impact: Medium Mitigating Measure: Ensure staff/partners/contractors involved in construction works or working with raw sewage wear appropriate PPE (Personal Protective Equipment) and follow established safety regulations; Conduct/repeat safety training for staff/partners/contractors. 4-Protection risk: Inequality in distribution; Physical and psychological harm arising from violence and coercion; Timing and process of distribution put women and girls at higher risk of GBV. Likelihood: High, Impact: High Mitigating Measure: Promote Establishment of adequate management plan; Promote involvement communities in distribution planning.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The targeted locations in Idlib governorate have been relatively safe. In addition, the locations are away from the clashes at the southern frontlines with the Government of Syria forces. However, despite the ceasefire agreement between Russia and Turkey from the 5th March 2020, Government of Russia increased their airstrikes, that are not only targeting the villages at the frontlines in southern Idlib countryside, but also randomly locations beyond frontlines. Recently, the Government of Russia targeted supply structures, including poultry farms, gas facilities and a hospital. In order to ensure the safety of staff and project activities, including beneficiaries, the safety and security department of Maram is already and will continue monitoring the security situation in the project area on a daily basis. The safety and security department are also including general security incidents in the Idlib governorate that might have an impact for the project locations. In addition to the risks arising from the conflict between the the risk of Covid-19 infection still exist. The cases did not increase during the first quarter of the year, but lately a high number of daily positive Covid-19 cases is registered. The number published by the Ministry of Health by the Syrian Interim Government only reflect registered cases, hence the number of positive Covid-19 is most likely higher due to the lack of testing stations. Since the beginning of the Covid-19 outbreak, Maram staff is obliged to wear masks and ensure the social distance during all activities. Moreover, Maram created SOPs for the staff on how to deal as a suspected/ positive Covid-19 case as well as developed a contact tracing form for positive/ suspected Covid-19 cases.
