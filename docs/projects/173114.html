Project Summary

Protection needs are the priority concerns address by this project, owing to the protracted conflict which erupted back in 2011. Both Libyan and non-Libyan children are disproportionately affected by the consequences of the active hostilities. Namely, they are at risk of domestic violence, recruitment by armed groups, SGBV, abduction, unlawful detention, ill-treatment, killing and maiming due to the presence of UXOs. In particular, children on the move face heightened risks of human trafficking, arbitrary detention, forced labor, physical abuse and sexual exploitation. In order to effectively reduce the vulnerabilities INTERSOS has established in 2018 a Center of Excellence, called Baity center, in Tripoli and implemented the same model in Sabha in 2020. Bayti center is a multi-sectoral programme involving education and child protection. The majority of the participants are children aged 6-18 and youth up to 24 years old, both Libyans (host community and IDPs) and non-Libyans (refugees, migrants). Baity aims at creating a safe and supportive environment and providing a range of education services that encourage children and youth’s self-development and well-being. Indeed, the center is a safe space which provides non-formal education services, life skills through learning support services as well as awareness raising sessions to children and families. In fact, both children’s families and the local community are involved in the project through outreach and awareness-raising activities. The center also acts as a referring point for boys and girls to other specialized services such as formal education and case management for CP and GBV.

1 - Needs

In 2020, dramatic changes in the operational context, including a shift in the conflict and the COVID19 pandemic further exacerbated the humanitarian crisis in Libya. COVID19 forced the country to adopt stringent measures, including full lockdowns, roadblocks, partial or full closure of facilities (including schools and health facilities), and severe limitations in provision of services and availability of basic goods. The pandemic affected people’s mental health and psychosocial wellbeing, particularly women, children, migrants and refugees. COVID-19 exposed not only fears but also heightened distress and feelings of hopelessness and helplessness. Conflict-related displacement continued throughout 2020. During the month of June alone, nearly 28,000 people were displaced following an intensification of the conflict, bringing the total number of IDPs in Libya to 392,241.The lack of access to formal education due to the COVID-19 pandemic has negatively impacted the physical and mental health and wellbeing of children, exacerbated by living conditions characterized by insecurity, poverty, exposure to explosive ordnance, limited recreational activities and inability to access safe spaces.

2 - Activities

GBV: 1.1 Conduct community outreach and awareness sessions on GBV prevention and response: The activities provided will be focused on Sexual and reproductive health (SRH) and Gender Based Violence (GBV), maternal and antenatal health and children care with the activity taking place in the form of both awareness sessions and ad hoc counselling, based on the different cases.GBV: 1.2 Establish new and improve existing referral pathways: The most vulnerable and in need among the targeted group will be able to access basic health visits through referral system with local health service providers. To this aim, agreements will be established beforehand with selected clinics and hospitals and a fee will be paid for the most vulnerable cases. Cases in need of psychological support will be internally and externally referred according to the available services.GBV: 2.1 Provide structured PSS services to women and girls: under the supervision of a dedicated social worker, a specific space within INTERSOS premises will be created for adolescent girls and women, a protected environment fully dedicated to the needs, concerns and constraints faced by this particular target group. Women and girls in need of help will be supported with structured and semi-structured counselling. The need of each person will be identified and support program so number and frequency of sessions will be established consequently. Within the women space, distribution of dignity kits and NFI will also take place, for women in need to access basic necessities items. The distributions will be requested by the case manager for each case and it will be up to the case manager to decide for a one-off urgent distribution or to frame it within a counselling program within the Women space. Finally, within the framework of this space, women committees will be created, composed of volunteer women who, after training, will aid the outreach team on case identification and support.

6 - AAP/ Accountability mechanisms
6.1 - How is your project accountable to affected populations?

Common feedback mechanism

6.2 - Other accountability mechanisms

INTERSOS' Feedback and Complaint Mechanism: Suggestion and complaint boxes will be located on each targeted location, in a place that encourages discretion and confidentiality. In addition to the complaint box, a dedicated telephone line will be published for complaint through SMS or call. Two INTERSOS staff (1 male and 1 female) will act as complaint focal persons entitled to deal with the complaints received. Each complaint will be registered and given a reference number and an acknowledgement of receipt for the complaint will be sent to the complainants when they are known. INTERSOS always pays special attention to cater for the different needs of the beneficiaries according to their age and gender and special needs, which are then reflected in programme activities and outputs. For instance, activities are developed according to age and gender; awareness sessions specifically tailored according to the participants. Equal access to such activities is guaranteed constantly; ad hoc gender and age FGDs are periodically organized in order to ensure this approach. Diversity and inclusion are also mainstreamed in the proposed programme.
