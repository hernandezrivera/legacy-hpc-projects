Project Summary

FAO Somalia will contribute to the Nutrition Cluster’s efforts to reduce the current rates and prevent new cases of malnutrition across Somalia. Through the project, FAO will specifically improve access to and promote the consumption of micronutrient rich foods for better nutrition outcomes.
This project will contribute to the Nutrition Cluster’s Strategic Objective 2. In summary, FAO will promote the consumption and utilization of micronutrient rich fruits and vegetables, through:
(1) Cash to facilitate the purchase of nutrient dense foods for children 6-23 months, pregnant and lactating women and girls (15-49 Years)
(2) Nutrition education and cooking demonstrations to influence food choices, IYCF practices and maternal health.
(3) Mother to mother peer groups will include the provision of a kitchen garden package to improve family food supplies and nutrition year round.
(4) E-nutrition vouchers will support access to fruits, vegetables and Aquatab to improve the nutrition and hygiene status of households.
More specifically, micronutrient deficiencies continue to be high in Somalia, particularly among children 6-59 months, and pregnant and lactating women. In 2021, FAO plans to support a total of 1,600 households (800 children 6-59 months and 800 pregnant and/or lactating women) with e-nutrition vouchers. Communities that have recurrently been in the IPC 3 and 4 will be the beneficiaries of this activity. The e-nutrition vouchers will provide these households with access to fruits, vegetables, aqua tabs and soap for their household use. The primary care givers of the children as well as the pregnant and/or lactating women will participate in nutrition education and communication trainings. This will involve lessons tailored to share knowledge and skills on nutrition needs of all household members from infancy to old age, food safety and hygiene at the household level as well as cooking demonstrations.
To complement the Nutrition Cluster’s efforts to provide counselling on appropriate infant and young child feeding, FAO will also support the creation of mother-to-mother peer groups. A total of 67 groups will be established, with each group composing of 15 mothers with children between the ages of 6-59 months and with cases of malnutrition in their household. Around 1,000 women in these peer groups will be supported with cash assistance to provide for the immediate needs of their households. In addition, FAO will ensure that these women are provided with training on infant and young child feeding, learn how to select nutritious locally available foods and prepare them for their children. These women will also have sessions on small-scale kitchen gardening to learn sustainable approaches to growing vegetables.
Cases of malnutrition continue to rise particularly within the IDP community. In response, FAO will provide cash assistance to 2,000 rural IDP households, e-nutrition vouchers as well as nutrition awareness to inform the choice of food and care practices within these households.
All of the nutrition education, awareness and training sessions will be conducted by locally recruited and trained members of the community, referred to as Nutrition Champions (one male and one female in every village). There will be two nutrition champions in each selected village, tasked with sharing the information and knowledge with the target beneficiaries.
Gender sensitive approaches will also be used to ensure that women are included and participate fully in activities without compromising on their role as the primary caregiver in the household.

Project Activities

1. Provision of e-nutrition vouchers, comprising a weekly package of locally produced fruits and vegetables as well as aqua tabs for water treatment and soap for hygiene. The e-nutrition vouchers will be provided to beneficiary households for a total of six months. This will be complemented by nutrition education and communication to enhance participants’ knowledge, skills, attitudes and food consumption practices. Local community members will be trained as Nutrition Champions (one male and one female per village) to promote positive nutrition messages in the target community. Target: 1,600 households. 2. Provision of cash assistance in communities with high rates of malnutrition. Cash will be provided for a duration of six months. There will be two different modalities that complement the cash assistance: (a) Establish 67 mother-to-mother groups in agropastoral communities for mothers with children 6-59 months. The assistance package includes nutrition education and education on infant and young child feeding, cooking demonstrations, and provision of gardening kits containing nutrient-dense, fast-growing varieties of fruit and vegetable seed (e.g. watermelon, carrots, leafy greens, tomatoes and onions) and user-friendly farm gardening notes.Target: 1,000 households. b) Provide cash assistance, e-vouchers and nutrition education to rural IDP communities with high rates of malnutrition. This will target children 6-59 months as well as pregnant and/or lactating women from vulnerable households. Target: 2,000 households.

What is the justification for using cash or vouchers instead of in kind?

This will reduce vulnerabilities and allow for the target households to attend to their immediate household needs such as clothing, health care, etc. without compromising the nutrition needs of the family members. Empowering households to make such decisions also avoids exploitation by vendors and supports best value for money as households purchase the speciﬁc items they require, in the quantities they need, when they need them.

Explain how the project design considered market functionality ?

The cash transfer values are based on the Cash Working Group recommendations. Market monitoring reports (with data from FSNAU and VAM) shows markets are adequately supplied with price volatility within acceptable price range. FAO will continually monitor markets in Somalia through the Food Security and Nutrition Analysis Unit (FSNAU) for any signiﬁcant issues related to market dynamics that would impact the eﬃcacy of cash and other interventions.

Mitigation Measures

FAO mitigates the risk that beneﬁciaries are exposed to violence during registration/distribution as a result of the distance to or location of humanitarian assistance and services by: (i) Carrying out beneﬁciary registration and service delivery at village level. (ii) Providing services to beneﬁciaries remotely through mobile phone, including e-nutrition vouchers disseminated by SMS. (iii) Using a consensus-based approach to beneﬁciary targeting and selection through a tripartite, that includes a committee of elders (representative of the local community), NGOs (ensuring compliance to FAO SOPs), and district authorities (ensuring the safety of the intervention and representing the government). (iv) Contracting NGOs and traders, authorized by local authorities to work in selected districts. FAO mitigates the risk of women, girls and boys exposed to sexual violence, including sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), when accessing humanitarian assistance, through: (i) Developing activities that do not require travel outside of the targeted villages. (ii) FAO’s Compliance, Complaints and Feedback (CoCO) system that enables two-way communication with communities, including conﬁdential reporting on SEA. (iii) Toll-free hotline number (disseminated by bulk SMS before activities start, monitored through systematic surveys by FAO's Call Centre). (iv) Mandatory training to partners on PSEA and AAP . FAO mitigates the risk that marginalized groups, persons with disabilities, women, boys and girls, do not participate in consultations or decision-making processes for the project or are excluded from humanitarian services/assistance/activities through: (i) The above-described consensus-based approach to beneﬁciary selection and targeting. (ii) Systematic Call Centre surveys, which embed AAP questions, query the fairness of beneﬁciary selection and ensure at least 30% of beneﬁciaries are women. (iii) FAO’s Hotline, through which communities are able to report back. (iv) Mandatory training to partners on how to target and select beneﬁciaries against FAO criteria, AAP, Gender and PSEA, among other topics. (v) Provision of UCTs, to ensure CFW and other activities do not exclude people living with disability or illness, elderly, among other groups. (vi) Mobile money, e-vouchers and applications (e.g. voice recognition, voice messages) to provide more convenient, inclusive services to people who are less mobile, illiterate, etc. (vii) Field and Third Party Monitoring (TPM). FAO mitigates the risk that beneﬁciaries are not accessing/using complaint and feedback mechanisms, through: (i) FAO’s CoCO system (explained above). (ii) Call Centre systematic surveys, for a sample of beneﬁciaries, to receive feedback and complaints. (iii) Provision of SIM cards and allowance for the purchase of a mobile device (under FAO cash-based interventions) ensuring access to complaint and feedback mechanisms. (iv) TPM and ﬁeld monitors, who have direct interaction with beneﬁciaries and report feedback. (v) Communication and sensitization sessions with communities, bulk text and voice SMS and radio campaigns to communicate FAO’s complaint and feedback mechanisms, and to update beneﬁciaries on their entitlements and other key information. FAO mitigates the risk of interference from external stakeholders in the impartial and needs-based selection of beneﬁciaries through. (i) Building consensus on the selection of villages and beneﬁciaries, through a tripartite approach (as above). (ii) District targeting by FAO, based on IPC data. (iii) Training partners on beneﬁciary selection criteria, AAP , PSEA and other mandatory topics. (iv) Mediation mechanisms, in place with district authorities, community representatives and government (federal and state). (v) TPM and FAO ﬁeld monitors, who review and mediate cases of external interferences.
