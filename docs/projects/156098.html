Project Summary

The projects aim is to respond to the acute needs of conflict affected population through provision of NFIs and the rehabilitation of shelters in Aleppo, Raqqa and Deir-ez-Zor Governorates.
NFIs will be provided through NFI Kits, NFI vouchers, Kerosene stoves, etc. Concern will distribute a combined NFI/Winterization/Shelter kit to IDPs and families recently affected by the conflict. The NFI/Einterization/Shelter kit contains blankets, mattresses, tarpaulin, sleeping mat, solar light, rope and jerry cans and is in line with NFI/shelter kits being distributed by other members of the NFI/Shelter working group. NFI/Shelter kits will be stored in Concern warehouses and held as a contingency stock to be used in emergency response depending on the conflict dynamics and the displacement of civilians during the project period.
For shelter rehabilitation a minimal amount is spent on rehabilitation works to finish an unfinished building in which IDPs are staying. The owner must agrees to an MoU that IDPs can stay there, rent free for 1 year after completion of the rehabilitation works. Shelter rehabilitation includes repair of WASH facilities in each apartment as well as doors, windows and repairs to walls in order to ensure that the beneficiaries have a minimum level of shelter and have sufficient privacy and security.

Needs

The current offensive in Deir Ez Zor, currently the only active conflict zone, is targeting the last IS strongholds in Al Mayadin and Abu Kamal districts along the Euphrates Rivers towards the Iraqi border. According to the last HNO, approximately 700,000 people live in Deir Ez Zor Governorate. Due to the increasing of the conflict in the last months of 2018, is estimated that around 400,000 people are currently displaced, while almost 515,000 live in hard-to-reach areas poorly accessible by humanitarian actors. An increasing number of people are reported on the move towards the West line and more stable areas in Ar-Raqqa and NES, Distribution of NFI items and guaranteed access to emergency water and sanitation facilities are crucial within the first 72 hours of displacement. Displacement in other Governorates remains very high. The high proportion of IDPs to host community has the potential to negatively impact social cohesion in these areas. IDPs, living in camps, informal settlments or in urban settings, are particularly vulnerable and subjects to challenges in accessing adequate shelter. When it comes to shelter, there remains an acute housing crisis in Manbij, Tabqa and Raqqa and a large number poorly serviced collective centres in Der Ez Zor, that have left large numbers of IDPs living in adequate, unfinished or damaged housing. This presents a serious health and protection risk to thousands of the already most vulnerable displaced households. While the most vulnerable, impoverished and conflict-affected returnees to Raqqa city are also unable to return to their homes as they lack the resources to undertake essential repairs. Concern household demographic emergency response data in 2018 showed that 55.8% of IDPs living in informal camps are women and 65.0% are children under the age of 18. 31.7% of households are female headed households, while 23.4% said that they had someone with a disability or chronic illness in their household. When asked what their priority needs were, 23.3% stated shelter as being a priority need. Implementation of activities

Shelter/NFI support to IDPs, returnees and vulnerable families in informal camps, collective centers and settlements is designed to meet the needs of IDPs who are currently living in informal camps across NE Syria as well as respond to future waves of displacement during 2019/20. Multi-sector emergency response activities will include NFI/shelter kits/vouchers, hygiene kits and winterisation support. The contents of hygiene kits Shelter/NFI are based on the recommendations of the respective clusters and incorporating beneficiary and staff feedback from previous Concern emergency response activities. 12,500 Hygiene and NFI/Winterization/Shelter kit contents will be procured and are designed to meet the specific age and gender needs of beneficiary households; including sanitary items for women and nappies for babies. Context appropriate hygiene promotion Information Education and Communication (IEC) materials will be distributed with the kits. As temperatures in North East Syria vary between 0 to 5 degree is winter, kerosene heaters and cash for fuel will be given to households in camps and informal settlement to provide heat and a place to cook during the cold winter months. Fire extinguishers (with fire safety IEC materials and training) and jerry cans will be distributed alongside the heaters. Shelter assistants for recently displaced IDPs to be provided through rehabilitation of unfinished buildings in urban areas to provide shelter for 750 households. In Aleppo, Raqqa and Deir Ez Zor Governorates there is a very large number of IDPs looking for shelter and camping in open areas or unfinished buildings. Therefore Concern will make an agreement with the landlords of these unfinished buildings to rehabilitate the buildings in order to improve the standard of shelter. Shelter rehabilitation will help to protect the IDPs from the environment and ensure minimum level of privacy, dignity and access to WASH facilities for each household. The shelter rehabilitation in each apartment will be based on an individual assessment of the work which needs to be done per apartment and will include the following aspects: installation of internal doors and external front door; installation of all missing windows; filling holes in block work walls, installing a roof tank and piped water connection, installing a toilet (WC) with water supply and hand washing sink; installing a sink and water supply to the kitchen; providing a waterproof cement floor in the toilet and wash room, connecting the house to the electricity supply and installing lights and electrical sockets in every room. Concern will only select those apartments for rehabilitation that provide the minimum adequate living space of 21 m2 (3.5 m2 per person X 6 people = 21 m2) as per Sphere standards.

Protection Risk Analysis

Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) or Unexploded Ordnances (UXOs) are DiscoveredProbability: Moderate / Severity: Moderate - CatastrophicThere remain numerous IEDs and UXOs in Northern Syria that pose a significant risk to civilians and NGO staff. Concern and Bahar staff, as well as program participants, will receive mine awareness training with specific focus on high-risk activities such as rehabilitating irrigation channels, tilling soil, or conducting other activities involving digging. Concern will consult with local authorities and with mine clearance organizations before starting work. Those Concern staff who have been trained to give mine risk awareness trainings are responsible to share this training with all program staff and project participants. Concern maintains regular coordination on de-mining and mine risks. Mine removal authorities will be called to deal with any suspicious devices. Attack Targets NGO StaffProbability: Unlikely / Severity: Severe - CatastrophicBoth Concern and Bahar are careful to maintain their neutrality and independence and take a conflict-sensitive approach in order to avoid being targeted by any party to the conflict. This is especially important in North East Syria where any NGO implementing activities may be perceived to be aligned with the Self-Administration who are currently controlling the program target areas. An attack could take form of a direct attack against an NGO office, vehicle, or individual. The cross-border security group – in which Concern is a member – provides a forum for Concern staff at different levels in the organizational structure to analyze security risks and to update the security management plan to reflect evolving risk. Security groups are used for tracking staff movements within Syria and for dissemination of information about security incidents to all staff. Should Concern receive credible evidence that its staff, volunteers, or beneficiaries will be targeted, all stakeholders will be informed and all program activities will be immediately suspended until Concern determines the risk has adequately diminished. Local Officials Pose a Threat to Concern’s IndependenceProbability: Moderate / Severity: MajorIn the Kurdish-controlled regions in which this program will operate, the Democratic Union Party (PYD) maintains political dominance and does not represent all of the political and ethnic groups in the area. PYD could use humanitarian assistance as a political tool, e.g., intervening in selection of beneficiaries or staff.Local authorities may present candidates whom they wish considered for work with Concern, but as the recruitment process is competitive and transparent they have grown to understand Concern will only select qualified candidates and those which also support ambitions for diversity and gender balance. Concern avoids interference from local authorities in the creation of beneficiary lists by conducting 100% household assessments with Concern staff to select project participants. Households who are not selected through this household assessment process are to contact Concern through our Complaints Response Mechanism via phone, WhatsApp, email, or feedback boxes at office locations. Additionally, MEAL personnel systemically visit samples of targeted beneficiaries in order to verify the household meets the selection criteria.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk- There is a broader risk of more widespread resumption of conflict between the SDF, GOS or FSA groups supported by Turkey, especially in border districts such as Manbij and Tabqa. In 2018, peaking at the close of the year, Turkey has taken a much more aggressive stance against areas controlled by the self-administration in North East Syria and is threatening to hold a third military campaign there to establish a buffer zone along the length of the border. There is also the risk that the fragile coalition of parties and groups that form the Syrian Democratic Council could unravel as a result of internal differences and/or external pressure, which would weaken the local authorities and likely spell increased conflict, criminality and insecurity. This situation might prevent Concern working in several areas in NES, could lead to the evacuation of international staff, scaling down to emergency assistance or closing down of some or all operations depending on the severity and scale of the conflict. - Further escalation of the conflict, or deterioriation in the security situation, in Der Ez Zor as a result of any of the following: IS resurgence particularly in the wake of reduction in support from coalition or redirection of military efforts to other conflict areas; tension between authorities and communities or inter-community conflict; movement of GoS troops into the area. In these instances, partial or complete access to Der Ez Zor - where a significant proportion of this Action's activities are targeted, would be prevented. AssumptionsThe security situation will continue to improve in Der Ez Zor, enabling Concern to establish a field office in Basira in the first quarter of 2019 and to expand activities from the West line to the North Line and East Line. - The self-administration/SDC retains control over key locations west and south of the Euphrates, specifically Manbij and Tabqa. - Concern will be able to access beneficiaries, assess their specific needs and implement the programme accordingly Mitigating MesuresConcern has developed, in coordination with other NGO actors and the NGO forum, its own scenario analysis and contingency-planning. In the event of wider political instability, insecurity or resumption of conflict - particularly in the wake of the likely US troop withdrawl in early-mid 2019. Concern's response would depend on the severity of the scenario. In the event of localised insecurity/access restrictions/power-changes in one or several locations, Concern would likely suspend operations in these areas and shift its resources to its nearest other functional field offices to assist those newly dispaced and affected by these events. If international staff had to be scaled down or pulled out completely (due to border access issues, loss of emergency evacuation options), Concern would remotely manage staff and the project from its support office in Dohuk and using its remote-management team based in Turkey
