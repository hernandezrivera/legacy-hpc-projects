Project Summary

Internally displace persons (IDPs) in Wau continue to live in the five collective centers due to unresolved political conflict in the country. The population of Wau and surrounding areas have been particularly affected significantly by outbreaks of violence and political conflict since early in the years resulting in significant influxes into collective sites. In Western Bhar el Ghazal, there are 13,034 in five collective sites in Wau (Lokoloko, cathedral, Nazereth, St. Joseph, Hai masna) and 3,843 in camp like setting located at Agok, Bazia roadside, Diem Zubier and Alel chok (CCCM Cluster Tracking August 2019). This project will target 13,034 beneficiaries (3,512 boys, 3,573 girls, 2,870 men, and 3,079 women) in 5 collective sites of Wau who have limited access to livelihood activities and in dire need of humanitarian assistants which is provided by various humanitarian actors. Therefore, its crucial to ensure timely, coordinated and need based humanitarian assistance are provided to the affected population through effective and efficient camp coordination and Camp management response mechanism. In 2020, AFOD will continue to provide extensive camp management support services in the five collective centers in Wau to meet the needs of the affected population through: continued community sensitization and mobilization, basic site maintenance and repairs; strengthening community structures and leadership for self-management, improve coordination and management of services with other partners; information management, sharing and advocacy for IDPs rights, and community engagement for self-management. AFOD will adopt combination of strategies for the CCCM response such as service delivery through effective stakeholders’ coordination, capacity building, and integration of CCCM services with Health, FSL, Education and protection of beneficiary by community for complementarity and synergy. Key stakeholders shall be engaged in implementation, monitoring and evaluation of the project including establishment of complaint feedback mechanism and complain desk, community dialogue meetings to obtain comprehensive feedback. Further to the current interventions, the primary focus remains mobile CCCM response that will deliver capacity building of site management committees (SMC), local authorities and support to ensure that dedicated skills are in place to manage the camps in an equitable manner, mitigate protection risks and play a vital role in facilitation of all sectors’ activities. AFOD together with the CCCM cluster seeks to address the anticipated and outstanding lifesaving needs of displaced, vulnerable populations in collective centres, camps and camp-like settings in and around Wau.
AFOD as an exit strategy will be working closely with RRC who is the camp administrator responsible for safety and protection of the collective sites, allocation of land for IDPs in collaboration with the community and ensuring safe return of the displaced persons into their communities. IDP leaders and local authorities have been trained on leadership and governance and will continue to develop their capacity for self-governance. Similarly, AFOD is going to increase the number of outreach assistants who shall be identified within the IDPs as an exit plan. Coexistence of the IDPs with host communities and durable solution for safe return of IDPs are formulated and strengthened through stakeholder engagement and dialogues.

1 - Needs

Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs) in South Sudan faced severe humanitarian needs and with many individuals displaced multiple times over the years of conflict, their vulnerability continued to rise (OCHA, March 2019) and humanitarian workers continue to risk their lives every day to provide life-saving assistance to people in need in South Sudan. Although the revitalization of peace agreement signed in September 2018 has registered relative improvement and calmness in security in Wau and Raga with some IDPs expressing interest to return to their homes, new arrivals are being reported in Hai Masna collective site due to conflict arising from cattle rustling in Jur River County. The conflict continues to impact negatively on the communities with many people in need of humanitarian assistance (Oct, July 2019) and the effects of years of conflict, displacement and lack of basic services continued to be felt throughout the country (OCHA, March 2019). A total of 241,000 IDPs in PoCs and collective sites and close to 1.07M in spontaneous settlements will benefit from site management activities in Protection of Civilians sites and collective centers in the country (CCCM cluster 2019; OCHA, Oct 2019). Currently there are 16,097 displaced persons living in the 5 collective sites of Cathedral, St. Joseph, Lokoloko, Hai Masna and Nazareth in Wau. However, due to frequent security incidences and the recurrent inter-tribal, inter clan conflicts over the past months, affected populations have lost the confidence to return to their original villages fully and that is why there is static presence as well as new influxes into collective centers in Wau. AFOD together with CCCM cluster and other partners continued to provide essential services to the IDPs in and around the collective sites. Therefore, there is need to continue strengthening the local community structures and capacities for full participation, ownership and sustainability (CCCM Sitrep August 2019). AFOD with CCCM cluster will continue to provide needs based CCCM activities in the five collective sites in Wau in close collaboration with local stakeholders and partners.

2 - Activities

AFOD through this project will conduct the following activities in all collective sites in Wau; 1. Identify spontaneous settlements or new arrivals in collective sites and report to appropriate clusters 2. Conduct site profiling and mapping of service provision and share appropriate information with beneficiaries. AFOD will conduct monthly site profiling, mapping and develop 5Ws in all collectives of Cathedral, Lokoloko, St. Joseph, Nazareth, and Hai Masna to identify gaps and share the report/information with the relevant clusters for decision making. 3. Strengthen Information Management for IDPs and complaints and feedback mechanisms (CFM roll out) and engage the site committee to address the issue identified. AFOD will continue to strengthen complaint feedback system including protection mainstreaming to ensure equal access and needs based assistance is provided to affected populations, strengthen engagement with the IDPs and continue to build inclusive community participation to ensure total local ownership, self-governance and self-reliance.4. Monitoring of quality service provision at collective sites and at out of camp like setting. AFOD will conduct safety audits and beneficiary satisfaction survey to inform decision making and service improvement.5. Conduct site maintenance activities to support minimum living standards and protection. AFOD will ensure all collective sites have specific services for People with Special needs (PSNs). AFOD will continue to implement activities of site maintenance & rehabilitation with a specific focus on PSNs and improving referral pathways. 6. Establish referral pathways for all services available in collective sites (GBV, health, education, protection, nutrition, WASH and NFI)7. Conduct community engagement, mobilization and sensitization. AFOD will carry out monthly community mobilization and sensitization to promote coexistence of the IDPs with host communities and durable solution for safe return of IDPs are formulated and strengthened through stakeholder engagement and dialogues.8. Support self-managed structures and community led site management in camp-like settings through site maintenance & rehabilitation and promoting IDPs participation. 9. Train staff in CCCM and humanitarian response.10. Train IDP site leaders and site committees on CFM to strengthen site management and accountability. AFOD will enhance the capacities of site management committees to be able to self- governed the collective sites including working camp administrator, who shall provide oversight and supervise the activities in camps and camp-like situations 11. Organize and conduct site level coordination meetings. AFOD will strengthen collaboration with other partners through information sharing, regular site level coordination meetings.12. Contingency planning. AFOD will lead process of contingency planning in all the collective sites and ensure contingency plans are updated regularly and shared with cluster members and stakeholders. 13. Select beneficiaries to be supported with Cash based initiative through cash for work in all the 5 collective sites of Cathedral, Lokoloko, St. Joseph, Nazareth, and Hai Masna. AFOD together with site committees identify individuals to benefit from cash for work modality.

6.6 - Will beneficiaries' rights be legally recognised?

Yes, all beneficiaries' rights will be respected and recognized to ensure they are not abused and are treated with dignity. The community structures such as community leaders, site management committees etc, will be empowered to demand for their rights for services and participation through the camp management support. AFOD shall ensure do no harm principle is adhered to, non-discrimination during service provision, safety, dignity, participation of affected IDPs, empowerment and accountability measures are integrated into all partner response.

6.5 - Includes perspectives of displacement-affected people?

Yes, the displaced and affected communities have full access to the CCCM services in collective sites in and around. Through mobile services, the affected communities will be reached and provided services as required in close coordination with RRC office and local authorities.

6.2 - Is the project linked to government/community systems?

Yes, the CCCM project is linked and oversight provided by the Relief and Rehabilitation commission (RRC) which is the the operational arm of the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management of the government responsible for the welfare of displaced persons. The IDP leadership and CCCM project team work hand in hand with RRC to coordinate and manage the project.

6.3 - Is the project aligned with national objectives?

Yes, the CCCM project is in line with the Relief and Rehabilitation Commission objectives under the Ministry of Humanitarian Affairs and Disaster Management that responsible for addressing the needs of the affected people.

6.1 - Is the protection environment sufficiently safe/stable?

Yes, the protection environment is safe and stable within the collective sites. All the collective sites have fully instituted security structures including availability of street lights, the IDP leadership that is active and responsible to identify, report and manage any protection related issue to the relevant clusters. AFOD will coordinate closely with the relevant cluster on protection matters. AFOD will observe and maintain safety and security measures to ensure beneficiaries are safe. Routinely AFOD will assess safety and security in its areas of operation including staff safety and report any security related issues to the local authorities in collaboration with RRC and other actors in the state like OCHA, UNMISS, IOM and t national level, OCHA and UNMISS to prioritise safety and security in all collective sites and around.

6.4 - Does the project include sustainability/handover?

Yes, the CCCM project takes into consideration the sustainability plan including handover to the community members to self managed themselves. AFOD has trained IDP leaders and committees including women who are supporting the management and coordination of CCCM activities in all the collective sites. It is expected that the CCCM activities are fully managed by the IDP leadership in near future. Right from project inception, the site committees and camp leaders will take lead in coordinating activities of CCCM in the collective centers.

8.18 - Please indicate how the risks selected above will be mitigated.

AFOD will ensure women and girls are fully involved and consulted during the project implementation. Through the IDP leadership system, the women and girls will be involved and participate in the management of the IDP sites. The project will promote harmony among the IDPs and services will be provided to every beneficiary regardless of race, tribe and ethnic dimensions. AFOD will coordinate with protection cluster to ensure the target beneficiaries have dignity and safety while lifesaving interventions are provided to them regardless of race, tribe, sex and special needs. AFOD will facilitate training from protection partners to the site committees and local leadership. AFOD shall ensure do no harm principle is adhered.All identified protection threats or inequalities will be addressed collectively through the different clusters taking into account the different needs of the population groups. Through the feedback mechanism system established and IDP leadership, protection threats or inequalities are reported and tracked by the site committees and community outreach assistants. AFOD will ensure the already transparent feedback mechanism system that has been established, will be strengthened in all collective sites. AFOD Shall ensure two-way feedback platforms are created so that IDPs can share their feelings and perception about the services being provided. During Community engagement, mobilization and sensitization, any feedback shall be transmitted to the IDPs, and the site committees will be empowered to monitor, report and represent the needs of the community.
