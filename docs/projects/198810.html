Project Summary

The purpose of the project is to contribute to protection of civilians from the threat of explosive ordnance in Ngala and Gwoza LGAs of Borno State in northeast Nigeria.
In line with UNMAS Nigeria’s Programme Strategy, the project contributes to a vision of national authorities protecting civilians from the threat of EO in north-east Nigeria and the programme’s mission is to support national capacities active within the Mine Action sector, including the security sector and civil society. The implementation of this project advances two key outcomes: (1) relevant national and local entities mitigate the explosive threat in north-east Nigeria, based on an accurate understanding of the scale and scope of EO contamination, and (2) the immediate threat posed by explosive ordnance to civilians and humanitarian and development actors is mitigated.
To advance the above-stated outcomes, UNMAS will deploy specialist staff to Gwoza and Ngala LGAs to train and equip select members of the Community Stabilization Committee and the Community Security Group, community groups established by UNDP/ Grassroot Initiative For Strengthening Community Resilience (GISCOR) as explosive ordnance risk education (EORE) facilitators. If appropriate, additional community members who have already established trust with, and have access to people affected by the presence of EO will be trained and equipped.
Following training, UNMAS will provide mentoring, further supporting the newly trained EORE facilitators. As experienced EORE facilitators themselves, UNMAS staff will provide hands-on guidance and advice during the first EORE sessions carried out by the newly trained facilitators. As such, they will fulfill quality assurance (QA) through real-time feedback post-EORE sessions, which will foster an environment of continuous learning and improvement, as well as enabling monitoring and evaluation of the impact and effectiveness.
UNMAS Nigeria will also work with LGA-based National Security and Civil Defense Corps (NSCDC) staff that were previously trained by UNMAS in Non-Technical Survey to establish a community-endorsed reporting chain. The approach is intended to encourage civilians to report on EO and suspected hazardous areas (SHA) and is aligned with the Information Management System for Mine Action (IMSMA), allowing for accurate data collection on EO or SHA reported by the population, following EORE sessions. The IMSMA reports will then be made available to, and coordinated with the UNMAS-trained Nigerian Police Force Explosive Ordnance Disposal (NPFEOD) teams stationed in the LGA, or when they deploy to the LGA to clear the explosive hazard.

1 - Needs

The conflict in north-east Nigeria is in its 12th year and continues to cause suffering for millions of Nigerians, particularly women and children. The use of explosive weapons during the ongoing conflict, primarily Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) by Non-State Armed Groups (NSAG), has resulted in deaths and severe injuries among civilians. Between January 2019 and November 2022, UNMAS recorded a total of 2,220 explosive incidents. From January 2016 till date, 2,450 people were injured and 1,464 killed, out of which 1,472 civilians were injured and 836 civilians killed. According to the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) of 2022, women and children constitute 81 per cent of the overall population affected by the crisis and 87 percent of new displacements. In particular, risk of injury and death caused by Explosive Ordnance (EO) has become more acute since 2021, as there has been an increase in the movement of people following the closure of IDP camps within the Maiduguri metropolitan area. As of 30 June 2022, six out of the eleven IDP camps set up in government purpose-built buildings within Maiduguri have been closed. According to the IOM Displacement Tracking report published in June 2022, the closure of the IDP camps has resulted in the movement of over 66,000 individuals to areas of return. The Borno State Return Strategy emphasizes the following: “restoration of security in areas of return is the main concern of IDPs and returning refugees. IDPs and returning refugees must be protected from violence, threats and intimidation, undue restrictions to their freedom of movement, mines, and unexploded ordnances and any other threats to their rights and security”. Despite the mitigation measures implemented so far, people resettling in areas in proximity to EO contamination remain at a heightened risk, which has resulted in a sharp increase in civilian casualties in 2022. Especially in Gwoza and Ngala Local Government Areas (LGAs), UNMAS noted a rise of 80 percent in civilian victims caused by EO, impacting security, freedom of movement and safe access to agricultural land, which in turn affects food security and is an obstacle to early recovery. The project is aligned with UNMAS Nigeria’s strategic, multi-year comprehensive approach to the protection of civilians and humanitarian actors through humanitarian mine action in north-east Nigeria. The funding will contribute to the implementation of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development Goals. This project specifically contributes to “SDG 16: Promote peaceful and inclusive societies for sustainable development, provide access to justice for all and build effective, accountable and inclusive institutions at all levels”, as well as contributing to other SDGs ranging from food security and gender equality to poverty reduction. In line with Security Council Resolution 1325 (2000) on Women, Peace and Security, this project will give special attention to the participation of women and youth.

2 - Activities

The project will implement the following activities:Training of EORE FacilitatorsThe training will focus on building a local and sustainable capacity to deliver EORE. UNMAS staff will deliver a three-day EORE Facilitator Course in Gwoza and Ngala LGAs. The course package includes an overview of Humanitarian Mine Action and the role that EORE plays, recognition of EO and hazardous areas, in-depth discussion on key safety messages and safe behavior for adults, children, farmers, scrap metal collectors and other people at greater risk from EO. The course package further elaborates on the use of information, education and communication (IEC) tools and facilitation skills to be applied during face-to-face EORE sessions. Each course participant will also be trained in the use of Electronic Talking Devices (ETD) with pre-loaded EORE content.UNMAS anticipates training 30 EORE facilitators per LGA.Provision of EORE materials and equipmentAfter successful completion of the training, each participant will be equipped with an EORE Facilitators Kit containing the following IEC materials (in Hausa or Kanuri language):⦁ 1 Flipchart for adults⦁ 1 Flipchart for children⦁ 750 Leaflets for adults⦁ 500 Leaflets for children⦁ 10 Posters⦁ 2 BannersIn addition, each participant will be provided with 5 ETDs with pre-loaded content in either Hausa or Kanuri languages.The IEC materials have been developed in close consultation with Mine Action partners in north-east Nigeria and have been adopted by organizations conducting EORE as the standardized IEC kit in the north-east. The flipcharts are used during the face-to-face EORE sessions to explain and transfer key safety messages to the participants of the session and focus on recognition of EO and suspected and confirmed hazardous areas. During the interactive sessions, the EORE facilitators present what constitutes unsafe behavior and consequences, as well as safe behavior and how to report EO and hazardous areas when seen or suspected. As part of the EORE sessions, leaflets are handed out to participants and posters are used within the community, serving as a reminder of the safety messages received. The use of Electronic Talking Devices was piloted by UNMAS earlier in 2022 and has proven to be an effective tool that further supports and extends EORE sessions in the community. An ETD is a handheld solar-powered device that is pre-loaded with content to reinforce EORE key safety messages in the form of stories, songs and case studies of EO victims. The advantage of the ETD is that it remains in the community after the project is completed and thus becomes a community resource that continues to reach people, including new community members. UNMAS anticipates providing in each LGA 30 Adult flipcharts, 30 Children Flipcharts, 22,500 Adult leaflets, 15,000 Children leaflets, 300 posters, 60 banners and 300 ETDs.Mentoring and QA of EORE sessionsThe UNMAS team will be available to the newly trained EORE facilitators to assist with the coordination, planning and organization of EORE sessions in the first few days after the end of the training. The UNMAS instructors have a wealth of experience as EORE facilitators themselves and will plan together with each facilitator at least one joined session to provide guidance, feedback and, if required, further training on how to run an effective EORE session. The UNMAS staff will conduct QA assessment per EORE facilitator as part of an evaluation of the effectiveness of their EORE session. Provision of advice on the establishment of an EO reporting chain within the community During project implementation, UNMAS will engage with NSCDC staff present in the LGA that were previously trained by UNMAS in EORE and NTS . UNMAS staff will advise the NSCDC, the Community Stabilization Committee and the Community Security Group on the establishment of a repository for information on EO contamination. The local population will be involved in the project.
