Project Summary

Partindo de uma estratégia que assenta na correlação entre a assistência humanitária, o desenvolvimento e a sua contribuição para a paz, isto é, uma resposta de emergência, o projeto centra-se num conjunto de ações interligadas que respondem às necessidades mais imediatas (acesso a insumos para produção agrícola e acesso a alimentos) ao mesmo tempo que aumentam a resiliência e abordam as causas sistêmicas da vulnerabilidade das famílias ao impacto de situações de crise e de estabilidade social. A acção centra-se nas províncias de Nampula nos distritos de Nemba, Ilha de Moçambique, Mossuril, Mongicual, Liupo, Meconta e Erati. Em Cabo Delgado nos distritos de Namuno, Necufi, Chiure, Montepuez e Ancuabe. O projeto tem como objetivo geral Aumentar a resiliência das comunidades em situações risco através de uma abordagem integrada de produção e segurança alimentar., através do objetivo especifico de ‘Recuperar a capacidade produtiva de pequenos produtores afetados pelas situações de risco e de emergencia, como forma de contribuir para a segurança alimentar das suas famílias e para a resiliência da comunidade’ (OE). Para isso, a projecto estrutura-se em 3 eixos de intervenção, interligados entre si e coincidentes com 3 resultados que serão alcançados através da implementação de actividades de dotação de meios e capacidades, apoiada em actividades transversais.
No entanto, a abordagem metodológica, vai mais além, no sentido de apoiar a retoma urgente aos processos e dinâmicas comerciais e de mercado onde estavam integrados e tornar esta população mais resiliente perante potenciais eventos semelhantes: i) R.1. ‘Restaurada a produção agrícola das famílias afectadas’, orientado para a recuperação produtiva das famílias afectadas e das áreas produtivas; ii) R.2. ‘Melhorada a capacidade das famílias afetadas de armazenar, preservar e maximizar os benefícios nutricionais dos alimentos, orientado para incrementar a resiliência das famílias e a sua segurança nutricional; iii) R.3. ‘Aumentadas as capacidades locais para mitigar e responder aos impactos futuros de situações de emergencia na segurança alimentar e meios de vida’.
O projecto apoiará 7,050 famílias (correspondendo a > 35,100 pessoas), integradas ou com interesse em se integrarem em 22 organizações de produtores, em 1050 são de Nemba, 3860 da Ilha de Moçambique, 2050 Mossuril, 3010 Mongicual, 7855 Liupo, 3495 Meconta, 2220 Erati, 1395 Namuno, 1395 Mecufi, 2135 Chiure, 5065 Montepuez, 1570 Ancuabe.
O projecto pretende contribuir de forma positiva para uma maior igualdade de género, desenhando uma estratégia concreta que promova o empoderamento da mulher e o seu maior acesso às oportunidades concedidas pelo projecto.

1 - Briefly summarize your project, including key target groups

Based on a strategy that is based on the correlation between humanitarian assistance, development and its contribution to peace, that is, an emergency response, the project focuses on a set of interconnected actions that respond to the most immediate needs (access to inputs for agricultural production and access to food) while building resilience and addressing the systemic causes of household vulnerability to the impact of crisis situations and social stability. The action is centered in the provinces of Nampula in the districts of Nemba, Ilha de Moçambique, Mossuril, Mongicual, Liupo, Meconta and Erati. In Cabo Delgado in the districts of Namuno, Necufi, Chiure, Montepuez and Ancuabe. The project's general objective is to increase the resilience of communities in risk situations through an integrated approach to production and food security, through the specific objective of 'Recovering the productive capacity of small producers affected by risk and emergency situations, as a way to contribute to their families' food security and community resilience' (OE). To this end, the project is structured around 3 intervention axes, interconnected and coinciding with 3 results that will be achieved through the implementation of activities to endow means and capacities, supported by transversal activities.However, the methodological approach goes further, in the sense of supporting the urgent resumption of the commercial and market processes and dynamics in which they were integrated and making this population more resilient in the face of potential similar events: i) R.1. ‘Restored agricultural production of affected families’, aimed at the productive recovery of affected families and productive areas; ii) R.2. ‘Improved the ability of affected households to store, preserve and maximize the nutritional benefits of food, aimed at increasing household resilience and nutritional security; iii) R.3. ‘Increased local capacities to mitigate and respond to future impacts of emergencies on food security and livelihoods’.The project will support 7,050 families (corresponding to > 35,100 people), integrated or interested in joining 22 producer organizations, 1050 from Nemba, 3860 from Ilha de Moçambique, 2050 Mossuril, 3010 Mongicual, 7855 Liupo, 3495 Meconta, 2220 Erati, 1395 Namuno, 1395 Mecufi, 2135 Chiure, 5065 Montepuez, 1570 Ancuabe.The project intends to make a positive contribution to greater gender equality, designing a concrete strategy that promotes women's empowerment and greater access to the opportunities provided by the project.

5 - Protection Risk Analysis
5.1 - Describe how the proposed project identifies potential risks, and avoid causing harm?

Low Risk - Political instability caused by changes in the sociopolitical context lead to political changes and discontinuity in implementation of reconstruction/DRR policies.The project will be implemented transparently and through a participatory approach aimed at ongoing dialogue and involvement of all actors (which happens from the initial phase of formulating the concept note). This will allow for a good understanding of the project by the local actors involved, promote ownership of the project by local entities and communities and facilitate the adaptation of strategies in case of changes. will be Collaboration agreements established at the beginning of the project with a view to engaging local political authorities in addition to the people (individual contacts). Low Risk - Reduction of political interest by the local government for participation in activities and support for producers.The project will promote the training of technicians from the sectors government at the local level via participation in all consultations and training, awareness and information activities, which will lead to ownership of the project and consequent interest in the activities and their inclusion in the district plans after the project. Medium Risk - Access to the intervention zone is affected by weather shocks/weather conditions.Activity planning will take into account the constraints of the station and the project team will monitor the situation. If one worst case scenario arrives, the project can be prepared to propose appropriate aid alternatives. Medium/High Risk - Potential conflicts/attacks in the intervention zone make it difficult to access and implement activities.Oikos has a security plan for the regions, which will be adapted and used within the scope of this project. The implementation of the plan considers a training component to project team. Medium Risk - Lack of seed/input stocks. High cost of materials.Diversification of suppliers; Coordination with Food Security Cluster, FAO, UN Logistics, involved in the distribution of inputs. Negotiations with commercial agents already carried out before the start of project execution. Prices of the main products agreed according to the budget. Possibility of consultation in the international market. Low Risk - Beneficiaries are not interested in training/awareness raising due to the urgency of basic needs.Articulation with local authorities and leaders and involvement of beneficiaries. Prior information on project support conditions involving participation in training sessions.Consultations for project design were carried out with the active participation of local leaders and beneficiary representatives. In addition, Oikos will hire Field Technicians extension agents responsible for the organization, mobilization and community participation. Low Risk - Women do not participate in project activitiesThe selection of beneficiaries will promote the involvement and participation of women and consider gender criteria. The project foresees mechanisms to facilitate the participation of women, both in training and in all activities. The development of a gender strategy by the project aims to ensure the application of the most appropriate approaches for the involvement and empowerment of women/girls.

5.2 - How does the proposed project encourage meaningful access to assistance and there is participation and empowerment of people specifically women and elderly person?

The action recognizes the socioeconomic disadvantage and unequal access of women with regard to land, agricultural inputs, education/training and decision-making, a fact that represents a significant weight considering that they are in greater numbers in agricultural production in rural areas.It is intended, namely, to increase the access and participation of women and young people in associative life, through their participation in production, commercialization, management of savings and involvement in business opportunities. Gender issues will also be taken into account in training, capacity building, as well as in sensitizations on nutrition that will be addressed to women and men, including actions for their mobilization and participation.In addition to a transversal gender approach, based on a project gender strategy that will be prepared at the beginning of the action, and which ensures that all planned and implemented activities benefit both men and women (with a focus on increasing the participation of women), specific strategies aimed at groups of women will also be implemented, namely support to groups/organisations of women producers and savings groups led by women, with a view to strengthening community structures led by women that contribute to the empowerment of women in the rural world. The project's gender strategy will also serve as a monitoring and evaluation tool for the project, allowing an analysis of information disaggregated by gender.With regard to specific gender issues in the context of emergency and post-emergency/recovery humanitarian assistance, the Oikos team, as part of its involvement in the emergency response, received training of trainers, facilitated by specialists from the Security Cluster Food/Humanitarian Aid Coordination, on gender violence in the context of emergencies/post-disasters, namely the prevention of abuse and sexual exploitation of women in the context of humanitarian assistance, which will be replicated to the project team, so to raise awareness about the prevention of gender violence. Overall, the surveys confirm the urgent need to secure women's livelihoods in the recovery and reconstruction phase, and here is an opportunity to promote women's participation and inclusion. In terms of strategies to ensure this participation within the scope of the proposed action, as mentioned above, attention will be given to aspects such as: acquisition of agricultural inputs and equipment to be managed by women; support for economic initiatives complementary to agriculture managed by women's groups, support for the creation of savings groups managed by women to promote management capacity and financial decision-making; between others.In terms of methodology, the GALS (Gender Action Learning) methodology will be used, a very participatory methodology, to be successfully implemented in the context of other Oikos projects, which is based on community awareness sessions conducted within and with the participation of the company itself. community. In general, some couples (families) are identified with whom various gender issues are worked on, which then have the role of mobilizing other couples. Awareness raising is carried out using theater and radio messages to expand information. Strategies are promoted, such as: reflection and discussion sessions, which will be integrated into training sessions on other topics, radio messages with content that promote gender equity and equal opportunities for men and women.

As in the selection of women as a beneficiary group, attention will also be paid to the inclusion of people with disabilities among the beneficiaries. 7 - Environmental considerations
7.1 - How does your project impact on the environment, is there mitigation measures or recommended actions such as environment assessments that are planned for?

Environmental issues and adaptation to climate change will be transversally integrated into the different components of the project, since the underlying action is intended to increase knowledge about these issues and increase the resilience of populations in the face of the impacts of climate change/natural disasters, in order to reduce their impact on human lives, property and livelihoods. It is intended inin particular increase knowledge about climate change and risk management/necessary adaptation measures among agricultural producers. This will be achieved through actions to strengthen risk management mechanisms, such as the creation/training of Disaster Risk Management Committees, and specific training sessions incorporated in the training plan, which includes training packages on climate change and conservation agriculture that include information on protective measures against climate change: drought, irregular rainfall, high rainfall and very large discharges in a short time, erosion, strong winds, etc. Awareness-raising actions will also be carried out, using participatory tools, such as social theatre, and awareness-raising through radio messages, for the expansion of information. On the other hand, the project includes approaches, means and knowledge that aim to increase the resilience of producers and communities in the face of potential natural disasters. This perspective of resilience, inserted in the objective of the project, is a guiding aspect of the entire intervention: The project is oriented not only to restore the food security of the affected families, but mainly to provide them with resilience in the face of similar future situations. This resilience comes from a reduction in vulnerability, either through the provision of adequate means, diversification of productive and economic alternatives, increased skills and knowledge, but also from greater information, preparation and structuring of the community itself and the support structures on the part of the local authorities.Additionally, the introduction of technologies or materials with a negative environmental impact is not foreseen. On the other hand, appropriate practices and technologies with a positive impact on biodiversity, such as conservation agriculture, will be promoted.
