Project Summary

A leader in the fight against malnutrition, AAH implements multipurpose cash emergency interventions targeting the most vulnerable households to meet their basic needs with dignity. AAH assessments confirmed that the income generated by the households is insufficient to cover the basic needs of food and non-food items, therefore, households are implementing several negative coping strategies to survive with limited financial resources. Purchasing on credit is common as well as reducing food intake. AAH also documented increasing GBV cases as child labour for boys and early marriage for girls because of the financial hardship. Syria ranked amongst the ten most food-insecure countries globally by mid-2021. In 2022, 14.6 million people need humanitarian assistance, an increase of 1.2 million from 2021. The overall estimated Food Security and Agriculture sector PiN for 2022 - of food insecure plus those at risk of food insecurity - has increased from 14.2 million to 14.6 million, with 12.8 million facing acute food insecurity and 1.8 million at risk of sliding into food insecurity. Furthermore, 1.8 million people living in camps are deemed to be 100 percent food insecure, with an increase of 470,000 people facing acute food insecurity in 2022 compared to 2021. In November 2021, the national average price of WFP’s standard reference food basket reached its highest ever recorded level since monitoring started (2013), increasing by five percent between October and November 2021 and reaching SYP 213,223 (USD 85 at the official exchange rate of SYP 2,500/USD). AAH will target vulnerable groups (families including PLWs, female headed households, persons with disabilities, persons with chronic diseases) with a multipurpose cash transfer through Financial Service Providers located near the targeted households. The amount of the cash will be based on the recommendation of the Cash Working Group in Syria and on the latest revision of the Minimum Expenditure Basket. In July 2021, the MEB stood at SYP 546,215 for a family of five, a 97 percent increase from 12 months earlier (July 2020 (SYP 277,084). The amount that a family could not cover to meet their basic needs (MEB gap) was set at SYP 404,481. The Cash Working Group recommended providing 60% of the MEB gap to meet as the higher number as possible considering the increase in the cost of the basic items and the deterioration of the socio-economic situation of the people and the purchasing power of the households. The recommended value is (SYP 243,000) as a national recommended value, taking into account this rounded national figure could be adjusted to reflect the geographical areas (in north Syria = SYP 211,000; in North West = SYP 243,000; in Middle of Syria = SYP 247,000; Costal = SYP 254,000; in South of Syria = SYP 220,000). AAH will directly select beneficiaries and inform them. There will be two distributions at one-month interval. Post distribution monitoring surveys will be conducted 4-6 weeks following the distribution of the cash to determine where households spent the distributed money, and their general satisfaction. Baseline and endline surveys will be conducted with the impact of the project based on quantitative measurement of the change. A session to review the Action implementation, identify the best practices, and for recommendation in future interventions involving all stakeholders will be conducted. The planned cash is an unconditional and unrestricted cash transfer based on the recommended CTV of the CWG in Syria. The expenditure allocation by sector (FSA 82% and WASH 18%) is an estimate following the CWG recommendations. As part of AAH integrated programme, beneficiaries of this intervention will receive sustainable health, nutrition, WASH services and livelihood support while the multipurpose cash provides immediate relief to their urgent needs. The project covers 2022 and 2023, AAH estimates the same beneficiary caseload each year.

4 - Needs

The overall estimated FSA Sector PIN for 2022 of food insecure and those at risk of food insecurity is estimated at 13 9 million with 12 million facing acute food insecurity and 1 9 million people at risk of sliding into food insecurity. The estimated PiN in Aleppo is 2.3 M, in Hama 1.1 M, in Dar’a 0.7 M. The FSA sector indicated the importance to sustain minimum food consumption needs. In December, the national average price of WFP’s standard reference food basket recorded the fourth consecutive month registering the highest ever recorded price since monitoring started 2013, reaching SYP 220,112. AAH assessments indicated that a large proportion of the income is spent on purchasing food, non-food items water and medicines. The results of AAH multipurpose cash project implemented last year support these spending patterns. Given the hyperinflation, and the stagnant economy of the country after more than ten years of conflict, household income is largely insufficient to cover the basic needs. Therefore, the households are implementing a number of coping strategies – for example: decreasing the number of meals eaten per day, decreasing the size of the meal, restricting adults from consuming food in order to let children eat, and selecting less preferable food and less expensive food items to consume by the families are confirmed and highlighted by the AAH’s needs assessments. GBV risks increased as more boys are sent to work and girls are married at a young age.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented by the AAH field offices directly with support from the technical team in Damascus. Cash distribution will be undertaken by FSPs. The FSPs selection will be conducted through AAH procurement procedures with a technically competitive tender. There will be two distributions at one-month interval. Beneficiaries will be selected through Beneficiaries Selection Committees in the targeted locations, key vulnerability criteria will include: HH with PLW, HH with disabled members, female headed HH. Beneficiaries’ data will include name, contact number and a named alternative (in case the beneficiary are not able to collect the money, they will select someone they trust). AAH MEAL department will verify households’ selection is against the vulnerability criteria. A comprehensive monitoring plan will be designed to support collection and tracking of sex and age disaggregated data in line with the proposed MEAL Framework. The FSP will inform the Beneficiaries by SMS to come to the FSP office to receive the cash. AAH team will contact beneficiaries to explain the project, the beneficiary selection, the amount they will receive, place and time of the transfer. The transfer value is the value recommended by the CWG (rounded national figure SYP 243,000). A PDM report will be prepared. Outputs are measured against defined indicators to quantify the impact through baseline/endline surveys. An accessible feedback and complaint mechanism is rolled out.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk: selecting beneficiaries may increase the tension within the community. Likelihood: medium; Impact: High; Mitigation measures: participatory approach in selecting the beneficiaries including women and marginalised community groups, accessible feedback and complaint mechanism in place and communities informed on it, AAH staff will address the complaint/feedback; information shared about the project and the selection criteria. Risk: beneficiaries will face security incidences while receiving their cash. Likelihood: medium; Impact: High Mitigation measure: AAH will ensure to select the closest Financial Service Provider to the beneficiaries. Ensuring that the FSP facility is secure. Risk: beneficiary data protection is compromised. Likelihood: low; Impact: medium. AAH has comprehensive measures to protect beneficiary data, advocacy and coordination with local stakeholders to avoid breach in data protection.


10 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

AAH recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect, and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. Implementation of humanitarian aid activities can sometimes have the unforeseen impact of creating unequal power balances within the AAH team and for recipients of aid; the organization is committed to mitigating against such risks. Sexual exploitation and abuse can do great harm to people affecting integrity and trust; AAH prohibits such exploitation by affirming a zero-tolerance approach. Disregard of the PSEA polices is regarded as gross misconduct, which could result in summary dismissal. AAH reserves the right to refer such cases to the relevant legal authorities in the abuser’s country of origin and in the host country. All AAH staff will always treat people with dignity and respect; undertake awareness raising of the PSEA policy and report any instances of a breach of this policy to the prescribed channels. All employees and representatives of AAH will not engage in sexually exploitative behaviour or linking sexual favour with the receipt of aid; engage in any sexual activity with a child under 18 years of age; or being involved with any forms of prostitution or payment for sex. AAH will investigate allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in a timely, professional, and confidential manner.
