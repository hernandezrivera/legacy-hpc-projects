Project Summary

Under DRC’s CCCM programme, the following major interventions will be implemented:
- Camp management of the Malakal Protection of Civilian (PoC) site, including ongoing management, service coordination, monitoring and community engagement.
- Mobile site management and site coordination for camps, collective centres and camp like settings in Unity State as part of the ‘Beyond Bentiu Response’, including service mapping, rapid assessments and monitoring, community engagement and community governance support.
- Mobile site management and site coordination for Fashoda, Malakal, Baliet and Canal counties including service mapping, rapid assessments and monitoring, community engagement and community governance support.
- National mobile emergency site management support in camps and camp like settings through emergency deployable CCCM teams.

Needs

There are currently an estimated 2 million people displaced across South Sudan as a result of conflict, economic decline and food insecurity. Of these 2 million, at least 1.4 million IDPs are living in camp or camp-like settings. This includes 198,000 IDPs living in PoC sites on UNMISS bases where they fled to seek physical protection from violence, as well as a significant number of IDPs living in camp like settings including collective sites, informal camps and spontaneously developed settlements. In all these locations, coordination of humanitarian assistance is essential to ensure that provision of services is efficient, accountable and needs-based. Malakal PoC: Currently nearly 25,000 people live in Malakal PoC, many of them seeking safety from armed conflict with limited options for access services, accommodation or livelihoods opportunities. Many of the current population come from nearby towns including Malakal Town where the land they used to own has now been occupied by people from other ethnic groups creating further barriers for return. As a result, it is highly likely that a significant number of those living in the PoC site will continue to live there, requiring coordination of services and assistance across the camp. Upper Nile & Unity camp like settings: In addition to the Malakal PoC, over 8,000 IDPs are living in the Aburoc displacement site where they are reliant on humanitarian assistance for basic services. There are also a significant number of displaced people and returnees living in collective centres and spontaneous sites across the West Bank of the Nile with upwards of 50,000 identified in settlements in Baliet, Fashoda, Malakal, and Panyikang counties. Many of these people have been disenfranchised from the traditional humanitarian response as they have not been living in recognised camp settings and face disproportionate difficulties in access needs-based service provision. In addition, the coordination of service, monitoring of populations and coordination community engagement is largely lacking in Upper Nile outside of the Malakal PoC and Aburoc IDP camp. This is a significant gap in an area where there is increasingly flows of IDPs and returnees across the area driven my both shortages in services and some pockets of stability. Mobile response: DRC’s deployment to areas of new or existing displacement have often noted a lack of capacity for coordination of humanitarian assistance at site level in camp like settings, a lack of coherent communications with communities and a lack of access to robust needs assessment information on displaced populations living in camp like settings. For example, DRC’s rapid multi-sectoral assessment to locations in Southern Unity state (June-July 2018) identified a significantly increasing population who had been displaced due to recent conflict but a limited presence on the ground to coordinate humanitarian assistance in collective centres or temporary camp-like settings. Similar needs have been identified in sites such as the Juba-Managteen site which required rapid emergency coordination and in Yirol East where a camp of 8,000 people was spontaneously established in an area with no static CCCM or coordination actors.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes, this project is specifically designed to strengthen community engagement and accountability for populations living in camps and camp like settings. The CCCM teams in PoCs and camps, and the mobile response teams will engage with beneficiaries through awareness raising, counselling, information campaigns, help desks, etc.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, DRC engages with entire camp populations through its CCCM activities. Messages on humanitarian service provision and designed to be tailored to specific audiences, both in terms of their content and distributions channels. DRC works closely with all other sectors to ensure that community messaging in camps and camp like settings is a coordinated and needs based as possible and that it reaches all members of a camp population. DRC will engage with the Communication and Community Engagement (CCE) working group at Juba level to continue to strengthen and coordinate messaging.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, DRC has put in place global organisational policies and procedures to safeguard against harmful behaviour; DRC is in full agreement with the UN zero tolerance policy relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. Violations constitute acts of serious misconduct and are grounds for disciplinary measures up to and including dismissal, as described in sections 3.7 and 3.9 of DRC’s Code of Conduct (CoC). DRC implements awareness raising in project sites on PSEA, provides training to staff on PSEA and CoC, and has complaints and feedback mechanisms in place in all operational areas. Moreover, DRC also commits to organisational diversity and gender equity through adherence to the DRC Age, Gender and Diversity (AGD) Vision and Standards policy.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

Yes, DRC has proactively engaged with community leaders, but has also engaged more widely with camp populations, including through women’s groups, disabled persons groups, youth committees and ‘persons with specific needs’ (PSN) committees. Beyond this, DRC has conducted representative camp satisfaction surveys to gather the views of all members of a community as well. DRC also regularly engages in focus group discussions and day to day consultations with non-leader individuals to gather views of the population in camps to inform programming.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

Yes, This project is designed to implement coordinated feedback and complaints mechanisms in camps and camp like settings, including the establish of Camp Management Desks for registering feedback and providing information on humanitarian services. In all project sites, DRC operates complaints desks to enable residents to flag concerns or complaints in person or anonymously, improving participation and accountability for all affected populations. DRC also has a feedback hotline number that beneficiaries can call to register complaints or requests.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, In static sites, DRC will roll out a quarterly participatory satisfaction survey on camp management and services to inform programming decisions and coordination. DRC will also engage in regular ongoing dialogue with community leaders and households to monitor its programmes. In mobile locations, rapid participatory assessments will be carried out to inform programming. DRC will jointly conduct a Real Time Evaluation (RTE) of it’s mobile response approach with ACTED in Q1-2 of 2019 which will include participatory engagement with beneficiaries where access allows.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, DRC is a major protection actor and conducts regular protection monitoring and protection analysis. In Malakal PoC, DRC conducts daily protection monitoring and outreach activities and shares relevant information with the CCCM team to inform camp management activities. In mobile locations, DRC has been conducting rapid protection assessments in areas of new displacement and in collective centres and urban settings. Recently this has included protection assessments in Yirol East, Fashoda County, Malakal County, Guit County, Koch County, Rubkona County and in a wide range of new displacement sites across South Sudan.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, DRC’s camp and site management projects are intended to specifically address barriers that different groups face. Infrstructure upgrades are designed to be as accessible as possible to persons with disabilities, the differing needs of men, women, boys and girls are taken into account in service coordination, surveys and site monitoring are age, gender and diversity sensitive and rapid assessments specifically addres barriers to access.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Yes, DRC mainstreams a Do No Harm approach into its CCCM approaches. For static camp management, DRC advocates to camp administration agencies on protection issues raised by partners and camp residents and moinotrs the situation in the camp for threats to individuals or their rights. IN mobile approaches, decisions to intervene and the modality for intervention is specifically designed with a do-no-harm approach in mind, and based on a standardised trigger criteria developed by the cluster in collaboration with partners.

Does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure access of beneficiaries?

Yes, the project measures access of beneficiaries by conducting regular protection mainstreaming FGDs to ensure and verify beneficiaries have access to services. DRC also monitors this through its M&E assessments and evaluations, such as post distribution monitoring (PDM), satisfaction surveys, etc.

Does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes

How does the project monitoring measure safety of beneficiaries?

Yes the project measures safety of beneficiaries by ensuring safety questions are mainstreamed into FGDs and PDMs. DRC also ensure safety of beneficiaries by monitoring the protection environment, having regular discussions and meetings with the community based protection networks, talking to women, to children, to men, and to community leaders. When safety concerns are identified, DRC raises these issues with specific agencies who can respond adequately (safety providers or authorities). Additionally, Cluster partners have collaboratively developed a CCCM beneficiary satisfaction survey tool.

Does the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes

How the project monitoring measure dignity of beneficiaries?

Yes, the project measures dignity of beneficiaries by ensuring continuous community engagement and feedback and ensuring safety and dignity of beneficiaries are prioritised throughout the intervention. Through its activities, DRC will ensure confidentiality of sensitive and personal information, while also ensuring data protection mechanisms are in place. Informed consent and people centred-approaches are sought throughout the activities, this is measured through information management tools, consent forms, and information sharing protocols.
