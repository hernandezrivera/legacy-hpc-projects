Project Summary

GOALPrime intends to target a total of 9353 individuals: out of which 9000 (60% girls and 40% boys)
are children including adolescent girls and boys and those living with disabilities in schools, camps, and
host communities. The adult target will be 353 (200 women and 153 men) who will be caregivers to be
reached with counseling and positive parenting sessions. A target of 5% of the overall individuals to be
reached will be persons living with disability.
The entire 5600 children to be reached will have access to continuous community-based MHPSS. Also,
out of the total children target 400 (0.7%) adolescent girls and boys will be reached with life skills
education, 200 girls and boys (3.57%) will be provided with case management support; 50children
(0,89%) formerly associated with the non-state armed groups (CAFAAGs) will be supported with
community-based socioeconomic reintegration (vocational skills and micro-enterprise fundamentals); 10
UASCs (0.18%) will be supported with FTR and reunified with their families in collaboration with the
social workers of the ministry of women affairs and social development; and 30 children (0.54%) with
high protection risk or with failed FTR will be placed in appropriate alternative care arrangements.
In schools, the children will be reached through structured recreational activities and creative events
such as Interclub and House competitions, child protection awareness, and peer support sessions
organized in school clubs and other club activities.
Working with the school-based and community-based child protection committees (SBCPCs/CBCPCs)
and the School Based Management Committees (SBMCs), GOALPrime will continue to strengthen the
child protection mechanisms in both schools and the communities in the target LGAs (Mafa and
Monguno, Damboa and Kala-Balge LGAs). GOALPrime will continue to work with all stakeholders in the
camps, host communities, and schools to enhance awareness on safeguarding and Sexual Exploitation
and Abuse (SEA) and also with the CP and GBV sub-sectors and Partners in Borno; as well as the case
management task force to develop or update and strengthen the referral pathways in the target LGAs.
The overall 5800 target beneficiaries (including caregivers and people living with disability) will be
reached with awareness raising on GBV prevention, PSEA, positive parenting, and child protection
concern risk communication using tools developed by consultants in the GPON Humanity Studio via
door-to-door, megaphones, motorized campaigns, and edutainment approaches. Using the community-
based PSS as an entry point, GOALPrime will work with the CBCPCs to identify and profile Out of
School Children (OOSCs) who in collaboration with Education partners will be enrolled back to school.
All approaches and activities will follow the COVID-19 Prevention and Control measures as provided by
World Health Organization (WHO), State Ministry of Health (SMoH), and the Nigerian Centre for
Disease Control (NCDC). GOALPrime will also integrate child-focused risk communication topics of
COVID-19 into school club and Inter-House activity topics and themes. GOALPrime will also work with
partners of other sectors across target locations and provide them with advice on how to mainstream
protection into their interventions. GOALPrime will be sensitive to the protection of the environment and
ensure that our approaches and tools do no harm to the environment and the beneficiaries.
GOALPrime, through her NHF-supported Humanity Studio, will also support CP AoR with the designs of
the sub-sector's referral pathways, newsletters, advocacy notes, and other relevant resource materials.
GOALPrime just wrapped up the implementation of the 2021 Consortium Allocation in Kala-Balge, Mafa,
Monguno, and Damboa LGAs in August. This project will support the continuity of CP service provision
to the children as well as CP AoR coordination as part of our mandate as Northeast CP AoR co-host
organization.

1 - Needs

Emergencies such as we have in Northeast Nigeria have severe consequences on children and their families as well as affect their ability tobuild back better. According to Nigeria’s HRP 2022 https://reliefweb.int/report/nigeria/nigeria-humanitarian-response-plan-2022-February-2022, 8.4 million people (4.2 M Borno, 2.5 M in Adamawa, and 1.6M in Yobe ) including people living with disability are in need ofHumanitarian support.The trend of recent activities of the Non-State Armed Groups (NASG) suggests that the major Humanitarian crises in the Northeasternstates of Borno, Adamawa, and Yobe (BAY states) will persist in2022. What started as a localized insurgency more than twelve years agohas turned into a regional armed conflict with no end in sight and severely affecting millions of people, subjecting them to displacement (newor continued), impoverishment, and the threat of violence. The humanitarian crises in Northeast Nigeria remain one of the largest and mostsevere in the world today and the effects of the insurgency in 2022 are severe with 8.4 million people in need. The 2022 Humanitarian needsoverview has projected at least 2 million children in need have extremely limited or no access to core child- protection services, (of whom27% are in Adamawa, 55% in Borno, and 18% in Yobe). Boys who comprise 46% are at particular risk of forceful recruitment and abductionby NASGs while girls who constitute 54% are particularly susceptible to denial of their basic right to education, child marriage, sexual abuse,emotion and physical violence, and exploitation. A rapid assessment by GOALPrime is Mafa, Kala-Balge, Monguno, and Damboa has alsovalidated the increasing child protection needs in these LGAs of Borno.The conflict has significantly impaired the capacities of families and communities to appropriately care for children. An estimated one in 50children have been separated from their families due to the conflict and urgently require family tracing, case management, and alternativecare. UNICEF estimates that 60% of children have experienced some form of violence.Furthermore, the DTM Site and Location Assessments estimate that 442,600 children have been displaced due to conflict (HNO 2022), witha significant impact on emotional wellbeing, which highlights the urgent need for multilingual MHPSS services The impact of COVID-19 andclimate change have further deepened the sufferings of the people affected by this conflict. About 400,000 IDP children attending some formof learning in the camps and host communities were affected by the stoppage of learning activities. Although schools have been reopened,but the psychological impact of COVID-19 on school children still remains unresolved and the school child protection structures need to bestrengthened to promote the well-being of school boys and girls in the context of COVID- 19/Climate change and promote learning amongthe school children.GOALPrime Organization Nigeria through this project will promote the psychosocial wellbeing of children, adolescent boys, and girlsincluding boys and girls living with disabilities in schools, camps, and communities in target LGAs and also enhancing access tocomprehensive community-based case management for girls and boys facing protection risk including adolescents and children living withdisabilities.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

By integrating every strategy with the protection principles, GOALPrime will mainstream protection (GBV & PSEA) throughout the executionof this project and advance meaningful access, safety, and dignity of both beneficiaries and of employers and volunteers. Safety, decency, and avoiding any kind of harm to both beneficiaries and employees are priorities for GOALPrime. Additionally, GOALPrime will also make sure that the target population has equitable access to the service, free from impediments and prejudice, in accordance with their needs.GOALPrime will pay attention to people and groups who could be especially vulnerable or have a hard time obtaining help and resources,including those with disabilities and GBV survivors who have been identified. GOALPrime will integrate GBV Risk Mitigation efforts to reduceor decrease GBV risk to beneficiaries.GOALPrime will work consciously in compliance with the Sphere Humanitarian Core Standards and Imperatives. All approaches will alignwith the principles of protection as we remain committed to ensuring that this project does not expose beneficiaries to more risk incompliance with the DO NO HARM Protection Principle.Also, GOALPrime will be intentional about maintaining an environment where there is zero Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (SEA). All staff,volunteers, contractors, and vendors engaged directly or indirectly to implement this project will be given PSEA orientation. They will befamiliarized with GOALPrime's policy on PSEA, and they MUST express their unwavering commitment by signing GOALPrime's ZeroTolerance to SEA Code of Conduct. Also, GOALPrime will also lay down reporting protocols for the reporting of PSEA cases if unfortunately,they occur. GOALPrime will also ensure that the identity of the reporter(s) is kept very confidential. When convenient,GOALPrime will encourage that reports are made on anonymous grounds.Also, being guided by the Inter-Agency mechanism for reporting SEA, GOALPrime will ensure a systematized engagement with affectedpopulations including confidential complaints and investigations procedures at both the system and individual agency levels. Asrecommended by IASC Task Force on PSEA which identified inter-agency cooperation in the creation and maintenance of community-basedcomplaints mechanisms (CBCMs) as a key component in the prevention and response to SEA.GOALPrime will continue to raise awareness to the target population that humanitarian assistance is free and never conditioned on sexualfavors as well as to be informed on how to access the appropriate complaints mechanisms, and support to set up in the community if SEAoccurs. Furthermore, GOALPrime will ensure that established CBCMs in target locations are effective and that there is inter-agencycoordination to ensure consistent messaging and that access to the mechanism is as broad and straightforward as possible for potentialcomplainants.GOALPrime will also be intentional about the environment and safety of the communities where we implement by ensuring that ourapproaches and tools do not contribute to the degradation of the environment. In addition, GOALPrime will also integrate climate changeand provide routine sensitization of the project beneficiaries on climate change prevention.

9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

GOALPrime will be consolidating its established community-based project accountability mechanism through this project to improvecommunity stakeholders and project beneficiary participation both in the project decision-making and implementation. This project wasdesigned in consultation with the community stakeholders and children based on lessons learned from our previous implementation in thetarget LGAs.GPON uses a people-centered feedback mechanism to gather opinions, and complaints of the community members and beneficiaries on itsproject to inform decision-making for quality project implementation. However, lessons learned from the approach includes, forming an LGA-level project committee that will include all community groups' representative and stakeholders to improve participation of the affectedcommunity in the project implementation process. GPON has also made provision for anonymous feedbacks complaint/feedback boxes,SMSs, and direct calls to the Compliance and Accountability team.GPON project team will conduct an LGA inception workshop with the established community-based project committee to discuss the projectdeliverables and the community-level needs and how this project will contribute to resolving the needs. Also, the LGA project committee willwork with the project team to define project beneficiaries’ vulnerability based on the context of the LGA, agree on vulnerability criteria, andprovide a recommended approach on how the project can be implemented to better reach and serve the project targeted groups.Also, the project team will conduct quarterly review meetings with the community-based project committee at the LGA level to inform howeach quarter can consolidate with the previous quarter's achievement to improve the quality of services rendered. GPON will also use afeedback mechanism through focus group discussions to gather perceptions of the project beneficiaries and community members.
