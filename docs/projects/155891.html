Project Summary

This project is designed to address the acute education needs of conflict-affected school-aged boys and girls children in Rural Idleb and Jandairis Sub-district through support to 25 schools in 14 villages with a focus on three main objectives:
1. Scale up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth (aged 5-17 years).
2.Enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth (aged 5-17 years) within a protective environment
3.Strengthen the capacity of the education system and communities to deliver a timely, coordinated and evidence-based education response
Schools within these villages are partially damaged and not fully effective with a lack of educational materials and families can’t afford to keep their kids in schools because of the cost of books and stationery.
By designing this project, Sadad puts in mind to address the most critical needs to support the education process in the above-mentioned areas through:
- Scale up safe and equitable access to formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children and youth (aged 5-17 years) through small-scale rehabilitation of 60 classrooms with average of 140 $ for small scale rehabilitation like; repair boards, windows, doors and repairing desks and small decoration for classrooms with focus on (gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive WASH modifications. etc). Moreover, building ramps and stairs side rails for children with special needs, in addition, to provide the schools with the winterization items by providing diesel for heaters. (4000) boys and girls children, 270 teachers and educational personnel will benefit from these schools support.
- Providing 4000 school-aged children (2000 boys and 2000 girls /aged 5 - 17) with formal education
- catchup and literacy classes for 502 out of school boys and girls children will be conducted in 2 cycles (one in June and July 2019 and one in Aug and Sep 2019) each cycle is two-months long.
- Providing boys and girls children (in-school or out of school children who are at risk of dropping out/falling behind in their education or have poor learning outcomes) with remedial classes; throughout the academic year, (four classes a week in each school).
These classes will be provided with the allocated subjects of the modified Syrian curriculum for every stage.
-Providing 4000 boys and girls school-aged children with learning materials (stationary, pens, math tools, textbooks, etc), besides to provide school bags to children
- Empowering the educational staff by providing teachers and education personnel with training in the following subjects: EiE, PSS, INEE and Child-Cantered and Protective pedagogy (Effective Teaching & Learning). The trainees will have a pre- and post-tests and evaluation for their understanding of the given training. The training will be conducted by specialized contracted partner/ six-days training for each subject.
- provide teachers and Educational personnel (270) with monthly incentives; 245 teachers for 9-months academic year / 25 facilitator for 12 months.
- Provide 4000 boys and girls children both in formal and non-formal settings with PSS activities like Psychosocial activities (Artistic Activities, Cognitive Activities, Kinetic Activities, and Recreational Activities), and Awareness-raising session.
- conduct Learning assessment pre and post of the academic year at the 25 targeted schools using ASER tools.

Needs

Assessments throughout Syria conducted by Syrian NGOs (MSNA 2018), which Sadad was one of them, have reported many education-related problems which have forced “2.1 million, over one-third of children, out-of-school and has put a further 1.3 million children at risk of dropping out. Over 5.8 million school-aged children (including more than 100,000 Palestine refugee children) and about 121,972 education personnel are in need of education assistance inside Syria, 61 percent of whom are in acute and immediate need. Hostilities have resulted in over one in three schools being damaged, destroyed, no longer accessible or occupied for shelter and other purposes. Schools are reported as the most common building type used for IDP sites in Syria, accounting for an estimated 32 percent of collective centers. Due to a shortage of functional classrooms, classes are overcrowded and over one million children required to attend double-shift schools. An extreme shortage and underpayment of qualified teachers also limit the quality and provision of education. Children who are out of school face increased protection risks including child marriage and the worst forms of child labor” HNO 2019 Education sector. This had its bad effect on school-aged children where access to educational services have been restricted and many of them are deprived of right to learn foundational literacy and numeracy skills and are always learning without textbooks and other learning materials as families can’t afford to keep their kids in schools because of the cost of books and stationery. In addition to teachers, incentives which considered insufficient to meet their families’ basic needs and “the lack of opportunities for professional development along with the recruitment of unqualified teachers on a temporary contractual basis” is seriously impacting the provision of quality education. (Syria Crisis Education Strategic Paper; London 2016 Conference). As indicated in Overview Child Protection Needs in Syria 2018, 82% of assessed communities reported child labor where many boys and girls became breadwinners performing work beyond their mental, social or physical capacities and many of them (69% of assessed communities) were affected by child marriage the things which may deny them their basic rights, including to an education. In addition, children are increasingly being recruited or used by all parties to the conflict to participate either in direct combat or supportive roles such as messengers, cooks, spies, etc. Indeed Children have paid the heaviest price of this war, and as indicated in Sadad Assessment within the projects targeted villages (14 villages in southern and Western Idleb countryside and Jandairis Sub-district) school-aged girls and boys children (including about 3530 boys and 3834 girls school-aged / of them about 502 out of school children) are really in urgent need with lack of education and child protection services as their villages considered marginalized, underserviced, and not reached before by such related services beside to 270 teachers (193 males and 77 females) are in need to be support with incentives to meet their family’s basic needs. Therefore these targeted villages reported many child protection risks and the absence of access to safe equitable educational services which badly affect the well-being of those boys and girls children threatening a whole generation of being lost with the absence of parents awareness of child rights.

Implementation of activities

The project will start by introducing Local Authorities to the project’s activities and signing MOUs with them. And will start with the following:- Small-scale rehabilitation and improvement process for 60 damaged classrooms with an average of 140 $ for small scale rehabilitation like; repair boards, windows, doors and repairing desks and small decoration for classrooms, toilets, playground -Selecting 25 education facilitators to start enrolling students for formal and non-formal activities, prepare students records.- Conducting Thematic Group Discussions (TGDs) targeting about 2000 parents (1200 men, 800 women) on the following subjects: The Right to Education, Child Labor & Recruitment and Early Marriages as well as Back to School campaigns.- Selecting the project staff, contracting with 270 teachers which will be supported through signing MoUs with the Educational Directorates to identify the teachers who are not be paid by other NGOs, and provide them with the selection criteria for choose teachers and support them with monthly incentives for 9 months based on the average of wages in the targeted areas.- Conducting two rounds of the training sessions, focusing on training teachers and education personnel on Education in Emergencies (EiE) and national/INEE (Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies) Minimum Standards (MS), the first round will take place in June and the second one in August. Pre and post assessment will be conducted during each course session implementation to measure the improvement in teacher’s knowledge and change in behaviors. - Fuel suppliers will be contracted as well in order to provide the fuel to the schools on a regular basis for four months until the end of winter.- The formal education will be provided for 4000 boys and girls students with the allocated subjects of the curriculum (Use the modified Syrian curriculum approved by the Interim Government) for every stage during the academic year (9 months) focusing on registering students data like; name, age, grade, and sex. -Delivery of non-formal education; catch up classes in the summertime and remedial classes during the academic school year; three days a week, one class after the end of formal school hours in each of the 25 schools. -PSS activities (in addition to awareness rising activities for student’s parents) will be provided through scheduling daily visits to the 25 targeted schools during the academic school year. Pointing out complicated cases and referring them to a specialized psycho-social expert after coordination with CP sub-cluster and the organizations working in the project’s areas such as Space of Hope, Women Now, and USSOM agencies to best meet the needs, deliver the service, cover the gaps.M&E tools of the project will be 1. Registration form, the field monitor will record basic information about schools’ students. 2. Semester Surveys, at the end of each semester, the field monitor is going to use a survey to get beneficiaries to feedback on the support. 3. Attendance sheet, the teachers’ classes will collect the beneficiaries’ attendance.4. Receipt form, for any kind of support (teachers’ salaries, equipment or fuel. etc.) 5. Teacher registration form, at the beginning of the school semester, the teacher will fill a small form contains basic teacher information.Feedback mechanism: Sadad has a solid feedback system, where the beneficiaries have access to the management team and share their feedback or opinion to improve the project quality or add any activities. Complaint Mechanism: Project locations will include complaint boxes, the messages will be collected on a weekly basis and will be sent to the project manager via email, field project coordinator and M&E officers are based in the project area. WhatsApp number will be provided to receive complaints and feedback from beneficiaries. All the complaints will be handled within 72 hours max.

Protection Risk Analysis

1. Protection risk: the difficulty in securing textbooks. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigating measures: 1.1 use books with and without logos depending on the location served. 1.2 Use books from previous years or old copies of curriculum. 2. Protection risk: some locations not accepting textbooks with MoE logos. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 2.1 use books with and without logos depending on the location served.3. Protection risk: the rising prices of materials and unavailability of fuel for heating in the targeted area due to the current and changing situations in Syria or the crossing border issues. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 3.1 Sadad will sign another contract with other suppliers to ensure the continuity of project and will inform the donor about all changes in the project implementation plans. 3.2 Sadad will be able to find other suppliers and to adapt the budget and spending according to any price fluctuations. 3.3 Sadad can also agree with suppliers on a fixed price for the duration of the action/project. 3.4 prior to start of implementation, a market assessment will be conducted in order to see what is available and what is not, and establishing contingency plans for materials that can be replaced with alternative resources, including determining purchase inside or outside Syria.4. Protection risk: children being out of school due to unavailability of school furniture upon rehabilitation. Likelihood: Low. Impact: Medium. Mitigating measures: 4.1 comprehensive needs assessment of schools to ensure that where furniture is unavailable, Sadad will procure and install the needed items.5. Protection risk: Insufficient safety and health standards at schools. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigating measures: 5.1 security/ safety training for organization, students and school staff. 5.2 allocate four safety and security focal points on the project locations.6. Protection risk: Child safety incidents in school due to bullying and overburdened teachers. Likelihood: medium. Impact medium. Mitigating measures: 6.1 Training of school’s staff and local stakeholders on Child safety, development of school specific child safety policies; establishment of complaint mechanisms in school.Protection risk 7: Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire. Likelihood: High. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 7.1 Duty of Care policies are in place, and the staff are informed on the responsibilities as well as limitations of the organization, where all staff, teachers and personnel in schools will sign a Duty of Care policy, code of conduct and Child Protection SOPs. 7.2 Security/safety training for organization, students and school staff, which will be conducted through Sadad safety and security staff members. 7.3 Communication with donors to accept flexibility and support relocation of activities to safer spaces whenever needed in coordination with Education cluster coordinators. 7.4 Alternative locations (community centers, homes, etc…) and methodologies of education should be considered to avoid attack-related risks. Protection risk 8: Interference of armed groups, including confiscation of sensitive materials (laptops, phones). Likelihood: Medium. Impact: Medium. Mitigating measures: 8.1 In order to ensure that armed groups do not get involved in programming, Sadad will engage with relevant stakeholders and make sure that the local councils and local authorities are aware of the projects and have provided adequate access and space to conduct activities.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. Changes in the security situation, particularly in project target areas, affects the safety/mobility/ displacement of beneficiaries/staff and results in being unable to participate in project. This includes the risk of targeted attacks and bombing of schools/ communities and the risks of mines, airstrikes and cross fire. Likelihood: Medium. Impact: High. Mitigating measures: 1.1 Ensure relevant authorities/groups are aware of, and technical authorities engaged with, the project and commit to ensuring basic safety/access for its implementation. 1.2 Ensure that staff members, teachers and education personnel are trained in safety and security, including mine risk education. 1.3 During the rehabilitation phase, ensure that windows are being replaced with plastic sheets instead of glass, and all safety tools are in place, and rehabilitate the basement “if any” to be used as temporary shelter. 1.4 Suspension of field activities and constant monitoring of the situation until security allows resumption of field activities. 1.5 constant coordination and monitoring of the security context with other stakeholders; 1.6 strictly adherence to Sadad security plans and risk assessments, and if sudden evacuation/movement of affected population in target areas, Sadad will review and assess conditions and possible change in location to be requested to the donor. 1.7 alternative locations (community centers, homes, etc…) and methodologies of education should be considered to avoid attack-related risks.
