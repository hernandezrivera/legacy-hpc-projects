Project Summary

The Food Security Sector coordination mechanism in Libya – composed of a sector coordinator and a dedicated Information Management Officer – is committed to saving lives and improving livelihoods in Libya through the coordination of food security responses for an enhanced food security situation in the country. This is done through
• Cooperation and partnerships: through working directly with its partners and stakeholders that include NGOs, INGOs, the Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement, UN organizations, university and research institutions, government institutions, local committees and Donors.
• Inter-sectoral and humanitarian coordination: through active participation in inter-sectoral fora contributing to and making use of multi-sector needs assessments and supporting joint analyses in order to identify gaps and needs and to ensure coordinated early action and rapid response.
• Communication of food security information: through the dissemination of timely information management products in order to ensure that all partners can make informed decisions based on reliable and timely evidence
• Strengthening the performance of the sector and its partners: through steering the coordination of food security responses in terms of geographic reach, vulnerability targeting of the different population groups, response type, and delivery modalities, while also promoting the synergy of food assistance with agriculture, livelihoods and resilience interventions as well as with other sectors such as Education, Protection, Cash & Markets and Nutrition. This will ensure no overlaps are made so that more vulnerable people are reached, and resources are well placed.
• Improve monitoring and evaluation systems to enhance the quality and appropriateness of food security responses and accountability to affected populations. The sector continuously monitors and evaluates its responses against the overall food security response plan, while at the same time partners evaluate the sector’s performance during the year. In terms of accountability, the sector seeks to enhance the evaluation of the feedback from the available AAP systems, such as the inter-agency Common Feedback Mechanism and M&E systems of its partners, to ensure the engagement of the crisis-affected populations and communities in the whole humanitarian programme cycle and. The sector moreover seeks to ensure that protection principles are mainstreamed in all activities.

1 - Needs

In 2020, the food security situation in Libya has worsened, leading to a doubling of the number of people in need of assistance from the previous year. There is more need now have the necessary tools to understand better the food security situation and respond to the needs of the affected populations. Enabling proper service delivery necessitates strategic coordination among partners and stakeholders along with reliable, timely and evidence-based information for decision-making.Considering the increased needs of the affected populations, compounded by the complexity of the Libyan situation with the multiple crises, there is an unquestionable need to strengthen the capacity of and provide enough resources to the Food Security Sector to allow it to fulfil its core functions, including coordination, information management, assessments, gap analyses and capacity building.

2 - Activities

The proposal aims to provide adequate resources to the Food Security Sector mechanism to enable it to fulfil its core functions and role at local and global levels. A sector coordinator and a dedicated information management officer are crucial to deliver support in a timely and effective manner and enable the implementation of the sector’s short-, medium- and longer-term activities in a coordinated way based on sectoral strategies and objectives.Main activities include, among others: • Organize regular coordination meetings with FSS partners and establish technical working groups; • Participate in inter-sectoral meetings, including ISCG, IMWG, AWG, ACGs, etc;• Support and provide timely inputs to the HPC process;• Coordinate planning of emergency responses to avoid overlaps and reach more people in need;• Monitor performance against sectoral indicators;• Engage in multi-sectoral and coordinated assessments; • Collect, analyse, and share food security related information (needs, gaps, achievements) that is important for the sector stakeholders to make informed, evidence based and strategic decisions, through reports, databases, maps and dashboards; • Provide capacity building (trainings and workshops) for FSS partners on IM (e.g. on monitoring / reporting formats, ActivityInfo, project submission, excel use);• Attend relevant trainings and workshops with the global Food Security Cluster to enhance technical capacity and knowledge base;• Enhance the evaluation of the feedback from the available AAP systems to improve programme quality and appropriateness.
