Project Summary

Of a total population of over 4 million people in north-west Syria, it is estimated that around 2.8 million IDPs are considered as people in need of humanitarian assistance. New and protracted IDPs share the same urgent needs, in an area that has limited shelter options and where the fighting has caused the destruction of civilian infrastructure, including health facilities, schools, and bakeries, and has disrupted the provision of basic services. IDA as an organization that is working in the health sector and the protection sector is considered integrated into the health sector IDA will provide integrated protection services which include specialized Child Protection (CP) case management and referral services, which will be focused on the vulnerability cases and those who are survived from the Gender-based violence- and will be for all the categories who are suffering from the violence and its type.
Children are still exposed to multiple protection risks severe abuses and grave rights violations, Child labor, including in its most hazardous forms such as recruitment and use of children in combat and support roles, violence, abuse, neglect, and exploitation, including GBV, early marriage, early pregnancy, the psychosocial needs of many girls and boys are left unmet.
Those children, according to previous reports, will suffer from a lot of consequences. Mostly, they will show post-trauma symptoms, these symptoms may include one or more of these: irritable, angry, or aggressive behavior, including extreme temper tantrums, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response, problems with concentration, and difficulty sleeping.
This program aims to build the resilience and affected by the war and earthquake by providing a holistic approach to mental health and psychological support interventions and prevention, for people who are suffering from psychological disorders because they are permanently displaced through:
- Create 2 child friendly spaces.
Individual sessions for children at risk by case management services in addition to safe referrals that target Women, men, girls, and boys, including PWDs.
- Parenting skills for children’s caregivers, with the aim of Providing them with the knowledge and skills to manage their feelings and understand the feelings and needs of their children.
- Mental health support group sessions using TRT methodology for children and PM+ for caregivers.
Group work will provide space for beneficiaries with similar issues, problems, or circumstances to come together for mutual support.
- Outreach services: will spread information on the importance of mental health besides protection risks, as well as assist in reaching the most vulnerable in communities. Awareness-raising activities aim to decrease the stigma around accessing services by encouraging people to be more aware of the protection risks faced. Activities will be accessible by all, efforts committed to creating an accessible environment for PWD and elderlies and balanced between the staff gender for better inclusion the communities.
All activities will be based upon the principles of the IASC Guidelines on MHPSS in Emergency Settings, Minimum Standards for Child Protection, and Guidelines for Integrating Gender-Based Violence Interventions in Humanitarian Action.
Furthermore, the IDA provides integrated protection and child protection activities in the supported health facitlities. These activities inlude case management, MHPSS, community awareness, and phychosocial support within the health facilities. This approach of having health facility as an entry point for protection activities proved to be very effective in reaching people in need, especially with having the healthcare providers aware about protection issues and have the capacity to refer the cases successfully. Such activities are provided within IDA health facilities in Bab Al Salama Hospital, in addition to, Rayyan, Kelly, Dana, Azaz, and Ghandourah PHCs.
