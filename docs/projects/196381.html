Project Summary

In response to the demonstrated nutrition needs on the ground, Human Appeal Turkey (HAT) is planning to operate 16 nutrition rapid response teams (RRTs) and 5 outpatient therapeutic program (OTP) centers across North West Syria (NWS) targeting children under 5 (CU5) and pregnant and lactating women (PLWs) among others of the most vulnerable groups with essential life-saving curative and preventative nutrition services as follows:
The project will be implemented in 20 communities across 2 sub-districts (Dana, and Maaret Tamisrin) to provide nutrition services for 142,486 beneficiaries including (48,610 Pregnant and lactating women and 77,761 children under five years) (118,084 IDPs and 24,403 HCs including 21,373 PWDs). The total project beneficiaries will be 43,782 males, and 98,704 females, including 77,617 CU5 (39,929 boys, and 37,688 girls) (Note: the percentage of boys and girls was estimated based on nutrition HNO 2023 data and HAT database from 2021-2022 RRTs records).
However all these beneficiaries will receive 4 services on average during one year, as the RRTs and the staff in the OTPs will conduct malnutrition screening every 2-3 months on average, and each RRTs will reach out to 77 individuals per working day.
HAT will promote the ultimate utilization of the pre-existing resources and expertise relevant to the intervention including the use of the trained staff in the organization and the usage of the assembly points (Atma and Kelly Center), warehouses, OTPs (Mashhad Ruhin PHCC, Mariam Center, and Iman Hospital) as well as the relevant technical expertise and measures of access and coordination at the field level. The proposed RRTs and the nutrition worker in OTPs are planned to function according to the TORs and SOPs adopted by the nutrition cluster, and they will be providing the following nutrition services:
• Screen 77,617 children 6-59 months, and 48,610 PLWs, referral and follow-up who are malnourished.
• Provide emergency IYCF counselling for 7,799 caregivers including PLWs.
• Disseminate messages on IYCF-E recommended feeding practices for 39,105 caregivers including PLWs.
• Screen, and refer CU5 and PLWs with dangerous signs and Medical complications.
• Support hygiene promotion and rising WASH awareness by providing Key messages to prevent the risk of Cholera and COVID-19 transmission and other infections.
• Identify visual signs of abuse/violence against child U5/PLWs during Nutrition screening and refer them to the PSS center.
• Train 5,406 caregivers on the family MUAC approach.
• Capacity building of 104 frontline nutrition services providers on IYCF-E and CMAM programs in the context of COVID-19 and providing continuous technical support for human appeal teams to build their capacity and strengthen their experience in nutrition fieldwork.
• Distribute micronutrient supplementations for preventing anemia and malnutrition among 14,322 pregnant, lactating women (PLWs)
• Enroll 14,157 boys and girls (6-23 months) in Blanket Supplementary Feeding Programme.
• Distribute CVA linked to a nutrition objective to 200 case to improve the access to nutrition services.
The project team will ensure effective coordination with the nutrition cluster and the partners through the submission of monthly 4Ws, supplies assessment, surveys, and services mapping. In addition to sharing HAT RRTs work locations, and updates with the Cluster partners to avoid any duplication in the services or overlapping.
Effective monitoring of the project's activities, outputs, and outcomes will be done where HAT MEAL team will regularly conduct beneficiaries' interviews, satisfaction surveys, monitoring visits, FGDs, reflection sessions, sensitization on the complaints mechanism, and PSEA hotline.

3 - Needs

All of the factors that contribute to malnutrition are on the rise, so it is likely that trends in the coming months will get worse. Acute food insecurity affects 13 million Syrians, or more than half the country's population, according to estimates from the food security sector (HRP 2023). This could exacerbate acute malnutrition and anemia among children and women who are already at risk.As per nutrition sector severity report in HNO 2023, Maaret Tamsrin (99,914 PIN) and Dana (337,200 PIN) sub-districts are classified among the areas of most people in need with severity scores of 4 and 5 consistently.The rising food insecurity will impact the nutritional status of the PLWs who consume a poor diet, where the anemia rate almost in these two sub-districts reached 53% among the PLWs. According to community surveillance (Oct-22), the PLW MAM trend reached 14.1 in Idleb district (which is the second higher percentage in Idleb governorate) while it is 7.7 in Harim district. Moreover, the GAM trend within CU5 in Idleb and Harim districts reached 2.0 and 1.3 consistently and 68,125 Children 6-59 in Dana and 18,635 in Maaret Tamasrin are suffering from anemia as well.Furthermore, 8,364 in Dana plus 2,288 SAM cases in Maaret Tamsrin require SAM management support within the 2 sub-districts which are full of IDPs.Furthermore, cholera outbreak is likely to put additional strain on the fragile healthcare system constraining access to already limited nutrition and healthcare services.

4 - Implementation of activities

HAT will undertake the required preparations that can ensure the needed supply chains and human resources are in place and maintained throughout 2023. For this purpose, HAT will establish technical, and operational support for the 16 RRTs and 5 OTPs. In 2022, HAT reached Nutriset and now HA is coordinating to start the procurement process using the private fund for MAM treatment.HAT will conduct capacity building of 104 services providers on IYCF-E and CMAM programs, and conduct GBV and PSEA training for project staff.After that, the staff will start doing the activities as follows:*Screen PLW on infant feeding difficulties and provide counseling on WHO-recommended IYCF-E practices, and provide essential WASH messages.*Provide micronutrient supplementation to PLW, CU5, and adolescent girls to prevent anemia.*Improve access to nutrition services through delivering CVA support linked to a nutrition objective.*Scale-up blanket supplementary feeding program to prevent Acute Malnutrition.*Strengthen early identification and referral pathways by screening the PLWs and CU5 for malnutrition and following up on the malnutrition cases.Also, the staff will train the caregivers on MUAC screening to scale up the FMA.MEAL team will conduct monitoring visits and satisfaction surveys with the beneficiaries and prepare reports.HAT will ensure close coordination with the nutrition cluster, this includes the 4Ws reporting and participation in the technical groups, surveys and etc..

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Protection Risk: The staff not following up IPC measures may increase the risk of transmission COVID-19 and CholeraLikelihood: mediumImpact: HighMitigation measurement: HAT will conduct training on IPC measurement and will increase the awareness of the staff on COVID-19 and Cholera. Also, HAT will ensure using PPE during the activities. Moreover, the project team will coordinate with Syria Immunization Group to conduct awareness sessions and provide the staff with the available vaccines.HAT will decrease the number of beneficiaries in group activities and keep close coordination with cluster to evaluate the situation and suspend the activities if needed. Protection Risk: Women and girls may face the risk of harassment during receiving the servicesLikelihood: lowImpact: HighMitigation measurement: Do No Harm, PSEA, and child safeguarding will all be covered in staff training; also all staff will agree to abide by a code of conduct and female staff will be in all RRTs and in the OTPs. Set up communication and complaints mechanisms that are clearly marked. Protection Risk: Traditional habits prevent women from leaving homes to get servicesLikelihood: mediumImpact: HighMitigation measurement: HAT will bring the services to communities and camps through RRTs, to include all people in need focusing on children, women and adolescent girls, especially those who may have physical limitations and/or are socially isolated and may not be able to access basic services.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

This project reflects the different needs of women, men and children throughout the project cycle (design, implementation and monitoring). The nutrition care needs of women, men, girls, boys, and disabled are well analyzed. More than 77% of project staff will be female staff to address the needs of PLWs in the field. Project team will identify the facilities which have specialized services for GBV survivors. So the team will refer any case to the specialized facilities.For protection from PSEA the following mitigation measures will be used:HAT will conduct PSEA training for all staff to raise their awareness and familiarize them with SEA reporting mechanism stressing the importance of reporting any SEA misconduct. HAT will print IEC materials and teams will show them in the operation areas. Also, the staff will raise the beneficiaries' awareness about the PSEA reporting channels and provide information to communities and targeted beneficiaries about HAT, principles it adheres to, how it expects its staff to behave, the project it is implementing, and what it intends to deliver.HAT will ensure the emplacement of effective complaint and feedback mechanisms, in addition to effective recruitment and performance management of staff. Do no harm will be further mainstreamed as follows: The targeted locations were identified upon the needs and gap analysis. Also, all beneficiaries host and IDPs including men, women, boys, and girls will be able to access quality free services.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Risk1: Inadequate acceptance of the intervention within the targeted camps, communities and houses.Likelihood: medium.Impact: High.Mitigation Measures: Special consideration will be paid to contract staff members from within the same communities targeted with the intervention, and ensuring high level of coordination with the local authorities to ensure proper facilitation of the teams’ mobility within and among the planned locations. Risk 2: Conflict escalation and deterioration in securityLikelihood: Medium.Impact: High.Mitigation Measures: Assessing the security situation are up to date with any road closures or changes in road conditionsIf the security situation increasingly deteriorated, we can wait or postpone activities for a few days or go to change targeted areas to another accessible area in need of support, Any major change will be reported and conducted in coordination with the donor and nutrition cluster. Risk 3: Increase influxes of IDPs due to security-related incidents, which may result in increased rates of Cholera and COVID-19 transmission due to overcrowding within the targeted areas.Likelihood: Medium.Impact high.Mitigation Measures: HAT will train the staff on IPC practices and the staff will apply a triage system that enables categorization of the beneficiaries according to their symptoms to limit the risk of transmission of cholera and coronavirus, especially for the most vulnerable groups including children and women with malnutrition.
