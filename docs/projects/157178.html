Project Summary

To protect and improve food security of highly vulnerable, conflict affected populations through an integrated programme of assistance: repeat life-sustaining food assistance (monthly assistance to the most vulnerable households and in-kind support to nine bakeries to maintain availability of bread at an economically accessible price); emergency life-saving food security assistance; and, a complementary package of livelihoods assistance to increase household resilience, improve agricultural production and increase the ability of targeted households to meet food needs.
Please note that for all activities implemented in partnership with a Lead Partner INGO (''Lead Partner"), that Lead Partner will input into the HPC separately. This submission only covers our organization.

Needs

The Syrian conflict continues to result in unprecedented levels of humanitarian need, especially in terms of food access. The 2019 Whole of Syria Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) estimates that and estimated 9 million people are in need of FSL assistance, of which 6.5million are food insecure and 2.5 million are at risk of food insecurity. The primary drivers of food insecurity remain the protracted and volatile nature of the conflict, corresponding levels of displacement and disruption to livelihood activities, as well as high market prices for food due to increased inputs and fuel prices.For this activity, we are targeting vulnerable, rural areas that typically present high concentrations of IDPs (Idleb), large influx of IDP returnees and significant increases in commodity prices (Aleppo), and varying levels of civil insecurity (Hama). In these areas, food access is particularly limited and food availability & cost are at risk of further deterioration in 2019.Our staff completed a rapid assessment in March 2018 in targeted areas. Results show severe disruption to income generation activities amongst both displaced and host families, high levels of food insecurity (80% of HHs did not have sufficient food rations in the 7 days prior to the assessment), and considerable reliance on negative coping strategies to meet food needs (100% of resp.). Areas of displacement are in general more food insecure than other areas because of increased pressure on already scarce resources. While the assessment shows high food insecurity overall, there is no significant difference in food insecurity between host families and newly displaced families in these areas. Based on Food Consumption Score (FCS), 66% of respondents are poor or borderline in terms of food security. After lack of income (cited by 70% of resp.), volatility of prices is the second most important challenge cited by respondents (52%) to explain their lack of access to food.According to our surveys, 60% of the population spends more than 75% of their available income on food. Respondents indicated that whenever food supplies are limited due to high prices or insufficient funds, they reduce number of meals per day and portion sizes so as to feed their whole family. These coping strategies carry a significant risk of malnutrition. The proposed activities look both to improve income generation and to prevent food insecurity. .Our assessments also shows that markets – the predominate choice for buying food - play a major role in meeting food needs in both governorates, and that bread is an important staple of the food basket. On average, individuals consume 300gr of bread per day. Bread is also typically the food item HHs turn to when other resources are unavailable. This emphasizes the importance of supporting stable bread prices, especially since input and fuel prices are increasing and particularly volatile in these areas. The bakery component of the program also offers a sustainable exit strategy once prices will have stabilized after the conflict has resolved.We also already have indications that the proposed approach is likely to succeed and have a positive impact. We have been supporting bakeries in these areas since 2015. In addition to the expertise that we have developed by supporting 9 bakeries in Atareb and Haritan sub-district and helping up to 49 villages/80,320 individuals to access reliable bread at an economically accessible price, we have evidence that the program is effective. In our areas of intervention, food security has increased and 65% of beneficiaries now have acceptable FCS. Yet, considering the current volatility of prices and the new population displacements expected in 2019, it is more important than ever to sustain the assistance provided and to maintain availability of bread at an economically accessible price for highly vulnerable populations.

Implementation of activities

From Jan - March, we will maintain FFP-funded repeat, monthly food assistance to approx. 9,400 households in Ariha, Madiq Castle and Atareb sub-districts (total 25,000 food kits per month) and provide in-kind support (flour and yeast) to 9 bakeries in Atareb and Haritan sub-districts, Aleppo to maintain bread availability and support economic access to bread for a catchment population of approx. 80,000 beneficiaries. The current SUSTAIN action closes 31 March with follow-on funding expected to be secured from FFP from 01 April 2019, under which we plan to reach 4,500 households each month with repeat, monthly food kits in Ariha, Kafr Nobol and northern Aleppo. Our FFP-funded food assistance caseload will decrease from approx 9,400 households to 4,500 households from 01 April, resulting in a critical gap in sub-districts currently served. Alternative funding is sought to maintain, repeat food assistance to approximately 5,000 households. Modality will be determined based on market access for targeted groups and market functionality. Complementary funding is also be sought from April to maintain in-kind (flour and yeast) assistance to the nine bakeries in Atareb and Haritan sub-districts currently supported under the FFP-funded SUSTAIN which serve approx. 80,000 people. Repeat food assistance to the most vulnerable will be complemented by one-off life-saving ECHO-funded assistance delivered in partnership with the Lead Partner INGO ("Lead Partner") through the NSRF. This emergency response mechanism provides rapid, targeted assistance to populations identified by sudden shocks across a wide geographic area. During 2019, we will deliver life-saving food assistance to 4,500 households (2,000 hhs with food kits and 2,500 hhs with RTEs) under the NSRF. This assistance is fully funded up to 30 June under an ECHO-funded grant, implemented by our in partnership with Lead Partner. A follow-on action has been proposed to ECHO (starting 01 July) under which we will deliver life-saving food assistance to 1,750 households affected by a sudden shock, including conflict-related displacement. From 01 Jan - 30 April 2019, we will continue rehabilitation of the Al Roj Irrigation system under a THF funded project. This action protects food security and livelihoods through the rehabilitation of agricultural infrastructure and creation of CfW income generation opportunities in Idleb. Building on this action, we are seeking funding for the rehabilitation of critical irrigation infrastructure in Jandairis sub-district of Afrin district. Rehabilitation will target 24 kilometres of irrigation channels from Burj Abddullah village to Mahmudiyah village and serve approx. 10,000 households in 13 villages. The targeted irrigation system was partially-functional during the 2018 season, however essential repairs are needed to pumps, valves and gates. All channels require clearing and leveling to ensure optimum system efficiency. Rehabilitation works are planned from Sept. to ensure full system functionality by the 2020 season and will be completed through CfW providing essential income generation opportunities to 200 households. Funding is also sought for a complementary package of livelihoods assistance designed to shore-up household resilience, improve agricultural production and increase the ability of targeted households to meet food needs. This will included two targeted distributions of conditional agricultural vouchers (summer and winter planting seasons) to 600 households in Mhambal and Ariha sub-districts of Idleb. This is intended as an entry-point to a watershed approach and will support food production in the area, increase dietary diversity and protect agricultural livelihoods.

Protection Risk Analysis

The project will follow cross-cutting protection mainstreaming guidelines as per the protection cluster guidelines, including collection of sex and age disaggregation of information, participation, access, and an understanding of how different needs will be met. To reduce risks or harm to vulnerable populations in the face of conflict sensitivity, beneficiaries in Syria will be systematically involved in all stages of the project cycle wherever possible – for example, needs and resource identification, design, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.Gender based violence disproportionately affects women and girls, especially those displaced or recently returned. This was highlighted in the Whole of Syria Protection Needs Overview, 2018. This project will work with female and male leaders and members of the affected community to ensure that the distributions are equitable and gender sensitive, for example having seperate lines for male and female beneficiaries at distribution points and ensuring female staff are present whenever possible. The main protection risk of the loss documents will be mitigated by using a community council registration and community verification mechanism. We design our projects with an emphasis on meaningful access, safety, dignity and non-discrimination so that all potential beneficiaries will benefit from inclusive service provision to the greatest extent possible. Moreover, by applying lessons learned from previous interventions, we will engage with local councils, relief committees and other key actors to mitigate risks faced by women and children, in particular, and to build an environment that guarantees basic human rights, though we will keep in mind the security environment within Syria and the safety of its potential beneficiaries at all times.To mitigate against protection risks, we work closely with Local Councils and other administrative bodies to ensure open and accountable feedback mechanisms for beneficiaries served. In this regard, and informed by an understanding of the preferences of men and women and their access to various communication options, we also utilize face-to-face interviews with beneficiaries and non-beneficiaries in our offices, Distribution points and fixed places not related to Local Councils. Interviews are held with our M&E member, who registers the feedback, and uploads it to a dedicated system, while at the same time M&E members follow up with the concerned party to fix the issue (if any). A second feedback method is a direct Whatsapp phone number for registering any complaints or issues. This will be held by the M&E officers in Antakya office and will provide a direct link with the field to achieve the maximum available transparency, while also allowing for a way to handle complaints (follow up with the concerned party, etc.).Wherever possible and culturally acceptable, female beneficiaries meeting the selection criteria will be prioritized for cash programs. We also require that all staff sign the organisation’s code of conduct, which includes the obligation to report abuse by staff or beneficiaries, including misuse of assets or funds. We have strict protocols against any sexual relations with minors or beneficiaries of any age. Additionally, we will ensure that assessments and beneficiary selection will assess women’s and girls’ unique needs in addition to other vulnerable groups. Inclusion of vulnerable groups within the implementation decisions will ensure that interventions are effective for the largest possible amount of affected people.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Consideration of the highly volatile situation within Syria has been built into program design. Real-time analysis of the security situation will continue to allow for rapid adjustments to planned activities as necessary. This can include temporary suspension of field activities during times of extreme conflict. During such times, our field teams employ various techniques to maintain communication and support to project stakeholders and beneficiaries. In line with well established operating procedures, distributions will be placed on temporary hold during periods of heightened risk to staff and beneficiaries. We employ multiple measures to maintain delivery assistance, which can include door-to-door distributions, or phased distribution times to reduce the risks of large numbers of people gathering. We are in the process of finalizing updated Framework Agreements with food kit and RTE suppliers. Reliability and ability to consistently deliver has been considered when assessing bids, which will mitigate against the risk of supply chain breaks delaying assistance delivery. Should supply chain breaks be experienced due to border closures or other, unanticipated events, we have the ability to immediately transition from in-kind assistance to conditional food vouchers to ensure targeted beneficiaries can maintain access to food. With reference to planned bakery support, we have a robust process of rolling verification in place to mitigate against the risk of external influence on bakeries or the bread produced. A comprehensive monitoring system will continue to verify all bread is sold at the agreed stable price. This includes price and weight verification at the bakery, in addition to at the distribution points. We are continually updating its contingency plans to adapt to the changing context in NWS, these plans detail various scenarios and required actions for mitigating risks, including operational and programmatic changes. Risks beyond our control could include: shifts in administrative powers; heightened insecurity restricting access; targeting of aid organisations or staff. We have dedicated Security staff that closely monitor and advise on the dynamic, continually evolving security situation. All field staff are well trained and incidents trackers are maintained to identify trends and flashpoints. As referenced above, we have recently commenced operations in Afrin district, Aleppo. This will allow us to respond to need in these areas should significant incoming displacement be experienced as a result of further, possible conflict in Idleb and western Aleppo. Securing this access also allows us to re-programme planned food assistance to areas in northern Aleppo should security prevent ongoing operations in Idleb, Hama or western Aleppo.
