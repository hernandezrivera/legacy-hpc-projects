Project Summary

Youth development and empowerment are vital stages in life for building the human capital that allows young people to avoid poverty and lead better, and possibly have a more fulfilling life. The human capital formed in youth is thus an important determinant of long-term growth that a nation can invest on.
International organizations and governments have invested in the youth sector over the past decades, Where it has a good impact on the political and social stability of societies. It also contributes to the end of the conflict and peace, whether as a result of external wars or internal conflicts.
In the areas where refugees and IDPs are hosted, Inside and outside Syria, the economic empowerment of youth contributes to reducing the economic burden of receiving refugees and IDPs and reducing tensions with the host community as a result of the depletion of existing resources, especially in economically vulnerable areas.
Therefore, based on the difficulty of the situation of young Syrian refugees to find jobs because of the legal conditions and policies imposed in the host country to work with any official party or the establishment of private work, as well as young people displaced within Syria under conditions of war, and the waste of the ability of young people in work in the poor jobs that consume their energy
The QRCS has developed the following proposal to support a range of youth initiatives for internally displaced persons in Syria.
Through the provision of grants that enable them to establish innovative, income-generating pilot projects to support themselves and their families. As well as providing them with the skills and experience necessary.

Needs

Despite the reduced level of hostilities and violence in many key densely populated areas of the country by the end of 2018, millions of Syrians continue to face huge challenges in accessing basic services, due to widespread infrastructure destruction, erosion of the productive base, scarce resources, and massive internal displacement. The social fabric of the country has been severely damaged. Inside Syria, some 11.7 million people are currently in urgent need of humanitarian assistance. An estimated 64-to-84 percent of the population is living below the poverty line. The erosion of living conditions has severely increased vulnerability and undermined the ability of affected people to cope with the consequences of the existing crisis as well as future shocks. The after-effects will likely continue for many years, especially for women and girls due to the entrenched complexity of the issues they continue to face. With an increasing proportion of the country now moving towards de facto post-conflict conditions, the acute life-saving and protection needs still remain the paramount focus of the HRP. The response, however, must evolve to better take into account longer-term drivers of vulnerability and capitalize on opportunities to rejuvenate social and economic dynamics, while continuing to focus on humanitarian needs. Moreover, the anticipated spontaneous returns significantly increased numbers of IDPs and refugees in 2019 would add to the socio-economic stress in the near future. Ensuring safe, dignified and voluntary return and sustainable reintegration of displaced people will depend on enhanced availability of basic and social infrastructure and services, a secure environment, and viable livelihood opportunities. All activities addressing the longer-term vulnerabilities of the affected people will be guided by humanitarian principles and would be distinct from broader reconstruction efforts which lie outside the spectrum of humanitarian action
