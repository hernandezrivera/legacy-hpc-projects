Project Summary

The project objective is to ensure the continuity and quality of education for vulnerable children who are at risk of education dropping out by supporting 12 schools, 15,600 students, and 480 education personnel, in addition to strengthening 120 families (600) members with specialized services, including remedial classes, vocational training, winter clothes, and cash for education, in 6 targeted areas within Rural Damascus. (sahnaya, Qudsya, kisweh, Markaz Darayya, Qatana , Jaramana), 66% of targeted beneficiaries are within severity 3, 17% are within severity 4, and 17% are within severity 5.
Over12 months the project aims at supporting schools with needed educational resources by providing teaching materials and enhancing educational personnel's professional development, including active learning training sessions. That will ensure the continuity and sustainability of the project's impact after its completion.
The project’s activities contribute to the education sector objectives 1 and 2, which link to the third HRP strategic goal and aim to Increase safe and equitable availability and access to in-person and home-based formal and non-formal education for crisis-affected children/youth and to enhance the quality of formal and non-formal education for children and youth.
These actions will be supported by a group of activities specially designed to increase access for children and families to quality interventions in targeted locations through case management.
Moreover, the families will be supported through the wide connection channels of SOS Children’s Villages Syria with partners. Also, they will be referred to other NGOs that provide support based on their identified needs.
Target groups will be Children, Youth including Children with disabilities, Education personnel, Parents/Households affected by economic factors, and their children at risk of education dropping out.

3 - Needs

Children are deeply affected by the Syrian crisis and aggravated by multiple rights violations. Humanitarian and economic indicators keep deteriorating, and essential services are collapsing; these factors have resulted in many children dropping out of school. Consequently, children out of school are more exposed to negative coping mechanisms and protection risks such as child labor, early or forced marriage, neglect, and abuse. Based on the 2023 HNO Report, the main reasons for children not attending schools are the inability to afford education and the need for children to work to support households. Schools and learning spaces remain unconducive to learning due to overcrowding, lack of school supplies, and teacher shortages (especially in rural schools).In Rural Damascus and based on the Key Findings 2023 HNO, 52% of households can’t afford their children to attend school, and 74% of children face obstacles to attending school because they are too tired from helping support their families meet their needs. The prioritized education improvements in Rural Damascus are for 50% improvement in teachers’ ability to teach, 19% for providing school supplies, and 38% for providing cash for school-related expenses.Regarding the above needs and findings, SOS proposes this intervention because no child must be deprived of education regardless of the causes. The intervention will target six areas in Rural Damascus under the severity scales 3, 4, and 5.

4 - Implementation of activities

SOS will start by supporting 12 schools with the equipment, stationeries and teaching materials that are required for the execution of the education activities The organization will as well enhance 480 educational personnel’s professional development, including active learning training in addition to increasing the ability of 15,600 children at risk of education dropping out to access and continue education this will begin with the development of defined criteria for selecting the most vulnerable children. All selected children will receive school supplies, 480 will receive winter clothes, remedial classes, and cash for education through a cash Transfer Company for 5 months, and 200 youths will receive vocational education training through the Vocational education institutes. The project will provide psychosocial counseling sessions to children showing signs of psychosocial distress. The project's indicators will be monitored and evaluated, through SOS monitoring and evaluation adviser as well as its alignment with the overall procedures and processes of the national SOS Children’s Villages policy. This process will include monitoring field visits, surveys, staff meetings, Family development plan to monitor the cash assistance impact, and communication channels such as complaint hotline.SOS standards monitoring tools and technics for data collection are PDB, Surveys, Questionnaires,Focus Group Discussion FGD, monthly cash transfer report, Monthly repots, 4WS, BVA reports.

7 - Protection Risk Analysis

Risk 1: Members of the project targeted communities might try to over-influence or take actions that prevent, or try to change, the type of support provided to education services or who will benefit from the support provided; Mitigation Measure SOS team will consult and listen to the community members through the life of the project (assessments, design, monitoring, corrective actions, evaluation, etc.).Risk2: Tensions within the community or between the organization and community due to the feeling of being unfairly treated, insufficiently consulted or listened to, or concerns with the pro; Mitigation Measure: SOS team will ensure that the local education and other relevant authorities and community members aware of the project’s duration, goal, objectives, activities beneficiary selection criteria, and feedback mechanisms, the project will have indicators related to beneficiary satisfaction. The local education, relevant authorities, and community members will be kept informed of the project's progress and their opinions sought to improve it. Risk 3: Children are over-exposed to the risk of, or experience, harm by teachers, school personnel, service providers, or related entities. Mitigation Measure: SOS Team will ensure that Child Safeguarding cover all people who may interact with children. These include SOS staff, teachers, other school personnel, etc.


8 - PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) strategy

Aid workers or associated people may use their power or influence to exploit or abuse the Children, education personnel sexually, and families they should be serving. To prevent SEA, SOS ensures that all the staff, volunteers, and representatives are educated about the SOS code of conduct before acknowledging commitment to it by signing it. Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) procedures include standards, guidelines, accountability measures, and available complaint mechanisms (hotline, SMS, and focal points). Our PSEA procedures identify risks at the organizational level and the program level. During our interventions, we ensure that targeted communities are aware of the SOS’s policies and procedures. The SOS’s staff and targeted communities have access to safe and effective reporting systems, including knowing when they will get a response to their report and having the right to report anonymously.SOS ensures that the prevention of Sexual Exploitation & Abuse must include the full range of actors engaged in aid activities in addition to the humanitarian actors such as local authorities, community leaders, private vendors and distributors, and others participating in aid provision to beneficiaries.


9 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

The chance for political or security events in the targeted areas may disrupt the project and its activities. The probability of such a risk is low, but the impact is very high. However, we always follow up on the political and security situation in the governorates where we operate to take necessary actions. Project activities will occur in areas with no security events, and safe spaces will be chosen for conducting sessions. There is also the risk of an unstable economic situation with a high probability. This will lead to instability in the expenses needed. However, the project budget was established after studying the price changes in the region in addition to the rate of inflation during the previous years.Cash assistance is constantly subjected to the risk of theft, primarily when it is physically delivered to the beneficiaries. However, cash transfer programming through official money transfer companies improves this service by reducing and managing fraud risks. Finally, a delay might happen in obtaining the project’s official approvals from the Ministry Of Education. This risk will be mitigated by ensuring good coordination with the Ministry Of Education and informing them of the project implementation plan.
