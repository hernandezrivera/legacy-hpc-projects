Project Summary

The project will provide direct interventions to survivors of violence through quality response and prevention initiatives and link up women with income generating activities through cash programming. The project will adhere to survivor centred approaches, improve mental and psychosocial well-being and mitigation strategies to improve the safety, protection and dignity of women and girls in selected refugee camps in Cox’s Bazar, Bangladesh. The population reach will be 2,500 households (12,500 people) in camp 15 and 21.

Needs

Since August 2017, more than 900,000 Rohingya individuals have fled violence and persecution in Myanmar. Now the second year into the crisis, the scale, severity and complexity of needs across the hazardous conditions in the camps remain overwhelming and services continue to be insufficient or of poor quality. The refugees are highly vulnerable with many being traumatized and facing continuous exposure to a multitude of protection risks. The Rohingya women and girls have experienced significant sexual- and gender-based violence (GBV) and abuses while fleeing from Myanmar. In addition, women and girls are continuously the group at highest risk in the current settlements including risks of trafficking, exploitation, sexual assault and violence, GBV and sexual harassment. Overall, the situation forces them to resort to damaging coping strategies such as child or forced marriage, and/or survival and transactional sex. Women and girls are restricted in their movement partly due to risks of harassment and GBV and due to restrictive cultural traditions and gender barriers. According to DCA assessments, women (including female-headed HHs and pregnant and lactating women (PLW)), girls, persons with disabilities (PWDs) and elderly face severe restrictions in accessing services and have multiple unmet and critical needs. The Multi Sectoral Needs Assessment conducted by Reach and UNHCR in July 2018, shows clearly the greater need for services especially with engaging adolescent girls and boys. The assessment indicates that households reporting safety outcomes for children under 18 has three most common safety concerns for boys and girls. For Boys, 49% of the respondents fear the risks of kidnapping, 27% fear violence within the community and 26% fear armed group recruitment. While for girls 41% fear the risk of sexual violence, 38% fear kidnapping and 20% fear child marriage. There is greater need to work with women, girls, men and boys especially on social behaviour change strategies as well as provide economic empowerment for women and girls. With host community living around the project locations they will benefit from the WGSS services and will also be linked up and referred based on need to food security and livelihood opportunity including provision of cash through vouchers to establish start up business. The project will therefore enhance the wellbeing and dignity of Rohingya refugee women and girls in camps and host communities, empower women and girls to access services, and make positive life choices. The project will furthermore mitigate negative coping mechanisms, enhance wellbeing in families, and support women and girls living in both host community and refugees to better lives in an intermediary situation. Individual health, psychological and social consequences of GBV will be reduced, ensuring that survivors will have improved their psychosocial abilities and reduce health impacts.

Activities or outputs

Community participation is one of DCA’s key operating principles and regarded as intrinsic to good programming and accountability. DCA is a certified member of the Core Humanitarian Standards (CHS) and is committed to sharing information, participation and handling complaints during the project cycle. The design is informed by DCA’s experience in GBV programming in Cox Bazar and across the world. The following activities will be implemented.1.0. Women and girls at risk and GBV survivors receiving PSS support including GBV case management- Construction and establishment of one Women and Girls Safe - Provision of focused individualized PSS intervention and case management for GBV. GBV survivors disclosing their incidents are provided with timely services through the case management process where they will be offered psychosocial support and referred to other service providers through the existing referral pathway. Referral pathways and mechanisms has been developed and updated to facilitate safe and ethical referrals and to cover the needs of the survivors responding to survivors wishes in the project location. - Hot spot mapping to identify the risk of GBV in the project location - Recreational activities such as dancing, drama, arts and crafts, story-telling for women and girls- Tailored sessions with adolescent girls using different adolescent girls’ toolkits - Organize monthly staff wellbeing and staff care sessions to provide the better services to community members.- Provision of material assistance such as Dignity Kits 2.0. Engaging the community in GBV prevention and strengthening the community resilience.- Interactive sessions with the community members using "Walk in her shoes tool kit" - FGDs with men and boys including religious/ camp leaders on gender, Human Rights etc.- Promote survivors’ access to IGA based on their own needs and wishes3.0 Strengthening the capacity of services providers internally and from other actors on the GBV guiding principles and survivor centered approach- GBV training of duty bearers and humanitarian staff on GBV prevention and response, GBV guiding principles, Case Management, Human Rights and IASC guidelines for integrating GBV in humanitarian settings- Training DCA staff on comprehensive psychosocial well-being.
