Project Summary

The overarching objective is to strengthen the capacity of especially vulnerable households to
manage COVID-19-exacerbated food security challenges in at-risk communities in Niono. Running for seven
months from July 1 to January 31, 2021, the program will achieve its objective through the targeted delivery
of immediate food support to vulnerable individuals and households in Niono cercle. The project will provide
food assistance and complementary COVID-19 related support to 1,300 displaced and host households
(9,750 individuals) over the course of 7 months.

1 - Contexte

COVID-19 is steadily spreading in Africa. As of May 14, there are 44 deaths across Mali and more than 750confirmed cases.1 Testing capacity, however, remains low and the true number of cases is likely muchhigher. Mali is among the poorest countries in the world, and faces a host of challenges including endemicpoverty, illiteracy, ongoing conflict, and food insecurity.2 It does not have the resources to handle a majorpublic health crisis. There is concern that COVID-19 will exacerbate existing issues and that the secondaryimpacts of the pandemic will debilitate this fragile nation, disproportionately affecting the most vulnerablepopulations.To stall the spread of the virus, Mali has imposed social distancing measures and halted many economicactivities, but it has not implemented a nationwide lockdown and instead has instructed localized restrictionsfor gatherings and public activity. The day-to-day socio-economic reality for many people also puts them athigher risk of catching the virus and unfortunately, Mali lacks the critical health infrastructure needed to helpthose infected. Millions of people live in crowded conditions, are in poor health, and have inadequate accessto food, information, clean air, sanitation facilities and basic services. The most susceptible are the peoplewho have been driven out of their homes due to rising conflict and are now forced to find shelter in informalsettlements, internal displacement sites and host communities in urban and peri-urban areas. InternallyDisplaced People (IDPs) have minimal access to basic services and the state is largely absent, providinglittle to no assistance. IDPs are already highly vulnerable and food insecure, with most having lost theirlivelihoods and depleted what little savings they had to meet their basic needs. Adding the impacts ofCOVID-19 to the already tenuous situation of IDPs will push many past their ability to cope. COVID-19 Food Response in Mali (COFRIM)proposes to respond to this especiallyvulnerable population of IDPs by helping themmeet their food needs in the largely underservedand marginalized area of Niono, Mali.Up until 2019, violence in Mali had largelybeen confined to the eastern cercle (anadministrative subdivision) within the Moptiregion; violence has recently begun eruptingbeyond the existing hotspots and has enteredthe Segou region. The Segou region, Nionocercle in particular, has seen a rapid increasein violence and conflict. This rising conflict,both directed against the Malian state andintercommunal violence between differentethnic groups, has triggered widespreaddisplacement. Since February 2019, thenumber of IDPs has more than doubled in Niono cercle and more than tripled in the Segou region. With an observed increase in arrivals in January 2020, UNHCR led a Rapid Protection Report in the area.Surveyed focal points shared that they receive new arrivals daily, primarily from the regions of Mopti andGao, as well as areas in Segou along the Mauritanian border, due to the deteriorating security situation inthese areas.5 Increasing levels of violence are having a range of effects, including negatively impactingagricultural activity in areas including Niono and driving an increase in protection incidents such as assaultand battery and gender-based violence. In March, Niono cercle reported the highest number of protectionincidents in the Segou region, which accounted for nearly half of those recorded in the region.6Niono’s growing IDP population, combined with an already sizable and marginalized migrant population fromMauritania, has created a population highly susceptible to the expected primary and secondary impacts ofCOVID-19, specifically food insecurity. The impacts of COVID-19 are also beginning to be felt at the sametime as the lean season (April to October), when agro-pastoral livelihoods face scarcity in harvest and whenseasonal food insecurity is forecasted to spike for IDPs and communities. UN OCHA projected that 1.1million people across the country will be severely food insecure during the 2020 lean season and listedNiono cercle as a priority area with food-insecure populations in need of humanitarian support.7Assessments done through Mercy Corps Mali’s existing food voucher programming have shown foodaccess to be a major issue among displaced households, primarily related to lack of financial means topurchase food. Furthermore, prospects for improvements in food security outcomes among IDPs tend to bepoor in situations of conflict and insecurity, which limit one’s ability to resume livelihood activities. It is in thiscontext of rising food insecurity and the threat of COVID-19 to livelihoods that COFRIM will respond with food assistance.
