Project Summary

The purpose of this project is to prevent protection risk and provide quality service on time to women, girls, men, and boys in the two Counties cutting across 7params. The target population is 12,000 (5,000 Women, 4000 Girls, 2000 Boys, and 1000 Men. with a budget of USD 256,000. This is a continuation of a series of projects. CAO is implementing protection with all the components of GBV, CP EORE in Nasir and Fangak for the last four years and is also a build-up following the lessons learned over time. CAO will strengthen community-based structures that have been developed together with other protection partners and update the referral pathway. The other activities to be conducted include case management, WGFS programming, creating awareness in the community on the effects of GBV to the whole community to build resilience and mitigate GBV risks and direct effects it causes on the survivors, training men and women in the community on PFA/PSS, GBV principle guidelines, training more front line workers on case management, PFA and PSS, directly support survivors of GBV through direct support depending on their needs, coordinate with other actors to update referral pathways and conduct appropriate integration and referrals to Health, Nutrition, WASH, Child Protection and Education sectors. Besides, CAO will conduct GBV Safety Audits to assess protection risks to beneficiary women and children on a semi-annual basis. GBV Safety Audit reports will suggest prevention and mitigation measures for identified risks, staff will ensure the timely implementation of such measures through monitoring visits, CAO will Conduct comprehensive case management to the women and girls of GBV survivors, COVID-19 awareness, children women and men will receive messages on COVID-19, which emphasize handwashing, social distancing as a key prevention method for transmission of the virus, establish WGFS. Among the direct beneficiaries will be men, women, girls, and boys.CAO maintains a strong presence in Nasir, Fangak, and Pigi/canal, it has been implementing protection projects on the target counties for the last 4 years with support from UNICEF, SSHF, UNHCR.CAO will scale up its operation especially in the hard to reach areas of locations where the caseload is high

1 - Needs

There is a significant increase of gender-based violence and psycho-social distress to women, girls including mothers, according to the IRNA assessment which was conducted in Jan 2021 by all the agencies on the ground in Fangak, Nasir, and Pigi/Cana. The assessments reported the following findings : A total of 48,103 individual with 7,944 households,15% are children under five are living in Fangak as IDPs,38,950 individual living in Pigi/Canal, and 45,290 living in Nasir as results of flood and intercommunal fighting, there is an alarming and acute need for protection, especially SGBV, This report indicated that over 30,000 live in Fangak, 25,000, in Pigi/canal and 32,00 in Nasir are IDPs.The emergence of a large number of vulnerable women-headed households within conservative and patriarchal communities has heightened the acute exposure of women and children to protection threats, especially GBV cases such as sexual violence, SEA, Intimate partner violence, economic violence, forced marriage, and harmful traditional practices like blood compensation for the girl child. In addition to S-GBV perpetrated within the community. Women and girls fear reporting rape cases due to the weak judicial systems. This puts them in a position of increased vulnerability in an environment where the only access to justice is via community-based justice adjudicated by the male boma chief or administrator who often does not favor women and girls. Community Action Organization (CAO) also conducts a monthly protection monitoring assessment in Nasir with the ongoing protection projects Nasir which covers four Payams (Mandeng, Jikmir, Makak and Maker of Nasir, two Payams of Fangak (Phom and Jaibour), and two Payams of Pigi/Canal (Dhel and Korwai) counties, The report underline series of protection concerns, it further reported that the entire population of women, girls, boys, and men are affected by floods, civil war, inter-clan and revenge killings and that much of the work is done by women and girls which include fetching water and collecting firewood from bushes that put the safety of these girls and women at high risk of sexual violence. Boys and male youth have been abducted in both Counties and forced to Join rebel militia groups further increasing the stress levels of families of the abducted children. According to the CITISAMM report of October 201 on cantonment sites, there is a need to mitigate GBV risks of women and girls living near these sites through proper coordination of partners to target community leaders on GBV mitigation strategies. There is a need for community GBV awareness-raising to women, girls, boys, and men on behavior change in support of their female counterparts. GBV services are cover fewer Payams in the counties, CAO will increase access to GBV services in the three locations targeting men, boys, girls, and women who are returnees, IDPs, and host communities. The assessment report recommends that among the items that are supposed to be distributed, which would include dignity kits, hygiene materials, there is, need to coordinate with WASH partners on the ground for an integrated GBV/WASH response.

2 - Project activities

The project will conduct the following activities: Activity 1. Provide comprehensive case management to GBV survivors/individual PSS and follow up for women and girls GBV survivors using appropriate and ethical guidelines in women and girls’ friendly spaces in Fangak, Nasir, and Pigi/Canal, Activity 2. CAO will conduct awareness–raising on the GBV risks affecting women and girls, availability of GBV services, and use of referral pathway in coordination with other sectoral partners in WASH FSL and Education. Activities 3. CAO will plan the awareness meetings with Health, WASH, Education, Nutrition, and FSL partners to integrate GBV prevention in sectoral activities using GBV guiding principles and mainstreaming guidelines in Fangak Nasir and Pigi/Canal. Activity 4. Establishment of three women and Girls' friendly spaces (1 in Fangak) (1in Nasir) (1 in Pigi/canal) to cover more beneficiaries in both project locations with GBV services such as case management, group, and individual PSS, livelihood referrals and provision of dignity kits. Activity 5. strengthen the capacity of static service providers and communities to deliver quality GBV services in Fangak Nasir and Pigi/canal through training on comprehensive case management and PSS. Activity 6. Conducting training of male and female front-line service providers on protection risks mitigation and early warning systems. Activity 7. Conduct monthly community dialogue forums for EMAP with male community leaders, local authority, and community-based protection committees to strengthen protection mechanisms and mitigate GBV risks in Fangak Nasir and Pigi/canal Activity 8, CAO will provide legal service and awareness assistance to the GBV survivors. In strengthening this activity, CAO will train the police and the justice authorities, including the traditional justice mechanism.

7.4.2. How will your project provide beneficiaries information about Protection against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA)?

In line with the core humanitarian standards and IASC Guidelines, CAO will incorporate its accountability framework through the establishment of beneficiary feedback mechanisms (grass-root and outreach dialogues, consultation communication channels, and suggestion boxes for use beneficiaries) to enable feedback through and /or after all our activities.
