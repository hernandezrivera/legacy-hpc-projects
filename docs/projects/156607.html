Project Summary

This project will support the monitoring of population movements across non-government controlled areas in northwest Syria to make available reliable and verified data and information for the humanitarian community, in order to address new or changing demographics and needs in an efficient and coordinated manner. An online monitoring dashboard will provide humanitarian actors with the latest information per community level, regular/monthly overviews and trend analysis will be shared with Cluster members, and ad-hoc emergency updates will be produced and shared when required. Technical guidance will be provided to Cluster members to support with the collective gathering of data.

Needs

Due to the lack of access to areas in northern Syria and the remote management of the cross-border operation, obtaining comprehensive and accurate data on populations and displacements is a challenge for humanitarian actors. The CCCM Cluster has developed an efficient inter-agency based system to collect data in a coordinated manner, triangulate, and when possible, verify the data. According to the Cluster as mentioned in the draft 2019 HNO, it has tracked almost 1.1 million population movements in northern Syria between September 2017 and August 2018 which was shared with and used by all actors in the humanitarian response. On average, the Cluster tracked more than 96,000 population movements per month in 2018, compared to 82,000 the previous year, and 40,000 in 2016. The main causes for these displacements were (renewed) military offensives in the governorates of Idleb, Aleppo, Hama, Ar-Raqqa, Deir-ez-Zor, and rural Damascus (e.g. Eastern Ghouta). By doing this, the Cluster heavily relied on the support of its Cluster members as the data for population movement monitoring was provided by some 28 organisations. In addition, the Cluster also relied on specialized organizations to provide data and information on population movements to ensure sufficient geographical coverage and technical capacity to collect and disseminate this information in a coherent and rapid manner for the humanitarian response. UNHCR through its partner REACH aim to continue providing such dedicated support to the CCCM Cluster under the IDP Situation Monitoring Initiative (ISMI).

Implementation of activities

The main objective of this project is to obtain and disseminate timely, accurate and comprehensive data on displacements and population movements in northern Syria, support CCCM members’ capacity to collect quality data, and support the CCCM Cluster in providing quality reports and analyses to humanitarian actors. Building upon the existing system and strengths of the CCCM’s population movement monitoring in northern Syria, UNHCR as the Cluster lead agency, will continue to contribute to the technical expertise, geographical coverage, and frequency in this monitoring exercise. The scope of data collection, using a ‘baseline’ to compare tracking efforts to and enabling more frequent, consistent and uniform approaches to population movement monitoring across northern Syria. The modalities for presentation and dissemination of information includes maps, factsheets, reports, trend analyses, and rapid assessment profiles. These are adapted to the type of assessments, e.g. baseline situation overview, monthly overviews, ad-hoc updates, thematic assessments and trends analysis. Reports analyse a number of topics including displacement patterns, trends, intentions of movement and triggers, in addition to numbers and locations of IDPs. During periods of rapid displacement, regular updates on population movements are provided every two to four days, presented in the form of rapid assessment profiles. This information will support humanitarian actors in their programming and (geographical) priority setting. Overall, this intervention furthermore will contribute to empowering the local NGOs and local actors’ capacity.

Protection Risk Analysis

Do-No-Harm should be an integral principle of the humanitarian response in (northwest) Syria. UNHCR, as lead agency of the CCCM Cluster, strives to strike the fine balance between ensuring that dignified life-saving assistance is provided to IDPs in sites while also making sure that these sites remain a last resort option and do not contribute to any push/pull factor or create aid dependency. Any assistance provided to IDPs in sites is upon consultation and agreement with the CCCM Cluster. There are some specific risks related to the implementation of this project, namely: - Risk that displaced people seek refuge in areas where there is no monitoring and/or assistance being provided. UNHCR and its partner REACH established almost complete geographical coverage on population movement monitoring in non-government controlled areas in northwest Syria.- Risk of monitoring/assessment fatigue amongst beneficiaries and Cluster members. The purposes of monitoring and assessments are clarified to beneficiaries without raising expectations. The tools and questionnaires are developed to be as light as possible.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

In a humanitarian emergency with a highly volatile and unpredictable context, a wide variety of risks which might affect the implementation of activities are considered in the planning and implementation phase of this and other projects. UNHCR and its partners have several procedures and tools in place to identify and mitigate risks (e.g. corporate risk register, risk-based monitoring and control plans, risk assessment and management plan). Firstly and foremost, the security and safety of humanitarian staff working and living in northwest Syria are at risk, and UNHCR fully supports and encourages partners to develop and implement Safety & Security and Duty of Care policies. Partners can suspend activities when this is required by the local conditions and should take preventive measures to ensure the safety of their staff. Secondly, access to beneficiaries is regularly disrupted due to developments beyond the control of the humanitarian community. Therefore UNHCR works with a variety of local NGOs who have presence and networks on the ground and are aware of the local context. Sharing information on incidents is done at the inter-agency level to promote collective action and messaging. More specific examples of risks and mitigating measures are:- Risk of irregular procurement processes: bulk procurement of core-relief/non-food items is done in Turkey or internationally and goods are transhipped to northern Syria.- Risk of aid diversion: partners are supported with capacity-building initiatives, closely monitored and verification is done by independent third party monitoring.- Risk of interference in humanitarian assistance by armed groups: open communication lines and relationship are maintained with partners on local developments and specific incidents, and the inter-agency coordination is informed about (major) incidents to ensure unified and effective messaging to AOGs.- Risks of physical and sexual exploitation and abuse: UNHCR ensures that partners have a code of conduct (CoC) in place, supports developing/reviewing PSEA prevention materials, organizes internal and external CoC/PSEA sessions, and follows-up on PSEA on a regular basis. PSEA focal points are identified in the operation whom individuals can contact and/or report misconduct by humanitarian staff. To address the indicated risks above, UNHCR and its partners have policies and SOPs in place to guide their programme, finance, procurement, logistics, and human resource management, and conduct third-party monitoring and audits to confirm assistance and services have reached beneficiaries without major discrepancies.
