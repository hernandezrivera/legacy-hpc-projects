Project Summary

30 field assessments were conducted by the FSL and MEAL teams through meetings with KIs or FGDs. AAH developed an internal mission strategy in which priority locations have been identified based on historic areas of intervention where AAH has proven capacity and resources to intervene, areas with access by the agency, locations where needs and vulnerabilities have been confirmed through field assessments and locations identified by OCHA as highly vulnerable. Beneficiary Selection Committees established at sub-district/village levels are responsible for initial identification of beneficiaries’ dependent on AAH criteria (needs and vulnerability based). Beneficiary data segregated by age and gender. Discussions are currently on-going with MAAR to include ACSAD as a member of the selection committee. AAH defined selection criteria will identify the most vulnerable households in the targeted locations as follows: household does not have more than one source of income – total income should not exceed 327,330 SYP (figure set by the CWG); woman head of household; families which include an elderly man or women (more than 60 years); persons with disability registered with MoSAL (confirmed with a disability card); Pregnant and Lactating Women; a divorced or widowed woman; families with at least two children under 5 years of age. A validation survey of a random sample will be conducted to confirm the beneficiaries adhere to the defined selection criteria. AAH adheres to the Principle of “Do No Harm” with transparent beneficiary selection. The targeted locations are suitable for MPC distribution as the need assessment reports confirmed the imperative of increasing access to cash to cover the expenses incurred by community members. In-kind distribution does not always equate with fluid beneficiary needs, which can change from week to week; MPC would resolve this impasse and be of greater benefit to beneficiaries. During March 2021, the Syrian pound depreciated by 18% month-on-month reaching an average of 3,972 SYP/USD. This has significantly affected commodity prices with the Minimum Expenditure Basket (MEB) reaching 327,330 SYP for a family of 5. AAH will support vulnerable households who have insufficient financial resources to cover the MEB indicated above. The transfer value the multipurpose cash support provided by AAH will be based on the CWG February 2021 data analysis. The MEB for a household of 5 members is 442,029 SYP with an MEB gap of 327,330 SYP. The CWG recommends covering 60% of the MEB gap for multipurpose cash, valued at 200,000 SYP with adjustments made dependent for location (200,000 SYP for central areas, 225,000 SYP in Aleppo and 220,000 in the North West). The time of the distribution will be determined based on several factors including funding availability. Budget allocation of MPC will be 70% Food Security, 15% WaSH and 15% relief NFIs although the actual allocation depends on households’ own priorities. Cash distributions will be undertaken by selected FSPs. AAH will directly inform beneficiaries of their selection. The FSP will send an SMS to beneficiaries who will be requested to come to the FSP office to collect their cash. Post-distribution monitoring surveys will be conducted 4-6 weeks following the distribution of the cash to determine where households spent the distributed money; it will also determine the perception of the beneficiaries. There will be a specific emphasis in monitoring and evaluating the training's impact and satisfaction. Baseline and endline surveys will be conducted with the impact of the project based on quantitative measurement of the change in defined indicators. A session to review the Action implementation, identify the best practices and for recommendation in future interventions involving all stakeholders will be conducted.

4 - Needs

There are 13.4 million people in Syria in need of humanitarian assistance. 12.4M people are estimated as food insecure (1.3M are considered severely food insecure). The WFP’s national standard reference food basket price in February 2021 was SYP 136,398. The prolonged nature of the crisis has strained the ability of the population to cope; negative coping strategies are on the rise. Households responded by reducing the number of meals per day and not consuming expensive food categories; the water consumption and limiting NFI purchases. As hostilities reduced in many areas, particularly those that have shifted control, the demands from the population relating to livelihood opportunities and basic services have increased as they seek to rebuild their lives. AAH conducted 30 needs assessments in 8 governorates at sub-district level during 2020-21. The assessment methodology involves collection of information from KIs in targeted sub-districts to complete a questionnaire (sections on context, livelihood activities, food security and production status, access to water and NFIs). The assessment undertaken through a gender perspective. Households indicated there is limited access to agricultural inputs and market support to establish or maintain sustainable agricultural livelihoods. Budget allocation of the multipurpose cash will be 70% Food Security, 15% WaSH and 15% relief NFIs.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented by the AAH FSL team based in the Damascus, with regional offices in Aleppo and Hassakeh. Cash distribution will be undertaken by FSPs. The FSPs selection will be conducted through AAH procurement procedures with a financially and technically competitive tender; the successful bidder will sign a contract. There will be three distributions at one-month intervals during October, November and December. Beneficiaries will be selected through Beneficiary Selection Committees in the targeted locations and kept informed by AAH. Beneficiary data will include name, contact number and a named alternative. The FSP will inform the beneficiary by SMS to come to the FSP office to receive the cash. The transfer value is the value recommended by the CWG (200,000 SYP for central areas (Hama) and 220,000 SYP in the North West (Aleppo)). AAH has a dedicated MEAL department with staff in the hub and regions. A comprehensive M&E plan will be designed to support collection and tracking of sex and age disaggregated data in line with the proposed MEAL Framework. AAH staff conduct regular monitoring field visits to confirm beneficiary selection criteria have been maintained, aid distribution proceeded correctly (a PDM report is prepared). Outputs are measured against defined indicators to quantify the impact through baseline/endline surveys. All monitoring information is used to capture good practices and lessons learned. The MEAL team collects testimonies from the beneficiaries; this material can be used for evidence-based advocacy. A feedback mechanism is in place. A telephone number and contact information is included in the package given to beneficiaries at the time of aid distribution.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Prioritise safety and dignity, and principle of “Do No Harm”: AAH implements a transparent beneficiary selection procedure to target highly vulnerable households. These procedures are circulated to ensure transparency and reduce possible tension in the community, thus reducing the negative impact on social cohesion. AAH will communicate directly with beneficiaries to confirm selection. The time and location of the cash distribution will be publicised to allow beneficiaries time to travel to the distribution point. The distribution points will be managed to ensure effective crowd control and with gender considerations in place. The amount of cash distributed adheres to the recommendations from the CWG defined in the Transfer Values for Multipurpose Cash. Meaningful access: AAH ensures special arrangements for people with protection concerns (e.g., PWDs, the elderly) for a representative of the beneficiary to collect the aid on their behalf. AAH ensures protection of the rights of the marginalised or those facing discrimination by identification of these groups during assessment and beneficiary selection. Accountability, Participation and Empowerment: AAH ensures KIs represent all groups in the community during the assessments to understand their needs and methodology of assistance; identifies measures to help people avoid resorting to negative coping strategies; establishes a complaints feedback mechanism. PSAE: Ensure procedures are put in place to prevent sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH follows internal policies relating to fraud, abuse of power and whistle-blower mechanisms. The Code of Conduct is aligned with IASC principles. Employees and contractors sign a Code of Conduct. AAH has a PSEA Policy in place.

7.1 - Please list any risks related to sexual exploitation and abuse that have been identified during the project design phase and any risk mitigation and prevention measures that have been adopted and built into the project

AAH recognizes the right of everyone to be treated with dignity and respect, and protected from sexual exploitation and abuse. AAH policy on PSEA complies with the IASC six principles relating to sexual exploitation and abuse. Implementation of humanitarian aid activities can sometimes have the unforeseen impact of creating unequal power balances within the AAH team and for recipients of aid; the organization is committed to mitigating against such risks. Sexual exploitation and abuse can do great harm to people affecting integrity and trust; AAH prohibits such exploitation by affirming a zero-tolerance approach. Disregard of the PSEA polices is regarded as gross misconduct, which could result in summary dismissal. AAH reserves the right to refer such cases to the relevant legal authorities in the abuser’s country of origin and in the host country. All AAH staff will always treat people with dignity and respect; undertake awareness raising of the PSEA policy and report any instances of a breach of this policy to the prescribed channels. All employees and representatives of AAH will not engage in sexually exploitative behaviour or linking sexual favour with the receipt of aid; engage in any sexual activity with a child under 18 years of age; or being involved with any forms of prostitution or payment for sex. AAH will investigate allegations of sexual exploitation and abuse in a timely, professional, and confidential manner.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

CVA risk for liquidity, inflation and impact on the market. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: cash amount only covers basic needs based on CWG recommendations, coordinate with other agencies implementing MPC, reducing the targeted beneficiaries to increase the tranche value based on conflict sensitivity analysis. Risk of COVID-19 spread. Likelihood: high. Impact: high. Mitigation: social distancing; safe hand-washing area; distribution PPE; COVID-19 SOP implemented. Risk of unstable security situation; access to targeted locations prevented; additional lengthy administrative procedures. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: use local staff and SARC; advocacy, coordination with local authorities/Ministries to monitor situation, negotiate access. Adhere to security systems, Conflict-Sensitivity approach. Risk of inflation; International bank transfers to Syria hindered. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: situation closely monitored with communication to donors with options defined. Risk of aid diversion. Likelihood: medium. Impact: high. Mitigation: diversification of suppliers, triangulation of information, monitoring procedures in place, security plan and risks assessments updated. Ethical risk. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: provide trainings to staff on organizational ethical policies. Follow the internal policies relating to fraud, abuse of power, and whistle-blower mechanisms. Risks associated with conduct of the FSP. Likelihood: low. Impact: low. Mitigation: ensure communication with the FSP, define SOP and modality of cash transfer, AAH inform beneficiary of time to receive cash, AAH representative at the FSP office during cash distribution, implement data protection policy.
