Project Summary

The project aims to provide a wide-ranging support from emergency to regular food assistance as well as household based agricultural and livelihoods assistance. The project aims to target populations who are both food insecure as well as at risk of food insecurity through multiple but mutually reinforcing activities. The project will contribute to the following two objectives of Food security cluster:

Objective 1: Improve the food security status of assessed food insecure people through life-saving and life sustaining food assistance. Objective 2: Support self reliance of affected households by protecting and building productive assets and restoring or creating income generating opportunities to save and sustain lives.

A total of 30,000 households (150,000 individuals) will be supported through multiple but integrated activities.

Under ﬁrst objective for life-saving and life-sustaining food assistance, the project will aim to meet the immediate food needs and also seasonal/supplementary food assistance. The Emergency Response will aim to reach crisis-affected people within 72 hours of the onset of a crisis to cover a minimum of one to four weeks of their immediate food needs through short-term support such as Ready-to-Eat Rations, cooked meals and/or baked bread. Baked bread will also be provided to the displaced families living in the camps. this will help to stabilize the market prices. further supplementary food assistance will be provided to improve the dietary diversity. such as Ramadan food distribution and Qurbani meat distribution.

under the objective 2, appropriate agricultural inputs (cereal as well as vegetable production) will be provided along with technical capacity building at household-level to ensure that most vulnerable farming households receive timely inputs as per seasonal calendar to be able to produce and contribute to domestic production requirements. further asset building and asset protection support mainly for livestock keepers and poultry will be provided. This include but not limited to provision of EWE sheep, poultry feed, and vaccination.

Finally, individuals including youth and people with disability will also be targeted for small scale income generation activities. such as barber shop, female saloon, grocery store etc. Local procurement will be encouraged to promote local markets, especially where markets have the capacity to absorb such demands. This objective also aim to restore and strengthen productive assets and create livelihood opportunities for the targeted populations, thus contributing to the overall food production and subsequently to food availability in Syria.

4 - Needs

Compared to last year, the PiN for 2020 has increased by 8 per cent, demonstrating the continued impact of the protracted crisis on the food security and agriculture situation of the country. Persistent conflict causing on going emergency response has shown minimal progress in terms of addressing the root causes of the crisis while at the same time affecting the economic growth which in turn has negatively impacted the market and livelihood opportunities.Ongoing violence and high levels of insecurity after almost a decade of conflict have impacted all aspects of the country and its people, with significant consequences on the pillars of food security. Unprecedented numbers of displacement have meant that almost two thirds of the Syrian population have been forced out of their homes causing a detrimental effect on the availability and access of food. With an increase of 8 percent in the people in need since last year, food security and poverty remain to be deeply intertwined as economic deterioration becomes more persistent over time. With over 50 per cent of Syrians thought to be living in urban areas, physical and financial access to markets is essential for food security. It is believed some 80 per cent of Syrians depend on markets to meet their food needs. Access to food remains constrained due to high transportation and living costs, reduced demand due to high unemployment rates and stagnant wages thus impeding the purchasing power of the Syrian population. Some 69 percent of Syrians are believed to be living on less than 1.90 USD a day, more than double since 2011. High levels of unemployment are persistently high estimated at 50 per cent. The national average price of a standard reference food basket at USD 77 based on formal exchange rates is more than 8 times higher than pre-crisis levels. In December 2019, the reference food basket had increased by 17% month on month and 43% year on year.

5 - Implementation of activities

The following are the main project activities. A total of 30,000 households (150,000 individuals) will be targeted. 1.Emergency response to crisis affected vulnerable people with short term food assistance through appropriate modalities. This intervention will target 5000 households (25,000 people). 2.Supplementary food assistance to Persons with Speciﬁc Needs (PSN) through appropriate modalities (complementary to above and inter linkage with nutrition sector). This also include seasonal food support such as during Ramadan and Qurbani. This intervention will target 17,000 household (85,000 people). 3. Support to bakeries (wheat ﬂour, yeast, etc.) through appropriate modalities. This intervention will target 3,500 households (17,500 people). 4. Distribution of agricultural inputs, such as seeds, fertilizer, pesticide and equipment. This activity will target 1,500 households (7,500 people). 5. Support to small scale food production ( poultry egg laying hens). This activity will target 1000 households (5,000 people). 6. Support to asset building and asset protection (small livestock and animal feed distribution) including winterization activities. This intervention will assist 1,000 households (5,000 people). 7. Emergency livestock treatment, and training for veterinary services. (same as of activity 6) 8. Support Income generating activities including vocational training. This activity will target 1,000 households (5,000 individuals). Most of the Youth are already economically active in Syria. The project will target the youth and people with disability for income generation activities that include vocational training to build the human capital. youth and people with disability will also be targeted for the support including provision of small enterprise grants or tools or equipment to start their own business and establish a livelihood base. Islamic Relief will promote gender sensitive programming at all stages of the project cycle.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

The following are the main protection risks and the mitigation measures: 1. In provision of general food assistance there is a risk of violence from the beneﬁciaries among themselves as well as the humanitarian staff. Also there is a risk of SEA by humanitarian workers male dominated, gate keepers and potential other beneﬁciaries. This risk will be mitigated through establishing a robust complaint and feedback mechanism so that any such cases are appropriately reported and followed up. The organisational staff will be trained on the Inter Agency Coordination in Community Based Complaint Mechanisms as well as code of conduct. To the extent possible, female staff will be recruited and community committees will be formed for women. post distribution monitoring will be conducted. it will be ensured that the distribution is well organised. For example identiﬁcation of sites ﬁrst, set up several distribution points , do joint distribution with other partners. 2. The beneﬁciary lists are not updated by the local council. Interference with beneﬁciary targeting. To mitigate this risk the staff will be well trained on the beneﬁciary selection criteria and mechanism to cross verify the vulnerability of the population. The communities will be informed of the beneﬁciary selection criteria and a through community mobilization will be conducted to ensure that the communities are sensitized and are able to voice their concerns in a timely manner. The local councils will be supported to prepare the beneﬁciary lists. Regular meetings will be organised with the community leaders or gate keepers on the targeting criteria. Community feedback system will be established. Well trained and gender balanced outreach team will be used so that they could check if some locations are not included in the beneﬁciaries list. 3. Diversion of aid is another possible risk. This will be mitigated through staff training, consultation and coordination with all the relevant stakeholders.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

some of the major risks and the mitigation measures are as follows: 1. Potential conﬂict between the beneﬁciaries and non-beneﬁciaries. Mitigation measure: To prevent conﬂict between the beneﬁciaries and non-beneﬁciaries, IR will ask the local councils and the residents of these areas to nominate a community representative to represent the people in the area. IR, in cooperation with the Community Representative, will choose the beneﬁciaries according to speciﬁc criteria to serve the community and will adopt a clear and transparent policy in the implementation of the project. 2. Outbreak of major livestock /poultry disease. Mitigation measure: The livestock and poultry will be vaccinated against the major diseases before distribution to the targeted families. The veterinary oﬃcer will ensure to keep a record of animal health and report immediately about any majordiseases outbreak so that appropriate action is taken. 3. Destruction of cultivated lands by air strike. Mitigation Measure: This is an external factor beyond the control of the project. In case of any such security incident donor will be informed in timely manner. Actions will be taken in consultation with local community, M&E and programme team and donor.
