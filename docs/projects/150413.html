Project Summary

Project Objective :
Is to enhance the income of vulnerable and poor female and male farmers in the most affected areas of the Gaza Strip.
Project Summary :
The project is mainly assisting vulnerable and poor female and male farmers by providing them with one or more of the following services:
* Rehabilitation of damage open lands
* Rehabilitation of existing water wells used to rung by electricity or fuel
* Installation solar system for agricultural water wells
* Rehabilitation of existing water ponds
* Construction of new water ponds
* Installation of water carrier lines which will be only carried out with water wells rehabilitation or with water wells that will utilize solar systems.
The project activities will be implemented all over the Gaza strip. NDC will partner with 5 local registered cluster NGOs who have extensive agricultural experience to implement the project. In addition, ,the project activities will be completely coordinated with the Ministry of Agriculture and the Food Security Sector.
The estimated number of direct beneficiaries is 842 female and male farmers while the total number of the farmers and their families is 4,715. The estimated total budget for the project is $2,141,688.

Needs

The 2020 forecast for the Gaza Strip warns that challenges including poverty and food insecurity will only intensify. The Gaza Strip has been under an economic blockade since June 2006. Its urban economy is severely crippled in the longer term under restrictions of movement and access to resources. This has been further worsened by 2014 war on Gaza causing massive damage to the entire Gaza region targeting all of its land including its agricultural land, water wells and other water related infrastructure. “In the Gaza Strip, the share of food insecure households sits at 47 %, or approximately 1 million people, with an increase of 86,000 individuals between 2013 and 2014 due to the closure of illegal tunnels with Egypt in 2013 and, in particular, recurrent conflict, in mid 2014, which compounded the existing and long standing difficulties caused by the blockade, leading to a 15 % GDP contraction.” (SEFSEC 2014– May 2016 update, p.35). Moreover, according to the Palestinian Bureau of Statistic, second quarter report, unemployment in the Gaza Strip is 53.7% which is one of the highest in the world. To alleviate food insecurity in the most vulnerable sectors, NDC is partnering with five NGOs operating within the food security sector. These NGOs have extensive experience in implementing agricultural projects including rehabilitation of damage lands, water wells, carrier lines, water ponds, and installing solar systems for water wells which are all vital components towards building resilience against food insecurity in the Gaza context. During October 2018, nine focus groups were conducted by three local NGOs in different areas of Gaza Strip to discuss the main agricultural demands and stand on the top priorities to help developing the agricultural sector and improving the farmers lives. Two focus groups were conducted in the north and attend by 33 persons (16 males and 17 females). One focus group was conducted in Gaza city and attend by 17 men. Two focus groups were conducted in Khanyounis and attended by 45 persons (18 males and 27 females). Four focus groups were conducted in Rafah and attended by 90 persons (43 males and 47 females). In total, the nine focus groups were attended by 185 persons (94 males and 91 females. In addition, the participants in the focus groups age were between 20 to over 60 years old. During the needs assessment, farmers, both men and women, indicated that open land rehabilitation, water wells rehabilitation, carrier lines installation and rehabilitation of existing water ponds and construction new ones. More importantly, all the farmers indicated that utilizing solar systems to irrigate their lands is the highest priority due to the sever lack of electricity and the high costs of fuel. Solar systems will provide them with water with very low costs. Due to the nature of these activities, there were no significant difference in needs between male and female farmers and also the same also applies for the different age groups. The beneficiaries are smallholder farmers, their immediate families and extended community who have minimum assets, little or no access to livelihood enhancing methods. These farmers have been selected through a consultative process undertaken by the partner NGOs. The process included a gaps analysis to identify indicators of food insecure or at risk of food insecure HHs that are excluded from the socio economic development process on grounds such as locality, poverty and gender. The selection is further based on a community gap analysis, involving village councils, cooperatives, HHs who lost their source of income, small farmers, livestock breeders and women headed HHs. In addition, farmers who are suffering from the sever electricity cuts and facing considerable difficulties in running their water wells and pumping systems and thus are not able to irrigate their fields will be also considered for this project. The top priorities are addressed as activities in this project.

Activities

The project targets two main categories. The first category is male and female farmers whose agricultural assets such as fields, agricultural water wells, and water carrier lines had been destroyed in the last wars such as the 2014 war. The second category is farmers who are suffering from the sever electricity cuts and facing considerable difficulties in running their water wells and pumping systems and thus are not able to effectively irrigate their fields. It is expected that 842 farmers (men and female) will directly benefit from the project's activities. Their immediate families’ members with 4715 persons will benefit as well. At the start of the project, local advertisement will be published in each of the five governorates to ask farmers to submit applications to benefit from the project based on predetermined general criteria such as being small farmers own less than 5 dunums, affected in the last wars, located in the target areas. More specific criteria will be developed for water wells rehabilitation and solar system installation such as licensed water wells, irrigating at least 40 dunums, located in a safe areas. The advertisement will be published in mosques, local NGOs premises, local committees locations, etc. Five selection committees from NDC and its Local NGOs partners, local committees’ members, local figures will be formed to select the best applications that meet the criteria. Final lists will be developed and passed to the Ministry of Agriculture (MoA) to get their feedback on the selected beneficiaries. Based on the comments of the MoA, a final list of beneficiaries will be developed. NDC will be responsible for the overall management of the project activities, procurement, financial disbursement, monitoring and evaluation, and reporting. Five local NGOs partners will be responsible for implementing the project activities on the ground, form the five local committees, finalize the beneficiary list in coordination with MoA, and implement the project activities on the ground. In implementing the project activities, NDC will coordinate with the MoA, FSS, and INGOs. Coordination with localNGOs, the local committees, municipalities, and other governmental bodies will be the role of the five partners. The project is a multi year project. Most of the current activities such as water wells rehabilitation and installing solar systems for water wells will start in year one and continue in the second and third year. In addition, open land rehabilitation will also continue. The main reason is that the needs are huge and it is not expected that they will be met in one year. This is clear from the cluster targets and needs as determined by the FSS. Furthermore, the project will build on some of the activities/outputs achieved in the first year for the second and third year. As example, the water wells rehabilitated in the first year, will be targeted to operate them utilizing solar/photovoltaic systems in the second year. Hence, solar systems will be implemented in the first, second, and third years. Project activities:• Open land rehabilitation targeting 1,000 dunums distributed equally all over Gaza Strip• Agricultural Water Well Rehabilitation (five in Gaza and two in Khanyounis)• Install Solar/photovoltaic Systems for 10 water wells (all over Gaza Strip)• Agricultural Water Pond Rehabilitation ( 20 in Middle area, 20 in Khanyounis , and 30 in Rafah)• Agricultural Water Pond provided ( 30 in Rafah)• Install Water Carrier Lines (10 KM all over the Gaza Strip ). Note that, the water carrier lines will be only carried out with water wells rehabilitation or with water wells that will utilize solar systems. Thus the water carrier lines will be used to carry the water from the rehabilitated water wells or the upgrades water wells with solar systems to lands that will be rehabilitated and need water sources.

Indicators

1. # of dunums rehabilitated in agricultural production (Target: 1,000) for 500 beneficiaries including 320 males and 180 females2. # of water wells rehabilitated (Target: Seven [7]) for 42 female and male farmers3. # of kilometers of water carrier lines rehabilitated and operating (Target: 10) for 80 male and 20 female farmers4. # of water wells installed solar systems (Target: Ten [10] with 250 KW output) for 100 female and male farmers5. # of water ponds rehabilitated (Target: 70 ponds, 7000m3) for 70 female and male farmers6. # of water ponds provided (Target: 30 ponds, 3000m3) for 30 female and male farmers To ensure good implementation and monitoring and evaluation, NDC's M&E Monitoring and Evaluation Officer will ensure that the protection standards are fully respected and followed. Additionally, a compliant response mechanism will be put in place to ensure that feedback and complaints from beneficiaries are reported confidentially. NDC management will ensure that these complaints are immediately investigated and addressed in the most appropriate manner. In addition, NDC will implement its well-developed system of Community Score Cards (the system was developed with the support of the World Bank) to ensure the quality of service provided to beneficiaries. NDC will implement 10 sessions during the project implementation (two sessions in every governerate). The first session will be implemented at the start of the project while the second session will be implemented by the end of the project. The results will show how the beneficiaries assess the quality of the service provided to them. If the quality of the service is not as expected, the Project Manager will immediately take corrective actions. Furthermore, a beneficiary satisfaction questionnaire will be distributed to selected representative sample of beneficiary to assess their satisfaction with the project activities. The findings of the survey along with the results of the community scorecard sessions will be used as learning tools to improve project implementation and the realization of objectives and indicators. Furthermore, 5 local committees will be formed and will participate in the implementing the project activities, monitoring and evaluation.
