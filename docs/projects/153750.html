Project Summary

GOAL’s primary objective for 2019 is to provide urgent and lifesaving assistance to 60,000HH (360,000 beneficiaries) through the distribution of emergency livelihood kits, seeds, and tools, support of market functionality interventions, training and capacity building to support resiliency, and diversification and restoration of livelihoods for vulnerable households across IPC 3 and 4 phases of food insecurity.

Needs

The protracted impact of conflict and recent macro-economic crisis have driven poverty rates in South Sudan to unprecedented levels; coupled with high inflation rates, many households continue to face hunger as they are now unable to meet the minimum food basket. Crisis/IPC3 and Emergency/IPC4 food insecurity levels persist throughout the country, with significant food consumption gaps expected during the lean season and little chance of improvement in the upcoming years. Despite extensive countrywide efforts to contain the food security crisis, most recent IPC analyses estimate 5.3 million people (48% of population) face at least IPC phase 3 or worse levels of food insecurity. Although humanitarian assistance will likely prevent more extreme food security outcomes, aid is expected to reach less than 50% of estimated need in South Sudan. Risk of IPC 5 "Famine" persists even in the presence of aid; thus, scaled up food security and nutrition interventions are needed to support most vulnerable households, especially female-headed households, internally displaced persons, and host communitiesTwic County is currently classified as IPC phase 3 ("Crisis"), with projections predicting it would be at least one phase worse off without humanitarian assistance. Further, alarmingly high GAM rates have been observed alongside significant gaps in acute malnutrition treatment coverage throughout the county, with a GAM prevalence of 35.9% (SAM 11.4%, MAM 24.5%; WVI SMART Survey, July 2017). To better address these needs, GOAL will adapt current programming to provide a more comprehensive package of interventions to increase access to food sources, protect livelihoods opportunities, support resilience to climate stresses, and improve nutritional status of vulnerable populations in South Sudan. Programming will focus on prevention of malnutrition (targeting PLWs and children under 5) while protecting immediate access to food and preventing the collapse of livelihoods to help communities withstand environmental and conflict-related challenges. The organisation is well placed to implement this project in Twic County. GOAL has worked with communities in Twic since 1998, and it currently implements BMZ-funded “Supporting the transition from humanitarian crisis through recovery to sustainable economic development in Warrap State, South Sudan” (SEWOH) in partnership with HelpAge. GOAL’s history in the region has helped in identifying most acute needs, namely short term relief through cash transfer and short cycle crops, and long term resilience building through crop diversification and training on sustainable agronomic practices. GOAL's country programme will implement integrated food security and nutrition programmes to respond to the needs of vulnerable populations through a comprehensive package of services as identified below.

Activities

GOAL will draw on lessons learnt from DFID-funded RECOVER and BMZ-funded SEWOH projects to scale up implementation of food production systems and market activities through provision of climate-smart and nutrition sensitive seeds (for example, dark green vegetables, vitamin A rich orange gourds, sorghum, sweet potatoes) and training on good agronomic practices, such as seed multiplication, that will have positive impact on environment protection. The project will conduct environmental impact assessment and identify mitigation measures, such as conservation agriculture and intercropping, from the start to ensure the environmental sustainability of agricultural activities. GOAL will leverage its extensive programmatic experience to provide capacity-building support to national and international partners across the country through cash transfers and resilience programming. The project will be designed based on community level participatory needs assessment, which will inform the needs of all stakeholders (men, women, boys, girls, people living with disability) and project interventions designed to address immediate needs of reducing hunger gaps and mitigate negative coping mechanisms during lean season and crisis times. Interventions aimed at building community assets and capacities will be tailored to the needs and priorities of target beneficiaries. The project is designed to benefit men, women, boys and girls through various interventions including community awareness, Behaviour Change Communication messages and other FSL and resilience.GOAL will focus on integration of nutrition, WASH, and Protection components into programming. GOAL will conduct integrated training to increase the knowledge and practise of positive behaviours which address the drivers of poor nutrition at the household level. Participatory learning activities promoting maternal and child health, which encompasses IYCF, health, and WASH, will enable these drivers of poor nutrition to be addressed. GOAL will also refer beneficiaries in need of malnutrition treatment to relevant local Nutrition actors for SAM or MAM management. GOAL will refer any GBV cases to relevant Protection partners, while taking steps to promote safe access for beneficiaries to its services; GOAL will also conduct refresher training for all staff on safeguarding and PSEA, while also encouraging communities to utilize its feedback, complaints and response mechanism (FCRM) as a resource to give feedback or voice concerns about programming. - Emergency food production: main season and dry season agriculture seeds & tool distribution; fish, vegetable & crop kits- Reducing pest and crop disease e.g. FAW surveillance, detection, prevention & eradication- Training & capacity building: both adaptive and absorptive on-farm production (demonstration plots;), income generation (business skills)- Diversification & restoration of livelihoods; small grants for petty trade; village saving & loan schemes.- Use of local biomass briquettes & fuel-efficient stoves- Livestock vaccination; disease prevention & treatment especially zoonotic e.g. RVF

Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Awareness raising sessions will be conducted regularly within target communities to inform programme participants of GOAL’s Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) across all sites, to ensure communities have a strong understanding of their rights as beneficiaries, what to expect from GOAL programming, how adequacy of interventions is evaluated, and provide an opportunity to address concerns and complaints.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Promotion messaging is tailored to relevant audiences to provide gender and age sensitive information regarding sexual and reproductive health, MIYCN practices, hygiene promotion, among others; these messages are designed to improve community health, nutrition and hygiene practices.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL will incorporate protection from sexual exploitation and abuse in all levels of programming; it will conduct routine PSEA and safeguarding training for all staff to educate on what constitutes sexual exploitation, proper reporting mechanisms if SEA is suspected, and ways to mitigate risk of SEA in programming, to better safeguard beneficiaries at all times; following training, all staff will sign the PSEA, Child Protection, Safeguarding and Code of Conduct policies to reflect their understanding of the consequences of breaches to these policies. During awareness campaigns, special emphasis will be placed on SEA so that communities are well-informed on various methods they may use to report suspected or perceived cases of SEA, which include GOAL’s internal reporting email (speakup@goal.ie), external mechanism (goal@safecall.co.uk) and South Sudan’s countrywide reporting through the IASC PSEA Task Force.

Nature of the consultations

Yes

Briefly describe the nature of the consultations

GOAL conducts periodic review meetings with stakeholders in its field sites to inform them about programme implementation and provide an opportunity for feedback about its projects. These consultations are used to improve subsequent programming and inform project design. Throughout the life of the project, awareness raising sessions will be conducted regularly within target communities to inform programme participants of GOAL’s Feedback, Complaints and Response Mechanism (FCRM) across all sites, to ensure communities have a strong understanding of their rights as beneficiaries, what to expect from GOAL programming, how adequacy of interventions is evaluated, and provide an opportunity to address concerns and complaints about the project.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

There is an existing feedback, complaints, and response mechanism (FCRM) in place, and GOAL will target raising awareness within communities of the FCRM and its use as a vehicle for beneficiaries to voice their feedback or concerns about programming. In particular, GOAL will focus on ensuring vulnerable groups, including women, elderly and disabled persons, are aware of their rights, entitlements, and methods to report issues related to programming, child protection or sexual exploitation and abuse.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL conducts periodic review meetings with communities to discuss programme implementation, achievements, and opportunities for improvement; additionally, community members may utilize the FCRM to provide feedback or complaints, which are routinely monitored for appropriate response.

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL’s programming will focus on enhanced integration of protection into project activities to safeguard beneficiaries. Protection considerations are incorporated from project design through monitoring and evaluation. GOAL operates an FCRM through which beneficiaries may voice any protection or implementation concerns; awareness for the mechanism is raised through community meetings, ensuring participation of vulnerable groups. In 2019, GOAL seeks to extend refresher training and safeguarding training to additional cadres of staff as well as strengthen the referral mechanism to relevant Protection partners. GOAL will also conduct a safeguarding self-audit in all field sites to evaluate existing measures as well as identify areas to expand protection activities; the audit will include standards on child protection (24 standards) and PSEA (27 standards) relating to people (including reporting channels), policies and procedures in place, and accountability mechanisms. Actions will be put in place to increase GOAL's scoring where required.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

The project considers barriers vulnerable groups, including women, the elderly or the disabled may face in accessing services; it seeks to address these barriers through implementation of projects within the communities to provide greater access and participation. Further, the project will consider additional burdens women in particular may face in participating, making attempts to limit the additional time burdens on beneficiaries.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Yes

Briefly describe how

GOAL’s humanitarian responses will routinely assess, analyze and monitor the risks to civilian safety, and take appropriate action to mitigate risks. Programme managers will ensure the programme brings human rights standards and principles to the centre of all interventions, recognizes programme participants as rights-holders, as well as focus on vulnerable and marginalized groups to ensure their safety and dignity. Adequate measure will be put in place at the distribution sites to ensure distribution takes minimal amount of time, including adequate staffing, clear communication to beneficiaries about when to come for supplies, and assessing the area to make sure sufficient supplies are available at the time of distribution. Furthermore, GOAL will ensure that adequate gender-disaggregated latrine facilities are available at facilities where programming is implemented to protect dignity of the users.
