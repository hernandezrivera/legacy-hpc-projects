Project Summary

The Syria Enhancement of Health Assistance (SEHA) Project aims to enhance health assistant provided to the Syrian population by implementing two activities:
Rehabilitation of health centers to increase population access to health services, and providing health supplies to the rehabilitated centers and providing disability devices of people with disabilities.
The SEHA project will benefit 25,000 highly vulnerable individuals, including 7,000 men, 7,000 women, 5,500 boys and 5,500 girls through the restoration of access health services through the rehabilitation of 5 health centers in the governorates of Deir Al Zor, Homs, Idlib and Rural Damascus, and then providing health materials to the same rehabilitated centers. The proposed activities fall within objective 3 of the Health Sector (strengthen health system capacity to support continuity of care, strengthen community resilience,
and respond to IDP movements and changes in context), activity 3.2 indicator 3.2.1 (health facilities rehabilitated), and objective 1 (Increase access to life saving and life sustaining coordinated, equitable humanitarian health services for those most vulnerable and in need) activity 1.5, indicators 1.5.2 (providing medical masks), 1.5.3 (providing N95 masks), 1.5.4 (providing medical gloves) and 1.5.5 (providing protective gowns). ADRA Syria will target 25,000 individuals through rehabilitation of 5 health centers that provide free services to the population, then providing the rehabilitated centers with medical materials to enhance the quality of services provided to the beneficiaries,
The SEHA project will target underserved communities with high IDP population and rural communities with no current access to local health services. Areas with high numbers of vulnerable individuals including women, boys and girls, older adults, pregnant and lactating women in addition to areas with high morbidity rates and health risks will also be prioritized.
Where possible, ADRA will select the areas where ADRA is already present in order to provide an integrated approach including the sectors of WASH, livelihood, food security or education.
Throughout all stages of the project, ADRA will receive feedback from beneficiaries including males, females, boys, girls, the elderly and disabled. ADRA will elicit feedback through multiple means, including direct communication with project beneficiaries (ADRA field officers and MEAL team), constant follow-up with key stakeholders and anonymously via the established ADRA Syria complaints mechanism. This feedback is used to plan project activities, undertake adjustments to ongoing activities, as well as help measure the impact of the activities.

4 - Needs

The ongoing conflict in Syria, in combination with the deteriorating economic condition and COVID-19 pandemic resulted in increased vulnerability of the Syrian population (HNAP Syria 2021), pushing the health system to the edge. By mid-2020, over 7.78 million people in 100 sub-districts were living below three critical emergency health standards (2021 HNO). As of March 2021, over 47,000 confirmed COVID-19 cases across Syria and positivity rates ranging between 24 to 38%, while more than 2,700 health care workers reportedly fell ill in 2020 (2021 mini-HRP for Syria -Health Chapter-draft) but with just 58% of hospitals and 53% of Primary Healthcare Centers are fully functional (2021 HNO for Syria-Health Chapter-draft), forcing already vulnerable individuals to cover long distances in search of adequate health care facilities and services.The health sector PiN has increased in Idleb Governorate by more than 200,000 people, (2021 HNO Syria). In Eastern Rural Deir Al Zor, the lack of functional health services continued causing increased pressure on local health services, particularly in the areas of high IDP and returnee population. Lack of health services are also identified in Rural Damascus and Mheen (Homs) which are conflict-affected hosting communities with a high number of IDPs and returnees. These areas also serve the neighboring areas serving additional patients who are seeking basic health care services that are not available in their home community. Many of these basic services are provided in damaged or neglected health facilities and are in need of rehabilitation.

5 - Implementation of activities

During the selection of locations, ADRA will conduct community-based needs assessments, where possible, through surveys and interviews including feedback from men, women, boys, girls, (children, adolescents, young adults, adults and older adults), people with disabilities, pregnant and lactating women. Based on this feedback, ADRA will ensure the needs of the local population are taken into consideration. ADRA will share the contact number of the complaint mechanism making sure the beneficiaries and stakeholders are aware of it and are able to provide anonymous complaints and to report any feedback to the organization. ADRA will conduct the technical assessment of each health center and will coordinate with the Centre management. The assessment will ensure the centers are accessible for people with disabilities, through installation of a disability ramp and ensure gender segregated disability friendly WASH facilities. ADRA will prepare the BoQs, conduct the procurement process in accordance with ADRA’s Procurement Policy and contract the necessary work. ADRA will rehabilitate lightly damaged centers however, in areas with critical need for medical services, ADRA will consider closed and more heavily damaged centers, provided they fit within the budget and are able to fully reopen. For the disability devises, ADRA will conduct the needs in coordination with SARC and hand the devises directly to the beneficiaries. The same procurement process will be followed as for the rehabilitation of centers. Upon completion, the work will be handed over to either SARC / MoLAE or the MoH who will be responsible for future maintenance works and operating costs. Post-implementation surveys will be conducted to assess the impact.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

1. The risk of inter-community tensions. Community based / local inequality may arise contributing to feeling of exclusion. Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: ADRA will ensure comprehensive service mapping and communities, municipalities and other relevant authorities’ involvement in the selection process.2. Discrimination of people with disabilitiesLikelihood: low. Impact: high.Mitigation: During the technical assessment stage and design of the intervention, ADRA will consult with the management of the health centers to ensure protection mainstreaming component is included. This will include, but not limited to, ensuing that the facilities are easily accessible and disability friendly. Attention to protection will be paid during rehabilitation of the centers to ensure proper marking of the building site to prevent access, especially for children, young adults, teenagers and elderly. The rehabilitated health facilities can be the first point of contact for Gender Based Violence (GBV), sexual exploitation and abuse medium. 3. An increase in GBV if the abuser finds out about the reported cases.Likelihood: low. Impact: high. Mitigation: ADRA will ensure that the ADRA staff present at the center are aware of the risk and know the safe referral pathways in place in the area. The staff will also be trained on communication with persons (male, female, boys and girls) with diverse disabilities. Also, to provide privacy and eliminate the risk of sexual harassment, assaults and exploitation of the staff and patients, the rehabilitated health centers will be equipped with WASH facilities and rooms with lockable doors.

8 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

1. The security situation in the targeted areas deteriorates, preventing access. Mitigation: Regular monitoring of the security situation with UNDSS, INGOs, sectors, stakeholders and staff. 2. Extended delays in fund transfers to Syria or the value of the Syrian Pound deteriorates to a level that activities cannot be implemented within the planned budget. Mitigation: Closely monitor exchange rates, use competitive tender processes, adjust targets or search for additional funding. 3. Necessary permissions are unable to be obtained within a reasonable period of time or do not allow for an effective implementation of the program. Mitigation: Apply for permissions in advance and conduct regular follow-up with the relevant ministries, including high-level meetings where necessary. 4. Qualified staff unable to be retained/recruited in the project area. Mitigation: Recruit early in project life cycle and ongoing capacity building. 5. Planned activities pose protection risks to staff, stakeholders or beneficiaries. Mitigation: Temporarily pause activities when necessary and utilize staff from the target area for monitoring. If the situation does not improve, select a new target area. 6. Undue pressure placed on ADRA staff by stakeholders to prioritize specific locations or activities outside of the selection criteria. Mitigation: Work closely with stakeholders and explain criteria and red lines in the early stages of target area selection. Where necessary, ADRA will coordinate with the sector leads and UN representatives or cease activities until support is granted7. Potential for corruption or fraud, especially with project procurement. Mitigation: Adhere strictly to the ADRA Syria Procurement and Finance Policies
