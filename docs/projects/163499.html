Project Summary

The project will contribute to promoting fair protection process and documentation, where in 2000 civil status documents will be issued and issuance of 12,000 individual documentation (ID Cards) supported. It will enable security from violence and exploitation, focused on reducing the risk of SGBV and improving the quality of response. 60 community working groups on SGBV prevention and response will be established and access to medical assistance by 2000 Victims of SGBV will be supported via cash based interventions. The project will equally provide direct in-kind(Dignity Kits) support to 4000, victims of SGBV and vulnerable women.
The project will involve the provision of protection from the effects of armed conflicts via the provision of protection by presence, protection monitoring and the provision of cash assistance to support most vulnerable POCs negatively affected by armed conflicts. Monitoring activities will cover 80% of the areas affected by the crisis. UNHCR will equally provide leadership and coordination and strengthen partnerships ensuring a target of 20 coordination meetings with 80% participation of protection cluster members. UNHCR will optimize logistics and supply chain to serve operations through transport and fleet management.

Besoins / Needs

All assessment missions to the affected areas indicated that most IDPs are going to the bush depths, where they have limited access to national services, including vital statistics documentation. Children born on the move face a serious protection risk, including the risk of statelessness. As part of the palliative actions, UNHCR is working to support the granting of civil status documents to some 1,500 internally displaced children born in places of displacement. The strategy is to provide authorities with birth registration materials, such as registers, office equipment, and also capacity building and awareness raising for parents on the importance of ensuring that their children are registered by children. authorities at birth. In addition to providing support to the authorities for the issuance of individual documents to displaced persons, UNHCR will deploy advocacy and capacity-building interventions to the authorities in order to sensitize them on their responsibilities regarding the granting of legal documents to citizens. Given the violence that forced people to move, most of the affected populations had to flee their homes, leaving behind their national identity cards. Others lost their identity cards in the ruins of their burnt down houses. They are therefore still at risk of arrest and detention. In addition, they have enormous difficulties in accessing local services and cannot enjoy their basic rights. This objective aims to provide some 9,000 IDPs with identity documents in their place of displacement by providing support (material and technical) to local authorities so that they can issue identity cards to IDPs. Protection monitoring activities carried out in the South West have already made it possible to identify certain victims of sexual and gender-based violence (in particular rape and domestic violence, which are on the increase). IDPs continue to face risks of violence and exploitation and the forced recruitment of children into armed groups. The capacity to respond is very limited, because there is currently no referral mechanism and the victims are deprived of any appropriate medical, psychosocial and legal assistance. The activities aim to provide holistic assistance to victims / survivors of sexual and gender-based violence, as well as the creation and support of community committees / groups working on the prevention and response to sexual and gender-based violence. This is a core responsibility of UNHCR.) Between November 1, 2018 and January 19, 2019, UNHCR protection monitoring recorded a total of 1,756 protection incidents in the two regions, including illegal or arbitrary arrests, summary executions, indiscriminate attacks, looting, burning of properties and entire villages, violence against children and women and gender-based violence, including rape. The first protection monitoring missions revealed an increasing number of people who have been arbitrarily arrested and who are detained in deplorable conditions. This objective concerns advocacy with the authorities and IDPs in order to raise their awareness of fundamental rights and freedom of movement. Twelve advocacy interventions will be carried out to prevent arbitrary detention. In addition 1,200 people will benefit from legal assistance against arbitrary detention. A disturbing number of atrocities involving physical violence have been reported in the two regions, including terrible acts of barbarity and torture during hostage-taking. The balance sheet of these reprehensible acts was, the exponential displacement of populations. Protective monitoring activities have reported numerous incidents of attacks, atrocities, murders, kidnappings and human rights violations, which have resulted in the present crisis. There is a need to rapidly deploy protection personnel to organize protection monitoring missions to displacement areas and to document the situation with regard to the protection of persons of concern.
