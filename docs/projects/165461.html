Project Summary

Mercy Corps actively participates in both WoS and regional-level coordination, and contributes to Syrian crisis reports including the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO) and the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP). Mercy Corps are currently the co-lead agency of the WoS Food Security Sector (FSS) Cluster, chair the Syria INGO Regional Forum (SIRF), and host the Partnership Coordination Advisor for the Beirut based Partnership Coordination Group (formerly known as SPIDER). Through these roles, we have amplified humanitarian advocacy efforts around access, besieged areas, provision of humanitarian assistance, and gender and protection mainstreaming. In 2020 Mercy Corps will increase the utility of coordination by leveraging these platforms to influence policies related to humanitarian assistance.
In 2020, the sector will build on its work of previous years and will focus on reviewing and updating needs periodically, response and gap analysis to operational planning and developing contingency/preparedness plans. The sector will continue to harmonize all aspects of the response such as assessments, analysis, geographical targeting, selection criteria (including targeting youth for livelihoods) and monitoring. The sector will lead cross-learning initiatives on outcome indicators monitoring, response modalities and cross cutting issues. The sector will also deliver on the key outputs for the Humanitarian Programme Cycle as well as feed into strategic discussions and decisions. Inter-sector work will focus on a variety of areas including (1) linkages and collaboration with WASH, NFI, Nutrition, Early Recovery and Protection sectors (2) deeper focus on protection analysis with the inclusion of the protection matrix at sector and projects level and ongoing collaboration with the Protection sector (3) an integration strategy aimed at linking people on food assistance with livelihoods support from other sectors such as the Early Recovery and Livelihoods sector (4) joint needs analysis and advocacy, common messaging to communities and assessing convergence at household level with nutrition and other closely linked sectors (5) improved linkages and coordination with ongoing cash-based transfer activities and to help guide new activities as well as learn from ongoing and past activities.

5 - Implementation of activities

The project will be implemented by Mercy Corps in collaboration with Cluster Lead Agencies, government, UN and NGO partners. The Food Security and Livelihoods INGO Co-Cluster Lead for Whole of Syria is responsible, along with their UN Counterpart for leading the Whole of Syria FSL cluster. This includes setting priorities for FSL work in the region, working to develop content, working with all three hubs to ensure information management harmonization, and representing the FSL cluster in the Inter Sector Cluster Coordination Group. He/She is based in Beirut and travels frequently to all three hubs.

6 - Protection Risk Analysis

Mercy Corps Syria has rolled out a protection risks analysis tool for staff and partners since 2018, as part of the protection mainstreaming strategies. Noting that the Mercy Corps submission is reflective of protection programming incorporated into several donor grants (often multi-sector), each project and key interventions will have a protection risk analysis. The principle of risk analysis and protection assessment is a corner stone to Mercy Corps protection mainstreaming. Mercy Corps staff and partners will be trained on key principles, staff will be gender balanced and complaints followed up on. The following illustrates the main protection risks associated with this program, and mitigating measures that will be taken by Mercy Corps. Mercy Corps acknowledges that there are a significant number of protection risks that individuals and communities can face as a result of poor programming or programming in a difficult environment. This does not capture them all. 1. Risk of exploitation or abuse by staff members/service providers/partners: Mercy Corps monitoring and evaluation systems include accountability frameworks, and Mercy Corps has a beneficiary complaints mechanism. Mercy Corps is committed to the Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse and Child Safeguarding through appropriate policies (Code of Conduct) which are enforced through on-boarding and training to all staff, suppliers and partners. Distribution are carefully planned to ensure they are done at times and in places that are safe and accessible to women and girls, as well as the elderly and people with disabilities, and that any barriers to access as a result of gender norms or physical ability are taken into account when planning for the distribution. 2. Programming exacerbates community tensions due to perceived discrimination/favouritism: Mercy Corps upholds a conflict sensitive and DNH approach throughout programming.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Mercy Corps closely coordinates our contingency planning alongside the Humanitarian Access Team (HAT) which is hosted by Mercy Corps. Along with INSO, OCHA and cluster analysis, Mercy Corps operational planning strives to minimise risk to beneficiaries, partners and staff. 2019 is a challenging operational context in northern Syria. The humanitarian situation in NWS deteriorated in 2018. The establishment of the demilitarised zone (DMZ) in Sep 2018 saw the numbers and frequency of displacement subside, however, inter-AOG conflict in January 2019 has the potential to further de-stabilize the area. According to the HAT’s Dec 2018 Report, “The DMZ Agreement”, there is likely to be a partial or complete breakdown in the political process if key provisions are not fully implemented, leading to a surge in IDP movement. Administered largely by the Kurdish Self Administration (KSA), NES contains pockets under Islamic State in Iraq and ash-Sham (ISIS) control. The large coalition presence is signalling a reduction, with the potential to destabilize a delicate equilibrium between conflict actors which has been an enabler of humanitarian access since 2017. Ongoing clashes in DEZ continue to cause periodic access problems. Tens of thousands of IDPs remain in camps and informal sites, unable to return to their homes. This has meant that there is questions of operational viability across northern Syria in 2019. Mercy Corps remains in close coordination with our donors and partners for appropriate contingency planning to enable and adaptable programming.
