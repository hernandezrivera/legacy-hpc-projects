Project Summary

The project aims at responding to health and nutrition needs across Sudan by providing access to pediatric primary and secondary health care services to children aged 0-14, maternal and reproductive health services, regular malnutrition screenings and supplementary feeding cycles for hospitalized malnourished children. It will take place in Port Sudan (Red Sea State), Nyala (South Darfur), and Mayo (Jabal Awlia district, Khartoum). In Mayo, EMERGENCY (EMR) runs a primary health facility providing pediatric services to children 0-14 and maternal and reproductive health services to women living in the catchment area. The Center, working five days per week, is composed of an observation ward (4 beds, guaranteeing inpatient treatment during the day for children mainly affected by dehydration and respiratory tract infections, thus decreasing the referrals to public hospitals and therefore their workload), 2 outpatient rooms for consultations and follow up, a pharmacy, a lab, an isolation room, a vaccination area and an obstetric sector. In all EMR facilities children are screened for malnutrition through MUAC screening and WHZ, and RUTFs are provided for SAM and MAM children. In Mayo, SAM cases are referred towards Stabilization Centres nearby if no further medical complications are, and towards higher-level health facilities in case of medical complications. EMR has also developed well-structured cooking classes three times per week for the caregivers of SAM and MAM cases run by experienced Community Health Promoters (CHPs). Additionally, EMR will ensure maternal health care services addressed to women of reproductive age living in the Mayo area, through a comprehensive set of reproductive health services such as ante/post-natal care and family planning, plus EPI services for newborns. On top of this and to ensure long-term sustainability and retention, the intervention will also focus on capacity building of national health staff. On the other hand, the Paediatric Centres in Port Sudan and Nyala are primary and secondary health facilities with integrated services. Both hospitals have a triage department, 3 outpatient rooms for consultations and follow-up and auxiliary services such as a pharmacy, a laboratory, a blood bank and an X-ray room. Vaccination services and malnutrition screenings are also carried out. Patients with medical complications and severely malnourished children will be hospitalised in the wards (where they will be given supplementary feeding until the right weight is reached), each of them containing 18 beds. RUTFs will be provided for both SAM and MAM children. Patients who don’t meet the Centre’s admission and treatment criteria will be stabilised and referred through an ambulance service to public facilities and feeding centres, if needed. Furthermore, in Port Sudan EMR is kickstarting ante/post-natal care activities within the premises of the Eskandaria Governmental Hospital, in agreement with the local MoH’s Midwifery Department. In all EMR facilities a specific referral system for children affected by congenital and rheumatic heart disease (RHD) is in place; EMR cardiologists periodically perform ad hoc cardiac assessments, and children who are in need of further specialized consultations are referred to EMR's Salam Centre for Cardiac Surgery in Khartoum where they will be further evaluated in OPD, and potentially admitted in ward for surgery (offered free of charge). Health and nutrition promotion sessions in the facilities and in outreach (always following COVID-19 prevention measures) will ensure long-lasting behavioral changes at local level and the participation of the population in building socially and culturally appropriate practices to improve the general health of the beneficiaries. EMR will encourage active participation of the local community including women in the implementation of the proposed project and will consult women, girls, men and boys, wherever possible.

1 - Needs

The humanitarian situation in Sudan remains complex, with 9.1 million of people in need. In this context, health and nutrition remain extremely important life-saving sectors, especially for children. According to data gathered in need assessments and meetings with SMOH in Red Sea, South Darfur and Khartoum States there are major gaps in the provision of health and nutrition services. The recent macro-economic and social shocks, compounded by the advent of the COVID-19 pandemic, are making the population increasingly vulnerable and forcing local communities to adopt harmful coping mechanisms, such as a reduction in health expenses and in food variety and quality. The nutrition situation in Sudan in 2022 is projected to remain at elevated high levels with overall GAM levels of 13.6%. According to figures from the preliminary phases of the 2022 HNO, there are more than 3 million children under 5 living with severe or moderate acute malnutrition, and the number is bound to keep increasing due to the economic struggles the country is currently facing. According to EMR’s data, around 25-30% of all children examined in our facilities (which are placed in very vulnerable areas) present some degree of malnutrition. Pediatric and maternal health services in Sudan are also critical, including the provision of essentials drugs, vaccinations, malnutrition screenings and referrals. Disability has also a detrimental effect on the health and nutritional status both of under-five children and PLW, most often contributing to their deteriorating status as well as impacting their mental and psychosocial wellbeing. Furthermore, health and nutrition education activities are needed to promote positive behaviors that can reduce malnutrition and the risk of vectorborne and waterborne diseases. Among non-communicable diseases, the burden of rheumatic heart disease (RHD) is to be mentioned, RHD being a preventable condition arising from acute rheumatic fever with an incidence of 1 per 1,000 inhabitants within Sudan. It is the most commonly acquired heart disease in people under the age of 25 across low- and middle-income countries. If neither prevention nor early detection programmes are conducted, specialised surgeries are needed to treat it, and a very low percentage of the population can afford them.

2 - Activities/Outputs

1. Children 0-5 have access to primary and secondary health services: OPD activities will include triage, visits, medications and lab services. Patients identified with RHD symptoms will be referred to the Salam Centre for Cardiac Surgery. In case of other medical complications, the child will be transferred to a public hospital (for Mayo) or admitted in EMR’s wards in Nyala and Port Sudan. CHPs will make sure to follow-up with them once they are discharged. EMR will also provide immunization services to newborns and children. 2. Increased access to maternal and reproductive health services in Mayo and Port Sudan: EMR will target PLW and support maternal healthcare through the provision of comprehensive ANC visits focusing on the identification and management of conditions such as infections and anemia. The ultrasound for fetal heart rate measurement and tetanus vaccination will be carried out. PNC will be provided to all newborns and mothers (at hospital and household level). Women who receive ANC services will be advised on the importance of PNC. FP services, including counselling, evaluation and provision of short and long-acting FP methods will be ensured. FP counselling in clinic will be offered to pregnant and non-pregnant women in childbearing age and women who request the service. 3. Children 0-5 have access to nutritional services: Malnutrition screenings will be performed on all patients through MUAC and WHZ systems, both at hospital and during outreach. RUTFs will provided for SAM and MAM children to stabilize them; children with further medical complications will be admitted in ward and treated with a TSFP; those with no further complications will be referred to the SCs nearby. Admitted children with signs of corneal ulceration will also be given vitamin A. Facilitated access to nutrition facilities for children with disabilities will be ensured through training of CHPs and health staff in the identification of CWDs to pay particular attention to their health and nutritional status, and treatment or referrals will be provided for free depending on the need. Simple cooking classes will also be carried out with caregivers in Mayo to raise awareness on how to prepare healthy and nutritious food with affordable and locally available products; once more room is available to ensure social distancing they will also be organized in Nyala and Port Sudan. 4. Health education sessions are carried out: Health promotion will advise the local population on good health and hygiene practices and encourage parents/caregivers to comply with prescribed treatments for their children and with scheduled follow-up consultations. They will focus on critical areas such as communicable and noncommunicable disease prevention, hygiene practices and nutrition. Specific messages will target PLW. The sessions will be held at health facility level every day and in outreach in the catchment area. 5. Equitable coverage and accessibility to quality referral services are ensured: Primary health services need to be integrated with the secondary and tertiary level in order to provide a continuum of care. EMR will strengthen its referral network, both towards public facilities and to the Salam Centre for cardiac patients that might suffer with congenital and rheumatic heart disease. 6. Health staff is regularly trained: Theoretical and on-the-job training sessions will be carried out for all medical staff and CHPs on topics such as integrated management of childhood illnesses, maternal and child health, disease surveillance and reporting. This well-established methodology has been applied consistently within EMERGENCY programming, and has proven successful in effectively building and consolidating capacities of the national staff. EMR has also concluded the process for the accreditation of its Centres as Paediatric training facilities by the Sudanese Medical Specialization Board, and is currently waiting for the assignment of the first residents.

5.2 - Impact on environment/natural resources

In all locations EMERGENCY ensures proper medical waste management to protect the patients, the communities and the environment. Additionally, following the ﬂoods in 2021, EMERGENCY’s staff observed a further deterioration of the environment situation in Mayo: the lack of waste management provides habitat for harmful animals and insects, drainage channels have been clogged, preventing dirty water from ﬂowing and creating breeding ground for insects. All of this can aggravate the endemic/epidemic potential for outbreaks of vector-borne diseases, such as malaria, and communicable water-borne disease contracted through direct contact with polluted waters. Therefore, during the health promotion activities EMERGENCY will promote hygiene messages that can raise awareness on the negative environmental impact of some.

6.2 - Explain how your project aims to ensure protection mainstreaming during its design and implementation

Ensuring access to high quality and free essential health care is a way to support and strengthen the resilience of vulnerable communities, protecting them from adopting harmful coping strategies that increase their vulnerability to exploitation and abuses (especially women and children). Additionally, the presence of health facilities that are able to respond to health threats such as increased morbidity or potential outbreaks also ensures the protection of communities.

7.2 - Please outline how the project or your agency will inform and interact with inter-agency approaches to accountability to affected people and feedback mechanisms, including SEA and abuse complaints?

In regards to the complaints mechanism, EMERGENCY NGO operates on the belief that beneﬁciaries are best equipped to voice their concerns; therefore, EMERGENCY liaises regularly with community focal points and local authorities to get feedback and to ensure the acceptance of the intervention. EMERGENCY has installed suggestion boxes within its health facilities and has developped a complaints and suggestions sheet to be ﬁlled out by the patients. Assigned staff will be responsible for regularly opening the boxes and, if necessary, directly respond or ﬂag to a higher-level of management. The focal points will provide monthly reports to EMERGENCY HQ on the complaints received. EMR also regularly holds training courses for service providers on AAP in order to ensure that our staff behaves responsibly towards affected populations.
