Building the resilience of conflict- and COVID-19-affected communities in Ukraine’s Eastern Conflict Area through livelihood restoration and socio-economic early recovery

Project Summary

The action will contribute to enhanced resilience of vulnerable communities affected by conflict and the COVID-19 pandemic through livelihood restoration and recovery activities aimed at strengthening food security and supporting employment and self-employment of IDPs, returnees and most vulnerable social groups.
IOM will use a variety of approaches to boost individual, household and community income-generation on both sides of the contact line. All activities will be tailored to the assessed needs, location, socio-political context and level of access granted. IOM will support individuals working in the following economic spheres: retail, production, agriculture and services.
Self-sustenance grants will be directed to communities residing along both sides of the line of contact (LoC), focusing on isolated areas with limited livelihood opportunities. Self-sustenance grants are meant to assist households scale up their self-subsistence activities, which can both enhance their food security and allow them to produce additional food products for small scale income generation.
Support will be provided to micro businesses to boost their income and employment capacities, through the procurement of necessary equipment, improvement of managerial and technical knowledge, as well as coaching and mentoring. The aim of these grants will be to reduce operational costs, maintenance expenditure and also the risk of failure due to sudden shocks in the marked, such as those induced by the COVID-19 pandemic.
To further support most vulnerable conflict-affected communities, IOM will invite SME’s to submit proposals for employment creation and for socio-economic early recovery. These grants will specifically target activities that will enable job creation for most vulnerable social groups in conflict-affected areas, as well as businesses that have a wide impact on the local community.
Lastly, as piloted in response to the COVID-19 pandemic, IOM will continue its MPC support to vulnerable livelihoods beneficiaries.
Additionally, IOM will support beneficiaries in increasing the production capacity of their businesses as well as to ensure that their business remain functioning and less affected by the impact of COVID-19 outbreak, enhancing the quality of their produced goods and provided services through the development of business education plans. After having identified needs, business owners will be able to attend education classes on various topics such as crises management, gender, environment, access to finance, customs rights, official employments, taxes, etc. that will be listed in the education plans agreed with each beneficiary separately. IOM plans to launch a hotline for entrepreneurs who are willing to receive up-to-date information on changes in the legislation, governmental projects for entrepreneurs and taxes. In case the hotline workers will not be able to address the issues and concerns raised by the entrepreneurs, the callers will be redirected to other hotlines run by the government, NGOs, INGOs and UN-agencies.

1 - Needs

The economic stability of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts has been significantly af¬fected by the conflict in eastern Ukraine and more recently further severed by the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic. Up until 2014, the re¬gion was characterised by a large presence of industrial en¬terprises which facilitated employment of the population. However, with the onset of the conflict, the number of ac¬cessible industrial enterprises shrunk. According to the data of the Ministry of Social Policy of Ukraine (MoSP), since 2014 almost half of the 1.4 million registered internally displaced persons (IDPs) moved to government-controlled areas (GCA), leaving their homes and livelihoods. The unemployment rates in the Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts increased from 9 % and 8 % respectively in 2014 to 14 % and 15 % in 2019. Unemployment rates in ECA are almost twice as high as numbers in the rest of the country, a fact which testifies to the higher vulnerability of these conflict-affected oblasts to unemployment. By the end of 2018, 7,967 microenterpris¬es operated in Donetsk Oblast GCA and over 2,797 oper¬ated in Luhansk Oblast GCA, reflecting a dramatic reduction compared to 2013 figures (23,049 in Donetsk oblast and 8,963 in Luhansk oblast respectively). The COVID-19 pandemic and the related risk mitigation measures have caused further damage to the already precarious livelihood of conflict-affected communities in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. The pandemic’s impact has particularly affected youth entering the labour market, now with little or no prospect to succeed, underemployed or in some cases taking jobs that poorly match their technical skills or knowledge. The State Employment Service (SES) data on unemployment in Donetsk and Lugansk oblasts reflect this negative trend, with April 2020 rates recording an increase by 68% in Donetsk and by 34% in Lugansk, compared to the end of April 2019. In addition, the number of newly opened job vacancies in the two oblasts have decreased by 64 per cent and 14 per cent respectively, compared to the same period in 2019. Many of those living areas within the NGCA that witnessed a major return process face extremely high unemployment rates. Based on IOM’s National Monitoring System (NMS) data (June 2020), the main source of income for more than half of the returnees to the NGCA remains retirement and long service pensions (53%). As highlighted in the NMS report, most of the returnees assessed their financial situation as “enough funds for food only” (39%) or “enough funds for basic needs” (45%), while 4% have to limit their expenses even for food. The NMS survey highlights how women have been disproportionately affected by the impact of the conflict and the COVID-19 pandemic, particularly in terms of income-generation and employment opportunities. The May 2020 Gender Responsive Budgeting Project (GRBP), which analyzed the gender aspects of the socio-economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, highlights how sectors that have experienced higher losses as a result of closures due to quarantine measures – including the nonfood retail, hotels and tourism, education, and restaurants – are also those where women are more highly represented. With an overall 31 % increase of the unemployment rate in Jan-Apr 2020 compared to corresponding period of 2019, women account for 57 % of the registered unemployed and only 25 % of those employed. Further, the report highlights that as a result of the existing gender pay gap, women who lost their jobs and income have a much lower amount of savings which disadvantages them more than men in their ability to survive the crisis decently. Based on IOM implementation experience and field observations, many vulnerable households could be relieved from relying on humanitarian support if they were enabled to start or resume a micro-business for family subsistence. In the post-quarantine period, the business sector is expected to play an important role in stabilizing the local economy
