Project Summary

The protracted conflict in Eastern Ukraine is well into its sixth year, and multiple attempts at ceasefires having yielded little respite overall. In 2019 so far, conflict intensity does seem to be on a downwards trend, with a decrease in the number of civilian casualties as compared to earlier years. However, the great majority of conflict-related incidents – 94% (INSO data) – continues to take place within 5km on either side of the Line of Contact (LoC), meaning that populations in these areas continue to be disproportionately affected, on a quasi-daily basis, by the conflict and associated protection risks. REACH has also illustrated how the LoC, in essence a de-facto border, has disconnected GCA urban peripheries from their NGCA urban centres now located in NGCA, disrupting pre-conflict networks and hindering access to markets and services, especially health care and employment. Analyses of humanitarian trends in 2018 and 2019 have confirmed that needs in GCA are correspondingly higher in areas closest to the LoC, especially within 5km. Protection risks related to shelling and mines, food insecurity, damage to property and barriers to accessing basic services (incl. healthcare) are more pronounced for populations living within 5km of the LoC as opposed to those living within 5-20km, and those living beyond 20km (REACH 2018, 2019). Overall, while core humanitarian indicators assessed in 2019 show a slight amelioration as compared to 2018, this is not evenly distributed: populations within 5km of the LoC, a substantial proportion of whom have overlapping vulnerabilities (e.g. elderly with a chronic illness, households with multiple children and members with a disability, etc.), continue to have severe humanitarian needs.
Notably, Ukraine is also experiencing overall inflation, which is reflected in the steady increase in the actual minimum subsistence level: this translates into constantly increasing food prices – 7.8% increase in 2019, projected increase of 6.9% in 2019 (National Bank of Ukraine). Incomes of vulnerable populations, especially those living in the 5km area – noting disproportionate representation of pensioners and pre-pensioners – struggle to follow. The gap between incomes and prices is especially pronounced in winter, with food and fuel costs rising sharply during this season, thus decreasing even further ability to meet basic needs and increasing the use of negative coping strategies.
In response to this situation, ACTED will implement this project in continuation of 2+ years of Multi-Purpose Cash (MPC) distribution, to provide the most vulnerable conflict-affected individuals residing close to the LoC in Donetsk and Luhansk (GCA) with the means to cover their critical needs and protection requirements through MPC grants. Focus will be on the most vulnerable groups, who do not have sufficient income to cover these needs, including women headed households, households of elderly, and households headed by pre-pensioners who are not eligible for pensions. Other vulnerable categories identified through the vulnerability assessment may also be targeted, in line with ACTED’s selection methodology which is based on a cumulative vulnerability approach approved by the CWG and adapted by ACTED to the Ukrainian context.

1 - Needs

While the actual subsistence level has been steadily increasing (34% increase since September 2017), the incomes of vulnerable populations living close to the LoC have not followed. As per analysis undertaken by ACTED for the CWG in October 2018, vulnerable population’s average income is less than half of actual subsistence level (47%). Increasing income gaps hinder the ability of vulnerable populations to meet basic needs, with key informant interviews also highlighting prohibitive transportation (access to markets), healthcare, food and NFI costs as barriers (REACH ABA 2017; ACTED program data 2017-19). ACTED program data suggests larger income gaps for households with particular vulnerabilities: 49% for women-headed HHs, 69% for women headed HHs with children, 38% for HHs of elderly; and 40% for single elderly HHs, of whom 90% are women.Market monitoring data shows prices for food increasing by 12% between August 2018-2019. Other costs also increased, e.g. hygiene (17%) and construction materials (19%). In August 2019, a ton of coal cost 3,786 UAH and a cubic meter of firewood 792 UAH, representing a 56% and 80% increase from August 2017, respectively. Affordability thus remains the main issue to meet basic needs, with 99% of market monitoring respondents reporting availability of food items in August 2019 – prices are perceived as more affordable during the summer than winter. PDM data from ACCESS II shows that beneficiaries spend a majority of the assistance on shelter, followed closely by health (in line with demographics) and food.Indeed, REACH data (July 2018) shows that the proportion of HHs in the 5km area with poor and borderline Food Consumption Scores increased from 13% in 2016 to 16% in 2018, with a peak during winter 2018 at 21%. An estimated 30% of all women headed HHs in GCA have poor or borderline Food Consumption Scores (FSLC, 2018), knowing that food insecurity increases along with proximity to the LoC (REACH, 2018). Notably, the population within 5km of the LoC is disproportionately female (57%), retired (36%) and elderly (33%). Women head 59% of all HHs and 90% of single parent HHs (GCA), and make up 67% of those above 60. Women heads of HHs are more likely to be widowed, and thus isolated, than their male counterparts.HHs with children are the poorest socioeconomic group in GCA (FSL Cluster Analysis, 2018), with children representing 20% of the population in the 5km area (45% girls). Male-headed households with 3 or more children also demonstrate higher levels of vulnerability (ACTED program data).With over 50% of all HHs in the region retired, poverty is driven by very low pensions: while reforms led to pension increases, the great majority of pensioners within 5km of the LoC report that this is insufficient to meet basic needs – in 2018, 79% received pensions that were below actual minimum subsistence level (FSL Cluster Analysis). As noted above, the income gap for HHs of elderly is 38%, and increases to 40% for single elderly HHs – 58% of women headed HHs are in fact single, elderly and isolated women. For HHs living close to the LoC with unemployed members aged 50-59, coping capacity is most stressed as this group struggles to find employment but is too young to receive pensions.REACH data (2018) highlights the prevalence of overlapping needs among conflict-affected populations. This is particularly the case for the 5km area, where 33% of households reported overlapping needs, compared to 15% in the 20 km area and 9% further than 20 km. Rural areas are also more likely to experience overlapping needs, with 19% of rural households overall, compared to 15% of households in small urban areas and 7% of households in large urban areas.Based on this needs assessment, ACTED will target the following, as groups with the lowest ability to meet basic needs:- 60% women;- 37% elderly;- At least 15% children;- 10% with a disability;- 5% IDPs.

2 - Activities & outcomes

Outcome:The most vulnerable individuals close to the line of contact increase their purchasing power and are able to meet their basic needs Multi-Purpose Cash:ACTED will provide 1 round of 3 months of MPC assistance to 2,200 individuals in Donetsk and Luhansk, urban and rural areas within 5km of the LoC. Assistance amount is benchmarked based on CWG recommendation–970 UAH/per/person until revision.Considering the protracted conflict and diversity of critical needs across sectors and affected groups, MPC is a cost-efficient modality to provide rapid and efficient assistance to highly vulnerable populations where banks and markets are functional and accessible. ACCESS II endline analysis shows that MPC distribution can reduce average CSI score by over 30 points; 78% of households reduced their CSI score, with 22% showing non-increasing tendency.99% of ACTED-led market monitoring respondents (under ACCESS, August 2019) confirm availability of food items. Cash is a preferred modality for over 70% of HHs within 5km of the LoC (REACH 2019), and 96% of ACCESS II beneficiaries were satisfied with a cash modality. Under ACCESS I and II, at least 98% of HHs surveyed post-distribution reported satisfaction with amount of assistance received and beneficiary assessment, selection and distribution processes.ACTED will coordinate with CWG partners to avoid geographic overlap and beneficiary duplication. Referrals will be sought from local and humanitarian (incl. protection) actors. Selection is based on cumulative vulnerability approach as recommended by CWG and adapted by ACTED – looking at HH status, gender and age-based indicators. ACTED will ensure through assessment, selection and verification processes that beneficiaries meet vulnerability criteria and that the most vulnerable groups are targeted.For cases of extreme isolation or specific vulnerabilities, ACTED will refer to projects with in-kind modalities and/or protection or sectoral assistance. ACTED will also facilitate referral of eligible beneficiaries to government safety nets programs. Under ACCESS, ACTED will use an adapted assessment methodology to enable referrals to Consortium partners of beneficiaries with particular sectoral needs who require other assistance as well as MPC.ACTED will consult at least 80% of communities targeted. The project and selection criteria will be presented in community meetings to local authorities, community representatives and volunteers to gather and incorporate input and feedback. Communities and individual beneficiaries will be informed about and have access to ACTED’s Complaints and Response Mechanism.Post-Distribution Monitoring will include a beneficiary satisfaction survey and aim to assess whether negative coping strategies have been prevented and to monitor the use of cash: information and feedback collected will be used to adjust subsequent programming.ACTED will use financial service providers (FSPs) to transfer MPC. Beneficiaries are invited to choose their preferred FSP, noting that one FSP has a home delivery system (to mitigate protection risks, reach beneficiaries in remote locations, and address particular vulnerabilities such as for beneficiaries with limited mobility). Beneficiaries already know and are comfortable with the two FSPs that ACTED works through since they distribute pensions. Needs assessments and market monitoring surveys do not suggest particular protection/access issues concerning women which need to be mitigated in target areas. Boys and girls are targeted through heads of HHs, who receive the cash: HHs with significant care responsibilities can choose to receive the cash at home. In-kind modalities would not provide additional mitigation of protection and safety concerns, would be less cost-effective, and may do harm to local markets which are currently functioning. For beneficiaries for whom cash is not suitable or who wish to receive other assistance, ACTED will make referrals.
