Project Summary

IOM plans to provide a protection response for vulnerable IDPs, returnees and migrants through various targeted protection activities.
In addition to IOM's existing emergency programs which focus on the distribution of shelter kits and non-food items, IOM's protection response in 2023 will focus on:
- Counter-trafficking activities through awareness campaigns and referrals in areas most prone to trafficking and exploitation
- Improving access to housing, land, and property
According to IOM's DTM, as of October 2022, the number of internally displaced persons (IDPs) in all 18 provinces stood at 75,300 (17,128 households) with a majority of women (55%) and children (57%). Most of the IDPs were living with host communities (95%), the rest was hosted at sites (5%) ). Among those IDPs, 8,283 were Burundian nationals who had been displaced outside the country and have since returned (spontaneously or assisted) to Burundi but continue being in displacement. In addition to these IDPs, Burundi has received 71’449, returnees and will receive more this year 2023.
In 2023, 235,765 individuals are in urgent need of protection according to the sector, among which 10’805 VoT, 67’423 people at risk or victims of GBV, and 18’161 persons in need of support to document their
IOM's proposed response is part of the protection sector's 2023 strategy and will aim to address Objectives 1.3.2; 1.3.3; 1.3.4 and 2.1.1.
In order to meet objective 1.3.2 of the protection sector strategy to provide people (women, men, girls, boys, elderly and people living with disabilities) affected by shocks with dignified assistance adapted
In line with objective 1.3.2 and 1.3.4, IOM as leading counter-trafficking agency, will conduct prevention campaigns, in partnership with the authorities in the most at-risk areas, capacity building of governmental and humanitarian actors, building on the efforts already carried out by IOM in 2022 and building on the existing referral system for assistance to 750 victim of trafficking.
Finally, in line with objective 2.1.1, IOM will support vulnerable people seeking administrative documents and support to obtain civil documentation of their land, housing or property through administration and/or legal assistance for 18,161 most vulnerable people. 10,661 vulnerable individuals will be supported in obtaining land certificates by prioritizing people with specific needs, while 7,500 individuals will participate in training-information sessions on the right to access land and the importance of respecting land-use standards during construction. IOM's approach to the implementation of this project is based on the "do no harm" and "leave no one behind" approach.
