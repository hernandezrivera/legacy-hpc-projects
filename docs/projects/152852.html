Project Summary

In line with the S/NFI Cluster strategic objectives, particularly objective 1 (improve access to safe, appropriate emergency shelter and life-saving non-food items to newly displaced or vulnerable populations in secondary displacement) and objective 2 (improve the living conditions of highly vulnerable IDPs in protracted displacement, returnees and host communities unable to meet their S/NFI needs), ACTED will provide emergency shelter and non-food items to the most vulnerable displaced populations and host communities impacted by conflict in an efficient and effective manner in Jonglei, Western Bahr el Ghazal and Western Equatoria states, specifically targeting Akobo, Wau, Jur River, Raja and Yambio counties. Through this project targeted beneficiaries will be given shelter and non-food items either in-kind or through vouchers, or multi-sectorial survival kits depending on their needs and location (e.g. hard to reach areas), to enable quick responses to meet their immediate needs. Cash Based Programming (CBP) will be critical to implementing this programme, and in particular vouchers will be preferred in areas where there is a functioning market and the items are available, and where access humanitarian partners is more consistent. ACTED will utilise the different modalities depending on the context and the needs of the area, as highlighted during assessments that will be conducted.

Needs

In 2018 Akobo remains an opposition held territory, & has experienced significant insecurity, linked to the national level political conflict between the SPLA & SPLA-IO, as well as inter-communal conflict. Following an influx of IDPs into Akobo in 2017/2018, 3,615 IDP households were registered in Akobo (WVI and ACTED). A significant challenge for IDPs is shelter & NFIs, with IDPs arriving empty handed. An assessment conducted by ACTED in early 2018 found that shelter was the primary need for 55% of IDPs, with very few having access to safe shelter. Moreover, the most vulnerable IDPs were staying in irregular settlements, in overcrowded temporary shelters, exposing them to additional protection risks. Based on the assessment, the most needed NFIs included blankets, mats, plastic sheets, cooking utensils, & jerry cans. A number of IDPs remain particularly vulnerable, as they have not integrated into the community & therefore have no community support. ACTED is one of very few organisations that has maintained a longstanding presence in Akobo and has a fully functioning base, and is highly accepted by the local community, and with a positive relationship with the local authorities. In Akobo, ACTED is also the S/NFI Cluster focal point, has conducted a number of assessments, and is currently conducting an S/NFI response. Due to insecurity & clashes across WBeG, especially around Deim Zubeir, Baggari, Mboro, Bazia & Gedi in 2018, there have been high movements & influxes of IDPs, however access for humanitarian partners has been severely restricted, resulting in gaps in humanitarian aid & basic services. Needs are reported to be high in a number of different sectors, including S/NFI, as many IDPs are reported to be living under trees/ in bushy areas, and many report not having access to essential NFIs (including plastic sheeting, mosquito nets, mattresses & kitchen sets), having fled without their belongings or assets (largely due to the looting of their settlements by armed actors), & are dependent on the host community & left with little to no access to basic services & shelter, which puts further strain on the already limited local resources (REACH). Between 1-20% of assessed settlements reporting 50% or more of shelters damaged due to conflict, and 41-60% reporting presence of IDPs in Wau County (REACH September 2018). It was reported that following clashes in early 2018 access to basic services (including food, shelter, clean water, markets, & education) for those who remained in Deim Zubeir was restricted, especially for those displaced in the bush nearby (REACH April 2018). ACTED has a base in Wau Town which conducts activities across WBeG, and has the operational capacity to increase its projects. In addition, ACTED is currently conducting FSL and S/NFI activities in the targeted locations, and has significant local contextual knowledge and experience, especially in distributions. Yambio has hosted IDPs from across WES & other states since mid-2015. In late 2016, violence also broke out in Yambio & its surroundings, & has since displaced thousands from Yambio Town & resulted in high food insecurity (REACH), with 61-80% of settlements assessed reporting presence of IDPs in September 2018. Thousands more are estimated to be displaced within communities & in smaller sites. REACH Data from March 2018 noted that a large proportion of shelter has been destroyed, with 15% of settlements assessed across Greater Equatoria reporting that shelters had been destroyed or partially destroyed in the previous month. Access to conventional shelters (such as tukuls) was reported to be low for IDPs, who mostly stay in abandoned buildings, community buildings, tents or rakoobas, which are more vulnerable to weather & rain. ACTED opened a base in Maridi in March 2018, and is currently conducting FSL activities in Maridi and Ibba. ACTED has the capacity to expand its presence in WES, and is targeting Yambio where the S/NFI needs are high.

Activities

Output 1: Emergency S/NFI kit distribution for recently displaced populations, through in-kind, or a voucher system for protracted displacementBeneficiaries: 30,000. Activity 1: Provide access to life-saving NFIs through in-kind or vouchers.An in-kind NFI kit will be distributed to 3,600 households (21,600 beneficiaries). The kit items will be based on the cluster’s standardised items and guidelines, but will be tailored to the specific needs of the IDPs/ beneficiaries based on assessments of needs that have been conducted, to ensure that their immediate S/NFI needs are met. The in-kind items will be procured through the S/NFI Cluster pipeline, in accordance with the S/NFI cluster guidelines. Beneficiaries will be targeted and selected based on vulnerability criteria to ensure that the most in need IDPs and host communities are reached. In locations where there is a functioning market and local supply of resources, a voucher approach will be utilised to stimulate economic activity and support the local market, as well as provide relative flexibility for the beneficiaries regarding which NFI items they prefer. Activity 2: Provide access to emergency shelter through vouchers or in-kind. 1,000 HH (6,000 beneficiaries) will be supported with a shelter voucher to enable them to have the resources to be able to build their own shelter using locally sourced items and materials. This methodology is being used based on availability, to ensure quick responses and to give beneficiaries the dignity to build their shelter to their specific needs and preferences. ACTED will provide guidance and support regarding materials, structure sizes (targeting sphere standards), protection considerations, identifying spaces and liaising with host communities etc. in situations where beneficiaries are unable to construct their own shelters, support will be provided through a CfW approach. In case there is a shortfall in the local market, shelter items will be procured through the S/NFI Cluster pipeline and distributed in-kind. Activity 3: Provision of NFIs and multi-sectorial survival kits to vulnerable populations through rapid response to hard to reach areas. ACTED will target 400 HH (2,400 beneficiaries) to distribute NFI and/ or multi-sectorial survival kits (procured through the S/NFI Cluster pipeline) to the most vulnerable populations to meet their most immediate FSL, WASH, S/NFI and other needs. This is a rapid emergency response activity, targeting locations with severe gaps in humanitarian services as well as where there is ongoing insecurity and access constraints which limit the amount of time humanitarian partners can spend on the ground in the locations. ACTED has a Cash/ Vouchers TC who will support with establishing the value and composition of the S/NFI vouchers, and will ensure that proper procedures and best practices are utilised. ACTED will conduct market assessments in the targeted cash/ voucher locations to ensure that the transfer values are appropriate and tailored to the local market and context. Market vendors will be engaged to agree on the cost of items in the vouchers. ACTED has significant experience conducting cash/ voucher programming in South Sudan, with projects in Maban, Akobo, Bor, Mingkaman and Maridi in 2018 alone covering CFW, CFA, unconditional emergency cash assistance, and GFD through vouchers. Given that women are disproportionally affected by displacement and specifically S/NFI needs due to the increased protection risks associated with inadequate shelter, privacy and overcrowding, women and girls will be targeted and prioritised throughout all phases of our response, and will be instrumental in the selection and distribution of items, as NFI kits and vouchers will prioritise the needs of women and girls. ACTED’s staff have been trained on protection, gender equality and sexual exploitation, and in addition will be supported with technical guidance by ACTED’s Protection Manager based in Juba.

Accountability to affected population
Does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

Yes

How does the project provide beneficiaries with information?

A community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach will be employed throughout the project to ensure that the affected population and beneficiaries are informed and included throughout all stages of the project, particularly regarding the objectives and activities of the project. In addition, the CBPP will ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community in each of the project locations, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. The CBPP approach will ensure that the project is accountable, accepted, and owned by the targeted population.

Are messages context-specific or/and tailored?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Messages are context specific, as they take into consideration the different contexts and dynamics of each project location, as well as the type of beneficiary (e.g. new or protracted IDP). In addition, the CBPP and Project Management Committees are representative of the different gender and age groups (male, female, youth, elderly), and specific information and messages are targeted towards specific groups.

Protection from sexual exploitation and abuse

Yes

Briefly describe how

ACTED will ensure that protection mainstreaming is included throughout all phases of the project. A focal point for protection and gender complaints will be established in each location, to address the targeted communities’ concerns and queries regarding protection and gender equality. Furthermore, during verifications, distributions and assessments/ monitoring, ACTED will ensure that the specific needs of women and girls are taken into consideration and prioritized. ACTED’s staff have all been trained on protection, gender equality and sexual exploitation, including on how to identify and address any issues or concerns, and in addition will be supported with technical guidance by ACTED’s Protection Manager based in Juba. Moreover, ACTED has a strong code of conduct and policy on sexual exploitation and abuse which all staff are required to review, agree to and sign.

Feedback /complaint mechanisms?

Yes

Briefly describe the mechanisms

ACTED has an independent Appraisal, Monitoring and Evaluation Unit (AMEU) with a dedicated Accountability Officer based in Juba will set up a Complaints and Response Mechanism (CRM) in all project locations, and oversee reporting for the project to ensure beneficiaries can objectively report complaints and feedback. Standardized CRM tools, including set SOPs and sensitivity levels, as well as the training of project staff on collecting and responding to complaints, and sensitization of the community in accountability practice will be utilized. All complaints will be compiled and reported on each month and the Accountability Officer will ensure that follow up is conducted by the relevant people. The CRM will also ensure accountability to beneficiaries’ and build trust between ACTED and the community, and improve the quality of our programmes. Complaints can be lodged through complaints desks/ boxes set up at distribution and project sites, over the phone to the designated CRM hotline, or directly to ACTED's staff. The beneficiaries and local community through awareness sessions, as well as the CBPP process.

Community Engagement?

Yes

Briefly describe how

Accountability to affected populations and community engagement will be considered at all stages of the project. A community based participatory planning (CBPP) approach will be employed in all locations to ensure that the beneficiary selection process is transparent and accepted by the community, with the most vulnerable populations targeted. As outlined above, the CBPP will ensure the transparency, accountability, acceptance and ownership of the project for the targeted community. Project Management Committees will be established in each location to ensure that there is ongoing communication and coordination between the beneficiaries and ACTED. The PMCs will monitor the project activities from the beneficiaries' perspective, and provide feedback and identify issues through meetings with ACTED. In addition, post distribution monitoring/ rapid assessments will also be conducted over the course of the project, and will serve as another form of AAP by evaluating the effectiveness of the project in addressing the beneficiaries’ most immediate needs, according to the beneficiaries themselves, as well as their level of satisfaction. The PDM will consist of representative surveys, as well as key informant interviews (KIIs) with beneficiaries to gather a more in depth body of information. ACTED will utilize and follow the S/NFI Cluster South Sudan AAP tools (e.g. Rapid PDM guidelines and questionnaire).

Protection Risk Analysis?

Yes

Briefly describe how

ACTED will ensure that protection mainstreaming is included throughout all phases of the project. ACTED will ensure that the most vulnerable IDPs are prioritized for selection, based on vulnerability criteria including household size/ number of dependents, income opportunities, and having persons with disabilities, persons with special needs, and the elderly. Similarly, ACTED will ensure that the needs of vulnerable groups, including persons with disabilities (PWDs), persons with special needs (PSNs), and the elderly, are prioritized and addressed during distributions. For example, ACTED will have a designated protection staff at each distribution that will identify and fast track beneficiaries that are identified as being particularly vulnerable, such as PWDs and PSNs. Moreover, ACTED will aim to, wherever possible/ feasible, conduct last mile distributions to the most vulnerable beneficiaries that are unable to reach the distribution site. Furthermore, the needs of particularly vulnerable groups will be considered and prioritized during S/NFI kit composition, following the verification of beneficiaries and assessment of needs. Additionally, by targeting shelter needs, S/NFI voucher and kit distributions aim to improve the shelter conditions of beneficiaries, and the improvement in shelter will contribute to improving their immediate protection and enhance their security, especially at night. In particular, inadequate shelter and overcrowded shelters pose high protection risks for females due to lack of privacy and security. Therefore, improving shelter will especially contribute to improving the protection of women and girls. In addition, as this project involves a cash/ voucher component, a risk and mitigation matrix will be utilised to ensure that the cash/ voucher activities and methodology "do no harm" to beneficiaries and that all risks are analysed and addressed to ensure the safety and security of beneficiaries.

Barriers to Access?

Yes

Briefly describe how

As the project is largely targeting IDPs and areas that have recently experienced insecurity or are in close proximity to areas of insecurity, there are a number of potential barriers to access. Firstly, ACTED may have restricted and limited access to the various locations targeted by the project due to insecurity, which could delay or prevent the implementation of the activities. To mitigate this, ACTED will monitor the security situation closely, and advocate with local authorities for access if/ when safe to do so to ensure that the project is implemented. In addition, the targeted IDPs could become displaced again over the course of the project due to further insecurity or their lack of access to basic services, which could prevent them from accessing the project sites and benefitting from the project activities.

Addresses inequalities or protection threats?

Moreover, the project targets the most vulnerable populations with the highest shelter and NFI needs.
