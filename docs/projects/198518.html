Project Summary

ACTED's proposed intervention will aim to strengthen humanitarian coordination, information management and assessment support will be provided to the humanitarian community by IMPACT Initiatives, through its initiative REACH. Within the proposed Action, the goal of REACH is to improve the quality of strategic decision making within the humanitarian coordination structure, and enhance decisions made by individual organisations by providing an improved evidence base. Through a Humanitarian Situation Monitoring (HSM) in hard-to-reach (H2R) areas and area-based assessments in areas of returns, REACH will improve the overall efficacy of the response, ensuring the correct prioritisation choices are made and strategies deployed. It will also increase the capability of all NGOs and other partners in their everyday decision making by providing up to date, and easily digestible multi-sector analysis on the situation.

1 - Needs

The crisis in Northeast Nigeria is now in its 12th year, with over 2 million people displaced across the three states of Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa (BAY states). Across the three crisis-affected BAY states, 8 million people out of a total of 13 million – more than one in two people – needed humanitarian assistance in 2022. Over 80 per cent of IDPs are in Borno State, the epicenter of the crisis. One in four internally displaced people are under five, and 79 per cent are women and children. Over 60 per cent of IDPs are living in host communities, making it harder to access them with assistance and putting additional pressure on the already stretched resources of these communities. Additionally, UNOCHA estimates that 1.2 million individuals remain isolated in areas inaccessible to the humanitarian response, with 1 million residing in Borno State. These individuals have limited access to basic services such as health care and education. Specific conditions and needs of the population in Hard to Reach (H2R) areas remain unknown, and population figures, as well as demographic composition and status (non-displaced, IDP, returnee), are formulated with little actual knowledge of the situation. Given the potential for critical levels of humanitarian needs in H2R areas highlighted by REACH’s assessments, it is evident that the lack of regular, reliable and predictable information on the scale of population, their needs and conditions in these areas is limiting the capacity of humanitarian organizations to both advocate for access and appropriately plan a response. To be able to properly deliver aid in accessible displacement locations to serve the needs of the newly displaced, a better understanding of the needs of arriving populations, as well as the potential scale and timing of influxes of new arrivals, is needed across the 3 BAY States.

2 - Activities

The proposed Action has been designed according to the needs identified in Nigeria by the Humanitarian Needs Overview (HNO), REACH’s multi-sector needs assessment, and through communication with OCHA, sector leads and donors. Its main aim is to facilitate evidence-based understanding of populations, needs and their conditions, and support evidence-based planning and programming for 60 humanitarian actors in North East Nigeria through information management and coordination support.

8.6 - Please describe briefly the modalities your project will put in place to ensure protection mainstreaming (particularly referring to the above questions).

Key protection principles have informed the design of the project and will be actively present as a core component of implementation. ACTED will ensure that activities conducted prioritize the wellbeing, safety and dignity of beneficiaries at all times. Activities will avoid incurring any harm, will ensure meaningful access to CCCM assistance, and will promote accountability, participation and empowerment of beneficiaries. 9.5 - How will your project ensure feedback is collected from the most vulnerable community members?

ACTED’s established Complaints and Response Feedback Mechanism (CRM), mainstreamed throughout all interventions in Northeast Nigeria, facilitates safe, accessible and transparent feedback channels for affected populations. ACTED’s CRM enables beneficiaries to voice concerns, complaints and suggestions, while informing ACTED’s partner coordination efforts at camp-level and thereby increasing overall accountability of service providers and the wider humanitarian response. ACTED’s trailed and tested CRM provides an effective two-way communication channel, ensuring direct links to target populations, and inclusion of beneficiary and broader community voices in program implementation. Further, information gained through the CRM is looped back into the design of program approaches, as ACTED continuously adjusts intervention activities according to feedback received. All ACTED staff will be strictly compliant with the code of conduct, and any breach of the code of conduct uncovered through the CRM will be responded to with serious measure.
