Project Summary

Since west Mosul (Ninewa Governorate) was recaptured from ISIL in July of 2017 and Al-Shirqat (Salah ad Din Governorate ) in September 2016, displaced families have begun to return to their areas of origin to rebuild their livelihoods and communities. Unfortunately due to the massive destruction left behind after the conflict, many do not have the resources needed to safely return in a dignified manner. This project focuses on improving the WASH conditions of vulnerable returnees and host communities in West Mosul and Center East Shirqat.
COOPI will work on the rehabilitation of WASH facilities (in schools), network and WTP, providing water supplies, cash for work for the rehabilitation in order to restore basic water and sanitation services. This intervention will facilitate takeover of care and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities by local authorities. Special attention will be paid to identify the barriers and the areas with severe shortages of drinking water. Incorporation of the research with local information provided by various stakeholders and a need assessment will identify the most reasonable, cost-effective, and practical solutions. To maximise the effects of the intervention COOPI will also organise a distributions of vouchers for NFI in order to encourage and sustain good hygiene practices but also to boost both the supply and demand side of local markets.Special attention will be paid to no discrimination, balancing the number of toilets in schools by GENDER and adding innovative (portable prefab toilets) toilets for students with disabilities. To tackle the issue of water quality in public places of interest, COOPI will pilot cost-effective, innovative and durable solutions to ensure water safety at school level. Beneficiaries will be involved directly in the quality monitoring so as to facilitate the phasing-out of the assistance and foster the long-term overall sustainability of the intervention.

Needs

The protracted conflict in Iraq caused a massive damage of water and sanitation infrastructures. Available resource and capacities have been drained in supporting displacement populations but also in some cases sustaining strained existing functional infrastructures. This has resulted in insufficient access to potable water and inadequate sanitation services of the affected population in many districts, slowing down the reconstruction and the returning home of IDPs. Although most of the damaged water supply infrastructures in re-taken areas of Iraq have been partially or fully rehabilitated in 2017-18, WASH services are still of major humanitarian concern in many areas of Salah Al-Din Governorate. During the field assessment conducted in East Shirqat in October 2018, COOPI team collected evidences of outstanding WASH vulnerabilities which could be mitigated by complementing Government’s efforts in the areas of return. A majority of piped water is not safe for drinking and lack of filters and depuration system prevent access to safe and clear water. The high majority of respondents declared that displaced population get their water from the municipality water network, 94% in West and 90% in East. In Shirqat East village of Al Sidder the population also gets the water from the river and from truck in Hugnah. Due to loss of livelihoods, many do not have the financial resources to afford water services form the private sector. all the areas showed evidences of inadequacy of the existing WASH infrastructures, especially Water Networks, WTP, pumps and tanks and drainage systems. Moreover, after surveys with women, from young adults to elders, of the areas, the issue of lack and need of basic Hygiene items for personal and domestic use has been raised (74%). In West Mosul, due to heavy damage to educational infrastructure, many schools still have to face deep WASH gaps and challenges, which continue to have adverse impact on health and environmental situation and further exacerbating difficult living conditions of children. According to the rapid assessment conducted by COOPI across several schools in 5 neighbourhoods in West Mosul (17 Tamooz, Mashirfa, Al Rifai, Al Najar, Al Jawsaq), almost all facilities showed evidences of inadequacy of the existing WASH infrastructure: damages to water tanks, WC siphons and stalls, basins and taps, lack of water pumps, drainage systems, as well as insufficient numbers of toilets, in particular for students with disabilities, or garbage containers were observed. The aim is to accelerate the early recovery of these sensitive structures – which are representing important aggregation points for the returned population.

Activities

The focus of the interventions will be to restore basic water and sanitation services and support good hygiene practices for only highly vulnerable populations in areas of return and facilitate takeover of care and maintenance of water and sanitation facilities by local authories. • Carrying out limited rehabilitation of dysfunctional water and sanitation infrastructures to facilitate return of displaced and handover facilities to local authorities and/ recovery actors for full rehabilitation/completion.• Carrying out temporary (water trucking, temporary latrines, solid waste facilities etc. while sustainable longer term interventions are being established).• Promoting good water quality through monitoring water supply distributions and service provision, testing monitoring and reporting water quality results.• Providing integrated WASH interventions (WASH in Schools and health facilities) to increase impact and coverage. Carry out limited environment clean up through support to proper municipal solid waste disposal and disposal, drainage management and wastewater treatment. • Restoring and extending sanitation services and facilities including durable solution to wastewater treatment/disposal and solid waste management and disposal systems and agreeing on a timeline and plan for the orderly transfer of operations and maintenance to local authorities and/recovery partners.• Strengthening community based hygiene promotion and water conservation practices through appropriately established approaches and community based structures.• Expanding market-based approaches to hygiene to phase out in-kind distribution of hygiene items for returnees. Moreover, the intervention aims to promote sustainable and cost effective water and sanitation services including community focused hygiene promotion in and out of camps locations. • Introducing cost effective, innovative and durable solutions for water supply provison, this include use of solar pumps, water network systems etc.• Expanding integrated WASH interventions ( WASH in schools, health facilities, cash/vouchers) to strengthen community resilience, and increase impact and coverage of WASH service deliveries.Introducing sustainable management of water resources through integrated water management approaches to conserve available water sources interms of quality and quantity. • Expanding market-based approaches to water supply provision to the affected population (IDPs/returnees/Host communities). Where appropriate, strengthen both the supply and demand side of key WASH markets to improve pricing, availability water supply item and quality of service.• Building and strengthening capacity of WASH actors and community for management of water resources capturing lessons learned.

Protection Risk Analysis

Increased insecurity in the area, new outbreaks of conflicts or severe tensions between returnees and host communities (discrimination, affiliation accusations) prevent humanitarian access to the select facilities.

Mitigation Measures

Security assessments and weekly check with INSO and local authorities will monitor constantly the security issues and provide eventual contingency plans.

How is your project accountable to affected population

During the assessment and project design phase, community engagement ensured that the affected population was involved in determining the beneficiary selection process and which WASH facilities and activities are the most relevant. Before the distribution of NFI vouchers a market assessment will take place as well as Focus Groups sessions with selected beneficiaries in each area to identify the NFI to insert in the vouchers list; after the distribution PDM activities, surveys and data collecting, will be held in order to measure the benefits of the distributions and receive a direct feedback from the beneficiaries. Accountability to the population is ensured through intense monitoring and evaluation but also thorough coordination with local authorities and stakeholders. Besides being available in the field on a daily basis, COOPI staff will organise biweekly sessions with local stakeholders and beneficiaries’ representatives selecting random beneficiaries for every school where activities will take place, in order to collect beneficiaries’ impressions about the works in progress. Furthermore, COOPI staffs will periodically visit and interview beneficiaries with respect to on-going activities, in order to facilitate the collection and registration of any possible complaint. In addition, feedback boxes will be available with the local mukhtars.
