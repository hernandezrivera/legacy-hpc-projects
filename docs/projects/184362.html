Project Summary

COVID 19, changing climatic conditions that cause floods and drought, displacement/migration, limited access to health facilities/services and poor nutrition status remain the leading contributors to poor health status of populations in target districts. Afmadow and Kismayo districts have chronically high levels of child and PLW acute malnutrition beyond the emergency threshold , which significantly contributes to poor health status due to the synergistic relationship between nutrition and disease. Besides this, limited access to health facilities and services, limited capacity and frequent pipeline breaks contribute as additional system aggravating factor (UNICEF, 2021).
Baidoa IDP remain some the most vulnerable in Somalia (Reach IDP profile 2019). IDP numbers continue to increase due to factors like: conflict, insecurity, flooding and drought among others.
WRRS is currently implementing health and nutrition interventions in Afmadow and Kismayo that it intends to sustain in 2022. In Baidoa, WRRS is involved in camp management covering most new IDP. Having identified health gaps in those camps, the organization plans to scale up and include health interventions in the camps where health services are unavailable.
In all target areas both fixed sites and mobile teams will be used to deliver services. Mobile teams will cover rural areas where services hardly reach so as to increase coverage, while fixed facilities will cover towns and IDP camps where facilities and services are within a walking distance. CHW will also be employed to ensure that the population is encouraged to use these services and offer some services at village/community level in a bid to improve coverage and enhance follow-up. Use of mobile teams and CHW will also work to enhance protection, mostly for women and children, who face multiple vulnerabilities while walking long distances to access health services. WRRS has a prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse policy which all staff and contractors will sign and adhere to in order to create a protected environment for all to work.
Proposed interventions in all target areas will integrate nutrition, food security, WASH and protection activities.
Project will also include aspects of mental and psychological health, management of rape, identification and referral of protection victims, prevention of COVID 19 and hygiene promotion with distribution of hygiene kits and risk communication.
Proposed interventions will also include all gender groups, marginalized groups and the disabled who are often left out or given unequal treatment.
Direct beneficiaries will include 11240 women with: 100 for post rape clinical management and GBV services, 1340 for ANC and delivery, 1990 with PNC, 3700 for outpatient consultations, 200 female headed households with hygiene kits and 4010 with RCC. 17741 Children, 130 with PRCM and GBV services,7240 with measles and Penta 3 vaccine,1340 with BCG vaccine,531 with treatment of complicated severe acute malnutrition and 4300 with RCC.
Indirect beneficiaries will include 12500 men with outpatient consultations, 500 male headed households with hygiene kits, 30 men for GBV services and 12500 with RCC , and 9 ministry of health officials involved in monitoring and supportive supervision.
Of these 320 (90 men,90 women, 40 boys , 40 girls and 60 elderly) will be disabled who have been given access to treatment, with 100 (30 men,30 women, 10 boys , 10 girls and 20 elderly) mentally disabled for mental health and psychosocial support.
All these will be done through 5 health centers, 4 PHU and 2 mobile teams. Both facility based staff and CHW will be trained on various aspects to build their capacity.
Disabled will participate at all levels of the project cycle. Staff will be trained on disability Inclusion as to to eliminate discrimination. CHW will work with the community to identify Disabled who need services. All data will be disaggregated according to Age, sex and disability.
