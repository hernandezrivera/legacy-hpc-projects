Project Summary

The overall aim of this project is to increase access to second-chance education (SCE) and employment readiness learning opportunities among 14-25 year old Rohingya refugee- as well as host community adolescent girls and young women who have either dropped out of or lacked access to educational opportunities, contributing to enhanced achievement of quality learning and employability outcomes among these groups.
The project builds on UN Women’s global SCE Programme-approach, while the content of the learning activities has been contextualized for the situation in Cox's Bazar and in the host community, adapting the approach to the current learning frameworks used in the camps and in Bangladesh respectively. With empowerment of vulnerable women and girls through learning and education opportunities at the centre, the project builds upon and expands UN Women's established expertise to address the urgent needs of women and young women in crisis through UN Women's Leadership, Empowerment, Access & Protection in Crisis Response (LEAP) model, which promotes resilience and the need to restore dignity as well as to provide durable solutions to refugees and displaced women, their families, and communities, including SCE for marginalized and displaced women.
Activities under this project will be implemented in the dedicated learning facilities located within the five UN Women Multi-Purpose Women's Centres (MPWCs) in camps 3, 4, 4-Extension, 5 and 18, and through community outreach, and through three UN Women MPWCs in the host communities in Ukhiya, Teknaf and Cox's Bazar Sadar. Designed as an entry point for holistic, multi-sectoral support for the most vulnerable women and girls within the Rohingya refugee camps and the host community, the MPWCs provide a safe and empowering woman and girls only space within the camps and the host community, including for learning and skills development.
The learning pathways that will be provided include basic literacy (in Bengali-for host community and Burmese and English for the Rohingya community); numeracy, financial literacy, and digital literacy (IT skills, online business etc.); and management skills. Learning activities also include life-skills development, covering inter alia awareness-raising sessions on gender-based violence, negative consequences of early and child marriages, menstrual hygiene and sexual and reproductive health, and human trafficking. The curricula are aligned with education sector standards, the International Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE) Minimum Standards, and Inter-Agency Standing Committee (IASC) standards for mental health and psychosocial support in emergencies. The curriculum includes a framework and detailed lesson plans for educators, while feedback is sought from the community through a 'feedback and learning loop', which is aligned with the Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP) developed by the Communications with Communities (CwC) Working Group.
Project activities also include computer literacy and handicrafts (embroidery, block-boutique, toy/ornament making) and basic cooking and food processing, which will be expanded further based on existing learning and skills development needs assessments for women and girls, conditional to funding availability.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs

Roughly half of the Rohingya refugees in the camps comprise school age children and while access to education and quality of education have significantly increased since 2017, major challenges remain. Barriers persist particularly for older Rohingya students, especially adolescent girls, due to the lack of tailored educational opportunities and repressive gender norms and practices such as child marriage, exacerbated further by insecurity. As demonstrated by the 2022 needs assessment conducted by UNHCR in the 34 camps, literacy levels are very low among women and girls. Both in the camps and in the host community, ensuring education of sufficient quality remains a challenge, which has a negative impact on community resilience and the development of transferable skills relevant to vocational training and further education. Further, many young Rohingya women continue to be held back by the lack of opportunities that they experienced while still in Myanmar to participate in education, curtailing their ability to participate in livelihoods activities and in decision-making within their family and community. Within the host community, school dropouts are high among adolescents and youth, especially adolescent girls and young women. This is attributed to factors such as increased living and transportation costs since 2017 due to the influx of Rohingya refugees, and a subsequent lack of family income to ensure continuous enrolment of particularly girls in school – alongside increased security concerns while travelling to school; and school dropouts attributed in part to young people pursuing jobs within the Refugee response instead of completing their education. COVID-19 further deteriorated access to education, halving access to education for children in the Rohingya refugee camps and with most children unable to continue their schooling from home during the closure of schools.Meanwhile, education of girls and young women is the best-known means to avert trafficking, early marriage, sexual exploitation, forced labour, and unwanted pregnancies, and is key to creating more resilient families and communities with improved health outcomes as well as food and livelihoods security. Yet, there is a lack of suitable educational opportunities for adolescent girls and young women who have either dropped out of or lacked access to basic education both in the Rohingya refugee camps and in the host community. Impediments also include the lack of learning materials, teachers, facilities, and lack of family support. Against this backdrop, the current project aims to enhance access to quality, age-appropriate ‘second chance education’ opportunities among Rohingya refugee as well as vulnerable host community adolescent girls and young women through non-formal delivery, to increase their literacy and numeracy skills as well as life- and employability skills, including digital literacy and self-confidence. This is expected to enhance their opportunities to participate in further learning and skills development opportunities and to improve their mental health and well-being as well as self-empowerment, contributing to increased ability to contribute to decision-making processes within their family and community, and enhanced sexual and reproductive health. The project will assess and pursue opportunities to expand the current SCE offer implemented through UN Women’s Multi-Purpose Women’s Centres to cover more tailored learning opportunities in support of employment readiness, based on market assessments.

2. Rationalization
2 - How is your project aligned to the Rationalization Principles (in particular reference # 3 - Adopt an integrated approach, # 4 - Ensure predictability and continuity)?

The UN Women SCE project covers 4 Rohingya camps of 3, 4, 4e, 5 and 18 where UN Women MPWCs are established. The project activities are firmly related to the education sector strategy and DCA, UN Women partner for the project activities, has obtained permission from the government to contribute to the Rohingya refugee response, and participates in the Education Sector meetings, sharing its activities with the sector. Further, very few organizations are working with the target group of beneficiaries in the non-formal education sector, reducing risk of duplication while underscoring the need to do more.

3. Cross-cutting Themes
3.1 - Protection

Literacy levels in the camps are low among refugee youth, particularly among females between 15 and 25 years old, due to restrictive gender norms and practices and the situation in Myanmar before the Refugee influx in 2017, where it was common for Rohingya girls to stop attending school when they reached puberty. Low educational outcomes among Rohingya adolescent girls and young women contribute to their limited self-reliance, increasing their vulnerability and exposure to multiple protection risks. Likewise, in the host community, reports indicate increased school dropouts among adolescents and youth, particularly adolescent girls, and risk of early marriage. In response, the project proposes to enhance the protection and resilience of Rohingya women and adolescent girls in the refugee camps and host community by developing and delivering a non-formal 'Second Chance Education' learning programme, which will focus on the provision of basic skills in numeracy and literacy.

3.2 - Gender

The primary beneficiaries of the SCE project are young women and adolescent girls in the Rohingya camps and host community, with the expectation that their families and community will benefit indirectly from the project, contributing to enhanced family and community resilience of which empowerment of women and girls is a key part. While recent years have seen marked progress in improving access to education in the Rohingya refugee camps and in the host community, thousands of girls and young women continue to lack access to educational and learning opportunities.

3.3 - Age including child safeguarding

The project operates from a"Do No Harm" principle aspect. All staff, partners and contractors are personally and collectively responsible for upholding and promoting their work's highest ethical and professional standards. All project staff have/will complete mandatory Child safeguarding, PSEA and anti-corruption training (e-learning and field-level refresher).

3.4 - Diversity

The project aims to include women, girls, and gender-diverse populations irrespective of ethnicity, religion and other identities. Particular consideration has been given to women and girls living with disability; namely, the learning facilities and teaching methodology have been designed to accommodate PWD needs in terms of mobility and learning convenience. There are disability inclusion systems built where the accessibility, seating arrangements etc., are in place at the MPWC level. In addition, there are home base and outreach education services for people in need because of their disability, age, or ethnicity. Gender and Age Marker (GAM) has been analysed during project design and implementation. All the participants have equal learning opportunities from the project, and community-level ALC (Alternative Learning Committee) have been and will have been in place to support the learners for their accessibility and protection.

3.5 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

A Complaint Feedback Mechanism (CFM) is established at partner and MPWCs level both in camps and host community level. UN Women is also establishing a well-designed CFM which will enable participants and the community to be more engaged with the project. Through this mechanism, complaints are received through various channels (group sharing, hotline, one-to-one session, voice message) covering verities of issues concerning educational assistance, harassment, protection, livelihood needs or other misconducts

3.6 - Does your project have any DRR/DRM elements and/or environmental issues and/or climate change mitigation measures? If yes, please explain.

There are disaster risk reduction (DRR) life skill sessions for the learners during the project period. Besides this, during disaster on-set, the extra sessions are conducted by the partner staff and the post-disaster survey among the participants as needed. The data can be shared with relevant sectors when required.
