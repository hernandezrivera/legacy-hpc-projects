Project Summary

SAWAB’s Shelter and NFI plan for the 2025 Joint Response Plan (JRP) is focused on improving resilience, safety, and quality of life for 32,000 households (HHs) of Rohingya refugees and host communities in Bangladesh. The plan addresses shelter and non-food item (NFI) needs, with an emphasis on supporting vulnerable groups, such as elderly individuals, people with disabilities, and gender-diverse populations. This initiative responds to the findings of a 2023 Multi-Sector Needs Assessment, which identified significant shelter damage, overcrowding, and a continued shortage of essential NFIs.
To address these needs, SAWAB plans to build 150 new Mid-Term Shelters (MTS) in Camp 14 (Block E), focusing on families whose shelters are beyond repair. These shelters will be constructed using weather-resistant materials and equipped with solar lighting for sustainability. Porter support will be provided to assist extremely vulnerable households (EVHHs), ensuring that they can access necessary resources. In addition to new construction, 300 existing shelters will be repaired and maintained in Camps 9 (Block C) and 12 (Block B), ensuring these shelters remain safe and livable for high-needs families.
Alongside shelter support, SAWAB will distribute essential NFIs to 27,200 Rohingya households in Camps 6, 8E, 9, 11, 12, and 14. The NFIs will include culturally appropriate clothing for women, men, and children, dignity kits, blankets, mosquito nets, kitchen sets, and other household items. The distribution plan prioritizes people with specific needs (PwSN), such as elderly individuals, people with disabilities, and gender-diverse groups, ensuring that these vulnerable populations receive the items they need in an accessible and dignified manner. Additionally, 4,800 households in the host community of Ukhiya will receive similar NFIs through three extra containers, extending support to the host population alongside the refugees.
Community engagement is a central component of the plan. SAWAB will offer training on gender-based violence (GBV) prevention and social cohesion for adolescents, women, and elderly community members. These training sessions aim to increase awareness of rights and responsibilities, while fostering a sense of community and cooperation. Refugees will also be involved in project implementation, taking on roles that promote self-reliance and reinforce social networks, ensuring the project’s sustainability and empowering community members.
To ensure that the most vulnerable households are reached, SAWAB will conduct systematic door-to-door assessments to identify EVHHs. This will allow the project to provide customized assistance and porter support to those in greatest need. By directly engaging with households, SAWAB can ensure that resources are allocated efficiently and effectively.
SAWAB is also committed to sustainability in its approach. In collaboration with the Energy & Environment Network (EEN), the plan ensures that solar lighting infrastructure is eco-friendly and supported by long-term maintenance plans. This collaboration ensures the continued functionality of solar systems beyond the initial installation. Additionally, the project adheres to Protection Mainstreaming guidelines to ensure that shelters and NFIs are accessible to people with disabilities and older persons. Training on Safeguard/PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) will reinforce SAWAB’s commitment to inclusivity and accountability.
The total budget for this initiative is $308,452, covering shelter construction, NFI procurement, porter support, and protective measures. This funding will address both immediate needs and long-term goals, promoting self-reliance, social cohesion, and resilience within the community. By actively involving community members at every stage, SAWAB’s plan helps create safer, more resilient environments for both Rohingya refugees and host communities, ensuring dignity and empowerment throughout the process.

1. Needs
1 - Identified Needs (including gender considerations)

This project is designed to meet urgent needs identified through multiple assessments, including the 2023 Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J-MSNA), Shelter & Camp Coordination and Camp Management (SCCCM) findings, and the Inter-Sectoral Needs Assessment (ISNA). Data highlights widespread shelter damage across the camps, with 72% of households reporting visible structural issues such as leaks during rain, damaged walls, and roof deterioration. Although 59% of households have attempted to improve their shelters in the last year, 41% were unable to do so due to limited humanitarian support. Funding for shelter activities currently stands at only 38%, further constraining the capacity to address these shelter needs. Camp-specific priorities have emerged from these assessments, revealing critical gaps. In Camp 9, 64% of households cannot afford needed shelter upgrades and report issues with enclosure and structure, while Camp 12 faces similar issues, with 66% of households unable to fund necessary improvements and widespread leaks and roof damage affecting livability. In response, this project will provide extensive repair support to Camp 9 (Block C) and Camp 12 (Block B), alongside the planned construction of 150 new Mid-Term Shelters in Camp 14 (Block E), incorporating solar energy at household level to ensure reliable lighting.The need for essential non-food items (NFIs) also emerged as a core priority, with nearly all households (97%) identifying shortages of blankets, kitchen sets, and mosquito nets. Specific needs within Camp 9 include high demand for mosquito nets, blankets, and kitchen sets, while Camp 12 households report needing kitchen sets, blankets, and solar lamps to ensure sufficient lighting. Given that 42% of households are running out of LPG for cooking before the next refill, many are resorting to firewood, increasing both expense and vulnerability. To address these issues, this project will integrate solar lighting infrastructure at household level into Camp 14 shelters to improve safety, particularly for women and girls, and to reduce GBV risks at night. All solar installations will follow established Solar Lighting Guidelines, incorporating a long-term maintenance plan to ensure sustainability.Feedback and communication challenges have also been prioritized, with only 52% of households aware of the complaint and feedback mechanisms available to them. Of those who have tried to submit feedback, 63% reported no action was taken, indicating a need for improved response systems. The project will thus strengthen face-to-face communication and feedback channels, aligning with community preferences for direct interaction, religious institutions, and observation-based information sources. To support vulnerable groups, particularly persons with disabilities and the elderly, the project follows ISCG guidance, including porter services, accessibility audits, and inclusive disaster preparedness. Additional support for women with disabilities will foster inclusion in community groups and leadership structures, ensuring that disaster management practices are participatory and accessible.Emergency preparedness remains a critical component, with 88% of households reporting they have received information on handling natural hazards. However, additional guidance is required for fire, landslide, flood, and cyclone scenarios. This project includes budget allocations for prepositioned stocks and accessible resources at emergency centers to ensure that all individuals, especially those with special needs, receive immediate support during crises. Overall, this project addresses urgent shelter, safety, and inclusion gaps by responding to community needs for safe housing, reliable energy, and comprehensive support, ensuring that resilience and preparedness are integral to camp life and that vulnerable populations have a secure environment in which to thrive.

2. Cross-cutting Themes
2.1 - Protection Mainstreaming

SAWAB’s Shelter and NFI plan for JRP 2025 integrates protection mainstreaming principles to ensure that all project activities foster dignity, safety, inclusivity, and access for Rohingya refugees and host communities. By prioritizing the most vulnerable, including people with disabilities, elderly persons, and extremely vulnerable households (EVHHs), SAWAB’s plan is built around door-to-door assessments to identify and support individuals with unique needs, providing tailored assistance such as porter services and prioritization during distribution. New and repaired shelters are designed for accessibility, ensuring individuals with mobility challenges can benefit fully from these safe spaces. Solar lighting is installed in high-risk areas to improve nighttime safety and reduce risks of gender-based violence (GBV), and culturally appropriate non-food items (NFIs) are distributed to meet the specific needs of women and gender-diverse groups with dignity. Training on GBV awareness and social cohesion engages adolescents, women, and elderly persons to promote understanding of rights, safety practices, and their roles in building community resilience. Staff and volunteers receive specialized training in Safeguard and Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) to maintain accountability, while accessible feedback mechanisms allow the community to report concerns. Information about emergency preparedness and response is made accessible, ensuring all community members, including people with disabilities, understand early warning systems and evacuation plans. SAWAB’s approach aims to establish a protective environment where vulnerable individuals are respected and empowered within the broader humanitarian response.

2.4 - Accountability to Affected Populations (AAP)

SAWAB’s Shelter and NFI plan for the 2025 JRP prioritizes AAP by ensuring that the voices of Rohingya refugees and host communities are heard and acted upon throughout the project lifecycle. Central to this approach is the establishment of accessible feedback and complaint mechanisms that allow beneficiaries to express concerns, offer suggestions, and report grievances regarding the assistance they receive. These mechanisms will be inclusive and accessible, particularly for individuals with disabilities, elderly persons, and marginalized groups.SAWAB will implement multiple channels for feedback, including SMS notifications, face-to-face meetings, community forums, and dedicated hotlines. Information shared will be simple, clear, and translated into languages spoken by the beneficiaries, ensuring accessibility for all. For those without access to digital platforms, community focal points and CiCs will hold community meetings to disseminate key information and address any concerns. Visual aids, such as posters and flyers, will also be distributed in high-traffic areas to further ensure inclusivity.Regular assessments and consultations will be conducted to gauge beneficiary satisfaction and gather input for continuous improvement. This process allows SAWAB to adjust its response based on the feedback received and ensures the assistance is relevant and effective.Staff and volunteers will receive training on AAP principles, emphasizing responsiveness, transparency, and respect for the dignity of all community members. This will foster trust and ensure that feedback is taken seriously and acted upon in a timely manner.Through these efforts, SAWAB aims to create a participatory environment where beneficiaries feel valued, informed, and actively involved in shaping the response. By embedding AAP into its operational framework, SAWAB ensures that its humanitarian work is not only effective but also responsive to the needs of the communities it serves.

2.5.2 Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) mainstreaming

SAWAB’s Shelter and NFI plan for JRP 2025 integrates Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse (PSEA) as a fundamental component of its operational framework. Recognizing the vulnerability of Rohingya refugees and host communities, SAWAB is committed to preventing any form of exploitation and abuse, ensuring that all interventions uphold the dignity and safety of affected populations.To mainstream PSEA, SAWAB will conduct comprehensive training for all staff, volunteers, and community mobilizers, equipping them with the knowledge and skills to recognize, prevent, and respond to potential incidents of sexual exploitation and abuse. This training will emphasize the importance of ethical conduct and respect for beneficiaries, creating a culture of accountability within the organization.SAWAB will establish clear reporting mechanisms that are accessible, confidential, and culturally sensitive, enabling survivors and witnesses to safely report incidents without fear of retaliation or stigma. These mechanisms will be communicated widely to ensure that all community members are aware of their rights and the channels available for reporting concerns.Additionally, the plan includes regular monitoring and evaluation to assess the effectiveness of PSEA measures and to identify any gaps in implementation. Feedback from affected populations will be solicited to continuously improve PSEA practices and ensure they are responsive to the community’s needs.By embedding PSEA into its Shelter and NFI initiatives, SAWAB aims to create a safe environment for all beneficiaries, promoting trust and respect while ensuring that humanitarian assistance is delivered without compromising the integrity and well-being of individuals. This commitment to PSEA is integral to fostering a protective environment that prioritizes the rights and safety of those most at risk.

SAWAB’s Shelter and NFI plan for JRP 2025 incorporates Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) and Disaster Risk Management (DRM) strategies to enhance the resilience of Rohingya refugees and host communities to climate-related risks. The plan focuses on integrating climate-sensitive approaches into shelter construction and NFI distribution to address both immediate and long-term needs while promoting sustainability and disaster preparedness.The project will construct Mid-Term Shelters (MTS) using climate-resilient materials and techniques that can withstand extreme weather conditions like heavy rainfall and flooding. These durable shelters will help reduce vulnerability to climate impacts and minimize the need for frequent repairs. By prioritizing robust construction, SAWAB aims to provide families with stable, long-term living conditions.In addition to shelter construction, SAWAB will focus on environmental sustainability by utilizing eco-friendly materials and implementing energy-efficient solutions, including solar lighting installations. This will help reduce the camps' carbon footprint, promote the use of renewable energy, and contribute to a more sustainable environment.Community preparedness is a key component of the DRR strategy. SAWAB will conduct training sessions to raise awareness about climate change adaptation and disaster preparedness. Refugees and host community members will be educated on how to respond to climate-related risks, including evacuation routes, safety measures during natural disasters, and emergency preparedness. This training will enhance the community’s capacity to manage climate threats and take proactive measures to protect themselves.Through these efforts, SAWAB’s approach to DRR and DRM will not only provide immediate shelter and NFI support but also foster long-term resilience and climate change mitigation, strengthening the ability of refugee and host communities to adapt to future environmental challenges.

Yes, SAWAB’s Shelter and NFI plan for JRP 2025 has embedded Emergency Preparedness and Response (EPR) measures as a critical component of its operational framework. Recognizing the vulnerability of Rohingya refugees and host communities to natural disasters and other emergencies, the project aims to enhance resilience and ensure effective response mechanisms are in place.The EPR measures include the development of comprehensive contingency plans that outline clear roles and responsibilities for staff and community members during emergencies. These plans are designed to facilitate rapid response actions, ensuring that shelter and NFI support can be mobilized quickly in the event of a disaster.Additionally, SAWAB will conduct regular training and simulation exercises for staff and community volunteers to ensure they are well-prepared to respond to emergencies. This training will cover topics such as evacuation procedures, resource allocation, and effective communication strategies during crises.Community engagement is also a key aspect of the EPR approach. SAWAB will work closely with refugee communities to raise awareness about emergency preparedness, helping them to understand risks, develop response plans, and establish community-led disaster management committees. This participatory approach empowers community members to take an active role in their own safety and resilience.Furthermore, the project includes provisions for maintaining essential supplies and resources in anticipation of potential emergencies. This includes pre-positioning non-food items and establishing accessible distribution points to ensure that vulnerable populations can receive immediate assistance when needed.By embedding EPR measures into its Shelter and NFI initiatives, SAWAB aims to build a proactive and resilient community capable of effectively responding to emergencies while minimizing the impact of disasters on their lives and livelihoods.
