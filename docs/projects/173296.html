Project Summary

Integration and institutionalization of C4D/Communication with Communities (CwC) approaches and interventions into health, nutrition, WASH, education, and child protection for sustained long-term and at-scale efforts. Strengthening the role of key influencers such as religious leaders towards increasing community ownership and participation in host and camps. In 2021, UNICEF will also focus on the integration and mainstreaming of Gender, Persons with Disability(PWD) and DRR in the key programme strategy and in behavioural matrix rapid community engagement and sensitization. C4D will develop key information package highlighting children in DRR. Enhanced and continued support to strengthen and reinforce community social accountability through information and feedback centres and closing the community feedback loop. Promoting social cohesion, confidence and resilience-building in the host communities through community engagement interventions in Ukhiya and Teknaf Sub-districts. As part of the global commitment, UNICEF will promote and operationalize Accountability to Affected Populations(AAP) theory and concept across the organization in Cox's Bazar including in C4D for enhanced accountability and broader organizational strategy with tailor-made and contextualized AAP interventions and plans i.e IFC. Supporting the capacity building efforts of local governance structures to improve results for children at the community level through the Local Governance for Children (LGC) programme.

1 - Identified Needs

The UNICEF 2018 Knowledge, Attitude, Practice and Behaviour (KABP) baseline survey confirmed the importance of face-to-face communication. Eighty-eight per cent of refugees described Mahjis as a source of information. Community mobilizers were the second most common source of information (28 per cent) while 20 per cent of refugees get information from religious leaders. In the recently published Joint Multi-Sector Needs Assessment (J MSNA) (1 October 2020), 25 per cent of households reported having never been consulted about needs, preferences and delivery of humanitarian assistance whereas 22 per cent mentioned of rarely consulted.A recent radio listenership survey found that about 90 per cent of people heard COVID-19 messages on the radio while 99 per cent wanted more information on COVID-19 through radio. We also made some progress which have come out through KABP Mid-line study 2019 which shows progress in households and communities on the key hygiene prevention and response practices including hand washing with soap, appropriate food safety handling and care for children. Hand washing practice before feeding children increased by 97.4 per cent from 92.5 per cent. Ukhiya and Teknaf are highly vulnerable upazillas with poor living conditions and coverage of basic services. Livelihood activities and markets have been significantly affected by the refugee influx. Eighty-five per cent of participants in a recent survey did not feel safe having Rohingya refugees nearby. They perceive that the influx has led to an increase in the cost of living (79 per cent), traffic accidents (74 per cent), and crime (24 per cent). Empowering Rohingya refugees and host communities to provide feedback is key to enable a relevant, conflict-sensitive and localised response. Using established feedback systems supplemented with additional feedback through targeted outreach and engagement, such as perception surveys and focus group discussions, will help provide data that can be consolidated and can support sectors’ in decision-making.To address the needs and gaps in integration of gender, age and disability perspectives into the response, UNICEF will: continue to collect disaggregate data by sex, age and disability; ensure gender-balanced teams (staff and volunteers); use mechanisms and create opportunities to engage community members and ensure equal access of women, girls, boys and men in the design, implementation and evaluation of integrated programs. Although a majority of refugees state that they are finding it easier to access information about the basics of services (who, what, where and when) and navigate their way around their camp, there are still many information gaps. For example, the elderly, those living with disabilities, adolescent girls, and women who are unable to attend a listening group or walk around the camp are still failing to get access to the basic information about delivery of services. It is essential to maintain and sustain community engagement interventions at the camp level through community mobilization volunteers. Language-appropriate radio and offline audio programmes and pictorial IEC materials have addressed some of the communication gaps in information provision but continued to be produced for 2020. Accountability will remain a key UNICEF priority in the years to come as well. UNICEF partners to collect feedback, grievances or complaints and respond to them with effective and timely data sharing and evidence generation with other agencies/sectors. While there has been some communication, accountability mechanisms are in place e.g Betar Sanglap with the host communities, there are still information gaps on key issues that will also be addressed in the 2021 UNICEF plan.
