Project Summary

The project aims to recover and expand the economic situation of the returnees, Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs), and host community business owners in northwest Syria.
These early recovery efforts will be achieved by, firstly, conducting a needs assessment on understanding the factors of production (Labor, Capital, Input, and Technology) for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) which will lead to the development of customized business development plans for local SMEs; and secondly, by providing a combination of business development services in the form of consultancy and seed grants based on the needs of the local businesses to revive and enhance the production capacities of local businesses in the targeted locations Ex: supporting the commuting sector, engine maintenance, and Support maintenance of water pumping. To enhance social cohesion and reduce competition for resources, the project will have two activities
Activity 1 - Financial support, including grants, seed funds, and subsidized loans to businesses with high potential for job retention and creation
1000 businesses will receive tools and equipment on average cost 2000 USD for each SME supporting 30% IDPs and 70% host community enterprises, including 30 female business owners.
Activity 2 - Provide vocational and skills training opportunities
1000 beneficiaries will receive vocational training for 3 months.
The project will be closely monitored through an array of instruments specifically developed for the purpose of the project and support these SME owners with business grants.

4 - Needs

Across Syria, 14.2 million people need humanitarian assistance, with 12.4 million people being considered food insecure, of which 1.3 million are severely food insecure and 1.8 million at risk of food insecurity. Over ten years of continued conflict causing protracted displacement and loss/lack of livelihoods opportunities and wide infrastructure destruction or deterioration, contribute to high socio-economic vulnerability levels, hampering people's access to food. Covid-19, depreciation of the local currency, inflation, rising fuel costs and electricity shortages, and economic crises have worsened the situation during 2021, further exacerbating food insecurity. Around, 6.7 million people are displaced within Syria, with 4.8 million people relying on monthly food assistance. Access to food, livelihoods, and employment opportunities are the top-three priority needs identified by the displaced populations, regardless of the length of their displacement. Over 1.7 million people currently living in last resort camps are among the population groups most affected by the deteriorating food insecurity situation with no access to livelihoods and total reliance on humanitarian assistance. Accordingly, approximately 50 percent of Syrians spend more than 75 percent of their expenditure on food.

5 - Implementation of activities

• Orientation with stakeholders to explain to them the project idea, objective, and responsibilities• Coordination and the identification of the community representative board (CRB), the CRB member will work as volunteers.• Identification of the sectors need in the community.• Develop a framework and template for local market production needs analysis• Analysis on factors of production, gaps, and potential business target groups• Develop seed grants mechanism in line with the outcomes/recommendations of the needs’ analysis• Conduct a prioritization assessment to select 1000 SMEs to be integrated into the program/call for expression of interest. (This will include financial and management assessments, taking into consideration operational status, size, production rate, growth potential, finances and asset/technical needs)• Deliver customized business development plans and consulting services to each SME, which will address the current operational/production issues, gaps and enhance overall productivity potential• Procure essential services or materials/assets/equipment according to the business development plans and submitted response plan from the business owners through in-kind seeds grants• WATAN team will Monitor and follow up job vacancies, referring job seekers to the appropriate vacancies• Conduct follow-up visits to SMEs.• Conduct needs assessment to define the needed vocations to conduct vocational training.

9 - Protection Risk Analysis

Resources and response capacities being limited and far below known needs, there will always be the perception of biased targeting of beneficiaries or of biased selection of response activities, thereby creating or exacerbating tension;Furthermore, the risk of aid diversion / Market distortion and markets volatility are very likely in such a complex context in Northwest.Mitigation measures: 1. Perform conflict analysis before planning the response; 2. Ensure that a comprehensive needs analysis informs the response formulation and that this is openly documented and made transparent; 3. Make sure those in similar situations of need, at least within the geographical areas of response, are reached equally; 4. Obtain validation of the prioritization and of the formulated response from a representative community group, or through well-selected key informants and/or participation mechanisms; 5. Communicate the planned response, and the rationale behind, it to the communities concerned; 6. Efficiently deliver on the obligation of ‘Accountability to Affected Populations’, by keeping the communities concerned regularly informed of the project and its implementation; 7. Make sure some process of receiving complaints and of responses to complaints is put in place; 8. Have in reserve a strategy in case the planned response does unexpectedly create tension (e.g. add-on of other activities or change in activities, in consultation with concerned communities.

11 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions: 1- Levels of support to targeted households are large enough to meet needs on the ground 2- Activities supported by the WATAN are coherent and lessons can be consolidated to have an impact on policy and implementation. 3- Approvals are granted by the local government to start project implementation 4- The exchange rate of local currency is stable. Risk: 1- Needs on the ground are higher than available resources. 2- Factors of implementation change making a change in the policy or implementation plan a necessity 3- Delay in implementing project activities due to delay in receiving consent from related government bodies. 4- Rapid fluctuation of the local currency will affect negatively the procurement process where some supplier might withdraw their offers in case of currency depreciation. Mitigation measures: 1- WATAN will apply solid selection criteria methods of beneficiaries. 2- WATAN will plan for different implementation methods to be able to adapt to abrupt changes. 3- WATAN will advocate through different means to chase up the approval process. 4- WATAN will speed up the bidding process to reduce the risk accompanied by currency fluctuation.
