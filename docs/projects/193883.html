Project Summary

DanChurchAid/Norwegian Church Aid (DCA/NCA) and its partner, MAAN Development Centre (MAAN) propose a project that aims at supporting 300 crisis-affected families whose children were exposed to child protection threats, enabling them to meet their basic needs through multipurpose cash assistance.
The pool of beneficiaries will be the families whose children were targeted previously by the child protection response through MAAN development center, where they received psychological first aid and referral services. However, based on the available data, the majority of previously targeted children and their families are socioeconomically vulnerable and are with poor access the basic services.
The 300 families will be targeted based on socioeconomic and vulnerability criteria. The targeted localities are Gaza north (Beit Hanoun, Beit Lahiya, Jabalia, Jabalia camp, AL Qarya Al Badawiya), Der Al Balah (Burij, Maghazi, Nusirat, Zawaida, Der Al Balah), Khanyounis (Mawasi, Qarara, Bani Suhaila, Khanyounis Camp, Qa' Al Quren) and Rafah (Al Bayuk, Rafah, Rafah camp, Shokat Al Sofi, Tal Al Soltan).
The targeted families will be selected based on their socioeconomic vulnerabilities making use of the UVAT. Criteria will include the families with monthly income below the poverty line, unemployed head of household and vulnerable female headed households. DCA/NCA and MAAN will ensure that beneficiaries have not received other assistance from other organizations and/or the governmental social protection schemes through coordination with relevant actors. The proposed intervention has been coordinated with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) and food security sector.
The project will provide the target families with multipurpose cash assistance for four months with a value of 1081 NIS per family per month.
The project contributes to HRP strategic objective #2: “The basic needs of vulnerable Palestinians living under occupation are met through the provision of quality basic services and improved access to resources, in accordance with the rights of protected persons under International Humanitarian Law”, and to the CWG MPCA objective: “Vulnerable HHs in oPt affected by shocks and stresses due to conflict and natural disaster supported with MPCA are able to meet their immediate essential needs and access to lifesaving services, decreases the incidence of negative coping strategies”.

1 - Needs

Since 2018, MAAN has been managing the child protection emergency team which contributes to timely and efficient responses to child protection risks and violations during emergencies. This aims at supporting stronger child response mechanisms and enhance access of emergency-affected children and caregivers to quality gender-sensitive and disability-sensitive child protection systems including emergency responses. The child protection emergency team is part of the national emergency preparedness mechanism in the Gaza Strip involving different actors of the Ministry of Health, UNMAS, MoSD, UNICEF, Human rights organizations, INGOs, NGOs, CBOs, and community members. The role of the child protection emergency team was significant in addressing the child protection risks during the military escalations on Gaza, where hundreds of children were supported with psychological first aid and referral services. As part of the process, MAAN has established a database of emergency-affected families whose children were exposed to child protection concerns which included an assessment of the families’ needs and priorities. Relying on the available data and the updated need assessment conducted in January 2022, a total of 397 families were identified as the most vulnerable with poor access to basic needs and services. The 397 families composed of 2959 individuals: 805 girls, 946 boys, 593 women and 615 men - including 68 elderlies, 207 people with disability and 95 families that are headed by either female or PWD. In reference to the families’ records and surveys, the number of children (females and males) who experienced child protection risks, is classified as a) child protection risks associated with military actions (209 boys, 140 girls), b) child protection risks associated with natural hazards (9 boys, 4 girls), c) violation of children’s rights (132 boys, 109 girls), d) violation of children’s safety (11 boys, 12 girls). 47% of the families are from Gaza north, 21.9% from Der Al Balah, 5.8% from Khanyounis and 24.7% from Rafah. Based on the assessment, 38% of the families rent the shelter, while 62% of them own their shelter, 59% of the families have shelters with winterization-related risks and 87% suffer from poor in-house WASH facilities (toilets or kitchen). 100% of the families reported that access to food is their priority need that they face challenges to address (30.2% of the families reported that food items are the first priority need, while 54.4% of them reported food items are the second priority need). Upon the assessment, other priority needs of the pool of beneficiaries are clothes (for 79.1% of the families), housing maintenance (for 60% of the families), non-food items (for 60% of the families), education expenses (for 38% of the families), health and medical expenses (for 18% of the families) and housing rent allowances (for 8% of the families). Among the assessed families, 63% are abject poor and other 37% of the families were found as poor. The majority of the families rely on negative coping strategies to handle the poor access to basic needs which included reducing the number of meals, reducing consumed food quantities and accumulating debts on food, shelter, and Nonfood items. In addition, the bad socioeconomic situation and food insecurity have negative reflections on the social relations of the targeted families and undermine the psychological wellbeing of women. Women and girls of the assessed families are bearing specific burdens due to poor access to basic services and the deteriorated socioeconomic situation, as they are majorly responsible for the domestic work and care of family members. Furthermore, it is found that insufficient source of food or income is a major factor in increasing the stress and tension inside the home. The assessment has revealed that poor access to basic services, especially food needs, is the main cause for undernutrition especially forwomen (lactating, neonates and pregnant), children especially those under 5 years and older people. Women and elderlies sometimes reduce the no. of meals per day to make food available for other family members. As a result of restricted access to economic resources - especially for females and vulnerable groups such as people with disabilities - the target group are relying heavily on humanitarian support, with living conditions severely undermined and high rates of poverty and unemployment. The inadequate access of the target group to basic services - especially in relation to food and water, undermines the ability of women and girls to meet their essential and secondary needs. Additionally, people with disabilities face severe barriers to access basic needs, especially for females with disabilities.Upon DCA/NCA and MAAN experience, unrestricted cash transfer modality proved to be more effective and relevant to address multisectoral needs of vulnerable families compared to restricted modalities (provide more flexibility to beneficiaries towards addressing the different needs of different vulnerable groups, including women, men, children, older people and PWD’s). DCA/NCA found that the MPCA is a relevant modality considering the current market situation in the target localities in terms of 1) accessibility by the project target groups, with no major safety/security concerns especially for women and girls to access the market, 2) availability of basic commodities in the markets with reasonable prices considering the endorsed transfer value.

2 - Activities

The project will target 300 vulnerable families from the Gaza Strip (out of the 397 families described under the needs section) with Multi-Purpose Cash Assistance (MPCA) over four months with a transfer value of 1081 NIS (app. 304 USD) per family per month – in accordance with the Gaza CWG standards and the endorsed minimum expenditure basket (MEB). The 300 family’s composition is as such: 2,236 individuals: 608 girls, 716 boys, 447 women, 465 men – including 51 elderlies, 157 PwD, 72 families headed by either female or PwD). The project will target Gaza north (Beit Hanoun, Beit Lahiya, Jabalia, Jabalia camp, AL Qarya Al Badawiya, ), Der Al Balah ( Burij, Maghazi, Nusirat, Zawaida, Der Al Balah), Khanyounis (Mawasi, Qarara, Bani Suhaila, Khanyounis Camp, Qa' Al Quren) and Rafah (Al Bayuk, Rafah, Rafah camp, Shokat Al Sofi, Tal Al Soltan). The MPCA modality will allow beneficiaries to address their basic needs and priorities as they deem relevant. Time and locations of cash distribution will be identified based on consultation with target group, women and people with disabilities in particular. Cash distribution facilities (banks) will be ensured to be easy to access for people with disabilities. Cash distribution modality through banks, in general, implies less direct contact between humanitarian staff who provide the service and beneficiaries which is considered as a risk mitigation measure for SEA. In case women and/or people with disabilities will not be able to receive MPCA (i.e., due to accessibility issue is relation to people with disabilities, women do not have control over the use of cash/can be subjected to violence due to receiving cash), the project will consider other modality for cash disbursement or by referring the beneficiaries to other modalities to address the mentioned groups’ needs (i.e. vouchers, in kind distributions). The targeted families will be selected based on clear criteria and scoring system, including their socioeconomic vulnerabilities and making use of the Unified Vulnerability Assessment Tool (UVAT). Criteria will include families with monthly income below the poverty line, unemployed head of household and vulnerable female headed households. Well trained social workers will conduct home visits to complete the UVAT before analyzing the data, scoring, and selecting beneficiaries. DCA/NCA staff and MAAN M&E department will conduct verifications for a random sample of the beneficiaries to ensure they are selected based on the agreed upon vulnerability criteria. Furthermore, the community engagement committees will be consulted to endorse the qualified list of beneficiaries. DCA/NCA and MAAN will ensure that the qualified beneficiaries have not received other assistance from other organizations and/or the governmental social protection schemes. Families including people with disabilities, children under five years, pregnant and lactating women will be prioritized. The intervention has been coordinated with the Ministry of Social Development (MoSD). Further coordination with CWG, MoSD and other relevant actors for beneficiary selection will be ensured during the implementation to avoid duplication. DCA/NCA will conduct a PSEA session with its partner’s staff at the beginning of the project and will ensure that a plan is put in place on how the project will raise awareness about PSEA among their targeted communities and considering safe and accessible complaint systems. DCA/NCA and its partner will develop and disseminate awareness raising materials including messages about PSEA and the available complaints mechanisms and their entry points to the target groups through the orientation sessions that will be conducted at the beginning of the project. Target groups will be consulted about the preferred complaint mechanisms entry points, especially when it comes to sensitive complaints PSEA awareness material will be disseminated and shared with target groups through SMS’s, leaflets and banners. Through orientation sessions, MAAN will provide target groups with information regarding MAAN staff code of conduct, addressing PSEA aspects. As part of transition strategy and to enhance longer term economic resilience of the targeted families, DCA/NCA will complement the proposed intervention by linking the MPCA beneficiaries to its economic empowerment program, aiming to support the target families to establish and manage their own businesses as a poverty graduation model. This will include to promote the financial and saving capacities, provision of technical trainings (business creation) and followed by productive assets transfer (note: this is not part of the HRP and will be funded through another program of DCA/NCA).

9.1c - How will feedback be used?

DCA/NCA ensures the complaints and feedback mechanism is in place to inform decision-making, enhance accountability and transparency. This will be ensured through engaging beneficiaries in addressing the expected risks and challenges and empowering them to participate in the monitoring and evaluation process of the project. Beneficiaries will have access to both DCA/NCA and its partner’s complaint mechanism. DCA/NCA and its partner will consider the beneficiaries feedback and recommendation through direct contact and consultation with them. The complaint mechanism will provide the chance for all beneficiaries especially women and people with disabilities to express their concerns freely and provide their feedback on the delivery of project activities through web site, hotline, complaints boxes, directly to the field monitors, and directly to partners’ or DCA/NCA offices. The applied complaint mechanism considers specific standard operational procedures in case of sensitive complaint ensuring confidentiality. The operational complaints and feedback will be documented and analyzed by the M&E and program staff to feed into the decision-making process and inform possible corrections during implementation that ensures a better response to the needs of the vulnerable groups. The project will organize regular focus group discussions with the community members to collect feedback on the provided services. The collected information will be used to inform decision making by searching for possible areas of improvement and revisiting the implementation approaches and tools.

11. - Equal and impartial access to aid
11.1 - Equal and impartial access to aid

The response will be directly implemented by DCA/NCA and its local partner in Gaza. DCA/NCA will manage this response through its office and staff in Gaza and its Country Office in Jerusalem. The selected local partner and DCA/NCA staff in Gaza have experience working under conditions that lead to restricted humanitarian access and adjust timing and approach as required. DCA\NCA access approach is consistent with the humanitarian core principles (impartiality, neutrality, independence, and humanity) in accordance with IHL: 1) Humanity in addressing the human suffering of most vulnerable women and girls; 2) Neutrality so that humanitarian aid must not favor any political side; 3) Impartiality in providing services to women and girls based on need and without discrimination; and 4) Independence in advancing the autonomy of humanitarian project objectives from political, economic, or other objectivesThis project aims at improving the access of the vulnerable and crisis affected families to basic needs and services, realizing the humanitarian principles in the targeting process and in the delivery of assistance. DCA/NCA and MAAN adopt Anti-discrimination policy that criminalizes all aspects of discrimination and guides the implementation of interventions. An assessment will be conducted to select the project beneficiaries based on clear selection criteria. The assessment will be conducted by trained field workers, who have the required skills and expertise to collect precise data. The collected data will be analyzed and scored following an accurate statistical methodology and using Excel to ensure the accuracy of selection. All selected beneficiaries will be treated equally, impartially, and transparently, with particular attention to gender issues, considering the extra vulnerability inflicted on women in the society. The selection criteria will be communicated with the target groups from the beginning of the project through orientation sessions to ensure accountability. The project applies human rights standards and gender-segregated data at all levels of intervention. DCA/NCA and MAAN apply needs and rights-based approach to their work within humanitarian response. The intervention will improve the access of the targeted communities to equitable and accountable humanitarian assistance in accordance with safety and dignity. The MPCA will be adopted through the intervention as a dignified approach, where it addresses the needs of the different vulnerable groups including women, children, elderly and people with disabilities as relevant to their priorities.
