Project Summary

The project aims to improve the academic performance for 1000-targeted vulnerable children students (Children at risk of drop out of school, Children with disabilities and children in labor) from the Gaza Strip (Gaza City and North of Gaza) through providing them with necessary remedial classes by well-designed programs and high trained teachers according to their needs in addition to facilitate for recreational activities. The project extends to 12 months and intended to be implemented in coordination with known, registered and reputable community based organizations in full coordination with Ministry of Education.
Project consists of five main activities: a) Conduct inclusive training program 50hrs for 80 (60 females) graduates/teachers as 20/each group on key topics linked to innovative teaching approaches for catch up/remedial classes to children (children in labor, CwDs and children at risk of dropout); b) Provide children students with remedial classes in different subjects according to their real needs in order to raise their academic achievement; c) Provide educational tool kits and books for students, to reduce the financial burden on students and their families; d) Provide students with disabilities with assistive devices and special learning materials in order to ensure their equitable access to the educational services and make sure that none of them aren’t left behind; and e) Facilitate for educational trips for students in order to enrich their knowledge and practical witnesses for different places in the Gaza Strip.

1 - Needs

Frequent wars, continuous conflicts, siege & closures causing death & destruction left the high densely populated in the Gaza Strip in a place with severe vulnerability. The current isolation has taken humanitarian situation to an unprecedented level, with exhausted coping mechanisms, widespread absolute poverty & an inability of civil society organizations & formal authorities to meet even the basic needs of the population. The collapse of the economy has left little money to afford basic needs and access to crucial services; that make access to education less priority for the families, which led that families to pay less attention to follow up the school achievement for their children or even their accessibility to educational services. According to the World bank, the proportion of children in Palestine is 42.6% of the total population (PCBS,2020), which indicates a majority of children in Palestinian society & these results show that Palestinian society is a young community.COVID-19 pandemic has posed additional challenges to the education system, including due to the related deterioration in socio-economic conditions and collapsed infrastructure at all levels (internet and electricity networks and prices); in adequate WASH facilities and hygiene kits at schools, impeding their safe reopening; and the school staff capacities in using new educational approaches, including mixing face-to-face and distance learning. The latest escalation imposed additional challenges to the education sector which already faces a shortage of adequate school infrastructure. This is driven by a range of factors, including the longstanding Israeli blockade and the internal Palestinian divide, electricity blackouts and the recurrent escalations of hostilities. To mitigate overcrowding, around 65 per cent of schools in Gaza operate on a double-shift system, resulting in reduced hours in core subjects and foundation learning. In addition, the chronic electricity deficit has serious implications for children’s rights and ability to access education, both at school and at home. The situation is further aggravated for children with disabilities; according to the PCBS, 20 per cent of the overall people with disability in Palestine are children of which 46 per cent between the age 6-17 years old are not enrolled in schools. 19% of HHs interviewed in the MSNA with at least one CWDs reported at least one CWDs was not attending school (25% in the Gaza Strip). For the rest of the children enrolled in education, they face higher risk of dropout, due to gaps in adapted facilities and assistive devices at schools, adequate transportation means, and staff capacity to accommodate for their needs; 61% of the HHs in MSNA reported that their CWDs is facing challenges in accessing education services (71% in Gaza Strip). About one quarter of children aged 16-17 throughout the oPt, and five per cent of children between 10 and 15, are out of school (either dropped out or never enrolled). Dropout rates are higher for boys, who are expected to contribute to household income in poor families. Early marriage, commonly used by families as a coping mechanism to reduce expenses, is the most significant cause of early dropout among girls, followed by protection concerns related to friction with Israeli forces and settlers According to the HHs interviewed in the MSNA, the main reasons reported behind school children dropping out are: lack of interest in continuing their education, COVID19 related school closure and poverty. (Education Cluster HNO, 2021)The mentioned situation associated with deterioration in qualities of lives given the collapsing in economic situation thus most families can barely afford basic needs and they don't pay that much attention to education for their children; thus, we decided to respond accordingly to this need and provide vulnerable students (boys and girls) with catch up/remedial classes in order to reintegrate with other students.

2 - Activities

- Conduct inclusive training program 50hrs for 60 (40 females) graduates/teachers as 20/each group on key topics linked to innovative teaching approaches for catch up/remedial classes to children (children in labor, CwDs and children at risk of dropout); The training will be divided into two phases as the following: 1. Interpersonal Skills (Soft skills): 20 training hours on (leadership, time management, communication skills, arts in dealing with human patterns) this will enable the teachers acting properly with the students as they from various mentalities and environments. 2. Specialized training: 30 training hours participants will be introduced on new techniques in training methodologies such: informal education, education by doing, interactive learning and recreational education) as the targeted vulnerable students won’t show high commitment when it comes to traditional education due to the living conditions for them. The previous training program is a crucial response to female graduates needs and based on previous learning and experience with such category as teachers expressed difficulties in which how to increase the level of commitment and interest for the vulnerable students through responsive methodologies. Provide children students with remedial classes in different subjects according to their real needs in order to raise their academic achievement. This will be implemented in cooperation with local organizations and educational centers (not profit) in order to build the capacities of CBOs that give remedial classes to poor school achievers as each student will benefit from 2 subjects according to his/her needs for 8 months over the year. This component will target 1000 students (70% females); SWG will make best coordination with supervision department at MoEHE in order to ensure the validity of targeted students in addition to make sure of the appropriateness of proposed selected CBOs in order to provide the remedial classes. Provide educational tool kits and books for students, to reduce the financial burden on students and their families, SWG will support students with assistance which will distribute school bags and stationery for the 1000- targeted students (70% females). Provide students with disabilities with assistive devices and special learning materials in order to ensure their equitable access to the educational services and make sure that none of them aren’t left behind; the estimated reach number of students with disabilities 50 students; needed and proper coordination will be conducted with protection cluster and disability referral network to avoid any possible duplication and make sure that we have reach the most needy children; Facilitate for educational trips for students in order to enrich their knowledge and practical witnesses for different places in the Gaza Strip, these trips will be facilitated sort of recreational and non-curricular activities for children which will enhance the indirect learning process for them; each trip will have a number of 50 students with consideration of gender and age marker during the selection of them.Safety measure, social distancing and all practices endorsed by Ministry of Health and World Health Organization will be applied in order to avoid the spread of COVID-19. A comprehensive monitoring & evaluation practices will be applied during and after the implementation of the project’s activities to ensure smoothness implementation of goals as follows: Focus group discussions (FDGs) with children students and project stakeholder to be conducted on a monthly basis; - Bi-weekly meetings with project staff, teachers and service providers to ensure appropriateness of the type of intervention to the beneficiaries needs and priorities, field visits and reporting with facilitators, centers coordinators, trainers and experts through the existed Management information digital system as we will gather, measure, update and evaluate the project activities and beneficiaries in each center, and will provide a detailed statistic about each child and his progress. Inception, interim and end (technical and financial reports).

Many people talkabout applying a “principle of do no harm” or using a “do no harm lens.” Some people andorganizations distinguish between capital Do No Harm (the framework) and lowercase dono harm (the principle or lens)” The potential negative effects of aid emerged as a subject of discussion during emergencysituations including different scenarios. We include the following four points in order to make sure that we are implementing the DNH properly: recognition of the potential negative effects of interventions; taking into consideration not only beneficiaries but also the wider environment; taking a step back from and even questioning interventions; the quality of relationships with beneficiaries.
