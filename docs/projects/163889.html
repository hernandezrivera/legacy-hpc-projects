Project Summary

In 2020, UNRWA will continue to provide critical protection assistance to Palestine refugees, including to those who are displaced and those returning to their habitual residence in Syria. The Agency will seek to improve the protection of Palestine refugees in Syria through various interventions. Mainstreaming protection within UNRWA services will remain a priority. Corresponding to needs, protection training, including on disability inclusion, GBV, PSEA, addressing violence against children, will be delivered to a wide range of staff and volunteers. In addition, UNRWA will continue to build the capacity of programmes to strengthen case management and referral processes for high-risk protection cases, including unaccompanied/separated Palestine refugee children, cases of early marriage, persons with disabilities, older persons and those affected by violence (including women), GBV, abuse and neglect. Dedicated programmatic responses will be implemented through regular service-delivery and stand-alone initiatives to support the needs of vulnerable populations including those related to psychosocial distress. Areas most affected by conflict-related violence as well as displaced and returning populations will be prioritized. Legal support services will be offered while prioritising women and children experiencing GBV by assisting them in obtaining civil documentation and referrals to external partners. Mine risk awareness campaigns will continue throughout the country targeting students and other community members, with a specific focus on areas of spontaneous returns.
Protection monitoring, reporting and advocacy will continue to be prioritised through the participation of UNRWA in global protection mechanisms and the international human rights system (IHRS). This will be done through the interaction with relevant stakeholders, including the protection sector of the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) in Syria. In parallel, UNRWA will focus specifically on monitoring the return of Palestine refugees displaced within and outside Syria.

4 - Needs

Of the 438,000 Palestine refugees currently estimated to be in Syria, two-thirds have been displaced at least once since the beginning of the conflict. End of 2019, an estimated 40 per cent continue to be displaced within Syria, with Yarmouk, Dera’a and Ein El Tal camps largely destroyed. Despite the challenging humanitarian situation in Syria it is expected returns in 2020 will increase, as will HLP and CD needs. Palestine refugees may face additional protection risks when returning to Syria due to their legal status. Many of those who have returned are suffering secondary displacement. Moving in with extended family has been negatively affecting women and adolescents due to the lack of privacy.A socio-economic assessment conducted by UNRWA in December 2017 estimated that 91 per cent of Palestine refugee households in Syria live in absolute poverty. Rising inflation, increasing prices of basic commodities, a depreciated value of the local currency, destruction and damage of houses, and limited livelihood opportunities continue to undermine resilience and coping capacities. UNRWA’s response to these issues in 2019 has been constrained due to underfunding. Failing to provide the needed support will have a negative long-term impact, particularly on vulnerable groups such as female-headed households, and families of older persons.Protection risks and vulnerabilities identified in 2019 are expected to continue in 2020. These include: psychosocial distress and disorders; ERW hazards; increased rates of GBV, including PSEA; child protection concerns, specifically violence, child labour and early marriage; exclusion and discrimination in regards to vulnerable groups. The 2019 UNRWA Syria protection audit indicates that there is a ‎need for training of UNRWA staff on protection issues, beneficiary participation and inclusion of the most vulnerable; ensuring safety and dignity of beneficiaries and access to UNRWA services; a more efficient case management system.

5 - Implementation of activities

The Agency will achieve its objectives by implementing the following activities: 1. legal awareness and support services through five Family support units to GBV and other protection cases 2. Carrying out training sessions and awareness campaigns on key protection areas including GBV, PSEA, disability inclusion, and addressing violence against children, for staff and the community 3.mine awareness raising sessions and campaigns targeting staff, community (with a specific focus on areas of spontaneous returns) and students 4. dedicated programmatic activities to strengthen the response to psycho-social needs, foster social cohesion and prevent the adoption of negative coping mechanisms. Particular focus will be on persons with disabilities, older persons and UNRWA students 5. protection monitoring visits 6. Capacity building of staff in case management and referrals. Areas most affected by conflict-related violence as well as displaced and returning populations will be prioritised. Protection monitoring, reporting and advocacy will continue to be prioritised through the participation of UNRWA in global protection mechanisms and the international human rights system (IHRS), and through interaction with relevant stakeholders, including with the protection sector of the Humanitarian Country Team (HCT) in Syria. In parallel, UNRWA will focus specifically on monitoring the return of Palestine refugees displaced within and outside Syria. Monitoring and Evaluation: UNRWA has a dedicated Area Support Team comprised of female and male staff members and an SFO based Programme Support Team. Monitoring will be guaranteed through regular field visits and feedback from implementing staff based in the field and their respective programme. The Programme Support Office has a Monitoring and Evaluation system in place that closely follows up on and provides feedback and donor reports for all projects.

7 - Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

There are some risks that are beyond the project’s control. The most important of which are the following:A) Funding risks: UNRWA relies completely on generous donors funding towards its HRP to provide humanitarian and other service to Palestine refugees in Syria. In January 2018, the Government of the United States decided to halt funding to UNRWA core programmes and humanitarian assistance. However, with donors’ remarkable response in face of unprecedented financial challenges, UNRWA was able to continue its core services towards vulnerable Palestine refugees through 2018 and 2019. However, the funding shortfall translated into a reduced amount of cash assistance provided to Palestine refugees in Syria, and in a reduced number of distribution rounds. In addition to this, the Syria Field Office protection structure was decreased and funds for dedicated protection activities were limited, this resulted in protection services being spread thinly and some unable to be implemented. The implementation of the project activities is completely contingent upon receipt of donors’ funds towards the HRP. If financial challenges continue to emerge in 2020, UNRWA, as a mitigation measure, will be forced to review and reduce protection services provided to Palestine refugees. B) Tension and geopolitics in Syria might affect the implementation of the project in terms of increasing the number of vulnerable Palestine refugees and access to previously restricted areas. UNRWA will continue lobbying with partners and international community to advocate for Palestine refugees’ smooth access as well as restoration of UNRWA key service in newly accessible areas.
