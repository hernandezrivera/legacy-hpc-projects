Project Summary

According to HNO of education for the year 2019, the Syrian crisis has left 2.1 million children in Syria as out of school which means over one third of children are out-of-school so far, in addition to 1.3 million children who are at risk of dropping out of school, and a significant number of children who do have access to learning opportunities fall behind in meeting the basic early grade learning outcomes, so if the situation continues/ deteriorates it would make it much more difficult for those and more children to gain the basic literacy& numeracy skills they are not gaining by the time, which leads to a lost generation seeking other alternatives of livelihood under the exploitative child labour.
Therefore, Hurras has planned for non-formal education intervention for those children, with a set of activities that aim to slow down the declining of those children’ educational levels, with longer term objective to getting them back to school and engage with their peers in schools.
The planned intervention of Hurras has direct impact on children, through focusing on the capacities of the teaching staff, and the good quality of learning environment.
HRS has over 6 years of implementing education programs experience in Deraa, Eastern Ghuta, Idleb and Aleppo, HRS team has developed, tested and will implement, evidence-driven, cost-effective, scalable and adaptable quality interventions based on needs with the purpose to improve cognitive & social -emotional skills of children. By introducing psychosocial support, literacy and numeracy initiatives through libraries (NFE spaces) and in-service professional development for school staff; and creating opportunities for children, including marginalized children, to access those integrated NFE spaces.
The purpose of this intervention is to reflect HRS 2019-2020 strategic plan to contribute to improved learning outcomes and resilience of children and create pathways for out of school children to have access for appropriate and safe learning opportunities. HRS team also understand that the benefit to children will occur both through their direct access to learning opportunities and indirectly because to changes to parent/caregiver skills, therefore the intervention includes three main target groups: Caregivers, children and school staff.
The intervention is supported by the established relationship between Hurras and 285 schools in the NW through ongoing child protection activities in those schools.
Communities were selected using the available needs assessment using primary data collected by HRS staff over the last 8 months who have been working in those schools and using HNO for both protection and education as a secondary source to identify subdistricts that have high needs.
Improvement of reading, numeracy and social-emotional skills will be measured using standardized baseline and end-line assessments. In addition, Social and behavioural change of parents and school staff to both assess and deliver better quality of services will be also measured.

Needs

According to a multi sector needs assessment HRS’ team participated in 2018 in 59 communities (31 in Idlib, 28 Aleppo), 51% of the key informants (30 out of 59 participants) stated, although of sensitivity of the subject, that there’s child recruitment within their communities, 17% of them are children below 12 years old, All the answers regarding the child labour except for two (57 answers) identified child labour within their communities as problem preventing children from attending schools. According to the EGRA/EGMA study conducted in Idlib and Aleppo in late 2017, more than 90 per cent of Grade 3 children are not reading at grade level, and numeracy rates are similarly low.The proposed project would respond to children who have been dropped out of schools due to reasons of internal displacement, lack of support by parents/caregivers, through continuing to provide non-formal education using contextualized literacy and numeracy interventions, and awareness raising to parents on how to support their children reading at home, with a distribution of literacy booklets that could be used at home

Implementation of activities

The project focuses on integration of literacy, numeracy and PSS activities into identified schools through establishing static or mobile libraries in schools. The Libraries are used as a bridge to formal education and long-term supports for vulnerable children in the education system. In addition, Both low and high contact programs were designed to build parents and education providers skills and professional development resources to promote children’s literacy, numeracy and psychosocial skills. This includes their capacity to safely identify and refer marginalized children who face barriers that would prevent their access to the offered quality interventions.HRS team identified and reviewed a number of strategies that contribute to have a measurable impact on students learning in literacy and numeracy, and focused on the following success factors while designing intervention- Well targeted instructional interventions- Regular professional development of school staff through coaching and improve their ability to assess children and use that assessment to inform their practice. - Sufficient relevant and quality classroom materials - More literacy and numeracy instructional time - Parents, caregivers and community support for children’s learning and inclusion. The spaces act as a pathway back to school for out of school children that are identified through the community-based committees established by HRS in 28 communities. HRS is currently operating in 285 schools in Idleb and Aleppo using this fund aims to introduce the following activities as part of summer semester that would continue through fall of the coming academic year: -Establishing libraries: this would include construct/ rehabilitate 60 libraries, and those libraries will offer 1 reading and 1 PSS session per week and as a total to 1500 children. Each library will have a librarian trained by HRS to deliver the sessions-Non-formal education: after schools' literacy and numeracy interventions, where Each child will receive 3 literacy sessions and 2 numeracy per week for 12 weeks to complete the cycle of intervention.-BTL and awareness raising campaigns to parents: HRS can conduct back to learning campaign of 2 hours session per a group of 20 parents per school and cover 200 schools, with a distribution of literacy booklets that could be used at home to improve children’s reading, and so a total 10 campaigns would be conducted.-Providing professional development to teaching staff, including training on child safe guarding to ensure a safe and inclusive learning environment.-Conduct out of school children mapping: Build the capacity of education actors at all levels in crisis-sensitive data collection to assess the barriers that prevent children from attending, identify and refer out of school children with accompanied child protection threats to case management services.-Conduct child functioning screening: Build the capacity of education actors at all levels in crisis-sensitive data collection to identify and safely refer children with functioning problems that impeded their learning and refer those children through case management and established referral pathways. HRS has the capacity to assess children in 200 schools and respond to 2000 cases.

Risks, Assumptions and Mitigating measures

Assumptions:• A safe environment allows the staff to implement the activities (low airstrike bombing).• No delay in the provision of financial resources.• Cooperation of local authority.• Engagement of local community with the committees.• Commitment of partner groups to Hurrass SOP.• Availability of services providers or services required as part of case management plan. Risks:Safeguarding incidents (Medium)HRS has a well-established safeguarding allegation system that is accessible by children, families and community members. Each activity is assigned a safeguarding officer and stakeholder are made aware of how to report incidents through multiple ways: online system, verbal communication with safeguarding staff and complaint boxes.Community and local council acceptance (Medium)Before engaging in any new community HRS outreach and community relation teams establish formal relationships with independent local councils and civil entities. The long history and positive reputation of HRS helped in establishing strong relations. Designated staff are assigned the role of dealing with any community conflict and all programs are reviewed prior to implementation to ensure they are both conflict sensitive and safe.The risk to personal safety of field staff (Medium)HRS has a duty of care policy and all staff are trained on safety measures including emergency locatorsSecurity incidents (Medium to High)HRs has a strong security department with qualified staff that monitors the security situation and put in place prevention and response plans. All staff receive safety training and all facilities are screened and provided with first aid and fire extinguishers in addition to a selected safe room and evacuation planInterference or influence by armed groups (Medium)HRs has strong relationships with the community and independent civil entities such as the health directorate, ED, and local councils and has been successful in the last 4 years in mitigating the risk and using such entities as a buffer. In addition, HRS has a clear SOP on how to behave in such incidents and clear disengagement criteria in case the risk could not be mitigated. Poor staff capacity (Medium)HRS invests in 4 weeks training of staff when they join, and ongoing coaching and supervision processes are in place. HRs work closely with partners to identify the capacity building gaps and put in place training programs to fill the gaps.Poor evidence-based and knowledge management due to remote management (Low)HRS has a strong M&E team that is independent of the program team. HRS uses staff from different offices and projects to cross-check its programming, in addition, the area managers who worked for 4 years with HRS are empowered to manage the day to day implementation.A potential loss of money due to theft or airstrikes (Medium)HRS’s finance team limit the cash on hand to a certain ceiling to minimize the risk and rely on an established network of cash transfer offices.
